The sun rose over the hills of Judah, and the shepherd led his sheep out from the fold. He walked ahead of them on the narrow path, his staff in his right hand, his rod tucked into his belt. The sheep followed in a long line, their hooves clicking against the stones.

He brought them to a wide meadow where the grass grew thick and soft. The sheep lowered their heads and began to eat. The shepherd stood among them, watching the edges of the field.

When the sun climbed higher, he led them down a slope toward a stream. The water moved slowly between smooth rocks. He stepped into the shallows and called to them, and one by one they came to drink. The sound of their drinking filled the small valley.

After they had eaten and drunk, the shepherd sat on a stone and looked across the flock. One of the sheep had wandered toward a thornbush. He rose, walked to it, and turned it gently back with the curve of his staff. He spoke its name, and it returned to the others.

In the afternoon he led them along a higher path. The trail narrowed between two cliffs, and the shadow of the rock fell cold across the ground. The sheep pressed close behind him. He held his rod ready in his hand and watched the ledges above. They passed through the dark place and came out again into the open light.

By evening he brought them to a flat place near a spring. He took oil from a small horn at his side and poured a little on the head of each sheep, rubbing it into the wool around their ears and brows. The flies that had gathered in the heat lifted away.

He set out their food on the ground, and they ate while a wolf watched from the far ridge. The wolf did not come down. The shepherd stood between the flock and the ridge with his staff across his shoulders.

When darkness came, he led them to the fold. The stone walls rose on three sides, and he lay down across the open gap. A cup of water stood beside him, filled to the brim and overflowing onto the dust. The sheep settled inside the walls, their breathing slow and even.

The shepherd looked up at the stars above the fold. He spoke softly into the night air.

"The Lord is my shepherd. I shall not want. He makes me to lie down in green pastures. He leads me beside still waters. He restores my soul."

The sheep slept. The shepherd kept watch. The house of the Lord stood before him, and his days stretched out ahead.
