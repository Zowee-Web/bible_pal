Evening came over the Sea of Galilee. The sun lowered behind the western hills, and the water turned the color of beaten copper. Along the shore, people still pressed close, calling out, reaching, lingering at the edge of the crowd that had followed Jesus all day.

Jesus turned to His disciples. "Let's go over to the other side," He said.

They left the crowd and brought Him along in the boat where He already sat. Other small boats pushed off with them, oars dipping into the dimming water. The shoreline shrank behind them. Lanterns flickered to life in the distant houses of Capernaum.

Out on the open lake, the wind shifted. It came down hard from the hills, dropping fast across the water, and the surface broke into white peaks. Waves climbed the sides of the boat and spilled over the planks. The disciples grabbed for ropes, for oars, for the mast. Water rose around their ankles, then their shins. The wooden hull groaned. The sail snapped and pulled.

In the stern, Jesus lay asleep on the cushion. His head rested against the wood. Water splashed across His feet, and still He slept.

Peter shouted over the wind. James and John bailed with cupped hands and a wooden bucket, but the water came in faster than they could throw it out. The boat tilted. A wave struck the side and sprayed across the men.

They stumbled back to where Jesus lay and shook Him awake.

"Teacher," they cried, "don't you care that we're perishing?"

Jesus rose. He stepped past them, steady on the rocking deck. He faced the wind and the rising sea, and He spoke.

"Peace. Be still."

The wind dropped. The waves flattened. The ropes that had been snapping went slack. The sail hung loose against the mast. Water lapped quietly against the boards, and the only sound was the dripping of the soaked men and the creak of the wet wood settling.

Jesus turned to His disciples. "Why are you so afraid? How is it that you have no faith?"

They stood in the stillness, water around their feet, hair clinging to their faces. They looked at the calm lake. They looked at one another. They looked at Him.

"Who then is this," they said to one another, "that even the wind and the sea obey Him?"

The boat drifted forward through the quiet water. Above the hills on the far shore, the first stars showed. The other boats that had set out with them drew near, their lanterns swaying, and together they crossed toward the eastern side.
