In the year King Uzziah died, the temple in Jerusalem stood quiet beneath a heavy sky. Inside, the lamps burned low along the walls, and the scent of old incense lingered in the stones. Isaiah stepped through the courts as he had many times before. His sandals brushed the threshold. His hands hung at his sides.

Then the air changed.

The ceiling seemed to lift away. Above him rose a throne, high and lifted up, and on it sat the Lord. The hem of his robe spilled down and filled the temple, fold upon fold of light pouring across the floor, around the pillars, over Isaiah's feet.

Above the throne stood the seraphim. Each one had six wings. With two they covered their faces. With two they covered their feet. With two they flew, hovering in the bright air. Their voices broke open the silence, calling one to another:

"Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of Armies!
The whole earth is full of his glory!"

The sound struck the doorposts. The thresholds shook beneath Isaiah's feet. The stones of the temple trembled, and smoke began to rise, curling up around the seraphim, filling the house until the lamps blurred and the walls dimmed behind the haze.

Isaiah dropped his eyes. His knees gave way. His voice came out cracked and small.

"Woe is me! For I am undone, because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips, for my eyes have seen the King, the Lord of Armies!"

One of the seraphim turned toward him. It flew down through the smoke, a live coal held in tongs taken from the altar. The coal glowed red, edged with white. The seraph came near and touched the coal to Isaiah's mouth.

"Behold, this has touched your lips," the seraph said. "Your iniquity is taken away, and your sin forgiven."

Isaiah pressed a hand to his mouth. The smoke kept rising. The wings kept moving in the high air.

Then another voice came, deeper than the seraphim, sounding from the throne itself.

"Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?"

Isaiah lifted his head. He drew in a breath. He spoke into the trembling room, his voice steadier now.

"Here I am. Send me!"

The seraphim still hovered above. The hem of the robe still filled the temple. The coal still smoked faintly upon the altar, and the doorposts settled into stillness as the words hung in the bright, smoke-filled air.
