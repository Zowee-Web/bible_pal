The dust of the temple courtyard rose under sandals and bare feet as the workers laid the last stones of the foundation. The morning sun climbed over the hills around Jerusalem, and the smell of cut cedar and fresh mortar drifted through the air. The walls of the city, broken and patched, stood in the distance behind the gathered crowd.

The priests stepped forward in their white robes, trumpets in their hands. The trumpets caught the light, polished and lifted to their lips. Beside them came the Levites, the sons of Asaph, holding cymbals of bronze. They took their places along the edge of the foundation stones, where the builders had set the first courses of the new house of the LORD.

A signal passed between them. The trumpets sounded, sharp and bright, and the cymbals crashed together. The Levites lifted their voices and began to sing, answering one another back and forth across the courtyard.

"For he is good,
for his loving kindness endures forever toward Israel."

The song moved through the crowd. The people pressed closer to the stones. Hands rose into the air. Voices joined the singing, rough and untrained beside the trained singers, and the noise climbed over the half-built walls and out into the surrounding hills.

Among the crowd stood old men, white-bearded, leaning on staffs. These were priests and Levites and heads of fathers' households who had seen the first temple before the Babylonians had burned it down. As the foundation course stretched out before them, marked and measured on the bare ground, they bent forward. They covered their faces. A great cry broke from them, and they wept aloud, their shoulders shaking, tears running into their beards.

Around them, others shouted for joy. Young men lifted their hands and called out praise. Children climbed onto their fathers' shoulders. The trumpets blew again, and the cymbals answered, and the singing rose louder.

The two sounds met in the courtyard and became one — the weeping of the old and the shouting of the young, the trumpets and the cymbals and the answering song. No one in the crowd could tell the joyful shouting from the sound of the weeping, for the people shouted with a loud shout, and the noise was heard far off.

Out beyond the broken walls, on the roads winding through the hills, travelers stopped and turned their heads toward Jerusalem. The sound carried over the stones and the valleys, rolling out from the bare foundation where the priests stood with their trumpets raised and the Levites struck their cymbals together again.
