The sun rose over Jerusalem, and the dust of the ruined hill stirred beneath the feet of many. Stones had been hewn, beams had been laid, and the builders stood now upon the foundation of the house of the Lord. The morning air was cool, and the scent of cedar drifted from the timbers brought down from Lebanon.

The priests came forth in their apparel, white linen falling about them, and trumpets of beaten silver in their hands. Behind them walked the sons of Asaph, the Levites, bearing cymbals of brass. They took their places upon the stones, their feet steady, their robes catching the light.

Zerubbabel stood among the elders. Jeshua the priest lifted his hand. The trumpets were raised. The cymbals were lifted high.

Then sounded the first blast, and it rang against the broken walls and went out over the valley. The Levites struck their cymbals together, and the brass rang sharp upon the morning. The singers opened their mouths, and one voice answered another, course by course, after the ordinance of David king of Israel.

They sang together by course in praising and giving thanks unto the Lord. The words rose plainly from the stones: that he is good, that his mercy endureth for ever toward Israel.

And the people stood round about the foundation. Men leaned upon their staves. Women held their children by the hand. Labourers set down their tools upon the heaps of stone, and the sound of the singing carried unto the houses of the city and the gardens beyond the gate.

Then a great shout went up. Voice was added to voice, and the noise of the people filled the place where the temple had stood and was rising again.

But among the multitude were old men, ancient priests and Levites and chief of the fathers, who had seen the first house in the days of Solomon. They beheld the new foundation laid before their eyes. Their hands trembled upon their robes. They lifted up their voices, and they wept with a loud voice.

And many shouted aloud for joy. The younger lifted their hands. The trumpets sounded again, and the cymbals answered, and the singers gave forth their praise.

So the weeping of the old was mingled with the shouting of the young, and the people could not discern the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping of the people. For the people shouted with a loud shout, and the noise was heard afar off.
