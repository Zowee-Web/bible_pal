The hills above the village rose green in the late morning, and a flock of sheep moved slowly along a narrow path worn smooth by many seasons. David walked ahead of them, his staff tapping the stones, his rod tucked into his belt. The sheep followed in a loose line, their hooves clicking against the rocks, their soft bleating carried on the warm wind.

He led them down into a low meadow where the grass grew thick and damp. The sheep lowered their heads and began to eat. David sat on a flat stone and watched them, then he opened his mouth and began to sing.

"The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want," he sang. "He makes me lie down in green pastures. He leads me beside still waters."

Beyond the meadow, a narrow stream curved between the rocks. The water moved slowly there, gathering in a shallow pool. One by one the sheep walked to the edge and drank, their reflections breaking gently across the surface.

"He restores my soul," David sang. "He leads me in paths of righteousness for his name's sake."

The path ahead bent into a ravine where the cliffs rose steep on either side. Shadows lay long across the floor of the gorge, and the air grew cooler. David rose and gathered the sheep with a low call. They moved together into the narrow place, pressing close around his legs.

"Though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for you are with me. Your rod and your staff, they comfort me."

He lifted his staff and tapped a stray lamb back into the group. His rod hung ready at his side. The cliffs echoed his voice softly. A bird called once from above and was silent. The sheep climbed steadily, their breath quick, until the ravine opened again into a wide field of sunlight.

David spread a cloth on a stone and laid out bread and a small flask of oil. He poured oil over his hands and rubbed it into the head of a ewe whose ear had been torn by thorns. He filled a cup with water from his skin until it spilled over the rim and ran down across his fingers.

"You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies. You anoint my head with oil. My cup runs over."

The sheep settled into the grass around him. The sun moved across the field, and the wind stirred the wool on their backs.

"Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life, and I will dwell in the house of the Lord forever."
