The builders set the foundation stones of the temple of the Lord into the cleared ground of the old courts. Dust rose from the broken places where weeds had grown for seventy years. Men hauled the heavy blocks on ropes, calling out together, setting each stone into its bed of mortar.

When the last foundation stone was settled, the workers stepped back and wiped their hands on their tunics. Then the priests came forward in their robes, carrying long silver trumpets. The Levites, the sons of Asaph, stood in their place with cymbals of bronze. They lifted the cymbals high, and the priests raised the trumpets to their lips.

The sound broke over the courts and rolled across the hill of Jerusalem. The trumpets rang sharp and clear. The cymbals clashed in steady rhythm. The Levites began to sing, answering one another, voice after voice carrying the song that David the king had given long ago:

"For he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever toward Israel."

The whole crowd took up the shout. Men lifted their hands above their heads. Women raised their voices with the men. Children stood close to their fathers, watching the priests, watching the cymbals flash in the sunlight. A great shout went up from the people, a single roar of praise, because the foundation of the house of the Lord was laid.

But among the crowd stood old men. Their hair was white. Their faces were lined. They had seen the first house, the house of Solomon, before the armies came and burned it. They had walked its courts as young men. Now they looked at the stones at their feet, at the small outline of what was rising, and they wept aloud. Their shoulders shook. Tears ran down into their beards. They cried with a loud voice, and their weeping carried over the courts.

Around them the younger men shouted for joy. They had never seen the first house. They saw only this beginning, these fresh-cut stones, this new foundation, and they lifted up their voices with gladness.

The two sounds met in the air above the temple mount. The shout of joy and the sound of weeping rose together, mingling, climbing the hills around Jerusalem. No one could tell the noise of the joyful shout from the noise of the weeping of the people. The sound was heard far off, carried on the wind, across the valleys and out to the villages beyond.

And the priests kept blowing the trumpets, and the Levites kept striking the cymbals, and the song went on.
