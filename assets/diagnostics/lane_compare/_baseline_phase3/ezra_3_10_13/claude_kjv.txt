And it came to pass, when the builders laid the foundation of the house of the LORD, that the company gathered upon the cleared ground where once the temple stood. The morning was bright over Jerusalem, and the dust of the labourers yet hung in the air. Stones, hewn and squared, lay set in their places, and the mortar was wet upon them.

Then came forth the priests in their apparel, robed and girded, bearing trumpets in their hands. With them stood the Levites, the sons of Asaph, holding cymbals of brass. They took their stations after the order which David king of Israel had appointed of old.

The trumpets were lifted up. A long blast went forth, and another, and the sound rolled over the hills round about. Then sounded the cymbals, brass striking brass, and the Levites lifted their voices in song. They sang one unto another, course answering course, after this manner: that He is good, for His mercy endureth for ever toward Israel.

And the people that stood by lifted up their hands. Some bowed their faces unto the earth. The shout that arose from them was great, so that the noise thereof was heard afar off, even unto the fields beyond the walls.

But among the multitude were the ancient men — priests and Levites and chief of the fathers — who had looked upon the first house in the days before its burning. These had grey beards and bent shoulders, and they had walked far from Babylon to come unto this place. When they beheld the foundation of this house laid before their eyes, they wept. They wept with a loud voice, and their weeping was bitter, and their hands they laid upon their heads.

Yet many shouted aloud for joy. The younger sort, who had not seen the former glory, lifted up a great cry of gladness. The trumpets ceased not, and the cymbals ceased not, and the song of the Levites went on.

So the weeping was mingled with the shouting. The one could not be parted from the other, neither could the people discern the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping of the people. For the people shouted with a loud shout, and the sound was heard afar.

The sun climbed higher over the walls of the city. The trumpets sounded yet again. The dust stirred about the feet of the singers, and the foundation stones lay fixed upon the earth, and the voice of that day went out into the hill country round about Jerusalem.
