In the year that King Uzziah died, Isaiah stood inside the temple. The lamps burned along the walls. The scent of oil and old cedar hung in the air. Outside, the city of Jerusalem moved through its ordinary day, but inside, the ordinary day broke open.

Isaiah looked up, and he saw the Lord seated on a throne, high and lifted up. The hem of his robe spread down and filled the temple. The stones of the floor were covered. The pillars stood inside the folds of that robe. The whole house seemed smaller than the edge of his garment.

Above the throne stood the seraphim. Each one had six wings. With two wings each seraph covered his face. With two wings each covered his feet. With two wings each one flew, hovering above the throne in steady motion.

One called to another, and his voice carried through the temple:

"Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of Armies! The whole earth is full of his glory!"

Then another answered, and another, until the calling moved back and forth above the throne. The sound struck the doorposts and the thresholds. The foundations of the temple shook beneath Isaiah's feet. The stones trembled. Smoke began to rise and fill the house, curling around the pillars, climbing toward the high ceiling. The light of the lamps blurred inside the smoke.

Isaiah fell back. He cried out in the shaking room:

"Woe is me! For I am undone, because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell among a people of unclean lips, for my eyes have seen the King, the Lord of Armies!"

His voice broke against the noise of the seraphim.

Then one of the seraphim flew down toward him. In his hand he carried a live coal, which he had taken with tongs from the altar. The coal glowed red. The seraph came near and touched the burning coal to Isaiah's mouth.

He said, "Behold, this has touched your lips; your iniquity is taken away, and your sin forgiven."

Isaiah stood there with the taste of fire and ash on his lips. The smoke still moved around him. The seraphim still hovered above the throne.

Then he heard the voice of the Lord, saying, "Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?"

Isaiah lifted his head. He spoke into the shaking temple, into the smoke, toward the throne.

"Here I am. Send me."

The seraphim kept their place above the throne. The smoke rose. The hem of the robe lay across the floor.
