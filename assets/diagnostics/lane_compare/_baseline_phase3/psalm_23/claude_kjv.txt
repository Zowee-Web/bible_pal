Now there was a shepherd that led his flock along the high places, and the sun was risen upon the hills, and the dew yet glistened in the low grass. The sheep walked behind him in a long line, their hooves soft upon the path.

The shepherd carried a staff of olive wood, worn smooth by the labour of many years, and a rod hung at his side. He went before them, and they followed after the sound of his voice.

He led them down from the stony ridge into a green hollow, where the grass grew thick and the wind moved gently through it. There he stayed his steps. The sheep lowered their heads and fed. Some lay down among the soft blades, folding their legs beneath them, and the youngest pressed close against the older ewes.

Below the meadow ran a still water, gathered in a quiet pool between the rocks. The shepherd led them down to its edge. They drank, and the surface of the pool barely stirred, save for the ripples about their mouths. The sound of their drinking was the only sound, and the cliffs above stood silent.

When they had drunk their fill, he led them onward by a narrow track between the hills. The path bent into a ravine, where the walls rose up steep on either hand, and the way grew dark beneath the overhanging rock. A jackal cried somewhere in the cleft. The sheep pressed in close upon his heels. He lifted his rod, and he held the staff before him, and he spake unto them in a low voice, calling each by name. They went through, and not one was lost.

Beyond the ravine the land opened again, and there in the high pasture a table of flat stone lay among the grasses, where the shepherd was wont to take his meal. He sat him down, and he brake bread, and he poured oil from a small horn into his hand, and he anointed the head of an ewe whose brow was torn upon the briars. The oil ran down. He poured wine into a wooden cup, and the cup was full, and the wine ran over upon the stone.

The sun moved westward, and the shadows of the sheep grew long upon the hillside. The shepherd rose, and he gathered them, and he turned their faces homeward. Before them, far across the valley, the walls of the fold stood in the last light, and the gate stood open, and he led them in.
