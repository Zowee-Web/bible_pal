When the builders had laid the foundation of the house of the Lord, the labour of many hands was made plain in the morning light. Stone was set upon stone, and the dust of the work yet hung low about the courts.

Then came forth the priests in their vestments, with trumpets in their hands. The sons of Asaph drew nigh also, with cymbals of bronze, to praise the Lord after the order which David king of Israel had appointed long since. The people gathered upon the broken ground. Wind stirred their garments. The hills round about Jerusalem stood up grey and quiet against the sky.

The priests lifted the trumpets to their mouths. The Levites took the cymbals in their hands. A sound went up from them, sharp and bright above the assembly, and the courses of the song were begun. They sang one to another by turns, praising and giving thanks unto the Lord.

"For he is good," they sang.

"For his mercy endureth for ever toward Israel."

The voices answered one another across the stones. The trumpets rang out a second time, and a third. Then the people shouted with a great shout, and the cry went out beyond the walls and into the open places of the city.

But among the multitude stood old men, ancient priests and Levites and heads of fathers' houses, which had seen the first house in the days before its breaking. They beheld the new foundation laid upon the earth. And they lifted up their voices and wept. They wept aloud, and the tears ran down upon their beards.

Yet many others shouted aloud for joy. The young men cried out, and lifted their hands toward the courts. The women raised their voices also. The children ran among the company, and their laughter was caught up into the noise.

So the weeping was mingled with the shouting, and neither could be parted from the other. The voice of joy and the voice of sorrow rose up together as one sound, and went forth from that place.

And the noise was heard afar off. Men in the fields lifted their heads. Travellers upon the highways stayed their steps and turned toward Jerusalem. They could not discern between the noise of the shout of joy and the noise of the weeping of the people, for the people shouted with a loud shout, and the sound was heard afar off.

The trumpets sounded yet again. The cymbals answered them. Above the foundation stones the great cry went on, and the dust rose into the air over the courts of the Lord.
