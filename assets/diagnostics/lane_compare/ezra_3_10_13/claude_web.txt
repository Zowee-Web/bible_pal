The workers gathered at the place where the temple had stood. Stones lay in neat piles across the open ground. Dust drifted in the morning air, and the smell of fresh-cut cedar mixed with the dry scent of the hillside.

The builders had finished laying the foundation. The wide base stretched across the cleared earth, and the cornerstones sat fitted in their places.

The priests came forward in their robes. They carried trumpets. They took their stations at the edge of the foundation, lifting the trumpets to their lips. Beside them stood the Levites, the sons of Asaph, holding cymbals of bronze. They raised the cymbals high.

A signal passed between them. The trumpets sounded a clear, ringing blast. The cymbals crashed together. The sound rolled across the foundation stones and out over the hills around Jerusalem.

The Levites began to sing. They sang the song that had been sung in the days of David, king of Israel. Their voices rose together, answering one another in turn.

"He is good. His loving kindness endures forever toward Israel."

The trumpets sounded again. The cymbals struck. The song repeated, voice answering voice across the cleared ground.

Then the people gathered around the foundation lifted their voices. They shouted with a great shout. The sound came up from every part of the crowd at once. Men stood with their arms raised. Women stood among them, calling out. Children pressed near to watch. The shout carried, loud and long, in praise to the Lord because the foundation of the house of the Lord was laid.

Among the crowd stood the older men. These were the priests and Levites and heads of families who had seen the first temple, the house built by Solomon. They looked at the new foundation laid out before them. They looked at the stones placed across the bare ground. They began to weep with a loud voice. Tears ran down their faces and into their beards. Some leaned on their staffs. Some sat down on the earth and covered their faces with their hands.

The younger people kept shouting for joy. They lifted their voices higher. They clapped their hands and called out across the foundation stones.

So the noise of the joyful shout mixed with the noise of the weeping. The two sounds rose together from the same crowd. No one in Jerusalem could tell one sound from the other. The trumpets kept sounding. The cymbals kept crashing. And the great noise was heard far away, carrying out beyond the city into the hills.
