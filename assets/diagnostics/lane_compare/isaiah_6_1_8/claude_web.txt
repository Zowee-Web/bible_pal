In the year King Uzziah died, Isaiah came into the temple. The stone floor was cool under his feet, and the smell of cedar and burning incense hung in the air. The lamps were lit along the walls, their small flames steady in the quiet.

Then Isaiah saw the Lord, seated on a throne, high and lifted up. The train of his robe filled the temple, spreading across the floor and folding around the pillars. Above the throne stood the seraphim. Each one had six wings. With two they covered their faces. With two they covered their feet. With two they flew.

One called to another, and their voices rolled through the temple like thunder breaking over stone.

"Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of Armies!
The whole earth is full of his glory!"

At the sound of their voices, the foundations of the thresholds shook. The great doorposts trembled. Smoke poured in and filled the house, rolling along the ceiling and pressing low until Isaiah could barely see the throne through it.

Isaiah fell to his knees. His hands pressed against the stone floor. His voice broke out of him.

"Woe is me! For I am undone, because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips, for my eyes have seen the King, the Lord of Armies!"

Then one of the seraphim flew to him. In its hand was a live coal, taken with tongs from the altar. The coal glowed red, and the air around it shimmered with heat. The seraph came down to Isaiah and touched the coal to his mouth.

"Behold, this has touched your lips. Your iniquity is taken away, and your sin forgiven."

Isaiah remained on the floor, his lips marked by the burning coal, the smoke still drifting around the pillars and across the stones.

Then the voice of the Lord came through the temple.

"Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?"

Isaiah lifted his head. He rose to his feet. His hands were still trembling, but his voice carried clear through the smoke and the trembling stone.

"Here I am. Send me!"

The seraphim hovered above the throne. The train of the robe still spread across the floor. The lamps still burned. And Isaiah stood in the middle of the temple, his lips touched by fire, waiting for the word of the Lord.
