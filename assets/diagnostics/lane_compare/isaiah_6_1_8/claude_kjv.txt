In the year that King Uzziah died, Isaiah came into the temple, and there, before his eyes, was the Lord seated upon a throne, high and lifted up. The hem of his robe filled the house, spilling down the steps and gathering along the stones of the floor.

Above the throne stood the seraphim. Each one bore six wings: two that covered the face, two that covered the feet, two that held them aloft above the burning place. The air around them shimmered, and the sound of their wings was a low rushing in the high places of the chamber.

One called to another, and the other answered back. "Holy, holy, holy," cried the first. "Holy is the Lord of hosts," answered the second. A third voice rose after them: "The whole earth is full of his glory." Their voices struck the pillars and the door-posts, and the great hinges trembled at the sound. The thresholds shook beneath Isaiah's feet. Smoke poured in, thick and slow, rolling along the floor and rising to the lamps until the house was filled with it.

Then cried Isaiah, and his cry came out of him broken. "Woe is me, for I am undone. I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell among a people of unclean lips. For mine eyes have seen the King, the Lord of hosts."

He stood there in the smoke, and his hands were lifted, and his face was bowed.

Then flew one of the seraphim unto him. In its hand was a live coal, taken with tongs from off the altar. The coal burned red, and the tongs glowed in the seraph's grip. It came down to where Isaiah stood, and it laid the coal upon his mouth.

"Lo," said the seraph, "this hath touched thy lips. Thine iniquity is taken away, and thy sin purged."

Isaiah stood still. The smoke moved about him. The seraphim went on with their crying above, and the door-posts went on with their trembling.

Then came the voice of the Lord, sounding through the house. "Whom shall I send? And who will go for us?"

The words rolled through the chamber and along the floor, and they passed by the burning altar and the trembling thresholds, and they reached the place where Isaiah stood with the coal yet warm upon his lips.

And Isaiah lifted up his voice and answered.

"Here am I. Send me."
