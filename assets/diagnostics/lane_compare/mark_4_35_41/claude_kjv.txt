When evening was come, Jesus turned unto his disciples and said, Let us pass over unto the other side. So they left the multitude upon the shore and took him, even as he was, into the ship. Other little ships also followed after.

The sun went down behind the hills of Galilee, and the water lay quiet beneath the keel. The men dipped their oars, and the prow cut a slow furrow through the deepening dark. In the stern lay Jesus, wearied from the long day of teaching. A cushion was beneath his head, and he slept.

Then arose a great storm of wind. It came down upon the lake suddenly, as the winds of that country are wont to come, sweeping from the heights and falling upon the waters. The sea was lifted up. The waves beat into the ship, and the ship began to fill. Timbers groaned. Ropes strained against the mast. The little ships that had followed were scattered into the blackness, and the men aboard the vessel of Jesus laid hold upon the oars and could make no headway.

One bailed with a bowl. Another clung to the side. The spray was upon their beards and their garments were heavy with the sea. And yet, in the hinder part of the ship, Jesus slept on.

They came unto him. They shook him by the shoulder, and they cried above the howling of the wind, Master, carest thou not? We perish.

He rose up. He stood in the rocking ship and stretched forth his hand toward the sea. Peace, said he. Be still.

The wind ceased. The waves, that had risen up as mountains, sank down. The water grew smooth as a polished stone. There was a great calm upon the face of the deep, and not a sound but the dripping of water from the gunwales and the soft creak of the timbers settling.

Then turned he unto them and said, Why are ye so fearful? How is it that ye have no faith?

They answered him not. They looked one upon another, and they looked upon the still water, and they looked upon him that stood among them with his garment yet damp from the spray. And they spake one to another in low voices, saying, What manner of man is this? For the wind and the sea obey him.

The little boat drifted on under the stars. The oars dipped again. The far shore drew near.
