Evening came over Galilee. The sun lowered behind the western hills, and the lake turned the color of beaten copper. Jesus had been teaching from a boat all day, and the crowds still pressed along the shore.

He turned to the disciples and said, “Let us go over to the other side.”

They left the crowd behind. Jesus stepped into the stern of the boat, and the disciples pushed off from the shore. Other small boats followed them out onto the water. The oars dipped, lifted, dipped again. The sound of the village faded. Only the creak of wood and the slap of waves remained.

Jesus lay down on a cushion in the back of the boat and slept.

Then the wind began to rise. It came down off the hills in sudden gusts, lifting the surface of the lake into white edges. The gusts grew stronger. The sail snapped. The water turned dark, and waves climbed the sides of the boat and broke over the bow. Water poured in across the planks and pooled around the disciples’ feet.

The men bailed with their hands and with whatever they could grab. Peter shouted to Andrew. James and John pulled at the ropes. The boat tilted hard, then dropped into the next trough. Spray covered their faces. The wind pulled at their cloaks.

Jesus was still asleep in the stern, his head on the cushion.

They scrambled back to him through the rocking boat. They shook him awake. Their voices rose above the wind.

“Teacher, do you not care that we are perishing?”

Jesus stood up. He braced himself against the side of the boat. He looked at the wind tearing across the water and at the waves rising over the sides. He spoke to the storm.

“Peace. Be still.”

The wind stopped. It did not fade. It stopped. The waves flattened. The slapping against the hull ceased. The ropes hung loose. Water still sloshed around their ankles, but the lake itself lay smooth, dark, and quiet under the last light of evening.

Jesus turned to them.

“Why are you so afraid? Have you still no faith?”

The disciples stood in the dripping boat. They looked at the calm water. They looked at one another. They looked at Jesus. They spoke in low voices, one to another.

“Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?”

The boat drifted forward on the still lake. Above them the first stars appeared. The far shore lay ahead in the gathering dark, and the oars went back into the water.
