David had walked behind sheep since he was a boy, guiding them across dry ground and through low hills where the wind bent the grass flat.

The Lord is my shepherd, he said. I shall not lack anything.

He had seen green pastures where the flock could lie down without fear. No predator near. No drought. Just thick grass growing along the valley floor, soft enough that the lambs folded their legs beneath them and slept. The shepherd led them there. He made them lie down.

He led them beside still waters. Not rushing currents where a sheep could lose its footing and drown, but quiet pools fed by slow streams. The water was clear. The banks were low. The flock drank without panic, their reflections steady on the surface.

He restored their souls. He led them in paths of righteousness for his name's sake.

David had walked through valleys where the shadows were so deep the sun disappeared. Rock walls rose on both sides. The light thinned. The air turned cold. Sounds echoed strangely. A man could not see what waited around the next bend. The shadow of death lay over everything.

He feared no evil. The shepherd was with him. The rod in the shepherd's hand was heavy enough to break a wolf's jaw. The staff had a crook that could pull a lamb back from the edge of a cliff. Those two tools were comfort enough.

A table was prepared. Not in a safe place. Not behind a wall. The table was set in the presence of enemies, in plain sight of those who wished him harm. The food was laid out. The cup was filled. Oil was poured on his head, running down across his forehead, catching in his eyebrows.

His cup ran over. It spilled past the rim and down the sides and onto the wood of the table.

Goodness and mercy would follow him. Not for a season. Not for a year. All the days of his life, those two would walk behind him the way a shepherd walks behind the flock.

And he would dwell in the house of the Lord forever.

The oil was still wet on his skin. The cup sat on the table, full past the brim, a thin line of wine tracing down its side to the wood below.
