David had kept sheep from his youth, leading them over parched ground and among the low hills where the wind pressed the grass unto the earth.

The Lord is my shepherd, he said. I shall not want.

He had known green pastures where the flock might lie down and fear nothing. No beast drew near. No drought withered the land. Only thick grass spreading along the floor of the valley, soft enough that the lambs drew their legs beneath them and were at rest. The shepherd brought them thither. He caused them to lie down.

He led them beside waters that were still. Not swift currents wherein a sheep might stumble and be carried away, but quiet pools that were fed by gentle streams. The water was clear. The banks were low. The flock drank and were not afraid, and their likenesses rested steady upon the surface.

He restored their souls. He guided them in the paths of righteousness for his name's sake.

David had passed through valleys where the shadows lay so thick that the sun was hidden from him. Walls of rock rose up on either side. The light grew faint. The air turned cold. Sounds returned strangely from the stone. A man could not perceive what awaited him beyond the turning of the way. The shadow of death was spread over all that place.

Yet he feared no evil. For the shepherd was with him. The rod in the shepherd's hand was heavy enough to shatter the jaw of a wolf. The staff bore a crook that could draw a lamb back from the brink of a precipice. Those two alone were his comfort.

A table was prepared before him. Not in a place of safety. Not behind a wall. The table was set in the very presence of his enemies, in the full sight of them that sought his harm. The food was laid out. The cup was filled. Oil was poured upon his head, and it ran down over his brow and gathered upon his brow and caught among his lashes.

His cup ran over. It spilled beyond the rim and down the sides and upon the wood of the table.

Surely goodness and mercy should follow after him. Not for a season. Not for the space of a year. All the days of his life, those two should walk behind him even as a shepherd walketh behind his flock.

And he would dwell in the house of the Lord for ever.

The oil was yet upon his skin. The cup sat upon the table, filled past the brim, and a thin thread of wine traced down its side unto the wood beneath.
