A shepherd tended his flock of one hundred sheep in the hills of Judea. Each morning he counted them, each evening he led them to safety. He knew every one by name, knew the markings on their wool, knew their voices when they called to him.

One evening, as the sun painted the hills gold, the shepherd counted his sheep as they entered the fold. Ninety-seven, ninety-eight, ninety-nine. He counted again. Only ninety-nine. One sheep was missing.

The shepherd's heart stirred within him. He could have stayed with the ninety-nine, safe within the fold. But he thought of that one lost sheep, perhaps caught in thorns or fallen into a ravine, frightened and alone as darkness gathered.

He secured the gate and set out into the wilderness. The path grew rocky beneath his feet. Shadows lengthened across the valleys. He called out, listening for any sound of bleating. Hours passed as he searched the hills and hollows where a sheep might wander.

At last, in a thicket near a steep cliff, he found her. The sheep had tangled herself in brambles, too exhausted to cry out. The shepherd knelt beside her, speaking gentle words as he freed her from the thorns. He lifted her onto his shoulders, bearing her weight gladly.

As he walked home under the stars, his heart overflowed with gladness. The sheep that was lost had been found. When he reached his village, he called to his friends and neighbors. They gathered around him in the lamplight, and together they rejoiced that the lost one had come home.

There is joy in heaven over one who was lost and is found. The shepherd who seeks, who searches through darkness and difficulty, who refuses to abandon even one of his own, reflects the heart of the Father who rejoices when his children return to him.
