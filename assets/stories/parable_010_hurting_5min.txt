In a small village lived a potter whose hands could create vessels of extraordinary beauty. His workshop was a place of joy, filled with laughter and the rhythmic turning of the wheel.

Then tragedy struck. A fire consumed his workshop, and in the chaos of trying to save his work, the potter's hands were severely burned. The physical pain was immense, but worse was the emotional devastation. His hands, once so skilled and sure, now trembled and ached.

For weeks, the potter sat in darkness, unable to work, unable to find purpose. Friends tried to encourage him, but their words seemed hollow. How could he be a potter who couldn't touch clay?

One morning, a small child from the village approached him. She held out a broken cup—a simple vessel the potter had made years ago. Can you fix it, she asked with the uncomplicated faith of youth.

The potter looked at the broken cup, then at his scarred hands. I don't know if I can anymore, he admitted, his voice breaking.

The child sat down beside him. My mother says that God is close to people with broken hearts. Is your heart broken?

The potter nodded, tears streaming down his face. In that moment of honest acknowledgment, something shifted. He remembered the psalm that speaks of the Lord being near to the brokenhearted, and he realized he had been trying to hide his pain rather than bring it into the light.

In the days that followed, the potter began a different kind of healing. He let himself grieve what was lost. He stopped pretending the pain wasn't real. And slowly, carefully, he let others help him.

The days that followed brought deeper understanding. What had seemed like a single moment of revelation became a journey of transformation, unfolding in ways that surprised and challenged. There were setbacks and moments of doubt, times when the old patterns tried to reassert themselves. But each time, there was a choice to return to the truth that had been discovered, to remember the faithfulness that had been proven, to trust in the One who had been faithful before and would be faithful again.

The transformation was not instantaneous or dramatic. It came in quiet moments—a shift in perspective during morning prayer, a different response to an old trigger, a newfound ability to pause before reacting. These small changes accumulated like drops of water wearing away stone, reshaping the landscape of the heart.

Friends and neighbors began to notice. They didn't always understand what had changed, but they sensed something different. There was a peace that hadn't been there before, a gentleness that softened sharp edges, a strength that came not from striving but from surrender. When asked about it, the answer came simply: I'm learning to trust more and control less.

The path forward wasn't always clear. There were mornings when the old fears returned, whispering their familiar lies. There were evenings when exhaustion made everything feel heavy again. But something fundamental had shifted. Where once there had been only one way of seeing, now there was another perspective available—a view from higher ground, a glimpse of how things looked from the vantage point of grace.

This new way of seeing didn't eliminate the challenges, but it changed the relationship with them. Problems that once felt like evidence of failure became opportunities for dependence. Weaknesses that once brought shame became places where strength could enter. The cracks in the vessel, it turned out, were exactly where the light got in.
