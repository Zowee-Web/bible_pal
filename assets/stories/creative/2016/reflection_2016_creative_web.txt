Worry has a way of narrowing the view — pulling the eyes down to screens and checklists and all the scenarios that haven't happened yet. The mind rehearses disaster as if rehearsal might prevent it, turning every unknown into evidence that something will go wrong.

But sometimes a wider view arrives uninvited. A sound from across the street, a child pointing at the sky, a rhythm that doesn't ask anything of us. The worry doesn't vanish — it simply moves to the side for a moment, and in that small clearing, we remember that the world is still larger than our fear of it.
