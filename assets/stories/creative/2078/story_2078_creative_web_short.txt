Archer woke up because the world was too quiet.

No garbage truck. No school bus brakes. No dog barking three houses down. He lay still under his quilt and listened to the nothing.

Then he looked at the window.

White light pressed against the glass, softer than any morning he could remember. He kicked off the covers and ran to the sill in his socks.

Snow. Everywhere snow.

It covered the mailbox and the fence posts and the trampoline. It sat in fat rolls along the branches of the oak tree. The street had disappeared completely. Where the road used to be there was only a long white field that went all the way to the Garcias' house.

His mom's voice came from the kitchen. "No school today, Arch."

He was already pulling on his boots.

Outside, the air tasted like cold metal and something clean underneath it. His breath made clouds. He caught a snowflake on his tongue and it melted into nothing before he could feel it.

The Huang twins were already building something in their front yard. Across the street, old Mr. Lindell stood on his porch in a bathrobe, holding coffee, just looking at it all. He waved at Archer with his mug.

Archer waved back.

The whole neighborhood came out slowly, one house at a time. Doors opened. Kids appeared. Someone's dad dragged a sled from a garage. A mom carried hot chocolate in a thermos and poured little cups for anyone who came close.

Nobody planned any of it. Nobody had to.

Archer lay down flat on his back in the front yard and made an angel. The snow pressed cold against his neck and ears. Above him the sky was white and close, still letting go of flakes that drifted down slow as feathers.

He stayed like that a long time. Just breathing. Just watching the snow come down from somewhere he couldn't see.

The whole day stretched ahead of him, open and unmarked, like the wide white yard.
