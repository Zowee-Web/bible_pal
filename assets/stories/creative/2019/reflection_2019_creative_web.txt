Encouragement doesn't always arrive as a grand gesture or a rousing speech. Sometimes it's a story told over a sandwich in a tired room. Sometimes it's a memory that surfaces at exactly the right moment, carrying with it the voice of someone who believed the work was worth doing.

There is something interesting about the way one person's faithfulness can reach across years and circumstances to steady another. A grandmother kneading dough, a stranger listening with her eyes closed, a student who asks to write one more essay just for practice — each one a small signal that the effort matters, that something is pushing back, alive and growing, even when the hands doing the work have grown weary.
