"That's not how you peel an orange," Nico said.

His grandfather looked down at the fruit in his hands. He'd been pulling the rind off in tiny, careful pieces, making a small pile of fragments on the porch railing.

"I've been peeling oranges for seventy-one years," his grandfather said mildly.

"You're doing it wrong, though. Watch." Nico took an orange from the bag and dug his thumb into the navel, pulling the skin off in one long spiral. He held up the peel like a trophy. "See? One piece."

His grandfather examined it. "Very efficient."

"Yours takes forever."

"Yes." The old man pulled another small piece of rind free and added it to his pile. The scent of citrus oil hung between them, sharp and sweet. "But mine smells like this the whole time."

Nico looked at the tiny orange fragments on the railing. Each one had released its own small burst of scent. His grandfather's version took five minutes. His own had taken eight seconds.

He sat down on the step. The afternoon was warm and still. Bees worked the jasmine along the fence. His grandfather's hands continued their slow, deliberate work — pinch, pull, place. Pinch, pull, place.

"Grandpa?"

"Hmm."

"Do you do everything this slow?"

The old man laughed. It was a good laugh — deep and unguarded, the kind that uses the whole chest. "Not everything. I eat cake quite fast."

Nico grinned. He reached into the bag and pulled out another orange. He looked at it for a moment. Then he pressed his thumb against the rind and pulled off one small, fragrant piece.

Then another.

His grandfather didn't comment. He just kept peeling his own orange, piece by piece, while the jasmine hummed with bees and the afternoon took its time becoming evening.

When they'd finished, they ate the oranges in sections, juice running down their fingers. The pile of tiny rinds on the railing was enormous. The porch smelled like a grove.

"Okay," Nico said, licking his thumb. "Maybe your way is better."

"It's not better," his grandfather said. "It's just longer. And sometimes longer is where the sweetness hides."
