Silas found the spot behind the library on a Wednesday.

He wasn't looking for it. He was looking for his soccer ball, which he'd kicked too hard at recess and sent sailing over the fence. He climbed through the gap in the chain-link and walked around the back of the building, scanning the grass.

The ball was there, resting against a tree trunk. But so was something else.

Between the back wall of the library and a row of tall bushes, there was a space. Not a room — just a gap, maybe five feet wide, hidden from the playground and the parking lot and everything else. The ground was covered in soft pine needles. A flat rock sat against the wall like a bench. Sunlight came through the bushes in bright coins that moved when the wind blew.

Silas picked up his ball. He stood in the gap and listened. He could hear the playground — shouts and whistles and the thump of jump ropes — but the sounds were muffled. Distant. Like listening to the world from underwater.

He sat down on the flat rock. The stone was warm from the sun. A beetle crawled across a pine needle. A bird sang three notes from somewhere above him.

Silas sat there for two minutes. Then three. The bell would ring soon. But right now the pine needles were soft and the rock was warm and the sounds of the world were far away.

He picked up his ball and walked back through the gap in the fence. At the edge of the playground he stopped and looked over his shoulder. The bushes had closed behind him. The spot was invisible again.

He went back the next Wednesday. And the one after that.
