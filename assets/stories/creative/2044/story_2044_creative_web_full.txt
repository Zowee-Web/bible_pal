"This is the worst Saturday ever," Cora said to nobody in particular. She sat on the front porch in the swimsuit she'd put on an hour ago, chin in her hands.

The trip to the water park had been canceled because of rain. Not a big exciting storm — just a steady, gray, boring drizzle that showed no signs of stopping.

Her father sat in the rocking chair behind her, reading. "It's not so bad, Cor."

"It is so bad."

He smiled but didn't argue.

Cora watched the rain drip from the porch roof. Each drop landed in the flower bed and made a tiny splash. She started counting. She got to forty-seven before she noticed something.

A spider had built a web between the porch railing and the rosebush. The rain had covered every strand in tiny water droplets, and the whole web sparkled like a necklace made of glass beads. Each strand curved out from the center in a careful spiral. The spider sat in the middle, small and brown, not bothered at all.

"Dad. Look at this."

Her father knelt beside her. "Well, would you look at that."

"How does something that small make something so perfect?"

"Carefully, I imagine. One strand at a time."

Cora sat back and looked around — really looked, for the first time that morning.

The rain had made the grass so green it almost glowed. A fat robin hopped across the lawn and pulled a worm from the soft earth. The maple tree at the edge of the yard held its leaves out flat, catching rain in tiny cascades.

Her cat, Biscuit, was curled on the doormat behind the screen door, purring. Cora scratched behind his ears. He stretched one paw out, yawned, and went back to sleep.

Inside, she could hear her mother in the kitchen. A warm smell drifted through the screen door — cinnamon and sugar and butter. Cinnamon rolls. Her mother only made them on rainy days.

Cora looked at the sparkling web. She looked at the green grass and the robin and sleepy Biscuit and the rain falling soft and steady on everything.

She was still wearing her swimsuit. She'd forgotten to be upset about it.

"Okay," she said. "Maybe it's not the worst Saturday."

Her father picked his book back up. "No?"

"The web is beautiful. And Biscuit is cozy. And Mom is making cinnamon rolls. And the grass has never been that green."

Cora pulled her knees up and wrapped her arms around them. A breeze came across the porch carrying the smell of wet earth and lavender.

"It might actually be a pretty good one," she said.

Biscuit purred. The rain kept falling, soft and silver, and the spider's web kept shining like a small gift that had been there all along, waiting for her to see it.
