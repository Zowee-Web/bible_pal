"This is the worst Saturday ever," Cora said to nobody in particular. She was sitting on the front porch with her chin in her hands.

The trip to the water park had been canceled because of rain. Not a big exciting storm with thunder and lightning — just a steady, gray, boring drizzle that turned everything damp and flat.

Her father sat in the chair beside her, reading a book. "It's not so bad," he said.

"It is so bad."

He smiled but didn't argue.

Cora watched the rain drip from the porch roof. Each drop landed in the flower bed below and made a tiny splash in the mud. Drip. Drip. Drip. She counted them. She got to forty-seven before she noticed something.

A spider had built a web between the porch railing and the rosebush. The rain had covered it in tiny water droplets, and now the whole web sparkled like a necklace made of glass beads. Each strand was perfect, curving out from the center in a careful spiral.

Cora leaned closer. The spider sat in the middle, small and brown, not bothered by the rain at all.

"Dad. Look at this."

Her father put his book down and knelt beside her. "Well, would you look at that."

"How does it make something so perfect?"

"Carefully, I imagine."

Cora sat back and looked around the porch — really looked, for the first time that morning. The rain had made the grass so green it almost glowed. A robin hopped across the lawn, pulling up a worm. The air smelled like wet earth and the lavender plant by the steps.

Her cat, Biscuit, was curled in a warm ball on the doormat, purring. Cora reached over and scratched behind his ears. He stretched one paw out and yawned.

Inside, she could smell her mother making cinnamon rolls. The warm, sweet smell drifted through the screen door.

Cora looked at the sparkling web. She looked at the green grass and the fat robin and sleepy Biscuit and the rain falling soft and steady on everything.

"Okay," she said. "Maybe it's not the worst Saturday."

Her father turned a page. "No?"

"It might actually be a pretty good one."
