There is something interesting about light — the way a single flame can touch another wick without losing any of its own brightness. A candle that lights a hundred others burns no less for the giving.

The keeper in this story spent long seasons tending a solitary beam, believing the light was his to protect, his to guard carefully against the dark. And perhaps there was faithfulness in that careful watching. But somewhere along the way, he noticed something unexpected — that light held tightly stays small, while light offered freely seems to find new places to glow.

This pattern shows up quietly in ordinary life. A kind word passed along doesn't leave the speaker emptier. A story shared at the right moment somehow grows richer in the telling. The things we offer from genuine warmth have a curious way of returning in forms we didn't anticipate.

Scripture often speaks of light not as a scarce resource to be rationed but as something that belongs on a stand, something meant for the whole room. There may be moments worth noticing — small, unhurried moments — where what feels like giving away turns out to be something closer to multiplying.
