Noel found the sprinkler already running when he came outside. His father had set it up in the front yard while Noel was eating lunch. Water arced across the grass in slow, sweeping fans, catching the light.

The sun was high and hot. The kind of summer day where the sidewalk burns your bare feet and the air shimmers above the driveway. Cicadas buzzed in the elm tree. The neighborhood smelled like cut grass and warm asphalt.

Noel stood at the edge of the spray. Cool mist touched his face and arms. He grinned.

He ran through.

The water hit his chest and arms and the top of his head. It was cold, shockingly cold, and perfect. He yelled — not a word, just a sound, pure and loud, the kind of sound that means nothing except happiness.

He ran through again. And again. Each time, the cold surprised him. Each time, he laughed harder.

His little cousin, Rosa, appeared on the porch. She was four and cautious about most things. She watched Noel from behind the railing with wide eyes.

"Come on!" he called, water running down his face. "It's amazing!"

Rosa took one step down the stairs. Then another. She held the railing with both small hands.

Noel ran through the sprinkler one more time, arms spread wide like an airplane. Water sparkled in the sunlight around him like a thousand tiny diamonds. Drops hung in his eyelashes and blurred the world into streaks of green and gold.

Rosa let go of the railing. She stepped onto the wet grass and scrunched her nose. The first spray caught her bare toes. She squealed — a high, bright sound that rang across the yard.

Then the caution melted from her face. Her eyes went wide, then wider, then lit up like someone turned on a lamp behind them.

She ran. Right through the middle, her sandals squelching, her dark hair flying. She came out the other side gasping, dripping, and radiant.

"Again!" she shouted. One word. The happiest word.

They ran through together, holding hands. Noel slowed his stride so Rosa could keep up. The water made rainbows in the air between them — red, orange, yellow, green, blue. Tiny rainbows that appeared and disappeared in a heartbeat.

"Do you see it?" he asked. "The rainbow?"

Rosa looked. Her mouth made an O.

His father sat on the porch with a glass of lemonade, smiling the kind of smile that comes when you see someone you love being happy.

They ran until they lost count. Their clothes were heavy with water and the grass was slippery under their feet. Rosa fell once, sat in the wet grass, and laughed so hard she couldn't stand up. Noel fell trying to help her.

Finally, they lay on the warm sidewalk, arms spread wide, letting the sun dry them. The concrete was hot against Noel's back, but the leftover cold balanced it perfectly.

Rosa reached over and held his hand. Her small fingers were wrinkled from the water.

"Best day," she said. She said it like a fact.

Noel looked at the sky. Blue and endless and bright, without a single cloud.

"Best day," he agreed.
