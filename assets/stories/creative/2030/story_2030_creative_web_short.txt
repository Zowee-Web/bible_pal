Noel found the sprinkler already running when he came outside. His father had set it up in the front yard while Noel was eating lunch. Water arced across the grass in slow, sweeping fans.

The sun was high and hot. The kind of summer day where the sidewalk burns your bare feet and the air shimmers above the driveway.

Noel stood at the edge of the spray. Cool mist touched his face. He grinned.

He ran through.

The water hit his chest and arms and the top of his head. It was cold, shockingly cold, and perfect. He yelled — not a word, just a sound, pure and loud, the kind of sound that means nothing except happiness.

He ran through again. And again.

His little cousin, Rosa, appeared on the porch. She was four and cautious about most things. She watched Noel with wide eyes.

"Come on!" he called. "It's amazing!"

Rosa took one step down the porch stairs. Then another. She held the railing with both hands.

Noel ran through the sprinkler one more time, arms wide, laughing. Water sparkled in the sunlight around him like a thousand tiny diamonds.

Rosa let go of the railing. She walked across the grass slowly. The first spray caught her toes. She squealed.

Then she ran. Right through the middle, her sandals squelching, her hair flying. She came out the other side with her mouth open in a perfect circle of surprise and delight.

"Again!" she shouted.

They ran through together this time, holding hands. The water made rainbows in the air. Noel could see all the colors — red, orange, yellow, green, blue.

His father sat on the porch with a glass of lemonade, watching them. He was smiling.

They ran until their clothes were heavy with water and the grass was slippery under their feet. Then they lay on the warm sidewalk, arms spread wide, letting the sun dry them. The concrete was hot against Noel's back.

Rosa reached over and held his hand. Her small fingers were wrinkled from the water.

"Best day," she said.

Noel looked at the sky. Blue and endless and bright. "Best day," he agreed.
