Every morning at five, before the buses started running, Tobias climbed the fire escape to the roof of his apartment building on Delancey Street. He carried a watering can in one hand and a coffee mug in the other, and he took the stairs slowly because his left knee had been unreliable since he turned seventy-three.

The rooftop garden had started as six tomato plants in buckets. That was eleven years ago. Now there were raised beds built from salvaged lumber, trellises made of old bicycle wheels and wire, herbs in coffee cans, and a fig tree growing in a halved oil drum. Tobias had hauled every bag of soil up those stairs himself.

He set his coffee on the ledge and began with the basil, pinching off the flower buds so the leaves would keep coming. His fingers were stained green by the time he moved to the peppers. Below him, a garbage truck groaned through the intersection. Someone honked. A dog barked from a window three floors down.

Tobias didn't mind the noise. It had its own rhythm, like weather. He worked around it the way his plants worked around wind.

The fig tree had dropped two ripe fruits overnight. He picked them up, split one open with his thumbs. The inside was pink and sweet, and he ate it standing there with dirt under his fingernails. He wrapped the second fig in his handkerchief and put it in his shirt pocket for Mrs. Kwan on the fourth floor, who always asked how the garden was doing but couldn't manage the stairs anymore.

A sparrow landed on the edge of the nearest raised bed and pecked at the soil. Tobias watched it without moving. It found something — a seed, maybe, or a small beetle — and flew off over the water towers.

He refilled the watering can from the rain barrel and moved to the lettuce. The leaves were coming in thick this week, more than he could eat. He'd bag some up for the Ortegas, and some for the young couple on the second floor who'd just had a baby.

Tobias tipped the can and watched the water darken the soil. Somewhere below, the city woke up and carried on. Up here, things grew.
