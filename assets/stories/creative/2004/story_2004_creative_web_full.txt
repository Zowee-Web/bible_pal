Elara lived in a yellow cottage at the end of a quiet lane. The cottage had a round blue door and a garden full of sunflowers. Every morning, she ate oatmeal with honey at the kitchen table. Her grandmother, Nana Jo, always hummed a soft tune while cooking. Through the open window, Elara could hear birds singing in the old maple tree. The air smelled like warm bread and fresh grass.

One Tuesday after breakfast, Elara walked into the garden to water the sunflowers. She carried her small green watering can with both hands. Near the fence, she noticed something moving in the clover. A tiny brown bird sat very still among the white flowers. One of its wings stuck out at an odd angle. Elara knelt down slowly and whispered, "Oh, little friend."

She cupped her hands gently around the bird. Its feathers felt soft and warm against her palms. The bird's round black eyes blinked up at her. Elara carried it carefully to the back porch. She set it down on a folded towel inside an old berry basket. The bird chirped once, very quietly.

"Nana Jo, come look," Elara called through the screen door. Her grandmother came outside, drying her hands on her apron. She bent down and studied the bird with kind eyes. "Its wing needs rest," Nana Jo said. "We can help it get strong again." Elara nodded and stroked the bird's head with one finger.

They made a cozy spot on the porch for the bird. Elara filled a tiny dish with water from the garden hose. Nana Jo found some seeds in the pantry. The bird drank a little water and ate three seeds. Elara named the bird Clover, after the place she found it. She sat beside the basket and talked to Clover in a soft voice.

Every day that week, Elara took care of Clover. She brought fresh water each morning and seeds each afternoon. She sang little songs while Clover tilted its head and listened. By Wednesday, Clover hopped around inside the basket. By Friday, the bird fluttered its wings and landed on the porch railing. Elara clapped her hands and laughed.

On Saturday morning, Clover flew from the railing to a low branch of the maple tree. Elara watched from below with her hands on her hips. The bird hopped along the branch, testing its wings again and again. Each flutter grew longer and smoother. Nana Jo came outside and stood beside Elara. "Clover is getting ready," she said.

Elara felt a tug in her chest. She loved having Clover close by. She loved the way the bird chirped when it saw her coming. Part of her wanted to keep Clover on the porch forever. She looked up at Nana Jo. "What if Clover flies away and I miss it?"

Nana Jo put her arm around Elara's shoulders. "Loving something means wanting the best for it," she said gently. "Even when that feels hard." Elara leaned into her grandmother and watched Clover hop higher in the tree. She thought about how good it felt to see Clover growing stronger each day. That growing had a direction, and the direction was upward, toward the wide open sky.

On Sunday morning, Elara brought seeds to the porch one more time. Clover sat on the railing, feathers smooth and bright. The bird looked different now. Its eyes were alert and its wings folded neatly against its body. Elara held out her palm with a few seeds. Clover ate them, then looked toward the garden and the fields beyond.

"It's okay, Clover," Elara said. Her voice was steady and warm. She lifted her hand slowly, and Clover hopped onto her finger. For a moment, they looked at each other. Then Clover spread its wings wide and lifted into the morning air. The bird circled the maple tree once, twice, then flew over the sunflowers toward the meadow.

Elara stood on the porch and watched until Clover became a tiny speck above the wildflowers. Her eyes were a little wet, but she was smiling. She pressed her hand against her heart. The place where Clover had been still felt warm.

Nana Jo brought two cups of apple cider to the porch. They sat together on the wooden bench. A breeze carried the smell of clover blossoms across the yard. From somewhere in the meadow, a bird sang a bright, clear song.

"That sounds like Clover," Elara said.

Nana Jo smiled. "Maybe so."

Elara sipped her cider and listened. The song came again, happy and free. She set her cup down and rested her head on Nana Jo's shoulder. The garden buzzed with bees visiting the sunflowers. A butterfly landed on the porch railing right where Clover used to sit.

Elara wiggled her fingers at the butterfly. It opened and closed its orange wings, then drifted off into the yard. She took a deep breath and let it out slowly. The morning was quiet and full and good.
