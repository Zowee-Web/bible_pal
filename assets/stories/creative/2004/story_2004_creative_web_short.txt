Elara lived in a small cottage near a meadow full of clover. Her grandmother kept a garden out back with tomatoes and sunflowers. The sunflowers grew so tall they bent their heads like friendly neighbors peeking over a fence.

One Tuesday morning, Elara walked outside with a bowl of blueberries. She heard a soft chirping sound near the porch steps. A tiny brown bird sat in the grass, tilting its head sideways. One of its wings stuck out at an odd angle.

"Oh, little bird," Elara whispered. She set the blueberries down and knelt on the cool ground.

Her grandmother came outside and looked at the bird carefully. "Its wing needs rest," she said. "Let's make it comfortable."

Together they lined a small basket with an old cotton scarf. Elara set the bird inside gently. It blinked up at her with round dark eyes. She placed a few blueberries and a tiny dish of water nearby.

Every morning that week, Elara checked on the bird before breakfast. She talked to it in a quiet voice. She told it about the clover meadow and the bees that hummed there. The bird ate seeds from her open palm. Its wing slowly tucked back into place.

By Saturday, the bird hopped from the basket onto the porch railing. It fluttered its wings once, then twice. Elara watched and felt a tug in her chest.

"Grandma," she said, "I think the bird wants to go."

Her grandmother sat beside her on the porch swing. "You took such good care of it," she said. "That's a gift you gave freely."

Elara cupped the bird in both hands one last time. Its heartbeat tapped against her fingers, quick and warm. She lifted her hands high and opened them wide.

The bird rose into the morning air. It circled the sunflower garden once, twice, then flew toward the row of oak trees at the edge of the meadow.

Elara kept her hands open for a long moment. The breeze moved through her fingers.

"Thank you, God, for letting me help," she said softly.

Her grandmother squeezed her shoulder. They sat together on the porch swing, rocking gently. A bee drifted past the tomato plants. Somewhere in the oak trees, a small brown bird began to sing.

Elara picked up her bowl of blueberries and ate one. It tasted sweet.
