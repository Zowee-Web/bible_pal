Delia caught the 5:40 bus without checking the schedule. Three years of early shifts had written the route into her body like a second pulse.

The bus was half full. She found a window seat near the back and ate a granola bar — oats and honey, the cheap kind. It tasted better than it should have at that hour. The morning was still dark. Streetlights slid past in amber streaks.

At Vine Street, an older man boarded with a cane and a paper bag. He sat across the aisle and arranged the bag on his knees. A loaf of bread poked from the top.

"Morning," he said.

"Morning."

He looked out his window. She looked out hers. The bus crossed the river bridge, and for a few seconds the water caught the first gray light — flat and silver, like hammered tin.

Delia almost missed it. She'd crossed this bridge a thousand times. But today the light hit at a particular angle, and the river looked like something worth seeing. She pressed her forehead to the cool glass.

The city opened on the other side — the hospital campus, parking garages, coffee carts setting up on the sidewalk. Steam rose from one cart into the cold air.

The bus stopped at Memorial. The man with the bread nodded. She nodded back.

She walked two blocks to the east entrance, badge in hand. The lobby smelled like floor wax and coffee. At the elevator, she paused. Through the glass doors behind her, the sky was turning peach — pink at the horizon, gold where the clouds thinned.

She hadn't watched a sunrise in months. Always walking toward something and never turning around.

She turned around. Watched it for thirty seconds. The color deepened, spread, then faded into ordinary daylight.

Thirty seconds. That was all.

She stepped into the elevator and pressed four. The doors closed. She could feel the granola bar settling in her stomach, the warmth of the lobby at her back, and the weight of the day ahead — not heavy, just present and waiting.

She was grateful for the bus. For the bridge. For bread in a paper bag and a stranger who said good morning. For a sunrise she almost missed and didn't.
