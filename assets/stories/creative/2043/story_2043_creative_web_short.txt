Rafe pulled his boots on by the back door and walked across the yard to the chicken coop. Morning chores were his job — water for the chickens, feed for the goat, eggs collected in the wire basket his grandfather had bent by hand.

He found his neighbor, Mr. Hadley, already outside, leaning on the fence between their properties. Mr. Hadley was old and thin and had a garden that used to be the nicest one on the road. But this summer it looked tired. Weeds pushed through the tomato rows. The bean poles leaned sideways.

"Morning, Rafe," Mr. Hadley said. His voice was scratchy and low.

"Morning." Rafe set his basket down. "Your sunflowers are getting tall."

Mr. Hadley looked over his shoulder at the two sunflowers by his porch. They were tall — almost as high as the gutter. But their heads drooped and their leaves had brown edges.

"They need water," Mr. Hadley said. "Everything needs water. I just can't keep up like I used to."

Rafe looked at the garden. He looked at Mr. Hadley's hands on the fence, rough and spotted with age.

"I could help," Rafe said. "After my chores. I'm good at weeding. My mom says I'm the fastest weeder in the county."

Mr. Hadley's eyebrows went up. "That so?"

"Well." Rafe grinned. "Second fastest. She's first."

After the chickens were fed and the goat had fresh water, Rafe climbed through the fence gap and spent an hour in Mr. Hadley's garden. He pulled weeds from the tomato rows. He straightened the bean poles. He carried bucket after bucket of water from the spigot.

Mr. Hadley sat on his porch step and watched. When Rafe finished, the old man's eyes looked bright and wet.

"Looks like a proper garden again," Mr. Hadley said quietly.

"It was always a proper garden," Rafe said. "It just needed a little help."

Mr. Hadley nodded. He reached into a paper bag beside him and held out a peach, perfectly ripe.

Rafe bit into it on the walk home. Juice ran down his chin and dripped onto his muddy boots. It was the sweetest thing he'd tasted all summer.
