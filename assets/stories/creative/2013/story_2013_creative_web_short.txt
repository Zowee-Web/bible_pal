Bramble was a small brown mouse who lived inside the wall of the village bakery. Every morning, warm bread smells drifted through the cracks and tickled his whiskers. He liked to sit on the windowsill and watch people walk by with their baskets and bundles.

At the center of the village stood a tall stone tower. A copper bell hung near the top. Each Sunday morning, old Mr. Garrick climbed the winding stairs and rang the bell. Its bright sound floated over rooftops and gardens, calling everyone together. Bramble loved that sound more than anything.

One cool autumn morning, Bramble scurried across the cobblestones toward the tower. He found a group of neighbors gathered at the bottom of the stairs. Mr. Garrick had hurt his ankle and could not climb. The stairs were narrow and steep. No one else felt sure about making the long climb up. Some tried a few steps and came back down, shaking their heads. "The bell won't ring today," someone said softly.

Bramble's heart beat fast. He looked up at the tower. It was very, very tall. His paws were very, very small. But he knew every crack and ledge in this village. He had climbed the bakery rafters hundreds of times.

He took a breath and whispered a quiet prayer. Then he began to climb.

Stone by stone, step by step, Bramble went up. His tiny paws — no, his tiny paws gripped each rough edge. Cool wind brushed his fur through the open windows of the tower. He did not look down. He just kept going, one paw after the next. Halfway up, his legs felt tired. He rested on a ledge and closed his eyes for a moment. A feeling of warmth filled his chest, like sunlight through glass. He opened his eyes and kept climbing.

At the very top, the copper bell gleamed in the morning light. Bramble ran along the wooden beam and jumped onto the bell rope. He swung his whole body back and forth. The rope moved. The bell tilted. And then it rang.

One clear, beautiful note sailed out over the village.

Down below, people looked up with wide eyes. Then they smiled. Then they laughed and clapped. The bell rang again and again as Bramble swung with all his might.

When he finally climbed back down, Mr. Garrick was sitting on a bench near the door. He cupped his hands gently, and Bramble hopped right in.

"Thank you, little friend," Mr. Garrick said. "That was the bravest thing I've seen all year."

Bramble's whiskers twitched. His heart still thumped from the climb. But sitting there in those warm, careful hands, with the last echo of the bell still humming in the air, he felt perfectly at home.
