Fennick had been walking for a long time. His small russet paws ached against the cool forest floor. Leaves crunched softly beneath each step. A yawn stretched his whole face wide, and his bushy tail drooped low behind him.

All day he had crossed meadows and hopped over little streams. He had climbed one grassy hill after another. Now the afternoon light was fading into soft orange and pink. His legs felt heavy. His eyelids kept wanting to close.

"I need somewhere to rest," Fennick whispered to nobody in particular.

He followed a mossy path between tall oak trees. Ferns brushed his sides like gentle fingers. The air smelled like damp earth and wild clover. Up ahead, a rocky hillside rose from the ground. Fennick noticed a round opening near the bottom, tucked between two smooth stones.

He padded closer and sniffed. Warm air drifted out from inside. Fennick poked his nose through the opening. Dry leaves lined the floor in a thick, soft pile. The walls curved inward, blocking the evening breeze. Someone had shaped this space with care, but nobody was here now. It was just the right size for a small fox.

Fennick stepped inside. The leaf bed crinkled under his weight. He turned in a slow circle, the way foxes do, and settled down. Warmth wrapped around him from every side. His breathing slowed. His sore paws finally relaxed.

Outside, a robin sang three clear notes from a branch. Wind moved through the oak leaves with a sound like a whispered lullaby. Fennick rested his chin on his front paws.

He had not built this den. He had not gathered these leaves or smoothed this earth. But here it was, waiting for him at the end of a long day. Ready and warm, exactly when he needed it most.

"Thank you," Fennick murmured. His eyes closed. He did not know who to thank, exactly. But gratitude filled his small chest like a deep breath of clover-scented air.

The robin sang again. Fennick's ears twitched once, then settled. His tail curled around his nose. Sleep came easily, the way a stream finds its way downhill. Soft and sure and unhurried.

The den held him gently through the whole quiet night.
