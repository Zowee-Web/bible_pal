Fennick was a small red fox with a white-tipped tail and tired brown eyes. He lived near a meadow where clover grew thick and soft. Most days, he liked to explore the hills and splash in the creek. But today was not most days.

His legs felt heavy. His paws ached from walking since early morning. He had spent the whole day looking for a new place to rest. His old burrow near the oak tree had filled with rainwater after three days of storms. Mud had pooled inside until there was no dry spot left. So Fennick had set out to find somewhere new.

He walked past the berry bushes where finches sang their evening songs. He walked past the wide flat rock where turtles liked to sit. He crossed the wooden footbridge over Penny Creek, where the water gurgled and splashed below. Every step felt slower than the one before.

"Maybe behind that big rock," Fennick said to himself. But behind the rock, the ground was damp and cold. "Maybe under the pine tree." But under the pine tree, sharp roots poked up everywhere. Fennick sat down and let out a long, slow breath. His tail curled around his paws. The afternoon had turned golden and warm, but his body felt so weary.

A round little hedgehog named Posy came waddling up the path. She had an acorn cap balanced on her head and bits of dried leaf stuck to her quills. Posy was the kind of neighbor who noticed things.

"Fennick, you look like you've been walking for a hundred years," she said.

"It feels like a thousand," Fennick said quietly.

Posy tilted her head. "What do you need?"

"A place to sleep. Somewhere dry and warm. My burrow flooded."

Posy's small black eyes went wide. "Oh! Follow me. I want to show you something."

Fennick almost said he was too tired to take another step. But Posy was already trundling down a gentle slope toward a cluster of hawthorn bushes. He pushed himself up and followed her, one slow paw after another.

They walked past a patch of wild mint. The smell floated up fresh and cool. They passed a mossy log where three ladybugs sat in a neat row. Then Posy stopped at the base of a small hill covered in soft grass.

"Look," she said, pointing with one tiny paw.

There in the hillside was a den. It had a round opening just the right size for a fox. The entrance was sheltered by thick hawthorn branches that kept rain from blowing in. Fennick stepped closer and peered inside.

The floor was covered in dry leaves and soft moss. Someone had gathered feathers — gray and brown and speckled white — and tucked them into a cozy pile near the back wall. A smooth stone sat near the entrance, just right for resting a chin on. The air inside smelled like earth and dried lavender.

"Who made this?" Fennick whispered.

"A few of us," Posy said. She shuffled her feet a little. "Old Barnaby the badger dug it out last spring, but he moved closer to the pond. When the storms came, I thought someone might need it. So yesterday I brought the feathers. Clementine the rabbit gathered the moss. And Griff the squirrel dropped off the lavender from his stash."

Fennick stared at the den. His throat felt tight in a good way.

"You didn't even know my burrow flooded," he said.

"No," Posy agreed. "We just had a feeling someone would need a warm place. Funny how that works."

Fennick stepped inside. The moss was soft under his sore paws. He turned around twice and settled down into the feather pile. It was exactly the right shape for curling up. Warmth wrapped around him like a blanket he didn't have to pull.

Posy poked her head in. "Good?"

"So good," Fennick murmured. His eyes were already half closed.

"Rest now," Posy said gently. "I'll bring you some berries in the morning."

She waddled away, the acorn cap still balanced on her head. Fennick listened to her small footsteps fade into the evening. Outside, a robin sang three clear notes from the hawthorn branch. The breeze carried the scent of mint and clover through the den's round door.

Fennick pressed his nose into the soft feathers. Everything his tired body had needed was already here, gathered and waiting. He hadn't had to build it or earn it or ask for it. It had simply been prepared.

His breathing slowed. His tail relaxed. The robin sang again, and somewhere far off, Penny Creek murmured its quiet song over the stones.

Fennick closed his eyes. For the first time all day, every part of him felt still. The den held him gently, and sleep came as easy as a leaf settling on calm water.
