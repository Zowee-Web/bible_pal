"Miriam, the harvest's been poor again." Miriam sighed, her calloused hands resting on the plow. Years ago, when her husband Samuel was alive, their fields were abundant. Now, she could barely coax a meager yield from the once-fertile soil.

She remembered when Samuel would sing while they worked, his voice as warm as the summer sun. Now, only the wind whispered through the barren rows of crops, carrying echoes of laughter that were no longer there.

A traveler appeared one day, dusty and tired from journeying. He offered to help Miriam in exchange for food and lodging. She agreed, grateful for the extra hands. Over shared meals, they talked about farming, life, loss. The stranger listened intently when Miriam spoke of Samuel, his eyes kind.

One evening, as they sat by the fire, the stranger asked, "Why do you think your fields are so barren now, Miriam?" She looked at him, surprised. No one had ever asked her that before.

She hesitated, then poured out her heart. "I fear God's blessing has left me. Samuel was always the light of our home. With him gone..." Her voice trailed off, eyes glistening with unshed tears.

The stranger nodded understandingly. "Miriam," he said gently, "God's blessings don't depend on what we've lost but on who He is. Remember Ruth and Boaz? Their fields bore fruit even in famine."

Days turned into weeks. The traveler taught Miriam new farming methods, shared stories of faith that inspired hope. One day, she noticed green shoots pushing through the earth where once there was only brown.

The harvest that year wasn't spectacular, but it was enough. Miriam stood among her fields, feeling a warmth in her heart that had nothing to do with the sun. She looked at the traveler, who was packing his bags, preparing to leave.

"You've brought life back into my land," she said, gratitude shining in her eyes.

He smiled. "It wasn't me, Miriam. It was your faith. You just needed someone to remind you."

As he walked away, Miriam watched him go. Then she turned towards her fields, ready to work the soil once more, trusting that this time, it would yield abundantly.
