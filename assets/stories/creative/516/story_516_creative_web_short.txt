"'I can't do this anymore, Rowan.'" Emma's voice cracked as she spoke, her hands buried deep in the earth, pulling out weeds that had taken root around their struggling crops.

Rowan looked up from his own labor, his calloused hands resting on the handle of his hoe. The sun bore down on them, a relentless taskmaster, much like the drought that had plagued their farm for months. He watched Emma, her shoulders slumped in defeat, and felt a familiar ache in his heart.

Years ago, when they were just children playing by the creek, he'd promised to protect her from all harm. But how could he shield her from the capricious nature of the weather, or the cruelty of fate that had brought them nothing but hardship since they'd taken over the family farm?

"Remember when we were kids, Em?" Rowan began, his voice a gentle rumble, "We used to pray for rain together." He smiled at the memory, but Emma didn't respond, her gaze fixed on the barren fields.

"We still can," he offered, his tone hopeful. "Maybe it's time to turn back to Him. We've faced worse than this."

Emma shook her head, a bitter laugh escaping her lips. "Where was He when Pa died? When Ma left us? Where is He now, Rowan?" She gestured wildly at the parched earth. "We're alone in this, just like we've always been."

Rowan's heart sank, but he pressed on, his faith unyielding. "We're not alone," he insisted, his voice steady. "He's here with us, even if we can't feel Him right now." He stood up straight, squaring his shoulders. "Let's pray together, Em. Let's ask for His mercy."

Emma hesitated, her eyes filled with tears and doubt. Rowan reached out a hand, offering it to her. She looked at him, then at the vast, empty fields, and finally, she took his hand.

Together, they bowed their heads, their voices barely above a whisper as they began to pray for rain. The sun beat down on them, but there was comfort in their shared faith, hope in their united stance against despair. And perhaps, just perhaps, there was a chance that the rain would come again.
