Suki's Saturday started noisy. Her baby brother cried at breakfast. The dog knocked over a glass of juice. Her father's phone rang three times before they finished eating.

"I need some quiet," Suki whispered to herself.

She slipped on her rain boots and walked out the back door. The yard was still wet from last night's rain. Drops hung on the fence like tiny glass beads. The grass squished softly under her feet.

At the far edge of the yard stood her grandfather's old garden bench. Green moss grew on its legs. Suki sat down and tucked her knees up.

The neighborhood was waking up slowly. A dog barked somewhere distant. A car door closed. But here, in this corner, everything was hushed.

A spider had built a web between the bench arm and the fence post. Raindrops still clung to the silk threads, and each one caught the morning light. The web looked like a tiny chandelier.

Suki watched a single drop slide down one thread and hang at the bottom, trembling. It held on for a long time. Then it fell into the grass without a sound.

A robin landed on the fence. It looked at Suki, decided she was harmless, and began to sing. The notes were clear and round, like small bells.

Suki closed her eyes. She listened to the robin and the distant hum of the neighborhood and the soft dripping of leftover rain. She felt the cool morning air on her cheeks. She smelled wet earth and the faint sweetness of her mother's rosebush.

Everything felt still. Not empty still, but full still. Like the world was holding something gentle in its hands.

She sat there for a long time. When she finally went inside, her brother had stopped crying. The dog was asleep on the rug. Her father was reading the paper quietly.

"Where were you?" her mother asked.

"On the bench," Suki said. "Listening."

She poured herself a glass of milk and drank it slowly. The quiet she had found outside was still inside her, warm and soft.
