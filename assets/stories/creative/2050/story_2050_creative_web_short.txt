The highway stretched through low green hills. Elliot had been driving for two hours without turning on the radio. He hadn't meant to leave it off — he'd just forgotten to turn it on, and by the time he noticed the silence, he didn't want to break it.

The rental car smelled like stale air freshener. The windshield was clean. The road ahead was mostly empty — a few trucks, a camper with Oregon plates, a motorcyclist who passed him and disappeared over a rise.

Elliot was driving from his mother's funeral to his apartment in the city. The service had been that morning. Sandwiches afterward in the church basement. Handshakes and hugs and the same sentences repeated by kind people who didn't know what else to say.

He hadn't cried yet. He kept waiting for it to come but it hadn't.

Somewhere past mile marker two hundred, the hills opened up into a wide valley. Green fields on both sides. A creek running parallel to the road, visible through gaps in the fencing. The sky was enormous — not dramatic, no sunset colors, just a plain gray-blue expanse that went on without interruption.

Elliot let the car slow down. Not a decision — just his foot easing off the gas. The speedometer dropped from seventy to fifty-five. Trucks passed him. He didn't mind.

A red-tailed hawk sat on a fence post. Cows stood in a field, facing the same direction, doing nothing. The creek caught the light and threw it back in small flashes.

He drove like that for twenty miles. Slow. Quiet. The valley holding him like a hand holds water — loosely, with no grip, just enough shape to keep it from spilling.

He didn't cry. But somewhere in that valley, the tight thing behind his sternum loosened. Not all the way. Just enough.
