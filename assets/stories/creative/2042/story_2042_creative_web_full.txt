The apartment was loud all morning. Isla's baby brother screamed in his high chair. Her older sister practiced trumpet in the hallway — the same eight notes, over and over. The dishwasher rumbled and clanked. Outside the kitchen window, a garbage truck beeped as it backed up.

Isla covered her ears. It didn't help much. She was full of noise. It pressed against the inside of her head like too much air in a balloon.

She slipped away from the kitchen table. Nobody noticed. She walked down the hall to the back of the apartment where a small white door opened onto the fire escape. Her mother said she could sit there as long as she didn't climb.

Isla pushed the door open and stepped outside.

The metal grating was cool under her bare feet. She could feel the diamond pattern pressing into her skin. The fire escape faced the back of the building, away from the street. Below was a narrow alley with a single tree growing up from a square of dirt in the concrete. The tree was old and tall. Its top branches reached Isla's level, three floors up.

She sat down cross-legged and leaned against the warm brick wall.

It was quiet here. Not silent — she could hear birds in the tree, and somewhere far away a siren rose and faded. But the sounds were soft and distant, like listening to rain from inside a car.

A breeze came through the alley. It moved through the leaves with a sound like whispering. It touched Isla's face, cool and gentle, carrying the smell of something green and alive.

She watched the leaves flicker — dark green on top, lighter underneath. Sunlight came through the gaps and made bright spots that danced on the alley floor below.

A sparrow landed on a branch close to her. So close she could see its small black eye, round and bright as a bead. It tilted its head, looked right at her, and sang three clear notes. Then it flew away, but the notes stayed in the air for a moment.

Isla breathed in slowly. The air tasted clean and cool.

She breathed out. Her shoulders dropped. She hadn't realized she'd been holding them up near her ears.

She sat there a long time. She watched a cloud change shape above the alley — first like a boat, then like a pillow, then like nothing at all. She listened to the leaves whisper. She felt the sun warm her knees.

Inside, the trumpet started up again. But out here, everything was still and calm and exactly right. The tree breathed with the wind. The sparrow came back and sang again.

Isla closed her eyes and let the quiet settle over her like a blanket.

When she finally went inside, the apartment was still noisy. But Isla carried the stillness with her, tucked away somewhere behind her ribs, warm and safe and hers.
