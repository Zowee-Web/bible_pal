The apartment was loud all morning. Isla's baby brother screamed in his high chair. Her older sister practiced trumpet in the hallway. The dishwasher rumbled and clanked. A garbage truck beeped outside the kitchen window, backing up, backing up, backing up.

Isla covered her ears with her hands and closed her eyes. It didn't help much.

She slipped away from the kitchen table and walked down the hall to the back of the apartment. There was a small door there that opened onto the fire escape. Her mother said she could sit on the fire escape as long as she didn't climb.

Isla pushed the door open and stepped outside.

The metal grating was cool under her bare feet. The fire escape faced the back of the building, away from the street. Below was a narrow alley with a single tree growing up from a square of dirt. The tree was tall enough that its top branches reached Isla's level, three floors up.

She sat down cross-legged on the grating and leaned her back against the brick wall.

It was quiet here. Not silent — she could hear birds in the tree, and somewhere far away, a siren. But the sounds were soft and distant, like listening to music from another room.

A breeze came through the alley. It moved through the tree's leaves with a sound like whispering. It touched Isla's face, cool and gentle.

She watched the leaves move. Green and gold, flickering in the morning light. A sparrow landed on a branch close to her, tilted its head, and sang three notes.

Isla breathed in. The air smelled like the tree — something green and alive and growing.

She breathed out.

The noise of the apartment was still there behind the door. The day was still busy and full. But right here, right now, everything was still.

Isla closed her eyes and listened to the leaves.
