Courage rarely looks the way we imagine it will. In our minds it arrives with certainty, with eloquence, with the steady hands of someone who knows exactly what to do. In practice it often shows up as a shaking voice in a conference room, six imperfect words spoken before the mind has time to talk the heart out of it.

Perhaps the truest kind of bravery is the small kind — the kind that doesn't make the news, that no one gives a speech about. Standing up when sitting would be easier. Saying the plain thing in a room full of silence. It costs something every time, and the trembling afterward is part of the price. But something shifts in a person who discovers they're willing to pay it.
