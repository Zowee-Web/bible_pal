There comes a point where the body overrules the will — where the hands let go of the wheel not because you've decided to stop but because you've simply run out of the thing that kept you gripping. It can feel like failure, that moment. Like the day has won.

But perhaps stopping isn't losing. Perhaps the mountains and the mist and the dark have been there all along, waiting for the moment you pull over and finally notice them. The rest doesn't fix everything. The road is still there in the morning, and so is the weight. But something loosens in the stopping — some tightly held thing releases, just enough to remind us that we were never meant to drive through the whole night without pulling over.
