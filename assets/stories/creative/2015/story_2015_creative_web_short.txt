In a wide garden near a stone wall, a tiny seed sat in the cool dirt. Her name was Pip. Brown soil pressed softly all around her. Earthworms moved slowly nearby, making the ground feel alive. A ladybug walked across the surface above, and its small feet made the faintest tap-tap-tap.

Pip was the smallest seed in the whole garden. Every other seed nearby seemed bigger and rounder. A plump bean seed rested just a hand's width away. A tall sunflower seed sat near the fence post. Pip looked at herself and wondered how anything could ever come from something so small.

"I'm no bigger than a crumb," she whispered to the earthworm passing by.

The earthworm paused. "I've seen all kinds of seeds down here," he said. "Big ones. Little ones. Round ones. Flat ones. Every single one surprised me."

Pip didn't know what he meant. She tucked herself deeper into the dirt and waited.

Days passed. Morning light warmed the ground above her. Cool nights let the soil rest. Pip stayed still and quiet. She could feel the other seeds around her beginning to stretch and crack open. But she stayed curled up tight.

Then one afternoon, a soft drumming sound came from above. Rain. Drop after drop soaked into the earth. The water reached Pip slowly, like a warm blanket being pulled over her. It filled the tiny spaces in the soil. It touched her gently on every side.

Something stirred inside her. A small green shoot, no thicker than a thread, began to uncurl. Pip felt it stretching upward. She wasn't pushing it. It just grew, the way a yawn stretches out on a sleepy morning.

The shoot reached through the dirt, past pebbles and roots, until it broke through the surface. Fresh air brushed against the tiny green tip. Rain kept falling softly, landing on her first small leaf.

Pip grew a little more each day after that. Her stem stood straight. Two round leaves opened wide. She tilted toward the morning light and felt its warmth settle on her like a hand resting gently on her back.

The plump bean had sprouted too. So had the sunflower seed. They all looked different. Some were tall and some were short. Some had wide leaves and some had thin ones. But every single plant was growing right where it had been planted.

Pip's leaves caught the rain and held tiny drops that sparkled. A bee landed on her stem and rested there for a moment before buzzing along. The earthworm poked his head up from the soil and looked at her.

"See?" he said. "Surprised me again."

Pip swayed gently in the breeze, her small leaves open wide, catching every drop the rain offered.
