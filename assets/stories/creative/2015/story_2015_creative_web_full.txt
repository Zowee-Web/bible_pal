A small seed named Pippa sat in the soft brown earth of a garden. The garden belonged to a cottage with a red door and yellow window boxes. Climbing roses grew along the fence in shades of pink and peach. Bees hummed from flower to flower in the warm afternoon. Pippa could feel the cool soil all around her, holding her gently. Everything smelled like fresh dirt and sweet clover.

Nearby, a tall sunflower named Grover stretched high above the garden bed. His bright yellow petals caught the light and swayed with every breeze. A round marigold named Della bloomed in a cheerful orange puff beside the fence. Even the little clover patches seemed to spread wider every day. Pippa watched them all from her spot under the soil. She wondered if she would ever grow like they did.

"I am so tiny," Pippa whispered to a passing earthworm. The earthworm wiggled and smiled. "Lots of good things start out tiny," the earthworm said. Then it burrowed deeper and was gone. Pippa tried to believe those words. But when she looked up through the thin layer of dirt above her, everything seemed so big. Grover's stem was thick and strong. Della's roots stretched far and wide. Pippa's own root was barely a thread.

"What if I stay this small forever?" she asked the soil around her. The soil did not answer, but it held her steady and warm. A ladybug crawled across the surface above and tapped the ground lightly. "Hello down there," the ladybug called. "Are you the new seed?" Pippa said yes in a very quiet voice. "Well, I'll check on you tomorrow," the ladybug said, and walked away on her tiny red legs.

Days passed slowly under the earth. Pippa felt the sun warm the ground above her each morning. She felt the cool of evening settle in at night. Grover and Della talked to each other about the weather and the birds. Pippa listened and wished she could join them up in the open air. She tried pushing upward, just a little. Nothing happened. She tried again the next day. Still nothing. Her small root held on, but her stem barely moved.

One morning, the air changed. A soft coolness spread across the garden. Pippa felt something new — a gentle drumming on the earth above. Rain. Drop after drop soaked into the soil. The water reached Pippa slowly, like a kind hand offering a drink. She felt it seep around her shell and touch her tiny root. Something stirred inside her. It was not a push. It was more like a stretch, the way arms reach out after a long sleep.

The rain kept falling through the morning, steady and soft. Pippa drank and drank. Her root grew a little longer. Her stem uncurled just a bit. By afternoon, a green tip poked through the surface of the soil. Fresh air touched it for the first time. Pippa could feel the breeze on her brand-new leaf. It tickled.

"Look at that!" Della said, leaning her orange head toward the ground. "Someone's coming up." Grover bent his tall stem down to see. "Well, hello there, little sprout," he said in his deep, friendly voice. Pippa's leaf trembled, but she held steady. The rain had given her just what she needed.

Over the next few days, more rain came and went. Each time, Pippa grew a little taller. Her stem turned a brighter green. Two more leaves unfolded, then four. The ladybug came back and landed right on one of them. "I told you I'd check on you," she said. Pippa laughed, and her leaves shook with the sound.

By the end of the week, Pippa stood several inches above the ground. She was still the smallest plant in the garden. Grover towered above her, and Della's bloom was wide as a teacup. But Pippa did not mind so much now. Her roots felt strong in the earth. Her leaves caught the light each morning. She could feel herself growing, even when she could not see it happening.

One evening, the gardener with the red door came outside. She knelt beside the garden bed and smiled. "Oh, there you are," she said softly, touching Pippa's smallest leaf with one finger. "You're doing just fine." Then she watered the whole row with her green watering can and hummed a quiet song.

Pippa swayed gently in the evening air. Grover nodded beside her. Della glowed orange in the fading light. The ladybug tucked herself under a clover leaf nearby. The garden was full and growing, and Pippa was part of it now. A thin curl of new growth was already forming at her tip, reaching upward, ready for tomorrow's rain.
