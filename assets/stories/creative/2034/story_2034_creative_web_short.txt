The laundromat on Hess Avenue stayed open until midnight, and by ten o'clock it was nearly empty.

Corinne sat in a molded plastic chair near the window, waiting for her last load to dry. The fluorescent lights hummed. Two dryers tumbled in slow rhythm. The air smelled like warm cotton and someone else's lavender detergent.

She wasn't in a hurry. Her apartment was four blocks away with nothing waiting but a cat and sorted mail. She had her book, but she wasn't reading it.

She was watching the woman at the folding table.

The woman was maybe seventy. Small, with dark skin and careful hands. Navy cardigan buttoned to the top, house slippers that whispered on the linoleum. She was folding towels — not quickly, not absently, but the way a person wraps a gift. Smoothing the fabric flat, matching corner to corner, pressing the crease with the heel of her palm.

Corinne had never seen anyone fold laundry like it mattered. Like the towels deserved attention.

The woman moved to pillowcases. Same rhythm. Smooth, fold, press, stack. Her face was quiet — not blank, but quiet the way a pond is quiet when nothing disturbs it.

A dryer buzzed. The woman didn't flinch. She finished the pillowcase in her hands, then walked to the dryer and opened it. Steam rose around her fingers. She pulled out a fitted sheet, held it wide, and began the familiar negotiation of folding it alone — tucking corners into corners, patient with the fabric's resistance.

Corinne realized she'd been holding her breath. She let it out slowly.

The woman glanced up and smiled — small, unhurried, expecting nothing in return.

"Peaceful in here at night," the woman said.

"It is," Corinne said.

They didn't speak again. The woman finished her folding, placed everything into a cloth bag, and left through the glass door. The bell chimed once.

Corinne sat in the warm quiet. Her dryer was still running. She picked up her book but didn't open it. She listened to the tumble and hum, the small sounds of an ordinary place doing its ordinary work, and felt something she hadn't felt in weeks settle into the center of her chest.

Rest. Not sleep. Just rest.
