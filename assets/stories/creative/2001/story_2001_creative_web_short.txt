Maren's fingers ached. She'd been at the loom since before dawn, and for the third time this week, the threads had tangled into a knotted mess where the diamond pattern should have been.

She yanked the shuttle back, tried to force the weft through. The yarn snapped. A whole row of work buckled and curled off the frame like a ribbon of smoke.

"No, no, no." She pressed her palms flat against the cloth, as if she could hold it together by sheer pressure. Her chest was tight. The order was due Friday — a table runner for the Hargrove wedding — and she had nothing to show but a pile of false starts and fraying edges.

She'd sketched the pattern six times. Measured the warp threads down to the millimeter. Calculated the color repeats on graph paper taped to the wall above her workspace. Every variable accounted for. And still, the cloth kept falling apart under her hands.

Maren sat back on her stool and stared at the ruined fabric. The studio was quiet except for the ticking of the baseboard heater. A spool of deep blue wool had rolled off the table at some point and come to rest against her boot.

She picked it up. Turned it over. It was the color of a lake in late evening — not part of her plan at all. She'd bought it months ago on impulse at a market in Ashford and never found a use for it.

Without thinking much about it, she wound the blue onto a bobbin and loaded it into the shuttle. She didn't check the graph paper. She didn't count repeats. She just began passing the shuttle through, letting the blue find its own path among the cream and rust threads already on the loom.

The tension held. Row after row, the fabric grew. Not diamonds — something looser, like water moving around stones. Her hands stopped gripping and started guiding. Her breathing slowed to match the rhythm of the shuttle's arc.

By noon, she had fourteen inches of cloth on the frame. She ran her thumb across the surface. It was nothing like what she'd planned. The blue wandered through it in unpredictable currents, and the whole piece held together.
