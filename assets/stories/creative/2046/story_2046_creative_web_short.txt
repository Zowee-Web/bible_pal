Have you ever held a piece of wood and felt that it wanted to become something? Juliana had. She felt it every time she walked into her aunt's workshop.

The workshop smelled like cedar shavings and linseed oil. Tools hung on the pegboard wall, each one in its own outline drawn in black marker. Sawdust covered the floor like pale gold snow.

Tia Carmen was building a rocking horse. The body was smooth and curved, sanded until it shone. But it had no head yet. Just a neck stump sticking up at the front.

"Can I make the head?" Juliana asked.

Tia Carmen looked at her over her safety glasses. "You think you're ready?"

"I carved a duck last week. It looked almost like a duck."

"Almost counts." Tia Carmen set a block of maple on the workbench. "The head is yours."

Juliana pulled on her safety glasses and drew the shape on the wood — a horse's head with ears forward, alert and friendly. She clamped the block in the vise and picked up the coping saw.

The saw buzzed through the wood. Sawdust fell in a soft stream. She followed her pencil line, cutting the rough shape free.

Then the chisel work. She tapped gently with the mallet, shaving away thin curls. The horse's face emerged — the curve of the cheek, the slope of the nose, the notch between the ears.

It took most of the afternoon. Her arms ached and her fingers were sore. But when she held the finished head up to the light, she grinned so wide her face hurt.

It looked like a horse. Not almost. Really.

Tia Carmen turned it in her hands. "This is beautiful work, mija."

They glued the head onto the body together. Juliana held it steady while the glue set, feeling the smooth wood warm under her palms.

She had helped make a real rocking horse.

Juliana sat on the workshop floor with sawdust in her hair and laughed.
