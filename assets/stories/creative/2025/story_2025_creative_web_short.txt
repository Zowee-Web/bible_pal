"I can't do it," Wren said, staring up at the high dive.

The ladder reached toward the sky like a metal beanstalk. The pool below looked very far away. Other kids climbed up and jumped off like it was nothing. Wren's hands were sweating.

Her older brother, Caleb, stood beside her. "You don't have to," he said simply.

But Wren wanted to. She had watched all summer from the shallow end. She had promised herself she would try before August ended. And today was the last day of August.

She grabbed the first rung. The metal was warm from the sun. She climbed one step. Then another. Her arms trembled, but they held.

Halfway up, she stopped. She could see the whole pool from here. The blue water sparkled. Kids splashed and laughed below. The lifeguard's whistle glinted in the sun.

"You're doing great," Caleb called up.

She kept climbing. At the top, the board stretched out over nothing. The wind blew against her wet swimsuit. Her toes curled around the rough edge.

She looked down. Her stomach flipped.

She almost turned around. Almost.

But she thought about all those days watching from below. She thought about what it might feel like to fly, even for one second.

Wren bent her knees. She took a breath that filled her whole chest.

She jumped.

The air rushed past her ears. The sky flashed above. Then the water swallowed her in one cool, blue gulp.

She burst through the surface, gasping and grinning.

"I did it!" she shouted.

Caleb was clapping. The lifeguard gave her a thumbs-up. The sun pressed warm against her face.

Wren swam to the ladder and climbed out. Water dripped from her hair onto the hot concrete. She looked back up at the high dive.

It didn't look so tall anymore.
