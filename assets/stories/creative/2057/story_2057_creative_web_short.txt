Penny saw the wrapper hit the ground before anyone else did. A boy from the grade above — tall, loud, always surrounded by friends — had finished his granola bar and tossed the wrapper on the sidewalk right next to the trash can. Not in it. Next to it.

His friends kept walking. Nobody said anything.

Penny was sitting on the bench by the bike rack, eating her apple. She watched the wrapper sit there on the concrete, six inches from where it was supposed to be.

She could leave it. It wasn't her wrapper. It wasn't her problem.

She stood up. Her legs did the shaky thing they did when she had to read aloud in class. She walked over, picked up the wrapper, and dropped it in the trash can.

The tall boy turned around. "What are you doing?"

"You missed," Penny said.

His friends looked at her. Three of them. All bigger.

"I didn't miss," the boy said. "I didn't care."

"The trash can's right there." Penny pointed at it. Her voice came out smaller than she wanted but it came out.

The boy stared at her for a long second. Then he laughed — not a mean laugh, a surprised one. "Okay, trash police." He turned and walked away with his friends.

Penny sat back down on the bench. Her heart was beating fast. Her apple had a bruise on it from where she'd gripped it too hard.

A girl she didn't know walked past the bench. She stopped, bent down, picked up a straw wrapper from the ground, and put it in the trash. She looked at Penny and nodded once.

Penny nodded back. She took a bite of her apple.
