The cafeteria was loud. Trays clattered. Chairs scraped across tile. Voices bounced off the walls like rubber balls.

Marco carried his tray with both hands, stepping carefully between backpacks on the floor. He was new. Five days new.

He scanned the room.

There were tables full of kids laughing, elbowing each other, trading chips for cookies. And then there was a table near the window where one boy sat alone.

The boy had dark hair that fell across his forehead. He was eating a sandwich slowly, looking at the table like it had something interesting written on it. It didn't.

Marco knew that look. He'd worn it at his old school the week his best friend moved away.

His sneakers squeaked on the floor. He stood at the edge of the table.

"Can I sit here?"

The boy looked up. His eyebrows lifted just a little.

"Yeah," he said. "If you want."

Marco set down his tray. The chair wobbled when he sat.

"I'm Marco."

"Elijah." The boy pulled at his sandwich crust. "Nobody really sits here."

"I know," Marco said.

He opened his milk carton. It took three tries because the flap was stubborn. Elijah watched him struggle and almost smiled.

"You have to pinch both sides," Elijah said.

Marco pinched. The carton opened.

"Thanks."

They ate. Elijah asked where Marco moved from. Marco told him — two states away, a house with a yellow door. Elijah said he'd never had a yellow door but he had a blue mailbox.

Someone at a nearby table threw a balled-up napkin and laughed. Marco and Elijah ignored it.

"You want half my orange?" Marco asked.

Elijah nodded.

Marco split the orange and handed the bigger half across the table. Juice ran down his thumb.

The cafeteria was still loud. Trays still clattered. But at the table by the window, two boys sat across from each other, chewing orange slices, their elbows resting on the same sticky surface.

The sun came through the glass and warmed the spot between their trays.
