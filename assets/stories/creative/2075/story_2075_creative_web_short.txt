Marta painted a tree.

It wasn't a good tree. She knew that. The trunk leaned sideways like it was tired, and the leaves were too big, and she'd accidentally mixed the green into the brown so the whole middle part looked like mud.

She painted it anyway. She used the fat brush for the sky and the thin brush for the grass and her finger for the sun because the yellow was almost gone and she had to press it out of the tube.

When it was finished she held it up. Water dripped down the bottom edge onto her shoe.

At school the paintings went on the drying rack. Then they went into cubbies. Then they went into backpacks. Then they went home.

Marta's painting went into her backpack folded once.

At home Grandpa was sitting at the kitchen table with his coffee. He had his reading glasses on. He was doing the crossword puzzle with a short pencil.

"What's that?" he said.

"Nothing." Marta set her backpack on the chair.

"Doesn't look like nothing."

The painting was sticking out of the top of the bag. Grandpa pulled it free with two fingers. He unfolded it. He held it away from his face the way he held the newspaper.

"That's a tree," he said.

"It's not very good."

Grandpa set down his pencil. He looked at the painting for a long time. He looked at the muddy middle and the leaning trunk and the finger-smeared sun.

"The sun is warm," he said. "I can feel it."

He stood up. He opened the junk drawer and found the magnet shaped like a strawberry. He pressed the painting flat against the refrigerator door with both hands, smoothing the fold crease with his thumb. He stuck the strawberry magnet right at the top.

The painting hung there, still a little damp, catching the light from the window above the sink.

Grandpa sat back down and picked up his pencil.

Marta opened the fridge to get the milk. The painting fluttered against her hand. She closed the door carefully, and it settled back into place.
