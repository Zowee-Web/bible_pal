"When will this rain ever let up?" Nova muttered, wiping sweat from her brow despite the chill. Her calloused hands, stained with earth and tiredness, gripped the plow as she urged her old mule, Bess, forward. The once-green fields lay waterlogged and heavy around them.

Years ago, when Nova's grandfather had farmed these lands, the rain had been a blessing, a sign of God's favor. But now, as Nova trudged through mud that sucked at her boots like a hungry mouth, she felt only weariness. She longed for the sun to break through the heavy clouds, but it seemed even God had forgotten this place.

One day, while plowing a stubborn furrow, Bess suddenly stopped. Nova looked up and saw an old man standing by the edge of the field. He was thin, his clothes worn, but there was a warmth in his smile that caught Nova off guard.

"Mind if I join you?" he asked. "I've been walking a long time."

Nova hesitated, then nodded. The stranger fell into step beside her, matching Bess's slow pace. They walked together for some time before the old man spoke again.

"You seem weary, my child," he said gently. "Not just from this rain, but something deeper."

Nova glanced at him sideways, surprised by his perceptiveness. She had not spoken to many people in recent years, preferring the company of her fields and animals. But there was something about this old man that invited confidences.

"It's this land," she admitted finally. "It's been hard since Grandfather passed. I feel like God doesn't care about us anymore."

The old man listened quietly, his gaze on the rain-soaked fields. Then he told her a story of another farmer, centuries ago, who had also felt forsaken by God. But when he spoke of how that farmer had turned to faith and found hope in the midst of his struggles, Nova felt something shift within her.

"God hasn't forgotten you," the old man said, turning to face her as they reached the end of the field. "He's right here with you, in every drop of rain, in every furrow you plow."

With that, he touched Bess gently on the nose and walked away, disappearing into the rain like a ghost.

Nova stood there for a moment, stunned. Then she looked around at her tired fields, feeling not weariness but determination. She would trust God again, even if it meant weathering this storm alone.

That night, as Nova sat by her fire, the first rays of sunlight broke through the clouds outside. It was a sign, she thought, smiling wearily but hopefully. A sign that even after years of rain, there could still be sunshine.

And so, Nova went out to her fields the next morning with renewed spirit. She would keep plowing, keep planting, keep hoping - because sometimes, faith is just putting one foot in front of the other until you see the sun again.
