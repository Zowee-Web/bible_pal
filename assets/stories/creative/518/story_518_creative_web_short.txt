"When will this rain ever stop?" Leo sighed, looking up at the relentless downpour from his porch. Years ago, he'd planted these crops in faith, but now, as harvest neared, weariness gnawed at him. His fields were flooded, and hope felt distant.

Years ago, when his wife was alive, they'd weathered storms together. Maria had been the sunlight to his earth, her laughter echoing through their home like a melody. She'd believed in him, even when doubts crept in. "Remember, Leo," she'd say, "faith is planting seeds in the darkness, trusting the sun will rise."

Back then, they'd taken refuge in each other during storms, but now, alone, Leo felt the weight of every droplet. His hands, calloused from years of tilling soil, ached with the effort of keeping his farm afloat.

One day, drenched and exhausted, Leo found an old, worn Bible in the mud by his well. He picked it up, shook off the water, and opened it randomly. "Consider the lilies... they toil not, neither do they spin." (Matthew 6:28) He'd heard these words before but never truly absorbed them.

He thought of Maria's laughter, her unwavering faith. She hadn't worried about storms or floods; she believed in the sun's eventual return. Leo realized he'd been so focused on the rain that he'd forgotten about the sun.

With renewed determination, he started planting again, not knowing if the seeds would sprout, but trusting they might. And as the days passed, the rain began to ease, and sunlight peeked through the clouds, casting golden rays over his fields.

The harvest wasn't bountiful that year, but it was enough. Enough to remind Leo of Maria's love and God's provision. He stood in his fields, wearied by life's storms but strengthened by faith. The bittersweet taste of loss mingled with the sweetness of hope, as he looked towards the rising sun.
