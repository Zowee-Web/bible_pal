Silas was a healer, but today his hands felt heavy, his heart weary from the unending stream of sick and suffering. He stood by the riverbank, watching the water flow as if it held some secret to easing his burden. The sun dipped low, casting long shadows across the cobblestones of Jericho.

A rustle in the bushes caught his ear. He turned to see an old woman emerge, her back bent like a gnarled olive tree. She carried a basket filled with rags and empty jars. Silas' eyes widened as he recognized her - Martha, who'd lost her sight after years of tears shed for her wayward son.

"What brings you here, Martha?" Silas asked gently, his voice barely above the whispering wind.

Martha looked up at him, her clouded eyes seeming to pierce right through him. "I heard whispers of a healer by the river," she said. "Thought I'd come see if it was true."

Silas sighed inwardly. Not another hopeless case. Yet, there was something about Martha's quiet dignity that stirred him. He stepped closer, taking her frail hand in his.

"I can't promise miracles, Martha," he warned softly. "My gifts are limited."

Martha smiled, a small, knowing curve of the lips. "Aren't we all?" she replied. "But tell me, Silas, do you believe God can see through our eyes?"

Intrigued, Silas nodded. "Yes, I do."

"Then let's pray together," Martha said, her voice gaining strength. "Pray that He might use our weaknesses to glimpse His glory."

They knelt there, by the river's edge, two wounded souls seeking solace in each other's company. As Silas prayed aloud, he felt a warmth spread from his chest, like sunlight breaking through clouds. When he opened his eyes, he saw Martha's gaze fixed on him, clear and bright.

"Can you see me, Martha?" he asked, astonished.

Tears welled up in her eyes. "I can't," she whispered. "But I feel Him. And that's enough."

Silas stood up, helping Martha to her feet. Together, they walked back towards Jericho, the setting sun painting their path with gold. In the quiet wonder of the moment, Silas realized his hands felt lighter, his heart filled with hope once more. For even in his weariness, he had been healed too.
