There's something about the quiet hours before dawn — the hum of ovens, the rhythm of kneading, the patience that bread demands. And there's something familiar about the impulse to keep working, to hover, to believe that more effort will always produce more goodness.

But bread has its own timing. The dough doesn't need watching to rise. It needs warmth, and stillness, and time.

It's interesting how often the most fruitful moments happen in the spaces we didn't fill with activity. A conversation that deepens during a pause. A solution that surfaces after stepping away. A kind of quiet restoration that arrives not because it was earned, but because there was finally room for it.

Scripture holds a recurring rhythm of work and rest woven together — not as opposites, but as companions. The fields are tended, and then there is evening. The seeds are scattered, and then comes sleep. And somehow, in the unseen hours, growth happens.

Sometimes the bread rises best when the baker's hands are still. Sometimes the most trusting thing a person can do is step back from the work and let the warmth do what warmth does.
