There is something quietly remarkable about hands that dig into the earth with no expectation of shade. Seeds pressed into soil by fingers that already know the arithmetic — that the years ahead belong to someone else's story. And still, the planting happens.

It is a pattern that shows up in so many ordinary places. The neighbor who fixes a fence along a property line they may not live behind much longer. The grandmother who tucks money into an envelope for a college fund, imagining a graduation she may only attend in spirit. The teacher who speaks a word of encouragement into a child who won't remember the source, only the warmth.

Faith often moves in this direction — forward, outward, beyond the borders of what we ourselves will touch or taste. There is a kind of love that doesn't ask to be present for the harvest. It simply trusts that the harvest matters.

Sometimes the most sacred work is the work that will never bear our name. The roots go down in silence. The branches reach toward a sky that someone, someday, will look up and notice — and feel, without quite knowing why, that they were loved before they arrived.
