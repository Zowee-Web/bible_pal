"What if the waves are too big?" Juliet whispered to her mother. They stood at the edge of the boardwalk, looking down at the beach below.

It was Juliet's first time at the ocean. She had seen pictures in books at school. She had watched videos on her grandmother's tablet. But the real ocean was louder than she expected. It crashed and hissed and pulled at the sand like it was hungry for something.

Her stomach felt tight, the way it felt before a spelling test or when the lights went out during a storm.

Her mother squeezed her hand. "We don't have to go in the water. We don't even have to go close. Let's just walk on the sand."

They took off their shoes and left them by the wooden steps. The sand was warm and soft between Juliet's toes. Almost like flour, she thought, except gritty. They walked along the waterline where the sand turned dark and firm.

A wave slid toward them. Juliet jumped back three big steps. But the water barely reached her — just a thin sheet of cool white foam that tickled her ankles for one second and then slipped away.

She looked down. Tiny bubbles popped and vanished in the wet sand. And there, right by her left foot, sat a shell. It was small and pink, no bigger than her thumbnail, curved like a little cup you might make for a fairy.

Juliet picked it up carefully. Inside, the shell was smooth and shiny, the color of a sunset fading into evening.

"Look," she said, holding it up.

Her mother knelt beside her. "Oh, that's a beautiful one, Jules."

"Do you think something lived in here?"

"Once, yes. Something very small."

Juliet turned the shell over in her fingers. She liked how it fit perfectly in the center of her palm, like it had been waiting there for her.

She looked at the ocean again. It still crashed and hissed. The waves still rose up tall and fell down hard. But now she noticed something else too — the way sunlight made the water sparkle in long, moving lines of gold. The way the breeze smelled like salt and something clean she couldn't quite name. The way sandpipers ran along the shore on their tiny stick legs, not afraid at all.

Another wave came sliding in. This time, Juliet didn't jump. She stood still and let it wash over her toes. The water was cold, much colder than the bath at home. But it felt good in the summer heat.

The wave pulled back and left a line of foam around her feet like a lace bracelet.

"Can we go a little closer?" Juliet asked.

Her mother smiled. "Of course."

They walked forward together, three small steps. The next wave came up to Juliet's shins. She felt the sand shift under her feet as the water pulled back. It was strange but not scary. More like the ocean was saying hello.

Juliet put the pink shell in her pocket. She could feel its smooth curve against her leg.

The ocean was still loud. The waves were still big. But her stomach didn't feel so tight anymore. It felt more like the fizzy feeling she got on Christmas morning — not worry, but something close to wonder.

She took one more step forward and let the water swirl around her knees.
