"What if the waves are too big?" Juliet whispered to her mother. They stood at the edge of the boardwalk, looking down at the beach.

It was Juliet's first time at the ocean. She had seen pictures in books. She had watched videos on her grandmother's tablet. But the real ocean was louder than she expected. It crashed and hissed and pulled at the sand like it was hungry.

Her mother squeezed her hand. "We don't have to go in. Let's just walk."

They took off their shoes and stepped onto the sand. It was warm and soft between Juliet's toes. They walked along the waterline where the sand turned dark and firm.

A wave slid toward them. Juliet jumped back. The water barely touched her feet — just a thin sheet of cool foam that tickled her ankles and then pulled away.

She looked down. Tiny bubbles popped in the wet sand. And there, right by her left foot, was a shell. It was small and pink, no bigger than her thumbnail, curved like a little cup.

Juliet picked it up. Inside, the shell was smooth and shiny, the color of a sunset.

"Look," she said.

Her mother knelt beside her. "That's a beautiful one."

Juliet held the shell in her palm. She looked at the ocean again. It still crashed and hissed. But now she noticed something else too — the way the sunlight made the water sparkle in moving lines, the way the breeze smelled like salt and something clean she couldn't name.

Another wave came. This time, Juliet stood still and let it wash over her toes.

It was cold. It was nice.

She put the pink shell in her pocket and took one step closer to the water.
