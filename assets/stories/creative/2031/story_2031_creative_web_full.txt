Lila's eyes were heavy before dinner even started. She had been up since dawn for the school field trip to the nature center. They walked for miles along muddy trails through the woods. She identified fourteen different trees, watched a hawk circle above the meadow, and found a rock shaped like a heart near the creek bed.

She had carried her backpack and her friend Maya's water bottle and the heart-shaped rock, which was heavier than it looked. By the time the bus brought them back, her legs ached and her eyelids felt like they had tiny weights attached.

Now she sat at the kitchen table, her head drooping toward her plate.

"Eat a little," her father said gently, setting the plate in front of her. Rice and green beans and chicken cut into small pieces.

She ate three bites of rice and two green beans. The fork felt heavy in her hand. She chewed slowly.

"That's enough," her father said. He carried her plate to the sink. "Bath time."

Lila shuffled to the bathroom. Her father ran the water, testing the temperature with his wrist. He added lavender soap, and bubbles bloomed across the surface.

The warm water wrapped around her like a liquid blanket. She sank down and leaned against the cool tile. Steam curled around her face. The lavender smelled like the garden at her grandmother's house, like summer evenings when lightning bugs blinked in the tall grass.

The warm water softened everything. Her sore muscles. Her tired thoughts. The long day that had filled her up from top to bottom.

She could barely keep her eyes open while her father combed her wet hair afterward. He hummed a song she had known since she was a baby — a simple melody that rose and fell like breathing. The comb moved through her hair in slow, even strokes. It caught once on a small tangle, and he worked it free so gently she barely felt it.

"I'm so tired, Daddy," she murmured.

"I know, little one. That means you had a good day."

He carried her to bed. She was almost too big to be carried — she was seven now, and her legs were getting long. But not quite too big. Not tonight. She rested her head on his shoulder and felt his steady heartbeat against her cheek.

Her bedroom was dark except for the nightlight shaped like a crescent moon. Her bed was cool and fresh. The sheets smelled like clean laundry, like wind and sunshine folded into cotton.

Her father tucked the blanket under her chin — snug but not too tight. He placed the heart-shaped rock on the nightstand, leaning it against the lamp so she could see it in the morning.

"Thank you, God, for this tired girl," he said softly, his hand resting on her forehead. "And for the good day that made her tired."

Lila smiled with her eyes closed. The room was dark and warm and exactly right. The house creaked gently, settling in for the night. She could hear her father's footsteps moving softly down the hall. Each step quieter than the last.

She was asleep before the house went fully still.
