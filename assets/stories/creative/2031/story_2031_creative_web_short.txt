Lila's eyes were heavy before dinner even started. She had been up since dawn for the school field trip to the nature center. They walked for miles along muddy trails. She had carried her backpack and her friend's water bottle and a rock shaped like a heart that she couldn't leave behind.

Now she sat at the kitchen table, her head drooping toward her plate.

"Eat a little," her father said gently.

She ate three bites of rice and two green beans. The fork felt heavy in her hand.

Her father carried her plate to the sink. "Bath time," he said.

The warm water wrapped around her like a blanket. She leaned her head against the cool tile and closed her eyes. Steam curled around her face. The lavender soap her father used smelled like the garden at her grandmother's house.

She could barely keep her eyes open while her father combed her wet hair. He hummed a song she had known since she was a baby. The comb moved through her hair in slow, even strokes.

"I'm so tired, Daddy," she murmured.

"I know, little one. That means you had a good day."

He carried her to bed. She was almost too big to be carried, but not quite. Not tonight. She rested her head on his shoulder and felt his steady heartbeat against her cheek.

Her bed was cool and soft. The sheets smelled like clean laundry. Her father tucked the blanket under her chin and set her heart-shaped rock on the nightstand.

"Thank you, God, for this tired girl," he said softly. "And for the good day that made her tired."

Lila smiled with her eyes closed. The room was dark and warm. The house creaked gently, settling in for the night. She could hear her father's footsteps moving softly down the hall.

She was asleep before they reached the kitchen.
