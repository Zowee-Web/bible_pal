Theo's backpack weighed a thousand pounds. At least it felt that way climbing the steps to his front door. His legs were heavy and his eyes were scratchy and his brain felt like cotton.

It had been the longest day. Math test in the morning. Soccer tryouts at lunch. A group project after school where he did most of the work while Jaylen and Mia argued about the poster color.

He dropped his backpack by the door. It landed with a thud.

The house was quiet. His mother worked late on Thursdays. But she had left the porch light on, and when Theo walked into the kitchen, he found a note on the counter next to a plate covered in foil.

The note said: Chicken soup on the stove. Cornbread under the foil. You worked hard today. I'm proud of you. — Mom

Theo peeled back the foil. The cornbread was still warm. He could smell butter and honey.

He ladled soup into a bowl and sat at the kitchen table. The soup was thick with noodles and carrots and chicken so tender it fell apart on his spoon. He ate slowly. Each bite spread warmth through his chest and into his tired legs.

After dinner, he washed his bowl and climbed the stairs to his room. His bed was made — his mother must have done that too.

Theo kicked off his shoes and lay down on top of the covers. The pillow was cool against his cheek. Through the window, the sky was turning purple and orange. A bird sang three notes in the tree outside, then went quiet.

His eyes closed. The math test and the tryouts and the poster argument drifted away, getting smaller and softer like a radio being turned down.

The house held him. His mother's note sat on the counter downstairs, and even though she wasn't here yet, he could feel her caring wrapped around him like a blanket.

Theo slept.
