Grief has a way of collecting in objects. A mug, a coat, a pair of shoes by the door — things that were ordinary when the person was here and become almost sacred when they are not. We handle them carefully, or we avoid them entirely, because the object holds something we are not ready to release.

Theo's fear was specific and understandable. He could still hear Joanna's laugh when he looked at the chip on the rim. He was afraid that using the mug would normalize it, would wear the memory smooth the way hands wear the finish off a railing. He was protecting the sound of her by refusing to touch the thing that carried it.

But Simone saw something her father could not yet see from where he stood. The mug was facing the wall. And a thing that faces the wall doesn't hold a memory — it hides one. Grief that hides behind the everyday cups, that turns its handle inward, that nobody is allowed to touch — that kind of grief doesn't preserve love. It seals it off.

Turning the mug around wasn't about moving on. It was about letting the memory face forward. Letting the chip show. Allowing the laughter to be present in the kitchen instead of locked behind glass and silence.

There may be something in your own life that you've placed on a high shelf and turned toward the wall — not a mug, perhaps, but a memory, a place, a name you no longer say aloud. It might be worth asking whether the thing you are protecting could bear a little more light. Not all at once. Not today, necessarily. But handle out. Front row. Ready for when you are.
