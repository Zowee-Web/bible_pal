The envelope sat on the kitchen counter for three days before Lorna opened it.

It was from the hospital — she could tell by the logo in the corner. She put the groceries away around it. She set her keys on top of it. She wiped the counter on both sides of it but never moved it.

Her daughter, Cass, noticed on the second day. "Mom. The letter."

"I see it."

"Are you going to open it?"

"When I'm ready."

The truth was that Lorna had been waiting for this letter for six weeks, and now that it had arrived she could not make her hands pick it up. The last time she'd gotten a letter like this — eleven years ago, with different handwriting on the front — she'd been sitting in her car in the hospital parking lot. She'd opened it with her fingernail and read it in the front seat with the engine still running. That one had been bad news. The kind that rearranges your life.

She hadn't opened an envelope the same way since.

On the third morning, Lorna came downstairs early. The house was quiet. Rain tapped the kitchen window. The envelope sat where she'd left it.

She picked it up. The paper was cool and smooth. She slid her thumb under the flap.

The results were normal. Everything was fine.

Lorna set the letter down and stood in the kitchen with both hands flat on the counter. The rain kept falling. The refrigerator hummed. The house was exactly the same as it had been ten seconds ago, but the air felt different — lighter, like someone had opened a window she hadn't known was closed.
