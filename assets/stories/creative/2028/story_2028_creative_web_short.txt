Priya woke up grumpy. Rain streaked the window. Her favorite cereal was gone. Her little sister had used the last of the milk on the floor, making what she called "a lake for ants."

"This day is already terrible," Priya said at the kitchen table.

Her grandmother, who was visiting from out of town, sat across from her drinking tea. "Hmm," she said. "Tell me three good things."

"There aren't any."

"Look harder."

Priya sighed. She looked around the kitchen. The overhead light was warm and yellow. The rain made a tapping sound on the roof that sounded almost like a song.

"The light is nice," she said reluctantly.

"That's one."

She listened. "The rain sounds okay, I guess."

"That's two."

Her sister ran through the kitchen wearing mismatched socks, giggling at nothing. She stopped and hugged Priya's leg. Her hair smelled like strawberry shampoo.

Priya almost smiled. "My sister is funny."

"Three." Her grandmother sipped her tea.

They ate toast with honey because there was no cereal. The honey was golden and sweet, and Priya decided toast might actually be better.

After breakfast, she stood by the window. The rain had slowed. Each raindrop on the glass caught a tiny reflection of the kitchen behind her — the yellow light, her grandmother's gray hair, her sister's pink socks.

"Grandma?" Priya said. "Can we do the three things again at dinner?"

Her grandmother smiled over her teacup. "We can do it at every meal if you want."

The rain stopped by noon. Priya went outside and found a puddle in the driveway that reflected the clearing sky. She stepped around it carefully so she wouldn't break the reflection.

The day that started terrible turned out to be full of small, good things. She just had to look.
