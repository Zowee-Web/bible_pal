Tomas sat on the back porch with an empty dog bowl in his lap. Biscuit had been gone for three days now. The vet said it was time, and his parents agreed, and Tomas had held Biscuit's soft ear in the waiting room until the very end.

The bowl still said BISCUIT in wobbly blue letters. Tomas had painted those letters when he was six.

He ran his thumb over the paint. Some of it was chipping away.

His mother opened the screen door. "Lunch is ready," she said softly.

"I'm not hungry."

She sat beside him. She didn't say anything about eating. She just sat.

The backyard was quiet without Biscuit. No jingling collar. No bark at the squirrels. No paws clicking on the porch boards. The silence was the loudest thing Tomas had ever heard.

"I keep thinking I hear him," Tomas said. His throat felt tight.

"Me too," his mother said. "This morning I almost poured food in his bowl."

A tear slid down Tomas's cheek. He didn't wipe it.

They sat together for a while. A breeze moved through the maple tree and scattered yellow leaves across the grass. One leaf landed in the dog bowl, bright and perfect.

Tomas picked it up. He turned it over in his fingers. The veins in the leaf made a pattern like tiny rivers.

"Can I keep this?" he asked.

"Of course."

He carried the leaf inside and pressed it between the pages of his favorite book. The book was about a boy and his dog, and Biscuit used to sleep on his feet while he read it.

That night, Tomas set the dog bowl on his bookshelf next to the book with the leaf inside. He looked at the wobbly blue letters.

"Goodnight, Biscuit," he whispered.

The house was quiet. But it didn't feel empty. It felt like love was still there, tucked into the corners, quiet but steady.
