Harriet had been fighting the garden hose for twenty minutes when she finally dropped it in the mud and sat down on the porch step.

The hose had a kink she couldn't straighten. The nozzle leaked. The spigot connection was stripped and water sprayed sideways every time she turned it on. She had tried pliers. She had tried tape. She had tried gripping the connection with a towel and twisting until her hands shook.

The garden needed water. The tomatoes were wilting. The basil looked like it had given up a week ago.

She sat on the step with mud on her knees and water soaking through her shoes. The hose lay in the grass like a green snake that had won.

Her neighbor, Arthur, appeared at the fence. He was eighty-one and moved slowly. He looked at Harriet. He looked at the hose. He looked at the garden.

"You could use a watering can," he said.

Harriet stared at him. "I have a hose."

"You had a hose." He nodded at the tangled mess in the yard. "Now you have a problem. My watering can holds two gallons. It'll take five trips."

"That's too many trips."

"It's five trips or no tomatoes." He disappeared behind the fence and came back with a green watering can. He set it on the ground next to Harriet.

She looked at it. Two gallons. Five trips. No fighting.

She filled the can at the outdoor tap. She carried it to the tomato bed and poured. The water soaked into the dry soil with a sound like breathing. She went back and filled it again.

Five trips. No kinks. No leaks. No pliers.

Arthur watched from his chair on the other side of the fence. When she finished, Harriet set the can by the spigot and sat down on the step again. Her shoes were still wet. But the tomatoes were watered.
