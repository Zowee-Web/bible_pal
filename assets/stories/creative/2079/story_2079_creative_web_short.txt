Mara's legs hurt.

Not the kind of hurt that goes away when you sit down. The deep kind. The kind that lives in your knees and your ankles and the bottoms of your feet all at once.

The trail went up. It kept going up. Rocks and roots and red dirt that slipped under her boots.

"I can't," she said.

Her dad stopped three steps ahead. He turned around. Sweat ran down the sides of his face and dripped off his chin.

"Okay," he said. "Let's rest."

They sat on a flat rock. Mara pulled her water bottle out and drank. It was warm. A lizard watched them from a crack in the stone, its tiny throat pulsing.

"How much farther?" she asked.

Her dad looked up the trail. "A good bit."

Mara put her face in her hands. Her backpack pressed against her shoulders. She could hear the wind moving through dry grass somewhere above them.

"I want to go back down," she said.

Her dad nodded. He didn't say anything for a while. He just sat there beside her, pulling a granola bar apart and eating it piece by piece.

Then Mara stood up. She didn't know exactly why. Her legs still ached. The trail still went up. Nothing had changed except that she had rested and the lizard had gone.

She took one step. Then another.

Her dad followed.

The switchbacks turned and turned. Mara counted her breaths. She watched her boots. Left, right, left, right. Dust rose in small clouds around her feet.

The trees got shorter. The rocks got bigger. The wind got louder.

And then there was no more up.

Mara stood at the top. The whole valley opened below her, golden and wide and stitched with the dark thread of a river. A hawk circled far beneath where she stood.

Her dad came up beside her. He was breathing hard. So was she.

The wind pressed against them both, steady and warm, and Mara's shadow stretched long across the stone.
