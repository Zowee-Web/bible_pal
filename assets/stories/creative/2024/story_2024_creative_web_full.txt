Amos sat on the edge of his bed, pulling his socks on slowly. Today was the first day at his new school. His stomach felt like a knot tied too tight.

His room still had boxes stacked in the corner. They had moved to this town just ten days ago. Everything smelled different here. The air coming through the window carried the scent of pine trees instead of the city bus exhaust he was used to.

"Breakfast is ready," his mother called from the kitchen.

He walked downstairs and stared at his toast. He took one small bite but could barely swallow it. The jam tasted too sweet. The milk was too cold.

"What if nobody talks to me?" he asked quietly.

His mother sat down across from him. She folded her hands around her coffee mug. "That's a big worry," she said. "Can I tell you something? Worries always look bigger in the morning. By afternoon, they usually shrink."

Amos wasn't sure he believed that. He put on his backpack. It felt heavier than usual, even though it only held a notebook and two pencils.

His mother drove him to school, and the building looked enormous. Red brick walls stretched in both directions. Kids streamed through the front doors like a river of bright jackets and lunchboxes. Everyone seemed to know exactly where to go.

He stood on the sidewalk. His feet didn't want to move. The knot in his stomach pulled tighter.

Then a boy with red sneakers stopped beside him. "Are you new? I'm Felix." The boy smiled easily, like smiling was the simplest thing in the world. He had a bandage on his left elbow and a backpack covered in stickers.

"I'm Amos," he managed.

"Want to see the fish tank in the library? It has a catfish named Potato."

Amos almost laughed. "A catfish named Potato?"

"Come on." Felix walked toward the doors, and Amos followed. His feet moved without him thinking about it.

Inside, the hallway smelled like floor wax and crayons. Bright paper cutouts decorated the walls. Felix pointed out the water fountain that made a funny gurgling sound and the janitor's closet that everyone called "the cave."

The library was warm and quiet. Bookshelves stood in neat rows like tall, patient friends. And there in the corner sat a large glass tank. A whiskered catfish drifted slowly behind the glass. It was brown and round and did look a little like a potato.

"See?" Felix said proudly.

Amos pressed his fingers against the cool glass. The catfish turned and seemed to look right at him. He felt the knot in his stomach loosen. Just a little. Then a little more.

Their teacher, Mrs. Navarro, had a voice like warm honey. She asked Amos to tell the class one thing about himself. His voice shook, but he said, "I like drawing animals." A girl in the second row whispered, "Me too."

By lunchtime, he sat with Felix and two other kids who argued cheerfully about whether dogs or cats were better. Amos said dogs. Everyone laughed, and it felt warm, like stepping into sunlight after being inside all day.

That afternoon, they had art class. Amos drew a catfish. He gave it a little smile. Felix leaned over and said, "That's definitely Potato."

Walking to his mother's car after the last bell, Amos noticed the school looked smaller than it had that morning. The brick walls didn't stretch so far. The doors didn't seem so tall.

"How was it?" his mother asked, searching his face.

Amos climbed in and buckled his seatbelt. He thought about Felix's red sneakers and Mrs. Navarro's warm voice and the girl who also liked drawing animals.

"There's a catfish named Potato," he said, grinning.

His mother laughed. The sound filled the car like music.

The knot was gone. And tomorrow morning, Amos thought, the school would look even smaller.
