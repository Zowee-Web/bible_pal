Amos sat on the edge of his bed, pulling his socks on slowly. Today was the first day at his new school. His stomach felt like a knot tied too tight.

"Breakfast is ready," his mother called from the kitchen.

He walked downstairs and stared at his toast. He took one small bite but could barely swallow it.

"What if nobody talks to me?" he asked quietly.

His mother sat down across from him. "That's a big worry," she said. "Can I tell you something? Worries always look bigger in the morning."

Amos put on his backpack. It felt heavier than usual. His mother drove him to school, and the building looked enormous. Kids streamed through the front doors like a river of bright jackets and lunchboxes.

He stood on the sidewalk. His feet didn't want to move.

Then a boy with red sneakers stopped beside him. "Are you new? I'm Felix." The boy smiled easily, like smiling was the simplest thing in the world.

"I'm Amos," he managed.

"Want to see the fish tank in the library? It has a catfish named Potato."

Amos almost laughed. "A catfish named Potato?"

"Come on." Felix walked toward the doors, and Amos followed.

Inside, the hallway smelled like floor wax and crayons. Felix showed him the library, and sure enough, a whiskered catfish drifted behind the glass. It did look a little like a potato.

Amos felt the knot in his stomach loosen. Just a little. Then a little more.

By lunchtime, he sat with Felix and two other kids who argued cheerfully about whether dogs or cats were better. Amos said dogs. Everyone laughed, and it felt warm.

Walking to his mother's car that afternoon, he noticed the school looked smaller than it had that morning.

"How was it?" she asked.

Amos climbed in and buckled his seatbelt. "There's a catfish named Potato," he said, grinning.

The knot was gone.
