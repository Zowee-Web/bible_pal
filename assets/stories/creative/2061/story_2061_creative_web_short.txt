Iris's best friend Kaya was moving to California on Saturday.

They had known for three weeks, but Iris hadn't wanted to talk about it. Every time Kaya brought it up — the new house, the school she'd go to, the beach that was walking distance — Iris changed the subject. She asked about homework. She asked about the cafeteria menu. She asked about anything that wasn't California.

On Friday after school, they sat on the swings at the park. The chains were cold. Their breath made small clouds. They swung slowly, feet dragging in the wood chips.

"I made you something," Kaya said. She pulled a bracelet out of her pocket — purple and green thread braided together, with a small silver bead in the middle.

Iris took it. The thread was soft between her fingers. "You made this?"

"It took three tries. The first two fell apart."

Iris tied it on her left wrist. The silver bead caught the light.

"I don't want you to go," Iris said. Her voice came out thick.

Kaya kicked at the wood chips. "I don't want to go either."

They swung in silence. The chains squeaked. A dog barked somewhere across the park. The sky was turning the color of peaches at the edges.

"Will you call me?" Iris asked.

"Every day."

"You won't."

"Okay, not every day. But a lot."

Iris looked at the bracelet on her wrist. She looked at Kaya — her dark curly hair, the blue coat she always wore, the gap in her teeth.

"Okay," Iris said.

They swung until their fingers were numb. Then they walked home the long way, taking every possible detour, making the last walk last as long as it could.
