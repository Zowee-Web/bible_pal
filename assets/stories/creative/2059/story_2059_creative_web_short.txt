Gabe's neighbor Mrs. Flores had a piano that nobody played anymore.

He knew this because he could see it through her front window when he walked to school — a dark brown upright with a bench and a stack of sheet music that never moved. Mrs. Flores used to play it every evening. Gabe could hear it from his bedroom. But she hadn't played in a long time.

One Saturday, Gabe was helping his dad carry groceries from the car when Mrs. Flores came out to get her mail. Her hands shook a little when she opened the mailbox.

"Mrs. Flores," Gabe said, "how come you stopped playing the piano?"

His dad gave him a look. Gabe ignored it.

Mrs. Flores held her mail against her chest. "My hands," she said. She held them up. Her fingers were bent at the knuckles, stiff-looking. "Arthritis. I can't reach the keys the way I used to."

"Can you reach some of them?"

She looked at him. "Some. Not enough for the pieces I used to play."

"But some."

Mrs. Flores tilted her head. "What are you getting at, young man?"

"I just think some notes are better than no notes."

She didn't say anything. Gabe picked up the grocery bag and went inside.

That evening, sitting on his bed doing homework, Gabe heard something through the wall. Slow, halting notes. Not a song — just a few keys pressed one at a time, with long pauses between them. Then a short melody, simple, the kind of thing you play with one hand.

It stopped. Started again. Three notes. Then five.

Gabe put his pencil down and listened. The notes kept coming — slow, patient, not the music Mrs. Flores used to play, but music all the same.
