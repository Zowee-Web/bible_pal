Hannah Hope stood at the back of the line and counted the kids ahead of her. Fourteen. Her number was fifteen.

The auditorium smelled like floor wax and old curtains. Rows and rows of parents sat in folding chairs. Some held phones up to record. Some whispered behind their programs.

A boy named Marcus stepped to the microphone. "Necessary," the judge said. Marcus spelled it right. Everyone clapped.

Hannah's stomach turned.

She pressed her fingers against the index card in her pocket. She had written her name on it that morning, just to practice holding something steady. Her letters had come out wobbly.

Another kid walked to the microphone. Then another. One girl missed her word and walked off the stage with her chin tucked down. Hannah watched her go.

Nine kids left. Then seven.

Hannah closed her eyes. She thought about her kitchen table at home, where she and her dad practiced words every night after dinner. He would say a word. She would spell it. When she got one wrong, he would just say, "Try it again, Hannah." That was all. Try it again.

Five kids left.

Her hands were cold. She rubbed them together.

Three kids.

One.

"Number fifteen," the judge called.

Hannah walked across the stage. Her shoes tapped on the wood floor. The microphone was thin and silver, taller than she expected. Someone adjusted it down for her. She could see the whole auditorium now. Hundreds of faces looked back at her.

"Your word is 'courage,'" the judge said.

Hannah breathed in. The air tasted like dust and warm lights.

"Courage," she said. Her voice came out small but clear through the speakers. "C-O-U-R-A-G-E. Courage."

The judge looked at his paper.

A long second passed.

"That is correct."

The audience clapped. Hannah stepped back from the microphone. Her hands were still shaking, but she held them open at her sides, palms up, like she was catching rain.
