Hannah Hope stood at the back of the line and counted the kids ahead of her. Fourteen. Her number was fifteen.

The auditorium smelled like floor wax and old curtains. Rows and rows of parents filled folding chairs that stretched all the way to the back wall. Some held phones up to record. Some whispered behind their programs. A baby fussed somewhere near the doors.

A boy named Marcus stepped up to the microphone. "Necessary," the judge said. Marcus spelled it right. Everyone clapped. He walked back to his seat and let out a long breath.

Hannah's stomach turned.

She pressed her fingers against the index card in her pocket. She had written her name on it that morning, just to practice holding something steady. Her letters had come out wobbly. Her mom had taped the card to the fridge and said, "For when you get home."

Another kid walked to the microphone. Then another. One girl missed her word — "rhythm" — and walked off the stage with her chin tucked down. Her mother met her at the side steps and put a hand on her back. Hannah watched them go.

Nine kids left. Then seven.

Hannah closed her eyes. She thought about her kitchen table at home, where she and her dad practiced spelling words every night after dinner. He would say a word. She would spell it out loud. When she got one wrong, he would just say, "Try it again, Hannah." That was all. No frowning. No sighing. Just try it again.

She had gotten "courage" wrong three times last Tuesday. Each time her dad waited. Each time she tried again. On the fourth try, she got it right, and he tapped the table once with his knuckle.

Five kids left.

Her hands were cold. She rubbed them together and tucked them under her arms.

Three kids.

Two.

One.

"Number fifteen," the judge called.

Hannah walked across the stage. Her shoes tapped on the wood floor, and the sound bounced up into the high ceiling. The microphone was thin and silver, taller than she expected. A woman in a black shirt reached over and adjusted it down. Hannah wrapped her fingers around the stand.

She could see the whole auditorium now. Hundreds of faces looked back at her. The lights were warm and bright, and beyond them the exit signs glowed red at every door.

She found her dad in the fourth row. He was sitting still, his hands folded in his lap. He didn't wave or mouth any words. He just looked at her the way he looked at her across the kitchen table.

"Your word is 'courage,'" the judge said.

Hannah breathed in. The air tasted like dust and warm lights and something sweet from the bake sale table in the hallway.

"Courage," she repeated. Her voice came out small but clear through the speakers. She could hear it bounce off the back wall. "C-O-U-R-A-G-E. Courage."

The judge looked down at his paper. He adjusted his glasses.

A long second passed. Someone coughed.

"That is correct."

The audience clapped. The sound filled the room like a wave rolling in. Hannah stepped back from the microphone. Her hands were still shaking, but she held them open at her sides, palms up, the way you might stand outside after a long dry spell, feeling the first drops of rain land one by one in your hands.
