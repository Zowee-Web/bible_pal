Grant saw the drawing before anyone else did. It was taped to Amara's locker — a mean cartoon of her face with her braids sticking out like wires and the word WEIRD underneath in big red letters.

Amara hadn't gotten to school yet. Grant looked up and down the hallway. Kids streamed past, but nobody stopped. Nobody looked at the drawing the way Grant was looking at it.

He knew who did it. Marcus and his friends had been making fun of Amara's braids all week, calling them antenna. They sat right behind Grant in class.

Grant reached up and pulled the drawing off the locker. The tape made a small ripping sound.

His hands were shaking. He folded the paper once, then again. Then he walked to Mrs. Patterson's classroom and stood in the doorway.

She looked up from her desk. "Good morning, Grant."

He held out the folded paper. His throat felt like someone had a hand around it.

"I found this on Amara's locker," he said. His voice came out thin and strange.

Mrs. Patterson unfolded it. Her face changed. "Do you know who made this?"

Grant's mouth went dry. Marcus sat behind him every single day. Marcus who was bigger than him, who had friends everywhere, who would know exactly who told.

"Yeah," Grant said. "I do."

He told her.

Walking back to his locker, Grant's stomach churned. By lunch, Marcus would find out. Everyone would find out. The hallway felt longer than usual, and his sneakers squeaked on the floor in a way that sounded too loud.

Amara arrived five minutes later. She opened her locker and didn't see anything wrong. She just grabbed her books and headed to class, humming something.

She had no idea. That was the thing about it — she would never know what had been there.

Grant sat down at his desk. Marcus dropped into the seat behind him. Grant opened his notebook and stared at a blank page. His pencil was steady, but his heart was not.
