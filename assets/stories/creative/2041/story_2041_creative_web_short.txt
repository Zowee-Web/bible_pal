Gideon saw it happen at recess. Three kids from the older class stood in a half circle around Felix, who was new and small and wore glasses that kept sliding down his nose.

"You can't play here," one of them said. "This is our spot."

Felix hugged his basketball to his chest. He didn't say anything. He just looked at the ground.

Gideon was sitting on the bench by the water fountain, eating an apple. He wasn't friends with Felix. He didn't really know him at all. Felix had only been at the school for two weeks.

But something in Gideon's chest felt tight and hot, like a fist squeezing.

He stood up. His legs felt wobbly, like they did before he had to read aloud in class. The older kids were taller than him. One of them was in fifth grade.

Gideon walked over anyway.

"Hey, Felix," he said, and his voice came out quieter than he wanted. He cleared his throat. "Want to come play with us? We need one more for our game."

There was no game. Gideon had been sitting alone.

The three older kids looked at Gideon. He looked back. His heart was hammering so hard he could feel it in his ears.

Nobody said anything for a long moment.

Then the oldest one shrugged. "Whatever." They walked away toward the swings.

Felix pushed his glasses up his nose. "You don't really have a game, do you?"

"No," Gideon admitted.

Felix almost smiled. "We could start one."

They walked to the empty court together. Gideon's hands were still shaking a little, but he dribbled the ball Felix passed to him, and after a few minutes, two more kids came over and asked to play.

By the time the bell rang, Gideon's heart had stopped hammering. His legs felt solid again.
