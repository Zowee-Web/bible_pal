Gideon saw it happen at recess. Three kids from the older class stood in a half circle around Felix, who was new and small and wore glasses that kept sliding down his nose.

"You can't play here," the tallest one said, crossing his arms. "This is our spot. Go find somewhere else."

Felix hugged his basketball to his chest. He didn't say anything back. He just looked at the ground, his shoulders curling in like a plant that needed water.

Gideon was sitting on the bench near the water fountain, eating an apple. He had a clear view of the basketball court from there. He wasn't friends with Felix. He didn't really know him at all — Felix had only been at the school for two weeks, and he was quiet, the kind of kid who ate lunch with a book open in front of him.

But something in Gideon's chest felt tight and hot, like a fist squeezing around his ribs.

He looked around. Mrs. Patterson was on the other side of the playground, helping a first-grader who had fallen off the monkey bars. She couldn't see what was happening.

Gideon looked back at Felix. One of the older kids had grabbed the basketball and was holding it above his head, just out of Felix's reach.

Gideon stood up. His legs felt wobbly and strange, the way they felt before he had to read aloud in class or when the dentist called his name in the waiting room. The older kids were all taller than him. One of them, the one holding the ball, was in fifth grade and played on the travel soccer team.

Gideon walked over anyway. Each step felt deliberate, like walking through deep sand.

"Hey, Felix," he said, and his voice came out quieter than he wanted. He cleared his throat and tried again. "Hey. Want to come play with us? We need one more for our game."

There was no game. Gideon had been sitting alone on a bench eating an apple. But he said it like it was true.

The three older kids turned to look at him. The tallest one still held the basketball high. Gideon looked up at him. He had to tilt his head back a little. His heart was hammering so hard he could feel it in his ears and his fingertips and the back of his throat.

Nobody said anything for what felt like a very long time.

Then the tallest one lowered the ball. He looked at Gideon, then at Felix, then back at Gideon. Something shifted in his expression — not kindness exactly, but something close to it. Maybe just the recognition that someone was watching and wouldn't look away.

"Whatever," he said. He tossed the ball at Felix's chest, not hard. "Come on, guys."

They walked away toward the swings.

Felix caught the ball and pushed his glasses up his nose with the back of his wrist. His face was red, but he wasn't crying. He looked at Gideon.

"You don't really have a game going, do you?"

"No," Gideon admitted. "I was just sitting there."

Felix almost smiled. It was small, just the left corner of his mouth turning up. "We could start one."

"Yeah," Gideon said. "Okay."

They walked together to the empty half of the court. Gideon's hands were still shaking a little, so he stuffed them in his pockets until the feeling faded. Felix dribbled the ball a few times, then passed it to him. Gideon caught it and shot. It missed the basket entirely and bounced off the fence.

Felix laughed. Not a mean laugh. A real one. "Nice try."

"Terrible try," Gideon said, and then he was laughing too.

After a few minutes, two more kids wandered over — a girl named Ada and a boy from Gideon's homeroom whose name he could never remember. They asked to play. Then they were four, enough for two-on-two, and the game became real. They played until their faces were red and their shirts were sweaty. Felix turned out to be a good passer — quick and accurate, always finding the open player. Gideon still couldn't make a basket, but he got better at defense, staying low and moving his feet the way the gym teacher always told them to.

By the time the bell rang, Gideon's heart had stopped its hammering. His legs felt solid and steady beneath him. The sun was warm on the back of his neck. Felix walked beside him toward the door, still holding his basketball, bouncing it once with each step.

"Thanks," Felix said quietly.

Gideon shrugged, but he felt something warm settle in his chest right where the tight, hot fist had been. "See you tomorrow?"

"Yeah," Felix said. "See you tomorrow."
