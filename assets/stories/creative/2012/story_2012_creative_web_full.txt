Warm sand pressed between Renzo's toes as he walked along the shore. Gulls called softly overhead, and small waves curled against the beach with a quiet hush. His older sister, Paloma, sat on a blanket nearby, reading a book with a blue cover. Their grandmother's cottage stood just up the hill, with its yellow door and window boxes full of pink flowers. Renzo loved this beach more than anywhere else. Every summer, he and Paloma came here for two whole weeks.

Renzo knelt near the water's edge and started to build. He scooped wet sand into a tall mound, then shaped it carefully with his hands. He pressed shells into the walls for windows. He dug a little moat around the base and filled it with water from his green bucket. For over a hour, he worked on his sandcastle. It had four towers, a bridge made of driftwood, and a doorway just big enough for his thumb. He sat back on his heels and smiled at it.

Then the tide began to come in. Renzo watched the first wave slide up the sand and touch the edge of his moat. The moat filled and overflowed. The next wave was bigger. It reached the castle walls and softened them. Renzo stood up quickly and tried to pack more sand around the base. But another wave came, and another. The driftwood bridge floated away. One tower slumped sideways and melted into the wet ground. Then the whole castle folded in on itself, like a cake left out in the rain. The shells he had pressed into the walls scattered across the sand.

Renzo stood there looking at the flat, wet place where his castle had been. His throat felt tight. He sat down right where he was, not caring that the shallow water soaked his shorts. He pulled his knees up to his chest. He had worked so hard. He had been so careful with every tower and every shell. And now it was just gone.

Paloma looked up from her book. She set it down on the blanket and walked over to him. She didn't say anything at first. She just sat down next to him in the wet sand. The water washed over both of their legs, cool and steady.

"It was a really good castle," Paloma said quietly.

Renzo nodded but didn't look at her. His eyes stung a little.

"I saw you make that bridge," she said. "That was my favorite part."

"It took me a long time," Renzo whispered.

Paloma picked up one of the scattered shells. She turned it over in her hand. It was small and white with a pale purple stripe. She held it out to him, and he took it. He rubbed his thumb across the smooth inside of the shell.

They sat together for a while. The waves kept coming and going. A sandpiper walked past them on thin legs, pecking at the sand. Paloma rested her chin on her knees.

"Do you want to build another one?" she asked. "I could help this time. We could build it farther up the beach, where the water doesn't reach."

Renzo looked at the dry sand higher up the shore. He looked at the shell in his hand. He didn't feel like building right now. Everything still ached a little inside his chest.

"Maybe in a minute," he said.

"Okay," Paloma said. "I'll wait with you."

So they sat. Paloma found a long piece of seagrass and braided it slowly. Renzo watched the waves smooth out the last traces of his castle until the sand looked like it had never been touched at all. That made his chest hurt a little more. But Paloma was right there beside him, and the sound of the water was steady and familiar.

After a while, Renzo took a breath. He stood up and brushed the sand off his hands. "Okay," he said. "Let's build it up there."

Paloma smiled and stood up too. They carried the green bucket to a dry, flat spot near the beach grass. Renzo started a new mound, and Paloma helped him pack it firm and tight. She found two perfect sticks for a new bridge. Renzo pressed the purple-striped shell right above the doorway.

"That's the special one," he said.

"It looks perfect there," Paloma told him.

They worked side by side as the afternoon stretched on. This castle grew even bigger than the first one. Paloma carved little steps up one side with a spoon from their lunch bag. Renzo made a path of tiny pebbles leading to the front door. They decorated the towers with bits of sea glass, green and white and soft brown.

When they finished, Renzo stepped back to look. The castle stood steady and solid on the dry sand. It wasn't the same as the first one. It couldn't be. But it was good. It was something new, built with his sister's hands next to his.

Paloma put her arm around his shoulder. Down the beach, the tide was already pulling back out again. Their grandmother called from the yellow door that supper was almost ready. Renzo picked up the green bucket, and they walked up the hill together, sand on their feet and salt in their hair.
