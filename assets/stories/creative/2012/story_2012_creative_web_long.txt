Callum pressed his hands into the wet sand. It was cool against his palms. He spread his fingers wide and pushed down, making a flat base. Then he scooped up more sand and packed it on top, shaping it with careful fingers.

He patted the sides of a tower. He used his thumb to smooth a curve near the top. A little ridge of sand stuck out on one side, so he pressed it flat. The tower grew taller, bit by bit.

The beach smelled like salt and seaweed. Warm afternoon air brushed his cheeks. His knees left two round dents in the sand where he knelt. Tiny grains stuck to his shins and the tops of his feet.

He dug a shallow trench around the tower with one finger. That would be the moat. Then he built up the walls, handful by handful, pressing each scoop firm before adding the next. The walls came up to his wrist on each side.

Callum found a flat shell near his left knee. It was smooth and gray with a thin white stripe. He laid it across the moat to make a bridge. It fit perfectly. He found a smaller shell and pressed it into the front wall to make a little doorway.

Small waves curled and uncurled far from his feet. They made a soft, steady sound. He had been working for a long time. His fingernails had wet sand packed underneath them. A smudge of sand sat on his nose where he had rubbed it.

"Look, Petra," he called to his sister. She sat on a striped towel nearby, reading a book with bent corners. Her sandals lay in a tumbled pile beside her. "It's the best one I ever made."

Petra looked up and smiled. She tucked a strand of hair behind her ear. "That's really good, Callum."

He sat back on his heels and admired every part. The tower stood straight. The shell bridge held firm across the moat. The doorway was just the right size. He tilted his head to check it from a different angle. Still good.

He felt proud and happy. He brushed the extra sand off his hands and rested them on his knees.

Then the tide came in a little closer. The water crept forward in a wide, foamy line. It moved slowly at first, spreading thin across the flat sand. Then a bigger wave followed right behind it. The foam slid all the way up to his castle.

The water touched the base of the walls. The sand turned dark and soft. The walls sagged. Another push of water came, and the tower crumbled sideways. Wet sand spread out flat. The shell bridge lifted off the moat and floated sideways. It spun once in the foam and disappeared.

The water pulled back. Where the castle had been, there was only a smooth, wet patch of sand. A few bubbles popped on the surface. Then those were gone too.

Callum stared at that smooth, wet place. His hands hung at his sides. His chest felt tight. His eyes burned. He pulled his knees up close to his body and rested his chin on them. He wrapped his arms around his shins.

The waves kept going back and forth like nothing had happened. The sound of them felt different now. Callum pressed his lips together hard.

Petra set her book down, face open on the towel. She stood up and walked across the sand toward him. Her bare feet left soft prints. She sat down beside him in the damp sand. She didn't seem to care that her shorts would get wet.

She didn't say anything for a while. She just stayed close. Her shoulder touched his shoulder. She rested her hands in her lap and looked out at the water with him.

A gull flew by overhead, white against the blue. Its wings tilted as it turned.

"It was a really good castle," Petra said quietly.

Callum nodded. A tear rolled down his cheek and dropped onto his knee. "I worked so hard on it."

"I know," Petra said. Her voice was soft and steady.

She picked up a handful of sand and let it fall slowly through her fingers. The grains made a tiny whispering sound as they landed. She did it again, letting the sand sift down in a thin stream.

Callum watched her hand. Another tear came, and he let it fall.

Petra brushed her palms together. "Want to build another one? Together this time." She looked over at him. "We could make it higher up the beach, where the waves can't reach."

Callum wiped his face with the back of his hand. A streak of sand stayed on his cheek. He looked over his shoulder at the dry sand further from the water. It was pale and soft up there, near the dune grass. The grass swayed a little in the breeze. That spot was far from the tide line.

He sniffed and rubbed his nose. "Okay," he said.

They stood up together. Petra picked up the yellow bucket that sat near her towel. She shook the sand out of it and tucked it under her arm. Callum grabbed the little blue shovel. It had a crack in the handle, but it still worked fine.

They walked side by side across the beach. Their feet moved from the dark, wet sand onto the lighter, dry sand. It was warm under their toes up here. Little bits of broken shell dotted the ground. A dried piece of seaweed lay curled near a clump of grass.

Petra set the bucket down. Callum dropped to his knees. She knelt beside him. Their elbows almost touched.

"I'll get water for packing," Petra said. She took the bucket and walked back toward the wet sand. Callum watched her scoop up a bucketful of water. She carried it back carefully, holding it with both hands so it wouldn't slosh.

She poured a little water onto the dry sand in front of them. It turned dark and firm. Callum pressed his hands into it. It held together much better now.

He started building the tower again. This time, Petra shaped a wide wall around it. She used both hands to push the sand up and smooth it flat on top. Her wall curved in a half circle, like a hug around the tower.

Callum packed the tower higher than before. He used the back of the shovel to flatten the top. Then he smoothed the sides with his palm, turning slowly on his knees to reach all the way around.

Petra walked a few steps away and crouched down. She picked something up and came back. "Look." She held out two perfect white shells. They were small and round, each one about the size of her thumbnail.

"Windows," Callum said.

"Windows," she agreed.

He pressed them into the tower, one on each side. They stuck firmly in the damp sand. He leaned close to check them. They looked just right.

Petra found a thin piece of driftwood nearby. It was gray and smooth, no bigger than her hand. She gave it to Callum. He pressed it into the very top of the tower. It stood up straight, like a little flag.

"This one has windows," Callum said. A small smile came back to his face. It started at one corner of his mouth and spread slowly.

"And a flag," Petra added. She sat back and wiped her sandy hands on her knees.

Callum used his finger to draw a path from the doorway out through the wall. Petra pressed tiny pebbles along both sides of the path, like a walkway. She found a round pink stone and set it by the entrance.

The new castle stood sturdy on the dry sand. The walls held firm. The tower rose tall with its two white windows and its driftwood flag. The little path led out through the curved wall, lined with pebbles.

A breeze carried the smell of the ocean toward them. It ruffled the dune grass behind their backs. Somewhere past the dunes, a bird sang three clear notes. Then it sang them again.

Callum leaned against his sister's arm. He could feel the warmth of her skin. "Thanks for helping me, Petra."

She bumped her shoulder gently against his. "Always."

Down by the water, the waves kept coming and going. But up here, the sand was dry and still. The castle held its ground.

They sat together, looking at what they had made. Petra's book waited on the towel. The bucket sat nearby with a little water still in the bottom. The afternoon stretched out easy and warm around them, and neither one of them moved to go anywhere at all.
