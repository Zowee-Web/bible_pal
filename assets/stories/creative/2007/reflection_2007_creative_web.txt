There is something quietly remarkable about faithfulness that continues when no one is watching — the kind that folds a piece of paper, presses ink to the page, and sends words out into the world without knowing where they will land.

A letter written to someone you haven't seen in years carries a particular tenderness. It says, "You still matter to me. The distance between us has not erased you." It is a small act, repeated, week after week — not because anyone asked for it, but because something in the heart insists on reaching out anyway.

Sometimes love looks like grand gestures and crowded rooms. But sometimes it looks like a kitchen table, a cup of tea gone cold, and a hand that keeps writing. There is a kind of prayer in that — in remembering people by name, in holding them close through words they may read once and carry for years.

The letters we receive at unexpected moments, from people we thought had forgotten us, tend to arrive with a weight that surprises us. And the ones we send without expecting anything back — those have a quiet weight of their own.

Faithfulness, it turns out, often looks remarkably ordinary.
