Pip had never been to the other side of the meadow.

She sat at the edge where the short grass met the tall grass. Her small green shell caught the morning light. Clover blossoms dotted the ground all around her, white and soft as cotton. A creek bubbled somewhere behind her, steady and familiar. She could hear it moving over smooth stones, making its quiet song.

Pip had been sitting here for a while now. Dew still clung to the clover. A few drops sat on top of her shell, round and cool. She watched one slide slowly down the curve and drip onto the ground. A tiny ant walked past her front foot, carrying something small and pale. It moved quickly and did not stop.

The meadow stretched out wide and open. Tall golden grass swayed back and forth, back and forth. Pip could not see the berry bushes on the far side, where her grandmother waited. All she could see was grass and more grass, bending and rising with each gust. Her stomach felt tight. Her legs felt heavy. She pulled her head partway into her shell and breathed.

Inside her shell, things were quieter. She could hear her own breathing, slow and close. She could feel the hard, smooth walls curving around her. It was safe in here. But her grandmother was waiting, and the morning would not last forever.

Pip peeked out again. The meadow had not gotten any smaller.

A gentle rustling came from the birch trees nearby. White bark peeled in thin strips along the trunks. A few yellow leaves drifted down and landed without a sound. A young deer stepped out from between two of the trees, tall and brown with white spots along her back. Her hooves pressed softly into the mossy ground. Her name was Clementine, and she had soft dark eyes.

Clementine blinked slowly and looked around. She noticed Pip sitting in the clover near the edge of the meadow. She walked over with easy, unhurried steps. Each hoof landed gently, barely bending the grass.

"Good morning, little one," Clementine said. "Are you heading across?"

Pip peeked out a little more. "It's very big," she whispered.

Clementine lowered her head until her nose was close to Pip's shell. Her breath was warm and smelled like fresh leaves. "It is big," she agreed. "But we could walk together, if you like."

Pip looked at the meadow again. A grasshopper leaped from one walk quietly to another, and the walk quietly wobbled. Her heart still beat fast. But Clementine stood beside her, calm and patient, not rushing at all. One of Clementine's ears turned toward the creek, then turned back. She waited.

"Okay," Pip said quietly.

They started across. Clementine took slow, easy steps so Pip could keep up. She matched her pace to Pip's small legs, placing one hoof and then pausing before placing the next. The tall grass brushed against Pip's shell, making a soft shushing sound. It bent around her and sprang back up after she passed.

Clementine told her about the wildflowers they passed. Purple ones called bee balm grew in a cluster to their left. Their petals stuck out in tiny spikes, like little fireworks made of velvet. Yellow ones called buttercups dotted a sunny patch just ahead, their faces turned upward.

"My grandmother likes buttercups," Pip said softly.

"They are cheerful flowers," Clementine said.

Pip kept walking. The ground changed beneath her feet. Near the edge, it had been firm and dry. Now it was softer, with dark soil showing between the grass roots. She could feel it press up between her toes, cool and damp.

"My legs are short," Pip said after a while.

"That's all right," Clementine said. "We have plenty of time."

A warm breeze carried the smell of honeysuckle. It moved through the grass in a long, rolling wave, and Pip watched the golden tops lean and straighten. She noticed a ladybug resting on a blade of grass just ahead. It was bright red with two black dots. It sat perfectly still, as if it were thinking about something.

"Look," Pip said.

Clementine looked. "A little traveler," she said.

Pip smiled. She kept walking. Step by step, the meadow didn't feel quite so big anymore. She noticed how each step brought something new. A small blue flower she did not know the name of. A flat gray stone half buried in the dirt. A fuzzy caterpillar curled on a fallen leaf.

Halfway across, Pip lifted her head all the way out of her shell. Her neck stretched long, and she could see farther now. The berry bushes were coming into view, dark green and full. They sat in a low row along the far edge of the meadow, thick with leaves.

"I can see them," Pip said. Her voice was stronger.

Clementine smiled. "You're doing so well."

A dragonfly zipped past them, its wings catching light for just a moment. It circled once and flew on ahead, toward the berry bushes. Pip watched it go.

"It knows where the berries are too," Pip said.

Clementine laughed softly. It was a quiet sound, like a hum.

They kept walking together, side by side. Pip's stomach didn't feel tight anymore. The breeze ruffled the grass around them like a whisper. She could hear birds now, small ones chirping from somewhere she could not see. Their songs overlapped, quick and bright.

The grass grew shorter as they neared the far side. Pip could see the ground more clearly now. Clover appeared again, mixed with dandelion leaves and patches of soft moss. The berry bushes stood just ahead, heavy with dark fruit. Some berries had fallen and lay on the ground, staining the moss purple.

When they reached the berry bushes, Pip's grandmother was sitting in a patch of clover. She was a large turtle with a shell the color of dark wood, worn smooth with age. She waved one wrinkled leg and grinned.

"There you are, my Pip!"

Pip walked right up to her grandmother and pressed her small head against her grandmother's front leg. Her grandmother smelled like clover and warm earth.

Pip looked back across the meadow she had crossed. From here, she could see the whole thing. The golden grass, the wildflower patches, the birch trees standing white and still on the far side. It was the same meadow. But it looked different now.

Then she looked up at Clementine.

"Thank you," she said.

Clementine dipped her head gently. "Anytime, friend."

Pip's grandmother nudged a few ripe berries toward Pip with her nose. "Eat, dear. You've had quite a walk."

Pip bit into a berry. Juice ran down her chin, sweet and cool. She ate another one. Clementine lay down nearby in the soft grass, folding her long legs beneath her. She rested her chin on the ground and let out a long, easy breath.

A butterfly landed on the edge of Pip's grandmother's shell. It opened and closed its orange wings slowly. Pip's grandmother held very still and smiled.

The three of them rested together in the warm afternoon. Bees hummed low among the clover. A soft wind moved through the berry bushes, and the leaves made a sound like paper turning. Pip tucked her legs beneath her and closed her eyes halfway. The ground was warm. Her grandmother was close. Her new friend was near.

Everything was quiet, and everything was good.
