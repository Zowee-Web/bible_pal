Clover was a small turtle with a shell the color of warm honey. She lived near a mossy log at the edge of a wide meadow. Soft clover grew all around her home, which is how she got her name. Every morning, she liked to nibble on tiny leaves and watch the butterflies drift by. Her favorite spot was a flat rock where the early light made everything feel golden. She felt cozy there, tucked close to what she knew.

But today was different. Clover's grandmother lived on the other side of the meadow. Clover had promised to bring her a basket of fresh berries. The basket was small enough to carry on her back, nestled right on top of her shell. She had picked the berries yesterday, one by one, choosing only the ripest. Now she stood at the meadow's edge and looked out across the tall grass. It stretched so far that she could barely see the old oak tree on the other side. Her legs felt heavy. Her heart beat fast, like a little drum inside her chest.

"That's a long way," Clover whispered to herself. She took one step forward, then stopped. The grass swayed and rustled, and everything seemed so big. She pulled her head partway into her shell and stood very still. A ladybug landed on a blade of grass near her nose. It didn't seem worried at all. Clover wished she could feel that way too.

A gentle voice came from behind her. "Good morning. Are you heading across?"

Clover peeked out and saw a young deer with soft brown eyes and spots on her back. The deer's name was Posey. She had long, graceful legs and a calm way of standing, like she had all the time in the world.

"I want to," Clover said quietly. "But it looks so far."

Posey lowered her head so she was closer to Clover's level. "I'm going that way too. Would you like to walk together?"

Clover blinked. "You wouldn't mind going slow? I'm not very fast."

"I don't mind one bit," Posey said. She smiled in the way deer do, with her ears relaxed and her eyes soft.

So they started across. Clover took small, steady steps. Posey walked right beside her, matching her pace. The grass brushed against Clover's shell and tickled her legs. At first, her stomach still felt tight and fluttery. She kept her eyes on the ground just ahead of her feet.

"Look," Posey said gently. "There's a patch of wild daisies."

Clover glanced up. White and yellow daisies dotted the grass to their left. A bumblebee hummed from flower to flower, round and fuzzy. Clover watched it for a moment and felt her breathing slow down just a little.

They kept walking. Posey told her about the creek that ran along the far side of the meadow. She described the smooth pebbles at the bottom and how the water sounded like a quiet song. Clover listened and took another step, then another. She noticed the ground changing beneath her feet — from soft dirt to a patch of warm sand, then back to cool grass again.

"How are you doing?" Posey asked after a while.

"My legs are a little tired," Clover admitted. "But I'm okay."

"We can rest whenever you want," Posey said.

They stopped near a cluster of purple wildflowers. Clover tucked her legs underneath her and sat in the grass. Posey folded her legs and lay down nearby. A breeze carried the smell of clover and warm earth. High above, two birds called back and forth to each other in cheerful, chirping voices.

"I prayed this morning," Clover said softly. "I asked God to help me be brave enough."

Posey tilted her head. "And here you are, right in the middle of the meadow."

Clover looked around. It was true. She was farther than she had ever been before. The old oak tree on the other side looked bigger now, closer. She could see its wide branches reaching out like open arms.

"Ready?" Posey asked after a few minutes.

Clover stood up. "Ready."

They walked the rest of the way together. Clover's steps felt a little lighter now. She noticed how the grass changed color in different patches — bright green here, golden there. A grasshopper hopped past, and Clover almost laughed at its springy legs.

When they finally reached the old oak tree, Clover could see her grandmother's garden just beyond it. Red tomatoes hung heavy on their stems. Sunflowers stood tall along the fence. The little gate was open, like it was waiting for her.

"Thank you, Posey," Clover said. Her voice was steady now.

"Thank you for letting me walk with you," Posey said. "It was a lovely trip."

Clover smiled and headed through the gate. Her grandmother was sitting on the porch, shelling peas into a blue bowl. She looked up and her whole face brightened.

"Clover! You made it, sweetheart."

"I brought your berries, Grandma." Clover set the little basket down on the porch step.

Her grandmother picked up a berry and popped it in her mouth. "Perfect," she said. "Just perfect."

Clover climbed up onto the porch and sat beside her. Together they shelled peas, dropping each one into the bowl with a satisfying little plink.
