Pip had never been to the other side of the meadow.

She sat at the edge where the short grass met the tall grass. Her small green shell caught the morning light. Clover blossoms dotted the ground all around her, white and soft as cotton. A creek bubbled somewhere behind her, steady and familiar.

The meadow stretched out wide and open. Tall golden grass swayed back and forth, back and forth. Pip could not see the berry bushes on the far side, where her grandmother waited. Her stomach felt tight. Her legs felt heavy. She pulled her head partway into her shell and breathed.

A gentle rustling came from the birch trees nearby. A young deer stepped out, tall and brown with white spots along her back. Her name was Clementine, and she had soft dark eyes.

"Good morning, little one," Clementine said. "Are you heading across?"

Pip peeked out. "It's very big," she whispered.

Clementine lowered her head until her nose was close to Pip's shell. "It is big," she agreed. "But we could walk together, if you like."

Pip looked at the meadow again. Her heart still beat fast. But Clementine stood beside her, calm and patient, not rushing at all.

"Okay," Pip said quietly.

They started across. Clementine took slow, easy steps so Pip could keep up. The tall grass brushed against Pip's shell, making a soft shushing sound. Clementine told her about the wildflowers they passed — purple ones called bee balm, yellow ones called buttercups.

"My legs are short," Pip said after a while.

"That's all right," Clementine said. "We have plenty of time."

A warm breeze carried the smell of honeysuckle. Pip noticed a ladybug resting on a blade of grass. She noticed how the ground felt cool and damp beneath her feet. Step by step, the meadow didn't feel quite so big anymore.

Halfway across, Pip lifted her head all the way out of her shell. She could see farther now. The berry bushes were coming into view, dark green and full.

"I can see them," Pip said. Her voice was stronger.

Clementine smiled. "You're doing so well."

They kept walking together, side by side. Pip's stomach didn't feel tight anymore. The breeze ruffled the grass around them like a whisper.

When they reached the berry bushes, Pip's grandmother was sitting in a patch of clover. She waved one wrinkled leg and grinned.

"There you are, my Pip!"

Pip looked back across the meadow she had crossed. Then she looked up at Clementine.

"Thank you," she said.

Clementine dipped her head gently. "Anytime, friend."

Pip settled into the clover beside her grandmother. The berries were ripe and sweet. Clementine lay down nearby in the soft grass, and the three of them rested together in the warm afternoon.
