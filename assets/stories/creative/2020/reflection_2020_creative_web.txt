Gratitude is a strange companion to grief. We sometimes imagine we must choose one or the other — that to be thankful means the loss doesn't matter, or that to grieve means the good things have disappeared. But they seem to coexist more comfortably than we expect, sharing the same breath, the same bus ride, the same afternoon.

Perhaps the noticing is the thing. The world is always doing its quiet work — planting flowers in unlikely places, teaching small hands to balance, feeding the ones who get overlooked. It doesn't erase what's heavy. But it widens the view just enough to remind us that abundance and loss can stand side by side, and the heart is somehow large enough to hold them both.
