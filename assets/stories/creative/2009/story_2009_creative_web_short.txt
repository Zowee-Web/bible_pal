A golden puppy named Biscuit lived near a wide meadow. Clover and buttercups dotted the grass in every direction. Warm morning air carried the smell of honeysuckle from a nearby fence.

Biscuit had one favorite thing in the whole world. It was a stick. Not just any stick — a perfectly smooth, perfectly sized, wonderful stick. He carried it everywhere. He carried it to the pond. He carried it past the garden. He even slept with it tucked under his paw.

One Tuesday morning, a small brown dog named Pepper sat by the pond. Pepper's tail drooped low. She looked at the water and then at the ground, over and over. Biscuit trotted closer, his stick bobbing in his mouth.

"What's wrong?" Biscuit asked, setting his stick down gently.

"I've been looking for something to play with all morning," Pepper said. "But I can't find a single good stick anywhere."

Biscuit looked at his perfect stick. He loved that stick so much. But Pepper's droopy tail made his heart feel heavy. He nudged the stick toward her with his nose.

"Here," he said. "You can have mine."

Pepper's ears lifted straight up. "Really? Your favorite one?"

Biscuit nodded. "Really."

Pepper grabbed the stick and bounced in a happy circle. Then she ran to tell the other dogs in the meadow what Biscuit had done.

Biscuit sat by the pond, watching dragonflies skim across the water. He missed his stick a little. But a good feeling hummed inside his chest, like sunshine warming his fur from the inside out.

A few minutes later, he heard paws padding through the grass. Pepper came back first, carrying a bumpy oak branch. Behind her came a spotted dog named Clementine with a birch twig. Then a fluffy white dog named Dumpling arrived, dragging an enormous maple branch that was almost bigger than she was.

One by one, seven dogs from the meadow each brought Biscuit a stick. They dropped them in a pile right at his feet.

"You shared your best thing," Pepper said. "So we wanted to share with you too."

Biscuit stared at the pile. Oak and birch and maple and pine — every shape and size. His tail wagged so fast his whole body wiggled.

He grabbed the bumpy oak branch and tossed it into the air. All the dogs barked and tumbled over each other, playing together in the tall clover.

By afternoon, Biscuit lay in the soft grass with sticks scattered all around him. Pepper dozed nearby. Clementine rested her chin on Dumpling's back. A gentle breeze ruffled their fur, carrying the sweet smell of buttercups across the meadow.

Biscuit closed his eyes and let out one long, happy sigh.
