A golden puppy named Biscuit lived in a little yellow house on Clover Lane. The house had a red front door and a wide green yard. Honeysuckle grew along the fence, and its sweet smell drifted everywhere. Birds sang from the old oak tree near the mailbox. A small garden of sunflowers stood tall by the porch steps. Biscuit loved every corner of this happy place.

Each morning, Biscuit would stretch on the warm porch boards and yawn. He could hear Mrs. Penny humming in the kitchen while she made toast. The smell of butter and jam floated through the screen door. Biscuit's tail would thump against the wood, keeping time with the song. Squirrels chattered from the oak tree, and he would tilt his head to listen. Everything about mornings on Clover Lane felt like a gift.

Now, Biscuit had one very special treasure. It was a stick — not just any stick, but the best stick in the whole neighborhood. He had found it under the oak tree after a spring rain. The stick was smooth and light brown, curved just right for carrying. It fit perfectly in his mouth, and he could toss it in the air and catch it again. Biscuit carried that stick everywhere he went.

He brought it to the yard when he played. He brought it inside when he napped. He even tucked it beside his water bowl during meals. Mrs. Penny would laugh and say, "Biscuit, you sure do love that stick." And Biscuit would wag his tail so hard his whole body wiggled. That stick was his favorite thing in the world, and he held it close every single day.

One bright Tuesday, Biscuit trotted down the sidewalk with his stick. He passed the blue house where a spotted dog named Clementine lived. Clementine sat by her gate, looking at the ground with droopy ears. Her tail was still, and she let out a soft little sigh. Biscuit stopped and tilted his head sideways. He set his stick down on the sidewalk and pushed his nose through the fence to say hello.

Clementine looked up and sniffed Biscuit's nose gently. "I had a nice stick," she said in her quiet dog way, with a whimper and a glance toward the yard. Her old stick had crumbled apart that morning into tiny dry pieces. She nudged the pile of crumbs with her paw. Biscuit looked at his own beautiful stick sitting on the sidewalk. He picked it up, pushed it under the gate, and dropped it right at Clementine's feet.

Clementine's ears lifted. Her tail began to swing back and forth like a little metronome. She picked up the stick and pranced in a circle, her paws tapping on the walkway. Biscuit watched her through the fence, and his own tail wagged too. Giving away his favorite thing made his chest feel warm and full. He trotted home without a stick, but his paws felt lighter than ever.

The next morning, Biscuit went outside and found something on the porch. A short, bumpy stick sat right by the welcome mat. It smelled like pine and felt rough under his paws. A little tag made of paper was tied to it with yarn. Mrs. Penny read it out loud: "For Biscuit, from Clementine." Biscuit sniffed it all over and carried it to his favorite spot under the oak tree.

Later that afternoon, Biscuit walked past the park on Maple Street. A small white dog named Juniper was sitting by the bench, looking at two puppies playing fetch nearby. Juniper didn't have anything to play with. Biscuit trotted over and set the pine stick down in front of Juniper. Juniper's eyes went wide, and she grabbed the stick with a happy bark. Biscuit's tail made big circles in the air as he watched her run off to join the game.

When Biscuit got home that evening, something wonderful waited on the porch. Three sticks were piled neatly by the door. One was pale and smooth like driftwood from the creek. Another was dark and twisted with a knobby end, perfect for chewing. The third was long and thin, just right for tossing high. A note tucked underneath said, "Thank you, Biscuit — from your friends at the park." Mrs. Penny scratched behind his ears and smiled.

The very next day, Biscuit took the driftwood stick to the schoolyard fence. He saw a puppy named Theo who had just moved to Clover Lane. Theo stood near the fence, watching other dogs play on the grass. His tail hung low and his nose stayed close to the ground. Biscuit wiggled under the lowest rail and dropped the pale stick right in front of Theo. Theo sniffed it, then looked up at Biscuit with bright, surprised eyes. They spent the whole afternoon rolling in the clover together, taking turns carrying the stick.

By the end of the week, something amazing had happened in Biscuit's yard. Sticks of every shape and size appeared on the porch, by the gate, and under the oak tree. Thick ones, thin ones, curly ones, and straight ones formed a big happy pile. Dogs from all over the neighborhood had brought them — Clementine, Juniper, Theo, and dogs Biscuit had never even met. Each stick carried a different smell — cedar, maple, birch, and pine. Mrs. Penny counted fourteen sticks in all and laughed until her cheeks turned pink. Biscuit rolled onto his back right in the middle of the pile, paws in the air.

That evening, Biscuit lay on the porch with sticks scattered all around him. Fireflies blinked in the yard like tiny floating lanterns. Mrs. Penny sat in her rocking chair, sipping lemonade that clinked with ice. A cool breeze carried the honeysuckle smell across the porch. Biscuit rested his chin on a smooth maple stick and let out a long, happy sigh. His tail gave one last slow wag before his eyes drifted shut, and the sounds of crickets and the creak of the rocking chair sang him softly to sleep.
