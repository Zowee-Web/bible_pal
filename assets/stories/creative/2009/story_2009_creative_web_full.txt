Bramble was a small brown puppy with floppy ears and a white spot on his nose. He lived in a cozy cottage at the edge of a meadow with his family. Every morning, warm oatmeal smells drifted from the kitchen window. Bramble would stretch on his soft blue blanket and yawn a big puppy yawn. Then he would trot outside to feel the cool grass under his paws. The meadow spread out wide and green, dotted with buttercups and clover.

Bramble had one favorite thing in the whole wide world. It was a stick. Not just any stick, but a perfect one. It was smooth and light brown, about as long as his tail. He had found it under the old apple tree on a sunny afternoon. He carried it everywhere — to the pond, through the garden, even to his water bowl. Sometimes he slept with it tucked right next to his blanket.

One Tuesday morning, Bramble trotted down the path toward the village pond. He held his favorite stick proudly in his mouth. At the pond, he found his friend Clover, a fluffy gray rabbit, sitting by the water. Clover was trying to build a tiny raft from leaves and twigs. But the twigs kept breaking apart. Clover's nose twitched with frustration. "I can't get it to stay together," Clover said softly.

Bramble set his stick down and watched. Clover tried again, pressing two small twigs together. They snapped right away. Bramble looked at his favorite stick. It was strong and smooth. It would make a perfect base for a little raft. His tail drooped just a tiny bit. He really loved that stick. But Clover looked so hopeful, trying again and again.

Bramble picked up his stick and gently set it in front of Clover. "Here," he said. "You can use this one." Clover's eyes went wide and round. "But Bramble, that's your favorite stick in the whole meadow!" Bramble wagged his tail, even though it felt a little strange to let go. "That's okay," he said. "I want your raft to float." Clover smiled a big rabbit smile and tucked the stick into the center of the leaf raft. It held everything together perfectly. The little raft bobbed on the pond, steady and proud.

Word about Bramble's gift spread quickly through the meadow. Nell the hedgehog heard about it while gathering berries near the fence. Pip the bluebird heard about it while splashing in the birdbath. Old Fern the tortoise heard about it while sunning herself on a warm rock. They all knew how much Bramble loved that stick.

That afternoon, Bramble was resting under the apple tree. He felt happy about helping Clover, but his paws kept reaching for the empty spot where his stick usually lay. A soft rustling sound came from the path. Nell appeared first, carrying a beautiful knotted stick with swirly patterns in the bark. "I found this by the blackberry hedge," Nell said. "It made me think of you."

Before Bramble could even sniff it, Pip swooped down with a slim birch twig, pale and smooth. "This one is light enough to carry anywhere," Pip chirped. Then Old Fern came slowly up the hill, rolling a thick oak branch with her sturdy foot. "This one is good for chewing," Fern said with a wink.

But they were not the last. Clover came hopping back with two willow switches tied together with a piece of grass. A family of field mice brought a tiny bundle of cedar twigs that smelled wonderful. Even Gus the barn cat, who usually kept to himself, dropped off a crooked maple branch and nodded quietly before padding away.

By the time the afternoon light turned golden, a whole pile of sticks sat under the apple tree. Bramble could hardly believe his eyes. There were long ones and short ones. Thick ones and thin ones. Sticks that smelled like pine and sticks that smelled like rain. More sticks than he could ever carry at once.

Bramble rolled onto his back right in the middle of the pile. Sticks poked out in every direction around him. His tail wagged so fast it made the leaves flutter. All his friends gathered close, laughing and talking. Nell nibbled a berry. Pip sang a bright little song from the lowest branch. Clover sat right next to Bramble and leaned against his warm side.

"You gave away your favorite thing," Clover said quietly.

Bramble licked Clover's ear. "And look what came back," he said.

The evening air smelled like apples and fresh wood. Bramble picked up the knotted stick Nell had brought and carried it in his mouth, trotting in a happy circle around his friends. One by one, they all walked together back toward the cottage as fireflies began to blink across the meadow. Bramble's blue blanket was waiting inside, and tonight, a whole pile of wonderful sticks waited just outside the door.
