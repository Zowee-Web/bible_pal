Mila had one slice left.

The pizza box sat open on the park bench, and the last piece of pepperoni glistened in the afternoon sun. She could smell the cheese. Her stomach growled.

A boy stood near the water fountain. He was smaller than Mila, maybe five or six. His shoes were too big for his feet. He kept looking at the pizza box, then looking away, then looking back.

Mila picked up the slice. It was warm in her hand.

She walked over to the boy. "You want this?"

He stared at her. "That's your last one."

"I know."

He took it with both hands. He bit into it and closed his eyes, and a string of cheese stretched from his mouth to the crust. He laughed. Mila laughed too.

"I'm Joaquin," he said with his mouth full.

"I'm Mila."

Joaquin sat down on the curb and Mila sat beside him. He told her about his dog named Biscuit who could catch grapes in the air. She told him about the treehouse her dad was building in the backyard. He said he'd never been in a treehouse.

"You could come see it," Mila said.

"Really?"

"When it's done. My dad's putting in a rope ladder."

Joaquin grinned. Cheese was still on his chin.

They sat there while the shadows of the oak trees grew longer across the grass. Mila's stomach was empty, but something else was full. She couldn't name it. It sat warm behind her ribs like a second lunch.

Her mom called from the parking lot. Mila stood up and brushed off her shorts.

"Saturday," Joaquin said. "For the treehouse."

"Saturday," Mila said.

She walked across the park. The empty pizza box flapped once in the breeze. Behind her, Joaquin was already telling his mother about the rope ladder, his hands moving wide in the golden air.
