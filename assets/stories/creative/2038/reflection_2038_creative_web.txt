Joy is unpredictable. That may be the most important thing about it. You cannot schedule joy, or earn it, or arrive at it by following directions. It shows up when and where it pleases, and it seems to prefer the places nobody expected — a paint aisle, a Tuesday afternoon, two strangers who had no reason to meet and every reason to keep walking.

Paloma went to the store for washers. Vernon went for paint. Neither of them went looking for a moment that would crack them open with laughter. But the music was playing, and their bodies remembered something their minds had filed away — that movement is its own kind of prayer, that dancing with a stranger is its own kind of communion, that sometimes the most sacred thing you can do on a Tuesday is stop being serious for forty-five seconds.

There is a thread in scripture — woven quietly, often overlooked — about joy that arrives uninvited. Unexpected harvests. Water from unlikely rocks. A celebration thrown not because everything is perfect but because something good happened and it would be wrong not to notice.

Vernon didn't plan to dance in the hardware store. His feet just moved. And when they did, Paloma's feet answered, because joy is like that — it's contagious in a way that bypasses permission. You see someone lit up, and something in you lights up too, not because you decided to, but because that's how the wiring works.

You may be standing in your own version of aisle seven, sorting through small necessary things, your shoes muddy, your list in your pocket. Keep listening. The music might already be playing.
