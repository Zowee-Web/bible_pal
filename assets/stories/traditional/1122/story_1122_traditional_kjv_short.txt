Eight days had passed since the day the doors had been shut. The disciples were gathered again in the same upper room. Thomas was with them this time. He sat among the others. He had not seen the Lord. He had said so plainly. "Except I shall see in his hands the print of the nails, and put my hand into his side, I will not believe."

The lamps burned along the wall. The table held bread and a pitcher of clay. The doors were shut. Without, the evening had turned the rooftops unto a dim grey.

Then, without warning, Jesus stood in the midst of them.

He had not opened the door. No footsteps had come up the stairs. He was simply there. Alive. Breathing. Beholding the room.

"Peace be unto you," he said.

Thomas was as one frozen. The cup he had been holding tilted in his hand and he set it down without looking. The other disciples were already on their feet. Thomas only sat there, his eyes upon the man standing in the room.

Jesus looked upon him.

He came nigh. He lifted not up his voice. He rebuked not. He held forth his hands that Thomas might behold them, and the wounds therein were plain.

"Reach hither thy finger," Jesus said, "and behold my hands. Reach hither thy hand, and thrust it into my side. Be not faithless, but believing."

Thomas had no need to touch him. He saw enough.

Something within him broke open. Eight days of striving to hold himself steady. Eight days of saying he would not, could not, would never believe. And here he was, looking into the face of the one with whom he had walked three years, returned, alive, and standing near.

He fell forward upon his knees.

"My Lord and my God," he said.

Jesus looked down upon him with a quiet smile. His voice was kind.

"Thomas, because thou hast seen me, thou hast believed," he said. "Blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed."

The room held its breath. The lamps kept burning. And Thomas kneeled there, his face wet, in the presence of the risen Lord.
