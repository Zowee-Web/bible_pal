Eight days had passed since the day the doors had been locked. The disciples had gathered again in the same upper room. Thomas was with them this time. He sat among the others. He had not seen the Lord. He had said so plainly. "Unless I see the print of the nails, and put my hand into his side, I will not believe."

The lamps burned along the wall. The table held bread and a clay pitcher. The doors were shut. Outside, the evening had turned the rooftops to dim gray.

Then, without warning, Jesus stood in the middle of them.

He had not opened the door. No footsteps had come up the stairs. He was simply there. Alive. Breathing. Looking around the room.

[warmly] "Peace be to you," he said.

Thomas froze. The cup he had been holding tilted in his hand and he set it down without looking. The other disciples were already on their feet. Thomas only sat there, his eyes on the man standing in the room.

Jesus looked at him.

He came closer. He did not raise his voice. He did not rebuke. He held out his hands so that Thomas could see them, and the wounds in them were plain.

[gently] "Reach here your finger," Jesus said, "and see my hands. Reach here your hand, and put it into my side. Don't be unbelieving, but believing."

Thomas did not need to touch him. He saw enough.

Something inside him broke open. Eight days of trying to hold himself steady. Eight days of saying he would not, could not, would never believe. And here he was, looking into the face of the one he had walked with for three years, returned, alive, and standing close.

He fell forward onto his knees.

[softly] "My Lord and my God," he said.

Jesus looked down at him with a quiet smile. His voice was kind.

"Because you have seen me, you have believed," he said. "Blessed are those who have not seen, and have believed."

The room held its breath. The lamps kept burning. And Thomas knelt there, his face wet, in the presence of the risen Lord.
