The morning sun arose over the hills, casting golden light across the land. People from every village drew near unto the house of the Lord, their voices rising like a joyful river. Men, women, and children walked along the dusty way, clapping their hands and singing aloud, for the day was bright with gladness.

At the gates, they paused as one. The doors loomed high, set wide open unto all. Some lifted up their faces, smiling, as if meeting an old friend. They entered the courts together, laughter echoing, song filling the air:

"Make a joyful noise unto the Lord, all ye lands.
Serve the Lord with gladness.
Come before his presence with singing."

Children spun in circles, their laughter blending with the songs. Grandmothers hummed softly and swayed from side to side. Shepherds, who yet bare the savour of grass and wool, lifted up their voices, their gratitude plain upon sun-browned faces.

Within, the people greeted one another with kindness. From the highest unto the least, all came to give thanks. They brought baskets of fruit and loaves yet warm from the fire, setting them down with gentle hands.

A father lifted his son upon his shoulders. The little one beheld about him in wonder as his father spake, steady and sure:

"Know ye that the Lord he is God.
It is he that hath made us, and we are his.
We are his people, and the sheep of his pasture."

The people pressed forward, filling the courtyard with their warmth. They bowed their heads in thanks, voices weaving together:

"Enter into his gates with thanksgiving,
And into his courts with praise.
Be thankful unto him, and bless his name."

A cool breeze stirred the air. Sunlight flickered upon the old stones. The gathered throng recalled all that had come before — trials and blessings, hunger and bounty. Through it all, God had been faithful.

One elder, leaning upon his staff, spake for all:

"For the Lord is good.
His mercy is everlasting,
And his truth endureth to all generations."

With those words, the people lifted up their praise higher yet. The sound rolled through the temple courts and spilled out into the streets, ringing across fields and rooftops, until it seemed all creation echoed the song.

The morning moved gently toward noon. The people, filled with thanks and joy, tarried in the courts. Their hearts rested, certain in the goodness of the Lord — whose love never faded, whose promise belonged unto children yet unborn.
