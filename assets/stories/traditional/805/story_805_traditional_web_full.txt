Make a joyful noise to the Lord, all the earth!  
Morning sunlight pours over the grasslands and climbs the slopes of every hill. In distant villages, farmers pause their labor to raise their voices, calling out together: “Yahweh is good!” By the glimmering rivers and crowded city streets, children shout and clap in praise.  
Even the birds in the trees and flocks in the pastures seem to join in—filling the air with cheerful sounds that rise up to God.

Worship the Lord with gladness.  
Men and women gather, wide-eyed, from every land. Some come to the temple gates, bearing baskets of grain or oil, their steps quickened by the hope in their hearts. Others stand outside, hands lifted, faces turned upward. Their laughter and singing mingle, working alongside the tinkling of a musician’s lyre. There is no shadow of burden or weight; all who come are wrapped in the warmth of joy.

Come before his presence with singing.  
Fathers teach their sons old songs in clear morning light; mothers hush their babies, humming gentle tunes of praise. Friends grip each other’s arms as they enter the courts—one begins a melody, another harmonizes, all carried together on a river of song. Their voices fill the holy place, echoing off smooth stones and timber beams, until it seems the whole house breathes with music.

Know that Yahweh, he is God.  
Stories are told among the people: of oceans held in place by his hand, mountains raised by the working of his will, rains given in season, and harvest gathered in by his command. Elders remind the young—quiet confidence settling in their faces—“He, and no other, made the seas. He stretched out the heavens like cloth. That same God listens to our songs.”

It is he who has made us, and we are his.  
Some stand in the shade, remembering: “I was a child once, lifted up by his mercy. He formed my lungs, painted the features of my face.” Others, with faces lined by years, say softly, “He carried us through famine and feast. When I was lost, he called my name.” Every memory drawn close to the heart is a reminder of the hands that shaped and cared for them since the beginning.

We are his people, and the sheep of his pasture.  
In the warmth of late afternoon, shepherds sit on rocky outcrops, watching their flocks graze on green hillsides. The sheep wander beneath the open sky, bound to a gentle master with a steady rod and staff. Just as their shepherd guards them from all harm, so the people know they are watched and cared for—guided to cool waters and safe places for rest. Uncertainty fades; every heart knows, “He is our shepherd, and we belong to him.”

Enter into his gates with thanksgiving and into his courts with praise.  
The city gates stand open. Pilgrims—old and young—stream in, clutching baskets, dried fruit, handfuls of barley, and sometimes just a song or a grateful tear. Each step draws them closer to the courts where the Lord’s name is praised. Voices lift: “Thank you! Thank you for rain! For peace! For waking us each day!” Their words are honest—sometimes loud, sometimes whispered—but always brimming with thanks.

Give thanks to him, and bless his name.  
In quiet corners, a grandmother lays a hand on her grandson’s head, blessing the Lord for his laughter. At a market doorway, a merchant bows his head, whispering thanks for another day’s bread. Together, they bless the name that never fails them—the name that is spoken in the fields, at weddings, at home as the lamps are lit.

For Yahweh is good.  
His goodness is tasted in ripe figs, felt in strong arms that lift burdens, seen in the rain that falls softly on the soil. There is no bitterness in him—every act is kindness, every judgment is wise. The people know, “When we stray, he calls us back. When we are hungry, he feeds us. When we are frightened, his words are a balm.”

His loving kindness endures forever.  
From the first footsteps in the sand to the last star of twilight, God’s love never runs out. Fathers recall his patience when the people wandered. Mothers tell children how the Lord healed their hurts and delivered them from fear. Stories sung at harvest and told by the fireside all end the same: “His mercy is new each morning. It never fades. It never fails.”

His faithfulness endures to all generations.  
Elders sit among olive trees, remembering long journeys and ancient covenants. “He was faithful to our fathers, and to their sons. He is faithful to us, and will be with those yet unborn.” Young ones gather at their knees, wide-eyed with trust. The song goes on, a golden thread passed from one voice to another—never broken, never forgotten.

So, all the earth sings together. Hands and voices rise, hearts alight with joy and gratitude. Their song, bright as dawn, drifts upward like an offering:  
"Thank you, Lord! You are good, and your love lasts forever!"
