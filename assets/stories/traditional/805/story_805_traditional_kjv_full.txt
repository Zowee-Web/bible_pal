Make a joyful noise unto the Lord, all the earth.
Morning sunlight poureth over the grasslands and climbeth the slopes of every hill. In distant villages, husbandmen pause from their labour to lift up their voices, calling out together, "The Lord is good!" By the glimmering rivers and crowded city streets, children shout and clap in praise.
Even the birds in the trees and the flocks in the pastures seem to join in — filling the air with cheerful sounds that rise up unto God.

Serve the Lord with gladness.
Men and women gather, wide-eyed, out of every land. Some come unto the temple gates, bearing baskets of grain or oil, their steps quickened by the hope within their hearts. Others stand without, hands lifted, faces turned upward. Their laughter and singing mingle, working alongside the tinkling of a musician's lyre. There is no shadow of burden nor weight; all that come are wrapped in the warmth of joy.

Come before his presence with singing.
Fathers teach their sons old songs in the clear morning light; mothers hush their babes, humming gentle tunes of praise. Friends grip one another's arms as they enter the courts — one beginneth a melody, another harmonizeth, all borne together upon a river of song. Their voices fill the holy place, echoing off smooth stones and timber beams, until it seemeth the whole house breatheth with music.

Know ye that the Lord he is God.
Stories are told among the people: of the seas held in their place by his hand, of mountains raised by the working of his will, of rains given in their season, and harvest gathered in by his commandment. Elders remind the young — quiet confidence settling in their faces — "He, and none other, made the seas. He stretched out the heavens like a curtain. That same God hearkeneth unto our songs."

It is he that hath made us, and not we ourselves.
Some stand in the shade, remembering: "I was a child once, lifted up by his mercy. He formed my lungs, and painted the features of my face." Others, with faces lined by the years, say softly, "He carried us through famine and feast. When I was lost, he called my name." Every memory drawn close unto the heart is a reminder of the hands that shaped and cared for them from the beginning.

We are his people, and the sheep of his pasture.
In the warmth of late afternoon, shepherds sit upon rocky outcrops, watching their flocks graze upon green hillsides. The sheep wander beneath the open sky, bound unto a gentle master with a steady rod and staff. Even as their shepherd guardeth them from all harm, so the people know they are watched and cared for — guided unto cool waters and safe places of rest. Uncertainty fadeth; every heart knoweth, "He is our shepherd, and we belong unto him."

Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise.
The city gates stand open. Pilgrims — old and young — stream in, clutching baskets, dried fruit, handfuls of barley, and sometimes only a song or a thankful tear. Each step draweth them closer unto the courts wherein the Lord's name is praised. Voices lift: "We thank thee! We thank thee for rain! For peace! For waking us each day!" Their words are honest — sometimes loud, sometimes whispered — yet always brimming with thanks.

Be thankful unto him, and bless his name.
In quiet corners, a grandmother layeth her hand upon her grandson's head, blessing the Lord for his laughter. At a market doorway, a merchant boweth his head, whispering thanks for another day's bread. Together, they bless the name that never faileth them — the name that is spoken in the fields, at weddings, and at home as the lamps are lit.

For the Lord is good.
His goodness is tasted in ripe figs, felt in strong arms that lift burdens, and seen in the rain that falleth softly upon the soil. There is no bitterness in him — every act is kindness, every judgment is wise. The people know, "When we stray, he calleth us back. When we are hungry, he feedeth us. When we are afraid, his words are a balm."

His mercy is everlasting.
From the first footsteps in the sand unto the last star of twilight, God's love never faileth. Fathers recall his patience when the people wandered. Mothers tell their children how the Lord healed their hurts and delivered them from fear. Stories sung at harvest and told beside the fireside all end the same: "His mercy is new every morning. It never fadeth. It never faileth."

His truth endureth to all generations.
Elders sit among the olive trees, remembering long journeys and ancient covenants. "He was faithful unto our fathers, and unto their sons. He is faithful unto us, and will be with those yet unborn." Young ones gather at their knees, wide-eyed with trust. The song goeth on, a golden thread passed from one voice unto another — never broken, never forgotten.

So all the earth singeth together. Hands and voices rise, hearts alight with joy and gratitude. Their song, bright as the dawn, drifteth upward like an offering:
"We thank thee, Lord! Thou art good, and thy mercy endureth for ever!"
