Across the breadth of the earth, the morning light spread gently. Villages — of clay and stone — rested beneath the pale pink of dawn. Fields waved with barley and wheat, the dew glistening like a thousand tiny mirrors. In distant hills, shepherds were awaking beside their flocks, and smoke curled from chimney holes in the homes below.

As the sky brightened and the sun warmed the earth, a call travelled upon the breeze, rising above the chatter of birds and the soft clatter of daily chores. The people were gathering. They came out of the valleys, out of the fold of distant mountains, out of small houses beside the way, and out of great walled towns. Men, women, and children, some old and slow, some young and eager, all walked unto a place of worship, their steps light upon the dust.

Some clapped their hands, some lifted up their voices in laughter, and some bare small children upon their shoulders, while others leaned upon staves. Their faces were filled with gladness, as if their spirits welled up with joy that could not be contained. Their smiles caught the light. Their hearts beat with a single purpose: to bring glad sounds and songs unto the Lord.

The sound grew and swelled, like rushing water in a riverbed after rain. It was cheerful. Some tapped out rhythms upon old drums, the hollow beats rising as steady as the march of their feet. Songs floated up, lifted by strong and timid voices alike. "Make a joyful noise unto the Lord, all ye lands!" called a child as she skipped beside her father. The note was taken up and echoed by others. Soon, the throng was a living psalm: joyful, strong, united.

The farmland fell behind as the way led closer unto the gates. The great doors of the temple stood tall, polished by endless seasons. Stone pillars glowed in the early sun. Carved wooden doors waited, thrown open wide to receive the coming throng.

The people drew nigh, and the rhythm of their walk deepened. No face was troubled. Each step was sure and light; they came not as strangers, but as guests welcomed unto a home. Together, they entered the wide courts. The flagstones echoed beneath their sandaled feet.

Within the gates, laughter mingled with melody. The courtyard was alive with colour: woven robes, bright shawls, tanned skin gleaming from field-work and sunlight. The priests awaited, their garments white and fine. Incense drifted faint and sweet upon the air, incense offered unto the One that had made them, who was the cause of their coming.

Children tugged upon sleeves, pointing at doves perched upon the lintels or at the carved lilies blooming across the walls. Old men leaned together and spake, their voices soft, their eyes bright as the dawn shining overhead.

"Serve the Lord with gladness! Come before his presence with singing!" called out a chorus of clear voices. The people answered with their songs. Some voices rang out, bright and sure; some were soft, scarce louder than a breath, yet all joined in the melody of gladness that filled the temple's open courts.

No note of anger or bitterness was heard. The gathering was peaceful, each one open-faced, turned toward the holy, ready to honour the Lord even as his people had done for generations.

Their voices arose, shaping words known unto them all. "Know ye that the Lord he is God." The words drifted from mouth to mouth, lips to ears, echoing softly behind the thick pillars and among the pigeons resting beneath the eaves.

The very air beneath the high roof stirred with these words. All understood them. All stood in agreement. The women adjusting their headscarves. The boy cradling a lamb at his knees. The merchant with hands rough from labour. All knew the same thing: the Lord was not merely a name or a tale, but the one true God.

"It is he that hath made us, and not we ourselves." The truth of it stood strong as stone walls or the dawn's first light. The people came not by their own power, neither did they belong unto themselves. Their shapes, their lives, the breath within their lungs — all things were given.

"We are his people, and the sheep of his pasture." For a moment, all thoughts of hunger and toil fell away. The fields without, the flocks grazing along the hillsides, were not merely daily chores. They were images of belonging. Every man, every woman, every child — cared for, guided, watched over by the hand of their Lord. Joyful gratitude grew. It filled their eyes with light, and their lips with song.

"Enter into his gates with thanksgiving," said the priests, their voices woven with the melody of the throng. The people obeyed, some with hands raised, others with heads bowed. Their hearts were wide open, poured out in thanks. For rain, for bread, for the kindness of family and the peace of the day, for the care that had brought them from birth unto this very morning.

"And into his courts with praise!" the people cried in return, their words carrying high into the rafters. Songs arose again, some high and keen, some low and steady, blending into one cloud of praise. Strings sounded as a few played harps and lyres, the plucked notes drifting like sunshine.

Children clapped time. Elders swayed and nodded, their hands shaking in the rhythm of the throng. The sound was as rich and sweet as fresh honeycomb, as warm as the sun that streamed through the open arches.

"Be thankful unto him! Bless his name!" boomed a voice from the midst of the throng, and the echo was repeated all about the court. The people remembered the times they had been hungry and had found food, afraid and had found shelter, lonely and had found companionship. They blessed God's name, their voices trembling with gladness.

No present trouble dulled their thanks. Fear and worry faded at the gate, left in the soft dust without the walls. Here, within these courts, only gratitude and joy remained.

"For the Lord is good!" sang the people, declaring that which strangers and visitors alike could behold upon their faces. The smallest kindness, the greatest mercy, all came from the same well: the goodness of the Lord. None doubted.

"His mercy is everlasting!" The words thundered and echoed. They rang in every corner, rose over every head, pressed gently into every ear. The youngest child and the oldest woman alike heard and knew it was true. Each remembered moments when mercy had reached them — a cool drink upon a hot day, a gentle hand in trouble, forgiveness where anger had been expected.

"His truth endureth to all generations," they sang, and the words became a bridge from old unto young, from memory unto hope. Parents stood with their children, and grandchildren clung unto the hands of elders. All understood that the care of the Lord would not cease with them. It would cross years, reaching into unknown days, stretching long after their own voices had grown silent.

So the people stood, their hearts lifted, their faces warm and open, a people united in joy. Together, they filled the courts with glad sounds and sweet singing, filling their mouths with praise unto the God that had made them, tended them, claimed them as his own flock, and called them, again and again, to enter his presence with thanksgiving.

And as the sun climbed higher, flooding every stone and flag and smiling face with bright, honest light, their song died not away. It carried into the streets, drifted over the distant fields, and scattered into sky and wind. Their joy belonged not only unto this place or this moment, but unto every land and every day. For the Lord was good, his mercy without end, his truth enduring as long as hearts would lift up their songs in his name.
