The man Nabal was great and rich. He had three thousand sheep and a thousand goats. He was shearing his sheep in Carmel. The man himself was churlish and evil in his doings.

David in the wilderness had heard of the shearing. He had sent ten young men to Nabal with a courteous greeting and a request for provisions. David's men had been with Nabal's shepherds for many days. They had guarded them. They had taken nothing of theirs. The young men had asked Nabal for whatever came to his hand.

Nabal had answered them roughly. He had said: who is David? who is the son of Jesse? There are many servants now that break away every man from his master. Shall I take my bread and my water and my flesh that I have killed for my shearers and give it to men whom I know not whence they be? He had sent the young men back empty.

David had heard the answer. He had girded on his sword. He had said: surely in vain have I kept all that this fellow has in the wilderness. He had said: so shall God do unto the enemies of David, and more also, if I leave of all that pertain to him by morning so much as one. He had set out with four hundred men.

A young man of Nabal's household told Abigail. He told her what David's men had been to them in the field. They had been a wall about them by night and by day. He told her what David had said about coming. He told her quickly. She did not tell Nabal.

She made haste. She took two hundred loaves, two bottles of wine, five sheep ready dressed, five measures of parched corn, a hundred clusters of raisins, and two hundred cakes of figs. She laid them on donkeys. She sent the servants on before. She followed on her own donkey. She did not stop on the way.

She came down by the covert of the hill. David and his men came down against her. When Abigail saw David, she hasted and lighted off the donkey. She fell before David on her face. She bowed herself to the ground at his feet.

She spoke for a long while. She took the iniquity on herself. She asked David to disregard Nabal. She said Nabal's name meant folly. She gave him the gifts. She said the LORD would make him a sure house because he fought the LORD's battles. She named the thing she had come for. She said: when the LORD has dealt well with my lord, let this be no grief unto you, that you have shed blood causeless. She asked him to remember her then.

David heard her. He blessed her. He blessed her advice. He said: blessed is the LORD God of Israel that sent you this day to meet me. Blessed are you, who have kept me this day from coming to shed blood and from avenging myself with my own hand.

He received what she had brought. He said: go up in peace to your house. See, I have hearkened to your voice and have accepted your person.
