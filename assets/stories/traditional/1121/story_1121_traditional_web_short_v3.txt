The house of Yahweh at Shiloh stood quiet in the late hour. Hannah had eaten little at the family meal. The lamps burned low in their sockets, and the air smelled of oil and old stone. She rose from the table where her family had gathered and walked alone into the temple courts.

Eli the priest sat on his seat by the doorpost. He watched her come in.

Hannah went past him and knelt down. Her hands were folded in her lap. For a long while she said nothing.

Then the weeping began. It was a quiet weeping — no wail, no cry. Only her shoulders moved. The tears ran down without sound. Year after year of barrenness had come to rest in her at last, here in this still place.

She made a vow. She said the words inside herself, where no one could hear them. [whispers] If you will look on your servant's affliction, and remember me, and not forget me, but give your servant a son — I will give him to Yahweh all the days of his life.

Her lips moved. Her voice did not.

Eli was watching. He saw the lips move, the silence, the swaying of her body, and he drew the wrong conclusion. He stood and came toward her.

"How long will you be drunk?" he said. "Put away your wine."

Hannah looked up. Her face was wet.

[gently] "No, my lord," she said. "I am a woman of a sorrowful spirit. I have not been drinking wine or strong drink. I have been pouring out my soul before Yahweh. Don't count your servant for a wicked woman, for I have been speaking out of the abundance of my complaint and my provocation."

The old priest stood still. Something in her words turned him. He had been wrong. He saw it now.

[warmly] "Go in peace," he said. "And may the God of Israel grant your petition that you have asked of him."

Hannah rose. She wiped her face with the edge of her shawl. She went back to her family, and she ate, and her face was no longer sad.

In time, Yahweh remembered her. A son was given. [softly] She named him Samuel, saying, "Because I have asked him of Yahweh."
