The house of the Lord at Shiloh stood quiet in the late hour. Hannah had eaten little at the family meal. The lamps burned low in their sockets, and the air bore the savour of oil and old stone. She arose from the table where her family had gathered and walked alone into the temple courts.

Eli the priest sat upon his seat by the doorpost. He beheld her come in.

Hannah went past him and kneeled down. Her hands were folded in her lap. For a long while she said nothing.

Then the weeping began. It was a quiet weeping — no wail, no cry. Only her shoulders moved. The tears ran down without sound. Year after year of barrenness had come to rest in her at last, here in this still place.

She made a vow. She spake the words within herself, where no one could hear them. If thou wilt look upon thine handmaid's affliction, and remember me, and not forget me, but give unto thine handmaid a son — I will give him unto the Lord all the days of his life.

Her lips moved. Her voice did not.

Eli was watching. He saw the lips move, the silence, the swaying of her body, and he drew the wrong conclusion. He arose and came toward her.

"How long wilt thou be drunken?" he said. "Put away thy wine from thee."

Hannah lifted up her eyes. Her face was wet.

"No, my lord," she said. "I am a woman of a sorrowful spirit. I have drunk neither wine nor strong drink. I have poured out my soul before the Lord. Count not thine handmaid for a daughter of Belial, for out of the abundance of my complaint and grief have I spoken hitherto."

The old priest stood still. Something in her words turned him. He had been wrong. He saw it now.

"Go in peace," he said. "And the God of Israel grant thee thy petition that thou hast asked of him."

Hannah arose. She wiped her face with the edge of her shawl. She went back unto her family, and she did eat, and her countenance was no more sad.

In time, the Lord remembered her. A son was given. She called his name Samuel, saying, "Because I have asked him of the Lord."
