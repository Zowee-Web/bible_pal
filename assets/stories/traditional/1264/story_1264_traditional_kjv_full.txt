In the third year of Cyrus king of Persia, Daniel was an old man, and a thing was revealed unto him, whose name was called Belteshazzar. The thing was true. It was a great warfare. He understood it, and he could not eat.

In those days he was mourning three full weeks. He ate no pleasant bread, neither came flesh nor wine in his mouth. He did not anoint his head. He did not soften his beard with oil. The weeks went by, and his clothing hung looser on him, and his servants set food before him and took it away again untouched. In the four and twentieth day of the first month, he rose from where he had been mourning and went forth unto the bank of the great river, which is Hiddekel.

A handful of men walked with him. The river was wide and brown. The reeds along its edge stirred in the early light.

He lifted up his eyes.

Behold, a certain man stood before him on the bank, clothed in linen, whose loins were girded with fine gold of Uphaz. His body also was like the beryl. His face was as the appearance of lightning. His eyes were as lamps of fire. His arms and his feet were like in colour to polished brass. And when he spoke, the voice of his words was like the voice of a multitude — not one voice, but the sound of many, layered and pressing into the air above the river.

The men that were with Daniel saw not the vision. But a great quaking fell upon them. They turned and fled into the reeds and the broken ground beyond the bank, and they hid themselves there.

Daniel was left alone.

He stood on the bank and saw this great vision, and there remained no strength in him. The blood drained from his face. His comeliness was turned in him into corruption. His knees gave. His hands could not grip. The sound of the voice went on above him, and he heard the voice of his words, and his body could not hold him any longer. He was in a deep sleep on his face, his face toward the ground.

And, behold, an hand touched him.

The hand set him upon his knees and upon the palms of his hands. He felt the river stones cold beneath his palms. He felt the grit pressing into his skin. He could not yet lift his head.

A voice spoke above him. "O Daniel, a man greatly beloved, understand the words that I speak unto thee, and stand upright: for unto thee am I now sent."

Daniel stood. He was trembling. His hands shook. His knees would not hold steady. He stood at the edge of the river before the shining figure, and he listened.

"Fear not, Daniel: for from the first day that thou didst set thine heart to understand, and to chasten thyself before thy God, thy words were heard, and I am come for thy words. But the prince of the kingdom of Persia withstood me one and twenty days: but, lo, Michael, one of the chief princes, came to help me."

One and twenty days. Daniel knew the number. It was the count of his mourning. While he had been fasting by his window, the answer had been on its way.

The voice went on. There were things to come. Days yet ahead. Daniel listened, and his strength left him again. He set his face toward the ground. His mouth would not open. He could not speak.

Then there came one in the likeness of the sons of men, and touched his lips.

His mouth opened. Words came. "O my lord, by the vision my sorrows are turned upon me, and I have retained no strength. For how can the servant of this my lord talk with this my lord? for as for me, straightway there remained no strength in me, neither is there breath left in me."

The figure touched him again.

A third time. And this time the touch did not set him upon his hands and knees. It did not open his mouth. It strengthened him. He felt it move into his arms and into his chest and into the place beneath his ribs where the breath had gone out of him.

The voice spoke once more. "O man greatly beloved, fear not: peace be unto thee, be strong, yea, be strong."

Daniel stood by the great river. The reeds moved. The brown water ran. The men who had fled were somewhere out among the broken ground.

He drew a long breath, and his shoulders steadied, and he answered. "Let my lord speak; for thou hast strengthened me."
