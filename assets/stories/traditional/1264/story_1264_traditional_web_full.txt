In the third year of Cyrus king of Persia, Daniel was an old man, and a thing was revealed to him. The thing was true. It was a great warfare. He understood it, and he could not eat.

For three whole weeks he mourned. He took no pleasant bread. No meat nor wine came into his mouth. He did not anoint his head. He did not soften his beard with oil. The weeks went by, and his clothing hung looser on him, and his servants set food before him and took it away again untouched. On the twenty-fourth day of the first month, he rose from where he had been mourning and went out to the bank of the great river, which is Hiddekel.

A handful of men walked with him. The river was wide and brown. The reeds along its edge stirred in the early light.

He lifted up his eyes.

A man stood before him on the bank. He was clothed in linen, and his waist was adorned with pure gold of Uphaz. His body was like beryl. His face had the appearance of lightning. His eyes were as flaming torches. His arms and his feet were the color of burnished bronze. And when he spoke, the voice of his words was like the voice of a multitude — not one voice, but the sound of many, layered and pressing into the air above the river.

The men with Daniel did not see what he saw. But a great quaking fell upon them. They turned and fled into the reeds and the broken ground beyond the bank, and they hid themselves there.

Daniel was left alone.

He stood on the bank and saw the great vision, and no strength remained in him. The blood drained from his face. His comeliness was turned to corruption. His knees gave. His hands could not grip. The sound of the voice went on above him, and he heard the voice of the words, and his body could not hold him any longer. He fell into a deep sleep upon his face, his face turned toward the ground.

Then a hand touched him.

The hand set him upon his knees and upon the palms of his hands. He felt the river stones cold beneath his palms. He felt the grit pressing into his skin. He could not yet lift his head.

A voice spoke above him. "Daniel, you greatly beloved man, understand the words that I speak to you, and stand upright. For I have been sent to you, now."

Daniel stood. He was trembling. His hands shook. His knees would not hold steady. He stood at the edge of the river before the shining figure, and he listened.

"Don't be afraid, Daniel. For from the first day that you set your heart to understand, and to humble yourself before your God, your words were heard. I have come for your words' sake. But the prince of the kingdom of Persia withstood me twenty-one days. Michael, one of the chief princes, came to help me."

Twenty-one days. Daniel knew the number. It was the count of his mourning. While he had been fasting by his window, the answer had been on its way.

The voice went on. There were things to come. Days yet ahead. Daniel listened, and his strength left him again. He set his face toward the ground. His mouth would not open. He could not speak.

Then one in the likeness of the sons of men touched his lips.

His mouth opened. Words came. "My lord, by reason of the vision my sorrows have overtaken me, and I retain no strength. How can the servant of this my lord talk with this my lord? For as for me, there remained no strength in me, and there is no breath left in me."

The figure touched him again.

A third time. And this time the touch did not set him on his hands and knees. It did not open his mouth. It strengthened him. He felt it move into his arms and into his chest and into the place beneath his ribs where the breath had gone out of him.

The voice spoke once more. "Greatly beloved man, don't be afraid. Peace be to you. Be strong. Yes, be strong."

Daniel stood by the great river. The reeds moved. The brown water ran. The men who had fled were somewhere out among the broken ground.

He drew a long breath, and his shoulders steadied, and he answered. "Let my lord speak. For you have strengthened me."
