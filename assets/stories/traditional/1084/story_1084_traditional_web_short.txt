One day, as Jesus was teaching a crowd in the open air, a certain lawyer stood up to test him. He wanted to see how Jesus would answer.

"Teacher," he said, "what must I do to inherit eternal life?"

Jesus looked at him. "What is written in the law?" he said. "How do you read it?"

The man answered, "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, with all your strength, and with all your mind — and your neighbor as yourself."

"You have answered correctly," Jesus said. "Do this, and you will live."

But the man was not satisfied. He wanted to justify himself. So he pressed Jesus with another question.

"And who," he asked, "is my neighbor?"

Jesus answered him with a story.

"A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho. The road was long, and it wound through rocky hills and dry riverbeds. On his way, he fell among robbers. They stripped him of his clothes. They beat him. And they went away, leaving him half dead in the dirt.

"By chance, a priest came down that same road. He saw the man lying there. But he passed by on the other side.

"After him came a Levite. He reached the place and looked down at the wounded man. Then he, too, passed by on the other side.

"But a Samaritan came along, traveling the same road. When he saw the man, he was moved with compassion. He went to him. He knelt down beside him. He washed the wounds and bound them up, pouring on oil and wine. He lifted the man onto his own animal and brought him to an inn, and there he cared for him through the night. The lamps burned low. The road grew quiet outside.

"In the morning, the Samaritan took out two denarii and gave them to the innkeeper. 'Take care of him,' he said. 'Whatever you spend beyond this, I will repay you when I return.'"

Jesus looked at the lawyer.

"Which of these three," he said, "do you think was a neighbor to the man who fell among the robbers?"

The lawyer answered, "The one who showed mercy on him."

Then Jesus said to him, "Go and do likewise."
