It is an easy thing to pass by one who hath need. There are ever reasons — the hour is late, the burden is great, it is not our concern, we know not what to say. The road is long and the day waxeth hot and there is yet another place we must be.

What is most notable in this account is not the failure of the two who passed by. It is the plainness of the one who stopped. He was no great man. He was a traveler with oil and wine in his provision and a few coins in his purse. He saw a man in pain and did what lay in his power to do.

Sometimes to be a neighbour is not a matter of place or obligation. It is the matter of seeing another and choosing not to turn aside.
