One day, as Jesus was teaching a crowd in the open air, the afternoon sun fell warm on the group gathered around him. People sat on low stones and on the grass, listening. Among them sat a certain lawyer — a man who studied the law of Moses closely and knew the scriptures well. He stood up from the group to test Jesus.

"Teacher," he said, "what must I do to inherit eternal life?"

Jesus looked at him. "What is written in the law?" he said. "How do you read it?"

The man answered without hesitation. "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, with all your strength, and with all your mind — and your neighbor as yourself."

"You have answered correctly," Jesus said. "Do this, and you will live."

But the man was not satisfied. He wanted to justify himself — to draw a cleaner line around his obligation, to know exactly where his duty ended. So he pressed Jesus with another question.

"And who," he asked, "is my neighbor?"

Jesus looked at him for a moment. The crowd was quiet. Then he answered with a story.

"A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho. The road was long and wound through rocky hills and dry riverbeds. It was a lonely stretch — steep in places, with sharp turns and narrow passages cut between high stone walls where the shadows lay thick even at midday. Robbers were known to wait along that road.

"On his way, the man fell among them. They stripped him of his clothes. They beat him. And they went away, leaving him half dead in the dust at the side of the road. His blood soaked into the dry ground. No one was near.

"By chance, a priest came down that same road. He was a man of the temple, a man who knew the law and the prophets. When he saw the wounded man lying in the dirt, he looked at him. Then he crossed to the far side of the road and passed by without stopping.

"After him came a Levite — another man of religious duty. When he reached the place, he slowed his step and looked down at the wounded man. He saw the blood. He saw the torn clothing. He saw the stillness of the body. Then he, too, crossed to the other side and went on his way.

"But a Samaritan came along, traveling the same road. He was a man from a people the Jews looked down upon, a man who would not have been welcomed in the temple or greeted in the street. When he saw the wounded man, he was moved with compassion. He did not cross to the other side. He stopped. He climbed down from his animal. He went to him.

"He knelt in the dust beside the man. He cleaned the wounds and bound them up carefully, pouring oil and wine over the torn flesh. He lifted the man onto his own animal, holding him steady, and walked alongside, leading the animal slowly down the road toward the nearest inn.

"At the inn, the Samaritan stayed beside him through the night, tending to his wounds as the lamps burned low and the road grew quiet outside. In the morning, when it was time to leave, the Samaritan took out two denarii and gave them to the innkeeper. 'Take care of him,' he said. 'Whatever you spend beyond this, I will repay you when I return.'"

Jesus paused. The crowd was still. The lawyer stood where he had risen, watching Jesus with careful attention.

"Which of these three," Jesus said, "do you think was a neighbor to the man who fell among the robbers?"

The lawyer answered quietly. "The one who showed mercy on him."

Jesus looked at him steadily. "Go," he said, "and do likewise."
