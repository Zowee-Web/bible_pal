It is easy to walk past someone who needs help. There are always reasons — we are busy, we are tired, it is not our responsibility, we would not know what to say. The road is long and the day is hot and there is somewhere else we need to be.

What stands out in this story is not the failure of the two who passed by. It is the ordinariness of the one who stopped. He was not a hero. He was a traveler with oil and wine in his bag and a little money in his pocket. He saw someone in pain and did what he could.

Sometimes being a neighbor is not about geography or obligation. It is about seeing someone and choosing not to look away.
