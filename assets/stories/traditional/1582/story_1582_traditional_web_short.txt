David set captains over his men and sent the army out in three companies. He would have gone with them, but the people held him back. "You are worth ten thousand of us. Stay in the city." So the king stood at the gate as they marched out, and gave every captain one command, where all could hear it. "Deal gently, for my sake, with the young man Absalom."

The battle went into the forest of Ephraim, and Israel was beaten there. That day the forest devoured more men than the sword. Absalom, riding his mule, passed under the boughs of a great oak, and his head caught fast in the branches. The mule ran on, and he hung between the sky and the earth.

A man told Joab he had seen it. "Why did you not strike him down?" Joab said. But the man would not lift his hand against the king's son, for he had heard the king's command. So Joab went himself. He took three darts and drove them through Absalom's heart while he still lived in the oak, and his armor-bearers surrounded him and killed him. Then he sounded the trumpet and called the army back.

Joab sent a runner to the king. David was sitting between the gates when the watchman called down that a man was coming alone. "There is news in his mouth," the king said. The runner bowed to the ground. "Blessed be Yahweh, who has handed over the men who rose against you." But the king asked one thing only. "Is it well with the young man Absalom?" A second runner came, and to him the king asked the same. "May the enemies of my lord the king be as that young man is," he answered.

The king said no more. He went up to the room over the gate, and wept as he went. "My son Absalom. My son, my son Absalom. I wish I had died instead of you — Absalom, my son, my son."
