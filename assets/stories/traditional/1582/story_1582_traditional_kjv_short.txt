David set captains over his people and sent them forth in three parts. He would have gone forth himself, but they said, Thou art worth ten thousand of us; succour us out of the city. So the king stood by the gate as they came out by hundreds and by thousands, and he charged every captain in the hearing of all the people. Deal gently, for my sake, with the young man Absalom.

The battle was in the wood of Ephraim, and Israel was smitten there; and the wood devoured more people that day than the sword. Absalom rode upon his mule, and the mule went under the thick boughs of a great oak, and his head caught hold of the oak, and he was taken up between the heaven and the earth.

A man told Joab he had seen it. Why then didst thou not smite him? said Joab. But the man would not put forth his hand against the king's son, for he had heard the king's charge. So Joab went himself, and thrust three darts through the heart of Absalom while he was yet alive in the oak; and his armour-bearers slew him. Then Joab blew the trumpet, and called the people back from the pursuit.

Joab sent a runner to the king. David sat between the two gates, and the watchman cried down that a man came running alone. There is tidings in his mouth, said the king. The runner fell down before him. Blessed be the Lord, which hath delivered up them that rose against thee. But the king asked one thing only. Is the young man Absalom safe? A second runner came, and to him the king asked the same. The enemies of my lord the king, he answered, be as that young man is.

The king said no more. He went up to the chamber over the gate, and wept: and as he went, thus he said, O my son Absalom, my son, my son Absalom! Would God I had died for thee, O Absalom, my son, my son!
