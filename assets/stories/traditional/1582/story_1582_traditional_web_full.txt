David numbered the men who were with him and set captains over them. He sent the army out in three companies — one under Joab, one under Abishai his brother, one under Ittai of Gath. He meant to march out himself, but the people would not have it. "You are worth ten thousand of us," they said. "Stay in the city, and be ready to help us from there." So the king stood beside the gate as the army went out by hundreds and by thousands. And to each of the three captains, in the hearing of all the people, he gave the same command. "Deal gently, for my sake, with the young man Absalom."

The battle spread into the forest of Ephraim, and there Israel was beaten before David's servants. Twenty thousand fell that day, and the forest itself devoured more of them than the sword.

Absalom, riding his mule, came upon David's men. The mule went in under the thick boughs of a great oak, and Absalom's head caught fast in the branches. The mule ran on, and he was left hanging between the sky and the earth.

A man saw it and told Joab. "I saw Absalom hanging in an oak." Joab said, "You saw him? Why did you not strike him to the ground? I would have given you silver and a soldier's sash." But the man would not. "Though you weighed a thousand pieces of silver into my hand, I would not lift it against the king's son. We all heard the king charge you — for his sake, spare the young man Absalom." Joab said, "I will not wait here with you." He took three darts in his hand and drove them through Absalom's heart while he still lived in the oak. Ten of Joab's armor-bearers surrounded him and killed him. Then Joab sounded the trumpet, and the army turned back from the pursuit. They threw Absalom into a great pit in the forest and raised a heap of stones over him.

Ahimaaz the son of Zadok wanted to run and carry the king the news. But Joab said, "Not today. The king's son is dead; you shall carry news another day." He sent a Cushite instead. "Go, tell the king what you have seen." Still Ahimaaz begged. "Come what may, let me run too." At last Joab let him, and Ahimaaz took the way of the plain and outran the Cushite.

David was sitting between the two gates. The watchman on the wall saw a man running alone. "If he is alone," the king said, "there is news in his mouth." The runner came nearer. Then the watchman saw another. "He also brings news." "The first runs like Ahimaaz," said the watchman, "a good man; he comes with good news."

Ahimaaz called out as he came, "All is well," and bowed to the ground before the king. "Blessed be Yahweh your God, who has handed over the men who lifted their hand against my lord the king." The king asked one thing only. "Is it well with the young man Absalom?" And Ahimaaz said he had seen a great tumult when Joab sent him, but did not know what it was. "Stand aside," the king said. "Wait here."

Then the Cushite came. "Good news, my lord the king. Yahweh has avenged you today of all who rose against you." And the king asked him the same thing. "Is it well with the young man Absalom?" The Cushite answered, "May the enemies of my lord the king, and all who rise against you to do you harm, be as that young man is."

The king said no more. He went up to the room over the gate, and wept as he went. "My son Absalom. My son, my son Absalom. I wish I had died instead of you — Absalom, my son, my son."
