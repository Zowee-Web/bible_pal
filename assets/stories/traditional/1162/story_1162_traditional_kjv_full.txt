The sound from the upper room had drawn them in from every street and stairway of Jerusalem. The multitude filled the square below, shoulder against shoulder, faces turned upward. They were perplexed. Some asked one another what it meant. Others mocked. "These men are full of new wine," a voice called from the back, and laughter scattered through one corner of the gathering.

Then a man stepped forward to the front of the upper room. The eleven rose with him and stood at his back. Peter lifted up his voice.

"Ye men of Judaea, and all ye that dwell at Jerusalem, be this known unto you, and hearken unto my words. For these are not drunken, as ye suppose, seeing it is but the third hour of the day."

The laughter at the back of the crowd faltered. Faces in the front lifted. The mockers grew quiet, not yet convinced, but listening.

"But this is that which was spoken by the prophet Joel. 'It shall come to pass in the last days, saith God, I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh. Your sons and your daughters shall prophesy. Your young men shall see visions. Your old men shall dream dreams. And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be saved.'"

A murmur moved through the gathered. Old men looked at one another. Young men closed their mouths. The square stilled further.

Peter lifted his voice again. "Ye men of Israel, hear these words. Jesus of Nazareth, a man approved of God among you by miracles and wonders and signs, which God did by him in the midst of you, as ye yourselves also know — him, being delivered by the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God, ye have taken, and by wicked hands have crucified and slain. Whom God hath raised up, having loosed the pains of death, because it was not possible that he should be holden of it."

The square went quieter still. A woman at the front pressed her hand against her mouth. A man beside her had set down what he was carrying without noticing.

"For David saith concerning him, 'I foresaw the Lord always before my face. Moreover my flesh also shall rest in hope, because thou wilt not leave my soul in hell, neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption.' Men and brethren, let me freely speak unto you of the patriarch David — that he is both dead and buried, and his sepulchre is with us unto this day. Therefore being a prophet, he foresaw this. He spake of the resurrection of Christ. This Jesus hath God raised up, whereof we all are witnesses."

Peter paused. The eleven stood with him.

"Therefore let all the house of Israel know assuredly, that God hath made that same Jesus, whom ye have crucified, both Lord and Christ."

The words landed in the square like a stone dropped into still water. The silence that followed was not the silence of confusion. It was the silence of a wound opening.

They were pricked in their heart. They turned one to another and could not finish their sentences. A man near the front lifted his face to Peter and the eleven and called out, his voice catching, "Men and brethren, what shall we do?"

Others took up the cry. The square trembled with it.

Peter answered them. "Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost. For the promise is unto you, and to your children, and to all that are afar off, even as many as the Lord our God shall call."

He testified further with many other words. He exhorted them. "Save yourselves from this untoward generation."

And they came. They came in their hundreds and their thousands, down through the streets, through the gates, unto the waters. They that gladly received his word were baptized. And there were added in that day about three thousand souls.

On the steps of the upper room, in the evening that followed, the eleven stood beside Peter and looked out across the city. The streets were quieter now. The shouting was gone. In its place, in house after house, a new sound was beginning — the sound of bread broken, of names spoken in prayer, of the gathered hearing one another in their own tongues. The young church was finding its rhythm in the cool evening air, and the city of Jerusalem settled into its long night under the slow and steady rising of one new song.
