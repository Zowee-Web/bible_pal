The sound from the upper room had drawn them in from every street and stairway of Jerusalem. The crowd filled the square below, shoulder against shoulder, faces turned upward. They were perplexed. Some asked one another what it meant. Others mocked. "They are filled with new wine," a voice called from the back, and laughter scattered through one corner of the gathering.

Then a man stepped forward to the front of the upper room. The eleven rose with him and stood at his back. Peter lifted up his voice.

"You men of Judea, and all you who dwell at Jerusalem, let this be known to you. Listen to my words. For these are not drunken, as you suppose. It is only the third hour of the day."

The laughter at the back of the crowd faltered. Faces in the front lifted. The mockers grew quiet, not yet convinced, but listening.

"This is what was spoken through the prophet Joel. 'It will be in the last days,' says God, 'that I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh. Your sons and your daughters will prophesy. Your young men will see visions. Your old men will dream dreams. And it will be that whoever will call on the name of the Lord will be saved.'"

A murmur moved through the gathered. Old men looked at one another. Young men closed their mouths. The square stilled further.

Peter lifted his voice again. "Men of Israel, hear these words. Jesus of Nazareth, a man approved by God to you by mighty works and wonders and signs which God did by him among you — even as you yourselves know — him, being delivered up by the determined counsel and foreknowledge of God, you have taken by the hand of lawless men, crucified, and killed. Whom God raised up, having freed him from the agony of death, because it was not possible that he should be held by it."

The square went quieter still. A woman at the front pressed her hand against her mouth. A man beside her had set down what he was carrying without noticing.

"For David says concerning him, 'I saw the Lord always before my face. My flesh also will dwell in hope, because you will not leave my soul in Hades, neither will you allow your Holy One to see decay.' Brothers, I may tell you freely of the patriarch David — that he both died and was buried, and his tomb is with us to this day. Being therefore a prophet, he foresaw this. He spoke about the resurrection of the Christ. This Jesus God raised up, of which we all are witnesses."

Peter paused. The eleven stood with him.

"Let all the house of Israel therefore know assuredly that God has made him both Lord and Christ — this Jesus whom you crucified."

The words landed in the square like a stone dropped into still water. The silence that followed was not the silence of confusion. It was the silence of a wound opening.

They were cut to the heart. They turned to one another and could not finish their sentences. A man near the front lifted his face to Peter and the eleven and called out, his voice catching, "Brothers — what shall we do?"

Others took up the cry. The square trembled with it.

Peter answered them. "Repent, and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. For the promise is to you, and to your children, and to all who are far off — even as many as the Lord our God will call to himself."

He testified further with many other words. He exhorted them. "Save yourselves from this crooked generation."

And they came. They came in their hundreds and their thousands, down through the streets, through the gates, to the waters. Those who gladly received his word were baptized. There were added in that day about three thousand souls.

On the steps of the upper room, in the evening that followed, the eleven stood beside Peter and looked out across the city. The streets were quieter now. The shouting was gone. In its place, in house after house, a new sound was beginning — the sound of bread broken, of names spoken in prayer, of the gathered hearing one another in their own tongues. The young church was finding its rhythm in the cool evening air, and the city of Jerusalem settled into its long night under the slow and steady rising of one new song.
