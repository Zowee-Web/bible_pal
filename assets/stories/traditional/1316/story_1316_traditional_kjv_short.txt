The manna had come every morning, year after year, in the wilderness. The people gathered it. They ground it. They baked it into cakes. And in those days they began to weep at the doors of their tents — weeping for the fish they had eaten in Egypt, for the cucumbers and the melons and the leeks and the onions and the garlic. Their souls had grown weary of the manna. They wept for flesh.

And Moses heard their weeping. And he stood before the Lord.

Moses heard the people weep throughout their families, every man in the door of his tent: and the anger of the Lord was kindled greatly; Moses also was displeased.

And Moses said unto the Lord, "Wherefore hast thou afflicted thy servant? and wherefore have I not found favour in thy sight, that thou layest the burden of all this people upon me?

Have I conceived all this people? have I begotten them, that thou shouldest say unto me, Carry them in thy bosom, as a nursing father beareth the sucking child, unto the land which thou swarest unto their fathers?

Whence should I have flesh to give unto all this people? for they weep unto me, saying, Give us flesh, that we may eat.

I am not able to bear all this people alone, because it is too heavy for me.

And if thou deal thus with me, kill me, I pray thee, out of hand, if I have found favour in thy sight; and let me not see my wretchedness."

And the night came on in the wilderness. The tents were quiet. Moses stood alone before the Lord, and the weight of leading the people did not lift. And so it came to pass at the camp in those days, when the manna fell and the people wept, and Moses stood alone with their weeping in his ears.
