The manna had come every morning, year after year, in the wilderness. The people gathered it. They ground it. They baked it into cakes. And in those days they began to weep at the doors of their tents — weeping for the fish they had eaten in Egypt, for the cucumbers and the melons and the leeks and the onions and the garlic. Their souls had grown weary of the manna. They wept for meat.

And Moses heard their weeping. And he stood before Yahweh.

Moses heard the people weeping throughout their families, every man at the door of his tent; and Yahweh's anger was kindled greatly; and Moses was displeased.

Moses said to Yahweh, "Why have you treated your servant so badly? Why haven't I found favor in your sight, that you lay the burden of all this people on me?

Have I conceived all this people? Have I brought them out, that you should tell me, 'Carry them in your bosom, as a nurse carries a nursing infant, to the land which you swore to their fathers?'

Where could I get meat to give to all these people? For they weep to me, saying, 'Give us meat, that we may eat.'

I am not able to bear all this people alone, because it is too heavy for me.

If you treat me this way, please kill me right now, if I have found favor in your sight; and don't let me see my wretchedness."

And the night came on in the wilderness. The tents were quiet. Moses stood alone before Yahweh, and the weight of leading the people did not lift. And so it was at the camp in those days, when the manna fell and the people wept, and Moses stood alone with their weeping in his ears.
