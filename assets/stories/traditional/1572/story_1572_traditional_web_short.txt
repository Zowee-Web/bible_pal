Isaac was old, and his eyes were too dim to see. He called Esau his elder son and asked him to take his bow, hunt venison, and make him the savory food he loved. "Bring it to me to eat," he said, "so that my soul may bless you before I die."

So Esau went out to the field. But Rebekah had heard, and she went quickly to Jacob. "Obey my voice, my son. Bring me two young goats, and I will make the food your father loves. You will carry it in — and he will bless you before he dies."

"But Esau is hairy, and I am smooth," Jacob said. "If my father touches me, I will seem a deceiver, and bring a curse on myself."

"Let your curse be on me, my son. Only obey my voice."

So Rebekah made the food, dressed Jacob in Esau's clothes, and covered his hands and neck with the goatskins. Jacob carried the meal in to his father. "I am Esau, your firstborn," he said.

Isaac called him near and felt his hands — hairy, like Esau's. "The voice is Jacob's voice, but the hands are the hands of Esau. Are you really my son Esau?"

"I am."

So Isaac ate, and kissed him, and smelled Esau's clothes on him — and he blessed him. He gave him the dew of the sky and the fatness of the earth, grain and new wine, and lordship over his brothers. "Cursed be everyone who curses you, and blessed be everyone who blesses you."

Jacob had scarcely gone out when Esau came in from his hunting, with food of his own. "Let my father arise and eat, that your soul may bless me."

"Who are you?"

"I am your son — your firstborn — Esau."

Then Isaac trembled violently. "Who was it, then, who brought me venison? I ate it all before you came, and I blessed him — yes, and he will be blessed."

Esau cried out with an exceedingly great and bitter cry. "Bless me — me also, my father! Do you have only one blessing?" And he lifted up his voice, and wept. What was left for Esau was this: he would live by his sword, and he would serve his brother, until the day he broke loose and shook the yoke from his neck.

So Esau hated Jacob, and said in his heart that when the days of mourning for his father came, he would kill him. When his words reached Rebekah, she sent for Jacob and told him to flee to her brother Laban in Haran, and to stay there until Esau's anger turned away. "Why should I be bereaved of you both in one day?"
