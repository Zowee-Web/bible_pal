Sarai had given her Egyptian handmaid Hagar unto Abram to wife, that peradventure a child should come unto the household through her, seeing the years had passed and Sarai herself had not conceived. Hagar had conceived. And in the day that she knew it, she had begun to look upon Sarai with other eyes; and Sarai had said unto Abram, "My wrong be upon thee." And Abram answered Sarai. And Sarai dealt hardly with Hagar. And Hagar fled.

But Abram said unto Sarai, "Behold, thy maid is in thy hand; do to her as it pleaseth thee." And Sarai dealt hardly with her, and she fled from her face.

And the angel of the Lord found her by a fountain of water in the wilderness, by the fountain in the way to Shur.

And he said, "Hagar, Sarai's maid, whence camest thou? and whither wilt thou go?"

And she said, "I flee from the face of my mistress Sarai."

And the angel of the Lord said unto her, "Return to thy mistress, and submit thyself under her hands."

And Hagar stood by the fountain in the way to Shur with the water of the well beside her and the angel of the Lord before her. The way behind her ran back across the wilderness toward the camp of Abram; the way before her ran on toward the land of Egypt out of which she had once come up; and the angel had called her by name, and had asked her whence she came, and had spoken unto her in the wilderness where she had thought herself alone.
