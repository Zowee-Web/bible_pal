Israel could not stand before its enemies, Yahweh told Joshua, because a devoted thing was hidden among them. So the people came near in the morning, tribe by tribe and man by man, until the lot fell on Achan, of the tribe of Judah. "Give glory to Yahweh, and confess," Joshua said. And Achan confessed: among the plunder he had seen a beautiful robe from Babylon, silver, and a bar of gold; he had coveted them and taken them, and hidden them in the ground inside his tent. Messengers ran and found it all.

Then Joshua and all Israel took Achan, with the silver and the robe and the gold, his sons and daughters, his cattle, his tent, and all he had, up to the valley of Achor. "Why have you troubled us? Today Yahweh will trouble you." And all Israel stoned him, and burned them with fire, and raised over him a great heap of stones. So Yahweh turned from his fierce anger, and the place was called the valley of Trouble.

Then Yahweh told Joshua not to be afraid, but to go up against Ai, whose king and city were given into his hand, and to set an ambush behind it. Joshua sent his men out by night, and in the morning drew the men of Ai out of the city by feigning flight. When all Ai had gone out in pursuit, the ambush rose, took the empty city, and set it on fire. The men of Ai looked back and saw the smoke going up, and had no strength to flee; Israel turned back on them, and they were caught between and struck down, until none remained, and their king was taken. Twelve thousand fell that day. Only the livestock and goods Israel kept, as Yahweh commanded. Joshua burned Ai to a heap, and hanged its king on a tree until evening, then laid his body at the city gate under a great heap of stones.
