Israel could not stand before its enemies, the Lord told Joshua, because an accursed thing was hidden in the midst of them. So the people came near in the morning, tribe by tribe and man by man, until the lot fell upon Achan, of the tribe of Judah. Give glory to the Lord, and make confession, said Joshua. And Achan confessed: among the spoils he had seen a goodly Babylonish garment, silver, and a wedge of gold; he had coveted them and taken them, and hid them in the earth in the midst of his tent. Messengers ran and found it all.

Then Joshua and all Israel took Achan, with the silver and the garment and the gold, his sons and daughters, his cattle, his tent, and all that he had, up unto the valley of Achor. Why hast thou troubled us? The Lord shall trouble thee this day. And all Israel stoned him, and burned them with fire, and raised over him a great heap of stones. So the Lord turned from the fierceness of his anger, and the place was called the valley of Trouble.

Then the Lord told Joshua to fear not, but to go up to Ai, whose king and city were given into his hand, and to lay an ambush behind it. Joshua sent his men out by night, and in the morning drew the men of Ai out of the city by feigning flight. When all Ai had gone out in pursuit, they that lay in ambush rose, seized the empty city, and set it on fire. The men of Ai looked behind them and saw the smoke ascend, and had no power to flee; Israel turned back upon them, and they were caught between and smitten, till none remained, and their king was taken. Twelve thousand fell that day. Only the cattle and spoil Israel kept, according to the word of the Lord. Joshua burnt Ai to a heap, and hanged its king on a tree until evening, then laid his body at the gate under a great heap of stones.
