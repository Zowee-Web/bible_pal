Israel could not stand before its enemies, Yahweh told Joshua, because a devoted thing was hidden among them; until it was removed, they would keep on falling. So in the morning the people came near, tribe by tribe, family by family, household by household, man by man — and the lot fell at last on Achan, of the tribe of Judah. "My son," Joshua said, "give glory to Yahweh, and make confession to him; tell me what you have done." And Achan confessed: "I have sinned against Yahweh. Among the plunder I saw a beautiful robe from Babylon, two hundred shekels of silver, and a bar of gold; I coveted them and took them, and they are hidden in the ground inside my tent." Messengers ran and found it all, and laid it before Yahweh.

Then Joshua and all Israel took Achan, and the silver and the robe and the gold, and his sons and daughters, his oxen and donkeys and sheep, his tent and all he had, up to the valley of Achor. "Why have you troubled us? Today Yahweh will trouble you." And all Israel stoned him, and burned them with fire, and stoned them, and raised over him a great heap of stones that stands to this day. So Yahweh turned from his fierce anger, and the place was called the valley of Achor — the valley of Trouble.

Then Yahweh told Joshua not to be afraid, but to go up against Ai, for its king and city were given into his hand; this time Israel could take the livestock and goods for themselves, and were to set an ambush behind the city. So Joshua chose thirty thousand men and sent them by night, with a plan: he and the army would draw the men of Ai out by feigning flight, and the ambush would take the empty city and burn it. In the morning he came up and camped north of Ai. The king of Ai hurried out to battle, not knowing an ambush lay behind him. Israel let themselves be beaten and fled toward the wilderness, and all Ai was drawn out in pursuit, until the gates stood open and empty.

Then Yahweh said, "Stretch out toward Ai the javelin in your hand." Joshua stretched it out, and the men in ambush rose, ran into the city, and set it ablaze. When the men of Ai looked back and saw the smoke going up, they had no strength to flee, and the Israelites turned back on them; so Ai was caught between them on both sides and struck down, until none remained, and their king was taken alive. Twelve thousand fell that day, all the people of Ai; for Joshua did not draw back the hand that held the javelin until all was destroyed. Only the livestock and goods Israel took, as Yahweh commanded. Joshua burned Ai and made it a heap forever; and the king he hanged on a tree until evening, then they threw his body at the entrance of the city gate under a great heap of stones that stands to this day.
