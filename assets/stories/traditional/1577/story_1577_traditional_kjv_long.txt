Israel could not stand before its enemies, the Lord told Joshua, because an accursed thing was hidden in the midst of them; and until it was taken away, they would keep on falling. So the people were to sanctify themselves, and in the morning come near by their tribes. The tribe the Lord took would come by families, and the family by households, and the household man by man — and he that was taken with the accursed thing should be burnt with fire, he and all that he had, because he had transgressed the covenant of the Lord and wrought folly in Israel. In the morning Joshua brought Israel near. The tribe of Judah was taken; then the family of the Zarhites; then the household of Zabdi; and man by man, until the lot fell upon Achan the son of Carmi, of the tribe of Judah.

My son, said Joshua, give, I pray thee, glory to the Lord God of Israel, and make confession unto him; tell me now what thou hast done, and hide it not. And Achan confessed that he had indeed sinned against the Lord God of Israel. Among the spoils, he said, he had seen a goodly Babylonish garment, and silver of two hundred shekels, and a wedge of gold that weighed fifty; he coveted them, and took them, and they lay hidden in the earth within his tent, the silver beneath.

Messengers ran to the tent, and there it all was, as he had said; and they brought it out and laid it before the Lord. Then Joshua and all Israel took Achan up unto the valley of Achor, and with him the silver, the garment, and the wedge of gold; his sons, his daughters; his oxen, his asses, his sheep; his tent, and everything that was his. Why hast thou troubled us? Joshua asked him; this day shall the Lord trouble thee. And all Israel stoned him, and burned them with fire, and stoned them, and over the place they piled a great cairn of stones that stands there yet. So the fierce anger of the Lord was turned away; and the valley was named Achor — the valley of Trouble — to this day.

Then the Lord bade Joshua fear not, nor be dismayed, but take all the men of war and go up against Ai; its king and people and city and land were given into his hand. As Israel had done to Jericho and her king, so should they do to Ai and hers — save that this time the spoil and the cattle they might take for a prey to themselves; and they were to lay an ambush behind the city. So Joshua chose thirty thousand mighty men of valour, and sent them away by night, with a plan: he and the people would go up to the city, and when the men of Ai came out against them as at the first, Israel would flee before them and draw them away; then they that lay in ambush would rise, seize the empty city, and set it on fire, doing all as the Lord had commanded.

By night the ambush took its place west of the city, between Bethel and Ai, while Joshua lodged among the people. At dawn he led them up and pitched on the north, a valley lying between the camp and the town. The king of Ai, seeing them, hurried out early to give battle on the plain, never guessing that armed men waited at his back. It went as Joshua had designed. Israel gave way as though beaten and fled toward the wilderness, and every man of Ai was drawn out after them. Not a man was left in Ai or in Bethel; all of them had gone out in the chase, and the city stood open and unguarded behind them. Then the Lord told Joshua to stretch his spear toward the city, for Ai was given into his hand; and at that sign the men in hiding sprang up, rushed the undefended town, and set it ablaze. The pursuers looked back to see their own smoke climbing into heaven, and the strength went out of them — they could flee neither this way nor that. And when Joshua and all Israel saw that the ambush had taken the city, and the smoke going up, the fleeing men wheeled and came back upon their pursuers, so that the men of Ai were caught between the two forces and cut down until none remained or escaped; and their king they took alive, and led him to Joshua.

When the slaughter in the open country was finished, Israel turned back to the city itself and put every soul in it to the sword — twelve thousand in all, both men and women, the whole people of Ai; for Joshua held out the spear and did not lower his hand until all the inhabitants of Ai were utterly destroyed. The cattle and the plunder alone they kept for themselves, as the Lord had allowed. Ai itself Joshua burned, leaving a mound of ruin that lies desolate unto this day. Its king he hanged upon a tree until the light failed; then, at the going down of the sun, the body was taken down and cast at the entering of the city gate, and a great heap of stones was piled over it, standing there yet.
