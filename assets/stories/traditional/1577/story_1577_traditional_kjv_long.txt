Israel could not stand before its enemies, the Lord told Joshua, because an accursed thing was hidden in the midst of them; and until it was taken away, they would keep on falling. So the people were to sanctify themselves, and in the morning come near by their tribes. The tribe the Lord took would come by families, and the family by households, and the household man by man — and he that was taken with the accursed thing should be burnt with fire, he and all that he had, because he had transgressed the covenant of the Lord. In the morning Joshua brought Israel near. The tribe of Judah was taken; then the family of the Zarhites; then the household of Zabdi; and man by man, until the lot fell upon Achan the son of Carmi, of the tribe of Judah.

My son, said Joshua, give, I pray thee, glory to the Lord God of Israel, and make confession unto him; tell me now what thou hast done, and hide it not. And Achan confessed that he had indeed sinned against the Lord God of Israel. Among the spoils, he said, he had seen a goodly Babylonish garment, and silver of two hundred shekels, and a wedge of gold that weighed fifty; he coveted them, and took them, and they lay hidden in the earth within his tent, the silver beneath.

Messengers ran to the tent, and there it all was, as he had said; and they brought it out and laid it before the Lord. Then Joshua and all Israel took Achan up unto the valley of Achor, and with him the silver, the garment, and the wedge of gold; his sons, his daughters; his oxen, his asses, his sheep; his tent, and everything that was his. Why hast thou troubled us? Joshua asked him; this day shall the Lord trouble thee. And all Israel stoned him, and burned them, and over the place they piled a great cairn of stones that stands there yet. So the fierce anger of the Lord was turned away; and the valley was named Achor — the valley of Trouble — to this day.

Then the Lord bade Joshua fear not, nor be dismayed, but take all the men of war and go up against Ai; its king and people and city and land were given into his hand. As Israel had done to Jericho and her king, so should they do to Ai and hers — save that this time the spoil and the cattle they might take for a prey to themselves; and they were to lay an ambush behind the city. So Joshua chose thirty thousand mighty men of valour, and sent them away by night, with a plan: he and the people would go up to the city, and when the men of Ai came out against them as at the first, Israel would flee before them and draw them away; then they that lay in ambush would rise, seize the empty city, and set it on fire.

The ambush was set between Bethel and Ai, on the west of the city, and Joshua lodged that night among the people. In the morning he mustered them and went up before Ai, and pitched on the north side, and there was a valley between. When the king of Ai saw it, he and all his people hasted and rose up early and went out against Israel to battle at the appointed place before the plain — but he knew not that there was an ambush against him behind the city. Joshua and all Israel made as if they were beaten, and fled by the way of the wilderness; and all the people of Ai were called together to pursue after them, until not a man was left in the city, and they left it open. Then the Lord said unto Joshua, Stretch out the spear that is in thy hand toward Ai; for I will give it into thine hand. And Joshua stretched it out, and they that lay in ambush rose quickly, and ran into the city, and took it, and hasted and set it on fire. When the men of Ai looked behind them, and saw the smoke of the city ascend up to heaven, they had no power to flee this way or that way; and the people that fled to the wilderness turned back upon their pursuers. So Ai was caught between Israel on both sides, and smitten, so that none remained or escaped; and the king of Ai was taken alive, and brought to Joshua.

When Israel had made an end of slaying all the men of Ai in the field, in the wilderness where they chased them, they returned unto the city and smote it with the edge of the sword. Twelve thousand fell that day, even all the people of Ai; for Joshua drew not back his hand, wherewith he stretched out the spear, until he had utterly destroyed all the inhabitants of Ai. Only the cattle and the spoil of the city Israel took for a prey unto themselves, according to the word of the Lord. So Joshua burnt Ai, and made it a heap for ever, a desolation unto this day. And the king of Ai he hanged on a tree until evening; and at sundown they took his body down from the tree, and cast it at the entering of the gate of the city, and raised thereon a great heap of stones that remaineth unto this day.
