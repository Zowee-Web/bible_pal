Israel could not stand before its enemies, Yahweh told Joshua, because a devoted thing was hidden among them; and until it was taken away, they would keep on falling. So the people were to sanctify themselves, and in the morning come near tribe by tribe. The tribe Yahweh chose would come by families, and the family by households, and the household man by man — and whoever was found with the devoted thing would be burned with fire, he and all he had, because he had broken Yahweh's covenant and done a disgraceful thing in Israel. In the morning Joshua brought Israel near. The tribe of Judah was taken; then the family of the Zerahites; then the household of Zabdi; and man by man, until the lot fell on Achan son of Carmi, of the tribe of Judah.

"My son," Joshua said, "give glory to Yahweh, the God of Israel, and make confession to him. Tell me what you have done; don't hide it from me." And Achan answered, "It is true; I have sinned against Yahweh, the God of Israel. This is what I did: I saw among the plunder a beautiful robe from Babylon, two hundred shekels of silver, and a bar of gold weighing fifty shekels. I coveted them, and I took them. They are hidden in the ground inside my tent, with the silver underneath."

Messengers ran to the tent and found it all, just as he had said, and laid it out before Yahweh. Then Joshua and all Israel took Achan, and the silver and the robe and the bar of gold, and his sons and his daughters, his oxen and donkeys and sheep, his tent and all that he had, and brought them up to the valley of Achor. "Why have you troubled us?" Joshua said. "Today Yahweh will trouble you." And all Israel stoned him, and they burned them with fire, and stoned them, and raised over him a great heap of stones that stands to this day. So Yahweh turned from the fierceness of his anger; and the place was called the valley of Achor — the valley of Trouble — to this day.

Then Yahweh said to Joshua, "Don't be afraid or dismayed. Take all the fighting men and go up against Ai; I have given its king and its people and its city and its land into your hand. Do to Ai and its king as you did to Jericho and its king — only this time you may take its livestock and its goods for yourselves. Set an ambush behind the city." So Joshua chose thirty thousand mighty men and sent them out by night, and he commanded them what to do: he and the army with him would go up to the city, and when the men of Ai came out against them as they had at the first, Israel would flee before them and draw them away; then the men in ambush would rise from their place, seize the city while it stood empty, and set it on fire, doing all according to Yahweh's word.

The ambush went into place between Bethel and Ai, on the west of the city, and Joshua spent the night among the people. In the morning he mustered them and came up before Ai and camped to the north, with a valley between. When the king of Ai saw them, he and all his people hurried out to battle at the appointed place before the plain — not knowing an ambush lay behind the city. Joshua and all Israel let themselves be beaten, and fled toward the wilderness; and all the people of Ai were called out to pursue them. Not a man was left in Ai or Bethel who did not go out after Israel, and they left the city open behind them and chased after Israel. Then Yahweh said to Joshua, "Stretch out toward Ai the javelin in your hand, for I will give it into your hand." Joshua stretched it out, and the men in ambush rose quickly, ran into the city, took it, and set it ablaze. When the men of Ai looked back and saw the smoke of their city going up to the sky, they had no strength to flee this way or that; and the Israelites who had fled turned back on them. So Ai was caught between Israel on both sides, and struck down until none remained or escaped, and the king of Ai was taken alive and brought to Joshua.

When Israel had finished killing all the men of Ai in the open field and the wilderness where they had chased them, until they had all fallen by the edge of the sword, they returned to the city and struck down with the sword all who were in it. Twelve thousand fell that day, all the people of Ai; for Joshua did not draw back the hand that held out the javelin until all of Ai was destroyed. Only the livestock and the goods of the city Israel took for themselves, as Yahweh had commanded. So Joshua burned Ai and made it a heap of ruins, a desolation to this day. The king of Ai he hanged on a tree until evening; and at sundown they took the body down, and threw it at the entrance of the city gate, and raised over it a great heap of stones that stands to this day.
