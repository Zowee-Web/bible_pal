Israel could not stand before its enemies, the Lord told Joshua, because an accursed thing was hidden in the midst of them; until it was taken away, they would keep on falling. So in the morning the people came near, tribe by tribe, family by family, household by household, man by man — and the lot fell at last upon Achan, of the tribe of Judah. My son, said Joshua, give glory to the Lord God of Israel, and make confession unto him; tell me what thou hast done. And Achan confessed that he had sinned against the Lord: among the spoils he had seen a goodly Babylonish garment, silver, and a wedge of gold, and had coveted them and taken them, and hidden them in the earth within his tent. Messengers ran, and found it all, and laid it before the Lord.

Then Joshua and all Israel took Achan, and the silver and the garment and the gold, and his sons and his daughters, his oxen and asses and sheep, his tent and all that he had, up unto the valley of Achor. Why hast thou troubled us? this day shall the Lord trouble thee. And all Israel stoned him, and burned them, and over the place piled a great cairn of stones that stands there yet. So the fierce anger of the Lord was turned away, and the valley was named Achor — the valley of Trouble.

Then the Lord told Joshua to fear not, but to go up to Ai, for its king and city were given into his hand; this time Israel might take the cattle and spoil for a prey, and were to lay an ambush behind the city. So Joshua chose thirty thousand men and sent them by night, with a plan: he and the people would draw the men of Ai out by feigning flight, and the ambush would seize the empty city and burn it. In the morning he went up and pitched on the north of Ai. The king of Ai hasted out to battle, knowing not that an ambush lay behind him. Israel made as if they were beaten, and fled toward the wilderness, and all Ai was drawn out in pursuit, until the city stood open and empty.

Then the Lord said, Stretch out the spear that is in thy hand toward Ai. And Joshua stretched it out, and they that lay in ambush rose, ran into the city, and set it on fire. When the men of Ai looked behind them and saw the smoke ascend, they had no power to flee, and the Israelites turned back upon them; so Ai was caught between them on both sides, and smitten, till none remained, and their king was taken alive. Twelve thousand fell that day, all the people of Ai; for Joshua drew not back the hand wherewith he stretched out the spear until all was destroyed. Only the cattle and the spoil Israel took, according to the word of the Lord. Joshua burnt Ai, and made it a heap for ever; and the king he hanged on a tree until evening, then cast his body at the entering of the gate under a great heap of stones that remaineth unto this day.
