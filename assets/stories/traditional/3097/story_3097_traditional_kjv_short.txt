In the seventh year, Jehoiada the priest fetched the rulers over hundreds, with the captains and the guard, unto the house of the Lord. There he made a covenant with them, took an oath of them, and shewed them the king’s son.

Jehoiada divided their duties for the sabbath. Some would keep watch at the king’s house, some would stand at the gate of Sur, and some would guard the gate behind the guard. Those going forth from duty would remain about the king in the house of the Lord. “Ye shall compass the king round about,” he commanded. Every man was to keep his weapon in his hand and stay with the king as he went out and came in.

The captains obeyed all that Jehoiada commanded. They brought both the men entering on the sabbath and those going forth. Jehoiada gave them King David’s spears and shields from the temple. Armed guards stood from the right corner of the temple unto the left, along by the altar and the temple, round about the king.

Then Jehoiada brought forth the king’s son. He put the crown upon him and gave him the testimony. They made him king, anointed him, clapped their hands, and cried, “God save the king.”

Athaliah heard the noise of the guard and the people. She entered the temple of the Lord and saw the king standing by a pillar, with princes and trumpeters beside him. The people rejoiced and blew with trumpets. Athaliah rent her clothes and cried, “Treason, Treason.”

Jehoiada ordered the captains to bring her forth without the ranges and slay any who followed her. She was not to be slain in the house of the Lord. They laid hands on her, took her by the way where the horses came into the king’s house, and slew her there.

Jehoiada made a covenant between the Lord, the king, and the people that they should be the Lord’s people, and another between king and people. The people brake down Baal’s house, his altars, and his images, and slew Mattan the priest of Baal before the altars. Jehoiada appointed officers over the house of the Lord. They brought the king to the royal house, and he sat on the throne. The people rejoiced, and the city was in quiet.
