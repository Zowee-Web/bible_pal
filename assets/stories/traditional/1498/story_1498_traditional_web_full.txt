The decree had been signed. The writing had gone out throughout all the king's provinces. It said that on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, in the month Adar, all the Jews — old and young, women and children — would be destroyed and their goods spoiled.

When Mordecai heard, he rent his clothes. He put on sackcloth and ashes. He went out into the midst of the city. He cried with a loud and bitter cry. He came as far as the king's gate. He could not enter in. No one might enter the king's gate clothed with sackcloth.

In every province where the king's commandment came, the news brought mourning among the Jews, and weeping, and fasting. Many of them lay in sackcloth and ashes.

Esther's maids and her chamberlains came in and told her about Mordecai. She was greatly grieved. She sent clothing out to him to put on, that he might come into the gate. He would not take it.

She sent Hatach the chamberlain. Hatach went out to him in the open square that was before the king's gate. Mordecai told him everything. He told him the king had been bribed to destroy them. He told him the silver Haman had promised to weigh into the king's treasuries. He gave him a copy of the writing of the decree, that he might show it to Esther, and that she might know.

He sent her a charge with the decree. She should go in unto the king. She should make supplication for her people. "Who knoweth," Mordecai said, "whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?"

Hatach carried the message back to Esther.

Esther sent her answer back. She did not send it quickly. She thought about it first. She knew what going in unto the king without being called could mean. She knew the law. There was one law for him or her that went in unto the king and had not been called. They should be put to death, unless the king held out his golden scepter, that they might live.

She had not been called for thirty days.

"Go," she said when she had thought. "Gather all the Jews that are present in Shushan. Fast for me. Eat not, neither drink, for three days, night and day. I and my maidens will fast in the same manner. And so will I go in unto the king, which is not according to the law. And if I perish, I perish."

When the answer was carried out, Mordecai went his way and did all that she had commanded him. The Jews of Shushan stopped their eating. The houses went quiet. The three days began.

In the inner chamber Esther's table was set aside. The lamps burned through the night. The maidens did not speak. The dishes remained where they had been put. The door to the corridor leading to the king's hall stood closed. Beyond it, somewhere in the palace, the king sat where he had been sitting, with the gold scepter resting near him.

The first day went. The second came. The third came. The houses of the city stayed quiet. Esther did not eat. She did not drink. The morning of the fourth day was coming on.
