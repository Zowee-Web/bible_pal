The decree had been signed. The writing had gone out throughout all the king's provinces. It said that on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, in the month Adar, all the Jews — both young and old, little children and women — should be destroyed, and their goods taken for a spoil.

When Mordecai heard, he rent his clothes. He put on sackcloth with ashes. He went out into the midst of the city. He cried with a loud and a bitter cry. He came even before the king's gate. He could not enter in. None might enter into the king's gate clothed with sackcloth.

In every province whithersoever the king's commandment came, there was great mourning among the Jews, and fasting, and weeping, and wailing. Many lay in sackcloth and ashes.

Esther's maids and her chamberlains came in and told it her concerning Mordecai. She was exceedingly grieved. She sent raiment out to him to put on, that he might come into the gate. He would not receive it.

She sent Hatach the chamberlain. Hatach went forth unto him into the open square that was before the king's gate. Mordecai told him all. He told him the king had been bribed to destroy them. He told him the silver Haman had promised to pay into the king's treasuries. He gave unto him a copy of the writing of the decree, that he might shew it unto Esther, and that she might know.

He sent her a charge withal. She should go in unto the king. She should make supplication for her people. "Who knoweth," Mordecai said, "whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?"

Hatach carried the word back unto Esther.

Esther sent her answer back. She sent it not in haste. She thought upon it first. She knew what going in unto the king without being called could mean. She knew the law. There was one law for him or her that went in unto the king and was not called. They should be put to death, except such to whom the king should hold out the golden sceptre, that they might live.

She had not been called for thirty days.

"Go," she said when she had thought. "Gather together all the Jews that are present in Shushan. Fast ye for me. Neither eat nor drink three days, night nor day. I also and my maidens will fast likewise. And so will I go in unto the king, which is not according to the law. And if I perish, I perish."

When the answer was carried out, Mordecai went his way and did according unto all that Esther had commanded him. The Jews of Shushan ceased their eating. The houses were silent. The three days began.

In the inner chamber the table of Esther was set aside. The lamps burned through the night. The maidens spake not. The dishes remained where they had been laid. The door unto the corridor leading to the king's hall stood shut. Beyond it, somewhere within the palace, the king sat where he had been sitting, with the golden sceptre resting near him.

The first day went. The second came. The third came. The houses of the city stayed silent. Esther did eat not. She did drink not. The morning of the fourth day was coming on.
