The news came back to the palace before the fast was set. Mordecai stood in the city, dressed in sackcloth and ash, weeping with a loud and bitter cry in the open square before the king's gate. Esther's maids told her, and she was very greatly grieved. She sent clothing out to him to put on, that he might come into the gate again. He would not take it.

She sent Hatach the chamberlain. Hatach went out and found Mordecai in the street. Mordecai told him everything. He told him the king had been bribed to destroy them. He told him the silver Haman had promised to weigh into the king's treasuries. He gave him a copy of the writing of the decree, that he might show it to Esther, and that she might know.

He sent her a charge with the decree. "Go in unto the king. Make supplication for thy people. Who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?"

Esther sent her answer back. She did not send it quickly.

"Go," she said. "Gather all the Jews that are present in Shushan. Fast for me. Eat not, neither drink, for three days, night and day. I and my maidens will fast in the same manner. And so will I go in unto the king, which is not according to the law. And if I perish, I perish."

When the answer was carried out, Mordecai went his way and did all that she had commanded him. The Jews of Shushan stopped their eating. The houses went quiet. The three days began.

In the inner chamber Esther's table was set aside. The lamps burned through the night. The maidens did not speak. The dishes remained where they had been put. The door to the corridor leading to the king's hall stood closed. Beyond it, somewhere in the palace, the king sat where he had been sitting, with the gold scepter resting near him.
