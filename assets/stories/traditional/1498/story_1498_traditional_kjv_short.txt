The news came back to the palace or ever the fast was set. Mordecai stood in the city, clothed with sackcloth and with ashes, and cried with a loud and a bitter cry in the open square before the king's gate. The maids of Esther told her, and she was exceedingly grieved. She sent raiment unto him, to be put on him, that he might come again into the gate. He would not receive it.

She sent Hatach the chamberlain. Hatach went forth and found Mordecai in the street. Mordecai told him all. He told him the king had been bribed to destroy them. He told him the silver Haman had promised to pay into the king's treasuries. He gave unto him a copy of the writing of the decree, that he might shew it unto Esther, and that she might know.

He sent her a charge withal. "Go in unto the king. Make supplication for thy people. Who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?"

Esther sent her answer back. She sent it not in haste.

"Go," she said. "Gather together all the Jews that are present in Shushan. Fast ye for me. Neither eat nor drink three days, night nor day. I also and my maidens will fast likewise. And so will I go in unto the king, which is not according to the law. And if I perish, I perish."

When the answer was carried out, Mordecai went his way and did according unto all that Esther had commanded him. The Jews of Shushan ceased their eating. The houses were silent. The three days began.

In the inner chamber the table of Esther was set aside. The lamps burned through the night. The maidens spake not. The dishes remained where they had been laid. The door unto the corridor leading to the king's hall stood shut. Beyond it, somewhere within the palace, the king sat where he had been sitting, with the golden sceptre resting near him.
