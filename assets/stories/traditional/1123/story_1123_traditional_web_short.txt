Saul was still speaking threats of violence against the disciples of the Lord. He went to the high priest and asked for letters to the synagogues in Damascus. If he found any who belonged to the Way, both men and women, he would bring them bound to Jerusalem. He set out on the road with his companions, and the dust of the highway rose in pale clouds behind them.

Near Damascus, in the bright heat of midday, a light from heaven flashed around him. It came suddenly, and Saul fell to the ground. The men who traveled with him stood speechless, hearing the voice but seeing no one. Then a voice spoke and called him by name. "Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?"

He answered with his face against the warm earth. "Who are you, Lord?" The voice came back steady and unhurried. "I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting. But rise up and enter into the city, and you will be told what you must do."

When Saul got up from the ground he opened his eyes and could see nothing. The light had taken his sight and left only darkness. His companions led him by the hand the rest of the way into Damascus, and for three days he was without sight and neither ate nor drank.

In the same city lived a disciple named Ananias, and the Lord spoke to him in a vision and told him to go to the house of Judas on the street called Straight and ask for one named Saul of Tarsus. Ananias hesitated, for he had heard from many about this man and the harm he had done to the saints in Jerusalem. But the Lord told him to go, for Saul was a chosen vessel to carry his name before the nations and the children of Israel.

Ananias went and entered the house and laid his hands on Saul and said, "Brother Saul, the Lord, even Jesus, who appeared to you on the road by which you came, has sent me that you may receive your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit." At once, something like scales fell from Saul's eyes, and he could see again, and he rose and was baptized. Then he took food and was strengthened.

He stayed for several days with the disciples in Damascus, and at once he began to proclaim Jesus in the synagogues, that he is the Son of God. All who heard him were amazed and asked, "Is this not the man who in Jerusalem made havoc of those who called on this name?" But Saul grew stronger and confounded the Jews who lived at Damascus, proving that this is the Christ.
