Saul was yet breathing out threatenings of violence against the disciples of the Lord. He went unto the high priest and desired of him letters unto the synagogues at Damascus. If he found any of this way, whether they were men or women, he would bring them bound unto Jerusalem. He set forth upon the road with his companions, and the dust of the highway rose in pale clouds behind them.

Near unto Damascus, in the bright heat of noonday, a light from heaven shined round about him. It came suddenly, and Saul fell to the earth. The men which journeyed with him stood speechless, hearing the voice but seeing no man. Then a voice spake and called him by name. "Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?"

He answered with his face against the warm earth. "Who art thou, Lord?" The voice came back steady and unhurried. "I am Jesus whom thou persecutest. But arise, and go into the city, and it shall be told thee what thou must do."

When Saul arose from the earth he opened his eyes and could see no man. The light had taken his sight and left only darkness. They which were with him led him by the hand the rest of the way into Damascus, and three days he was without sight, and neither did eat nor drink.

In the same city dwelt a certain disciple named Ananias, and the Lord spake unto him in a vision and bade him go to the house of Judas in the street which is called Straight, and inquire for one named Saul of Tarsus. Ananias hesitated, for he had heard by many of this man and the harm he had done to the saints at Jerusalem. But the Lord bade him go, for Saul was a chosen vessel unto him, to bear his name before the Gentiles and the children of Israel.

Ananias went his way and entered into the house, and put his hands on Saul and said, "Brother Saul, the Lord, even Jesus, that appeared unto thee in the way as thou camest, hath sent me, that thou mightest receive thy sight, and be filled with the Holy Ghost." Immediately there fell from Saul's eyes as it had been scales, and he received sight forthwith, and arose, and was baptized. Then he took meat and was strengthened.

He tarried certain days with the disciples which were at Damascus, and straightway he preached Christ in the synagogues, that he is the Son of God. All that heard him were amazed, and said, "Is not this he that destroyed them which called on this name in Jerusalem?" But Saul increased the more in strength, and confounded the Jews which dwelt at Damascus, proving that this is very Christ.
