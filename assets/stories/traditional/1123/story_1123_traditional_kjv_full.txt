Saul rode out of Jerusalem yet breathing out threatenings and slaughter against the disciples of the Lord. The high priest's letters were folded within his cloak, sealed and addressed unto the synagogues of Damascus. If he found any of this way, men or women, he had authority to bind them and bring them bound unto Jerusalem. He moved at a steady pace along the road. His companions rode behind him, silent. The sun climbed.

Near unto midday he came nigh Damascus. The white walls of the city were visible across the plain. The road curved between low orchards and dry brush.

Then a light from heaven shined round about him.

It came suddenly. The light was brighter than the noonday sun. Saul fell to the earth. The horses jolted and shied, and the men which journeyed with him pulled up short. They stood speechless, hearing the sound of a voice, but seeing no man.

A voice came down to Saul where he lay. "Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?"

His face was against the warm dirt of the road. He answered up into the brightness. "Who art thou, Lord?"

The voice came back, unhurried. "I am Jesus whom thou persecutest. But arise, and go into the city, and it shall be told thee what thou must do."

Saul arose from the earth. When his eyes were opened, he saw no man. The brightness was gone, and so was the road, and so were the faces of his men. He stood in the midst of darkness. His hand reached out, found a sleeve, gripped it. The companions led him forward. They brought him by the hand into Damascus, into the house of Judas on the street called Straight. They laid him on a mat. Three days he was without sight, and neither did eat nor drink.

In that same city dwelt a certain disciple named Ananias.

The Lord spake unto him in a vision. "Ananias."

He answered, "Behold, I am here, Lord."

The Lord said, "Arise, and go into the street which is called Straight, and enquire in the house of Judas for one called Saul, of Tarsus. For, behold, he prayeth, and hath seen in a vision a man named Ananias coming in, and putting his hand on him, that he might receive his sight."

Ananias was yet on his knees. The name had reached him, and he rose not. "Lord, I have heard by many of this man, how much evil he hath done to thy saints at Jerusalem. And here he hath authority from the chief priests to bind all that call on thy name."

The Lord answered him. "Go thy way: for he is a chosen vessel unto me, to bear my name before the Gentiles, and kings, and the children of Israel. For I will shew him how great things he must suffer for my name's sake."

Ananias rose. He went out into the morning. He walked the street called Straight. He came unto the door of the house of Judas, and the disciples there let him in. Saul lay on the mat where his men had set him. His eyes were open and saw no man. His hands rested upon his chest. Three days of silence had passed over his face.

Ananias knelt beside him. He laid his hands on the bowed head. He spake, and his voice was steady in the quiet room.

"Brother Saul, the Lord, even Jesus, that appeared unto thee in the way as thou camest, hath sent me, that thou mightest receive thy sight, and be filled with the Holy Ghost."

Immediately there fell from his eyes as it had been scales. They came loose and fell away, and the light of the room reached him. He saw the beams of the ceiling. He saw the face above him. He saw the hands resting upon his head. He arose from the mat. They led him out and baptized him.

He took meat and was strengthened.

He tarried certain days with the disciples which were at Damascus. Then he went into the synagogues. He stood up among the men who had been waiting for the letters in his cloak. And straightway he preached Jesus, that he is the Son of God.

All that heard him were amazed. They turned one to another and said, "Is not this he that destroyed them which called on this name in Jerusalem? And he had come hither for that intent, that he might bring them bound unto the chief priests."

But Saul increased the more in strength. He stood among the disciples each morning. They walked beside him in the street called Straight, where Ananias had come unto him, and the brother who had once been an enemy went out among them in the open air of the city.
