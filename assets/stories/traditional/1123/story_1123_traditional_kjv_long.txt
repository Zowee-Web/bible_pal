Saul rode out of Jerusalem yet breathing out threatenings and slaughter against the disciples of the Lord. He had gone unto the high priest and desired of him letters unto the synagogues of Damascus. The letters were folded now within his cloak, sealed with the priest's mark. If he found any of this way, men or women, he had authority to bind them, and to bring them bound unto Jerusalem. The road ran north out of the city, climbed through the hill country, and dropped down toward the open plain. He set a steady pace. His companions rode behind him in silence. The dust of the highway rose in pale clouds behind them and settled on the brush at the road's edge.

At Damascus, in the houses of the disciples, word of his coming had already reached them. They sat in their rooms and prayed. They named him in their prayers. They moved not toward the road. The morning was warm.

Near unto midday Saul came nigh Damascus. The white walls of the city were visible across the plain. The road curved between low orchards and dry brush. A flock of birds lifted from a thorn tree as the riders passed.

Then a light from heaven shined round about him.

It came suddenly. The light was brighter than the noonday sun. It came down all around him at once and filled the road and the orchards and the air above the orchards. The horses jolted and shied. Saul fell from his place to the earth. His shoulder struck the ground. His face turned into the warm dirt. The men which journeyed with him pulled up short. They stood speechless, hearing the sound of a voice, but seeing no man.

A voice came down to him where he lay. "Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?"

He answered up into the brightness. The dust was in his mouth. "Who art thou, Lord?"

The voice came back, steady, unhurried. "I am Jesus whom thou persecutest. But arise, and go into the city, and it shall be told thee what thou must do."

The brightness lifted. The sun came back into the sky over the road, the orchards stood in their places, the birds had not returned to the thorn tree.

Saul arose from the earth. When his eyes were opened, he saw no man. He stood in the midst of darkness. He raised his hands before him. He turned slowly in a half circle. His companions spake not. He spake at last, quietly, into the silence. "Lead me."

A hand found his. The hand was a companion's. They led him forward step by step. They brought him by the hand into Damascus. They walked the long road unto the gate, and through the gate, and into the street called Straight, and to the door of the house of Judas. They laid him on a mat in an inner room. The men which had ridden with him stood in the courtyard with the letters in a leather pouch, and they knew not what to do with the letters now.

Three days Saul lay there. He neither did eat nor drink. His eyes were open in the darkness and saw no man. He prayed. The disciples of the city, which had heard he was within the house of Judas, came not unto the door.

In that same city dwelt a certain disciple named Ananias.

The Lord spake unto him in a vision. "Ananias."

He answered, "Behold, I am here, Lord."

The Lord said, "Arise, and go into the street which is called Straight, and enquire in the house of Judas for one called Saul, of Tarsus. For, behold, he prayeth, and hath seen in a vision a man named Ananias coming in, and putting his hand on him, that he might receive his sight."

Ananias was yet on his knees. The name had reached him, and he rose not. He answered into the room. "Lord, I have heard by many of this man, how much evil he hath done to thy saints at Jerusalem. And here he hath authority from the chief priests to bind all that call on thy name."

The room held the protest. The Lord answered him. "Go thy way: for he is a chosen vessel unto me, to bear my name before the Gentiles, and kings, and the children of Israel. For I will shew him how great things he must suffer for my name's sake."

Ananias rose. He stood a long moment in the room. Then he took his cloak and went out into the morning.

He walked the street called Straight. It was the longest street in Damascus, running clear across the city from wall to wall. Doorways opened to either side. Children sat at the thresholds. The street had been quiet three days where the house of Judas stood, and the door was closed when Ananias came unto it.

He knocked. They let him in. They led him through the front room into the inner room. Saul lay on the mat where his companions had set him three days before. His robes had not been changed. His eyes were open and saw no man. His hands rested upon his chest. Three days of silence had passed over his face, and his lips moved at the prayer that had not ceased.

Ananias knelt beside him.

He laid his hands on the bowed head. He spake, and his voice was steady in the quiet room.

"Brother Saul, the Lord, even Jesus, that appeared unto thee in the way as thou camest, hath sent me, that thou mightest receive thy sight, and be filled with the Holy Ghost."

Immediately there fell from his eyes as it had been scales. They came loose and fell away. Light reached him. He saw the beams of the ceiling. He saw the face above him. He saw the hands resting upon his head. He saw the brother who had come unto a man hated and called him brother.

He arose from the mat. They led him out of the house. They went down unto the water, and Ananias baptized him there. He took meat in the house afterward and was strengthened.

He tarried certain days with the disciples which were at Damascus. They taught him in the long mornings. They walked with him in the street called Straight. The men which had ridden with him from Jerusalem went away, and the letters from the high priest stayed folded in the leather pouch, and the pouch was set aside.

Then Saul went into the synagogues.

He stood up among the men who had been waiting for the letters in his cloak. He stood up where his name had been spoken with fear. He preached Jesus, that he is the Son of God.

All that heard him were amazed. They turned one to another. "Is not this he that destroyed them which called on this name in Jerusalem? And he had come hither for that intent, that he might bring them bound unto the chief priests."

But Saul increased the more in strength. He stood among them each morning. He confounded the Jews which dwelt at Damascus, proving that this is very Christ. The disciples walked beside him in the street called Straight, where Ananias had come unto him on the third day, and the brother who had once been an enemy went out among them in the open air of the city.
