Saul rode out of Jerusalem still breathing threats and slaughter against the disciples of the Lord. The high priest's letters were folded inside his cloak, sealed and addressed to the synagogues of Damascus. If he found any who belonged to the Way, men or women, he had authority to bind them and carry them back. He moved at a steady pace along the road. His companions rode behind him, silent. The sun climbed.

Near midday he came close to Damascus. The white walls of the city were visible across the plain. The road curved between low orchards and dry brush.

Then a light from the sky shone around him.

It came suddenly. The light was brighter than the noonday sun. Saul fell to the earth. The horses jolted and shied, and the men who traveled with him pulled up short. They stood speechless, hearing the sound of a voice, but seeing no one.

A voice came down to Saul where he lay. "Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?"

His face was against the warm dirt of the road. He answered up into the brightness. "Who are you, Lord?"

The voice came back, unhurried. "I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting. But rise up and enter into the city, and you will be told what you must do."

Saul arose from the ground. When his eyes were opened, he saw nothing. The brightness was gone, and so was the road, and so were the faces of his men. He stood in the middle of darkness. His hand reached out, found a sleeve, gripped it. The companions led him forward. They brought him by the hand into Damascus, into the house of Judas on the street called Straight. They laid him on a mat. For three days he was without sight, and he neither ate nor drank.

In that same city lived a disciple named Ananias.

The Lord spoke to him in a vision. "Ananias."

He answered, "Behold, it's me, Lord."

The Lord said, "Arise and go to the street which is called Straight, and inquire in the house of Judas for one named Saul, a man of Tarsus. For behold, he is praying, and in a vision he has seen a man named Ananias coming in and laying his hands on him, that he might receive his sight."

Ananias was still on his knees. The name had reached him, and he did not stand. "Lord, I have heard from many about this man, how much evil he did to your saints at Jerusalem. Here he has authority from the chief priests to bind all who call on your name."

The Lord answered him. "Go your way, for he is my chosen vessel to bear my name before the nations and kings, and the children of Israel. For I will show him how many things he must suffer for my name's sake."

Ananias rose. He went out into the morning. He walked the street called Straight. He came to the door of the house of Judas, and the disciples there let him in. Saul lay on the mat where his men had set him. His eyes were open and saw nothing. His hands rested on his chest. Three days of silence had passed over his face.

Ananias knelt beside him. He laid his hands on the bowed head. He spoke, and his voice was steady in the quiet room.

"Brother Saul, the Lord, who appeared to you on the road by which you came, has sent me that you may receive your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit."

Immediately something like scales fell from Saul's eyes. They came loose and fell away, and the light of the room reached him. He saw the beams of the ceiling. He saw the face above him. He saw the hands resting on his head. He rose up from the mat. They led him out and baptized him.

He took food and was strengthened.

He stayed several days with the disciples who were at Damascus. Then he went into the synagogues. He stood up among the men who had been waiting for the letters in his cloak. And he proclaimed Jesus, that he is the Son of God.

All who heard him were amazed. They turned to one another and asked, "Isn't this he who in Jerusalem made havoc of those who called on this name? And he had come here intending to bring them bound before the chief priests."

But Saul increased more in strength. He stood among the disciples each morning. They walked beside him in the street called Straight, where Ananias had come to him, and the brother who had once been an enemy went out among them in the open air of the city.
