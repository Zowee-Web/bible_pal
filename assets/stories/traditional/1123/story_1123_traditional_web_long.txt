Saul rode out of Jerusalem still breathing threats and slaughter against the disciples of the Lord. He had gone to the high priest and asked for letters to the synagogues of Damascus. The letters were folded now inside his cloak, sealed with the priest's mark. If he found any who belonged to the Way, men or women, he had authority to bind them and to bring them back. The road ran north out of the city, climbed through the hill country, and dropped down toward the open plain. He set a steady pace. His companions rode behind him in silence. The dust of the highway rose in pale clouds behind them and settled on the brush at the road's edge.

At Damascus, in the houses of the disciples, word of his coming had already reached them. They sat in their rooms and prayed. They named him in their prayers. They did not move toward the road. The morning was warm.

Near midday Saul came close to Damascus. The white walls of the city were visible across the plain. The road curved between low orchards and dry brush. A flock of birds lifted from a thorn tree as the riders passed.

Then a light from the sky shone around him.

It came suddenly. The light was brighter than the noonday sun. It came down all around him at once and filled the road and the orchards and the air above the orchards. The horses jolted and shied. Saul fell from his place to the earth. His shoulder struck the ground. His face turned into the warm dirt. The men who traveled with him pulled up short. They stood speechless, hearing the sound of a voice, but seeing no one.

A voice came down to him where he lay. "Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?"

He answered up into the brightness. The dust was in his mouth. "Who are you, Lord?"

The voice came back, steady, unhurried. "I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting. But rise up and enter into the city, and you will be told what you must do."

The brightness lifted. The sun came back into the sky over the road, the orchards stood in their places, the birds had not returned to the thorn tree.

Saul arose from the ground. When his eyes were opened, he saw nothing. He stood in the middle of darkness. He raised his hands in front of him. He turned slowly in a half circle. His companions did not speak. He spoke at last, quietly, into the silence. "Lead me."

A hand found his. The hand was a companion's. They led him forward step by step. They brought him by the hand into Damascus. They walked the long road into the gate, and through the gate, and into the street called Straight, and to the door of the house of Judas. They laid him on a mat in an inner room. The men who had ridden with him stood in the courtyard with the letters in a leather pouch, and they did not know what to do with the letters now.

For three days Saul lay there. He neither ate nor drank. His eyes were open in the darkness and saw nothing. He prayed. The disciples of the city, who had heard he was inside the house of Judas, did not come to the door.

In that same city lived a disciple named Ananias.

The Lord spoke to him in a vision. "Ananias."

He answered, "Behold, it's me, Lord."

The Lord said, "Arise and go to the street which is called Straight, and inquire in the house of Judas for one named Saul, a man of Tarsus. For behold, he is praying, and in a vision he has seen a man named Ananias coming in and laying his hands on him, that he might receive his sight."

Ananias was still on his knees. The name had reached him, and he did not stand. He answered into the room. "Lord, I have heard from many about this man, how much evil he did to your saints at Jerusalem. Here he has authority from the chief priests to bind all who call on your name."

The room held the protest. The Lord answered him. "Go your way, for he is my chosen vessel to bear my name before the nations and kings, and the children of Israel. For I will show him how many things he must suffer for my name's sake."

Ananias rose. He stood for a long moment in the room. Then he took his cloak and went out into the morning.

He walked the street called Straight. It was the longest street in Damascus, running clear across the city wall to wall. Doorways opened to either side. Children sat at the thresholds. The street had been quiet for three days where the house of Judas stood, and the door was closed when Ananias came to it.

He knocked. They let him in. They led him through the front room into the inner room. Saul lay on the mat where his companions had set him three days before. His robes had not been changed. His eyes were open and saw nothing. His hands rested on his chest. Three days of silence had passed over his face, and his lips moved at the prayer that had not stopped.

Ananias knelt beside him.

He laid his hands on the bowed head. He spoke, and his voice was steady in the quiet room.

"Brother Saul, the Lord, who appeared to you on the road by which you came, has sent me that you may receive your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit."

Immediately something like scales fell from Saul's eyes. They came loose and fell away. Light reached him. He saw the beams of the ceiling. He saw the face above him. He saw the hands resting on his head. He saw the brother who had come to a man hated and called him brother.

He rose up from the mat. They led him out of the house. They went down to the water, and Ananias baptized him there. He took food in the house afterward and was strengthened.

He stayed several days with the disciples who were at Damascus. They taught him in the long mornings. They walked with him in the street called Straight. The men who had ridden with him from Jerusalem went away, and the letters from the high priest stayed folded in the leather pouch, and the pouch was set aside.

Then Saul went into the synagogues.

He stood up among the men who had been waiting for the letters in his cloak. He stood up where his name had been spoken with fear. He proclaimed Jesus, that he is the Son of God.

All who heard him were amazed. They turned to one another. "Isn't this he who in Jerusalem made havoc of those who called on this name? And he had come here intending to bring them bound before the chief priests."

But Saul increased more in strength. He stood among them each morning. He confounded the Jews who lived at Damascus, proving that this is the Christ. The disciples walked beside him in the street called Straight, where Ananias had come to him on the third day, and the brother who had once been an enemy went out among them in the open air of the city.
