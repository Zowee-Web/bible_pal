In the days when the judges ruled, a famine came over the land. A man from Bethlehem in Judah set out, with his wife and two sons, to live in the country of Moab. The man’s name was Elimelech. His wife’s name was Naomi. Their two sons were Mahlon and Chilion, Ephrathites from Bethlehem. They settled in Moab to escape the famine.

But tragedy came. Elimelech died, leaving Naomi with her two sons. The young men married Moabite women—one named Orpah, and the other named Ruth. Ten years passed. Then, both Mahlon and Chilion died also. Naomi was left alone, without her husband or her sons.

News reached Naomi in Moab that the Lord had visited His people back in Judah, giving them food. She rose to return home. Ruth and Orpah, her daughters-in-law, set out with her on the dusty road. The sun pressed hot on their backs, the hills of Moab behind them, and ahead stretched the road to Judah.

Naomi paused. She turned to her daughters-in-law. “Go, return each of you to your mother’s house. May the Lord deal kindly with you, as you have dealt with the dead and with me. May the Lord grant that you may find rest, each of you in the house of her husband.” She kissed them. Grief welled. All three wept together by the roadside.

Orpah and Ruth protested. “No, we will return with you to your people.”

But Naomi insisted. “Turn back, my daughters. Why will you go with me? Do I have sons in my womb, that they could be your husbands? Return, my daughters, go your way. I am too old to have a husband. And even if I thought there was hope, if I had a husband tonight and bore sons, would you wait for them to grow? No, my daughters, it is more bitter for me than for you, for the Lord’s hand has gone out against me.”

Again, they wept. With tears, Orpah kissed her mother-in-law goodbye and turned back to Moab. But Ruth clung to Naomi tightly.

Naomi said, “See, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people, and to her gods. Return after your sister-in-law.”

But Ruth replied, her voice trembling with conviction: “Don’t urge me to leave you, or to turn back from following you. For where you go, I will go. Where you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my people, and your God my God. Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May the Lord deal with me, be it ever so severely, if anything but death separates you and me.”

When Naomi saw Ruth’s steadfastness, she stopped urging her. Together, the two women walked on toward Bethlehem.

They entered Bethlehem, and the whole town stirred at their arrival. The women of the city murmured, “Is this Naomi?”

She replied, “Don’t call me Naomi. Call me Mara, for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me. I went out full, and the Lord has brought me back empty. Why do you call me Naomi, since the Lord has testified against me and the Almighty has afflicted me?”

So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess, her daughter-in-law, with her, who returned out of the country of Moab. They came to Bethlehem in the beginning of barley harvest.
