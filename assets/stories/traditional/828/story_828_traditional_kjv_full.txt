In the days when the judges ruled over Israel, there came a famine in the land. A man of Bethlehemjudah took his wife and his two sons, and went to sojourn in the country of Moab. The man's name was Elimelech, his wife's name was Naomi, and his sons were Mahlon and Chilion. They forsook their home and crossed distant hills, seeking refuge in a strange field.

In Moab, sorrow came upon them. Elimelech died, and left Naomi a widow with her sons. The young men took them wives of the women of Moab — the name of the one was Orpah, and the name of the other Ruth. Together, the household continued there about ten years. But grief was not yet finished, for Mahlon and Chilion died also, and Naomi was left of her husband and her two sons. Only she and her two daughters in law, Ruth and Orpah, remained. Three widows now stood against an uncertain morrow, with the hardness of famine yet upon their thoughts.

Tidings came unto Naomi in Moab that the Lord had visited his people, in giving them bread in Bethlehem. With hope stirring, Naomi made ready to return unto her own country. She went forth out of Moab, her two daughters in law following along the dusty way, the hills rising afar off. The way was hard, and the weight of loss hung heavy upon every footstep.

As they went, Naomi stood still. She turned unto her daughters in law, and said, "Go, return each unto her mother's house: the Lord deal kindly with you, as ye have dealt with the dead, and with me. The Lord grant you that ye may find rest, each of you in the house of her husband." Tears glistened as she kissed them, and they lifted up their voice and wept together, the ache of parting swelling in the silence of the way.

Orpah and Ruth made answer, "Surely we will return with thee unto thy people." But Naomi pressed them, her voice weary, yet tender: "Turn again, my daughters: why will ye go with me? Are there yet any more sons in my womb, that they may be your husbands? Turn again, my daughters, go your way; for I am too old to have an husband. If I should say, I have hope, if I should have an husband also to night, and should also bear sons; would ye tarry for them till they were grown? would ye stay for them from having husbands? Nay, my daughters; for it grieveth me much for your sakes that the hand of the Lord is gone out against me."

Again the women lifted up their voice, and wept together. Orpah lingered, sorrow and love mingling in her tears. At length, she kissed her mother in law, and held her one moment longer. With a heavy heart, Orpah turned back toward Moab, unto the land and kindred she had known. But Ruth clave unto Naomi. She would not let her go.

Naomi spake yet again, that she might persuade Ruth. "Behold, thy sister in law is gone back unto her people, and unto her gods: return thou after thy sister in law."

But Ruth made answer, stedfast and unmoved. She looked upon Naomi and said, "Intreat me not to leave thee, or to return from following after thee: for whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God: where thou diest, will I die, and there will I be buried: the Lord do so to me, and more also, if ought but death part thee and me."

When Naomi beheld that Ruth was stedfastly minded to go with her, then she left speaking unto her. The two women went on together, leaving the place of lamentation behind them, step by step toward Bethlehem. The ways were empty, their feet stirring dust into the morning air, the fields before them lying ripe for harvest.

At length, they came to Bethlehem. Their coming made a stir throughout all the city. The women of the city beheld them with wonder, whispering one to another, "Is this Naomi?" She said unto them, "Call me not Naomi, call me Mara: for the Almighty hath dealt very bitterly with me. I went out full, and the Lord hath brought me home again empty: why then call ye me Naomi, seeing the Lord hath testified against me, and the Almighty hath afflicted me?"

Through the dusty streets, Naomi and Ruth went on, the sorrows of the past mingling with new beginnings. So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess, her daughter in law, with her, which returned out of the country of Moab: and they came to Bethlehem in the beginning of barley harvest.
