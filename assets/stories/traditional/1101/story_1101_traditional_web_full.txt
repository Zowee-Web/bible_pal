Bartimaeus sat at the edge of the highway outside Jericho, near the place where the road passed through the city gate. He was blind. He had been sitting in this spot for years — long enough that the stones beneath him were familiar by shape, long enough that he could tell the time of day by the heat on his face and the changing sounds of the gate behind him. His cloak was spread under him on the packed earth. A wooden bowl sat beside his knee, a few small coins resting in the bottom.

The morning was already hot. Flies moved in slow circles near his face. He could hear the road as it lived around him — sandals on packed dirt, the grunting of donkeys under load, the voices of women carrying water jars, the distant shout of a merchant. He sat with his hands on his knees, his head slightly turned, listening. When a footstep came close, he called out. Sometimes a coin landed in the bowl with a small clink. More often, the footsteps passed without slowing.

Then a different sound came from the direction of the city. A crowd. Many feet pressing together. Voices overlapping — excited, close. The ground seemed to carry the vibration of their steps. Dust rose and thickened in the air. It settled on his lips and tongue.

He turned his head. "What is happening?" he asked.

A man's voice answered from nearby. "Jesus of Nazareth is passing by."

Bartimaeus drew a breath and cried out. His voice was not quiet. It rose above the crowd noise, sharp and clear in the dry air. "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!"

People near him turned. Hands pressed toward him. Voices spoke firmly. "Be quiet." "Stop shouting." They rebuked him — not once but several times, their voices overlapping.

He did not stop. He drew another breath and cried out louder, the cords of his neck straining. "Son of David, have mercy on me!"

The crowd's movement slowed. Something shifted in the press of bodies ahead. Jesus had stopped. He was standing still in the road.

"Call him," Jesus said.

The words moved back through the crowd. People near Bartimaeus changed their tone. "Take courage," they said now. "Get up. He is calling you."

Bartimaeus threw aside his cloak. It fell behind him, crumpled in the dust. He pushed himself up from the ground, both hands flat against the dirt, and stood. He moved forward — his bare feet finding the packed road. The crowd parted. Hands touched his arms, guiding him. He could hear the space opening ahead, voices falling quiet.

He stood before Jesus. The crowd pressed in around them but held back. The voices fell still.

"What do you want me to do for you?" Jesus said.

Bartimaeus answered without hesitation. "Rabboni, that I may see again."

Jesus said to him, "Go your way. Your faith has made you well."

Immediately Bartimaeus received his sight. Brightness flooded in — white, overwhelming. He shut his eyes against it. He opened them again slowly. Shapes formed. The pale road. Dark figures standing on it. Color came next — the brown of robes, the gold of the city wall beyond them, the blue of the sky overhead. He blinked. His eyes watered. He looked at the man standing before him — Jesus, plain-robed, the dust of the road on his sandals.

Bartimaeus lowered his gaze to his own hands. He turned them over. The lines of his palms, the dirt beneath his nails, the rough skin of his knuckles — all visible. Faces came into focus around him. Beyond them, the road stretched south, lined with low stone walls and fig trees.

Then Jesus turned and continued walking. The crowd gathered around him and moved forward along the road. Bartimaeus left his cloak where it lay in the dust beside the city gate and walked after them, his feet bare on the warm ground, the road ahead of him open and clear.
