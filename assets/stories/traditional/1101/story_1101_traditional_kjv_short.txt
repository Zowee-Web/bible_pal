Bartimaeus sat beside the highway outside Jericho. He was blind. His cloak lay spread beneath him upon the packed earth, and his hands rested upon his knees. The sun was hot above. Flies moved about his head. He could hear the road — the scraping of sandals, the breathing of beasts of burden, the creak of wheels, voices passing. He called out to those who went by, asking alms. Some cast copper near his feet. Most passed by.

Then a sound came from the direction of the city gate. Many feet, many voices. The noise swelled — not the common traffic of merchants and pilgrims but a multitude pressing together. Dust rose thick in the air. Bartimaeus felt it upon his face.

He turned his head. "What is this?" he said.

One near him answered. "Jesus of Nazareth passeth by."

Bartimaeus opened his mouth and cried out, his voice lifting above the din. "Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me!"

Those about him turned. "Hold thy peace," they said. They charged him that he should be quiet.

He cried out the more, louder still. "Thou Son of David, have mercy on me!"

Jesus stood still in the way. The crowd about him ceased their pressing. "Call him," he said.

They called the blind man. "Be of good comfort," they said. "Rise; he calleth thee."

Bartimaeus cast away his garment. It fell behind him in the dust. He rose — swiftly, pressing both hands against the ground — and came toward Jesus, his bare feet upon the way, the crowd making way before him.

He stood before him. The way was still.

Jesus said unto him, "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?"

The blind man answered. "Lord, that I might receive my sight."

Jesus said unto him, "Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole."

And immediately he received his sight. Light came upon him — the road, the dust, the faces gathered round, the sky above white with heat. He opened his eyes wide. He looked upon the man before him. He looked upon the way stretching toward the distant hills. He looked down at his own hands.

Then he followed Jesus in the way. The crowd moved forward, and Bartimaeus walked among them, his garment left behind upon the road.
