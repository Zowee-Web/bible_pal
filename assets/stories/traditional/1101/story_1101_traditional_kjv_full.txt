Bartimaeus sat beside the highway outside Jericho, near the place where the road passed through the city gate. He was blind. He had sat in that place many years — long enough that the stones beneath him were known by their shape, long enough that he could mark the passing of the sun's shadow without seeing it. His cloak was spread upon the earth. A small vessel sat beside his knee, a few coins within.

The morning was hot. Flies moved in slow turns about his face. He heard the road as it moved around him — sandals upon the dust, the breathing of beasts of burden, the voices of women bearing water jars, the cry of a merchant in the distance. He sat with his hands upon his knees, his head turned and listening. When a step drew near, he called out. Sometimes a coin fell into the vessel. More often the steps passed by.

Then a different sound arose from the city. A great multitude pressing together. Voices overlapping — eager, close. The earth carried the weight of their coming. Dust rose thick in the air. Bartimaeus felt it upon his face.

He turned his head. "What is this?" he said.

A voice answered from nearby. "Jesus of Nazareth passeth by."

Bartimaeus drew breath and cried out, his voice rising above the noise of the multitude. "Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me!"

Those near him turned. Hands pressed toward him. "Hold thy peace," they said. "Be still." Many charged him that he should be quiet.

But he would not cease. He cried out the more, a great deal, the cords of his neck drawn tight. "Thou Son of David, have mercy on me!"

The movement of the crowd slowed. A change passed through the press of bodies. Jesus stood still in the way.

"Call him," he said.

The word passed back through the people. Those nearest Bartimaeus changed their speech. "Be of good comfort," they said. "Rise; he calleth thee."

Bartimaeus cast away his garment. It fell behind him upon the ground. He pressed both hands flat and rose quickly. He came toward Jesus, his bare feet finding the way, the crowd parting before him. Hands touched his arms as he passed. The voices fell quiet.

He stood before him. The way was still.

Jesus said unto him, "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?"

Bartimaeus answered without delay. "Lord, that I might receive my sight."

Jesus said unto him, "Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole."

And immediately he received his sight. Light came upon him — sudden and full. The world opened before him. He saw the way, pale and dusty beneath the sun. He saw the crowd — faces, garments, sandals, hands. He saw the city wall rising behind them, the stones golden and uneven. He saw the sky, wide above him. He blinked against the brightness.

He looked upon the man who stood before him — Jesus, plainly robed, the dust of the way upon his sandals.

He lowered his gaze to his own hands and turned them over. Faces came into focus around him. Beyond them, the way stretched southward, bordered with low walls and fig trees.

Then Jesus turned and went forward upon the way. The multitude gathered about him. Bartimaeus left his garment where it lay in the dust beside the gate and followed after him, his feet upon the warm ground, the way before him open and clear.
