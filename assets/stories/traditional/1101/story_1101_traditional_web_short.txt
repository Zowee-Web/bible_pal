Bartimaeus sat at the edge of the highway outside Jericho. He was blind. His cloak was spread beneath him on the packed dirt, and his hands rested on his knees. The sun pressed down on his head and shoulders. Flies circled in the heat. He could hear the road — sandals scraping past, donkeys breathing under their loads, the creak of cart wheels, voices passing in both directions. He called out to those who came near, asking for coins. Some dropped copper into the dust beside his feet. Most passed by without slowing.

Then a sound rose from the direction of the city gate. Many feet moving together. Many voices overlapping. The noise grew — not the ordinary traffic of merchants, but a crowd pressing close, their footsteps running together. Dust thickened in the air. Bartimaeus felt it against his face, fine and dry.

He turned his head toward the sound. "What is happening?" he asked.

Someone near him answered. "Jesus of Nazareth is passing by."

Bartimaeus drew breath and cried out. His voice cut across the noise of the crowd. "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!"

People near him turned. Several spoke sharply. "Be quiet," they said. They told him to stop.

He cried out again, louder than before. "Son of David, have mercy on me!"

Jesus stopped. He stood still in the road. The crowd around him slowed and pressed together. "Call him," Jesus said.

They called the blind man. "Take courage," they said. "Get up. He is calling you."

Bartimaeus threw off his cloak. It fell behind him in the dirt. He stood — pushing himself up from the ground with both hands — and moved toward the voice. People stepped aside. His bare feet found the packed road.

He stood before Jesus. The crowd was quiet around them.

"What do you want me to do for you?" Jesus said.

The blind man answered. "Rabboni, that I may see again."

Jesus said to him, "Go your way. Your faith has made you well."

Immediately Bartimaeus received his sight. Light flooded in — the road, the dust, the faces of the crowd around him, the sky overhead. He blinked. He looked at the man standing before him. He looked at the road stretching toward the hills. He looked down at his own hands.

Then he followed Jesus on the way. The crowd moved forward, and Bartimaeus walked among them, his cloak left behind in the dust at the side of the road.
