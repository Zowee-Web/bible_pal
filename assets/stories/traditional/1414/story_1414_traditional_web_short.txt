The fire had burned down on the altar. The ram caught by its horns in the thicket had been taken and offered up in the place of the son. Isaac stood beside his father unbound. Abraham looked at the place where the altar had been built — the altar he had made of stone with his own hands, in the country that Yahweh had shown him on the third day of the journey from Beersheba — and he gave the place a name.

Abraham called the name of that place Yahweh Will Provide. As it is said to this day, "On Yahweh's mountain, it will be provided."

Yahweh's angel called to Abraham a second time out of the sky,

and said, "I have sworn by myself, says Yahweh, because you have done this thing, and have not withheld your son, your only son,

that I will bless you greatly, and I will multiply your offspring greatly like the stars of the heavens, and like the sand which is on the seashore. Your offspring will possess the gate of his enemies.

All the nations of the earth will be blessed by your offspring, because you have obeyed my voice."

So Abraham returned to his young men, and they rose up and went together to Beersheba. Abraham lived at Beersheba.

And the father and son went down the mountain together to the young men who had been waiting with the donkey at the base. The road back to Beersheba opened ahead of them; the wells of Abraham's sojourning waited along the country; and the two of them walked back to their people in the long afternoon, with the name "Yahweh Will Provide" left in the place behind them on the mountain.
