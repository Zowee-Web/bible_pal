Paul and his company had come up by ship from Patara unto Tyre, had tarried seven days with the disciples there — which said unto him through the Spirit that he should not set foot in Jerusalem — and then had sailed on unto Ptolemais and walked down unto Caesarea. They abode in the house of Philip the evangelist, which had four daughters, virgins, which did prophesy. And then a prophet came down from Judaea.

And as we tarried there many days, there came down from Judaea a certain prophet, named Agabus.

And when he was come unto us, he took Paul's girdle, and bound his own hands and feet, and said, "Thus saith the Holy Ghost, 'So shall the Jews at Jerusalem bind the man that owneth this girdle, and shall deliver him into the hands of the Gentiles.'"

And when we heard these things, both we, and they of that place, besought him not to go up to Jerusalem.

Then Paul answered, "What mean ye to weep and to break mine heart? for I am ready not to be bound only, but also to die at Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus."

And when he would not be persuaded, we ceased, saying, "The will of the Lord be done."

And the company tarried at the house of Philip in Caesarea a few days more. The girdle which the prophet had taken was given back unto Paul; the way up unto Jerusalem waited at the city gate; and the company that had besought him not to go gathered itself for the journey, saying among themselves the words they had spoken at the end — the will of the Lord be done — and going on with him into Judaea.
