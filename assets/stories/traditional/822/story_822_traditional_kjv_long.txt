The evening descended softly upon the house of the Lord at Shiloh. The last traces of sunlight faded, and the glow from the lamp of God flickered upon the golden walls, filling the tabernacle with its gentle, warm light. Shiloh, quiet and still, rested beneath a sky sprinkled with stars. Within the sacred place, Eli the priest, now old and weary, found his place for the night, his eyes dim and heavy with age. Not far from him, young Samuel lay resting, his small form close beside the holy ark. Samuel ministered unto the Lord faithfully, brought as a child to dwell before God under the care of Eli, his every day shaped by the rituals and reverence of this sacred dwelling.

The hush of night settled round about them. In that silent stillness, the only sounds were the faint crackling of the oil lamp and the occasional soft movement of the old priest stirring in his sleep. Samuel's breathing grew slow and deep as he closed his eyes in the warmth of the tabernacle, comforted by the familiar peace of the house of God. Without, the world was shrouded in darkness and calm, yet within, a gentle expectancy seemed to linger, a sense that something unseen hovered nigh, near unto the treasured ark.

As Samuel rested, a voice brake through the stillness, calling his name with quiet clarity. "Samuel." The boy awoke, startled but attentive. He hearkened for a moment, uncertain of what he had heard. Rising quickly, he went straightway unto Eli, the old priest roused from his shallow sleep by the approach of footsteps. "Here am I," said Samuel. "For thou calledst me." But Eli, slow with age and unaware of the voice, shook his head. "I called not," he answered gently. "Lie down again." Samuel obeyed, returning unto his place and wrapping himself once more in the hush and lamplight.

A little while after, as the night deepened and shadows shifted softly upon the ageless stones, the voice called again. "Samuel." Again, Samuel arose, stepping softly across the darkened room. He drew near unto Eli, repeating the words he had spoken before: "Here am I; for thou didst call me." But Eli's voice was the same, marked by confusion and weariness: "I called not, my son. Lie down again." Samuel obeyed, lying down once more, but the stillness now seemed to hang with a new weight. The voice had come twice, clear and unmistakable. Yet Samuel did not yet know the Lord, neither was the word of the Lord yet revealed unto him.

Silence enveloped the tabernacle again, broken only by the soft light of the lamp that had not yet gone out, casting steady illumination upon the floor of stone and the golden furniture. The sacred place held its breath. Then a third time, the voice called: "Samuel." The boy arose without hesitation, his heart perhaps quickening but his trust unwavering. He went unto Eli, as he had done before. "Here am I; for thou didst call me."

This time, as his words fell upon Eli's ears, a dawning understanding grew in the old priest's mind. Eli hearkened to the repetition and the earnestness in Samuel's tone. A remembrance stirred within him, a sense of something holy now moving in their midst. His thoughts reached back unto times when God had spoken to his servants of old, and understanding dawned. He beheld tenderly the young boy standing before him, humble and willing. Eli said unto Samuel, "Go, lie down: and it shall be, if he call thee, that thou shalt say, Speak, Lord; for thy servant heareth." Eli's eyes shone in the lamplight with understanding and gravity, for he knew at last that it was the Lord who called Samuel.

Samuel returned unto his place once more upon the quiet floor of the house of God, the words of Eli echoing in his mind. He lay in the stillness, his eyes open and alert, his heart attuned for the call. The silence of the night pressed gently upon him, broken only by the steady breathing of the old priest nigh him and the unwavering flame of the lamp. Then the Lord came, and stood, and called as at the other times, "Samuel! Samuel!" There was a nearness and a presence in the darkness, gentle but commanding. Drawing in a breath, Samuel did as he had been taught. "Speak; for thy servant heareth," he answered softly.

Then the Lord spake unto Samuel, his words solemn and full of purpose. God told Samuel that he was about to do a thing in Israel, at which both the ears of every one that heareth it should tingle. The Lord revealed unto Samuel all that should come upon Eli and his house, because the sons of Eli were become corrupt and Eli restrained them not. The Lord declared that the iniquity of Eli's house should not be purged with sacrifice nor offering for ever. Samuel hearkened, his young heart quiet beneath the burden of this message from God. The night deepened, shadows stretched along the floor, and the words of God lingered in the air, heavy with meaning and solemnity.

In the hush before the dawn, Samuel lay until morning, still and hesitant to shew the vision. The gentle glow of morning touched the sacred lamps, and Samuel arose to begin his tasks. As ever, he opened the doors of the house of the Lord, letting in the new light of day. Eli called unto him, his voice gentle but insistent, "Samuel, my son." Samuel answered, "Here am I." Eli beckoned him near. "What is the thing that the Lord hath said unto thee? I pray thee, hide it not from me: God do so to thee, and more also, if thou hide any thing from me of all the things that he said unto thee."

Thus Samuel told Eli every whit, hiding nothing from him, even though the weight of the vision was great. Eli received the message in quiet acceptance, knowing that the word had come from God. "It is the Lord," said Eli. "Let him do what seemeth him good." In those words, he yielded once again unto the purposes of the Most High, even in the face of judgment.

From that day forward, Samuel grew, and the Lord was with him, and did let none of his words fall to the ground. All Israel, from Dan even to Beersheba, came to know that Samuel was established to be a prophet of the Lord. In the years that followed, the Lord continued to appear in Shiloh, revealing himself unto Samuel by his word. The lamp of God still shone in the quiet hush of the house at Shiloh, its gentle glow reflecting the steady presence of God, who spake in the stillness unto them that hearkened.

As the days passed, the tale of Samuel and the word he received quietly spread among the people. Pilgrims that came to offer sacrifice at Shiloh brought with them tidings from distant places, and slowly a new reverence grew for the boy who had once swept the floors and tended the lamps. Samuel himself moved through each day carefully, honouring Eli, ministering faithfully, yet bearing within him the remembrance of the voice that had come in the night. He set his hand to every task with more purpose, knowing that hearkening was now woven into his service as surely as prayer and offering.

Eli beheld Samuel with affection and sorrow in his old eyes. He continued to teach the boy what he was able, guiding him in the ways of the Lord and the service of the tabernacle. There were quiet evenings when they sat together beneath the soft lamplight, sharing stories of Israel of old and songs of praise. The sons of Eli came and went, but their hearts remained far from the ways of God, and Samuel learned more by Eli's patience and grief than by their example.

The seasons turned, each feast passing in its course until the voices of children running in the courts mingled with the chanting of the priests. Amid it all, Samuel remained stedfast. Sometimes, late in the night, he would sit alone in the shadowed chamber, hearkening, ever hearkening, for the voice that had once called him. And so the lamp of God continued to burn, steady and sure, in the house at Shiloh.
