In the days when young Samuel served before the Lord under Eli, the word of the Lord was rare. There were few visions, and the nights in Shiloh drifted softly and silent, shadowed under the hush of the tabernacle’s veil. Lamps burned gently, casting warm pools of light that touched wooden walls and stone floors, while Eli, the aged priest whose eyes had grown dim, lay in his usual place. Not far from him, Samuel rested within the sanctuary itself, near to the ark of God, the precious lamp of God flickering nearby.

It was a time of stillness, and throughout the holy places, only the quiet sounds of breath and gentle movement broke the silence. Suddenly, into the hush, a voice called out. “Samuel!” The boy stirred, uncertain, but the call was clear. Samuel rose and hurried to Eli’s side, gentle feet padding across the stone. He said, “Here I am; for you called me.” But Eli, heavy with age and sleep, replied, “I did not call; lie down again.” Samuel returned to his resting place. Outside, the night held its peace, and inside, the lamp burned on.

Once again, the voice broke through. “Samuel!” The boy woke and went to Eli, saying, “Here I am, for you called me.” But Eli’s answer was the same as before, “I did not call, my son; lie down again.” Samuel obeyed, lying quietly in his place, his heart perhaps wondering, but not yet understanding. Samuel did not know the Lord yet—the word of the Lord had not yet been revealed to him.

A third time, the call sounded, just as it had before. “Samuel!” Again, Samuel rose and went to Eli and said, “Here I am, for you called me.” This time, the old priest understood something deeper was happening. Eli realized that it was the Lord who was calling the boy. Sitting up, Eli gave Samuel new instructions, his voice calm and steady. “Go, lie down; and it shall be, if He calls you, that you shall say, ‘Speak, Lord, for your servant hears.’” Samuel returned to his bed, the words of Eli resting in his mind, and the quiet of the tabernacle settling once again.

As Samuel lay where he had been, still and listening in the gentle darkness, the Lord came and stood, calling as at the other times, “Samuel! Samuel!” This time, the boy answered as he had been told, “Speak, for your servant hears.” In the soft and holy quiet, the Lord spoke to Samuel, saying that He was about to do something in Israel that would cause the ears of all who heard it to tingle. The Lord told Samuel about judgment against Eli’s house because of the sins Eli’s sons had committed and because Eli had not restrained them. The Lord declared that the guilt of Eli’s house would not be purged by sacrifice or offering forever.

Morning came, and the soft light crept over Shiloh. Samuel opened the doors to the house of the Lord, but he was afraid to tell Eli about the vision. Yet Eli called to him, “Samuel, my son.” Samuel answered, “Here I am.” Eli said, “What is the thing that He has spoken to you? Please do not hide it from me. May God do so to you, and more also, if you hide anything from me of all the things that He spoke to you.” So Samuel told him everything and hid nothing. Eli listened and said, “It is the Lord. Let Him do what seems good to Him.”

So Samuel grew, and the Lord was with him, and let none of his words fall to the ground. All Israel, from Dan to Beersheba, knew that Samuel was established to be a prophet of the Lord. The Lord appeared again in Shiloh, for the Lord revealed Himself to Samuel at Shiloh by the word of the Lord.

As days and seasons passed in Shiloh, Samuel moved among the people quietly, learning to listen for God’s voice in ways both loud and soft. The elders came to him with troubled questions, and mothers brought their children to the tabernacle, trusting that the one who heard the Lord might carry their prayers. There was longing in the land, hope like frail green shoots, as people spoke of Samuel with respect and curiosity. Sometimes Samuel paused by the old lamp as dusk fell, feeling the hush of evening settle on his shoulders—a gentle memory of the night the Lord called him.

Eli watched Samuel with a tenderness that did not show in his face, but shone in his careful words. Though his steps grew slower, Eli offered counsel for as long as he could, encouraging Samuel to be faithful, to love mercy, and to walk humbly. The old priest’s heart was heavy for his sons, but he trusted Samuel’s calling, and sometimes he found a measure of peace looking upon the boy who listened and always answered. The days carried Eli gently forward, and the people of Shiloh began to see that change had come, subtle and quiet, but unmistakable.
