In the days when young Samuel ministered unto the Lord before Eli, the word of the Lord was precious. There were few visions, and the nights at Shiloh drifted softly and silent, shadowed beneath the hush of the tabernacle's veil. Lamps burned gently, casting warm pools of light that touched the wooden walls and the floors of stone, while Eli, the aged priest whose eyes had waxed dim, lay in his accustomed place. Not far from him, Samuel rested within the sanctuary itself, near unto the ark of God, the precious lamp of God flickering nigh.

It was a time of stillness, and throughout the holy places, only the quiet sounds of breath and gentle movement brake the silence. And it came to pass, that into the hush, a voice called out. "Samuel!" The boy stirred, uncertain, but the call was clear. Samuel arose and hasted unto Eli's side, his gentle feet padding across the stone. He said, "Here am I; for thou calledst me." But Eli, heavy with age and sleep, answered, "I called not; lie down again." Samuel returned unto his resting place. Without, the night held its peace, and within, the lamp burned on.

And the Lord called yet again, "Samuel!" The boy awoke and went unto Eli, saying, "Here am I; for thou didst call me." But Eli's answer was the same as before, "I called not, my son; lie down again." Samuel obeyed, lying down quietly in his place, his heart perhaps wondering, yet not yet understanding. Samuel did not yet know the Lord, neither was the word of the Lord yet revealed unto him.

A third time, the call sounded, even as before. "Samuel!" Again, Samuel arose and went unto Eli and said, "Here am I; for thou didst call me." This time, the old priest perceived that something deeper was come to pass. Eli understood that the Lord had called the child. Sitting up, Eli gave Samuel new instructions, his voice calm and steady. "Go, lie down: and it shall be, if he call thee, that thou shalt say, Speak, Lord; for thy servant heareth." Samuel returned unto his bed, the words of Eli resting in his mind, and the quiet of the tabernacle settling once again.

As Samuel lay where he had been, still and hearkening in the gentle darkness, the Lord came, and stood, and called as at other times, "Samuel! Samuel!" This time, the boy answered as he had been taught, "Speak; for thy servant heareth." In the soft and holy quiet, the Lord spake unto Samuel, saying that he was about to do a thing in Israel, at which both the ears of every one that heareth it should tingle. The Lord told Samuel of judgment against the house of Eli, because of the iniquity which Eli's sons had wrought, and because Eli restrained them not. The Lord declared that the iniquity of Eli's house should not be purged with sacrifice nor offering for ever.

The morning came, and the soft light crept over Shiloh. Samuel opened the doors of the house of the Lord, but he feared to shew Eli the vision. Yet Eli called unto him, "Samuel, my son." Samuel answered, "Here am I." Eli said, "What is the thing that the Lord hath said unto thee? I pray thee, hide it not from me: God do so to thee, and more also, if thou hide any thing from me of all the things that he said unto thee." So Samuel told him every whit, and hid nothing from him. Eli hearkened and said, "It is the Lord: let him do what seemeth him good."

And Samuel grew, and the Lord was with him, and did let none of his words fall to the ground. And all Israel, from Dan even to Beersheba, knew that Samuel was established to be a prophet of the Lord. And the Lord appeared again in Shiloh: for the Lord revealed himself to Samuel in Shiloh by the word of the Lord.

As the days and seasons passed in Shiloh, Samuel moved among the people quietly, learning to hearken for the voice of God in ways both loud and soft. The elders came unto him with troubled questions, and mothers brought their children unto the tabernacle, trusting that he that heard the Lord might bear up their prayers. There was longing in the land, hope like frail green shoots, as the people spake of Samuel with reverence and curiosity. Sometimes Samuel paused beside the old lamp as the evening fell, feeling the hush of evening settle upon his shoulders, a gentle remembrance of the night the Lord had called him.

Eli beheld Samuel with a tenderness that did not shew upon his face, but shone in his careful words. Though his steps grew slower, Eli offered counsel for as long as he was able, encouraging Samuel to be faithful, to love mercy, and to walk humbly. The old priest's heart was heavy for his sons, but he trusted Samuel's calling, and sometimes he found a measure of peace beholding the boy who hearkened and ever answered. The days carried Eli gently forward, and the people of Shiloh began to perceive that change was come, subtle and quiet, but unmistakable.
