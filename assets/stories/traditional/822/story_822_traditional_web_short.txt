In the quiet darkness of the night, young Samuel lay in the house of the Lord at Shiloh. The lamp of God was still burning, casting gentle light across the place where the ark rested. Eli the priest, an old man with failing sight, slept in his own room nearby. The air was soft and hushed, deep with the stillness that settles before dawn.

As Samuel rested, a voice broke the silence, calling softly in the dark, “Samuel!” The boy rose quickly and ran to Eli, thinking the priest had called him. “Here I am!” Samuel said, standing near Eli’s bed. But Eli looked at the boy and answered quietly, “I did not call. Lie down again, my son.” Samuel returned to his place and lay down beneath the pale, flickering light.

Again, the Lord called, “Samuel!” The voice drifted through the quiet. Samuel rose and hurried to Eli, his heart attentive. Again he said, “Here I am. For you called me.” Eli shook his head, his voice gentle but firm. “I did not call, my son. Lie down again.” Samuel obeyed, lying down once more.

Samuel did not yet know the Lord. The word of the Lord had not yet been revealed to him. For the third time, the voice came—calling his name in the peaceful dark. Samuel got up and went to Eli, bringing the question once more: “Here I am, for you called me.” Then Eli understood. He realized it was the Lord calling to the boy. Eli spoke to Samuel with calm wisdom, “Go, lie down again. If He calls you, say, ‘Speak, Lord, for your servant is listening.’”

Samuel returned to his place and lay still, heart brimming with wonder and awe. When the Lord came and stood there, calling as before, “Samuel! Samuel!” the boy answered just as Eli had taught him: “Speak, for your servant is listening.” The voice of God spoke to Samuel, revealing things hidden and weighing heavy about the house of Eli. Samuel listened with care, holding the words close.

Morning light crept into the tabernacle. Samuel lay until dawn, then opened the doors of the Lord’s house. He held the vision in his heart, afraid to share it, but Eli called for him. Samuel came to Eli, and at the priest’s urging, he told him everything, hiding nothing. Eli heard and said, “It is the Lord. Let Him do what seems good to Him.” Thus Samuel grew, and the Lord was with him, and all Israel learned that Samuel was established as a prophet of the Lord.
