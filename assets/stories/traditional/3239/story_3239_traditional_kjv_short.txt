Nebuchadnezzar the king told Belteshazzar the visions that had come to him upon his bed. In the midst of the earth he had seen a mighty tree. Its height reached unto heaven, and it was visible to the ends of the earth. Fair leaves and abundant fruit clothed it; beneath it the beasts found shadow, and in its boughs the fowls dwelt. It furnished meat for all flesh.

A watcher, a holy one, descended from heaven and cried aloud. He commanded that the tree be hewn down, its branches cut away, its leaves shaken off, and its fruit scattered. The beasts and fowls were to depart. Nevertheless, the stump of its roots must remain in the earth, bound with iron and brass in the tender grass and wet with heaven's dew.

The watcher continued, “Let his portion be with the beasts in the grass of the earth: let his heart be changed from man’s, and let a beast’s heart be given unto him; and let seven times pass over him.” This was the decree of the watchers and the demand of the holy ones, he declared, that the living might know the most High ruleth in men's kingdoms, bestowing them upon whomsoever he will, even the basest of men.

The king called upon Belteshazzar to declare the interpretation. None of his wise men could make it known, he said, but Belteshazzar could, for the spirit of the holy gods was in him.

Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, was astonied for one hour, his thoughts troubling him. The king bade him be untroubled by the dream or its interpretation. Daniel wished that the dream were for those who hated the king, and its meaning for his enemies.

He spoke of the tree's strength and height, its fair leaves, its plentiful fruit, and the creatures sheltered beneath and within it. The tree was Nebuchadnezzar himself, grown great and strong, his dominion reaching the end of the earth.

Daniel explained the watcher's command to destroy the tree yet leave the bound stump among the beasts and dew for seven times. The most High had decreed that the king should be driven from men. He would dwell with beasts, eat grass as oxen, and be wet with heaven's dew. Seven times would pass until he knew that the most High ruled men's kingdoms and gave them to whomsoever he would.

The command to preserve the stump meant that his kingdom should be sure after he knew that the heavens ruled. Daniel therefore besought him to accept counsel: to break off his sins by righteousness, and his iniquities by shewing mercy to the poor, if it might lengthen his tranquillity.
