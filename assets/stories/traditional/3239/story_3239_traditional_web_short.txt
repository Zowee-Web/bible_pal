King Nebuchadnezzar described to Belteshazzar the visions he had seen on his bed. A tree stood in the middle of the earth. It grew strong and reached the sky, visible to the ends of the earth. Its leaves were beautiful, its fruit abundant. Animals sheltered beneath it, birds lived in its branches, and all flesh found food there.

The king had seen a holy watcher descend from the sky. The watcher cried out an order to cut down the tree, cut off its branches, shake away its leaves, and scatter its fruit. Animals and birds were to leave it. But the stump and roots were to remain in the ground, bound with iron and bronze among the tender grass and wet with dew.

The watcher continued, “Let his portion be with the animals in the grass of the earth. Let his heart be changed from man’s, and let an animal’s heart be given to him. Then let seven times pass over him.” He called this the watchers' decree and the holy ones' demand, so that the living might know that the Most High rules human kingdoms, gives them to whomever he wills, and sets the lowest of men over them.

Nebuchadnezzar asked Belteshazzar to interpret the dream. He said his wise men could not explain it, but Belteshazzar could, because the spirit of the holy gods was in him.

Daniel, also called Belteshazzar, was mute for a while, troubled by his thoughts. The king told him not to be troubled by the dream or its meaning. Daniel answered with the wish that the dream concerned the king's enemies instead.

He identified the great, fruitful tree, with its sheltering branches and food for all, as the king himself. Nebuchadnezzar had grown strong; his greatness reached the sky and his dominion the earth's ends.

Daniel then explained the watcher's command to destroy the tree but preserve its bound stump, wet with dew among the animals for seven times. It was the Most High's decree concerning the king. He would be driven from people to live with animals, eat grass like oxen, and be wet with dew. Seven times would pass until he knew that the Most High ruled human kingdoms and gave them to whomever he wished.

The preserved stump meant that his kingdom would be secure after he knew that Heaven rules. Daniel urged the king to accept his counsel: break off his sins by righteousness and his iniquities by mercy to the poor. Perhaps, Daniel said, his tranquility might be prolonged.
