Darius the king made a decree, and search was made in the house of the rolls where the treasures were laid up in Babylon. At the palace in Achmetha, in the province of the Medes, a roll was found. Therein was written the record of the decree that Cyrus had made in the first year of his reign.

Cyrus had commanded that the house of God at Jerusalem should be builded for a place of sacrifice. The record set down the measure of the house and appointed that the charges should be given out of the king's house. It also required that the vessels of gold and silver which Nebuchadnezzar took out of the former temple should be restored unto their place at Jerusalem.

Therefore Darius sent commandment unto Tatnai, governor beyond the river, Shetharboznai, and their companions. They were to be far from thence and let the governor of the Jews and the elders build the house of God in his place.

Moreover, the builders' expenses were to be given speedily from the king's tribute beyond the river. Young bullocks, rams, lambs, wheat, salt, wine, and oil—whatsoever the priests required for offerings unto the God of heaven—were to be delivered day by day without fail. Thus might the priests offer sacrifices and pray for the life of the king and of his sons.

The king also decreed a penalty against any man who altered his word. Timber should be pulled down from that man's house, and his house should be made a dunghill. Darius called upon the God who had caused his name to dwell at Jerusalem to destroy every king and people who sought to put forth a hand against that house. He commanded that the decree be done with speed.

Then Tatnai, Shetharboznai, and their companions obeyed according to the king's word. The elders of the Jews builded and prospered through the prophesying of Haggai and Zechariah. They continued according to the commandment of God and the decrees of the kings of Persia. The house was finished on the third day of Adar, in the sixth year of the reign of Darius.
