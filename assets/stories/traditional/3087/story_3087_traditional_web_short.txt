King Darius commanded that a search be made in the archives where the treasures were stored in Babylon. A scroll was found in the palace at Achmetha in the province of Media. It contained a record of the decree Cyrus had issued in the first year of his reign.

Cyrus had ordered that God's house in Jerusalem be rebuilt as a place for sacrifices. The decree recorded the temple's measurements and directed that its cost be paid from the royal treasury. It also required that the gold and silver vessels Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the former temple be returned to their place in Jerusalem.

After the record was found, Darius sent an order to Tattenai the governor beyond the River, Shethar Bozenai, and their companions. They were to keep away from the work and leave the governor of the Jews and the Jewish elders free to rebuild God's house on its site.

Darius also commanded that the builders' expenses be paid promptly from the royal tribute collected beyond the River. Whatever the priests needed for offerings to the God of heaven—young bulls, rams, lambs, wheat, salt, wine, and oil—was to be supplied day by day without fail. Then the priests could offer sacrifices and pray for the life of the king and his sons.

The king added a penalty for anyone who altered his decree. A beam would be pulled from that person's house, and the house would be made a refuse heap. Darius called upon the God who had caused his name to dwell in Jerusalem to overthrow any king or people who tried to destroy that house. He ended his command by requiring that it be done with diligence.

Tattenai, Shethar Bozenai, and their companions obeyed the decree promptly. The Jewish elders built and prospered through the prophesying of Haggai and Zechariah. They continued the work according to God's command and the decrees of the kings of Persia. The house was finished on the third day of Adar, in the sixth year of Darius's reign.
