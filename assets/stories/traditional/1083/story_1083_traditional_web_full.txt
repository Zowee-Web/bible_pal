In a village near Jerusalem called Bethany, a man named Lazarus was very sick. The sickness came on suddenly and grew worse each day. Lazarus lived in a small stone house with his two sisters, Mary and Martha. The three of them were dear friends of Jesus — he had eaten at their table, rested in their home, and spent long evenings in their company. When Lazarus could no longer rise from his bed, the sisters sent a message to Jesus. "Lord, the one you love is sick."

When Jesus received the message, he said to those around him, "This sickness is not to death, but for the glory of God, that God's Son may be glorified by it." And though he loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus, he stayed two more days in the place where he was.

After two days, he said to his disciples, "Let us go into Judea again."

The disciples looked at him with concern. "Rabbi," they said, "the Jews were just trying to stone you. Are you going back there?"

Jesus answered them, "Our friend Lazarus has fallen asleep. I am going so that I may wake him."

The disciples said, "Lord, if he has fallen asleep, he will recover."

Then Jesus said to them plainly, "Lazarus is dead. And I am glad for your sakes that I was not there, so that you may believe. Nevertheless, let us go to him."

Thomas, the one called Didymus, turned to the other disciples. "Let us go also," he said, "that we may die with him."

By the time Jesus came to Bethany, Lazarus had already been in the tomb four days. The village was close to Jerusalem, about two miles away, and many people had come from the city to mourn with the sisters. The house was full of weeping. Voices carried from the rooms and across the hillside. Women sat in the doorway with their heads covered, rocking slowly.

Martha heard that Jesus was coming, and she left the house and went out to meet him on the road. Mary stayed behind, sitting among the mourners.

When Martha reached Jesus, her face was wet with tears. "Lord," she said, "if you had been here, my brother would not have died. But even now, I know that whatever you ask of God, God will give you."

Jesus looked at her gently. "Your brother will rise again," he said.

"I know that he will rise again," Martha said, "in the resurrection on the last day."

Jesus said to her, "I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in me, though he die, yet shall he live. And whoever lives and believes in me shall never die. Do you believe this?"

She looked at him steadily. "Yes, Lord," she said. "I believe that you are the Christ, the Son of God, who comes into the world."

Martha went back to the house. She found her sister and whispered, "The Teacher is here, and he is calling for you." Mary rose quickly and went out to him. The mourners followed her, thinking she was going to the tomb to weep.

When Mary reached Jesus — he had not yet entered the village but was still on the road where Martha had met him — she fell down at his feet. "Lord," she said, "if you had been here, my brother would not have died."

When Jesus saw her weeping, and saw all the others weeping with her, he was deeply moved in his spirit and troubled.

"Where have you laid him?" he asked.

"Lord, come and see," they said.

And Jesus wept.

Some of the people nearby said, "See how much he loved him." Others murmured, "Could not this man, who opened the eyes of the blind, have also kept Lazarus from dying?"

Jesus, again deeply moved within himself, came to the tomb. It was a cave cut into the rock, with a great stone laid across the entrance. The mourners gathered behind him. The sun was hot. Dust hung in the still air.

"Take away the stone," Jesus said.

Martha spoke softly. "Lord, by this time there is a stench. He has been dead four days."

Jesus looked at her. "Did I not tell you," he said, "that if you believed, you would see the glory of God?"

They rolled the stone away. The opening of the cave was dark and cool. Jesus lifted his eyes to heaven. "Father," he said, "I thank you that you heard me. I know that you always hear me, but because of the people standing here I have said this, that they may believe that you sent me."

Then he cried out with a loud voice:

"Lazarus, come out!"

And the man who had been dead came out. He was still wrapped in grave clothes, his hands and feet bound in strips of linen, his face covered with a cloth. He stood in the mouth of the cave, blinking against the bright light.

"Loose him," Jesus said, "and let him go."
