The foundation of the second temple was laid in the second year after the people of Judah had returned from Babylon.

The builders had cleared the place. The stones had been cut. The first stone was set down in its place that day.

The priests came out in their robes, with the silver trumpets in their hands. The Levites — the sons of Asaph — came out with the cymbals. They stood at the foundation where the stone had been laid, in the order that David king of Israel had given them long ago.

They began to praise Yahweh.

They sang to one another, the priests calling and the Levites answering, in the words that the people had sung in the first temple — "For he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever toward Israel."

The trumpets blew. The cymbals rang.

The people shouted with a great shout when they praised Yahweh, because the foundation of Yahweh's house had been laid.

But among them, many of the priests and Levites and heads of fathers' houses — the old men who had seen the first house, when the foundation of this house was laid before their eyes — wept with a loud voice.

These were the men who remembered Solomon's temple. They had seen the gold of the walls, and the bronze of the sea, and the cherubim of olive wood, and the lampstands burning in the holy place. They had seen Nebuchadnezzar's army break it apart in their lifetime.

They wept at the laying of this foundation, because they had known what had been lost, and because the foundation under their feet was smaller than the foundation they had once seen.

And the young men shouted for joy at the same hour. They had not seen the first house. They had been born in the years after the carrying away. The foundation under their feet was the first foundation they had ever known.

The shouting and the weeping rose together.

The people could not tell the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping of the people. The noise went up out of the city, and was heard far away.
