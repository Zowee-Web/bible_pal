Paul departed from Athens and came unto Corinth. There he found Aquila, a Jew born in Pontus, and his wife Priscilla. They had lately come from Italy because Claudius had commanded all Jews to depart from Rome. Paul came unto them and, being of the same craft, abode and wrought with them. Their occupation was tentmaking.

Every sabbath Paul reasoned in the synagogue and persuaded Jews and Greeks. When Silas and Timotheus came from Macedonia, Paul was pressed in the spirit and testified that Jesus was Christ. When some opposed themselves and blasphemed, he shook his raiment. He told them that their blood was upon their own heads, that he was clean, and that henceforth he would go unto the Gentiles.

Paul departed thence and entered into the house of a certain man named Justus, one who worshipped God and whose house joined hard to the synagogue. Crispus, the chief ruler of the synagogue, believed on the Lord with all his house. Many Corinthians heard, believed, and were baptized.

The Lord spake unto Paul in the night by a vision: “Be not afraid, but speak, and hold not thy peace. For I am with thee, and no man shall set on thee to hurt thee, for I have much people in this city.” Paul continued there a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them.

When Gallio was deputy of Achaia, certain Jews made insurrection with one accord against Paul and brought him to the judgment seat. They charged him with persuading men to worship God contrary to the law. As Paul was about to open his mouth, Gallio answered them. Had it been a matter of wrong or wicked lewdness, he said, reason would require him to bear with them. But questions of words and names and their own law were theirs to see to. He would be no judge of such matters, and he drave them from the judgment seat.

Then all the Greeks took Sosthenes, the chief ruler of the synagogue, and beat him before the judgment seat. Gallio cared for none of those things.
