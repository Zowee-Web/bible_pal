Paul left Athens and went to Corinth. There he found Aquila, a Jew from Pontus, and Aquila’s wife, Priscilla. They had recently come from Italy because Claudius had commanded all Jews to leave Rome. Paul went to them and, because they shared the trade of tent making, lived and worked with them.

Every Sabbath Paul reasoned in the synagogue and persuaded Jews and Greeks. When Silas and Timothy arrived from Macedonia, Paul was compelled by the Spirit and testified that Jesus was the Christ. When some opposed him and blasphemed, he shook out his clothing. He told them that their blood was on their own heads, that he was clean, and that he would now go to the Gentiles.

Paul left the synagogue and entered the house of Titius Justus, a worshiper of God whose house stood next door. Crispus, the synagogue’s ruler, believed in the Lord with his whole household. Many Corinthians heard, believed, and were baptized.

The Lord spoke to Paul in a night vision: “Don’t be afraid. Speak, and don’t be silent, for I am with you. No one will attack you to harm you, for I have many people in this city.” Paul remained in Corinth for a year and six months, teaching God’s word among the people.

While Gallio was proconsul of Achaia, a group of Jews rose together against Paul and brought him before the judgment seat. They accused Paul of persuading people to worship God contrary to the law. Before Paul could speak, Gallio answered them. He said he would reasonably hear a matter of wrong or wicked crime, but questions about words, names, and their own law were for them to address. He refused to judge those matters and drove them from the judgment seat.

Then all the Greeks seized Sosthenes, the ruler of the synagogue, and beat him in front of the judgment seat. Gallio did not concern himself with what happened.
