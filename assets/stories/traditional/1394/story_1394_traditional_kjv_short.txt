Heman the Ezrahite was one of the wise men of the land in the days of Solomon — known for his songs and the chambers of his teaching, named in the chronicles of Israel beside Ethan the Ezrahite and the sons of Mahol. He served also as a singer in the house of the Lord. And in one of the seasons of his life he composed a song that was unlike the others — a song that did not turn, that did not resolve, that went only down. The first words were these.

"O Lord God of my salvation,
I have cried day and night before thee.

Let my prayer come before thee:
incline thine ear unto my cry.

For my soul is full of troubles:
and my life draweth nigh unto the grave.

I am counted with them that go down into the pit:
I am as a man that hath no strength,

free among the dead,
like the slain that lie in the grave,
whom thou rememberest no more.
And they are cut off from thy hand.

Thou hast laid me in the lowest pit,
in darkness, in the deeps.

Thy wrath lieth hard upon me,
and thou hast afflicted me with all thy waves.
Selah.

Thou hast put away mine acquaintance far from me;
thou hast made me an abomination unto them.
I am shut up, and I cannot come forth.

Mine eye mourneth by reason of affliction:
Lord, I have called daily upon thee,
I have stretched out my hands unto thee."

And the psalm went on from thence. The song should close in the same darkness wherein it began, without the turn that other songs of lament made at the end; and the singers of the sons of Korah should set it to be sung upon Mahalath Leannoth — the affliction-after-sickness tune — and should keep it among the psalms of Israel for any that came after Heman and could not yet make the turn.
