Ezekiel the son of Buzi was a priest by the river Chebar in the land of the Chaldeans, among the captives of Judah which had been carried away with Jehoiachin. He had been on the riverbank when the heavens were opened over him, when the four living creatures had come up out of the whirlwind from the north, and he had beheld the wheels and the throne and the appearance of a man upon the throne. And in the vision he had been given a scroll to eat, sweet as honey in his mouth, and he had been bidden to go unto the house of Israel. And then the Spirit lifted him.

So the spirit lifted me up, and took me away, and I went in bitterness, in the heat of my spirit; but the hand of the Lord was strong upon me.

Then I came to them of the captivity at Tel-abib, that dwelt by the river of Chebar, and I sat where they sat, and remained there astonished among them seven days.

And Ezekiel sat for seven days among the captives at Tel-abib. The river Chebar ran beside the houses of the exile in the long days of those seven days; the captives went about their labour in their fields and their folds; the priest which had been called sat without speaking. The bitterness of his spirit took the seven days to settle. And on the eighth day the word of the Lord should come unto him again; but for the seven days he sat astonished by the river Chebar.
