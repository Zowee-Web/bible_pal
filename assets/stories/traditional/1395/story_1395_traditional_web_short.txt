Ezekiel the son of Buzi was a priest by the river Chebar in the land of the Chaldeans, among the captives of Judah who had been carried away with Jehoiachin. He had been on the riverbank when the heavens opened over him, when the four living creatures had come up out of the storm wind from the north and he had seen the wheels and the throne and the appearance of a man on the throne. And in the vision he had been given a scroll to eat, sweet as honey in his mouth, and he had been told to go to the house of Israel. And then the Spirit lifted him.

So the Spirit lifted me up, and took me away; and I went in bitterness, in the heat of my spirit; and Yahweh's hand was strong on me.

Then I came to them of the captivity at Tel Aviv, that lived by the river Chebar, and to where they lived; and I sat there overwhelmed among them seven days.

And Ezekiel sat for seven days among the captives at Tel-abib. The river Chebar ran beside the houses of the exile in the long days of those seven days; the captives went about their work in their fields and their pens; the priest who had been called sat without speaking. The bitterness of his spirit took the seven days to settle. And on the eighth day the word of the Lord would come to him again; but for the seven days he sat overwhelmed by the river Chebar.
