When the word first came unto the elders in the houses of exile, that Cyrus had spoken and the years of captivity were ending, the people sat still in their dwellings and could not speak. The threescore and ten years that Jeremiah had named upon the calendar of their bondage were drawing to a close. And when the sons of the captivity went up out of Babylon and passed over the long road through the wilderness unto the hills of Judah, they kept saying one to another that they were yet dreaming. They tasted the dry wind of the way. They lay down at night under stars that were the same stars which had hung over Jerusalem, and they could not believe that they were under them again. And afterward, when the road behind them had been long enough that the dust of Babylon had fallen from their cloaks, a singer of the temple lifted up a song for that which had been done — and the song went thus:

"When the Lord turned again the captivity of Zion,
we were like them that dream.

Then was our mouth filled with laughter,
and our tongue with singing.
Then said they among the heathen,
'The Lord hath done great things for them.'

The Lord hath done great things for us;
whereof we are glad.

Turn again our captivity, O Lord,
as the streams in the south.

They that sow in tears shall reap in joy.

He that goeth forth and weepeth, bearing precious seed,
shall doubtless come again with rejoicing, bringing his sheaves with him."

And the song was set down among the songs of degrees. They that had crossed the wilderness kept it with them upon the road and into the days of building; the streams of the south that filled in sudden flood after a year's drought stood in the imagination of the singer as a picture of that which had come to pass; and the laughter of them that dream became one of the songs of the climb up unto Zion.
