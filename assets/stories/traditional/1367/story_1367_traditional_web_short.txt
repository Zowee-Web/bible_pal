When the word first came to the elders in the houses of exile, that Cyrus had spoken and the years of captivity were ending, the people sat still in their dwellings and could not speak. The seventy years that Jeremiah had named on the calendar of their bondage were closing. And when the sons of the captivity went up out of Babylon and crossed the long road across the desert to the hills of Judah, they kept telling one another that they were still dreaming. They tasted the dry wind of the road. They lay down at night under stars that were the same stars that had hung over Jerusalem, and they could not believe they were under them again. And later, when the road behind them had been long enough that the dust of Babylon had fallen from their cloaks, a singer of the temple lifted up a song for what had been done — and the song went thus:

"When Yahweh brought back those who returned to Zion,
we were like those who dream.

Then our mouth was filled with laughter,
and our tongue with singing.
Then they said among the nations,
'Yahweh has done great things for them.'

Yahweh has done great things for us,
and we are glad.

Restore our fortunes again, Yahweh,
like the streams in the Negev.

Those who sow in tears will reap in joy.

He who goes out weeping, carrying seed for sowing,
will certainly come again with joy, carrying his sheaves."

And the song was set down among the songs of ascent. The travellers who had crossed the desert kept it with them on the road and into the days of building; the streams of the Negev that filled in flash flood after a year's drought stood in the imagination of the singer as a picture of what had happened; and the laughter of the dreamers became one of the songs of the climb back to Zion.
