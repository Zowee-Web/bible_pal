The messengers came unto Jerusalem with fear upon their faces. They had been running. Their garments were stained with dust and sweat, and their words tumbled forth: a vast host was on the march. The children of Moab and Ammon, with allies from the lands beyond the Dead Sea, had assembled a force so great the scouts could not reckon it. They had already crossed the water. They were climbing through the passes. They were at En-gedi — less than a day's march from Jerusalem.

King Jehoshaphat heard the tidings and was afraid.

He was a good king — the scripture saith so plainly. He had walked in the ways of his father Asa. He had removed the high places and the groves out of Judah. He had sent teachers throughout the land to instruct the people in the law of the Lord. He was faithful and devout. But he was not a warrior. He had not the army to match what was coming.

But Jehoshaphat called not first for weapons. He set himself to seek the Lord.

He proclaimed a fast throughout all Judah. Word went forth from Jerusalem unto every town and village: come to the temple. Bring your households. Come and pray. And they came — from the hill country and the lowlands and the south, from farms and vineyards and shepherds' folds. They gathered in the court of the house of the Lord, a great assembly of men, women, and their little ones, standing shoulder to shoulder in the open place before the temple.

Jehoshaphat stood before them. He prayed aloud, his voice carrying across the multitude.

"O Lord God of our fathers, art not thou God in heaven? And rulest not thou over all the kingdoms of the heathen? And in thine hand is there not power and might, so that none is able to withstand thee?" He traced the history — how God had driven out the inhabitants of the land, how the temple had been built as a place of refuge in times of trouble. Then he laid the present matter bare. The enemy was vast. The army of Judah was small. The invasion was already begun.

"O our God," he said, "we have no might against this great company that cometh against us; neither know we what to do: but our eyes are upon thee."

All Judah stood before the Lord. The wind stirred the edges of their garments. Children gripped their mothers' hands. Old men leaned upon their staves and wept. No man spake.

Then came the Spirit of the Lord upon Jahaziel, a Levite, standing in the midst of the congregation. He opened his mouth and spake with an authority that was not his own.

"Hearken ye, all Judah and ye inhabitants of Jerusalem, and thou King Jehoshaphat. Thus saith the Lord unto you, Be not afraid nor dismayed by reason of this great multitude; for the battle is not yours, but God's."

He told them where the enemy should come — by the cliff of Ziz — and what they were to do. "Ye shall not need to fight in this battle," he said. "Set yourselves, stand ye still, and see the salvation of the Lord with you, O Judah and Jerusalem: fear not, nor be dismayed; to morrow go out against them: for the Lord will be with you."

Jehoshaphat bowed with his face to the ground. All the people fell before the Lord. Then certain of the Levites stood and praised the Lord God of Israel with a loud voice, their singing rising above the courts.

Early in the morning they went forth unto the wilderness of Tekoa. The sun was rising over the brown hills. Jehoshaphat stood before the host and said, "Hear me, O Judah, and ye inhabitants of Jerusalem: Believe in the Lord your God, so shall ye be established; believe his prophets, so shall ye prosper."

Then he took counsel and appointed singers unto the Lord, that they should praise the beauty of holiness, as they went out before the army, and say, "Praise the Lord; for his mercy endureth for ever."

Singers. Not soldiers. Not archers. Not men of war. The choir went forth at the head of the army, unarmed, their voices lifted into the morning air.

And when they began to sing and to praise, the Lord set ambushments against the children of Ammon, Moab, and mount Seir. The Ammonites and Moabites rose up against them of mount Seir and utterly destroyed them. And when they had made an end of them of Seir, every one helped to destroy another.

When Judah came unto the watch tower in the wilderness, they looked toward the multitude, and behold, they were dead bodies fallen to the earth, and none escaped.

Three days were they in gathering of the spoil, for it was very much. On the fourth day they assembled themselves in the valley of Berachah; for there they blessed the Lord: therefore the name of that place was called, The Valley of Blessing, unto this day.

Then they returned, every man of Judah and Jerusalem, with Jehoshaphat in the forefront of them, to go again to Jerusalem with joy; for the Lord had made them to rejoice over their enemies. They came to Jerusalem with psalteries and harps and trumpets unto the house of the Lord.

And the fear of God was on all the kingdoms of those countries, when they had heard that the Lord fought against the enemies of Israel. So the realm of Jehoshaphat was quiet: for his God gave him rest round about.
