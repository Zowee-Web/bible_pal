Word came unto King Jehoshaphat at Jerusalem that a great host was come against Judah. The children of Moab and Ammon, together with allies from beyond the sea, had gathered a force past all numbering. They had already crossed the water. They were climbing through the passes toward Jerusalem. Scouts brought the tidings: the enemy was at En-gedi, less than a day's march distant.

Jehoshaphat feared. He was a good king — faithful, just, devoted to the God of his fathers — but he was not a man of war. He had not the army to match what came against him.

But he called not first for weapons. He set himself to seek the Lord. He proclaimed a fast throughout all Judah, and the people gathered in the court of the temple — a great assembly of men, women, and their little ones, standing shoulder to shoulder before the house of the Lord.

Jehoshaphat stood in the congregation and prayed aloud. "O Lord God of our fathers, art not thou God in heaven? And rulest not thou over all the kingdoms of the heathen? In thine hand is there not power and might, so that none is able to withstand thee?" He recounted the history and the covenant. Then he spake the truest words: "O our God, we have no might against this great company that cometh against us; neither know we what to do: but our eyes are upon thee."

All Judah stood before the Lord. The wind stirred the edges of their garments. Children held their mothers' hands. No man spake.

Then upon Jahaziel the Levite came the Spirit of the Lord. He said, "Hearken ye, all Judah, and thou King Jehoshaphat. Thus saith the Lord unto you, Be not afraid nor dismayed by reason of this great multitude; for the battle is not yours, but God's."

He told them where the enemy would come. "Ye shall not need to fight in this battle," he said. "Set yourselves, stand ye still, and see the salvation of the Lord with you."

Jehoshaphat bowed his face to the ground. All the people fell before the Lord. Certain of the Levites stood and praised God with a very loud voice.

In the morning they went forth into the wilderness of Tekoa. Jehoshaphat stood and said, "Believe in the Lord your God, so shall ye be established; believe his prophets, so shall ye prosper."

Then he appointed singers unto the Lord to go out before the army, saying, "Praise the Lord; for his mercy endureth for ever."

When they began to sing and to praise, the Lord set ambushments against the invaders. The children of Ammon and Moab rose up against them of mount Seir and utterly destroyed them; then they turned every one upon another. When Judah came unto the watch tower and looked toward the wilderness, the earth was strewn with dead bodies. None had escaped.

Three days they gathered the spoil, for it was exceeding much. On the fourth day they assembled in the valley of Berachah — which is, the valley of Blessing — and there they blessed the Lord.

Then they returned unto Jerusalem with joy, with psalteries and harps and trumpets, and came unto the house of the Lord.
