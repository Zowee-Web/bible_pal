Word came to King Jehoshaphat that a vast army was marching against Judah — the Moabites, the Ammonites, and others with them, a force so large it could not be counted. They were already across the Dead Sea. They were already coming.

Jehoshaphat was afraid. He was king, but he was not a warrior by nature. He did not call for weapons first. He called for prayer. He proclaimed a fast throughout all Judah and gathered the people in the court of the temple in Jerusalem.

He stood before the assembly and prayed aloud. "O Lord, God of our fathers," he said, "are you not God in heaven? Do you not rule over all the kingdoms of the nations? In your hand are power and might, so that none is able to withstand you." He laid out the danger plainly. The enemy was coming. The people had no strength against them. "We do not know what to do," Jehoshaphat said, "but our eyes are on you."

All of Judah stood before the Lord — men, women, and children — their faces turned toward the temple, waiting.

Then the Spirit of the Lord came upon Jahaziel, a Levite standing in the assembly. He spoke: "Listen, all Judah and inhabitants of Jerusalem, and King Jehoshaphat. Thus says the Lord to you: Do not be afraid. Do not be dismayed because of this great multitude. For the battle is not yours, but God's."

Jahaziel told them where the enemy would come, and what to do. "You will not need to fight in this battle," he said. "Position yourselves. Stand still. And see the salvation of the Lord."

Jehoshaphat bowed his face to the ground. All the people fell down before the Lord.

Early the next morning, they went out into the wilderness of Tekoa. Jehoshaphat stood and said, "Believe in the Lord your God, and you will be established. Believe his prophets, and you will succeed."

Then he did something no military commander would ever do. He appointed singers to go ahead of the army — not soldiers, not scouts, but singers — and they went out at the front of the line, singing: "Give thanks to the Lord, for his steadfast love endures forever."

And when they began to sing and praise, the Lord set ambushes against the enemy. The Moabites and Ammonites turned on each other. They destroyed themselves. By the time Judah reached the lookout point over the wilderness, the battlefield was covered with bodies. Not one of the enemy had escaped.

It took three days to collect the plunder. On the fourth day they assembled in the Valley of Beracah — which means blessing — and they blessed the Lord there.

Then they returned to Jerusalem with joy, with harps and lyres and trumpets, and went to the house of the Lord.
