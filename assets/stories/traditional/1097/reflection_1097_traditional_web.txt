There is a kind of courage that does not look like courage. It looks like singing.

The army of Judah went out to face a force they could not defeat — and instead of weapons, they sent the choir. Not after the battle, in celebration. Before it. While the outcome was still unknown. While the enemy was still on the march.

What Jehoshaphat admitted — "we do not know what to do, but our eyes are on you" — is not the prayer of a weak man. It is the prayer of an honest one. The kind of prayer that costs something to say out loud, in front of your people, when everyone is watching and waiting for you to have an answer.

Sometimes the bravest thing is not having a plan. It is admitting you don't have one — and still showing up.
