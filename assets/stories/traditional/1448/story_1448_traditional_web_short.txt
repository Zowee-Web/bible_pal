David came up alone to the cave of Adullam.

He went in through the mouth of it. The stone of the hills was cool against his shoulder. The day was hot outside. The day was quiet inside.

He sat down against the wall of the cave. He had run from Saul. He had run from Achish at Gath. He had run with no men.

The day went on. He did not sleep. He listened for footsteps at the mouth of the cave that did not come.

He stayed at the cave. The sun moved across the mouth of it. The water in the skin grew low. He went down to the spring. He filled it again. He came back.

The next day his brothers came. They came down from the hills and found him there. His father's house came with them. They sat with him in the cave.

Word went out from the cave.

Then others began to come.

A man who could not pay his debts came down to the cave. A man whose master had turned him out came down to the cave. A man whose family had fallen sick came down to the cave. They had heard where David was. They came in twos and threes through the rocks.

Everyone who was in distress came. Everyone who was in debt came. Everyone whose soul was bitter came.

By the end of the days, they were four hundred.

David was no longer alone in the cave.

He stood up among them. He became their captain. The cave was full of voices in the heat of the day. The four hundred men of Adullam were with him in the hills.
