The morning was bright over the eastern shore. The night wind had died. The sea behind them lay smooth and wide, holding the early light upon its surface, holding nothing else that moved. The children of Israel stood together upon the sand. Their cloaks were heavy with salt. Their hair was still wet. The shore was strewn with the wreckage of chariots and the bodies of horses, and the people stood and looked, and then they began to sing.

Then sang Moses and the children of Israel this song unto the Lord, and spake, saying.

He sang, "I will sing unto the Lord, for he hath triumphed gloriously: the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea." The words rose in the cool morning air, and the people gathered closer to hear. Children stopped at the edge of the water and turned to listen. Old men leaned upon their staves and let their faces lift toward the sound.

Moses sang, "The Lord is my strength and song, and he is become my salvation: he is my God, and I will prepare him an habitation; my father's God, and I will exalt him."

The song carried along the shore. It did not break.

He sang, "The Lord is a man of war: the Lord is his name. Pharaoh's chariots and his host hath he cast into the sea: his chosen captains also are drowned in the Red Sea. The depths have covered them: they sank into the bottom as a stone."

The people heard the words and remembered. They remembered the chariot wheels caught in the floor of the sea, the trembling of the walls of water, the long crash of the closing. The song held it all and gave it back unto them as praise.

Moses sang on. He sang, "Thy right hand, O Lord, is become glorious in power: thy right hand, O Lord, hath dashed in pieces the enemy." He sang of the blast of the Lord's nostrils, and how the waters had been gathered together, and how the floods had stood upright as an heap, and how the depths had been congealed in the heart of the sea.

He sang the boast of the enemy and the silence that had answered it. "The enemy said, I will pursue, I will overtake, I will divide the spoil; my lust shall be satisfied upon them; I will draw my sword, my hand shall destroy them." Then the singing turned. "Thou didst blow with thy wind, the sea covered them: they sank as lead in the mighty waters."

The people sang with him. The line repeated and returned, and the song rose along the eastern shore.

Then Moses lifted his voice on the great question.

"Who is like unto thee, O Lord, among the gods? who is like thee, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders?"

The question stood in the morning air. No one answered. The water lay flat behind them.

He sang of the mercy of the Lord, which had led forth the people he had redeemed. He sang that the Lord had guided them in his strength unto his holy habitation. He sang that the peoples had heard, and trembled. The inhabitants of Palestina were in sorrow. The dukes of Edom were amazed. The mighty men of Moab trembled. The inhabitants of Canaan had melted away.

He sang the last line, low and steady. "The Lord shall reign for ever and ever."

The song settled into the sand and into the salt-wet cloaks of the people.

Then Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a timbrel in her hand.

She lifted it. The morning light caught the small bright disks set into its frame. And all the women went out after her with timbrels and with dances. They moved out along the shore in a long line, women and maidens, their feet pressing into the wet sand, their voices rising above the steady ring of the timbrels.

And Miriam answered them, "Sing ye to the Lord, for he hath triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea."

The women sang back. The timbrels beat through the morning. The line of dancing women moved along the shore in steady rhythm, and the sound went out over the water and into the open air. Children ran in and out among them, laughing. The old men still leaned upon their staves, but their faces were lifted.

The song of Miriam went up unto the Lord, and the morning grew brighter still, and the shore where they stood was a shore of singing.
