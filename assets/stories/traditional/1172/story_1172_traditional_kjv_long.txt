Then sang Moses and the children of Israel this song unto the Lord upon the morning of their deliverance.

The night before, the wind had blown from the east, and the sea had stood as walls on the right hand and on the left, and the people had walked through upon dry ground. Behind them the host of Pharaoh had gone down. The corridor had closed. The chariots and the horsemen had been covered.

Now the morning was bright over the eastern shore. The sea lay smooth and wide. The light fell upon the surface of the water and held there. The wreckage of chariots drifted at the tide line. Bodies of horses lay still upon the sand. The people of Israel stood upon the shore in the new light. Their cloaks were heavy with salt. Their hair was wet against their necks. Their feet pressed into ground that did not move.

They had not spoken since they came up out of the sea.

Then Moses lifted up his voice and began the song.

He sang, "I will sing unto the Lord, for he hath triumphed gloriously: the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea."

The words went out across the sand. The people gathered closer. Children stopped at the edge of the water and turned. Old men leaned upon their staves and raised their faces toward the sound. The song was the shape of what had happened, given back unto the people as praise.

Moses sang, "The Lord is my strength and song, and he is become my salvation: he is my God, and I will prepare him an habitation; my father's God, and I will exalt him."

He sang, "The Lord is a man of war: the Lord is his name."

He sang, "Pharaoh's chariots and his host hath he cast into the sea: his chosen captains also are drowned in the Red Sea. The depths have covered them: they sank into the bottom as a stone."

The people heard the words and remembered the night. They remembered the wind in their cloaks, the long corridor between the walls of green water, the slow flicker of a fish suspended within the wall, the firm cold ground beneath their feet. The song held it all and gave it back unto them as praise.

Moses sang on. He sang, "Thy right hand, O Lord, is become glorious in power: thy right hand, O Lord, hath dashed in pieces the enemy." He sang of the greatness of the Lord's excellency, overthrowing those who had risen up against him. He sang of the wrath of the Lord, sent forth and consuming them as stubble.

He sang of the blast of the Lord's nostrils. The waters had been gathered together. The floods had stood upright as an heap. The depths had been congealed in the heart of the sea.

The people sang the lines back unto him. The song rose along the eastern shore. The wind moved across the water, soft now, carrying the singing outward.

Then Moses sang the boast of the enemy. He sang it the way it had been spoken, with the long pursuing breath of it. "The enemy said, I will pursue, I will overtake, I will divide the spoil; my lust shall be satisfied upon them; I will draw my sword, my hand shall destroy them."

The line hung in the morning. Every word of it had been answered.

Moses sang the answer. "Thou didst blow with thy wind, the sea covered them: they sank as lead in the mighty waters."

The people sang it with him.

Then Moses lifted his voice on the great question, and the song reached its high place.

"Who is like unto thee, O Lord, among the gods? who is like thee, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders?"

The question stood in the morning air. The water lay flat behind them. No host moved upon the far shore. No voice answered from any direction. The question filled the open sky and did not ask for any answer that was not already given.

Moses sang on. He sang, "Thou stretchedst out thy right hand, the earth swallowed them."

Then the song turned toward the road ahead.

He sang of the mercy of the Lord, which had led forth the people he had redeemed. He sang that the Lord had guided them in his strength unto his holy habitation. He sang of the peoples who had heard, and trembled. The inhabitants of Palestina were in sorrow. The dukes of Edom were amazed. Trembling had taken hold upon the mighty men of Moab. The inhabitants of Canaan had melted away. Fear and dread had fallen upon them, by the greatness of the Lord's arm, until the people of the Lord should pass over, until the people he had purchased should pass over.

He sang that the Lord would bring them in, and plant them in the mountain of his inheritance, in the place the Lord had made for himself to dwell in, in the sanctuary which the Lord's hands had established.

Then he sang the last line of his song, low and steady, like a stone set into place.

"The Lord shall reign for ever and ever."

The line settled into the sand. The people of Israel stood quiet upon the shore.

The song of Moses had ended.

Then Miriam came forward.

She was the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, and she took a timbrel in her hand. She lifted it. The morning light caught the small bright disks set into its frame. She struck the head of it once with the flat of her palm, and the ring of it went out over the wet sand.

The women of Israel came out after her. They had their timbrels also. They had their dances. They moved into a long line along the shore, women and maidens, mothers carrying small children upon their hips, grandmothers with grey braids loose at their shoulders. Their feet pressed into the wet sand. Their voices rose above the steady ring of the timbrels.

And Miriam answered the song that Moses had begun.

"Sing ye to the Lord, for he hath triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea."

The women sang it back. The timbrels beat through the morning. The line of dancing women moved along the shore in steady rhythm. The sound went out across the open water and rose into the bright air. The shore was a shore of singing.

The children of Israel watched. Some of them sang also, the elder ones who knew the line. The smaller ones ran in and out among the dancing women, laughing, kicking up sand. The old men still leaned upon their staves, but their faces were lifted, and one of them was weeping, and the salt of his tears mixed with the salt that the sea had left upon his beard.

The song of Miriam went up unto the Lord. The timbrels beat on. The morning grew brighter. The line of the dance moved along the shore, and the shore held the rhythm of it, and the sea behind them lay still beneath the singing.

The song was not forgotten.
