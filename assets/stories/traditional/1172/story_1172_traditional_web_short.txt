When the children of Israel had crossed the Red Sea on dry ground, and the waters had returned and covered the chariots and the horsemen of Pharaoh, Moses and the children of Israel sang this song to the Lord. The morning was bright over the eastern shore, and the people stood together in safety on the sand.

Moses lifted up his voice and sang, "I will sing to the Lord, for he has triumphed gloriously. The horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea."

He sang, "The Lord is my strength and my song. He has become my salvation. This is my God, and I will praise him; my father's God, and I will exalt him."

He sang, "The Lord is a man of war. The Lord is his name. He has cast Pharaoh's chariots and his army into the sea. His chosen captains are sunk in the Red Sea."

He sang, "The deeps cover them. They went down into the depths like a stone. Your right hand, O Lord, is glorious in power. Your right hand, O Lord, dashes the enemy in pieces."

The people of Israel sang with him, and the song rose along the eastern shore. The wind moved across the water, and the wreckage of the chariots was scattered upon the sea behind them.

Then Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a tambourine in her hand. All the women went out after her with tambourines and with dances. Miriam answered them, "Sing to the Lord, for he has triumphed gloriously. The horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea."

The women of Israel danced upon the shore, and the sound of the tambourines went out across the sand. The morning grew brighter, and the song of Miriam went up to the Lord.

The people of Israel had been brought through the sea, and the song of their deliverance was sung that day, and the words of it were not forgotten.
