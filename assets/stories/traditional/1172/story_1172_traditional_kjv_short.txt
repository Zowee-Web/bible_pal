When the children of Israel had passed through the Red Sea upon dry ground, and the waters had returned and covered the chariots and the horsemen of Pharaoh, Moses and the children of Israel sang this song unto the Lord. The morning was bright over the eastern shore, and the people stood together in safety upon the sand.

Moses lifted up his voice and sang, "I will sing unto the Lord, for he hath triumphed gloriously: the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea."

He sang, "The Lord is my strength and song, and he is become my salvation: he is my God, and I will prepare him an habitation; my father's God, and I will exalt him."

He sang, "The Lord is a man of war: the Lord is his name. Pharaoh's chariots and his host hath he cast into the sea: his chosen captains also are drowned in the Red Sea."

He sang, "The depths have covered them: they sank into the bottom as a stone. Thy right hand, O Lord, is become glorious in power: thy right hand, O Lord, hath dashed in pieces the enemy."

The people of Israel sang with him, and the song rose along the eastern shore. The wind moved upon the face of the water, and the wreckage of the chariots was scattered upon the sea behind them.

And Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a timbrel in her hand; and all the women went out after her with timbrels and with dances. And Miriam answered them, "Sing ye to the Lord, for he hath triumphed gloriously: the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea."

The women of Israel danced upon the shore, and the sound of the timbrels went out across the sand. The morning grew brighter, and the song of Miriam went up unto the Lord.

The people of Israel had been brought through the sea, and the song of their deliverance was sung that day, and the words thereof were not forgotten.
