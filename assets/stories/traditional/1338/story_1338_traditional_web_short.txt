The image stood ninety feet high in the plain of Dura. King Nebuchadnezzar had set it up, and the herald had cried out that all peoples, nations, and languages were to fall down and worship it at the sound of the music. The Chaldeans had come to the king and accused certain Jews who had not bowed; and the king had heard, and was filled with rage.

Then Nebuchadnezzar in rage and fury commanded that Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego be brought. Then these men were brought before the king.

Nebuchadnezzar answered them, "Is it on purpose, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, that you don't serve my god, nor worship the golden image which I have set up?

Now if you are ready whenever you hear the sound of the horn, flute, zither, lyre, harp, pipe, and all kinds of music to fall down and worship the image which I have made, good; but if you don't worship, you shall be cast the same hour into the middle of a burning fiery furnace. Who is that god that will deliver you out of my hands?"

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego answered the king, "Nebuchadnezzar, we have no need to answer you in this matter.

If it happens, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace; and he will deliver us out of your hand, O king.

But if not, let it be known to you, O king, that we will not serve your gods or worship the golden image which you have set up."

The three men stood before the king with their answer given, and the music waited, and the furnace waited, and the great image kept its silence over the plain. The matter was not yet finished; what would come had not yet come; but the answer had been spoken, and the king had heard it, and the words had been laid down by men who would not take them back. And so it was in the days when the king set up the image and the three Hebrews stood before him in the plain of Dura.
