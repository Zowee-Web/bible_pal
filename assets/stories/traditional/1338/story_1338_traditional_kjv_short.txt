The image stood threescore cubits high in the plain of Dura. King Nebuchadnezzar had set it up, and the herald had cried aloud that all peoples, nations, and languages should fall down and worship it at the sound of the music. The Chaldeans were come before the king and had accused certain Jews that had not bowed; and the king had heard, and was filled with rage.

Then Nebuchadnezzar in his rage and fury commanded to bring Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. Then they brought these men before the king.

Nebuchadnezzar spake and said unto them, "Is it true, O Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, do not ye serve my gods, nor worship the golden image which I have set up?

Now if ye be ready that at what time ye hear the sound of the cornet, flute, harp, sackbut, psaltery, and dulcimer, and all kinds of musick, ye fall down and worship the image which I have made; well: but if ye worship not, ye shall be cast the same hour into the midst of a burning fiery furnace; and who is that God that shall deliver you out of my hands?"

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, answered and said to the king, "O Nebuchadnezzar, we are not careful to answer thee in this matter.

If it be so, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of thine hand, O king.

But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up."

The three men stood before the king with their answer given, and the music waited, and the furnace waited, and the great image kept its silence over the plain. The matter was not yet finished; that which should come had not yet come; but the answer had been spoken, and the king had heard it, and the words had been laid down by men that would not take them back. And so it was in the days when the king set up the image and the three Hebrews stood before him in the plain of Dura.
