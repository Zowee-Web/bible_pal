When Jesus heard the tidings, he departed by ship unto a desert place apart. The water was calm. The oars dipped and the boat moved quietly across the lake toward a stretch of open ground upon the far shore, where the hills came down unto the water's edge. He sought a quiet place, away from the towns.

But the people heard thereof. They went forth out of the cities on foot, following the line of the shore. By the time the ship reached the far side, they were already gathered — a great multitude, waiting upon the open ground.

When Jesus went forth and saw them, he was moved with compassion toward them. He abode among them and healed their sick. Mothers brought their children. The lame were carried forward by their kinfolk. One by one he touched them, and one by one they were made whole. The hours passed. The sun moved across the sky.

When the evening was come, a soft golden light lay upon the hills. The air grew cool. Shadows stretched across the ground.

His disciples came unto him. They looked out across the vast company — thousands of people sitting upon the grass, spread across the hillside. "This is a desert place," they said, "and the time is now past. Send the multitude away, that they may go into the villages, and buy themselves victuals."

But Jesus looked upon them calmly. "They need not depart," he said. "Give ye them to eat."

The disciples looked one upon another. Their faces shewed the impossibility of the charge. They had so little. They turned again to Jesus.

"We have here but five loaves, and two fishes," they said.

He said, "Bring them hither to me."

And he commanded the multitude to sit down upon the grass. Thousands sat down across the hillside in the warm evening light. The grass was soft from the day's sun. The disciples brought the five small loaves and the two fishes and set them in his hands. The food seemed a small thing against the sea of faces.

Jesus looked up to heaven and blessed. He brake the loaves and gave them to his disciples, and the disciples gave to the multitude.

And they did all eat. And they kept eating. There was bread in every hand. There was fish for every mouth. The disciples went to and fro among the people, bearing the food in their arms, and it was not diminished. They reached the farthest part of the crowd and returned, and still there was more to give.

When all had eaten and were filled — truly filled, not merely satisfied — the disciples gathered up what remained. They walked among the seated people, taking up the fragments. They filled twelve baskets with the broken pieces that were left over.

Twelve baskets. From five loaves and two fishes.

And they that had eaten were about five thousand men, beside women and children.
