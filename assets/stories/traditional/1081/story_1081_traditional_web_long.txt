When Jesus heard the news, he went down to the shore and stepped into a boat. The water was calm and blue-green in the midday light. He pushed off from the beach and the boat moved out across the lake, the oars dipping quietly, the wake trailing behind in a thin white line. He was heading for a deserted place on the far side — open ground away from the towns, where the brown hills sloped down to the water and the only sound was the wind and the calls of birds along the shore.

He sought a quiet place. A place apart.

But the crowds saw the boat. They watched it move across the water, and they knew where it was headed. Word spread quickly — from one house to the next, from one village to another. People set out from the cities and walked around the shore on foot, following the curving line of the coast. Families walked together. Old men moved slowly, leaning on sticks. Mothers carried small children on their hips. The road along the shore filled with people moving in the same direction, all of them following the boat.

By the time Jesus reached the far shore and stepped out of the boat, they were already there. A great multitude, gathered and waiting on the open ground. Thousands of people sitting on the grass and standing along the low hills. Their faces were turned toward the water, watching for the boat.

When Jesus saw them, he was not angry. He had compassion on them. He went among them and healed their sick — the lame, the fevered, the blind, those who had been carried there by others. One by one, they came forward, and one by one, he made them well. The hours passed slowly. The sun tracked across the sky. The shadows shortened and then began to lengthen again.

The sun moved lower. Evening came, and with it a soft gold light that lay across the hills and turned the grass from green to amber. The air cooled. A breeze moved in from the lake, carrying the smell of water and damp stone.

The disciples came to Jesus. They stood near him and looked out across the vast crowd — thousands of people spread across the hillside, sitting in groups, talking quietly, waiting. The nearest village was far away. There was no food here, no market, no inn.

"This place is deserted," the disciples said, "and the hour is already late. Send the crowds away, so they can go into the villages and buy themselves food."

But Jesus looked at them calmly. There was no worry in his face. "They don't need to go away," he said. "You give them something to eat."

The disciples looked at one another. The request was impossible. They were standing in the middle of open country with thousands of hungry people and no provisions. They had nothing. They turned back to Jesus.

"We only have here five loaves and two fish," they said. Their voices carried the weight of the absurdity — five small loaves and two dried fish, for a crowd that stretched across the hillside as far as they could see.

Jesus said simply, "Bring them here to me."

So the disciples brought the food. Five small loaves of bread and two fish, placed into Jesus' hands. The food looked impossibly small — a child's lunch, a single portion, barely enough for a family, let alone a multitude.

Jesus told the crowd to sit down on the grass. Thousands of people settled onto the ground across the hillside, sitting in clusters and rows. The grass was warm from the day's sun. The evening light touched their faces and the tops of their heads. The murmur of their voices quieted.

Jesus held the five loaves and the two fish. He looked up to heaven. He blessed the food. He broke the loaves.

And he began to give the pieces to his disciples.

The disciples took the bread and the fish and carried it out among the people. They moved through the crowd, handing food to each person they passed. And the food did not run out. There was bread in every hand. There was fish for every mouth. The disciples walked from one end of the crowd to the other, and when they reached the far side they turned around and went again, and still there was more. The baskets in their arms stayed full. The bread kept breaking into more bread. The fish kept becoming more fish.

The people ate. They ate until they were full — not just satisfied, but filled. The murmur of the crowd grew warm with the sounds of eating and talking and the laughter of children.

When everyone had finished, Jesus told the disciples to gather what remained. They moved through the crowd again, picking up the broken pieces — fragments of bread, leftover fish, portions that had not been eaten. They filled twelve baskets. Full baskets. Heavy baskets. Twelve of them, brimming with what was left over from five loaves and two fish.

And the number of those who had eaten was about five thousand men, besides women and children.

The evening deepened over the hillside. The light turned from gold to amber to the first gray of dusk. The crowd sat still on the warm grass, their hunger gone, the twelve full baskets standing in a row beside the disciples like a quiet, unanswerable proof of what had just happened.
