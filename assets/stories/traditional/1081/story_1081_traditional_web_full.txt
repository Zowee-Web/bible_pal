When Jesus heard the news, he stepped into a boat and pushed off from the shore. The water was calm. The oars dipped and the boat moved quietly across the lake toward a deserted place on the far side — a stretch of open ground away from the towns, where the hills sloped down to the water's edge. He sought a quiet place apart.

But the crowds heard where he was going. They set out from the cities and walked around the shore on foot, hundreds of them, following the line of the coast. By the time the boat reached the far shore, they were already there — a great multitude, gathered and waiting on the open ground.

When Jesus came out of the boat and saw them, he was not angry. He was not frustrated. He had compassion on them. He stayed among them, and he healed their sick. Mothers brought their children. The lame were carried forward by their families. One by one, Jesus touched them, and one by one, they were made well. The hours passed. The sun moved across the sky.

Evening came, and with it a soft gold light over the hills. The air cooled. Shadows stretched across the ground.

The disciples came to Jesus. They looked out over the vast crowd — thousands of people sitting on the grass, spread across the hillside. "This place is deserted," they said, "and the hour is already late. Send the crowds away, so they can go into the villages and buy themselves food."

But Jesus looked at them calmly. "They don't need to go away," he said. "You give them something to eat."

The disciples looked at one another. Their faces showed the impossibility of the request. They had so little. They turned back to Jesus.

"We only have here five loaves and two fish," they said.

"Bring them here to me," he said.

So Jesus told the crowd to sit down on the grass. Thousands of people sat down across the hillside in the warm evening light. The grass was soft from the day's sun. The disciples brought the five small loaves and the two fish and placed them in his hands. The food looked impossibly small against the sea of faces.

Jesus looked up to heaven. He blessed the food. He broke the loaves. And he gave the pieces to his disciples, who carried them out among the people.

The people began to eat. And they kept eating. There was bread in every hand. There was fish for every mouth. The disciples moved back and forth through the crowd, carrying the food in their arms, and somehow the food did not run out. They reached the back of the crowd and turned around and went again, and still there was more to give.

When everyone had eaten and was full — truly full, not merely satisfied but filled — the disciples gathered what was left over. They walked among the seated crowd, picking up the broken pieces that remained. They filled twelve baskets, full and heavy, with what was left.

Twelve baskets. From five loaves and two fish.

And the number of those who had eaten was about five thousand men, besides women and children.
