When Jesus heard the tidings, he went down unto the shore and entered into a ship. The water was calm, blue-green in the light of midday. He put forth from the beach and the ship moved out across the lake, the oars dipping quietly, the wake trailing behind in a thin white line. He was making toward a desert place on the far side — open ground away from the towns, where the brown hills came down unto the water and the only sound was the wind and the cries of birds along the shore.

He sought a quiet place. A place apart.

But the multitude saw the ship. They watched it move across the water, and they knew whither it was bound. Word spread quickly — from house to house, from village to village. The people went forth out of the cities on foot, following the curving line of the coast. Families walked together. Old men moved slowly, leaning upon their staves. Mothers bore small children upon their hips. The road along the shore filled with people moving in one direction, all of them following the ship.

By the time Jesus reached the far shore and went forth out of the ship, they were already there. A great multitude, gathered and waiting upon the open ground. Thousands of them, sitting upon the grass and standing along the low hills, their faces turned toward the water, watching.

When Jesus saw them, he was moved with compassion toward them. He went among them and healed their sick — the lame, the fevered, the blind, those who had been borne thither by others. One by one they came forward, and one by one he made them whole. The hours passed slowly. The sun tracked across the sky. The shadows shortened and then began again to lengthen.

The sun moved lower. When the evening was come, a soft golden light lay across the hills and turned the grass from green to gold. The air grew cool. A breeze moved in from the lake, carrying the smell of water and damp stone.

The disciples came unto Jesus. They stood nigh unto him and looked out over the great company — thousands of people spread across the hillside, sitting in groups, speaking quietly, waiting. The nearest village was far off. There was no food here, no market, no place of lodging.

"This is a desert place," the disciples said, "and the time is now past. Send the multitude away, that they may go into the villages, and buy themselves victuals."

But Jesus looked upon them calmly. There was no trouble in his countenance. "They need not depart," he said. "Give ye them to eat."

The disciples looked one upon another. The charge was not possible. They were standing in the midst of open country with thousands of hungry souls and no provision. They had nothing. They turned again unto Jesus.

"We have here but five loaves, and two fishes," they said. Their voices bore the weight of the absurdity — five small loaves and two dried fishes, for a company that covered the hillside as far as the eye could see.

Jesus said simply, "Bring them hither to me."

So the disciples brought the food. Five small loaves and two fishes, set in his hands. It seemed a small and pitiful thing — a child's portion, a single meal, scarce enough for one household, let alone a multitude.

Jesus commanded the crowd to sit down upon the grass. Thousands settled upon the hillside in the warm evening light. The grass was soft from the day's sun. The light touched their faces and the tops of their heads. The murmur of voices quieted.

Jesus held the five loaves and the two fishes. He looked up to heaven. He gave thanks. He brake the loaves.

And he began to give unto his disciples.

The disciples took the bread and the fish and bare it out among the people. They moved through the crowd, giving to every man as he had need. And the food was not diminished. There was bread in every hand. There was fish for every mouth. The disciples went from one end of the company unto the other, and when they reached the far side they turned about and went again, and still there was more. The provision in their arms did not fail. The bread kept breaking into more bread. The fish became yet more fish.

And they did all eat, and were filled. Not merely satisfied but truly filled. The murmur of the crowd grew warm with the sounds of eating and speaking and the laughter of children.

When all had eaten, the disciples gathered what remained. They walked among the seated multitude, taking up the broken pieces — fragments of bread, portions that had not been consumed. They filled twelve baskets. Full baskets. Heavy baskets. Twelve of them, brimming with what was left over from five loaves and two fishes.

And they that had eaten were about five thousand men, beside women and children.

The evening deepened over the hillside. The light turned from gold to amber to the first gray of eventide. The crowd sat still upon the warm grass, their hunger gone, the twelve full baskets standing in a row beside the disciples — a quiet, unanswerable witness unto what had come to pass.
