Samson saw a Philistine woman at Timnah and asked his parents to get her for his wife. They questioned why he would choose from the uncircumcised Philistines instead of his own people. Samson insisted that she pleased him. His parents did not know this was of Yahweh, who sought an occasion against the Philistines, then ruling Israel.

On the journey to Timnah with his parents, a young lion roared at Samson in the vineyards. Yahweh’s Spirit came mightily on him. Barehanded, he tore the lion as he would a young goat, but told neither parent. He went on to speak with the woman, and she pleased him.

Returning later to take her, Samson turned toward the lion’s carcass. Bees had swarmed inside it, and there was honey. He scooped some into his hands and ate along the way. He shared it with his parents without telling them where it came from.

His father went down to the woman, and Samson held the feast customary for young men. Thirty companions were brought to him. Samson offered a riddle: if they solved it during the seven-day feast, he would give them thirty linen garments and thirty changes of clothing. Otherwise, they must give those things to him. They asked to hear it.

“Out of the eater came out food. Out of the strong came out sweetness.”

For three days they could not solve it. On the seventh day they threatened his wife: she must entice Samson to reveal the answer, or they would burn her and her father’s house. They accused her of inviting them to impoverish them.

She wept before Samson, accusing him of hatred rather than love because he had withheld the answer. He replied that he had not even told his parents. Why tell her? She wept throughout the seven days of feasting. Pressed severely, he told her on the seventh day, and she told her people.

Before sunset that day, the men of the city answered, “What is sweeter than honey? What is stronger than a lion?”

Samson replied, “If you hadn’t plowed with my heifer, you wouldn’t have found out my riddle.”

Yahweh’s Spirit came mightily on him again. At Ashkelon he struck thirty men, took their plunder, and gave the changes of clothing to those who had answered. Burning with anger, Samson returned to his father’s house.

His wife was given to the companion who had been his friend.
