At Timnath, Samson saw a woman among the daughters of the Philistines. Returning to his father and mother, he asked them to get her for his wife. They asked whether no woman among his brethren or his people would serve, that he must marry among the uncircumcised Philistines. Yet Samson desired this woman, for she pleased him well. His parents knew not that the matter was of the Lord, who sought an occasion against the Philistines while they held dominion over Israel.

With his parents, Samson went down to Timnath. In the vineyards a young lion roared against him. The Spirit of the Lord came mightily upon Samson, and with nothing in his hand he rent it as he would a kid. He told his parents nothing of the deed. He then spoke with the woman, and she pleased him well.

When he returned after a time to take her, he turned aside to the lion’s carcase. Within it were a swarm of bees and honey. Taking honey in his hands, he ate as he went and gave some to his parents. They ate, but he did not tell them he had taken it from the carcase.

His father went unto the woman. Samson made a feast there, as young men were wont to do, and thirty companions were brought to him. He proposed a wager: thirty sheets and thirty changes of garments for the answer to a riddle within the feast’s seven days. If they failed, they owed him the same. They bade him speak.

“Out of the eater came forth meat, and out of the strong came forth sweetness.”

Three days passed without an answer. On the seventh, they threatened Samson’s wife with burning, together with her father’s house, unless she enticed him to reveal it. They asked whether she had called them to take that they had.

Weeping, she charged Samson with hating her and withholding from her the riddle given to her people. He answered that he had not told even his father or mother. She wept during all seven days of the feast; on the seventh, because she pressed him sorely, he told her. She carried the answer to her people.

Before sunset the city’s men asked, “What is sweeter than honey? and what is stronger than a lion?” Samson answered, “If ye had not plowed with my heifer, ye had not found out my riddle.”

The Spirit of the Lord came upon him. He went to Ashkelon, slew thirty men, and took their spoil to give changes of garments to those who had expounded the riddle. His anger kindled, he returned to his father’s house. His wife was given to the companion whom he had treated as his friend.
