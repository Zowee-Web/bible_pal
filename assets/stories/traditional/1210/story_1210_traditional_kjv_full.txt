The vineyard sat on the slope below Jezreel, hard by the palace wall, so that a man on the upper terrace could see it. The vines were old. They had been planted by Naboth's grandfather, and trained along stone supports by Naboth's father, and pruned each spring by Naboth himself. The soil between the rows had been worked so long it held the print of a sandal. This was the inheritance of his fathers. This was the ground that bore them.

Ahab came down the path one morning with two attendants behind him. He stood at the low wall and looked across the rows. The leaves were green. The fruit was beginning to set. He called out unto Naboth, who came up from among the vines.

"Give me thy vineyard," Ahab said, "that I may have it for a garden of herbs, because it is near unto my house. I will give thee for it a better vineyard than it. Or, if it seem good to thee, I will give thee the worth of it in money."

Naboth listened. He spake not at once. He looked upon the king, and then he looked upon the rows behind him.

"The Lord forbid it me," he said, "that I should give the inheritance of my fathers unto thee."

Ahab turned and went back up the path. He spake not to the attendants. He came into his house heavy and displeased, and went into his chamber. He laid him down upon his bed, and turned away his face, and would eat no bread that was brought unto him. The light in the chamber slid across the wall through the long hours. He moved not.

Jezebel came in at evening. She stood by the bed and looked down upon him.

"Why is thy spirit so sad," she said, "that thou eatest no bread?"

He told her, his face still to the wall. He told her of Naboth, and of the vineyard, and of the inheritance of fathers. She listened to the end.

"Dost thou now govern the kingdom of Israel?" she said. "Arise, and eat bread, and let thine heart be merry. I will give thee the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite."

She went out. In another room she sat down at a table. She called for ink and for parchment. She wrote letters in Ahab's name. When each letter was written she folded it and pressed the king's seal into the wax. She sent the letters unto the elders and to the nobles of the city wherein Naboth dwelt. The letters bade them proclaim a fast. To set Naboth on high among the people. To set two sons of Belial before him, to bear witness against him, saying that he did blaspheme God and the king. And then to carry him out, and stone him, that he might die.

The men of the city did as the letters had bidden them. They proclaimed the fast. They set Naboth on high. The two sons of Belial came in and sat before him and bare witness in the hearing of the people. Then they took Naboth, and carried him forth out of the city, and stoned him with stones, that he died.

A messenger went unto Jezebel. "Naboth is stoned, and is dead."

She went unto Ahab. "Arise," she said. "Take possession of the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite, which he refused to give thee for money. For Naboth is not alive, but dead."

Ahab rose up. He took his attendants. He went down the path unto the vineyard. The gate was open. He stepped through it.

The vines were quiet in the sun. The fruit had begun to set upon them. The rows ran the way Naboth's father had laid them out, and the soil between the rows still held the print of a sandal. Ahab stood among the vines and looked upon the ground.

A man came down the path behind him. Ahab heard the steps. He turned.

Elijah stood at the gate.

The word of the LORD had come unto him in Samaria. "Arise, go down to meet Ahab king of Israel, which is in Samaria. Behold, he is in the vineyard of Naboth, whither he is gone down to possess it. Thou shalt speak unto him."

Ahab looked upon the prophet across the rows. "Hast thou found me, O mine enemy?"

"I have found thee," Elijah said. The vines were between them. The sun was upon the leaves. "Because thou hast sold thyself to work evil in the sight of the LORD."

He stood at the gate of Naboth's vineyard and spake the word he had been sent to speak.

"Hast thou killed, and also taken possession?"

Ahab answered nothing. The vineyard held still around them. The print of the sandal was in the soil between the rows.
