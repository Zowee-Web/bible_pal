The vineyard sat on the slope below Jezreel. It was hard by the palace wall, so that a man on the upper terrace could look down and see the rows. The vines were old. They had been planted by Naboth's grandfather. The stone supports along the rows had been set by Naboth's father. The vines had been pruned each spring by Naboth himself, since he was a boy. The soil between the rows had been worked so long that it held the print of a sandal. This was the inheritance of his fathers. This was the ground that bore them.

In the early summer, Ahab king of Samaria came down the path with two attendants behind him. He stood at the low wall of the vineyard and looked across the slope. The leaves were green. The fruit was beginning to set, hard and pale against the leaves. He called out unto Naboth, and Naboth came up from among the vines, wiping his hands on the front of his garment.

"Give me thy vineyard," Ahab said, "that I may have it for a garden of herbs, because it is near unto my house. And I will give thee for it a better vineyard than this one. Or, if it seem good to thee, I will give thee the worth of it in money."

Naboth listened. He spake not at once. He looked upon the king. Then he looked back at the rows behind him, at the supports his father had set, and at the prints of his own work in the soil.

"The Lord forbid it me," he said, "that I should give the inheritance of my fathers unto thee."

He said no more. He stood in the gate of the vineyard and waited.

Ahab turned and went back up the path. He spake not unto the attendants. The attendants spake not unto him. He came into his house heavy and displeased, and went through the courtyards and into his chamber. He laid him down upon his bed. He turned away his face. A servant brought bread upon a tray and set it beside him, and he would eat none of it. The light slid across the chamber wall through the long hours of the afternoon. Ahab moved not.

Jezebel came in at evening. She stood by the bed and looked down upon him.

"Why is thy spirit so sad," she said, "that thou eatest no bread?"

He told her, his face still to the wall. He told her of the vineyard, and of the offer, and of the inheritance of fathers. She listened to the end.

"Dost thou now govern the kingdom of Israel?" she said. "Arise, and eat bread, and let thine heart be merry. I will give thee the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite."

She went out. In another room she sat down at a table. She called for ink and for parchment. She wrote the letters in Ahab's name. Her hand was steady. When each letter was finished she folded it and pressed the king's seal into the wax. The wax took the print clean. She sent the letters by messengers unto the elders and to the nobles of the city wherein Naboth dwelt. The letters bade them proclaim a fast. To set Naboth on high among the people. To seat two sons of Belial before him, to bear witness against him in the hearing of all the city, that Naboth did blaspheme God and the king. And then to carry him out, and stone him, that he might die.

The men of the city did as the letters had bidden them. They proclaimed the fast. They gathered the people. They set Naboth on high among them, as a man being honored. The two sons of Belial came in and sat before him and bare witness, the same words spoken with the same voice, that Naboth had blasphemed God and the king. The people heard it. The elders rose up. They took Naboth, and carried him forth out of the city, and stoned him with stones in the place outside the gate, that he died.

A messenger went back unto the palace at Samaria. He spake unto Jezebel.

"Naboth is stoned, and is dead."

She went unto Ahab. He was at table. She stood beside him.

"Arise," she said. "Take possession of the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite, which he refused to give thee for money. For Naboth is not alive, but dead."

Ahab rose up. He called for his attendants. He went out from the palace and down the path that led to the slope below the wall.

The gate of the vineyard was open. He stepped through it.

The vines were quiet in the sun. The leaves had not yet begun to turn for the year. The fruit had set upon them, hard and pale, the way it had been when first he stood at the gate. The rows ran the way Naboth's father had laid them out. The stone supports were where Naboth's father had set them. The soil between the rows had been worked so recently that the print of a sandal was still in it, near one of the older vines, where the worker had braced himself to reach. Ahab walked along the row. He called not unto the attendants. He stopped near the print in the soil and looked upon it.

A man came down the path behind him. Ahab heard the steps before he turned. The steps hurried not. They came on with the rhythm of a man who had walked a long way and had been sent.

Ahab turned.

Elijah stood at the gate.

The word of the LORD had come unto him in Samaria. "Arise, go down to meet Ahab king of Israel, which is in Samaria. Behold, he is in the vineyard of Naboth, whither he is gone down to possess it. Thou shalt speak unto him." Elijah had risen and had come.

He stood at the open gate of Naboth's vineyard. His cloak was dusty from the road. He stepped not in. He looked across the rows at the king.

Ahab looked back upon him over the vines. He spake first.

"Hast thou found me, O mine enemy?"

"I have found thee," Elijah said. The vines were between them. The sun was upon the leaves. "Because thou hast sold thyself to work evil in the sight of the LORD."

He lifted not his voice. He raised not his hand. He stood in the gate of the vineyard where Naboth had stood, and he spake the word he had been sent to speak.

"Hast thou killed, and also taken possession?"

Ahab answered nothing. The vines were quiet between them. The fruit had set upon them, hard and pale. The print of the sandal was in the soil, near the older vine where the worker had braced himself to reach. The vineyard held still around the king and the prophet, and the sun was upon the leaves.
