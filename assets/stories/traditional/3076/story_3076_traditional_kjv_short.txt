When they brought forth the money from the house of the Lord, Hilkiah the priest found a book of the law of the Lord given by Moses. He told Shaphan the scribe that he had found the book of the law in the Lord’s house, and Hilkiah delivered the book unto Shaphan.

Shaphan carried it unto the king. He first brought word that the king’s servants did all that had been committed unto them. They had gathered the money found in the house of the Lord and delivered it into the hands of the overseers and workmen. Then Shaphan told the king that Hilkiah had given him a book, and Shaphan read it before the king.

When the king heard the words of the law, he rent his clothes. He commanded Hilkiah, Ahikam the son of Shaphan, Abdon the son of Micah, Shaphan, and Asaiah the king’s servant to enquire of the Lord for him and for those left in Israel and Judah. The king said that great was the wrath of the Lord poured out upon them, because their fathers had not kept the word of the Lord to do all that was written in the book.

The appointed men went unto Huldah the prophetess. She was the wife of Shallum, the son of Tikvath, the son of Hasrah, keeper of the wardrobe, and she dwelt in Jerusalem in the college. They spake to her concerning the king’s command.

Huldah bade them carry the Lord’s word unto the man who had sent them, saying, “Thus saith the Lord.” Evil would come upon that place and its inhabitants, even all the curses written in the book read before the king of Judah. The people had forsaken the Lord and burned incense unto other gods, provoking him with the works of their hands. His wrath should be poured out upon that place and should not be quenched.

Huldah then gave a word for the king of Judah. Because his heart was tender, and because he humbled himself before God when he heard the words against the place and its inhabitants, rent his clothes, and wept before the Lord, the Lord had heard him. He would be gathered unto his fathers and unto his grave in peace. His eyes would not see the evil that would come upon the place and its inhabitants. They brought the king word again.
