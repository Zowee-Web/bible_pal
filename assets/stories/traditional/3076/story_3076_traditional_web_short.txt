As the money brought into Yahweh’s house was being brought out, Hilkiah the priest found the book of Yahweh’s law given by Moses. He told Shaphan the scribe that he had found the book of the law in Yahweh’s house, and he placed the book in Shaphan’s hands.

Shaphan carried it to the king. He first reported that the king’s servants were doing everything committed to them. They had emptied out the money found in Yahweh’s house and delivered it to the overseers and the workmen. Then Shaphan told the king that Hilkiah had given him a book. He read from it before the king.

When the king heard the words of the law, he tore his clothes. He commanded Hilkiah, Ahikam son of Shaphan, Abdon son of Micah, Shaphan, and Asaiah the king’s servant to inquire of Yahweh for him and for those remaining in Israel and Judah. The king said Yahweh’s great wrath had been poured out on them because their fathers had not kept Yahweh’s word or done all that was written in the book.

The appointed men went to Huldah the prophetess. She was the wife of Shallum, son of Tokhath, son of Hasrah, who kept the wardrobe, and she lived in Jerusalem’s second quarter. They spoke to her about the king’s command.

Huldah told them to carry Yahweh’s word to the man who had sent them. Evil would come upon the place and its inhabitants, including the curses written in the book read before the king of Judah. The people had forsaken Yahweh and burned incense to other gods, provoking him with the works of their hands. His wrath had been poured out and would not be quenched.

Huldah then gave a separate word for the king of Judah. Because his heart was tender, because he humbled himself when he heard God’s words against the place and its inhabitants, and because he tore his clothes and wept before Yahweh, Yahweh had heard him. The king would be gathered to his fathers and to his grave in peace. His eyes would not see the evil coming upon the place and its inhabitants. The men brought this message back to the king.
