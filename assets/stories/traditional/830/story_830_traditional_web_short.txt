There was a man who had two sons. The younger of them came to his father one day and said, “Father, give me my share of your property.” So the father divided his estate between them.

Not many days later, the younger son gathered everything he had. He set out for a distant country. The road was long and dusty under his worn sandals. The air grew strange, filled with unfamiliar scents and new sights. There in the far-off land, the younger son wasted his entire inheritance with reckless living.

He spent freely, eating and drinking, seeking pleasure and celebration. But after he had spent all that he had, a severe famine arose in that land. The fields turned barren and empty. People grew hungry. The young man found himself in need, his clothes tattered and his purse empty.

Desperate, he went to work for a man in that country, who sent him into the fields to feed pigs. Hunger gnawed at him as he stood in the mud, surrounded by the stench and noise of the pigs. He longed to eat the carob pods that he fed to them, but no one gave him anything.

He came to himself. “How many of my father’s hired servants have bread enough to spare,” he thought, “and here I am dying with hunger! I will get up and go to my father. I will say, ‘Father, I have sinned against heaven and in your sight. I am no more worthy to be called your son. Make me as one of your hired servants.’”

He rose at once and started on the journey home. His feet were sore, his clothes dirty and torn, the road home stretching long before him. Yet even while he was still far off, his father saw him. Moved with compassion, the father ran. His footsteps pounded the road. He embraced the son and kissed him.

The son began, “Father, I have sinned against heaven and in your sight. I am no longer worthy to be called your son.”

But the father said to his servants, “Bring out the best robe and put it on him. Put a ring on his hand and sandals on his feet. Bring the fattened calf. Kill it. Let us eat and celebrate, for this, my son, was dead and is alive again. He was lost and is found.” So they began to rejoice. Music filled the house. The smell of a rich feast drifted through every room.

The older son was in the field. As he came near the house, he heard music and dancing. He called one of the servants and asked what was happening. The servant replied, “Your brother has come, and your father has killed the fattened calf, because he has received him back safe and healthy.”

The older son became angry and would not go in. His father came out and begged him. But he answered his father, “Look, these many years I have served you. I never disobeyed your command, but you never gave me even a young goat, that I might celebrate with my friends. But when this, your son, who has devoured your living with prostitutes, came, you killed the fattened calf for him.”

The father said to him, “Son, you are always with me, and all that is mine is yours. But it was appropriate to celebrate and be glad, for this, your brother, was dead, and is alive again. He was lost, and is found.”
