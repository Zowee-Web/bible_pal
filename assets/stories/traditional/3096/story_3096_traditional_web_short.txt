A prophet came to the king of Israel with a warning: “Go, strengthen yourself, and plan what you must do, for at the return of the year, the king of Syria will come up against you.” Another battle was coming, and the king was told to prepare.

Meanwhile, the servants of the king of Syria explained their defeat by saying that Israel’s god was a god of the hills. They urged their king to fight Israel on the plain, replace the kings in his force with captains, and muster another army with horse for horse and chariot for chariot. The king listened and did so.

At the return of the year, Ben Hadad gathered the Syrians and went to Aphek. Israel was mustered and supplied for battle. The children of Israel camped before the Syrian host like two little flocks of young goats, while the Syrians filled the country.

Then a man of God came to the king of Israel. Because the Syrians had said that Yahweh was a god of the hills but not of the valleys, Yahweh would deliver that great multitude into Israel’s hand. Israel would know that he was Yahweh.

The armies remained opposite one another for seven days. On the seventh day, battle was joined. Israel struck down one hundred thousand Syrian footmen in one day. The survivors fled into Aphek, where a wall fell on twenty-seven thousand men. Ben Hadad escaped into an inner room.

His servants put sackcloth on their bodies and ropes on their heads, then came out asking Israel’s king to let Ben Hadad live. The king called Ben Hadad his brother and brought him into his chariot.

Ben Hadad promised to restore the cities his father had taken and to allow Israel’s king streets in Damascus. Ahab made a covenant with him and let him go.
