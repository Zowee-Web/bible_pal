A prophet came unto the king of Israel with a warning: “Go, strengthen thyself, and mark, and see what thou doest: for at the return of the year the king of Syria will come up against thee.” Another battle was coming, and the king was commanded to prepare.

The servants of the king of Syria said that Israel’s gods were gods of the hills. They urged their king to fight Israel in the plain, remove the kings from their places, set captains in their rooms, and number another army, horse for horse and chariot for chariot. The king hearkened unto their voice and did so.

At the return of the year, Ben-hadad numbered the Syrians and went up to Aphek. The children of Israel were numbered, were all present, and went against them. Israel pitched before the Syrians like two little flocks of kids, but the Syrians filled the country.

Then a man of God came unto the king of Israel. Because the Syrians had said that the Lord was God of the hills but not God of the valleys, the Lord would deliver that great multitude into Israel’s hand. Israel would know that he was the Lord.

The armies pitched one over against the other for seven days. On the seventh day, the battle was joined. Israel slew an hundred thousand Syrian footmen in one day. The rest fled into Aphek, where a wall fell upon twenty and seven thousand men. Ben-hadad escaped into an inner chamber.

His servants girded sackcloth on their loins and put ropes upon their heads. They went out to Israel’s king and pleaded that Ben-hadad might live. The king called Ben-hadad his brother and caused him to come up into the chariot.

Ben-hadad promised to restore the cities his father had taken and to allow Israel’s king streets in Damascus. Ahab made a covenant with him and sent him away.
