Abram lived in a place called Haran. It sat on a wide, flat plain where trade roads crossed and caravans stopped to water their animals. His tents stood near a well, shaded by thin trees that bent in the afternoon wind. Goats moved slowly through the dry grass. Sarai, his wife, worked near the cooking fire, kneading dough on a flat stone. Lot, his nephew, called out to a servant about a wandering donkey.

It was the kind of day Abram had lived a hundred times before.

Then God spoke to Abram. The words were clear and strong. "Leave your country. Leave your relatives. Leave your father's house. Go to the land that I will show you."

Abram stood still. Leave Haran? Leave the well and the paths he had walked so many times? Go to a place he had never seen, a place God had not even named?

But God was not finished. "I will make you into a great nation. I will bless you and make your name great. You will be a blessing. All the families of the earth will be blessed through you."

That was an enormous promise. Abram did not have any children. He was seventy-five years old. How could a great nation come from a man with no sons or daughters? Abram did not know the answer. But God had spoken, and the words settled deep inside him.

So Abram began to pack.

He pulled tent poles from the hard ground one by one. Sarai rolled up the heavy tent cloths and tied them with strips of leather. She wrapped the cooking pots in thick wool and packed dried fruit into baskets. Water skins were filled to the top and hung from the donkeys' saddles.

Lot packed his own tents nearby. Everyone worked together, moving quickly but carefully.

The animals were gathered next. Sheep and goats moved in a slow, dusty group, nudged along by servants with wooden sticks. Donkeys carried bundles stacked high — jars of grain, rolled sleeping mats, tools for building fires. Everything Abram and Sarai owned was loaded and ready.

Abram looked around at the empty ground where his tent had stood. The circle of stones where the cooking fire had burned still held warm ashes. Haran was familiar. Haran was safe.

But God had said go. So Abram went.

The road led west and south. Abram walked at the front, his walking staff in one hand. Sarai walked near the donkeys. Lot followed with his own animals. Dust rose around their feet. The sun pressed down from a clear sky.

Day after day they walked. At night they set up camp. Sarai lit a fire and cooked lentils in a clay pot. The stars came out thick and bright overhead. In the morning they packed everything up and started again.

The land changed slowly. The flat plain gave way to rocky hills. Rocky hills became wider valleys with more grass and taller trees. They crossed shallow rivers, the water cool against their dusty feet.

Abram kept going. He did not know exactly where the road would end. He only knew that God had told him to go.

At last they came to the land of Canaan. It was wide and green in some places, golden and dry in others. Oak trees grew in thick groves on the hillsides. Wild herbs grew between the rocks, and the air smelled sharp and sweet.

Near a place called Shechem, Abram stopped. A great oak tree stood there, its branches spreading wide. The shade underneath was cool and dark.

Right there, by that great tree, God spoke again. "I will give this land to your offspring."

Abram looked out at the hills rolling away in every direction. This land, for his children. Children he did not have yet. But God had said it, and Abram believed it.

He bent down and picked up a stone. Then another. He stacked them carefully, building an altar right there under the oak tree. An altar to the Lord who had appeared to him. The stones fit together, rough and solid.

From Shechem, Abram moved on to the hill country. He found a spot between two towns called Bethel and Ai. The hill between them gave a wide view of the land — rolling green hills, distant valleys, the shimmer of heat on far ridges.

Abram pitched his tent on that hill. Sarai arranged the cooking stones and unpacked what they needed. Then Abram built another altar. He gathered stones from the hillside and stacked them high. He stood beside it and called on the name of the Lord. His voice rose into the open air, carried by the wind.

The wind moved across the hilltop. Stars began to appear in the darkening sky, one by one, more than anyone could count. Abram had left everything familiar behind. But God was with him, and God had made a promise.
