Abram lived in a place called Haran with his wife Sarai and his nephew Lot. Their tents stood near a dusty road where traders passed with camels and carts. Goats wandered through the dry grass. It was a quiet, familiar life.

Then God spoke to Abram. "Leave your country. Leave your family's land. Go to a place I will show you."

Abram's heart beat fast. Leave Haran? Leave the well where he drew water every morning?

But God said more. "I will make you into a great nation. I will bless you. All the families of the earth will be blessed through you."

Abram believed God. He did not know where this new land was. He had never seen it. But God had spoken, and that was enough.

So Abram packed up everything. He pulled tent poles from the ground. Sarai rolled the heavy tent cloths and wrapped the cooking pots in wool. Lot loaded his own donkeys. Servants carried jars of water on their shoulders.

The animals were gathered — sheep, goats, and donkeys moving together in a slow, dusty group. Everything the family owned was loaded and ready.

They started walking. Dust rose around their sandals. The sun moved slowly overhead. At night they set up tents under bright stars. In the morning they packed up and kept going. Abram was seventy-five years old.

After many days they came to a land called Canaan. Green hills rolled in every direction. Oak trees spread their wide branches over the ground.

Near a place called Shechem, under a great oak tree, God spoke again. "I will give this land to your children and their children after them."

Abram knelt down and stacked stones one on top of another, building an altar. It was his way of saying thank you to God.

He traveled on to a hill between two towns called Bethel and Ai. He pitched his tent and built another altar. His voice rose into the open air as he called on the name of the Lord.

The wind blew softly across the hilltop. Abram had left everything familiar behind. But God was with him, and God had made a promise.
