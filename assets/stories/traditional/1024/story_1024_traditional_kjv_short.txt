Abram dwelt in a place called Haran with Sarai his wife and Lot his nephew. Their tents stood near a dusty road wherein traders passed with camels and carts. Goats wandered through the dry grass. It was a quiet and familiar life.

Then the Lord spake unto Abram. "Get thee out of thy country. Leave the land of thy kindred. Go unto a land that I will shew thee."

Abram's heart beat fast. Leave Haran? Leave the well whereof he drew water every morning?

But God said yet more. "I will make of thee a great nation. I will bless thee. In thee shall all the families of the earth be blessed."

Abram believed God. He knew not where this new land was. He had never beheld it. But God had spoken, and that was enough.

So Abram packed up everything. He drew tent poles out of the ground. Sarai rolled the heavy tent cloths and wrapped the cooking pots in wool. Lot loaded his own asses. Servants bare jars of water upon their shoulders.

The cattle were gathered together — sheep, goats, and asses moving together in a slow and dusty company. All that the family possessed was loaded and ready.

They began to walk. Dust arose about their sandals. The sun moved slowly overhead. By night they pitched their tents under the bright stars. In the morning they packed up and journeyed on. Abram was seventy and five years old.

After many days they came unto a land called Canaan. Green hills rolled in every direction. Oak trees spread their wide branches over the ground.

Near a place called Shechem, under a great oak, the Lord spake again. "Unto thy seed will I give this land, and unto their seed after them."

Abram bowed himself down and stacked stones one upon another, and builded an altar. It was his way of giving thanks unto God.

He journeyed on unto a hill between two cities called Bethel and Ai. He pitched his tent and builded another altar. His voice rose into the open air as he called upon the name of the Lord.

The wind blew softly across the hilltop. Abram had left all that was familiar behind. But God was with him, and God had made a promise.
