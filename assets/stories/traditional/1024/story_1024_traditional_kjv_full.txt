Abram dwelt in a place called Haran. It sat upon a wide and flat plain wherein trade roads crossed and caravans tarried to water their beasts. His tents stood near a well, shaded by thin trees that bent in the afternoon wind. Goats moved slowly through the dry grass. Sarai his wife wrought near the cooking fire, kneading dough upon a flat stone. Lot his nephew called out unto a servant concerning a wandering ass.

It was the manner of day Abram had lived a hundred times before.

Then the Lord spake unto Abram. The words were clear and strong. "Get thee out of thy country. Leave thy kindred. Leave thy father's house. Go unto the land that I will shew thee."

Abram stood still. Leave Haran? Leave the well and the paths he had trodden so many times? Go unto a place he had never beheld, a place God had not even named?

But God was not yet finished. "I will make of thee a great nation. I will bless thee, and make thy name great. Thou shalt be a blessing. In thee shall all the families of the earth be blessed."

That was an exceeding great promise. Abram had no children. He was seventy and five years old. How could a great nation come of a man with neither sons nor daughters? Abram knew not the answer. But God had spoken, and the words settled deep within him.

So Abram began to pack.

He drew tent poles from the hard ground one by one. Sarai rolled up the heavy tent cloths and bound them with strips of leather. She wrapped the cooking pots in thick wool and packed dried fruit into baskets. The water skins were filled up to the top and hung upon the asses' saddles.

Lot packed his own tents hard by. All wrought together, moving with haste but with care.

The cattle were gathered next. Sheep and goats moved in a slow and dusty company, urged along by servants with wooden staves. Asses bare bundles stacked high — jars of grain, rolled sleeping mats, tools for kindling fires. All that Abram and Sarai possessed was loaded and ready.

Abram beheld the empty ground where his tent had stood. The circle of stones whereon the cooking fire had burned yet held warm ashes. Haran was familiar. Haran was safe.

But God had said go. So Abram went.

The road led west and south. Abram walked at the front, his staff in his hand. Sarai walked near the asses. Lot followed with his own beasts. Dust arose about their feet. The sun pressed down out of a clear sky.

Day after day they journeyed on. By night they pitched their camp. Sarai kindled a fire and sod lentils in a clay pot. The stars came forth thick and bright overhead. In the morning they packed up all and began again.

The land changed slowly. The flat plain gave way unto rocky hills. The rocky hills became wider valleys with more grass and taller trees. They passed over shallow rivers, the water cool against their dusty feet.

Abram pressed on. He knew not exactly where the road would end. He knew only that God had told him to go.

At the last they came unto the land of Canaan. It was wide and green in some places, golden and dry in others. Oak trees grew in thick groves upon the hillsides. Wild herbs grew between the rocks, and the air bore the savour, sharp and sweet.

Near a place called Shechem, Abram stayed. A great oak stood there, the branches whereof spread wide. The shade beneath was cool and dark.

Right there, by that great tree, the Lord spake again. "Unto thy seed will I give this land."

Abram beheld the hills rolling away in every direction. This land, for his children. Children he yet had not. But God had said it, and Abram believed it.

He stooped down and took up a stone. Then another. He stacked them carefully, and builded an altar right there beneath the oak. An altar unto the Lord who had appeared unto him. The stones fitted together, rough and solid.

From Shechem, Abram removed unto the hill country. He found a place between two cities called Bethel and Ai. The hill betwixt them gave a wide view of the land — rolling green hills, distant valleys, the shimmering of heat upon far ridges.

Abram pitched his tent upon that hill. Sarai set the cooking stones in order and unpacked what they had need of. Then Abram builded another altar. He gathered stones from the hillside and stacked them high. He stood beside it and called upon the name of the Lord. His voice rose into the open air, carried upon the wind.

The wind moved across the hilltop. Stars began to appear in the darkening sky, one by one, more than any man could number. Abram had left all that was familiar behind. But God was with him, and God had made a promise.
