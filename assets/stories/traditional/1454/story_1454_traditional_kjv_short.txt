The famine had endured seven years.

The Shunammite woman had taken her household out of Israel and had sojourned in the land of the Philistines, even as Elisha had bidden her. She had taken her son. She had taken her servants. She had left her house and her field behind her in Shunem.

At the end of the seven years she returned.

She came up the way from the south with her son walking beside her. He was grown tall in the seven years. The house in Shunem had a stranger within it. The field had been taken of another man. The grain that should have been hers was threshed by hands that were not hers.

She went unto the king at Samaria. She went to cry for her house. She went to cry for her land.

She came up into the court of the king on the day she arrived in the city.

In that same hour, the king talked with Gehazi the servant of the man of God.

"Tell me, I pray thee, all the great things that Elisha hath done," the king was saying.

Gehazi was telling him.

He was in the midst of the tale of the woman whose son Elisha had restored to life — when the door of the court opened.

The woman herself came in.

Gehazi held his peace.

"My lord, O king," said Gehazi, "this is the woman. And this is her son, whom Elisha restored to life."

The king turned in his seat. He beheld her. He beheld the boy beside her.

"Tell me," he said.

She told him. She told it from the beginning — the days of famine, the going forth from Shunem, the years in the land of the Philistines, the way home.

The king called an officer unto him.

"Restore all that was hers," he said. "And all the fruits of the field, from the day that she left the land, even until now."

She went forth out of the court of the king with the officer beside her. Her son walked behind her. The way back to Shunem awaited them at the gate of Samaria.
