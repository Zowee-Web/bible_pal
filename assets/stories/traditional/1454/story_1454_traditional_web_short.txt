The famine had lasted seven years.

The Shunammite woman had taken her household out of Israel and had lived in the land of the Philistines, as Elisha had told her to do. She had taken her son. She had taken her servants. She had left her house and her field behind her in Shunem.

At the end of the seven years she came back.

She came up the road from the south with her son walking beside her. He had grown tall in the seven years. The house in Shunem had a stranger in it. The field had been taken by another man. The grain that should have been hers was being threshed by hands that were not hers.

She went to the king at Samaria. She went to cry for her house. She went to cry for her land.

She came up into the court of the king on the day she arrived in the city.

In that same hour, the king was talking with Gehazi, the servant of the man of God.

"Tell me all the great things that Elisha has done," the king was saying.

Gehazi was telling him.

He was in the middle of the story of the woman whose son Elisha had brought back from death — when the door of the court opened.

The woman herself came in.

Gehazi stopped speaking.

"My lord, O king," Gehazi said, "this is the woman. And this is her son, whom Elisha restored to life."

The king turned in his chair. He looked at her. He looked at the boy beside her.

"Tell me," he said.

She told him. She told it from the beginning — the days of famine, the leaving of Shunem, the years in the land of the Philistines, the road home.

The king called an officer to him.

"Restore all that was hers," he said. "And all the fruits of the field, from the day that she left the land until now."

She went out of the court of the king with the officer beside her. Her son walked behind her. The road back to Shunem was waiting for them at the gate of Samaria.
