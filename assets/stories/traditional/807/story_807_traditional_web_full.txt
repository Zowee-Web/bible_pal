The sun was warm over a small town. Brick homes stood together in neat lines. Gardens grew bright flowers and green vegetables. Chickens clucked quietly near strong wooden fences. The air was gentle. Birds sang softly from leafy trees. In every home, children helped their parents with little jobs. Some brought water from the well. Others swept the ground or arranged baskets of fresh bread.

In the middle of this calm place, workers built a new house. Stones lay in piles. Wood waited in tidy stacks. The workers spoke in soft voices. They passed each stone from hand to hand. Little children watched and carried smooth pebbles. The sound of careful building filled the air. Hammers tapped gently. Birds kept singing overhead. The sky stretched wide and blue.

At the edge of the field, farmers moved slowly across rows of wheat. They checked green leaves and talked about the good weather. Wind danced through tall stalks. The wheat made a quiet rustle. Some people shaped the earth for planting. Others watered little seeds in long rows. The farmers worked with steady hands. The sun felt soft on their backs.

Families gathered bread and fruit. Laughter rose at the midday meal. Some played music on small pipes. Others clapped or tapped their sandals on the ground. Babies slept in shady spots under leafy trees. Dogs dozed at their feet. In every place, people worked with calm hands and calm voices.

Evening arrived with a soft golden glow. The workers set down their stones and tools. The farmers left the green fields. Mothers called children home. Fathers swept doorways. Families came together in their homes. The sun made long lines on the grass. Lamps glowed in windows as gentle night settled over the town.

All through the gentle evening, the families remembered something important. They knew people build houses with their hands. Builders carry stones, set up walls, and place each board. But the people also remembered words spoken long ago: Unless God builds the house, those who build it work for nothing. Unless God watches the city, guards watch for nothing.

The town gates stood open as families moved inside. Two men, wrapped in warm cloaks, quietly checked the gates and walls. They walked slowly, listening to the soft sounds of animals resting. They looked at each home, seeing the lights glow inside. In every home, families finished their work. Children washed their faces. Mothers folded cloth. Fathers gathered small baskets and set them near the door.

The night grew peaceful. People tucked their tools and baskets away. Even the dogs curled quietly at the doors. The workers and farmers remembered more words from long ago: It is useless to get up early in the morning and go late to rest just to eat bread. God gives sleep to the ones he loves.

Inside the houses, children climbed into soft beds. Blankets covered small shoulders. Pillows felt cool and smooth beneath sleepy faces. Lamps shined gently, making little pools of golden light. Mothers whispered quiet songs. Fathers kissed each child’s head. The air was calm and sweet.

Windows opened to the gentle night. The sky was deep and wide. The moon shone over the roofs. Stars sparkled above the fields and walls. All around the town, nothing hurried or rushed. No one needed to stay awake late, hoping their work would last longer. The people put away their tools. They trusted that their houses stood strong and safe. They knew the fields would wait for the morning.

Inside the gate, the two watchmen paused. They sat quietly on smooth stones. Their lanterns glowed a gentle yellow. They listened to the peaceful sounds. Owls called from the high trees. A donkey shifted its feet in the soft hay. Voices from the homes drifted out, low and calm. The watchmen knew they could watch well, but the whole town rested under God’s care.

The night air grew a little cooler. The lamps in the houses winked out one by one. Soon only soft lanterns and starlight filled the town. The builders and the farmers, the children and the mothers, the watchmen and the animals, all found comfort in their cozy places. Blankets covered small toes and big feet. Pillows welcomed every sleepy head. The gentle sounds grew quiet.

The whole town lay in deep, safe rest. Not one child cried out. No dog barked. The wind moved gently through the leaves. The moon quietly watched the sleeping houses. Even the men at the gate folded their arms and let their eyes close. Everything was still. Even the little children’s breathing became slow and soft.

In that calm night, the houses stood strong. The city was peaceful. The new home waited for the morning sun. Nothing hurried. The farmers and builders, the families and helpers, could rest without worry. Their work from the day was finished. Their sleep was given to them as a good gift.

Peace covered every street and backyard. Lamps finished their gentle glow. Windows closed to the cool air. Walls stood steady, built with work and watched by God. Beds held every tired child, each one safe and warm. Every family cuddled close, wrapped in gentle night.

Soon, only the softest music of the breeze and an owl’s call moved in the air. Everyone was peaceful. Safe in their beds. Resting, together. Cared for by hands that never hurried and never missed a thing.

Now, the builders, farmers, mothers, fathers, and every sleepy child closed their eyes. The town was quiet, hugged by moonlight and soft evening peace. It was time to rest all the way through until morning’s gentle light. Safe, sound, warm, and still. Good night, little one. Good night.
