The sun went down softly over the quiet little town. The houses stood close, and their windows caught the soft evening light. Smoke rose slowly from the chimneys. The air bore the savour of baking bread and of pottage upon the hearth. Birds settled upon the rooftops. In one courtyard, a household had finished the evening meal. The garden flowers closed for the night, and a gentle breeze passed through the tall grass.

Round about the town, builders and their helpers laboured the whole day long. Their tools made soft tapping sounds as they set wood and stones in place. Some swept up the sawdust, and others stacked bricks in orderly rows. Each man moved with care, setting every piece where it should rest. Ladders leaned against the new walls, and baskets of nails lay nigh. Children walked along the path, beholding the workmen and humming quiet tunes.

Near the gardens, the people went thoughtfully unto the gates. One man opened them wide at morning, and shut them again at evening. Others swept the stones, that the way might be clean. Door latches clicked softly. Flowers grew along the hedges, pink and blue and yellow. There was no haste, but only peaceful movement and the gentle sounds of home.

When the work was ended, one stood and quietly read the words from a holy scroll. The words said, "Except the Lord build the house, they labour in vain that build it: except the Lord keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain." All hearkened as the words drifted through the evening air. The households remembered these words as they finished their day. The helpers laid away their tools, and the keepers shut the gates in safety.

A gentle hush fell upon the town. Soft voices sang a quiet song beside the doorways. Lanterns shone with a warm light. Children sat near their fathers and their mothers, hearkening unto the peaceful words. The people spread their soft coverings and made ready their pillows. The breeze cooled the air, and the sky turned a deep and restful blue. All were still as the listeners heard, "It is vain for you to rise up early, to sit up late, to eat the bread of sorrows: for so he giveth his beloved sleep."

Soon, throughout the little town, the lights went out one by one. Windows glowed softly for a season, then dimmed. The people rested together, safe and warm. Quiet night sounds passed gently without. In every house, families laid themselves down in peace. The moonlight shone upon the rooftops and the gardens. There was only stillness, and slow and easy breathing. The people slept quietly, kept safe within the peaceful night, ready to wake unto another gentle day.
