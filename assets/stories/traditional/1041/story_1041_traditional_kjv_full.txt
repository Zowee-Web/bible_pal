A man of the house of Levi took to wife a daughter of the same house. They dwelt in Egypt, wherein all the Hebrew people were bondmen. The king of Egypt — Pharaoh — was cruel. He was afraid the Hebrew people were waxen too many, too strong. So he gave a terrible commandment: every man child born unto the Hebrews must be cast into the river.

Into that perilous time, a babe was born. A son. His mother held him and beheld his face. He was a goodly child. She made up her mind — she would suffer none to harm him.

She hid him within the house three months. When he wept, she rocked him. When he fretted, she fed him with haste. She kept the windows covered and the door shut fast.

But babes do grow. They wax louder. Their cries are heard the further. After three months, she could hide him no longer. Someone would hear. Someone would report them. She had to think of somewhat else.

She found an ark of bulrushes — the same kind of reeds that grew thick and tall along the river's brink. She daubed the outside with slime and with pitch, pressing it into every gap and crack until no water could come in. She lined the inside with soft cloth.

Then she did the hardest thing a mother could do. She laid her babe within the ark. She wrapped him snug and warm. She kissed his forehead. Then she bare the ark down unto the river and set it gently among the flags at the water's brink.

The ark bobbed upon the slow current. The tall flags hid it from any that walked by. The babe looked up at the blue sky and the green stalks swaying over him.

The babe's elder sister stood afar off, hidden nearby. She crouched in the flags where none could see her. She kept her eyes upon the ark. Her task was to watch and wait.

The sun climbed higher. The river glistened. Dragonflies hummed over the water. The babe was quiet for a season.

Then voices. Footsteps upon the path. The daughter of Pharaoh — the princess herself — came down unto the river to wash herself. Her handmaidens walked along the brink beside her, bearing towels and oils.

The princess stayed her steps. She had espied somewhat among the flags. A small ark, floating among the green stalks. "What is that?" said she. She sent one of her handmaidens to wade in and fetch it unto her.

The handmaid bare the ark up the bank and set it down. The princess kneeled and opened the lid. Within was a babe, weeping. His face was scrunched up and red. Tears ran down his round cheeks. His little fingers opened and closed.

The princess had compassion upon him. She reached in and touched his soft skin. "This is one of the Hebrews' children," she said softly. She knew what her father had commanded. But she could not turn away from a weeping babe.

That was when the elder sister made her move. She had been watching all from her hiding place. Her heart beat like a drum in her bosom. She was but a maid — to speak unto a princess was a fearful thing. But she came forth from the flags and walked right up unto Pharaoh's daughter.

"Shall I go and call thee a nurse of the Hebrew women," she said, "that she may nurse the child for thee?"

The princess looked upon this brave maid. "Go," said she.

The sister turned and ran. Her sandals slapped the dusty path. She ran all the way home and burst through the door. "Come!" she said unto her mother. "Come with haste!"

The mother came. She stood before Pharaoh's daughter, striving to keep her face calm, striving not to stare at her own babe in the princess's arms.

"Take this child away, and nurse it for me," said the princess. "I will give thee thy wages."

The mother took the babe. Her babe. She held him against her bosom and felt his warmth, his breathing, his heart beating against hers. He was safe. He was alive. And she would be the one to raise him, at least for a season.

The mother took the child home and nursed him. She fed him and sang unto him and watched him grow from a babe into a little child. She told him of God. She told him of his people.

And when the child was grown, his mother brought him unto Pharaoh's daughter. He became her son. She called his name Moses. "Because I drew him out of the water," said she.

The babe from the ark now dwelt in the palace. But he would never forget whence he came — nor the brave mother and sister who saved his life.
