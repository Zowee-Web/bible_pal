A man of the house of Levi took to wife a daughter of the same house. They dwelt in Egypt, wherein the Hebrew people were bondmen. Pharaoh was afraid the Hebrews were waxen too many. He gave a terrible commandment: every man child born unto the Hebrews must be cast into the river.

A man child was born. His mother held him and beheld his face. He was a goodly child. She would suffer none to harm him. She hid him within the house three months.

But a babe waxeth louder as it groweth. She could no longer hide him. She had to do somewhat brave.

She found an ark of bulrushes and daubed it with slime and with pitch, that the water might not come in. She laid her babe within, wrapped him snug, and bare the ark unto the river. She set it among the tall flags at the water's brink.

The ark rocked upon the slow current. The babe blinked up at the blue sky.

The babe's elder sister abode nearby, hidden in the flags. She crouched low. Her task was to watch and wait.

The daughter of Pharaoh came down unto the river to wash herself. Her handmaidens walked along the brink. The princess espied the ark among the flags. "Go fetch it unto me," she said unto her handmaid.

The handmaid brought the ark. The princess opened it. The babe wept, his face red and scrunched. She had compassion on him. "This is one of the Hebrews' children," said she.

The elder sister came forth from her hiding place. Her heart beat sore, yet she spake up. "Shall I go and call thee a nurse of the Hebrew women, that she may nurse the child for thee?"

"Go," said the princess.

The maid ran, and brought her own mother.

"Take this child away, and nurse it for me," said the princess. "I will give thee thy wages."

The mother took her babe again into her arms. She held him close. He was safe. He was alive.

And when the child was grown, she brought him unto Pharaoh's daughter. He became her son. She called his name Moses. "Because I drew him out of the water," said she.
