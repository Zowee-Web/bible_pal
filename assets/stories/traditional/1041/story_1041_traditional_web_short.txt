A man from the family of Levi married a woman from the same family. They lived in Egypt, where the Hebrew people were slaves. Pharaoh was afraid the Hebrews were becoming too many. He gave a terrible order: every baby boy born to the Hebrews must be thrown into the river.

A baby boy was born. His mother held him and looked at his face. He was beautiful. She would not let anyone hurt him. She hid him in the house for three months.

But a baby gets louder as it grows. She could not hide him any longer. She had to do something brave.

She found a basket made of reeds and covered it with tar and pitch to keep the water out. She laid her baby inside, wrapped him snug, and carried the basket to the river. She set it among the tall reeds at the water's edge.

The basket rocked on the slow current. The baby blinked up at the blue sky.

The baby's big sister stayed nearby, hiding in the reeds. She crouched low. Her job was to watch and wait.

Pharaoh's daughter came down to the river to bathe. Her servant girls walked along the bank. The princess noticed the basket among the reeds. "Go get that for me," she told her servant.

The servant brought the basket. The princess opened it. The baby was crying, his face red and scrunched. She felt sorry for him. "This is one of the Hebrews' children," she said.

The big sister stepped out from her hiding spot. Her heart pounded, but she spoke up. "Should I find a Hebrew woman to nurse the baby for you?"

"Yes, go," the princess said.

The girl ran and brought back her own mother.

"Take this child and nurse him for me," the princess said. "I will pay you."

The mother took her baby back into her arms. She held him close. He was safe. He was alive.

When the child was old enough, she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter. He became her son. She named him Moses. "Because I drew him out of the water," she said.
