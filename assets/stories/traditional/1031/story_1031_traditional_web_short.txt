The people of Israel had left Egypt. They traveled through the wilderness — a wide, dry land with rocky ground and very little shade. It had been about six weeks since they crossed the sea. Their food was running low.

The whole community began to grumble against Moses and Aaron. "If only we had died in Egypt," they said. "At least there we sat by pots of meat and ate bread until we were full. You have brought us out here to starve."

Then the Lord spoke to Moses. "I am going to rain down bread from heaven for you. The people shall go out and gather a day's portion every day."

God also told Moses that in the evening, the people would eat meat. And in the morning, they would have bread. Then they would know that the Lord was their God.

Moses and Aaron told the people what God had said. "In the evening you shall know that it was the Lord who brought you out of Egypt. In the morning you shall see the glory of the Lord."

That evening, quail came. The small birds covered the camp, landing everywhere on the ground. The people gathered them up for meat.

In the morning, something new appeared. When the dew dried up, thin flakes lay on the ground. They were small and white, like frost on the desert floor. The people looked at them and said to each other, "What is it?" They had never seen anything like it.

Moses said, "It is the bread the Lord has given you to eat. Everyone gather as much as you need. Take an omer for each person in your tent."

The people went out and gathered. Some gathered a lot. Some gathered a little. But when they measured it out, a wonderful thing happened. The one who gathered much did not have too much. The one who gathered little did not have too little. Everyone had exactly enough.

God fed his people in the wilderness. Right there in the empty, dry desert where nothing grew, bread appeared on the ground every single morning.
