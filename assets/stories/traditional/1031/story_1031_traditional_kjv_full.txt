The whole congregation of the children of Israel took their journey from a place called Elim and came unto the wilderness of Sin. It lay between Elim and Sinai, a wide stretch of dry, empty land. Rocky ground stretched on every side under a blazing sky. The sun beat down without mercy. There was no shade. No green fields. No markets with bread and fruit set forth in baskets.

It was the fifteenth day of the second month after they were departed out of Egypt. About six weeks of journeying. And the people were an hungered.

The whole congregation murmured against Moses and Aaron. The complaints rose up from every tent and every campfire.

"Would to God we had died by the hand of the Lord in the land of Egypt!" the people said. Their voices were wroth and afraid. "There we sat by the flesh pots. There we did eat bread to the full. But ye have brought us forth into this wilderness, to kill this whole assembly with hunger."

It was a hard thing to hear. Moses and Aaron had not chosen of themselves to lead the people into the wilderness. The Lord had sent them. But the people were afraid, and fearful folk speak sharp words.

Then the Lord spake unto Moses. "Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you. The people shall go out and gather a certain rate every day, that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no."

Bread to rain down. From heaven. In the midst of the wilderness, where nothing grew, the Lord was about to send food falling out of the sky.

The Lord added a special commandment for the sixth day. On that day, the people should gather twice as much as they gathered upon the other days.

Moses and Aaron spake unto the people. "At even," they said, "then ye shall know that the Lord hath brought you out from the land of Egypt. And in the morning, then ye shall see the glory of the Lord; for that he heareth your murmurings against the Lord."

Then Moses added somewhat weighty. "And what are we? Your murmurings are not against us, but against the Lord."

Moses told Aaron to speak unto the whole congregation. "Come near before the Lord," Aaron said, "for he hath heard your murmurings."

And it came to pass, as Aaron spake, the people turned and looked toward the wilderness. And behold, in the cloud that journeyed with them, the glory of the Lord appeared. Bright and shining, filling the sky.

The Lord spake unto Moses again. "I have heard the murmurings of the children of Israel. Speak unto them, saying, 'At even ye shall eat flesh, and in the morning ye shall be filled with bread. And ye shall know that I am the Lord your God.'"

That evening, the quails came up. The small brown birds flew in from somewhere beyond the horizon and settled upon the camp. They covered the ground, fluttering and resting every where — about the tents, between the cooking fires, across the open spaces. The people gathered them and had flesh at supper.

In the morning, when the dew was gone up from off the ground, somewhat else appeared. Behold, upon the face of the wilderness there lay a small round thing. They were small and fine, almost as the hoar frost or tiny seeds scattered upon the rocky ground. They were white and delicate, covering the earth round about the camp.

The people came forth from their tents and beheld. They stooped down and gathered the flakes, turning them over in their fingers. They had never seen the like.

"What is it?" they said one to another. In Hebrew, the words sounded like "man hu." And so it was called by that name — manna.

Moses said unto them, "This is the bread which the Lord hath given you to eat. This is the thing which the Lord hath commanded: Gather of it every man according to his eating. Take an omer for every man, according to the number of your persons which are in his tents."

An omer was a small measure, about enough to fill a large bowl. One omer for every man.

The children of Israel went forth and gathered. Some laboured swiftly and gathered much. Some moved more slowly and gathered less. Families with many children gathered more. Small families gathered less.

But when they brought the manna back to their tents and measured it with an omer, a wondrous thing came to pass. He that had gathered much measured it out, and it came to exactly one omer for every man. No more. He that had gathered little measured it out, and it was also exactly one omer for every man. No less.

Every man had exactly according to his eating. Nothing over. No lack. Even so.

The people stood in the morning light, holding bowls of manna, beholding one another in wonder. The Lord had fed them. In the midst of a wilderness where nothing grew and there was no food to be found, bread had appeared upon the ground as the dew. And every man had exactly according to his eating.
