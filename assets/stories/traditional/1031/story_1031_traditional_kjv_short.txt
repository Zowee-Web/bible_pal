The people of Israel had departed out of Egypt. They journeyed through the wilderness — a wide, dry land with rocky ground and very little shade. It had been about six weeks since they passed through the sea. Their victuals were running low.

The whole congregation began to murmur against Moses and Aaron. "Would to God we had died in the land of Egypt," they said. "There we sat by the flesh pots and did eat bread to the full. Ye have brought us forth into this wilderness to kill us with hunger."

Then the Lord spake unto Moses. "Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you. The people shall go out and gather a certain rate every day."

The Lord also told Moses that at even, the people should eat flesh. And in the morning, they should be filled with bread. Then should they know that the Lord was their God.

Moses and Aaron told the people what the Lord had said. "At even, then ye shall know that the Lord hath brought you out from the land of Egypt. And in the morning, then ye shall see the glory of the Lord."

That evening, the quails came. The small birds covered the camp, alighting every where upon the ground. The people gathered them for flesh.

In the morning, somewhat new appeared. When the dew was gone up, behold, upon the face of the wilderness there lay a small round thing. It was small and white, as the hoar frost on the ground. The people looked thereon and said one to another, "What is it?" They had never seen the like.

Moses said, "This is the bread which the Lord hath given you to eat. Gather of it every man according to his eating. Take an omer for every man, according to the number of your persons."

The people went out and gathered. Some gathered much. Some gathered little. But when they measured it with an omer, a wondrous thing came to pass. He that gathered much had nothing over. He that gathered little had no lack. Every man had exactly according to his eating.

The Lord fed his people in the wilderness. Right there in the empty, dry desert where nothing grew, bread appeared upon the ground every morning.
