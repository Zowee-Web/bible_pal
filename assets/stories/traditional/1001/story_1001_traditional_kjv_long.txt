The shepherd walketh at the fore, and the flock followeth after him upon a hillside where the grass groweth thick and green, each blade bending soft beneath the weight of morning dew. The earth is damp underfoot, and the smell of it riseth from the ground — rich, dark, carrying the scent of root and rain-soaked soil. His rod is in his hand, worn smooth at the grip where his fingers have closed around it through many seasons, the wood pale and polished there, though rough and knotted still along its length. His staff he carrieth upon his shoulder, the crook of it curving against the grey sky above him.

He leadeth the sheep downward along a gentle slope, and they come unto a place where the pasture openeth wide. The grass here is not sparse nor brittle, but full and soft, standing to the height of a lamb's shoulder. It swayeth when the wind passeth over it, and the whole field moveth together like water. The shepherd setteth himself upon a stone at the edge of the green place, and the sheep spread outward, lowering their heads to graze. The sound of their feeding is quiet — the tearing of grass, the shifting of small hooves upon soft ground. No predator stirreth at the borders. No cry of alarm breaketh the stillness.

And he maketh them to lie down in these green pastures, for the flock, having eaten, foldeth their legs beneath them one by one upon the cool earth. Their wool presseth flat against the grass. Their sides rise and fall with slow breath. A ewe lieth near the stone where the shepherd sitteth, her jaw working gently, her eyes half-closed. The lambs are gathered close beside their mothers, and the whole company resteth there upon the green.

Then the shepherd riseth and goeth before them again, and he leadeth them beside the still waters. The stream runneth through a low place between two hills, and it is not swift. It doth not churn over rocks nor rush between narrow banks. It moveth slowly, so slowly that the surface is nearly flat, and the reflection of the sky lieth unbroken upon it. The water is clear to its bed, and the stones beneath are smooth and brown and gold, visible through the stillness. The sheep come to the edge and lower their mouths to drink, and the water scarce rippleth where their lips touch it. The sound is faint — a soft lapping, a small disturbance, then silence again. The shepherd standeth upstream, watching, his staff planted in the mud beside him, and the print of it remaineth there, round and deep.

He restoreth their soul. When one lamb hath strayed too far and standeth bleating at the edge of a thicket, the shepherd goeth after it. His feet press through the undergrowth, snapping dry twigs, pushing aside the tangle of low branches. He reacheth down and taketh hold of the lamb and lifteth it, setting it upon his shoulders, and he carrieth it back to where the others graze. The lamb's legs hang down against his chest, and its small voice groweth quiet as he walketh.

He leadeth them in the paths of righteousness for his name's sake. The way is not broad, but narrow — a track worn into the hillside by the passing of many flocks before, the dirt packed firm, the edges lined with stone and thistle. The shepherd chooseth this path and not another. He turneth not aside to the left hand nor to the right, and the flock followeth close behind him, their hooves falling into the same beaten ground his sandals have marked.

Yea, though they walk through the valley of the shadow of death, the path descendeth. The hills rise up on either side, and the light groweth dim between them. The walls of rock are close, and the air is cooler here, carrying the smell of damp stone and moss. Shadows lie long across the trail, and the sky above is only a narrow band between the ridges. The sound of the flock's movement echoeth faintly against the rock — the click of hoof on stone, the rustle of wool brushing against a ledge. In this dark passage, the shepherd's rod is in his hand, and his staff is before him, and he walketh steadily. The sheep follow. They do not scatter. The rod and the staff are visible to them — the rod lifted where danger might lurk, the staff extended to guide a wandering lamb back toward the center of the path. These are the instruments of his keeping, and they are present in the dark as they were present in the open field.

The valley endeth, and the land openeth again. A table is prepared.

It is spread in the presence of enemies. Across the far ridge, figures are visible — distant, watching. Wolves, perhaps, or men whose flocks contend for the same ground. They stand along the height and do not approach. Yet here, in the open, the shepherd hath laid provision. A flat stone serveth as the place of eating, and upon it are bread and oil and fruit. The cup is set there also, and it is full — filled to the brim and past it, so that the wine runneth over the edge and down the side of the vessel, pooling dark upon the stone beneath. The cup overfloweth, and the excess runneth freely, and there is no scarcity at this table. The shepherd anointeth the head with oil, pouring it from a small vessel, and it runneth down over the brow and along the jaw, gleaming where the remaining light toucheth it. The scent of the oil is sharp and clean, carrying the fragrance of pressed olives and herbs.

The enemies do not draw near. The table remaineth undisturbed. The bread is broken, the cup is lifted, and the oil shineth upon the skin. All of this taketh place in full view of those who watch from the ridge, and yet the provision is not diminished, nor is the table overturned.

Surely goodness and mercy shall follow all the days of life. Not as a promise spoken into the air, but as a thing observable — the shepherd behind the flock as well as before it, the green pastures and the still waters and the narrow path and the dark valley and the table spread in the open. Goodness and mercy are the companions that walk the same road, present at each turning, visible at each station of the journey.

And the dwelling shall be in the house of the Lord for ever. The flock cometh at last to the fold — a stone enclosure set against the side of a hill, its walls built thick and high, its gate narrow. The shepherd standeth at the gate, and each sheep passeth before him, and he counteth them as they enter. His hand toucheth the back of each one. The last lamb passeth through, and the shepherd closeth the gate behind it. Inside, the ground is covered with dry straw, and the walls block the wind, and the fold is solid and enduring.

The shepherd sitteth at the gate. His rod lieth across his knees. His staff leaneth against the wall beside him. The flock is gathered within, and the gate is shut, and the night cometh down over the hills, and the fold standeth firm upon the earth.
