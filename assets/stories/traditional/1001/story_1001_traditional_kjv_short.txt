A shepherd walketh at the fore of his flock, and the sheep follow after him through a valley where the grass groweth thick and green beside still waters. His rod resteth upon his shoulder, and his staff toucheth the ground with each measured step. The flock moveth without haste. No beast driveth them forward, and no cry of alarm soundeth among them. The shepherd leadeth, and they follow.

He bringeth them down along the bank where the water lieth quiet, gathered in a shallow bend of the stream. It doth not rush. It doth not churn. The surface holdeth steady as glass, and the sheep lower their heads and drink. The shepherd standeth near, watching the far ridge and the low places between the hills.

When they have drunk their fill, he turneth and leadeth them along a path that windeth through a narrow place. The walls of stone rise close on either hand. Shadow falleth deep across the trail, and the light of the sun reacheth not to the floor of the pass. The air groweth cool. The sound of their hooves echoeth against the rock. It is the valley of the shadow of death, and the shepherd doth not slow his pace nor turn aside. He walketh forward, and the flock followeth behind him. His rod is in his hand. His staff is before him. He striketh the ground once, and the sound ringeth sharp through the darkness.

They pass through. The land openeth again, and there, upon a broad and level place, a table is spread. Bread and oil and a cup filled to the brim are set upon it. Round about, upon the far slopes, figures stand watching — those who have set themselves against the one who sitteth down to eat. Yet the table is laid, and the shepherd anointeth the head of his charge with oil. It runneth down, glistening. The cup is lifted, and it overfloweth, spilling across the stone.

Goodness and mercy follow after, as sure as the flock that traileth behind the shepherd — not as a promise spoken into the air, but as two presences that walk the same road, step upon step, day upon day. They do not lag behind. They do not lose the trail.

And the dwelling place at the end of the journey is the house of the Lord. The door standeth open. The shepherd leadeth the way through it, and the sound of his footfall upon the threshold is the last thing heard — steady, unhurried, passing from the bright afternoon into the deep stillness within. The flock gathereth at the gate. The green pastures lie behind them. The still waters lie behind them. The shadow of the valley lieth behind them. And the door remaineth open.
