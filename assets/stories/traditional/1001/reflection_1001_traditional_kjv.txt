Quiet waters rarely announce themselves. They do not rush or roar — they simply wait, steady and unhurried, offering what is needed without fanfare. A shepherd knows this. The flock does not.

Sheep wander toward noise, toward the flash of moving current, toward whatever seems most alive in the moment. And yet it is the still water that restores. It is the path they did not choose that turns out to be the right one. The shepherd's voice behind them, beside them, sometimes ahead of them — always closer than it feels.

There is a table mentioned in this ancient song, and it sits in no safe place. It is spread in the presence of enemies, not in their absence. The oil on the head, the cup running over — these arrive not when trouble has passed but while it lingers. Something about provision seems to work this way, appearing most clearly against the backdrop of what threatens.

Goodness and mercy following all the days of a life — not leading, not pulling, but following. As though they have been there longer than anyone noticed, quiet as still water, steady as a shepherd's hand.
