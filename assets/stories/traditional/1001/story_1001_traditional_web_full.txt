A man sits on a stone in open country, a lyre resting across his knees. The flock grazes on the slope below him, their wool catching the last warmth of afternoon. He draws a breath, and his voice lifts across the hillside.

"Yahweh is my shepherd; I shall not lack."

The sheep move slowly through tall grass that bends under a low wind. The pasture stretches wide, green and thick, fed by recent rain. The animals lower their heads and pull at the blades, unhurried. No predator circles. No drought has browned the ground. The land is full, and the flock eats.

"He makes me lie down in green pastures."

Some of the sheep have already folded their legs beneath them, settling into the soft turf. Their sides rise and fall. The grass cushions them, and the breeze passes over their backs without chill. The shepherd watches from his stone, his staff planted upright in the earth beside him.

"He leads me beside still waters."

Below the pasture, a stream moves through a low channel between mossy rocks. The water runs clear and quiet, barely making a sound where it pools in the shallows. It does not rush. It does not churn. The sheep that drink from it stand with their hooves at the edge, lowering their mouths to the surface without startling. The water holds steady, reflecting the green bank and the open air above it.

"He restores my soul."

The man pauses. His fingers brush the strings of the lyre, and a single note rings out and fades over the hillside.

"He guides me in the paths of righteousness for his name's sake."

A worn trail leads from the pasture down through a narrow pass between two ridges. The path is not wide, but it is firm underfoot — packed earth, cleared of loose stone. The shepherd has walked it many times. His sandals have pressed the dust smooth. The flock follows that same trail when he calls, their hooves falling into the grooves his feet have made.

"Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for you are with me."

The trail descends. The ridges on either side rise steep and dark, blocking the light. Shadows pool along the base of the rock walls. The air grows cooler. Sounds carry strangely — the click of a hoof echoes, the rustle of a lizard across gravel seems louder than it should. The path narrows further. Thorns grow from cracks in the stone. But the shepherd walks ahead, his rod in one hand, his staff in the other, and the sheep follow close behind his legs. His footsteps are steady. He does not slow.

"Your rod and your staff, they comfort me."

The rod is short and heavy, shaped from a dense branch, meant for striking. The staff is longer, curved at the top, used for pulling a wandering lamb back to the path. Both are worn smooth from years of gripping. The shepherd carries them where the flock can see them — one in each hand, visible against the dim walls of the valley.

"You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies."

The valley opens. Ahead, on a flat shelf of ground, a low table has been spread with bread, oil, dried fruit, and a skin of wine. The man sets down his lyre and walks to it. Around the edges of the clearing, shadows move — the shapes of jackals or wolves, watching from the rocks. They do not approach. The man sits at the table. He tears the bread. He eats.

"You anoint my head with oil."

He lifts a small clay vessel from the table and pours oil over his own head. It runs down through his hair, catching the remaining light, slipping along his temples and into his beard. The scent of it — olive, pressed and fragrant — rises in the still air.

"My cup runs over."

A clay cup sits on the table, filled past the brim. Wine spills over the edge and darkens the wood beneath it. The man lifts the cup with both hands and drinks. The excess runs down his wrists.

"Surely goodness and loving kindness shall follow me all the days of my life."

He sets the cup down and looks behind him. The trail he has walked — through green pasture, beside still water, down through the shadowed valley — stretches back as far as he can see. The flock has followed him out of the pass and now gathers near the table, pressing close.

"And I will dwell in Yahweh's house forever."

In the distance, beyond the clearing, a structure stands against the sky — stone walls, a doorway, lamplight visible from within. The man rises from the table. He takes up his staff. The sheep lift their heads as he begins to walk toward that doorway, and one by one, they follow. His sandals press the dust. The lamplight grows brighter with each step. The flock moves behind him in a long, quiet line, their wool brushing the grass on either side of the path.
