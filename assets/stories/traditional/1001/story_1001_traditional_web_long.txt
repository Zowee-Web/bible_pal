A shepherd walks at the front of his flock across a wide stretch of open country. His staff, a long rod of olive wood worn smooth along the grip, rests against his shoulder. The hem of his outer garment brushes the tops of low scrub as he moves. Behind him, sheep follow in a loose column, their hooves pressing soft prints into the dry soil. The morning air carries the faint smell of wild thyme crushed underfoot.

He leads them down a gradual slope where the ground begins to change. The pale dust gives way to darker earth, then to soil that holds moisture. Grass appears — first in scattered patches between stones, then thickening into an unbroken green that stretches ahead in every direction. The shepherd stops at the edge of this meadow and turns to watch his flock arrive. One by one the sheep step onto the green pasture. They lower their heads and begin to eat. The grass is dense and cool, beaded with dew that has not yet burned off. Small white flowers grow among the blades, and insects move between the stems with a low, steady hum.

The shepherd does not hurry them. He stands with his staff planted in the ground and lets them graze. The sun climbs higher, and the shadows of the sheep shorten on the green. A young lamb strays toward the edge of the flock, and the shepherd taps the ground once with his rod. The lamb turns back. The flock spreads across the meadow, feeding without disturbance. There is no sound of predators, no bark of wild dogs from the surrounding hills. Only the tearing of grass, the occasional bleating, and the drone of bees moving through clover.

When the sun reaches its highest point and the heat presses down, the shepherd lifts his staff and begins walking again. The sheep raise their heads and follow. He leads them along a worn track that curves between two low hills, and the ground descends gently. The sound of water reaches them before they see it — a soft, continuous murmur, almost hidden beneath the breeze. The track opens onto a flat bank where a stream runs slow and clear over a bed of smooth stones. The water is shallow, no more than a hand's breadth deep at the center, and it moves without turbulence. Sunlight catches the surface and breaks into shifting patterns on the pale stones beneath.

The shepherd crouches at the bank and cups water in his hands. He drinks, then steps aside. The sheep move to the edge and lower their muzzles to the still water near the bank where the current barely pulls. They drink for a long time. The water is cold and clean, fed from somewhere higher in the hills. Dragonflies skim the surface downstream, and a kingfisher sits motionless on a low branch that extends over the far bank. The flock drinks without crowding, spreading along the water's edge where the bank is level and the footing is firm.

After they have drunk, the shepherd leads them on. The track rises again, winding through rockier terrain where the soil is thinner and thorn bushes grow in dense clusters. The sheep move more slowly here. A ewe stumbles on a loose stone and staggers sideways. The shepherd is beside her in three strides. He braces her flank with one hand and steadies her until she finds her footing. She walks on. He guides them along the surest path, tapping stones ahead with his staff to test the ground, choosing the way where the footing is most solid.

The path narrows further and enters a valley where the walls rise steeply on both sides. Rock faces tower above, casting deep shadow across the trail. The temperature drops. The air smells of damp stone and moss. The passage is tight enough that the sheep must walk in single file, their wool brushing the rock on either side. Overhead, the strip of visible sky is thin and distant. Water seeps down the rock walls in dark streaks, and the sound of their hooves echoes off the close stone. The valley floor is uneven, strewn with fallen rock and patches of loose gravel.

The shepherd walks ahead, his staff striking the ground with a steady rhythm that carries back through the narrow space. Each tap sounds clearly against the stone — a measured, repeating beat. His rod is in his other hand now, held ready. The sheep follow the sound. Where the path turns sharply and the shadow deepens so that almost no light reaches the ground, the shepherd slows his pace. He speaks — a low, steady call, the same syllables repeated. The sheep press closer together and keep moving. The valley bends again, and ahead, a thin line of brightness marks the far opening. Step by step, the flock moves toward it. The shepherd's staff keeps its rhythm. The walls begin to pull apart, the shadow lifts, and the flock emerges into open air on the far side.

Beyond the valley, the ground levels into a broad clearing. A flat stone sits at the center, large enough to serve as a table, its surface swept clean by wind. The shepherd sets down his leather bag and unpacks what he has carried — a round of bread, a small jar of pressed oil, a skin of wine, dried fruit wrapped in cloth. He breaks the bread and lays it on the stone. He unstops the oil and pours a measure into a shallow clay cup. Around the clearing, the marks of other animals are visible — paw prints in the soft earth at the edges, scratches on the bark of a nearby tree. The shepherd eats in plain view of the open ground, his rod laid across his knees within reach.

He takes the clay cup of oil and turns to the nearest sheep. He presses his thumb into the oil and draws it across the animal's head, working it into the wool above the eyes and along the bridge of the nose where flies gather and thorns leave scratches. The oil is fragrant — a sharp, clean smell of pressed olives mixed with something herbal. He moves from one sheep to the next, anointing each head, rubbing the oil into small cuts and raw patches where the skin is broken. The sheep stand still under his hands.

Then he lifts the wineskin and fills a wooden cup, pouring until the dark liquid rises above the rim and spills over the edge, running down the side and dripping onto the stone below. He sets the cup down on the flat rock. The wine pools around its base in a dark ring. The bread, the oil, the overflowing cup — all of it rests on the stone in the open clearing while the marks of predators line the ground around the edges.

The afternoon light lengthens across the clearing. The shepherd gathers his things, shoulders his bag, and takes up his staff and rod. He begins walking again, and the flock follows. The path leads back toward lower ground where the grass grows thick and the land is familiar. The track is the same one they have walked before — the same meadow visible in the distance, the same stream curving through the low ground ahead. The shepherd moves at an unhurried pace, and the sheep walk behind him, their wool still carrying the scent of oil. The green pasture opens before them once more, and the sound of water reaches them again from below. The shepherd leads them forward along the path, and the flock follows, and the road continues on.
