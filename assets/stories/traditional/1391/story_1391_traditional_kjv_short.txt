It was the four and twentieth day of the eleventh month, in the second year of king Darius. Zechariah the son of Berechiah, the grandson of Iddo the priest, had been preaching now for several months in Jerusalem; the foundation of the second temple lay still in the ground, the work having been delayed and resumed and delayed again; and on that night the word of the Lord came unto him in a vision.

Upon the four and twentieth day of the eleventh month, which is the month Shebat, in the second year of Darius, the word of the Lord came unto Zechariah, the son of Berechiah, the son of Iddo the prophet, saying,

"I saw by night, and behold a man riding upon a red horse, and he stood among the myrtle trees that were in the bottom; and behind him were there red horses, speckled, and white.

Then said I, 'O my lord, what are these?'"

The angel that talked with me said unto me, "I will shew thee what these be."

The man that stood among the myrtle trees answered, "These are they whom the Lord hath sent to walk to and fro through the earth."

And they answered the angel of the Lord that stood among the myrtle trees, and said, "We have walked to and fro through the earth, and, behold, all the earth sitteth still, and is at rest."

Then the angel of the Lord answered and said, "O Lord of hosts, how long wilt thou not have mercy on Jerusalem and on the cities of Judah, against which thou hast had indignation these threescore and ten years?"

And the Lord answered the angel that talked with me with good words and comfortable words.

So the angel that communed with me said unto me, "Cry thou, saying, 'Thus saith the Lord of hosts: I am jealous for Jerusalem and for Zion with a great jealousy.

And I am very sore displeased with the heathen that are at ease; for I was but a little displeased, but they helped forward the affliction.'

Therefore thus saith the Lord: 'I am returned to Jerusalem with mercies: my house shall be built in it, saith the Lord of hosts, and a line shall be stretched forth upon Jerusalem.'

Cry yet, saying, 'Thus saith the Lord of hosts: My cities through prosperity shall yet be spread abroad; and the Lord shall yet comfort Zion, and shall yet choose Jerusalem.'"

And the vision faded into the dark of the night. The myrtle trees stood still in their bottom; the horses were gone back unto the going to and fro of their labour; and Zechariah turned and wrote down what he had been shewn. The angel's cry — how long? — had been answered with words of mercy; the work of the temple should not be put off for ever; and the city that had sat threescore and ten years under indignation should be comforted again at Jerusalem.
