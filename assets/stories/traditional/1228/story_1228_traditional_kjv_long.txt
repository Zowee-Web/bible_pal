"Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen."

It is the word the writer chose to begin with. He set it down at the head of his epistle the way a man setteth down a stone at the head of a road — the way is going to be long, and this is the first marker. Faith. Substance. Evidence. He did not begin with an argument. He began with a definition. Then he began to name the names of them that had walked by it before.

For by it, he wrote, the elders obtained a good report.

Through faith, he said, we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God — so that things which are seen were not made of things which do appear. There had been a word, and there had been a making. The substance of the world was older than the eye that looked upon it. To begin with this was to set the whole list of names that followed within a frame larger than any of them. Then he turned unto the men and women themselves.

He began with Abel.

By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain. The writer did not tell the story again. He had no need to. Every man that heard the epistle knew the field, and the altar, and the brother that came after with the stone. What the writer said of Abel was this: by the sacrifice he obtained witness that he was righteous. And by it, he being dead yet speaketh. The blood upon the ground had not finished its sentence. The voice of it was still going up out of the earth. Faith, when it had once spoken, ceased not to speak — not even after the throat had been stilled.

Then Enoch.

By faith Enoch was translated that he should not see death. He was not found, the writer said, because God had translated him. For before his translation he had this testimony, that he pleased God. The walking with God in the cool of the day. The years of it. The slow turning of seasons over the head of a man who kept walking — and then, one day, the place where he had been was empty, and the path he had been walking was empty, and his name passed out of the rolls of the living without an answer to give.

The writer paused, between Enoch and Noah, to say a thing he would have his hearers remember. But without faith, he wrote, it is impossible to please him. For he that cometh to God must believe that he is — and that he is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him. He set the sentence down within the list as a man setteth a lamp upon a shelf at the middle of a long room, that what came before and what came after should be lighted by the same light. Then he went on.

By faith Noah, being warned of God of things not seen as yet, moved with fear. He prepared an ark to the saving of his house. The sky above Noah's head had given no warning that any other could read. The ground beneath his feet was dry. There were neighbours that looked on. There were neighbours that laughed. The years passed. The wood was hewn. The pitch was laid. The rooms were framed. Noah ceased not. By the building, the writer said, he condemned the world. By the building, he became heir of the righteousness which is by faith.

Then Abraham.

By faith Abraham, when he was called to go out into a place which he should after receive for an inheritance, obeyed. He went out, the writer set down plainly, not knowing whither he went. There were no maps in Abraham's hand. There were no scouts before him. There was only the call, and the road, and the going. He left the country where his father's house stood. He left the country where his father's grave waited. He went out.

By faith he sojourned in the land of promise, as in a strange country. He dwelt in tabernacles with Isaac and Jacob, the heirs with him of the same promise. They could be folded. The stakes could be drawn out of the ground. Nothing he stood within was a wall. He looked, the writer said, for a city which hath foundations — whose builder and maker is God. He had been called unto a country, and he had been given tabernacles. The country was farther on.

Through faith also Sarah herself received strength to conceive seed, and was delivered of a child when she was past age, because she judged him faithful who had promised. The laughter that had risen in her at the tent door had not been the last word. The womb that had been shut had been opened. Therefore sprang there even of one, and him as good as dead, so many as the stars of the sky in multitude. The writer let the line stand without explanation. The dead had become a father. The barren had become a mother. The promise had broken open the bodies that had given it over.

Then the writer stopped naming, and he stepped back from his list, and he set down a sentence that was meant to be remembered.

These all died in faith, not having received the promises. They had seen them afar off. They were persuaded of them. They embraced them. They confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth. They desire a better country, that is, an heavenly. Wherefore God is not ashamed to be called their God. For he hath prepared for them a city.

The city was prepared. The names had been gathered. The list was not finished.

By faith Abraham, when he was tried, offered up Isaac. He that had received the promises offered up his only begotten son. He accounted — the writer used the word — that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead. From whence also, the writer said, he received him in a figure. The knife had been lifted, and the knife had been lowered, and the son had walked down the mountain at his father's side, and the father had received his son out of the place where faith had given him over.

By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau concerning things to come.

By faith Jacob, when he was a dying, blessed both the sons of Joseph, and worshipped, leaning upon the top of his staff. The old man, the writer wrote, did not lay his hands upon the heads of his grandsons in his own strength. The staff held him up. The blessing went forth.

By faith Joseph, when he died, made mention of the departing of the children of Israel. And he gave commandment concerning his bones.

The bones would not stay where they were. The body of Joseph would lie in Egypt, and the people of Joseph would lie in Egypt, but neither one of them was going to stay. Faith had reached past the grave again — even as it had reached past Abel's altar, even as it had reached past Enoch's empty path, even as it had reached past Sarah's shut womb. The promise was farther on. The writer set the last name down, and the faith of them went on speaking.
