"Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen."

It is the word the writer chose to begin with. He set it down at the head of his epistle the way a man setteth down a stone at the head of a road — the way is going to be long, and this is the first marker. Faith. Substance. Evidence. He did not begin with an argument. He began with a definition. Then he began to name the names of them that had walked by it before.

Through faith, he wrote, we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that things which are seen were not made of things which do appear. Then he turned unto the men and women themselves.

Abel.

By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain. By it he obtained witness that he was righteous. And by it, the writer said — he being dead yet speaketh. The blood upon the ground had not finished its sentence. The voice of it had not been stilled. Faith, when it had once spoken, ceased not to speak.

Enoch.

By faith Enoch was translated that he should not see death. He was not found, because God had translated him. For before his translation he had this testimony, that he pleased God.

The writer paused beside that name. But without faith, he wrote, it is impossible to please him. For he that cometh to God must believe that he is, and that he is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him. Then he went on.

Noah.

By faith Noah, being warned of God of things not seen as yet, moved with fear. He prepared an ark to the saving of his house. The sky above Noah's head had given no warning that any other could read. The ground beneath his feet was dry. He built notwithstanding. By the which he condemned the world, and became heir of the righteousness which is by faith.

Abraham.

By faith Abraham, when he was called to go out into a place which he should after receive for an inheritance, obeyed. He went out, not knowing whither he went. The writer set the line down plainly. There were no maps in Abraham's hand. There were no scouts before him. There was only the call, and the road, and the going.

By faith he sojourned in the land of promise, as in a strange country. He dwelt in tabernacles with Isaac and Jacob, the heirs with him of the same promise. They could be folded. The stakes could be drawn out of the ground. Nothing he stood within was a wall. He looked for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God.

Through faith also Sarah herself received strength to conceive seed, and was delivered of a child when she was past age, because she judged him faithful who had promised. Therefore sprang there even of one, and him as good as dead, so many as the stars of the sky in multitude.

Then the writer stopped naming, and he stepped back from his list, and he set down a sentence that was meant to be remembered.

These all died in faith, not having received the promises. They had seen them afar off. They were persuaded of them. They embraced them. They confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth. They desire a better country, that is, an heavenly. Wherefore God is not ashamed to be called their God. For he hath prepared for them a city.

Then he returned unto his list.

By faith Abraham, when he was tried, offered up Isaac. Yea, he that had received the promises offered up his only begotten son, accounting that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead. From whence also, the writer said, he received him in a figure.

By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau concerning things to come.

By faith Jacob, when he was a dying, blessed both the sons of Joseph, and worshipped, leaning upon the top of his staff.

By faith Joseph, when he died, made mention of the departing of the children of Israel, and gave commandment concerning his bones.

The bones would not stay where they were. The faith had reached past the grave again.
