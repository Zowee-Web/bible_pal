"Now faith is assurance of things hoped for, proof of things not seen."

It is the word the writer chose to begin with. He set it down at the head of his letter the way a man sets down a stone at the head of a road — the way is going to be long, and this is the first marker. Faith. Assurance. Proof. He did not begin with an argument. He began with a definition. Then he began to name the names of those who had walked by it before.

By faith, he wrote, we understand that the universe has been framed by the word of God, so that what is seen has not been made out of things which are visible. Then he turned to the men and women themselves.

Abel.

By faith Abel offered to God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain. Through it he had testimony given to him that he was righteous. And through it, the writer said — being dead, he still speaks. The blood on the ground had not finished its sentence. The voice of it had not stopped. Faith, when it had spoken once, did not stop speaking.

Enoch.

By faith Enoch was taken away, so that he would not see death. He was not found, because God translated him. Before his translation, he had testimony given to him that he had been well pleasing to God.

The writer paused beside that name. Without faith, he wrote, it is impossible to be well pleasing to him. For he who comes to God must believe that he exists, and that he is a rewarder of those who seek him. Then he went on.

Noah.

By faith Noah, being warned about things not yet seen, moved with godly fear. He prepared an ark for the saving of his house. The sky over Noah's head had given no warning that anyone else could read. The ground beneath his feet was dry. He built anyway. He became heir of the righteousness which is according to faith.

Abraham.

By faith Abraham, when he was called, obeyed to go out to the place which he was to receive for an inheritance. He went out, not knowing where he went. The writer set the line down plainly. There were no maps in Abraham's hand. There were no scouts ahead. There was only the call, and the road, and the going.

By faith he lived as an alien in the land of promise, as in a land not his own. He dwelt in tents with Isaac and Jacob, the heirs with him of the same promise. The tents could be folded. The tent stakes could be drawn from the ground. Nothing he stood inside was a wall. He looked for the city which has the foundations, whose builder and maker is God.

By faith even Sarah herself received power to conceive. She bore a child when she was past age, since she counted him faithful who had promised. Therefore as many as the stars of the sky in multitude were fathered by one man, and him as good as dead.

Then the writer stopped naming, and he stepped back from his list, and he set down a sentence that was meant to be remembered.

These all died in faith, not having received the promises. They had seen them. They had embraced them from afar. They had confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth. They desired a better country, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed of them, to be called their God. For he has prepared a city for them.

Then he returned to his list.

By faith Abraham, being tested, offered up Isaac. He who had gladly received the promises was offering up his only begotten son, concluding that God is able to raise up even from the dead. Figuratively speaking, he did receive him back.

By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau, even concerning things to come.

By faith Jacob, when he was dying, blessed each of the sons of Joseph, and worshiped, leaning on the top of his staff.

By faith Joseph, when his end was near, made mention of the departure of the children of Israel, and gave instructions concerning his bones.

The bones would not stay where they were. The faith had reached past the grave again.
