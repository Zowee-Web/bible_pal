Mesha king of Moab was a sheep breeder, and he supplied the king of Israel with one hundred thousand lambs and the wool of one hundred thousand rams. But when Ahab was dead, the king of Moab rebelled against the king of Israel. King Jehoram went out of Samaria, mustered all Israel, and sent to Jehoshaphat the king of Judah: “The king of Moab has rebelled against me. Will you go with me against Moab to battle?” Jehoshaphat answered, “I will go up. I am as you are, my people as your people, my horses as your horses.” They chose the way of the wilderness of Edom.

So the king of Israel went with the king of Judah and the king of Edom. They marched seven days by a circuitous route, and there was no water for the army or the animals with them. The king of Israel said, “Alas! For Yahweh has called these three kings together to deliver them into the hand of Moab.” But Jehoshaphat asked for a prophet of Yahweh, and a servant answered that Elisha the son of Shaphat was there, who poured water on the hands of Elijah. “Yahweh’s word is with him,” Jehoshaphat said, and the three kings went down to him.

Elisha told the king of Israel to go to the prophets of his father and of his mother, and only for the sake of Jehoshaphat’s presence would he look toward him. “But now bring me a musician.” When the musician played, Yahweh’s hand came on Elisha. Yahweh said to make the valley full of trenches; without wind or rain it would be filled with water for them and their livestock. This was an easy thing in Yahweh’s sight; he would also deliver the Moabites into their hand.

In the morning, about the time of offering the sacrifice, water came by the way of Edom, and the country was filled with water. The Moabites had gathered at the border, and when they rose early, the sun shone on the water, and they saw it opposite them as red as blood. “This is blood,” they said. “The kings are surely destroyed, and they have struck each other. Now therefore, Moab, to the plunder!” But when they came to the camp, the Israelites rose up and struck them, and the Moabites fled. Israel went forward into the land, beat down the cities, marred the good fields with stones, stopped the springs, and cut down the good trees, until only the stones of Kir Hareseth were left, and the slingers attacked it.

When the king of Moab saw that the battle was too severe for him, he took seven hundred swordsmen to break through to the king of Edom, but they could not. Then he took his oldest son, who would have reigned in his place, and offered him for a burnt offering on the wall. There was great wrath against Israel, and they departed from him and returned to their own land.
