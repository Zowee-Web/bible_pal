The king returned and came to the Jordan. Judah came to Gilgal to meet him and bring him across the river. Shimei son of Gera, the Benjaminite from Bahurim, hurried down with the men of Judah. A thousand men of Benjamin accompanied him. Ziba, servant of Saul’s house, also came with his fifteen sons and twenty servants. They crossed the Jordan in the king’s presence.

A ferry boat carried over the king’s household and served his wishes. When David had crossed the Jordan, Shimei fell before him. He asked the king not to charge him with iniquity or remember the wrong Shimei had done when David left Jerusalem. Shimei said he knew he had sinned and had come as the first from the house of Joseph to meet the king.

Abishai son of Zeruiah asked whether Shimei should die because he had cursed Yahweh’s anointed. David rejected the sons of Zeruiah as adversaries to him that day. He asked whether anyone should be put to death in Israel on the day he knew he was king over Israel. David told Shimei that he would not die, and the king swore this to him.

Mephibosheth son of Saul also came to meet the king. From the day David departed until the day he returned in peace, Mephibosheth had not groomed his feet, trimmed his beard, or washed his clothes. When he came to Jerusalem, David asked why he had not gone with him.

Mephibosheth answered that his servant had deceived him. Because Mephibosheth was lame, he had said that a donkey should be saddled so he could ride with the king. He said his servant had slandered him to David. Calling the king as an angel of God, Mephibosheth told David to do what was good in his eyes.

Mephibosheth said that all his father’s house had been as dead men before David, yet David had placed him among those who ate at the king’s table. He asked what further right he had to appeal. David ended the matter by ordering Mephibosheth and Ziba to divide the land. Mephibosheth answered that Ziba could take it all, since his lord the king had returned in peace to his own house.
