In the month of Nisan, in the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes, wine was set before the king. Nehemiah took the wine and gave it to him. He had never been sad in the king's presence before.

The king said, "Why is your face sad, since you are not sick? This is nothing but sadness of heart."

Nehemiah was very much afraid. He said, "Let the king live forever. Why should my face not be sad, when the city, the place of my fathers' graves, lies in waste, and its gates have been destroyed by fire?"

The king said, "What are you requesting?"

Nehemiah prayed to the God of heaven. Then he spoke. "If it pleases the king, and if your servant has found favor in your sight, send me to Judah, to the city of my fathers' graves, that I may rebuild it."

The king asked how long he would be gone. Nehemiah set a time. The king granted his request. Nehemiah asked for letters to the governors beyond the River for safe passage, and timber from the king's forest for the gates and the wall.

The king gave him everything.

Nehemiah came to Jerusalem. He rested three days. Then he rose in the night — he and a few men with him. He told no one what God had put in his heart to do.

He went out by night through the Valley Gate toward the Dragon's Spring and the Dung Gate. He inspected the walls of Jerusalem that were broken down and the gates that had been destroyed by fire. The stones lay tumbled. The charred wood of the old gates lay in heaps.

He passed on to the Fountain Gate and the King's Pool. There was no room for the animal to pass. He went up by the valley in the night, inspecting the wall, then turned back and entered by the Valley Gate.

The officials did not know where he had gone or what he was doing.

Then he said to them, "You see the trouble we are in. Jerusalem lies in ruins and its gates are burned with fire. Come, let us build the wall of Jerusalem, that we may no longer suffer derision."

He told them of the hand of God that had been upon him and the words the king had spoken.

They said, "Let us rise up and build." And they strengthened their hands for the good work.
