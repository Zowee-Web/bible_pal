In the month Nisan, in the twentieth year of Artaxerxes the king, wine was before him. And Nehemiah took up the wine and gave it unto the king, as was his custom. He was the king's cupbearer. He tasted the wine ere the king drank. His hands were steady. His countenance was trained to shew nothing.

But that day his face betrayed him.

And the king said unto him, "Why is thy countenance sad, seeing thou art not sick? This is nothing else but sorrow of heart."

Then was Nehemiah very sore afraid. A cupbearer with a troubled countenance was a cupbearer who might not be trusted. A sad face in the king's presence could betoken suspicion or worse. But he spake.

"Let the king live for ever: why should not my countenance be sad, when the city, the place of my fathers' sepulchres, lieth waste, and the gates thereof are consumed with fire?"

The walls of Jerusalem had lain broken these many years. The gates burned. The stones scattered abroad. Nehemiah had heard the reports from them that returned, and the weight of it sat behind his breast like a stone he could not set down.

Then the king said, "For what dost thou make request?"

So Nehemiah prayed. Not aloud — there was neither time nor place for it in the king's hall, the wine yet upon the table. He prayed unto the God of heaven in the space betwixt the question and his answer.

And he said, "If it please the king, and if thy servant have found favour in thy sight, that thou wouldest send me unto Judah, unto the city of my fathers' sepulchres, that I may build it."

And the queen sat by the king. The court was still. Nehemiah stood with the empty cup yet in his hand.

And the king said unto him, "For how long shall thy journey be? And when wilt thou return?"

So Nehemiah set him a time. And it pleased the king to send him. Nehemiah asked further — letters unto the governors beyond the river for safe conduct, and a letter unto Asaph the keeper of the king's forest, that he might give him timber to make beams for the gates of the palace by the temple, and for the wall of the city, and for the house that he should enter into.

And the king granted him all his requests, according to the good hand of his God upon him.

So Nehemiah came unto Jerusalem and was there three days. The city smelled of dust and ancient fire. Weeds grew up betwixt the fallen stones.

Then he arose in the night. He told no man what God had put in his heart to do at Jerusalem. He took with him some few men and one beast — the beast whereupon he rode — and went forth after dark.

He went out by night by the gate of the valley, even before the dragon well, and to the dung port, and viewed the walls of Jerusalem which were broken down, and the gates thereof which were consumed with fire. The stones lay where they had fallen — great blocks half buried in earth, lesser stones scattered upon the ground as broken teeth. The charred remains of the gates stood in blackened heaps. Thorns grew through the ruin.

He went on to the gate of the fountain and to the king's pool. But there was no place for the beast that was under him to pass. The rubble was so thick a man could scarce find footing. Nehemiah went down from the beast and continued on foot, picking his way among the broken masonry. He went up by the brook in the night and viewed the wall, running his hand along the shattered stone, measuring the ruin with his eyes and with his fingers.

Then he turned back and entered in by the gate of the valley.

And the rulers knew not whither he went, or what he did. Neither had he as yet told it to the Jews, nor to the priests, nor to the nobles, nor to the rulers, nor to the rest that did the work.

Then said he unto them, "Ye see the distress that we are in, how Jerusalem lieth waste, and the gates thereof are burned with fire: come, and let us build up the wall of Jerusalem, that we be no more a reproach."

Then he told them of the hand of his God which was good upon him, as also the king's words that he had spoken, and the provision that had been made — the letters, the timber, the leave to go.

Then said they, "Let us rise up and build."

So they strengthened their hands for this good work.
