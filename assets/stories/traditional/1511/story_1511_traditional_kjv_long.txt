The messengers came back to Jerusalem in the late afternoon. They had been sent by Hezekiah unto the camp of the Assyrians at Lachish, and they returned with a letter in their hands. The wax was still upon it. The seal was the seal of Sennacherib king of Assyria. The messengers walked through the streets of the city and up unto the palace, and the people watched them pass without speaking.

Hezekiah received the letter of the hand of the messengers. He turned it in his hands. The parchment was heavy. The seal sat hard against his thumb. He brake it. He read.

The letter said, "Let not thy God in whom thou trustest deceive thee, saying Jerusalem shall not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria." The letter named the cities the king of Assyria had taken. Hamath. Arpad. Sepharvaim. Hena. Ivvah. One by one, the cities. The letter named the gods of those cities, the gods that had not delivered them. "The gods of the nations have not delivered them," the letter said. "The God of Israel shall not deliver thee either."

Hezekiah finished reading. He spake not. He folded the letter once, slowly, along the crease that had been folded into it before. The chamber about him was quiet. His counsellors stood at a distance and came not near.

He went down from the palace. He went out into the street. He carried the letter with him in his hands.

The streets of Jerusalem were not empty. People stood in doorways. Women looked down from rooftops. The merchants had closed their stalls. Word had moved through the city already that the Assyrian army was at Lachish and that the messengers had brought a letter. Now the people saw the king walking, and they saw what was in his hands, and they called not out to him. He passed through them and they let him pass.

He walked up toward the house of the LORD.

The temple stood above the city on its high ground. The steps were stone, worn smooth in their middles by years of feet. Hezekiah climbed them. He hasted not. He paused not. He went up the steps with the letter in his hands. He came in through the gates. He crossed the outer court. He came into the inner court. There he stopped.

He stood before the LORD.

He bent down. He spread the letter open upon the stone floor of the temple. He smoothed it with his hands. He laid it flat that every line thereof might be read. The names of the cities. The names of the dead gods. The taunt against the living God. He spread it all out upon the floor, where the LORD could see it.

Then he kneeled down beside it.

He prayed. "O LORD God of Israel, which dwellest between the cherubims, thou art the God, even thou alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth. Thou hast made heaven and earth. LORD, bow down thine ear, and hear. Open, LORD, thine eyes, and see. Hear the words of Sennacherib, which hath sent him to reproach the living God. Of a truth, LORD, the kings of Assyria have destroyed the nations and their lands. They have cast their gods into the fire. They were no gods. They were the work of men's hands, of wood and of stone. Therefore they have destroyed them. Now therefore, O LORD our God, I beseech thee, save thou us out of his hand, that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that thou art the LORD God, even thou only."

He finished. He stayed upon the floor where he was. The letter lay open beside him. The temple was very quiet. Nothing answered him out of the air. The light through the high windows moved a little along the stones. He rose not. He waited.

Without the temple, in another part of the city, the word of the LORD came unto Isaiah the son of Amoz. Isaiah rose. He sent word unto the king.

The word came: "Thus saith the LORD God of Israel. That which thou hast prayed to me against Sennacherib king of Assyria I have heard."

The word kept coming. It was a long word. "The virgin the daughter of Zion hath despised thee, and laughed thee to scorn," the word said. "The daughter of Jerusalem hath shaken her head at thee. Whom hast thou reproached and blasphemed? and against whom hast thou exalted thy voice, and lifted up thine eyes on high? even against the Holy One of Israel. Thou hast boasted of thy chariots. Thou hast boasted of climbing the mountains and cutting down the cedars of Lebanon. Thou hast boasted of drying up the rivers of Egypt with the sole of thy feet. But the LORD hath known thee. The LORD knoweth thy sitting down, thy going out, thy coming in. Therefore I will put my hook in thy nose, and my bridle in thy lips, and I will turn thee back by the way by which thou camest."

Then the word concerning the city: "He shall not come into this city, nor shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with shield, nor cast a bank against it. By the way that he came, by the same shall he return, and shall not come into this city, saith the LORD. For I will defend this city, to save it, for mine own sake, and for my servant David's sake."

The word reached Hezekiah where he was yet kneeling. He heard it. He stayed upon the temple floor a little longer with the letter beside him. Then he rose.

That night the angel of the LORD went forth. The angel went forth from the LORD and the angel came unto the camp of the Assyrians. The camp lay without the city. The fires were burning low. The watchmen were at the edges. The horses were tied. The men were sleeping in their tents.

The angel passed through the camp. The angel smote in the camp of the Assyrians an hundred fourscore and five thousand.

The horses still stood where they had been tied. The fires had burned out. The watchmen lay where they had stood. The men in the tents had not risen. When the camp awoke in the morning, behold, they were all dead corpses. They lay as they had laid down. The few who remained alive walked out into the gray light and saw the rows of tents and could not believe what they were beholding.

Sennacherib king of Assyria departed. He went and returned. He went back the way that he had come, even as the word of the LORD had spoken. He came at last unto Nineveh, and he dwelt at Nineveh.

It came to pass, as he was worshipping in the house of Nisroch his god, that his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer came in upon him with the sword. They smote him there in the temple of his god. They escaped into the land of Armenia. Esarhaddon his son reigned in his stead.

And the letter that had been spread open upon the floor of the temple in Jerusalem, the letter that had named the cities and the dead gods and the threat against the living God, that letter had been answered.
