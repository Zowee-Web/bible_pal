The messengers came back to Jerusalem with a letter. They had been sent by Hezekiah to the camp of the Assyrians at Lachish. The letter was from Sennacherib king of Assyria. Hezekiah read it.

The letter said: do not let your God deceive you, saying Jerusalem shall not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria. The letter named the cities the king of Assyria had taken. The letter named the gods of those cities that had not delivered them. The letter said: the gods of the nations have not delivered them. The God of Israel will not deliver you either.

Hezekiah went up to the house of the LORD. He carried the letter with him through the streets of the city. He went up the steps of the temple. He came in before the LORD. He spread the letter open on the stone floor.

He prayed. "O LORD God of Israel, who dwells between the cherubim, you are the God, even you alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth. You have made heaven and earth. Bow down your ear, O LORD, and hear. Open your eyes, O LORD, and see. Hear the words of Sennacherib that he has sent to reproach the living God. It is true, LORD. The kings of Assyria have destroyed the nations and their lands. They have cast their gods into the fire. They were no gods. They were the work of men's hands, of wood and stone. Therefore they have destroyed them. Now therefore, O LORD our God, save us out of his hand. Let all the kingdoms of the earth know that you are the LORD God, even you only."

Isaiah the son of Amoz sent to Hezekiah. The word came: thus says the LORD God of Israel: I have heard you. The word of the LORD concerning the king of Assyria is this: He shall not come into this city. He shall not shoot an arrow there. He shall not come before it with shield. He shall not cast a bank against it. By the way that he came, by the same shall he return. He shall not come into this city.

That night the angel of the LORD went out into the camp of the Assyrians. The camp was outside the city. The fires were burning low. The watchmen were at the edges. The horses were tied. The men were sleeping in their tents.

The angel passed through the camp. The angel smote in the camp of the Assyrians a hundred and eighty-five thousand. The horses still stood where they had been tied. The fires had burned out. When the camp awoke in the morning, the men were not there. The morning found them dead in their tents, lying as they had laid down. The few who remained alive looked across the camp. They could not believe what they saw.

Sennacherib king of Assyria departed. He went and returned. He dwelt at Nineveh. There he was killed in his own house by his own sons, in the temple where he was worshiping.
