The messengers came back to Jerusalem in the late afternoon. They had been sent by Hezekiah to the camp of the Assyrians at Lachish, and they returned with a letter in their hands. The wax was still on it. The seal was the seal of Sennacherib king of Assyria. The messengers walked through the streets of the city and up to the palace, and the people watched them pass without speaking.

Hezekiah received the letter. He turned it in his hands. The parchment was heavy. The seal sat hard against his thumb. He broke it. He read.

The letter said, "Do not let your God deceive you, saying Jerusalem shall not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria." The letter named the cities the king of Assyria had taken. Hamath. Arpad. Sepharvaim. Hena. Ivvah. One by one, the cities. The letter named the gods of those cities, the gods that had not delivered them. "The gods of the nations have not delivered them," the letter said. "The God of Israel will not deliver you either."

Hezekiah finished reading. He did not speak. He folded the letter once, slowly, along the crease that had been folded into it before. The room around him was quiet. His advisors stood at a distance and did not come closer.

He went down from the palace. He went out into the street. He carried the letter with him in his hands.

The streets of Jerusalem were not empty. People stood in doorways. Women looked down from rooftops. The merchants had closed their stalls. Word had moved through the city already that the Assyrian army was at Lachish and that the messengers had brought a letter. Now the people saw the king walking, and they saw what was in his hands, and they did not call out to him. He passed through them and they let him pass.

He walked up toward the house of the LORD.

The temple stood above the city on its high ground. The steps were stone, worn smooth in their middles by years of feet. Hezekiah climbed them. He did not hurry. He did not pause. He went up the steps with the letter in his hands. He came in through the gates. He crossed the outer court. He came into the inner court. There he stopped.

He stood before the LORD.

He bent down. He spread the letter open on the stone floor of the temple. He smoothed it with his hands. He laid it flat so that every line of it could be read. The names of the cities. The names of the dead gods. The taunt against the living God. He spread it all out on the floor, where the LORD could see it.

Then he knelt down beside it.

He prayed. "O LORD God of Israel, who dwells between the cherubim, you are the God, even you alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth. You have made heaven and earth. Bow down your ear, O LORD, and hear. Open your eyes, O LORD, and see. Hear the words of Sennacherib that he has sent to reproach the living God. It is true, LORD. The kings of Assyria have destroyed the nations and their lands. They have cast their gods into the fire. They were no gods. They were the work of men's hands, of wood and stone. Therefore they have destroyed them. Now therefore, O LORD our God, save us out of his hand. Let all the kingdoms of the earth know that you are the LORD God, even you only."

He finished. He stayed on the floor where he was. The letter lay open beside him. The temple was very quiet. Nothing answered him from the air. The light through the high windows moved a little along the stones. He did not get up. He waited.

Outside the temple, in another part of the city, the word of the LORD came to Isaiah the son of Amoz. Isaiah rose. He sent word to the king.

The word came: "Thus says the LORD God of Israel. You have prayed to me against Sennacherib king of Assyria. I have heard you."

The word kept coming. It was a long word. The virgin daughter of Zion has despised him, the word said. She has laughed him to scorn. The daughter of Jerusalem has shaken her head at him. Whom has he reproached? Whom has he blasphemed? Against whom has he exalted his voice? Against the Holy One of Israel. He has boasted of his chariots. He has boasted of climbing the mountains and cutting down the cedars of Lebanon. He has boasted of drying up the rivers of Egypt with the soles of his feet. But the LORD has known him, the word said. The LORD knows his sitting down, his going out, his coming in. The LORD will put a hook in his nose and a bridle in his lips, and the LORD will turn him back by the way that he came.

Then the word concerning the city: he shall not come into this city. He shall not shoot an arrow there. He shall not come before it with shield. He shall not cast a bank against it. By the way that he came, by the same shall he return. He shall not come into this city. For I will defend this city to save it, says the LORD, for my own sake and for my servant David's sake.

The word reached Hezekiah where he was still kneeling. He heard it. He stayed on the temple floor a little longer with the letter beside him. Then he rose.

That night the angel of the LORD went out. The angel went out from the LORD and the angel came to the camp of the Assyrians. The camp lay outside the city. The fires were burning low. The watchmen were at the edges. The horses were tied. The men were sleeping in their tents.

The angel passed through the camp. The angel smote a hundred and eighty-five thousand in the camp of the Assyrians.

The horses still stood where they had been tied. The fires had burned out. The watchmen lay where they had stood. The men in the tents had not risen. When the camp awoke in the morning, behold, they were all dead bodies. They lay as they had laid down. The few who remained alive walked out into the gray light and saw the rows of tents and could not believe what they were seeing.

Sennacherib king of Assyria departed. He went and returned. He went back the way that he had come, just as the word of the LORD had spoken. He came at last to Nineveh, and he dwelt at Nineveh.

It came to pass, as he was worshiping in the house of Nisroch his god, that his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer came in upon him with the sword. They struck him there in the temple of his god. They escaped into the land of Ararat. Esarhaddon his son reigned in his place.

And the letter that had been spread open on the floor of the temple in Jerusalem, the letter that had named the cities and the dead gods and the threat against the living God, that letter had been answered.
