Isaac's two sons grew up, and they were not alike. Esau became a skillful hunter, a man of the open field. Jacob was a quiet man, who stayed among the tents. Isaac loved Esau, because he had a taste for the wild game his son brought home. But Rebekah loved Jacob.

One day Jacob was boiling stew. Esau came in from the field, and he was famished. He looked at the red stew and said, "Please, let me eat some of that red stew, for I am famished." That is why he was also called Edom, which means Red.

Jacob said, "First, sell me your birthright."

The birthright was Esau's as the firstborn son — his portion, and his place at the head of the family after his father. But Esau said, "Look, I am about to die. What good is a birthright to me?"

"Swear it to me first," Jacob said. So Esau swore an oath to him, and sold his birthright to his brother.

Then Jacob gave Esau bread and lentil stew. He ate, and he drank, and he rose up, and he went on his way. For a single bowl of stew he had traded away the birthright of the firstborn.

So Esau despised his birthright.
