Samuel came to Saul with the word of the Lord. The old prophet stood before the young king and spoke the charge plainly. The Lord remembered what Amalek had done to Israel in the way when they came up out of Egypt. Now Saul was to go and strike Amalek and utterly destroy all that they had. Nothing was to be spared.

Saul listened. Saul gathered the people.

He summoned them to Telaim and counted them, two hundred thousand footmen and ten thousand men of Judah. He moved on the city of Amalek and set an ambush in the valley. He sent word to the Kenites to depart from among the Amalekites, for they had shown kindness to Israel long ago. The Kenites went out. Then Saul struck the Amalekites, from Havilah as far as Shur, which is before Egypt.

He took Agag the king alive. He utterly destroyed all the people with the edge of the sword. But the best of the sheep and the cattle, the fat calves and the lambs, all that was good — these the king and the people spared. They were not willing to utterly destroy them. Everything that was vile and refuse, that they destroyed.

That night the word of the Lord came to Samuel.

"It grieves me that I have set up Saul to be king, for he has turned back from following me, and has not performed my commandments."

The old man heard it in the dark. He sat up. The grief in the word was not his alone now. Samuel was angry, and he cried to the Lord all night. The hours passed slowly. The lamp beside him burned low and went out. He did not relight it.

Samuel rose early to meet Saul in the morning. Word came to him on the road. Saul had gone up to Carmel and set up a monument for himself. Then he had turned and passed on and gone down to Gilgal. Samuel walked on toward Gilgal.

As he came near the camp, he heard it. The bleating of sheep. The lowing of cattle. The sound carried far on the morning air. Samuel slowed. He listened. The sound did not stop.

Saul came out to meet him with the greeting already on his lips. "Blessed are you by the Lord. I have performed the commandment of the Lord."

Samuel did not return the greeting. He stood in the road and looked at the king he had anointed.

"Then what does this bleating of the sheep in my ears, and the lowing of the cattle which I hear, mean?"

Saul's eyes shifted toward the herds behind him. He found the answer ready. "They have brought them from the Amalekites. The people spared the best of the sheep and of the cattle, to sacrifice to the Lord your God. The rest we have utterly destroyed."

Samuel raised his hand and stopped him.

"Stay, and I will tell you what the Lord said to me last night."

Saul said, "Say on."

Samuel spoke as the herds bleated on behind the king's shoulder. "Has the Lord as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the Lord? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to listen than the fat of rams. For rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft, and stubbornness is as iniquity and idolatry. Because you have rejected the word of the Lord, he has also rejected you from being king."

The words landed and stayed. Saul's face moved. "I have sinned. I feared the people and obeyed their voice."

Samuel turned to go.

Saul reached out. His hand caught the skirt of the prophet's robe. The fabric pulled taut between them. Then it gave. A long strip tore in the king's grip.

Samuel looked at the torn cloth in Saul's hand. He looked at the king.

"The Lord has torn the kingdom of Israel from you today, and has given it to a neighbor of yours, who is better than you."

The two men stood on the road. The herds bleated on. The morning sun was full on the hills of Gilgal. Samuel turned and walked away from the king. He did not look back. The torn strip of his robe stayed in Saul's hand, and the king stood holding it in the full sun, and the bleating of the sheep went on.
