Samuel came unto Saul with the word of the Lord upon him. The old prophet was the one who had poured the oil upon the young man's head. He had been there at the beginning. Now he stood before the king he had anointed and spake the charge plainly.

"The Lord sent me to anoint thee to be king over his people, over Israel. Now therefore hearken thou unto the voice of the words of the Lord. Thus saith the Lord of hosts, 'I remember that which Amalek did to Israel, how he laid wait for him in the way when he came up from Egypt. Now go and smite Amalek, and utterly destroy all that they have, and spare them not.'"

Saul hearkened. The charge was strict. The completeness of it was the point of it.

Saul gathered the people.

He summoned them unto Telaim and numbered them upon the plain. Two hundred thousand footmen. Ten thousand men of Judah. He came unto the city of Amalek and laid wait in the valley. Before the battle he sent word unto the Kenites which dwelt among the Amalekites, bidding them depart, for they had shewed kindness to Israel of old, when the people came up out of Egypt. The Kenites went out from among them. Then Saul smote. The host moved from Havilah as thou goest to Shur, that is before Egypt. The Amalekites fell.

He took Agag the king alive.

He utterly destroyed all the people with the edge of the sword. But the best of the sheep and of the oxen, the fatlings and the lambs, and all that was good — these the king and the people spared. They would not utterly destroy them. Every thing that was vile and refuse, that they destroyed. The herds that were kept were driven back with the host, lowing on the way, bleating as they walked.

The word of the charge had been strict. The doing thereof had been but in part.

That night came the word of the Lord unto Samuel.

"It repenteth me that I have set up Saul to be king, for he is turned back from following me, and hath not performed my commandments."

The old man heard it in the dark. He sat up where he lay. He spake not. The grief in the word was not his alone now, and it abode not in him quietly. Samuel was grieved, and he cried unto the Lord all night. The hours passed slowly. The lamp beside him burned low and went out. He kindled it not again. He prayed for the king. He prayed against the king. He knew not which prayer the Lord would answer, and he ceased not from praying either one.

Samuel rose early to meet Saul in the morning.

Word came unto him as he set out on the road. Saul had gone up to Carmel. There, in his triumph, he had set him up a place. Then he had turned, and passed on, and gone down to Gilgal. Samuel walked on toward Gilgal. The road ran between the hills. The morning was clear.

As he drew near the camp he heard it. The bleating of sheep. The lowing of oxen. The sound carried far on the morning air. Samuel slowed. He hearkened. The sound ceased not. The sound was the answer to the question he had already been told the answer to, and the sound was still the answer when he came into the camp.

Saul came out to meet him with the greeting already on his lips.

"Blessed be thou of the Lord. I have performed the commandment of the Lord."

Samuel returned not the greeting. He stood in the way. He looked upon the king he had anointed, the tall young man for whom he had once carried hope, and the king met not his eyes for long.

"What meaneth then this bleating of the sheep in mine ears, and the lowing of the oxen which I hear?"

Saul's eyes shifted toward the herds behind him. The answer was ready. He had thought upon it on the road.

"They have brought them from the Amalekites. The people spared the best of the sheep and of the oxen, to sacrifice unto the Lord thy God. The rest we have utterly destroyed."

Samuel lifted up his hand. He stayed him there.

"Stay, and I will tell thee what the Lord hath said to me this night."

Saul said unto him, "Say on."

Samuel spake as the herds bleated on behind the king's shoulder. He raised not his voice. The herds were loud enough. The prophet's words moved beneath the noise of them, and Saul heard every one.

"When thou wast little in thine own sight, wast thou not made the head of the tribes of Israel? And the Lord anointed thee king over Israel, and the Lord sent thee on a journey, and said, 'Go, and utterly destroy the sinners the Amalekites, and fight against them until they be consumed.' Wherefore then didst thou not obey the voice of the Lord, but didst fly upon the spoil, and didst evil in the sight of the Lord?"

Saul answered. The answer came out swiftly. "Yea, I have obeyed the voice of the Lord, and have gone the way which the Lord sent me, and have brought Agag the king of Amalek, and have utterly destroyed the Amalekites. But the people took of the spoil, sheep and oxen, the chief of the things which should have been utterly destroyed, to sacrifice unto the Lord thy God in Gilgal."

Samuel moved not. The herds bleated on. When he spake, his voice was the voice of the older man who had loved this king, and the voice of the prophet who could not unsay that which the Lord had said in the night.

"Hath the Lord as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the Lord? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams. For rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft, and stubbornness is as iniquity and idolatry. Because thou hast rejected the word of the Lord, he hath also rejected thee from being king."

The words landed and stayed. The camp behind Saul was still busy with the morning. The herds bleated. Somewhere a man laughed at somewhat his fellow had said, and the laugh knew not what had just been spoken in the way.

Saul's face moved. The look that came upon it was not the look of a man who had been taken aback by the verdict. It was the look of a man who had been awaiting it.

"I have sinned. For I have transgressed the commandment of the Lord, and thy words, because I feared the people, and obeyed their voice."

Samuel answered him not. He turned about to go.

Saul reached forth. His hand laid hold upon the skirt of the prophet's mantle. He held it. He meant to hold the old man by it, as a child holdeth his father's sleeve. Samuel kept walking. The fabric pulled taut between them. Then it gave. A long strip of the mantle rent in the king's grip.

Samuel turned again.

He looked upon the torn cloth in the king's hand. He looked upon the king. He spake the word which the cloth had already spoken without need of him.

"The Lord hath rent the kingdom of Israel from thee this day, and hath given it to a neighbour of thine, that is better than thou."

Saul let not go of the cloth. He could not seem to. He stood holding the torn strip of the prophet's mantle in his fist, and the herds bleated, and Samuel turned again and walked away from him.

The old man walked back the way he had come. The hills opened ahead of him. He looked not back at the king he had anointed. He carried the grief of the Lord within him, and his own grief along with it, and the two griefs walked together down the road into the morning.

Samuel saw Saul no more until the day of Saul's death, but he mourned for him many days after, and in the night he still cried unto the Lord for him, and the Lord still grieved over the king he had set up, and in the camp at Gilgal that morning the bleating of the sheep went on.
