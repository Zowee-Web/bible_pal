Samuel came unto Saul with the word of the Lord. The old prophet stood before the young king and spake the charge plainly. The Lord remembered that which Amalek had done to Israel in the way, when they came up out of Egypt. Now Saul was to go and smite Amalek, and utterly destroy all that they had. Nothing was to be spared.

Saul hearkened. Saul gathered the people.

He summoned them unto Telaim and numbered them, two hundred thousand footmen and ten thousand men of Judah. He came unto the city of Amalek and laid wait in the valley. He sent word unto the Kenites to depart from among the Amalekites, for they had shewed kindness to Israel of old. The Kenites went out. Then Saul smote the Amalekites, from Havilah as thou goest to Shur, that is before Egypt.

He took Agag the king alive. He utterly destroyed all the people with the edge of the sword. But the best of the sheep and of the oxen, the fatlings and the lambs, and all that was good — these the king and the people spared. They would not utterly destroy them. Every thing that was vile and refuse, that they destroyed.

That night came the word of the Lord unto Samuel.

"It repenteth me that I have set up Saul to be king, for he is turned back from following me, and hath not performed my commandments."

The old man heard it in the dark. He sat up. The grief in the word was not his alone now. Samuel was grieved, and he cried unto the Lord all night. The hours passed slowly. The lamp beside him burned low and went out. He kindled it not again.

Samuel rose early to meet Saul in the morning. Word came unto him on the road. Saul had gone up to Carmel and set him up a place. Then he had turned, and passed on, and gone down to Gilgal. Samuel walked on toward Gilgal.

As he drew near the camp, he heard it. The bleating of sheep. The lowing of oxen. The sound carried far on the morning air. Samuel slowed. He hearkened. The sound ceased not.

Saul came out to meet him with the greeting already on his lips. "Blessed be thou of the Lord. I have performed the commandment of the Lord."

Samuel returned not the greeting. He stood in the way and looked upon the king he had anointed.

"What meaneth then this bleating of the sheep in mine ears, and the lowing of the oxen which I hear?"

Saul's eyes shifted toward the herds behind him. He had the answer ready. "They have brought them from the Amalekites. The people spared the best of the sheep and of the oxen, to sacrifice unto the Lord thy God. The rest we have utterly destroyed."

Samuel lifted up his hand and stayed him.

"Stay, and I will tell thee what the Lord hath said to me this night."

Saul said unto him, "Say on."

Samuel spake as the herds bleated on behind the king's shoulder. "Hath the Lord as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the Lord? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams. For rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft, and stubbornness is as iniquity and idolatry. Because thou hast rejected the word of the Lord, he hath also rejected thee from being king."

The words landed and stayed. Saul's face moved. "I have sinned. I feared the people, and obeyed their voice."

Samuel turned about to go.

Saul reached forth. His hand laid hold upon the skirt of the prophet's mantle. The fabric pulled taut between them. Then it gave. A long strip rent in the king's grip.

Samuel looked upon the torn cloth in Saul's hand. He looked upon the king.

"The Lord hath rent the kingdom of Israel from thee this day, and hath given it to a neighbour of thine, that is better than thou."

The two men stood in the way. The herds bleated on. The morning sun was full upon the hills of Gilgal. Samuel turned and walked away from the king. He looked not back. The torn strip of his mantle stayed in Saul's hand, and the king stood holding it in the full sun, and the bleating of the sheep went on.
