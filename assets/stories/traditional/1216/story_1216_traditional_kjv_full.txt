The lamp of God had not yet gone out.

It stood in the temple of the LORD at Shiloh, a small flame within a vessel of clay, set upon its stand. The light thereof was thin, yet steady, and it reached just so far as to shew the seams of the curtain and the low shapes upon the floor. Beyond the curtain was the ark of God. Beyond the walls was the night.

The child Samuel was laid down in his place. He was not far from the ark. He had drawn his blanket up about his shoulders, and his breath came slow and even in the warm dim. He had ministered unto the LORD that day before Eli, and the day was done.

Eli was laid down in his place also. His eyes had begun to wax dim, that he could not see, and his lying down was the lying down of an old man whose body settleth by stages. In the rooms about the temple, in the houses of Shiloh, men and women had banked their fires and lain down. The word of the LORD was precious in those days. There was no open vision.

And the LORD called Samuel.

And the boy sat up.

"Here am I."

He pushed back the blanket and stood up in the dark and ran unto where Eli lay. His bare feet were quick upon the stones. He came unto the old man and bent close. "Here am I; for thou calledst me."

Eli stirred in his place. His voice came up from sleep, gentle. "I called not. Lie down again."

Samuel went back. He lay down in his place. He pulled the blanket up. The lamp was yet burning.

And the LORD called yet again, "Samuel."

He arose and went to Eli a second time and bent close to where the old man lay. "Here am I; for thou didst call me."

Eli answered, "I called not, my son. Lie down again."

The boy went back. He lay down. He hearkened. The temple was quiet. The lamp held its small bright point against the dark.

Now Samuel did not yet know the LORD, neither was the word of the LORD yet revealed unto him.

The LORD called Samuel again, the third time. The boy arose and went unto Eli, and said, "Here am I; for thou didst call me."

Eli perceived that the LORD had called the child.

He raised himself up a little where he lay. He set his hand upon Samuel's shoulder, where the cloth of his tunic gathered. "Go, lie down," Eli said. "And it shall be, if he call thee, that thou shalt say, Speak, LORD; for thy servant heareth."

So Samuel went and lay down in his place.

The lamp was yet burning.

And the LORD came, and stood, and called as at other times, "Samuel, Samuel."

Then Samuel said, "Speak; for thy servant heareth."

And the LORD told the child what he would do. Behold, he would do a thing in Israel, at which both the ears of every one that heard it should tingle. He would perform against Eli all that he had spoken concerning his house, from the beginning even unto the end. The iniquity of Eli's house should not be purged with sacrifice nor offering for ever.

The boy lay still in the dark. The word came down upon him. He moved not from his place.

And Samuel lay until the morning. When the sky beyond the curtain grew gray, he arose. He went unto the doors of the house of the LORD, and he opened them. The morning air came in. The lamp was low now, almost finished, but it was yet burning.

And Samuel feared to shew Eli the vision.

Eli called Samuel from his place. "Samuel, my son."

"Here am I."

"What is the thing that the LORD hath said unto thee? I pray thee hide it not from me."

And Samuel told him every whit, and hid nothing from him.

Eli received the word in his lying down. He lifted not up his hand. He cried not out. He said, "It is the LORD: let him do what seemeth him good."

And Samuel grew. The LORD was with him. He did let none of his words fall to the ground. And all Israel from Dan even to Beersheba knew that Samuel was established to be a prophet of the Lord.
