In those days the word of the Lord was rare in Israel. There were not many visions. The harvest came in, and the rains came down, and the lamps were lit at evening in the houses of Shiloh, and the people did what they did, but the voice that had spoken to the fathers spoke seldom now. Men listened and did not hear it. The priests at the temple offered the daily offerings, and the smoke rose, and the days passed.

In the temple of the Lord at Shiloh a lamp burned. It stood inside a clay vessel set upon a stand of bronze. Its flame was no bigger than a man's thumb. By night it kept a small bright point in the dim, and the light reached just far enough to show the seams of the curtain and the shapes of the low things on the floor. Beyond the curtain was the ark of God. Beyond the temple walls was the night.

The child Samuel was laid down in his place. He was a boy, and his place was near. He had drawn his blanket up around his shoulders, and his breath had grown slow and even. He had ministered to the Lord that day before Eli, as he had every day since his mother had brought him up to Shiloh in the linen tunic she had made for him. The day was done. He was asleep, or near sleep, in the small warm room he kept inside the house of the Lord.

Eli was laid down in his own place also. His eyes had begun to grow dim, so that he could not see, and his lying down was the lying down of an old man whose body settles by stages. He had said his prayers. He had put aside his outer robe. He breathed slowly in the dark, and the dark held him as it held the boy.

The Lord called Samuel.

The boy sat up.

"Here I am."

He pushed back the blanket and stood up in the dark and ran. His bare feet were quick on the stones. He went the way he knew to where Eli lay, and he bent close to the old man, and he said, "Here I am, for you called me."

Eli stirred in his place. His voice came up from sleep, gentle and a little surprised. "I did not call. Lie down again."

Samuel went back. He lay down in his place. He pulled the blanket up. The lamp was still burning. He listened a little while in the dark. Then his breath settled again.

The Lord called yet again. "Samuel."

He arose. He went the same way a second time. He stood at the old man's side and bent close. "Here I am, for you called me."

Eli answered, "I did not call, my son. Lie down again."

The boy went back. He lay down. The temple was quiet. The lamp held its small bright point against the dark. Outside, somewhere far off, a dog barked once, then was still. The boy lay listening. Nothing came.

Now Samuel did not yet know the Lord, neither was the word of the Lord yet revealed to him. He was a child in the house of the Lord, and he did not yet know the voice of the Lord.

The Lord called Samuel again, the third time.

The boy arose. He went to Eli a third time, and he said again, "Here I am, for you called me."

Eli perceived that the Lord had called the child.

He raised himself up a little where he lay. His hand found the boy's shoulder where the cloth of his tunic gathered. The old man's voice was low. "Go, lie down," Eli said. "And it shall be, if he calls you, that you shall say, Speak, Lord, for your servant hears."

So Samuel went and lay down in his place.

The lamp was still burning.

He lay on his back this time. He kept his eyes open. He looked up into the dim where the curtain hung. He did not speak. He waited.

The Lord came, and stood, and called as at other times. "Samuel. Samuel."

Then Samuel said, "Speak, for your servant hears."

The Lord spoke to the child. He told him what he would do. He said, "Behold, I will do a thing in Israel at which both the ears of every one who hears it will tingle. In that day I will perform against Eli all that I have spoken concerning his house, from the beginning even to the end. For I have told him that I will judge his house forever, for the iniquity which he knew, because his sons brought a curse on themselves, and he did not restrain them. Therefore I have sworn to the house of Eli that the iniquity of Eli's house shall not be removed with sacrifice or offering forever."

The boy lay still in the dark. The word came down upon him as he lay. He did not move from his place. He did not turn his head. The lamp burned beside him, smaller now, its flame low, and the dim held him.

When the word was finished, the temple was quiet again.

Samuel lay until the morning.

He did not sleep. He lay in his place, and the night went over him slowly. He turned the word in his mind. He kept it where the Lord had laid it. After a long time the gray came to the high windows. The shapes of the curtain showed. The shapes of the doors showed.

He rose. He set his blanket aside. He went to the doors of the Lord's house, and he set his hands against the wood, and he opened them. The cool morning air came in. Birds were calling somewhere among the houses of Shiloh. The lamp was very low now, almost finished, but it was still burning.

Samuel was afraid to show Eli the vision.

He went about the small tasks of the morning. He did not go in to where the old man lay. He did the things he did every day. His hands moved as they had always moved. The word he had been given lay inside him like a stone he did not yet know how to carry.

Then Eli called Samuel from his place. "Samuel, my son."

"Here I am."

"What is the thing that he has spoken to you? Please do not hide it from me."

Samuel came near. He stood by the old man. He told him every bit, and hid nothing from him.

Eli listened. He did not lift up his hand. He did not cry out. He did not turn his face to the wall. He received the word in his lying down, as a man receives a thing he has long known was coming. When the boy was finished he said, "It is the Lord. Let him do what seems good to him."

That was the beginning.

Samuel grew. He grew in the house of the Lord and afterward he grew in Israel. The Lord was with him. He let none of his words fall to the ground. The word he gave to Samuel went out and stood and held, and what Samuel spoke came to pass. All Israel from Dan even to Beersheba came to know that Samuel was established to be a prophet of the Lord. And the Lord appeared again in Shiloh; for the Lord revealed himself to Samuel in Shiloh by the word of the Lord.
