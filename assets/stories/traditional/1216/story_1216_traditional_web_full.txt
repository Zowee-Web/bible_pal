The lamp had not yet gone out.

It stood in the temple of the Lord at Shiloh, a small flame inside a clay vessel, set on its stand. Its light was thin but it was steady, and it reached just far enough to show the seams of the curtain and the low shapes of the floor. Beyond the curtain was the ark of God. Beyond the walls was the night.

The child Samuel was laid down in his place. He was not far from the ark. He had drawn his blanket up around his shoulders, and his breath came slow and even in the warm dim. He had ministered to the Lord that day before Eli, and the day was done.

Eli was laid down in his place also. His eyes had begun to grow dim, so that he could not see, and his lying down was the lying down of an old man whose body settled by stages. In the rooms around the temple, in the houses of Shiloh, men and women had banked their fires and lain down. The word of the Lord was rare in those days. There were not many visions.

The Lord called Samuel.

The boy sat up.

"Here I am."

He pushed back the blanket and stood up in the dark and ran to where Eli lay. His bare feet were quick on the stones. He reached the old man and bent close. "Here I am, for you called me."

Eli stirred in his place. His voice came up from sleep, gentle. "I did not call. Lie down again."

Samuel went back. He lay down in his place. He pulled the blanket up. The lamp was still burning.

The Lord called yet again. "Samuel."

He arose and went to Eli a second time and bent close to where the old man lay. "Here I am, for you called me."

Eli answered, "I did not call, my son. Lie down again."

The boy went back. He lay down. He listened. The temple was quiet. The lamp held its small bright point against the dark.

Now Samuel did not yet know the Lord, neither was the word of the Lord yet revealed to him.

The Lord called Samuel again, the third time. The boy arose and went to Eli and said, "Here I am, for you called me."

Eli perceived that the Lord had called the child.

He raised himself up a little where he lay. He set his hand on Samuel's shoulder, where the cloth of his tunic gathered. "Go, lie down," Eli said. "And it shall be, if he calls you, that you shall say, Speak, Lord, for your servant hears."

So Samuel went and lay down in his place.

The lamp was still burning.

The Lord came, and stood, and called as at other times. "Samuel. Samuel."

Then Samuel said, "Speak, for your servant hears."

The Lord told the child what he would do. He would do a thing in Israel at which both the ears of every one who heard it would tingle. He would perform against Eli all that he had spoken concerning his house, from the beginning even to the end. The iniquity of Eli's house would not be removed with sacrifice or offering forever.

The boy lay still in the dark. The word came down upon him. He did not move from his place.

Samuel lay until the morning. When the sky beyond the curtain grew gray, he rose. He went to the doors of the Lord's house, and he opened them. The morning air came in. The lamp was low now, almost finished, but it was still burning.

Samuel was afraid to show Eli the vision.

Eli called Samuel from his place. "Samuel, my son."

"Here I am."

"What is the thing that he has spoken to you? Please do not hide it from me."

Samuel told him every bit, and hid nothing from him.

Eli received the word in his lying down. He did not lift up his hand. He did not cry out. He said, "It is the Lord. Let him do what seems good to him."

Samuel grew. The Lord was with him. He let none of his words fall to the ground. All Israel from Dan even to Beersheba came to know that Samuel was established to be a prophet of the Lord.
