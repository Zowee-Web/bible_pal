When the seventh month came, all the people gathered themselves together as one man in the wide place before the water gate of Jerusalem. They spoke to Ezra the scribe, asking him to bring the book of the law of Moses, which the Lord had commanded to Israel.

Ezra the priest brought the law before the assembly. Both men and women had come, and all who were old enough to understand. From early morning until midday he read in their presence, and the ears of all the people were attentive to the book of the law.

Ezra stood on a wooden platform that had been made for the purpose. Priests and elders stood beside him on his right hand and on his left. When he opened the book in the sight of all the people, all the people stood up.

Ezra blessed the Lord, the great God. The people answered, "Amen, Amen," and they lifted up their hands. Then they bowed their heads and worshiped the Lord with their faces to the ground.

The Levites moved among the people and helped them to understand the law. They read in the book of the law distinctly, and they gave the sense, so that the people understood the reading.

When the people heard the words of the law, they wept. So Nehemiah the governor stood with Ezra the priest and the Levites who had taught them. Together they said to the people, "Today is holy to the Lord your God. Do not mourn, and do not weep."

Nehemiah said to them, "Go your way. Eat the fat, and drink the sweet." He continued, "Send portions to those for whom nothing is prepared, for this day is holy to our Lord. Do not be grieved, for the joy of the Lord is your strength."

The Levites moved through the assembly and stilled the people. They said, "Hold your peace, for the day is holy. Do not be grieved."

So all the people went their way to eat and to drink, and to send portions to others. They made great rejoicing, because they had understood the words that were declared to them.
