The writer of the epistle unto the Hebrews spake thus unto the people of the church.

He spake of two mountains.

He spake of the first mountain — the one unto which their fathers had come in the wilderness. Mount Sinai.

Ye are not come unto that mount, the writer said. Ye are not come unto the mount that might be touched, and that burned with fire, nor unto blackness, and darkness, and tempest. Ye are not come unto the sound of a trumpet, and the voice of words; which voice they that heard intreated that the word should not be spoken to them any more.

For they could not endure that which was commanded unto them then — "And if so much as a beast touch the mountain, it shall be stoned, or thrust through with a dart."

So terrible was the sight of the mount, the writer said, that even Moses himself had said, "I exceedingly fear and quake."

That was the mount unto which their fathers had come.

But ye are not come unto that mount.

Ye are come unto another.

Ye are come unto mount Sion, and unto the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem. Ye are come to an innumerable company of angels, to the general assembly and church of the firstborn, which are written in heaven. Ye are come to God the Judge of all, and to the spirits of just men made perfect. Ye are come to Jesus the mediator of the new covenant, and to the blood of sprinkling, that speaketh better things than that of Abel.

The writer set the two mountains before them, and suffered them to be beheld at the same time.

This was the gathering whereunto they had come. This was the city.

The fire of Sinai was no longer the gate where the people stood. The gathering of Sion was where they had come.
