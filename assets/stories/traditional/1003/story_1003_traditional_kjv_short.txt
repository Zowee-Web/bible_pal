Then Joseph could no longer contain himself before all them that stood by him, and he cried aloud, commanding every servant and attendant to depart from his presence. The Egyptian officials withdrew through the columned doorway, their sandals scraping against the stone floor, until the chamber stood empty save for Joseph and his brothers.

And when the last footstep faded beyond the threshold, Joseph wept. His voice broke forth with such force that the sound carried through the walls and corridors of that great house, and the Egyptians in the outer rooms heard it plainly.

He spoke to his brothers in their own tongue. The words came sharp and clear across the tiled floor where they stood clustered together.

I am Joseph, he said. Doth my father yet live?

His brothers gave no answer. They stood before him, and not one among them opened his mouth. Their bodies drew back where they stood.

Joseph spoke again. He bade them come near. They moved closer, their feet slow upon the stone. He told them plainly: he was Joseph, their brother, whom they had sold into Egypt. Then he lifted his hand and spoke further, telling them not to be grieved nor wrathful with themselves for what they had done, for God had sent him before them to preserve life.

He declared that the famine had endured now two years upon the land, and five years yet remained wherein there would be neither plowing nor harvest. God had sent him ahead, he said, to preserve for them a remnant in the earth and to keep them alive by a great deliverance. It was not they who sent him hither, he told them, but God, who had made him a father to Pharaoh, and lord of all his house, and a ruler throughout all the land of Egypt.

He charged them then with haste. They were to go up to his father and speak unto him, declaring that his son Joseph had been made lord over all Egypt, and that he must come down without delay. Joseph told them of the land of Goshen, where they would dwell near unto him — they and their children and their children's children, and their flocks and their herds and all that they possessed. There he would nourish them through the remaining five years of famine, lest they and their households come to poverty.

And behold, he said, your eyes see, and the eyes of my brother Benjamin, that it is my mouth which speaketh unto you.

He commanded them to tell his father of all his glory in Egypt and of all they had witnessed. They were to make haste and bring his father down.

Then Joseph fell upon his brother Benjamin's neck and wept, and Benjamin wept upon his neck. He kissed all his brothers and wept upon them. After that, his brothers spoke with him, their voices filling the chamber where moments before only silence and weeping had dwelt.
