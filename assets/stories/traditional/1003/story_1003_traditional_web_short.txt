Joseph could no longer hold himself back before all the servants and officials who stood around him. His voice broke out sharp and sudden: "Have everyone leave me!" The Egyptian attendants stepped back, sandals scraping against the polished stone floor, and filed through the doorway. The heavy doors closed. No one else remained in the hall except Joseph and his brothers.

Then Joseph wept. The sound of it carried through the walls — loud, ragged weeping that echoed down the corridors of the house. The household of Pharaoh heard it.

His brothers stood before him in the emptied room. The columns cast long shadows across the tile. Joseph spoke to them in their own tongue: "I am Joseph. Is my father still alive?"

Not one of them answered. They stood where they were, bodies stiff, faces turned partly away.

Joseph said, "Come near to me, please." They stepped closer, slowly, their feet barely lifting from the floor. He said to them, "I am Joseph, your brother, whom you sold into Egypt. Now don't be grieved, and don't be angry with yourselves, that you sold me here, for God sent me before you to preserve life. For these two years the famine has been in the land, and there are yet five years in which there will be neither plowing nor harvest. God sent me before you to preserve for you a remnant in the earth, and to save you alive by a great deliverance. So now it wasn't you who sent me here, but God, and he has made me a father to Pharaoh, lord of all his house, and ruler over all the land of Egypt."

His voice steadied as he spoke. He stood straight, dressed in Egyptian linen, gold at his neck, but the words came in the language of Canaan.

"Hurry and go up to my father, and tell him, 'This is what your son Joseph says: God has made me lord of all Egypt. Come down to me. Don't delay. You will dwell in the land of Goshen, and you will be near to me — you, your children, your children's children, your flocks, your herds, and all that you have. There I will provide for you, for there are yet five years of famine, lest you come to poverty — you, your household, and all that you have.'"

He opened his hands toward them. "Behold, your eyes see, and the eyes of my brother Benjamin, that it is my mouth that speaks to you. You shall tell my father of all my glory in Egypt, and of all that you have seen. You shall hurry and bring my father down here."

Then Joseph fell on his brother Benjamin's neck and wept. Benjamin wept on his neck. He kissed all his brothers and wept on them. After that, his brothers talked with him.
