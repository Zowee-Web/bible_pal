Behind the veil stood the Most Holy Place, and above the ark was the mercy seat, where Yahweh appeared in the cloud. After the two sons of Aaron died when they drew near before Yahweh, Yahweh gave Moses a warning for his brother: Aaron was not to come inside the veil whenever he chose, or he would die.

This was how Aaron was to come. He bathed his body in water and put on the holy linen garments. He brought a young bull for a sin offering and a ram for a burnt offering, and from the people two male goats and a ram.

He cast lots over the two goats — one lot for Yahweh, and one lot for the goat to be sent away. The goat chosen for Yahweh he offered as a sin offering. The other he kept alive, to be sent away into the wilderness.

First Aaron made atonement for himself and his household. He killed the bull, and carried a censer of burning coals and two handfuls of fragrant incense behind the veil, so that the cloud of incense covered the mercy seat and he would not die. He sprinkled the bull's blood with his finger on the mercy seat, and before it seven times.

Then he killed the goat that was for the people and did the same with its blood. In this way he made atonement for the Holy Place, because of the uncleanness and the sins of the children of Israel, and he did the same for the Tent of Meeting. No one else could be in the tent while he made atonement inside. He went out to the altar, put blood on its horns, and cleansed it.

When the atonement was finished, Aaron brought the living goat. He laid both his hands on its head and confessed over it all the iniquities and transgressions and sins of the children of Israel, putting them on the head of the goat. Then it was sent away into the wilderness by the hand of a man. The goat carried on itself all their iniquities to a solitary land, and there it was released.
