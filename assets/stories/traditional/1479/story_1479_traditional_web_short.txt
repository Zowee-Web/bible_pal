When Jesus drew near to Jerusalem, he saw the city.

He had come down the road from the Mount of Olives that morning, with his disciples and a multitude going with him. The way had been bright with palms and with shouting. Now the city was before him in the valley below, where the temple stood, and the walls, and the streets.

He stopped on the road.

He looked at it for a long time.

Then he wept over it.

His tears were on his face when he spoke.

He said, "If you, even you, had known today the things which belong to your peace. But now, they are hidden from your eyes.

For the days will come on you, when your enemies will throw up a barricade against you, and surround you, and hem you in on every side. They will dash you and your children within you to the ground. They will not leave in you one stone on another — because you didn't know the time of your visitation."

He said no more after that.

He went on down the road, into the city, toward the temple.

The people walking with him did not yet know what he had wept for.
