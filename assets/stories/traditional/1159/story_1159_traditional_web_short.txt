After the angel of the Lord had appeared to Gideon and called him to deliver Israel, Gideon prepared to go out against the Midianites. But before he went, he asked the Lord for a sign.

He said to God, "If you will save Israel by my hand, as you have spoken, behold, I will put a fleece of wool on the threshing floor. If there is dew on the fleece only, and it is dry on all the ground, then I shall know that you will save Israel by my hand, as you have spoken."

It was so. He rose up early in the morning, and pressed the fleece together, and wrung the dew out of the fleece. He filled a bowl of water with it. The ground around the fleece was dry.

Yet Gideon was not yet sure, and he wished to know with certainty.

He said to God, "Don't let your anger be kindled against me, and I will speak only this once. Please let me make a trial just this once with the fleece. Let it now be dry only on the fleece, and on all the ground let there be dew."

God did so that night. It was dry on the fleece only, and there was dew on all the ground. The morning came, and Gideon went out and looked, and the fleece was dry as Gideon had asked, and the dew lay heavy on the ground around it.

Then Gideon rose up early in the morning. He gathered all the men of Israel, and they camped beside the spring of Harod, and the camp of Midian was on the north side of them, by the hill of Moreh in the valley.

Gideon went out to deliver Israel, and the day of battle was near.

The wind moved across the threshing floor where the fleece had lain, and the morning sun was warm on the dry stones.
