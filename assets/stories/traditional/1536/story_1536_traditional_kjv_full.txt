He that hath made his dwelling in the secret place of the Most High abideth under the shadow of the Almighty. The place is hidden. The way therein is not marked upon any road, nor hewn into any hillside. But the man who findeth it standeth beneath a covering that doth not shift with the wind, nor grow thin with the turning of the seasons. The shadow falleth broad and dark and cool, and he is within it.

He saith of the LORD, "He is my refuge and my stronghold. My God. In him do I place my trust."

The words come forth from his mouth in the open air. Not whispered. Not halting nor uncertain. He speaketh them as a man declareth his own name — plainly, for it is simply true.

The Almighty delivereth him from the snare of the fowler. The trap is laid in the grass, the cord drawn tight between the stakes, concealed beneath leaves and dust. But it doth not close upon him. He passeth over it and knoweth not how near it lay. The deadly pestilence drifteth through the land, silent and thick, settling upon doorposts and into the lungs of men. It toucheth him not.

God covereth him with his feathers. Beneath his wings the man findeth shelter. The image is of a great bird drawing its young close against the breast, the broad span of its feathers falling about them like walls. Warm. Dark. Safe. And the faithfulness of God standeth round about the man as a shield upon the arm and a breastplate across the chest. The blows fall against it and pass no further.

There is no terror of the night to fear. The hours between sundown and the coming of dawn hold no dread for him. No arrow that flieth by day, cutting the bright air with its whistle and its shaft. No pestilence that walketh through the darkness, unseen, touching all that it meeteth. No destruction that layeth waste whole fields at noonday, when the sun is at its height and the light hideth nothing.

The man standeth still. The shadow of the Almighty doth not move from over him.

A thousand fall at his left hand. Ten thousand fall at his right. The ground about him is covered with the fallen, thick as grain laid flat by hail. The dust riseth. The sound thereof is terrible. But the destruction cometh not nigh unto him. His garments are clean. His flesh is whole. He looketh out with his own eyes and beholdeth the recompense of the wicked spread across the open field. He seeth it, but he hath no portion therein.

Because he hath made the LORD his refuge — the Most High his dwelling place — no evil reacheth him. No plague cometh nigh his door. The threshold of his house is clean. The walls stand plumb and true. The air within is clear and still, and nothing creepeth beneath the frame nor seepeth through the cracks. The dwelling holdeth, for he that built his life therein built it upon the Most High God.

God giveth his angels charge concerning him. They are sent forth with commandment — particular, appointed unto him, and full. They keep him in all his ways. Not in some of his ways. In all of them. The path where he walketh, the road where he journeyeth, the ground whereon he setteth his foot in the darkness. Their hands bear him up, that his foot dash not against a stone. The path is rough ground, loose rock, sharp edges that would cut and trip and break. But his step holdeth firm. The hands beneath him are steady and do not weary.

He treadeth upon the lion. The beast croucheth in the grass, its sinew drawn tight, its jaws set wide. The man walketh over it. He treadeth upon the serpent, the coiled body pressed flat beneath his heel. The young lion, fierce and swift, and the great beast — the dragon — he trampleth them beneath his feet. They rise not. They lunge not. They lie still beneath him as he passeth.

Then speaketh the voice of God. Not through a prophet. Not through a messenger. The voice itself, direct, naming the man and naming the cause.

"Because he hath set his love upon me, therefore shall I deliver him. I shall set him on high, for he hath known my name."

The voice continueth. It doth not hasten.

"He shall call upon me, and I will answer him. I will be with him in his trouble. I will deliver him, and bring him unto honour."

A pause. The stones beneath the man's feet hold their warmth.

"With length of days will I satisfy him, and show unto him my salvation."

The man standeth beneath the broad shadow, his face lifted, his hands open at his sides. The secret place is quiet. The ground beneath his feet is solid and clean.
