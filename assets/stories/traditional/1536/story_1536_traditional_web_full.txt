The one who dwells in the secret place of the Most High rests under the shadow of the Almighty. The place is hidden. The way in is not marked on any road or cut into any hillside. But the man who finds it stands beneath a covering that does not shift with the wind or thin with the seasons. The shadow falls broad and dark and cool, and he is inside it.

He says of the LORD, "He is my refuge and my fortress. My God. In him I will trust."

The words come from his mouth in open air. Not whispered. Not uncertain. He speaks them the way a man states his own name — plainly, because it is simply true.

The Almighty delivers him from the snare of the fowler. The trap is set in the grass, the cord pulled tight between the stakes, hidden beneath leaves and dust. But it does not close on him. He walks past it and does not know how near it was. The deadly pestilence drifts through the land, silent and thick, settling on doorframes and into lungs. It does not touch him.

God covers him with his feathers. Under his wings the man finds shelter. The image is of a bird drawing its young close against the breast, the broad span of feathers falling around them like walls. Warm. Dark. Safe. And God's truth stands around the man like a shield on the arm and a heavy plate across the chest. The blows land against it and go no further.

There is no fear of terror by night. The hours between sundown and dawn hold no dread. No fear of the arrow that flies by day, cutting the bright air with its whistle and shaft. No fear of the pestilence that walks through the darkness, unseen, touching everything. No fear of the destruction that wastes whole fields at noonday, when the sun is highest and the light hides nothing.

The man stands still. The shadow of the Almighty does not move from over him.

A thousand fall at his left side. Ten thousand fall at his right hand. The ground around him is covered with the fallen, thick as grain laid flat by hail. The dust rises. The sound is terrible. But the destruction does not come near him. His clothing is clean. His skin is whole. He looks out with his own eyes and sees the reward of the wicked spread across the open field. He sees it but he does not share in it.

Because he has made the LORD his refuge — the Most High his dwelling place — no evil reaches him. No plague comes near his door. The threshold of his home is clean. The walls stand plumb. The air inside is clear and still, and nothing creeps beneath the frame or seeps through the cracks. The dwelling holds because the one who built his life there built it on the Most High God.

God gives his angels charge over him. They are sent with orders — specific, personal, complete. They keep him in all his ways. Not some of his ways. All of them. The path where he walks, the road where he travels, the ground where he sets his foot in the dark. Their hands bear him up so that his foot does not strike against a stone. The path is rough ground, loose rock, sharp edges that would cut and trip and break. But his step holds firm. The hands beneath him are steady and do not tire.

He treads upon the lion. The beast crouches in the grass, muscle drawn tight, jaws wide. The man walks over it. He treads upon the serpent, the coiled body flattened under his heel. The young lion, fierce and quick, and the great beast — the dragon — he tramples them under his feet. They do not rise. They do not lunge. They lie still beneath him as he passes.

Then the voice of God speaks. Not through a prophet. Not through a messenger. The voice itself, direct, naming the man and naming the reason.

"Because he has set his love upon me, I will deliver him. I will set him on high, because he has known my name."

The voice continues. It does not rush.

"He will call upon me, and I will answer him. I will be with him in trouble. I will deliver him and honor him."

A pause. The stones beneath the man's feet hold their warmth.

"With long life I will satisfy him, and show him my salvation."

The man stands beneath the broad shadow, his face lifted, his hands open at his sides. The secret place is quiet. The ground beneath his feet is solid and clean.
