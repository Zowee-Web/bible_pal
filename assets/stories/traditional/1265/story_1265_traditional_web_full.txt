In the days when the judges judged, the rain failed in Bethlehem Judah. The barley did not come up. The wells dropped low. A certain man named Elimelech took his wife Naomi and his two sons, Mahlon and Chilion, and went to live in the country of Moab. They were Ephrathites of Bethlehem Judah. They crossed the Jordan and the highlands beyond it, and they came into Moab and lived there.

Elimelech died in that foreign country. Naomi was left with her two sons. The boys grew, and they took for themselves wives of the women of Moab. The name of the one was Orpah. The name of the other was Ruth. They lived there about ten years. Then Mahlon and Chilion both died. The woman was bereaved of her two children and of her husband.

She heard at last that the Lord had visited his people in giving them bread. Naomi arose. Her daughters-in-law arose with her. The three women went out of the place where they had been, and they went on the way to return to the land of Judah.

The road climbed. The Moabite hills fell behind them. Naomi stopped at a turning of the road. She turned to face her daughters-in-law. Her hands were empty at her sides.

"Go, return each of you to her mother's house. May the Lord deal kindly with you, as you have dealt with the dead and with me. May the Lord grant you that you may find rest, each of you in the house of her husband."

Then she kissed them. They lifted up their voices and wept.

"No, but we will return with you to your people."

Naomi shook her head. "Go back, my daughters. Why would you go with me? Do I still have sons in my womb, that they may be your husbands? I am too old to have a husband. Even if I had hope tonight and bore sons, would you wait until they were grown? It grieves me seriously for your sakes, for the Lord's hand has gone out against me."

They lifted up their voices and wept again. Orpah leaned in and kissed her mother-in-law on the cheek. Then she turned and went back the way they had come. Her form grew smaller along the road.

But Ruth stayed. Her hand was at Naomi's sleeve. She did not let go.

Naomi looked at her. "Behold, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people and to her god. Follow your sister-in-law."

Ruth did not move. Her grip on the older woman's arm tightened. The wind from the hills pulled at the edge of her cloak.

"Do not urge me to leave you, and to return from following you. For where you go, I will go. Where you stay, I will stay. Your people shall be my people, and your God my God. Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May the Lord do so to me, and more also, if anything but death parts you and me."

The words settled in the air between them. Naomi saw that Ruth was determined to go with her. She stopped urging her. She turned again toward Judah. Ruth turned with her. The two women walked on together.

They came at last to Bethlehem. The road into the city wound between low walls of pale stone. Word ran ahead of them through the gate. Women came out from their doorways. Men paused in the lanes. All the city was stirred. They looked at the bowed figure walking with a younger foreign woman beside her, and they began to ask one another, "Is this Naomi?"

She lifted her head. Her face was drawn from the road and from the years.

"Do not call me Naomi. Call me Mara, for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me. I went out full, and the Lord has brought me home again empty. Why do you call me Naomi, since the Lord has testified against me, and the Almighty has afflicted me?"

The neighbors fell quiet around her. The two women crossed into the city in the slanting afternoon. So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess, her daughter-in-law, with her, who returned out of the country of Moab. They came to Bethlehem in the beginning of barley harvest, and the fields outside the walls were ripening.
