It came to pass in the days when the judges ruled, that the rain failed in Bethlehemjudah. The barley came not up. The wells dropped low. A certain man named Elimelech took his wife Naomi and his two sons, Mahlon and Chilion, and went to sojourn in the country of Moab. They were Ephrathites of Bethlehemjudah. They crossed the Jordan and the highlands beyond it, and they came into Moab and continued there.

Elimelech died in that foreign country. Naomi was left with her two sons. The boys grew, and they took them wives of the women of Moab. The name of the one was Orpah. The name of the other was Ruth. They dwelled there about ten years. Then Mahlon and Chilion died also both of them. The woman was left of her two sons and her husband.

She heard at last that the LORD had visited his people in giving them bread. Naomi arose. Her daughters in law arose with her. The three women went forth out of the place where they had been, and they went on the way to return unto the land of Judah.

The road climbed. The Moabite hills fell behind them. Naomi stopped at a turning of the road. She turned to face her daughters in law. Her hands were empty at her sides.

"Go, return each to her mother's house. The LORD deal kindly with you, as ye have dealt with the dead, and with me. The LORD grant you that ye may find rest, each of you in the house of her husband."

Then she kissed them. They lifted up their voice, and wept.

"Surely we will return with thee unto thy people."

Naomi shook her head. "Turn again, my daughters: why will ye go with me? Are there yet any more sons in my womb, that they may be your husbands? I am too old to have an husband. If I should say this night, I have hope, and should also bear sons, would ye tarry for them till they were grown? It grieveth me much for your sakes, for the hand of the LORD is gone out against me."

They lifted up their voice, and wept again. Orpah leaned in and kissed her mother in law upon the cheek. Then she turned and went back the way they had come. Her form grew smaller along the road.

But Ruth clave unto her. Her hand was at Naomi's sleeve. She would not let go.

Naomi looked upon her. "Behold, thy sister in law is gone back unto her people, and unto her gods: return thou after thy sister in law."

Ruth moved not. Her grip on the older woman's arm tightened. The wind from the hills pulled at the edge of her cloak.

"Intreat me not to leave thee, or to return from following after thee: for whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God: where thou diest, will I die, and there will I be buried: the LORD do so to me, and more also, if ought but death part thee and me."

The words settled in the air between them. When Naomi saw that she was stedfastly minded to go with her, then she left speaking unto her. She turned again toward Judah. Ruth turned with her. The two women walked on together.

They came at last to Bethlehem. The road into the city wound between low walls of pale stone. Word ran ahead of them through the gate. Women came out from their doorways. Men paused in the lanes. All the city was moved about them. They looked upon the bowed figure walking with a younger foreign woman beside her, and they began to say one to another, "Is this Naomi?"

She lifted her head. Her face was drawn from the road and from the years.

"Call me not Naomi, call me Mara: for the Almighty hath dealt very bitterly with me. I went out full, and the LORD hath brought me home again empty. Why then call ye me Naomi, seeing the LORD hath testified against me, and the Almighty hath afflicted me?"

The neighbours fell quiet around her. The two women crossed into the city in the slanting afternoon. So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess, her daughter in law, with her, which returned out of the country of Moab. They came to Bethlehem in the beginning of barley harvest, and the fields outside the walls were ripening.
