It came to pass in the days when the judges ruled, that there was a famine in the land. The barley stood not in the fields outside Bethlehem. The wells of the city dropped low. Men measured what was left at the bottom of their grain jars and measured again. Children grew thinner. Wives mended the same cloak for the third winter.

A certain man named Elimelech, an Ephrathite of Bethlehemjudah, gathered his household and turned his face east. He took his wife Naomi. He took his two sons, Mahlon and Chilion. They went forth from the city gates with what could be borne. They crossed the Jordan in the dry place where the cattle waded. They climbed the road up into the country of Moab. There the rains had not failed, and there they came to sojourn.

They continued there. They built a fire under another sky. The bread on their table was Moabite bread. The neighbours spake a tongue not their own.

Elimelech died in that foreign land. Naomi was left with her two sons. Mahlon and Chilion grew into men, and they took them wives of the women of Moab. The name of the one was Orpah. The name of the other was Ruth. They dwelled there about ten years. Then Mahlon died. Then Chilion died. The woman was left of her two children and of her husband. Three graves stood upon a Moabite hillside, and Naomi sat in an house that had once held an household.

Word came at last from across the river. A trader at the marketplace, a man passing through, spake of Bethlehem. The LORD had visited his people. He had given them bread again. The fields were rising. The harvest was nigh.

Naomi arose. Her two daughters in law arose with her. The three women went forth out of the place where they had been, and they went on the way to return unto the land of Judah.

The road climbed out of the valley. The Moabite hills fell behind them. The wind off the highlands pulled at their cloaks. Naomi walked ahead. Her hands were empty at her sides. Then, at a turning of the road, she stopped. She turned to face the two younger women.

"Go, return each to her mother's house. The LORD deal kindly with you, as ye have dealt with the dead, and with me. The LORD grant you that ye may find rest, each of you in the house of her husband."

She kissed them upon the forehead, first one and then the other. They lifted up their voice, and wept. The sound carried back along the empty road.

"Surely we will return with thee unto thy people."

Naomi shook her head. The hood fell back from her hair. "Turn again, my daughters: why will ye go with me? Are there yet any more sons in my womb, that they may be your husbands? Turn again, my daughters, go your way. I am too old to have an husband. If I should say this night, I have hope, and should also bear sons, would ye tarry for them till they were grown? Would ye stay for them from having husbands? Nay, my daughters. It grieveth me much for your sakes, for the hand of the LORD is gone out against me."

The wind moved through the brush at the road's edge. The women lifted up their voice, and wept again.

Then Orpah leaned close. She kissed her mother in law upon the cheek. She held the older woman a long moment. Then she stepped back. She turned. She began to walk the way they had come. Her form grew smaller against the brown hills. She looked not behind her.

Naomi watched her go. Then she turned unto the other.

Ruth had not moved. Her hand was upon Naomi's sleeve. The cloth of the older woman's garment was bunched between her fingers. She would not let go.

"Behold, thy sister in law is gone back unto her people, and unto her gods: return thou after thy sister in law."

Ruth's grip tightened. Her body moved not forward toward Bethlehem, neither moved back toward Moab. She stood between, with one hand fastened to the woman she would not leave. Her voice when it came was low and very steady.

"Intreat me not to leave thee, or to return from following after thee: for whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God: where thou diest, will I die, and there will I be buried: the LORD do so to me, and more also, if ought but death part thee and me."

The words settled in the air between them. Naomi looked upon her a long time. She looked upon the foreign daughter who had buried a son of hers in Moabite ground and would not turn back unto the house of her mother. When she saw that Ruth was stedfastly minded to go with her, then she left speaking unto her. She turned her face again toward Judah.

Ruth turned with her. The two women walked on together.

The road dropped down out of the hills and crossed the Jordan in the low place. The fields on the far side were green. The sun was westering by the time the road climbed up again toward Bethlehem. The walls of the city showed pale against the slope.

Word ran ahead of them through the gate. Word travelleth faster than two weary women on a road. The neighbours of the old days came out from their doorways. Men paused with their water jars at the well. Women set down what they were bearing. They looked upon the bowed figure walking up the path with a younger woman beside her, a Moabitess by her head covering and the cut of her cloak, and they began to speak one to another, and the speaking grew, and at last all the city was moved.

"Is this Naomi?"

The name reached her. She lifted her head. The face the neighbours saw was not the face that had gone out from Bethlehem in the famine years. The years had wrought upon her. The losses had wrought upon her. Her eyes had a weight in them that had not been there before.

She softened not the answer. She put not her grief away for the comfort of the gathering. She stood on the road that had carried her out and brought her back, and she spake plainly into the watching faces.

"Call me not Naomi, call me Mara: for the Almighty hath dealt very bitterly with me. I went out full, and the LORD hath brought me home again empty. Why then call ye me Naomi, seeing the LORD hath testified against me, and the Almighty hath afflicted me?"

The neighbours fell quiet around her. None corrected her. None answered. The two women crossed into the city in the slanting afternoon light. Ruth walked an half step behind, the road dust still upon her hem.

So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess, her daughter in law, with her, which returned out of the country of Moab. They came to Bethlehem in the beginning of barley harvest. Beyond the walls, the fields lay golden in the long evening, and the reapers were sharpening their sickles for the morning.
