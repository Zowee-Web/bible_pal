God told Jeremiah to go visit a potter. So Jeremiah walked down the hill to where the potters worked, near the stream at the bottom of the valley. He could hear the hum of the wheel before he even reached the door.

He found the potter's shop. The door was open. Inside it was cool and quiet. Clay dust floated in the air. Shelves along the walls held bowls and jars and cups, all smooth and finished and dry. The smell of wet earth filled the room.

The potter sat at his wheel — a flat stone that turned round and round. He had a lump of wet clay on the stone, and his hands were wrapped around it. He was an older man with strong fingers, dark from years of working with clay.

As the wheel spun, the potter's fingers pressed and shaped the clay. It started to grow taller. A jar was forming — round at the bottom, thin at the top. Water dripped down from the potter's hands. The clay moved like it was alive, rising and changing shape with every turn of the wheel.

Then something went wrong. The clay buckled. A weak spot opened in the wall — maybe a tiny bubble of air inside, maybe a bit of grit that didn't belong. One side caved in. The jar that was forming crumpled and sagged on the wheel.

Jeremiah waited for the potter to throw the ruined clay away.

But the potter didn't throw the clay away. He didn't get mad. He didn't even sigh. He just pressed the clay back down into a flat lump again, smooth and round. The wheel kept spinning. In a few seconds the clay looked like it had never been a jar at all.

Then the potter dipped his hands in water and started again. This time he made something different — wider at the bottom, shorter at the top. A bowl, maybe, or a wide cup. His hands were gentle but firm. The walls rose evenly. The rim came out smooth. The new shape held.

God spoke to Jeremiah right there in the potter's shop. "See this? Like the clay in the potter's hand — that's how you are in my hand."

The wheel turned. The potter's wet fingers lifted away. And the bowl sat on the spinning stone, whole and new and ready.
