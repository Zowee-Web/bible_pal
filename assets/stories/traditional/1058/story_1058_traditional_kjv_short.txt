The Lord bade Jeremiah go visit a potter. So Jeremiah went down the hill unto the place where the potters wrought, hard by the brook at the bottom of the valley. He could hear the humming of the wheel ere he reached the door.

He found the potter's house. The door stood open. Within it was cool and quiet. Clay dust hung in the air. Shelves along the walls bare bowls and jars and cups, all smooth and finished and dry. The savour of wet earth filled the chamber.

The potter sat at his wheel — a flat stone that turned round and round. He had a lump of wet clay upon the stone, and his hands were wrapped about it. He was an aged man with strong fingers, dark from years of working with clay.

As the wheel spun, the potter's fingers pressed and shaped the clay. It began to grow taller. A jar was forming — round at the bottom, narrow at the top. Water dripped down from the potter's hands. The clay moved as though it were alive, rising and changing shape with every turning of the wheel.

Then something went amiss. The clay buckled. A weak place opened in the wall — peradventure a tiny bubble of air within, peradventure a bit of grit that belonged not. One side caved in. The jar that was forming crumpled and sagged upon the wheel.

Jeremiah waited for the potter to cast the marred clay away.

But the potter cast not the clay away. He waxed not wroth. He sighed not, even. He did but press the clay down again into a flat lump, smooth and round. The wheel kept turning. In a few moments the clay looked as though it had never been a jar at all.

Then the potter dipped his hands in water and began again. This time he made another thing — wider at the bottom, shorter at the top. A bowl, peradventure, or a wide cup. His hands were gentle but firm. The walls rose evenly. The rim came forth smooth. The new shape held.

The Lord spake unto Jeremiah even there in the potter's house. "Seest thou this? As the clay is in the potter's hand — so are ye in mine hand."

The wheel turned. The potter's wet fingers lifted away. And the bowl sat upon the spinning stone, whole and new and ready.
