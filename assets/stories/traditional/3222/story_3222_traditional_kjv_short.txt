In all Israel there was no man whose beauty was so much praised as Absalom’s. From the sole of his foot unto the crown of his head there was no blemish in him. At every year’s end he polled the hair of his head, for it had become heavy upon him. When he weighed the hair of his head, its weight was two hundred shekels after the king’s weight. Three sons and one daughter were born unto Absalom. The daughter’s name was Tamar, and she was a woman of fair countenance.

Absalom dwelt two full years in Jerusalem, and throughout those years he saw not the king’s face. He sent word unto Joab, asking him to come, that he might send him unto the king. Joab would not come unto him. Absalom sent a second time for Joab to come, but again Joab would not come unto him.

Then Absalom said unto his servants, “See, Joab’s field is near mine, and he hath barley there; go and set it on fire.” The servants of Absalom set Joab’s field on fire.

Joab arose and came unto Absalom at his house. He asked him, “Wherefore have thy servants set my field on fire?”

Absalom answered Joab, “Behold, I sent unto thee, saying, Come hither, that I may send thee to the king, to say, Wherefore am I come from Geshur? it had been good for me to have been there still: now therefore let me see the king’s face; and if there be any iniquity in me, let him kill me.”

Joab went unto the king and told him the message Absalom had given him. The king called for Absalom, and Absalom came before him. He bowed himself with his face to the ground before the king. Then the king kissed Absalom.
