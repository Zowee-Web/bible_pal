In all Israel there was no one whose beauty was praised as much as Absalom’s. From the soles of his feet to the crown of his head, there was no defect in him. He cut the hair of his head at the end of every year, for it had become heavy on him. When he weighed the hair from his head, it weighed two hundred shekels according to the king’s weight. Three sons and one daughter were born to Absalom. His daughter’s name was Tamar, and she was a woman with a beautiful face.

Absalom lived in Jerusalem for two full years, and during those years he did not see the king’s face. He sent word asking Joab to come to him, so that he could send him to the king. But Joab would not come. A second time Absalom sent for Joab, asking him to come, and a second time Joab would not come to him.

Absalom said to his servants, “Behold, Joab’s field is near mine, and he has barley there. Go and set it on fire.” His servants set Joab’s field on fire.

Then Joab arose and came to Absalom’s house. “Why have your servants set my field on fire?” he asked.

Absalom answered Joab, “Behold, I sent to you, saying, ‘Come here, that I may send you to the king, to say, “Why have I come from Geshur? It would be better for me to be there still. Now therefore, let me see the king’s face; and if there is iniquity in me, let him kill me.”’”

So Joab went to the king and delivered Absalom’s message. The king called for Absalom to come before him. Absalom came to the king and bowed himself with his face to the ground in the king’s presence. The king kissed Absalom.
