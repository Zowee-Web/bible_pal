In the days before Samuel became a prophet, the word of the Lord was precious in Israel; there was no open vision. Samuel was a young child, dwelling in the tabernacle at Shiloh. He had been given unto the Lord by his mother, Hannah, and he ministered under the care of Eli the priest. The night was very quiet, and the soft light from the golden lamp in the house of God flickered gently in the calm air. Each man had his place in the tabernacle. Eli, who was old and whose eyes began to wax dim, was laid down in his own place. Samuel was laid down to sleep nigh unto the ark of God, close by the lamp, as he was wont.

The world without was wrapped in darkness. Within, the tabernacle felt warm, peaceful, and safe. Samuel hearkened unto the hush. Only the faint sound of Eli's slow breathing and the soft crackle of the lamp's oil broke the silence. Suddenly, in the stillness, a gentle voice called unto him, "Samuel!" Samuel awoke quickly. He thought it must be Eli that called him. He ran unto Eli, and said, "Here am I; for thou calledst me." Eli was half asleep. He shook his head and answered softly, "I called not. Go, lie down again." Samuel crept back unto his place and laid him down once more.

Sleep settled about him like a blanket. But again, the voice came: "Samuel!" Samuel sat up, his heart beating a little faster now. He went unto Eli and whispered, "Here am I; for thou calledst me." Eli blinked and looked upon Samuel, confused. Then he said unto him gently, "I called not, my son. Go, lie down again." Samuel crept back, feeling perplexed but safe. In those days, Samuel did not yet know the Lord, neither was the word of the Lord yet revealed unto him. The young child laid him down again, compassed about by the golden glow and calm, and waited for sleep.

Then, a third time, the voice called, "Samuel!" Samuel arose again. This time he went unto Eli, sure that the priest had called him. "Here am I; for thou calledst me!" he said again. Eli sat up. Something deeper stirred in his mind. Eli perceived now that it was not the voice of man that called Samuel, but the Lord himself. The thought made the chamber feel even more still. Eli looked upon Samuel with kindness and spake quietly, "Go, lie down: and it shall be, if he call thee, that thou shalt say, Speak, Lord; for thy servant heareth."

Samuel bowed his head, seeking to understand. He walked back unto his little bed beside the ark. He hearkened unto the gentle sound of the lamp burning. It felt holy, waiting. Then the Lord came, and stood by Samuel. In the soft quiet, the voice called, "Samuel! Samuel!" The Lord spake his name twice. Samuel sat up bravely. Even as Eli had told him, he answered, "Speak; for thy servant heareth."

In the peaceful glow of the lamp, God spake unto Samuel. He told him that a weighty message was coming for Israel. The words were grave, though Samuel was yet very young. God said that he would do a thing in Israel, at which both the ears of every one that heareth it should tingle. The Lord spake gently but firmly, declaring unto Samuel all that he would have Eli to know. The voice of God was strong and kind in the darkness.

After God had made an end of speaking, the hush returned. The flickering oil lamp was nearly spent, and a little of the dawn began to creep across the sky without. Samuel lay in his bed until the morning, his heart full of wonder and the precious words of the Lord. When Eli awoke, the air was cool and fresh with the promise of a new day. Samuel opened the doors of the house of the Lord, letting the light pour in.

Samuel was afraid to shew Eli what he had heard, for the message was not easy. But Eli called him, "Samuel, my son!" Samuel answered, "Here am I." Eli looked kindly upon him and asked softly, "What is the thing that the Lord hath said unto thee? I pray thee hide it not from me." He urged Samuel, wishing to know all that the Lord had spoken. Samuel trusted Eli and told him every whit, and hid nothing from him. Eli hearkened and understood that it was the will of the Lord.

Eli bowed his head quietly, accepting all that Samuel told him. He said, "It is the Lord: let him do what seemeth him good." The words bore kindness and peace within them. Eli grew not wroth nor troubled, but sat quietly, trusting God in all things.

And Samuel grew, and the Lord was with him, and did let none of his words fall to the ground. God was with him as he lived and ministered at Shiloh. All that the Lord told Samuel came to pass. The people of Israel began to hear that the Lord was speaking again, and they saw that Samuel was a true prophet. And all Israel from Dan even to Beersheba knew that Samuel was established to be a prophet of the Lord. The Lord appeared again in Shiloh and spake more unto Samuel. The words of the Lord filled Samuel's heart with light, and he declared them faithfully. The quiet nights in the house of God were never empty again. With every word and every day, Samuel learned to hearken, and the gentle voice of God guided him alway.

These things came to pass even as the Lord had spoken, bringing peace to Samuel, to Eli, and unto all Israel.

As the seasons turned, Samuel grew in wisdom and strength. The people learned to seek his counsel, coming unto Shiloh with their questions and burdens. Sometimes they came at the dawning, dusty from the way, and waited in the shade until Samuel appeared. He hearkened with gentle eyes and careful silence, weighing each story and guiding with the words that God gave him. There were days when Samuel's heart felt the sorrow or the care of them that came. But always, the quiet certainty of the presence of the Lord brought light into every trouble, even as the morning sun over the hills.

Eli beheld Samuel with pride and quiet joy. The old priest moved more slowly now, leaning upon his staff. Yet he found hope in each day, strengthened by the knowledge that the days to come for Israel rested in hands both young and faithful. Eli would at times sit with Samuel beneath the olive trees, letting the hush of the wilderness bear their prayers up unto God. In those moments, Samuel felt both the nearness of the Lord and the warmth of Eli's care, even as lamp and fire burning together.

There were times when hard tidings had to be declared. Samuel, though gentle, hid not that which God had spoken unto him. Yet the people beheld his honesty and faithfulness. Even in sadness, hope grew, as the people saw that the words of God, though sometimes hard, alway brought truth. In villages and over the fires of the camp, the story of Samuel's call passed among Israel, putting them in remembrance that God was nigh and that his light was not gone out.

As years went by, the peace from those silent nights settled deeper over the land. Families gathered at feasts, and children ran while elders recounted how God had called a boy in the hush of the temple. Samuel himself, though now called a prophet, never lost the wonder he had felt that first night. He ministered unto the Lord with a steady heart, opening the doors each morning, hearkening through the quiet hours, speaking that which was given unto him.

So, hope grew again in Israel. The people looked unto the days ahead without fear, trusting the God who spake in the darkness and brought his word to light. With every gentle dawn and every hushed night, God abode nigh unto Samuel and unto all that sought him, guiding them softly through each new day.
