Long ago, in the town of Shiloh, there stood the tabernacle of the Lord—a beautiful tent where God’s people came to worship. Inside this holy place, the golden lamp burned quietly through the night, filling the air with gentle, warm light. Samuel, a young boy, lived in the tabernacle. He served the Lord under Eli, the old priest with kind eyes and a gentle voice. Samuel’s mother had brought him here when he was just a little child, and now he slept and worked in God’s house each day and night.

One night, as darkness filled the tabernacle and every sound seemed hushed, Samuel was lying down in his usual place. Eli rested nearby, his sight now dim and weak with age. Outside, the world was silent; even the oil lamp’s flame flickered softly without a sound. As Samuel’s eyes grew heavy, he settled down into his blanket, ready to sleep.

Suddenly, into this quietness came a voice. It sounded as clear as someone calling in the room. “Samuel!” came the call. Samuel opened his eyes, wide awake. He thought Eli might have called him. Quickly, Samuel got up from his bed and hurried to Eli’s side. “Here I am. You called me,” he said. Eli shifted in his bed and looked at the boy. “I didn’t call. Lie down again,” Eli replied. Samuel, not understanding, went back to his bed, wrapped himself in his blanket, and closed his eyes once more.

Soon again, the stillness of the night was broken. “Samuel!” the voice called for the second time. Samuel woke and ran to Eli. “Here I am, for you called me,” he said softly. But Eli shook his head gently. “I didn’t call, my son. Lie down again.” Samuel returned to his place, puzzled and maybe a little curious. He did not know yet that it was the Lord who was calling him, for Samuel had never heard God speak before.

A third time, the voice called, “Samuel!” The boy sat up and hurried once more to Eli. This time his heart beat quickly, but he only said what he knew, “Here I am, for you called me.” Eli’s brow furrowed. He was silent for a moment, then his eyes grew thoughtful. By now, Eli realized that something special was happening. He understood that the Lord was calling the boy.

Eli looked tenderly at Samuel and spoke gently. “Go and lie down again. If he calls you, say, ‘Speak, Lord, for your servant hears.’” Samuel nodded, feeling safe and brave now, and walked quietly back to his bed. The room was still and full of wonder, the flame of the lamp glowing softly. Samuel waited, his heart listening.

Then the Lord came and stood there, calling as before, “Samuel! Samuel!” This time, Samuel answered as Eli had told him, his voice steady and calm, “Speak, for your servant hears.” God then spoke to Samuel, telling him words that Samuel would remember forever. The Lord explained that He was going to do something in Israel that would make people’s ears tingle. God trusted Samuel, and spoke to him kindly.

Samuel stayed in his bed until the morning, thinking about all that had happened. When day arrived, he opened the doors of the Lord’s house as he did each day. Eli called for him. Samuel came, afraid to tell Eli what the Lord had said. But Eli gently asked Samuel to share, not to hide anything, and Samuel told him everything, word for word. Eli listened quietly, and when Samuel finished, Eli said, “It is the Lord. Let him do what seems good to him.”

From that day on, Samuel grew, and the Lord was with him. Everyone in Israel learned that Samuel was God’s special prophet, because every word God spoke to him truly happened. The Lord continued to appear in Shiloh, calling and guiding Samuel, and Samuel always listened. The gentle voice in the darkness had become Samuel’s guide, and he grew up walking in God’s light.

As Samuel grew older, peace settled in Shiloh for many years. People came to seek his wisdom, and hope returned to their hearts. Samuel remembered that first call, and quietly, he gave thanks.
