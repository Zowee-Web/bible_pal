Long ago, in the town of Shiloh, there stood the tabernacle of the Lord—a goodly tent wherein the people of God came to worship. Within this holy place, the golden lamp burned quietly through the night, filling the air with gentle, warm light. Samuel, a young child, dwelt in the tabernacle. He ministered unto the Lord under Eli, the old priest with kind eyes and a gentle voice. Samuel's mother had brought him hither when he was but a little child, and now he slept and laboured in the house of God each day and night.

One night, as darkness filled the tabernacle and every sound seemed hushed, Samuel was laid down in his accustomed place. Eli rested nigh at hand, his sight now dim and waxed weak with age. Without, the world was silent; even the flame of the oil lamp flickered softly without a sound. As Samuel's eyes grew heavy, he settled down into his blanket, ready to sleep.

Suddenly, into this stillness came a voice. It sounded as clear as one calling within the chamber. "Samuel!" came the call. Samuel opened his eyes, wide awake. He thought that Eli had called him. Quickly, Samuel arose from his bed and ran unto Eli, and said, "Here am I; for thou calledst me." Eli stirred upon his bed and looked upon the child. "I called not. Lie down again," said Eli. Samuel, not understanding, went back unto his bed, wrapped himself in his blanket, and closed his eyes once more.

Soon again, the stillness of the night was broken. "Samuel!" the voice called the second time. Samuel awoke and ran unto Eli. "Here am I, for thou calledst me," he said softly. But Eli shook his head gently. "I called not, my son. Lie down again." Samuel returned unto his place, perplexed and perchance a little curious. He knew not yet that it was the Lord that called him, for Samuel did not yet know the Lord, neither was the word of the Lord yet revealed unto him.

A third time the voice called, "Samuel!" The child sat up and hastened once more unto Eli. This time his heart beat quickly, yet he only spake what he knew, "Here am I, for thou calledst me." Eli's brow was furrowed. He was silent for a moment, then his eyes grew thoughtful. By now, Eli perceived that something marvellous was come to pass. He understood that the Lord had called the child.

Eli looked tenderly upon Samuel and spake gently. "Go, lie down: and it shall be, if he call thee, that thou shalt say, Speak, Lord; for thy servant heareth." Samuel bowed his head, feeling safe and bold now, and walked quietly back unto his bed. The chamber was still and full of wonder, the flame of the lamp glowing softly. Samuel waited, his heart hearkening.

Then the Lord came, and stood, and called as at other times, "Samuel! Samuel!" This time, Samuel answered as Eli had told him, his voice steady and calm, "Speak; for thy servant heareth." Then God spake unto Samuel, telling him words that Samuel should remember for ever. The Lord declared that he would do a thing in Israel, at which both the ears of every one that heareth it should tingle. God trusted Samuel, and spake unto him kindly.

Samuel lay in his bed until the morning, pondering all that had been done. When the day was come, he opened the doors of the house of the Lord as he was wont to do each day. Eli called for him. Samuel came, fearing to shew Eli what the Lord had said. But Eli gently bade Samuel to declare it, and to hide nothing from him, and Samuel told him every whit, and hid nothing from him. Eli hearkened quietly, and when Samuel had made an end, Eli said, "It is the Lord: let him do what seemeth him good."

From that day forth, Samuel grew, and the Lord was with him. All Israel knew that Samuel was God's chosen prophet, for every word that God spake unto him surely came to pass. The Lord continued to appear in Shiloh, calling and guiding Samuel, and Samuel always hearkened. The gentle voice in the darkness had become Samuel's guide, and he grew up walking in the light of God.

As Samuel waxed older, peace settled in Shiloh for many years. The people came to seek his wisdom, and hope returned unto their hearts. Samuel remembered that first call, and quietly, he gave thanks.
