In the days before Samuel became a prophet, Israel had few words from God. Samuel was a young boy, living in the tabernacle at Shiloh. He had been given to the Lord by his mother, Hannah, and he served under the care of Eli the priest. The night was very quiet, and the soft light from the golden lamp in God’s house flickered gently in the calm air. Each person had their spot in the tabernacle. Eli, who was old and whose eyesight was growing weak, lay resting in his own place. Samuel lay near the Ark of God, close to the lamp, as he always did.

The world outside was wrapped in darkness. Inside, the tabernacle felt warm, peaceful, and safe. Samuel listened to the hush. Only the faint sounds of Eli’s slow breathing and the soft pop from the lamp’s oil broke the silence. Suddenly, in the stillness, a gentle voice called out to him, “Samuel!” Samuel woke quickly. He thought it must be Eli calling. He hurried to Eli’s side and said, “Here I am, for you called me.” Eli was half asleep. He shook his head and answered softly, “I didn’t call. Go back and lie down.” Samuel tiptoed back to his place and lay down once more.

Sleep settled around him like a blanket. But again, the voice came: “Samuel!” Samuel sat up, his heart beating a little faster now. He walked to Eli and whispered, “Here I am, for you called me.” Eli blinked and looked at Samuel, confused. Then he told him gently, “I did not call, my son. Go lie down again.” Samuel crept back, feeling puzzled but safe. In those days, Samuel did not yet know the Lord’s voice, for God had not spoken to him before. The young boy lay down again, surrounded by the golden glow and calm, and waited for sleep.

Then, for a third time, the voice called, “Samuel!” Samuel got up again. This time he went to Eli, sure that the priest had called him. “Here I am, for you called me!” he said again. Eli sat up. Something deeper flickered in his mind. He realized now that it was not a man’s voice calling Samuel, but the Lord Himself. The thought made the room feel even more still. Eli looked at Samuel with kindness and spoke quietly, “Go and lie down. If you are called again, say, ‘Speak, Lord, for your servant hears.’”

Samuel nodded, trying to understand. He walked back to his little bed next to the Ark. He listened to the gentle sound of the lamp burning. It felt special, waiting. Then, the Lord came and stood near Samuel. In the soft quiet, the voice called, “Samuel! Samuel!” The Lord spoke his name twice. Samuel sat up bravely. Just as Eli had told him, he answered, “Speak, for your servant hears.”

In the peaceful lamp glow, God spoke to Samuel. He told him that a special message was coming for Israel. The words were important, though Samuel was still very young. God said that He would do something in Israel that would make people’s ears tingle. The Lord spoke gently but firmly, sharing everything with Samuel that He wanted Eli to know. God’s voice was strong and kind in the darkness.

After God finished speaking, the hush returned. The flickering oil lamp was nearly out, and a little bit of dawn began to sneak across the sky outside. Samuel lay in bed until morning, his heart full of wonder and the precious words of the Lord. When Eli woke up, the air was cool and fresh with the promise of a new day. Samuel opened the doors of the Lord’s house, letting light pour in.

Samuel felt shy and afraid to tell Eli what he had heard, for the message was not easy. But Eli called him, “Samuel, my son!” Samuel replied, “Here I am.” Eli looked kindly at him and asked softly, “What was the thing that God told you? Please don’t hide it from me.” He urged Samuel, wanting to know all that the Lord had said. Samuel trusted Eli and told him everything, keeping nothing back. Eli listened and understood it was the Lord’s will.

Eli nodded quietly, accepting all that Samuel told him. He said, “It is the Lord. Let Him do what seems good to Him.” The words held kindness and peace in them. Eli did not grow angry or upset, but sat quietly, trusting God in everything.

As Samuel grew, he stayed close to the Lord. God was with him as he lived and served at Shiloh. Everything the Lord told Samuel came true. The people of Israel began to hear that the Lord was speaking again, and they saw that Samuel was a true prophet. From Dan to Beersheba, all Israel knew that Samuel was trusted as a prophet of the Lord. God continued to appear at Shiloh and spoke more to Samuel. The Lord’s words filled Samuel’s heart with light, and he shared them faithfully. The quiet nights in God’s house were never empty again. With each word and every day, Samuel learned to listen, and God’s gentle voice guided him always.

These things happened just as the Lord had spoken, bringing peace to Samuel, Eli, and all of Israel.

Through the turning seasons, Samuel grew in wisdom and strength. The people learned to seek his counsel, coming to Shiloh with their questions and burdens. Sometimes they arrived at dawn, dusty from the road, and waited in the shade until Samuel appeared. He listened with gentle eyes and careful silence, weighing each story and guiding with the words God gave him. There were days when Samuel’s heart felt the sorrow or the worry of those who came. But always, the quiet certainty of God’s presence brought light into every trouble, like morning sun over the hills.

Eli watched Samuel with pride and quiet joy. The old priest moved more slowly now, leaning on his staff. Still, he found hope in each day, strengthened by the knowledge that Israel’s future rested in hands both young and faithful. Eli would sometimes sit with Samuel under the olive trees, letting the hush of the wilderness carry their prayers to God. In those moments, Samuel felt both the nearness of the Lord and the warmth of Eli’s care, like lamp and fire burning together.

There were times when difficult news had to be shared. Samuel, though gentle, did not hide what God had spoken to him. Yet the people saw his honesty and faithfulness. Even in sadness, hope grew, as people saw that God’s words, though sometimes hard, always brought truth. In villages and over campfires, the story of Samuel’s call circled among Israel, reminding them that God was near and that His light had not gone out.

As years passed, the peace from those silent nights settled deeper over the land. Families gathered at feasts, and children ran while elders recalled how God had called a boy in the temple’s hush. Samuel himself, though now called a prophet, never lost the wonder he had felt that first night. He served the Lord with a steady heart, opening the doors each morning, listening through the quiet hours, speaking what was given him.

So, hope grew in Israel again. The people looked to the days ahead without fear, trusting the God who spoke in the darkness and brought His word to light. With every gentle dawn and every hushed night, God stayed near to Samuel and to all who sought Him, guiding them softly through each new day.
