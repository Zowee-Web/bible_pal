David spake the song unto the Lord on the day when his enemies were all behind him. The wars with the Philistines and the Moabites and the Syrians had been won. Saul was long dead. The kingdom was at peace; and the king sat in his own house in Jerusalem, upon the cedar of Lebanon that Hiram had sent, and he set down a song — the same song that the singers would later weave into the psalter as the eighteenth psalm. The words began thus.

David spake unto the Lord the words of this song in the day that the Lord had delivered him out of the hand of all his enemies, and out of the hand of Saul,

and he said:

"The Lord is my rock,
and my fortress,
and my deliverer;

The God of my rock; in him will I trust:
he is my shield, and the horn of my salvation,
my high tower, and my refuge,
my saviour; thou savest me from violence.

I will call on the Lord, who is worthy to be praised:
so shall I be saved from mine enemies.

When the waves of death compassed me,
the floods of ungodly men made me afraid;

The sorrows of hell compassed me about;
the snares of death prevented me;

In my distress I called upon the Lord,
and cried to my God:
and he did hear my voice out of his temple,
and my cry did enter into his ears."

And the song went on for many more lines after these. The king set the scroll down upon the table beside him and beheld the city that had been given into his hand. The walls of Jerusalem stood firm; the children of the household were in their courts; and the seven words for what the Lord had been unto him — rock, fortress, deliverer, shield, horn, high tower, refuge — kept their place at the head of the song that would be sung in Israel for the rest of the days of his line.
