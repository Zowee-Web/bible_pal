When Jacob looked up and saw Esau coming, and four hundred men at his back, he set his household in order to meet him. The handmaids and their children he placed in front, Leah and hers behind them, and Rachel with Joseph last of all. Then he went out ahead of them every one, and bowed himself to the earth seven times as he drew near to his brother.

But Esau did not wait for the seventh bow. He ran to meet him, and threw his arms about his neck, and kissed him; and the two of them wept.

Then Esau lifted his eyes to the women and the little ones behind, and asked who they were. They were the children, Jacob said, whom God had graciously given his servant; and one company after another — the handmaids with their children, then Leah with hers, and last of all Rachel and Joseph — came forward and bowed low before him.

And what, Esau asked, was the meaning of all that drove of cattle he had met upon the road? It was sent, said Jacob, to find grace in the sight of his lord. But Esau had no need of it. I have enough, my brother, he said; keep what is thine unto thyself. Yet Jacob pressed the gift upon him. If he had found grace in his brother's sight, then let the present be received; for to look upon Esau's face, and be met so, was to him as though he had seen the face of God, and been received. God had dealt graciously with him, and he had enough. So he urged him, until Esau took it.

Let us journey on together, Esau said then, and I will go before thee. But Jacob would not drive his household so hard. The children were tender, he said, and the flocks and herds had their young; overdriven a single day, they would die. Let his lord go on ahead, and he would follow at a gentle pace, as the cattle and the children were able, until he came to him at Seir. Esau offered to leave some of his men behind, but Jacob asked for nothing more than his brother's goodwill; and so Esau turned back that day toward Seir.

Jacob himself journeyed on to Succoth, where he built a house and made booths for his cattle, and gave the place its name. From there he came to Shalem, a city of Shechem in the land of Canaan, and pitched his tent before it. The field where he had spread his tent he bought from the children of Hamor for a hundred pieces of money. And there he raised an altar, and called it El-elohe-Israel — God, the God of Israel.
