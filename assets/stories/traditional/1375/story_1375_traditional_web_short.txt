The herd of swine had run down the steep place into the lake of Galilee. The herdsmen had fled into the country and the city, telling what had happened. The people of the region had come out to see — and they had seen the man who had lived in the tombs, the one who had cried day and night among the mountains and the tombs and cut himself with stones, sitting at the feet of Jesus, clothed and in his right mind. They had been afraid, and had begged Jesus to depart from their borders. Jesus had turned back toward the shore. And the man who had been restored followed him.

As he was entering into the boat, he who had been possessed by demons begged him that he might be with him.

He didn't allow him, but said to him,

"Go to your house, to your friends, and tell them what great things the Lord has done for you, and how he had mercy on you."

He went his way, and began to proclaim in Decapolis how Jesus had done great things for him, and everyone marveled.

And the boat pushed away from the shore of the country of the Gerasenes. The man who had been restored stood on the bank and watched it until it could no longer be seen, and then he turned and walked back toward the cities of the Decapolis — the ten cities scattered through the country east of the lake — and the people in the streets and at the gates and in the courts of those cities heard from his own mouth what had been done for him, and they could not keep from marveling.
