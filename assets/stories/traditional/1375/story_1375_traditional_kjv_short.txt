The herd of swine had run down the steep place into the lake of Galilee. The herdsmen had fled into the country and the city, telling what had come to pass. The people of the region had come out to see — and they had beheld the man which had dwelt among the tombs, the one which had cried day and night among the mountains and the tombs and cut himself with stones, sitting at the feet of Jesus, clothed and in his right mind. They had been afraid, and had besought Jesus to depart from their coasts. Jesus had turned back toward the shore. And the man that had been restored followed after him.

And when he was come into the ship, he that had been possessed with the devil prayed him that he might be with him.

Howbeit Jesus suffered him not, but saith unto him,

"Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee."

And he departed, and began to publish in Decapolis how great things Jesus had done for him: and all men did marvel.

And the ship pushed away from the shore of the country of the Gadarenes. The man that had been restored stood upon the bank and beheld it until it could no longer be seen, and then he turned and walked back toward the cities of the Decapolis — the ten cities scattered through the country east of the lake — and the people in the streets and at the gates and in the courts of those cities heard from his own mouth what had been done for him, and they could not keep from marvelling.
