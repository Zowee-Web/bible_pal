A Pharisee named Simon invited Jesus to eat at his house. It was an occasion of some formality — the table laid with care, the lamps lit, the guests arranged on cushions around the low table, reclining on their sides with their feet stretched out behind them. The bread was broken. The wine was poured. The conversation moved in careful, measured tones — the kind of talk that happens when a man of religious standing is observing a guest more than he is welcoming one.

Then a woman came in from the street.

She was known in the city. Known as a sinner — that was the word used, and no one needed to explain what it meant. She had not been invited. She carried a small alabaster flask of ointment in her hands, and she moved through the room without speaking until she stood behind Jesus, near his feet.

She was weeping. Tears fell from her face onto his feet — not a few drops, but enough to wet the dusty skin. She knelt down, and with her hair loose around her shoulders, she began to wipe his feet with it. She kissed them — again and again — and then she opened the flask and poured the ointment over them. The fragrance filled the room, rich and sweet, spreading into every corner.

No one spoke. The guests watched. Some looked away, uncomfortable. Others stared. Simon watched with sharp, silent attention.

He thought to himself, "If this man were really a prophet, he would know who is touching him and what kind of woman she is. He would know that she is a sinner."

Jesus turned to him. "Simon, I have something to say to you."

"Say it, Teacher."

"A certain moneylender had two debtors. One owed five hundred denarii, and the other owed fifty. When neither could pay, he forgave them both. Now which of them will love him more?"

Simon considered the question. "I suppose," he said slowly, "the one who was forgiven more."

"You have judged correctly," Jesus said.

Then he turned toward the woman. She was still kneeling at his feet, her hair damp, her hands folded around the flask. Jesus spoke to Simon, but his eyes were on her.

"Do you see this woman?" he said. "I entered your house. You gave me no water for my feet — but she has wet my feet with her tears and wiped them with her hair. You gave me no kiss — but from the time I came in, she has not stopped kissing my feet. You did not anoint my head with oil — but she has anointed my feet with ointment."

He paused. The room was quiet.

"Therefore I tell you, her sins, which are many, are forgiven — for she loved much. But the one who is forgiven little, loves little."

Then he looked at the woman directly and spoke to her.

"Your sins are forgiven."

The other guests began to murmur among themselves. "Who is this man," they said, "who even forgives sins?"

But Jesus kept his eyes on the woman. His voice was gentle.

"Your faith has saved you," he said. "Go in peace."
