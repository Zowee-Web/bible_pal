A Pharisee named Simon invited Jesus to eat at his house. The table was set. The lamps were lit. Guests reclined on cushions around the low table, leaning on their elbows, their feet stretched out behind them. The food was laid out — bread, olives, herbs, wine. The conversation was polite, measured, the kind of talk that happens when a religious leader is sizing up a guest.

Then a woman came in.

She was known in the city as a sinner. No one said her name. She had not been invited. She came in from the street carrying a small alabaster flask of ointment, and she moved through the room until she stood behind Jesus, near his feet.

She was weeping. The tears fell from her face onto his feet — not a few tears, but a steady stream, enough to wet the dust-covered skin. She knelt down and began to wipe his feet with her hair. She kissed them. She opened the flask and poured the ointment over them. The smell of the perfume filled the room.

No one spoke. The guests watched. Simon watched.

Simon thought to himself, "If this man were a prophet, he would know who is touching him and what kind of woman she is — that she is a sinner."

Jesus turned to him. "Simon," he said, "I have something to say to you."

"Say it, Teacher."

"A certain lender had two debtors. One owed five hundred denarii, and the other fifty. When they could not pay, he forgave them both. Now which of them will love him more?"

Simon answered, "I suppose the one who was forgiven more."

"You have judged correctly," Jesus said.

Then he turned toward the woman, though he spoke to Simon. "Do you see this woman? I entered your house. You gave me no water for my feet, but she has wet my feet with her tears and wiped them with her hair. You gave me no kiss, but she has not stopped kissing my feet since she came in. You did not anoint my head with oil, but she has anointed my feet with ointment."

He paused.

"Therefore I tell you, her sins, which are many, are forgiven — for she loved much. But the one who is forgiven little, loves little."

Then he said to her, "Your sins are forgiven."

The guests at the table began to murmur among themselves. "Who is this, who even forgives sins?"

But Jesus looked at the woman.

"Your faith has saved you," he said. "Go in peace."
