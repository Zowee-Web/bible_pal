One day, a man named Simon invited Jesus to his house for dinner. Simon was a Pharisee — a religious leader who knew all the rules about the Bible. He wanted to watch Jesus up close and decide for himself whether Jesus was really special or not.

The guests sat around a low table on cushions, the way people did back then. They leaned on one side with their feet stretched out behind them. Bread and olives and wine were on the table. The conversation was polite but careful — Simon was studying Jesus more than he was being friendly.

Then something happened that nobody expected.

A woman walked into the room from the street. She hadn't been invited. In fact, she was known around the whole town as a "sinner" — someone who had made a lot of bad choices. Most people at Simon's dinner would not have wanted her anywhere near the table.

But she came anyway. She was carrying a small jar made of a special white stone called alabaster, filled with very expensive perfume — the kind you might save up for years to buy.

She walked through the room without saying a word until she was standing behind Jesus, near his feet.

And then she started to cry.

Not quiet little tears. Big, heavy sobs that she couldn't stop. The tears poured down her face and dripped onto Jesus' feet, making the dusty skin all wet. She knelt down, and — this was really shocking to everyone watching — she let her hair down, which was a very personal thing to do in front of a room full of people. She used her hair to dry Jesus' feet. She kissed his feet over and over. And then she opened the jar and poured the perfume on them.

The room filled with the most beautiful smell. It was sweet and rich, and it floated into every corner.

Nobody moved. Nobody spoke. Everyone was staring.

Simon was thinking hard. "If Jesus were really a prophet," he thought, "he'd KNOW what kind of woman this is. He wouldn't let a sinner touch him like that."

But Jesus knew exactly what Simon was thinking. He always did.

"Simon," Jesus said, "I want to tell you a little story."

"Go ahead, Teacher."

"Two people owed money to the same person. One owed a LOT — five hundred silver coins. The other owed a little — only fifty coins. But neither of them could pay it back. So the person they owed said, 'You know what? Forget about it. You don't owe me anything anymore.' He forgave both of them."

Jesus looked at Simon. "So here's my question: Which one of those two people do you think loved him MORE?"

Simon thought about it. "Well... I'd say the one who was forgiven more."

"Exactly right," said Jesus.

Then Jesus turned and looked at the woman, who was still kneeling by his feet. He spoke to Simon, but his eyes stayed on her.

"Simon, do you see this woman? I came into YOUR house. You didn't offer me water to wash my feet — but SHE washed them with her own tears and dried them with her own hair. You didn't greet me with a kiss — but she hasn't stopped kissing my feet since she got here. You didn't put oil on my head — but she poured expensive perfume on my feet."

Then Jesus said something that made the whole room go quiet.

"I'm telling you — her many, many sins are forgiven. And do you know how I can tell? Because she LOVES so much. The person who has been forgiven only a little bit? They only love a little bit."

Then Jesus looked right at the woman. His voice was kind and warm.

"Your sins are forgiven," he said.

Some of the other guests started whispering. "Who does he think he is? How can he forgive sins?"

But Jesus didn't pay any attention to them. He just kept looking at the woman.

"Your faith has saved you," he said gently. "Go in peace."

And the woman stood up, gathered her jar, and walked back out through the door into the evening air.

The smell of the perfume stayed in the room long after she was gone.
