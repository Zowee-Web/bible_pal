One of the Pharisees, a man called Simon, desired Jesus that he would eat with him. The table was laid with care, the lamps were lit, and the guests reclined upon cushions about the low table. The bread was broken and the wine poured. The discourse moved in careful, measured tones — the manner of conversation that passeth when a man of religion observeth his guest more nearly than he welcometh him.

Then a woman came in from without.

She was known in the city as a sinner. No man spake her name. She had not been bidden. She bore a small alabaster box of ointment, and she passed through the room without speaking until she stood behind Jesus, at his feet.

She was weeping. Tears fell from her face upon his feet — not a few drops, but enough to wet the dusty skin. She kneeled, and with her hair loosed about her shoulders she began to wipe his feet therewith. She kissed them, again and again. Then she opened the box and poured the ointment upon them. The fragrance filled the room — rich, sweet, spreading into every corner.

No man spake. Some turned their faces aside. Simon watched with sharp and silent regard.

He thought within himself: "This man, if he were a prophet, would have known who and what manner of woman this is that toucheth him: for she is a sinner."

Jesus turned unto him. "Simon, I have somewhat to say unto thee."

"Master, say on."

"There was a certain creditor which had two debtors: the one owed five hundred pence, and the other fifty. And when they had nothing to pay, he frankly forgave them both. Tell me therefore, which of them will love him most?"

Simon answered carefully. "I suppose that he, to whom he forgave most."

"Thou hast rightly judged," said Jesus.

He turned toward the woman. She was yet kneeling at his feet, her hair damp, her hands clasped about the flask, her face wet. Jesus spake unto Simon, but his eyes rested upon her.

"Seest thou this woman? I entered into thine house, thou gavest me no water for my feet: but she hath washed my feet with tears, and wiped them with the hairs of her head. Thou gavest me no kiss: but this woman since the time I came in hath not ceased to kiss my feet. My head with oil thou didst not anoint: but this woman hath anointed my feet with ointment."

He paused. The room was still.

"Wherefore I say unto thee, Her sins, which are many, are forgiven; for she loved much: but to whom little is forgiven, the same loveth little."

He looked upon the woman and spake unto her directly.

"Thy sins are forgiven."

They that sat at meat began to murmur. "Who is this that forgiveth sins also?"

Jesus kept his eyes upon the woman. His voice was gentle.

"Thy faith hath saved thee," he said. "Go in peace."
