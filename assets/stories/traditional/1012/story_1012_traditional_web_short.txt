A quiet night rested over the Sea of Tiberias. Small waves lapped against the rocky shore. The air smelled like salt and wet stone. Stars dotted the wide sky above the water.

Simon Peter stood near a wooden boat with six other disciples. He said to them, "I am going fishing." They said, "We will go with you too." So they all climbed into the boat together. Oars splashed softly as they pushed out onto the dark water. Nets unrolled over the side with a gentle swoosh. They fished all through the quiet hours. But nothing came up in their nets. Not one fish. The nets came back empty again and again. Water dripped from the ropes as they pulled them in each time.

Morning light began to spread across the sky. Pink and orange colors stretched over the water. A figure stood on the sandy shore. It was Jesus, but the disciples did not recognize him from so far away. He called out across the water, "Children, do you have any fish?" They called back, "No." His voice carried clearly over the calm surface. He said, "Cast the net on the right side of the boat, and you will find some." So they threw the net to the right side. It splashed into the water and sank down. Then the net grew heavy. Fish filled it from every direction. They pulled and pulled, but they could not haul it into the boat. There were so many fish inside.

The disciple whom Jesus loved looked toward the shore. He said to Peter, "It is the Lord!" When Peter heard those words, he tucked his outer garment around himself. Then he jumped right into the water. He waded and swam toward the shore. The other disciples followed in the little boat. They dragged the heavy net full of fish behind them. They were not far from land, only about a hundred yards.

When they stepped onto the beach, they saw a small charcoal fire already burning. Fish lay cooking over the coals. Bread sat nearby on a flat stone. Warm smoke drifted up into the cool morning air. Jesus said to them, "Bring some of the fish you have just caught." Simon Peter climbed back aboard and helped drag the net onto the shore. It was full of large fish — one hundred and fifty-three of them. Even with so many, the net was not torn.

Jesus said, "Come and have breakfast." He picked up the bread and gave it to them. Then he gave them fish from the fire. The disciples sat near the warm coals. They ate together on that quiet beach. Morning sunlight touched the water and the sand around them. Smoke from the little fire curled gently upward. The sound of small waves came and went along the shore. This was the third time Jesus appeared to the disciples after he was raised. They gathered close, eating bread and fish beside the sea.
