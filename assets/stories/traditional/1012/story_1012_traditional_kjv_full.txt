The lake called Tiberias lay still and wide. Smooth water stretched out under a quiet night sky. Stars dotted the air above, and a cool breeze moved across the surface. Small waves lapped against the rocky shore. The sound was soft and steady, like a gentle pat upon the ground. Fishing boats rested near the water's edge, their ropes tied to wooden posts. The savour of old nets and damp wood hung in the air.

Simon Peter stood near one of the boats. Thomas was there also, and so was Nathanael, who came from a town called Cana in Galilee. Two sons of Zebedee stood close by. Two other of his disciples were also with them. Seven men gathered together by the lake that evening.

Simon Peter spake up. "I go a fishing," he said. His voice carried across the quiet shore. The others looked at him and nodded. "We also go with thee," they said. So all seven of them entered into the boat. Peter pushed it away from the shore. The wood scraped against the pebbles and then slid into the water. Someone took hold of the oars. The boat moved out slowly, rocking from side to side.

They cast their nets into the lake. The heavy ropes splashed as they spread wide and sank below the surface. The men waited. They drew the nets back up. Nothing came in. They tried again in a different place. The nets came up empty once more. Hour after hour, they cast and drew, cast and drew. The cool air pressed against their faces. Their hands grew weary from gripping the wet ropes. Water dropped from the nets each time they hauled them in. That night they caught nothing at all.

Then morning began to come. A thin line of light appeared along the hills to the east. The sky changed from deep blue unto a soft, pale colour. Mist sat low upon the water. The men could see the shore again, though everything still looked hazy and far away.

A figure stood upon the beach. The disciples looked toward the shore, but they knew not who it was. The man called out unto them across the water. His voice was clear in the morning air.

"Children, have ye any meat?" he asked.

The men in the boat answered him. "No," they said.

Then the man on the shore spake again. "Cast the net on the right side of the ship, and ye shall find," he said.

So the disciples took up the net. They swung it over the right side of the boat. It splashed into the water and sank down. Almost straightway, the net grew heavy. The ropes pulled tight. The men laid hold of it and tried to haul it in, but they were not able. There were so many fishes therein that the net bulged and stretched. The boat tilted slightly from the weight.

Therefore that disciple whom Jesus loved looked toward the shore. He looked carefully at the figure standing there. Then he turned unto Simon Peter. "It is the Lord," he said.

When Simon Peter heard those words, he laid hold of his fisher's coat. He had put it off while working. He girt it unto him and cast himself into the sea. Splashing and wading, he moved toward the shore as fast as he could. Water rose up about his legs and breast.

The other disciples remained in the little ship. They were not far from land, but as it were two hundred cubits away. They drew the heavy net full of fishes after them as the boat moved slowly toward the beach. The net bumped and scraped along in the water. Fish flipped and turned within it.

When they were all come to shore, they stepped out upon the sand and pebbles. And lo, they saw a wondrous thing. A small fire of coals was already burning upon the ground. Orange and red coals glowed beneath a layer of ash. Fish lay thereon, cooking slowly. Bread sat nigh. The warm savour of roasting fish and fresh bread filled the morning air.

Jesus saith unto them. "Bring of the fish which ye have now caught," he said.

Simon Peter went up unto the net. He took hold of the edge and drew it up onto the land. It was full of great fishes — an hundred and fifty and three. And for all there were so many, yet was not the net broken. Peter counted them as he drew them out, one by one. Wet fish flopped upon the sand. Water dropped from the net onto the ground.

Then Jesus saith unto them, "Come and dine."

The disciples came nigh unto the fire. They sat down upon the sand near the warm coals. And none of them durst ask him, "Who art thou?" knowing that it was the Lord. The morning light rested upon the water behind them. Smoke rose gently from the little fire.

Jesus then cometh and taketh the bread. He gave it unto them, passing pieces from hand to hand. Then he took the fish from the fire and gave likewise unto them. Each man received bread and fish. They did eat together upon the shore of the lake. The food was warm. The bread was soft. The coals crackled quietly beneath the remaining fish.

This is now the third time that Jesus shewed himself to his disciples, after that he was risen from the dead.

The lake lay calm and bright in the new morning. Small waves still lapped at the shore. The seven men sat together near the fire, eating the breakfast that Jesus had made for them. Bread crumbs dotted the sand. Fish bones rested near the coals. The savour of wood smoke hung gently in the still air. A few birds called out across the water. The sun climbed higher, warming their weary shoulders. Jesus was there with them, close and present, sharing a simple meal upon the quiet beach.
