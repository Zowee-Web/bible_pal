A quiet night rested over the Sea of Tiberias. Small waves lapped against the rocky shore. The air smelled like salt and wet stone. Stars dotted the wide sky above the water. Smooth pebbles lined the beach where the waves came in and went back out. A wooden boat sat at the water's edge, rocking gently with each small wave. Ropes lay coiled beside it on the damp ground. The hull creaked softly against the stones beneath it.

Simon Peter stood near that boat with six other disciples. His sandals pressed into the wet sand. He looked out across the still water. A light breeze moved through his hair and tunic. He said to them, "I am going fishing." They said, "We will go with you too." Thomas stood beside Nathanael, and they both stepped toward the boat. The sons of Zebedee followed close behind. Two other disciples walked with them. Their feet crunched softly on the pebbles as they gathered around the hull.

Together they pushed the boat down into the water. Wood scraped against stone as it slid forward. Cool water swirled around their ankles and shins. One by one, they climbed over the side and settled onto the wooden benches. Peter gripped an oar. Another disciple took hold of the second one. Oars splashed softly as they pushed out onto the dark water. The shore grew smaller behind them. Ripples spread in wide circles from each stroke.

Out on the open water, the air was cooler. The boat rocked gently from side to side. Stars reflected on the calm surface all around them. Nets unrolled over the side with a gentle swoosh. The woven ropes stretched out and sank slowly beneath the surface. The disciples waited. They held the edges of the net and watched the water. Minutes passed. Then they pulled the net back in. Water dripped from the ropes as they hauled them up. The net was empty. Not one fish lay inside.

They moved the boat to a new spot. Oars dipped and pulled through the water. Again they lowered the nets. Again they waited in the quiet. Waves tapped lightly against the hull. One disciple adjusted the ropes. Another shifted on the bench. They pulled the nets up once more. Still nothing. The nets came back empty again. Water ran off the ropes and dripped back into the sea.

They tried again. And again. Through the quiet hours of the night, they cast their nets over and over. Each time, they pulled them back with nothing inside. Wet rope piled on the floor of the boat. Their hands were damp and wrinkled from the work. The boat drifted slowly as they moved from place to place across the water. The stars turned overhead as the hours passed. Still, the nets held no fish.

Morning light began to spread across the sky. A thin line of pink appeared along the far edge of the water. Orange colors stretched slowly upward. The surface of the sea changed from dark to soft silver. The rocky hills along the shore became visible again. Birds began to call from somewhere near the land. The air grew just a little warmer.

A figure stood on the sandy shore. He was about a hundred yards away. From the boat, he looked small against the wide beach. It was Jesus, but the disciples did not recognize him from so far away. The early light made it hard to see clearly. He stood near the water's edge where the wet sand met the dry.

His voice carried across the calm surface. He called out, "Children, do you have any fish?" The disciples looked toward the shore. They called back, "No." Their voices traveled over the still water. The figure on the beach did not move. Then he spoke again. His words came clearly across the distance. He said, "Cast the net on the right side of the boat, and you will find some."

So they gathered the net from the floor of the boat. Wet rope slid through their hands. They lifted it together and threw it over the right side. It splashed into the water and sank down. The ropes spread wide beneath the surface. For a moment, everything was still. Then the net grew heavy. The ropes pulled tight. Fish filled the net from every direction. The disciples gripped the edges and pulled. The net stretched and bulged with fish. More and more pressed inside. They pulled and pulled, but they could not haul it into the boat. The weight was too great. The boat tilted slightly toward the right side. Water splashed against the hull as they steadied themselves.

The disciple whom Jesus loved looked toward the shore. He shielded his eyes with one hand. The morning light was growing brighter. He studied the figure standing on the sand. Then he turned to Peter. He said, "It is the Lord!" His voice was clear and sure.

When Peter heard those words, he tucked his outer garment around himself. He pulled it tight and tied it at his waist. Then he climbed onto the side of the boat. His feet gripped the wet wood. He jumped right into the water. A splash rose up around him. The sea came to his chest at first. He waded forward, pushing through the water with strong steps. Where it grew deeper, he swam. His arms moved steadily. Where it grew shallow again, he stood and walked. Water streamed off his clothes and dripped from his sleeves. He moved toward the shore with quick, steady steps.

The other disciples stayed in the little boat. They gripped the oars and began rowing toward land. Behind them, the heavy net dragged through the water. The ropes stretched tight from the back of the boat. Fish moved and splashed inside the net. The boat moved slowly because of the weight. Oar strokes sent small ripples out on each side. The shore came closer with every pull. They were not far from land, only about a hundred yards. The sandy bottom grew visible beneath the clear water as they approached.

Peter reached the beach first. Water ran from his clothes onto the sand. His wet footprints marked the shore behind him. He stood on the firm ground and looked ahead.

When the others stepped out of the boat, they saw a small charcoal fire already burning on the beach. Orange coals glowed beneath a thin layer of white ash. Fish lay cooking over the coals. Their skin had turned golden from the heat. Bread sat nearby on a flat stone. It was round and warm. Smoke drifted up into the cool morning air. The smell of cooking fish and fresh bread spread across the beach.

Jesus stood near the fire. He said to them, "Bring some of the fish you have just caught." Simon Peter turned and walked back toward the water. He climbed aboard the boat and gripped the net with both hands. Other disciples helped him. Together they pulled the heavy net up over the side and dragged it onto the shore. Sand stuck to the wet ropes. Fish spilled across the beach as the net opened. They were all large fish. One hundred and fifty-three of them. The disciples could see them clearly in the growing light. Even with so many, the net was not torn. Every knot held firm. Every rope stayed whole.

Jesus said, "Come and have breakfast." The disciples walked toward the fire. Their sandals pressed into the warm sand near the coals. Jesus reached down and picked up the bread. He held it in both hands and gave it to them. Then he gave them fish from the fire. The cooked fish was warm and flaky. The bread was soft.

The disciples sat near the warm coals. Some sat on smooth stones. Others rested on the dry sand. They ate together on that quiet beach. No one needed to ask who he was. They could see him there beside the fire, handing them bread and fish.

Morning sunlight touched the water and the sand around them. The sea had turned a gentle blue. Small waves came and went along the shore with a soft, steady sound. Smoke from the little fire curled gently upward into the bright air. Birds called again from the rocky hills above the beach.

This was the third time Jesus appeared to the disciples after he was raised. They gathered close, eating bread and fish beside the sea. The coals glowed warm. The bread passed from hand to hand. And the morning stretched out wide and bright over the water.
