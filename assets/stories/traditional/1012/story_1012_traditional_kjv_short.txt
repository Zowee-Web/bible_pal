A quiet night rested over the sea of Tiberias. Small waves lapped against the rocky shore. The air bore the savour of salt and wet stone. Stars dotted the wide sky above the water.

Simon Peter stood near a wooden boat with six other disciples. He said unto them, "I go a fishing." They said unto him, "We also go with thee." So they all entered into the boat together. Oars splashed softly as they pushed out upon the dark water. Nets unrolled over the side with a gentle swoosh. They fished all through the quiet hours. But that night they took nothing. Not one fish. The nets came back empty again and again. Water dropped from the ropes as they drew them in each time.

Morning light began to spread across the sky. Pink and orange colours stretched over the water. A figure stood upon the sandy shore. It was Jesus, but the disciples knew not that it was Jesus from so far away. He called unto them across the water, saying, "Children, have ye any meat?" They answered him, "No." His voice carried clearly over the calm surface. He said unto them, "Cast the net on the right side of the ship, and ye shall find." So they cast it on the right side. It splashed into the water and sank down. Then the net grew heavy. Fish filled it from every direction. They drew and drew, but now they were not able to draw it for the multitude of fishes.

Therefore that disciple whom Jesus loved looked toward the shore. He said unto Peter, "It is the Lord." When Peter heard those words, he girt his fisher's coat unto him. Then he cast himself into the sea. He waded and swam toward the shore. The other disciples came in a little ship. They drew the heavy net full of fishes behind them. They were not far from land, but as it were two hundred cubits.

When they were come to land, they saw a small fire of coals already burning. Fish lay cooking thereon. Bread sat nigh upon a flat stone. Warm smoke drifted up into the cool morning air. Jesus saith unto them, "Bring of the fish which ye have now caught." Simon Peter went up and helped draw the net to land. It was full of great fishes — an hundred and fifty and three. And for all there were so many, yet was not the net broken.

Jesus saith unto them, "Come and dine." He took the bread and gave it to them. Then he gave them fish from the fire. The disciples sat near the warm coals. They did eat together upon that quiet beach. Morning sunlight touched the water and the sand round about them. Smoke from the little fire curled gently upward. The sound of small waves came and went along the shore. This is now the third time that Jesus shewed himself to his disciples after that he was risen. They gathered close, eating bread and fish beside the sea.
