A quiet night rested over the sea of Tiberias. Small waves lapped against the rocky shore. The air bore the savour of salt and wet stone. Stars dotted the wide sky above the water. Smooth pebbles lined the beach where the waves came in and went back out. A wooden boat sat at the water's edge, rocking gently with each small wave. Ropes lay coiled beside it upon the damp ground. The hull creaked softly against the stones beneath it.

Simon Peter stood near that boat with six other disciples. His sandals pressed into the wet sand. He looked out across the still water. A light breeze moved through his hair and tunic. He said unto them, "I go a fishing." They said unto him, "We also go with thee." Thomas stood beside Nathanael, and they both stepped toward the boat. The sons of Zebedee followed close behind. Two other disciples walked with them. Their feet crunched softly upon the pebbles as they gathered round the hull.

Together they pushed the boat down into the water. Wood scraped against stone as it slid forward. Cool water swirled about their ankles and shins. One by one, they climbed over the side and settled upon the wooden benches. Peter gripped an oar. Another disciple took hold of the second one. Oars splashed softly as they pushed out upon the dark water. The shore grew smaller behind them. Ripples spread in wide circles from each stroke.

Out upon the open water, the air was cooler. The boat rocked gently from side to side. Stars reflected upon the calm surface round about them. Nets unrolled over the side with a gentle swoosh. The woven ropes stretched out and sank slowly beneath the surface. The disciples waited. They held the edges of the net and watched the water. Minutes passed. Then they drew the net back in. Water dropped from the ropes as they hauled them up. The net was empty. Not one fish lay therein.

They moved the boat to a new place. Oars dipped and pulled through the water. Again they lowered the nets. Again they waited in the quiet. Waves tapped lightly against the hull. One disciple adjusted the ropes. Another shifted upon the bench. They drew the nets up once more. Still nothing. The nets came back empty again. Water ran off the ropes and dropped back into the sea.

They tried again. And again. Through the quiet hours of the night, they cast their nets over and over. Each time, they drew them back with nothing therein. Wet rope piled upon the floor of the boat. Their hands were damp and wrinkled from the labour. The boat drifted slowly as they moved from place to place across the water. The stars turned overhead as the hours passed. Still, the nets held no fish.

Morning light began to spread across the sky. A thin line of pink appeared along the far edge of the water. Orange colours stretched slowly upward. The surface of the sea changed from dark unto soft silver. The rocky hills along the shore became visible again. Birds began to call from somewhere nigh the land. The air grew just a little warmer.

A figure stood upon the sandy shore. He was as it were two hundred cubits away. From the boat, he looked small against the wide beach. It was Jesus, but the disciples knew not that it was Jesus from so far away. The early light made it hard to see clearly. He stood near the water's edge where the wet sand met the dry.

His voice carried across the calm surface. He called out, "Children, have ye any meat?" The disciples looked toward the shore. They answered him, "No." Their voices travelled over the still water. The figure on the beach did not move. Then he spake again. His words came clearly across the distance. He said, "Cast the net on the right side of the ship, and ye shall find."

So they gathered the net from the floor of the boat. Wet rope slid through their hands. They lifted it together and cast it over the right side. It splashed into the water and sank down. The ropes spread wide beneath the surface. For a moment, all was still. Then the net grew heavy. The ropes pulled tight. Fish filled the net from every direction. The disciples gripped the edges and drew. The net stretched and bulged with fish. More and more pressed within. They drew and drew, but they were not able to haul it into the boat for the multitude of fishes. The weight was too great. The boat tilted slightly toward the right side. Water splashed against the hull as they steadied themselves.

Therefore that disciple whom Jesus loved looked toward the shore. He shielded his eyes with one hand. The morning light was growing brighter. He beheld the figure standing upon the sand. Then he turned unto Peter. He said, "It is the Lord." His voice was clear and sure.

When Peter heard those words, he girt his fisher's coat unto him. He drew it tight and bound it at his waist. Then he climbed upon the side of the boat. His feet gripped the wet wood. He cast himself into the sea. A splash rose up about him. The water came to his breast at first. He waded forward, pushing through the water with strong steps. Where it grew deeper, he swam. His arms moved steadily. Where it grew shallow again, he stood and walked. Water streamed off his garments and dropped from his sleeves. He moved toward the shore with quick, steady steps.

The other disciples remained in the little ship. They gripped the oars and began rowing toward land. Behind them, the heavy net dragged through the water. The ropes stretched tight from the back of the boat. Fish moved and splashed within the net. The boat moved slowly because of the weight. Oar strokes sent small ripples out on each side. The shore came closer with every pull. They were not far from land, but as it were two hundred cubits. The sandy bottom grew visible beneath the clear water as they drew nigh.

Peter reached the beach first. Water ran from his garments onto the sand. His wet footprints marked the shore behind him. He stood upon the firm ground and looked ahead.

When the others were come out of the boat, they saw a small fire of coals already burning upon the beach. Orange coals glowed beneath a thin layer of white ash. Fish lay cooking thereon. Their skin had turned golden from the heat. Bread sat nigh upon a flat stone. It was round and warm. Smoke drifted up into the cool morning air. The savour of cooking fish and fresh bread spread across the beach.

Jesus stood near the fire. He saith unto them, "Bring of the fish which ye have now caught." Simon Peter turned and walked back toward the water. He went up unto the boat and gripped the net with both hands. Other disciples helped him. Together they drew the heavy net up over the side and onto the shore. Sand stuck to the wet ropes. Fish spilled across the beach as the net opened. They were all great fishes. An hundred and fifty and three. The disciples could see them clearly in the growing light. And for all there were so many, yet was not the net broken. Every knot held firm. Every rope stayed whole.

Jesus saith unto them, "Come and dine." The disciples came nigh unto the fire. Their sandals pressed into the warm sand near the coals. Jesus reached down and took up the bread. He held it in both hands and gave it unto them. Then he gave them fish from the fire. The cooked fish was warm and flaky. The bread was soft.

The disciples sat near the warm coals. Some sat upon smooth stones. Others rested upon the dry sand. They did eat together upon that quiet beach. None of them durst ask him who he was. They could see him there beside the fire, handing them bread and fish.

Morning sunlight touched the water and the sand round about them. The sea had turned a gentle blue. Small waves came and went along the shore with a soft, steady sound. Smoke from the little fire curled gently upward into the bright air. Birds called again from the rocky hills above the beach.

This is now the third time that Jesus shewed himself to his disciples after that he was risen. They gathered close, eating bread and fish beside the sea. The coals glowed warm. The bread passed from hand to hand. And the morning stretched out wide and bright over the water.
