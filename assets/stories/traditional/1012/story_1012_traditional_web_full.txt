The lake called Tiberias lay still and wide. Smooth water stretched out under a quiet night sky. Stars dotted the air above, and a cool breeze moved across the surface. Small waves lapped against the rocky shore. The sound was soft and steady, like a gentle pat on the ground. Fishing boats rested near the water's edge, their ropes tied to wooden posts. The smell of old nets and damp wood hung in the air.

Simon Peter stood near one of the boats. Thomas was there too, and so was Nathanael, who came from a town called Cana in Galilee. Two sons of Zebedee stood close by. Two other disciples were also with them. Seven men gathered together by the lake that evening.

Simon Peter spoke up. "I am going fishing," he said. His voice carried across the quiet shore. The others looked at him and nodded. "We will go with you," they said. So all seven of them climbed into the boat. Peter pushed it away from the shore. The wood scraped against the pebbles and then slid into the water. Someone grabbed the oars. The boat moved out slowly, rocking from side to side.

They cast their nets into the lake. The heavy ropes splashed as they spread wide and sank below the surface. The men waited. They pulled the nets back up. Nothing came in. They tried again in a different spot. The nets came up empty once more. Hour after hour, they cast and pulled, cast and pulled. The cool air pressed against their faces. Their hands grew tired from gripping the wet ropes. Water dripped from the nets each time they hauled them in. All through the night, they caught nothing at all.

Then morning began to come. A thin line of light appeared along the hills to the east. The sky changed from deep blue to a soft, pale color. Mist sat low on the water. The men could see the shore again, though everything still looked hazy and far away.

A figure stood on the beach. The disciples looked toward the shore, but they did not recognize who it was. The person called out to them across the water. His voice was clear in the morning air.

"Children, do you have any fish?" he asked.

The men in the boat called back. "No," they answered.

Then the man on the shore spoke again. "Cast the net on the right side of the boat, and you will find some," he said.

So the disciples picked up the net. They swung it over the right side of the boat. It splashed into the water and sank down. Almost right away, the net grew heavy. The ropes pulled tight. The men grabbed hold and tried to haul it in, but they could not lift it. There were so many fish inside that the net bulged and stretched. The boat tilted slightly from the weight.

The disciple whom Jesus loved looked toward the shore. He looked carefully at the figure standing there. Then he turned to Simon Peter. "It is the Lord!" he said.

When Simon Peter heard those words, he grabbed his outer garment. He had taken it off while working. He wrapped it around himself and jumped into the water. Splashing and wading, he moved toward the shore as fast as he could. Water rose up around his legs and chest.

The other disciples stayed in the boat. They were not far from land — only about two hundred cubits away. They dragged the heavy net full of fish behind them as the boat moved slowly toward the beach. The net bumped and scraped along in the water. Fish flipped and turned inside it.

When they all reached the shore, they stepped out onto the sand and pebbles. Right away, they saw something wonderful. A small charcoal fire was already burning on the ground. Orange and red coals glowed beneath a layer of ash. Fish lay on the fire, cooking slowly. Bread sat nearby. The warm smell of roasting fish and fresh bread filled the morning air.

Jesus spoke to them. "Bring some of the fish that you have just caught," he said.

Simon Peter walked over to the net. He grabbed the edge and pulled it up onto the land. It was full of large fish — one hundred and fifty-three of them. Even with so many fish packed inside, the net had not torn. Peter counted them as he pulled them out, one by one. Wet fish flopped on the sand. Water dripped from the net onto the ground.

Then Jesus said to them, "Come and have breakfast."

The disciples came closer to the fire. They sat down on the sand near the warm coals. None of them asked him, "Who are you?" They all saw that it was the Lord. The morning light rested on the water behind them. Smoke rose gently from the little fire.

Jesus walked over and picked up the bread. He gave it to them, passing pieces from hand to hand. Then he took the fish from the fire and gave that to them too. Each man received bread and fish. They ate together on the shore of the lake. The food was warm. The bread was soft. The coals crackled quietly beneath the remaining fish.

This was now the third time that Jesus appeared to his disciples after he was raised from among those who had passed on.

The lake lay calm and bright in the new morning. Small waves still lapped at the shore. The seven men sat together near the fire, eating the breakfast that Jesus had made for them. Bread crumbs dotted the sand. Fish bones rested near the coals. The smell of wood smoke hung gently in the still air. A few birds called out across the water. The sun climbed higher, warming their tired shoulders. Jesus was there with them, close and present, sharing a simple meal on the quiet beach.
