The lamp of God had not yet gone out in the temple. The boy was lying down in the place where he slept, near the ark where the lamp burned, and Eli had been put to bed in his own room and was sleeping in the dimness of the night.

A voice came across the chamber. "Samuel."

The boy got up at once and went into Eli's room and stood at the side of the bed. "Here I am," he said. "You called me."

The old man stirred. "I didn't call," he said. "Go back and lie down."

Samuel went back. The lamp was still burning, low and steady, and he lay down again in the soft of the night.

The voice came a second time. "Samuel."

He got up and went and stood again at Eli's side. "Here I am. You called me."

"I didn't call you, my son," Eli said. "Go back and lie down."

It happened a third time, and after the third time something opened in Eli that had been closed for a long while. He sat up in his bed. He looked at the boy in the doorway. He told him what to do.

"Go back," Eli said, "and lie down. If you hear the voice again, you are to say: 'Speak, LORD, for your servant is listening.'"

The boy went back to his place. The lamp had not yet gone out. The country outside the temple was very still — only the night birds calling somewhere, far off, and the wind moving across the stone of the courts. He lay down where he had been lying.

Yahweh came and stood and called as before. "Samuel. Samuel."

The boy answered as Eli had told him to answer. "Speak. Your servant is listening."

The voice spoke. The lamp burned on through the night, and the words that came were the first words the boy would carry, and there would be many more after them, but only this first one in the quiet at the beginning.
