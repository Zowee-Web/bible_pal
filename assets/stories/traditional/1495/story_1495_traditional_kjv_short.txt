The lamp of God had not yet gone out in the temple. The boy was lying down in the place where he slept, near the ark where the lamp was burning, and Eli was laid down in his own chamber and was sleeping in the dimness of the night.

A voice came across the room. "Samuel."

The boy arose at once and went unto Eli and stood at his bedside. "Here am I," he said, "for thou calledst me."

The old man stirred. "I called not, my son," he said. "Go again, and lie down."

Samuel went again. The lamp was yet burning, low and steady, and he laid him down in the soft of the night.

The voice came the second time. "Samuel."

He arose and went and stood again at Eli's side. "Here am I, for thou didst call me."

"I called thee not, my son," Eli said. "Lie down again."

It came the third time, and after the third time something opened in Eli that had long been shut. He sat up in his bed. He looked upon the boy in the doorway. He told him what he should do.

"Go," Eli said, "lie down: and it shall be, if he call thee, that thou shalt say, 'Speak, LORD; for thy servant heareth.'"

The boy went back unto his place. The lamp was not yet gone out. The country without the temple was very still — only the night birds calling somewhere afar off, and the wind moving across the stone of the courts. He laid him down where he had lain.

The LORD came and stood and called as at other times. "Samuel. Samuel."

The boy answered as Eli had taught him to answer. "Speak; for thy servant heareth."

The voice spake. The lamp burned on through the night, and the words that came were the first words the boy should carry, and there would be many after them, but only this first one in the stillness at the beginning.
