Micah’s prophecy passed through promise, travail, deliverance, and cleansing. In the last days, the mountain of the house of the LORD would be established above the hills, and people would flow unto it. Many nations would call one another to ascend unto the house of the God of Jacob. They desired that he teach them his ways so that they might walk in his paths, for the law would go forth from Zion and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem.

The LORD would judge among people and rebuke strong nations afar off. Beneath his rule, swords were beaten into plowshares and spears into pruninghooks. Nation no longer lifted sword against nation, and men learned war no more. Every man sat under his vine and fig tree, with none to make him afraid. The certainty of that peace rested upon the mouth of the LORD of hosts. Other peoples might walk in the name of their gods, but the LORD’s people declared that they would walk in his name for ever.

The coming kingdom was also a gathering of those who had been cast away. The LORD would assemble her that halted, collect her that had been driven out, and bring together her whom he had afflicted. From her that halted he would make a remnant, and from her that was cast far off a strong nation. He himself would reign over them in mount Zion. The first dominion would come again unto the daughter of Jerusalem.

That promise did not remove the travail appointed before it. “Now why dost thou cry out aloud?” the prophecy asked as Zion’s anguish came like a woman in travail. She would go forth out of the city, dwell in the field, and come even unto Babylon. Yet Babylon was named not only as a place of distress, but as the place from which the LORD would redeem her. Many nations likewise assembled against Zion and desired to see her defiled. They knew neither the thoughts nor the counsel of the LORD. He had gathered them as sheaves unto the threshingfloor. Zion was called to arise and thresh, and the gain of the nations would be consecrated unto the Lord of the whole earth.

The prophecy then held siege and promise together. Troops gathered while the judge of Israel was smitten upon the cheek. But out of Bethlehem Ephratah, though little among the thousands of Judah, would come unto the LORD one who should be ruler in Israel, whose goings forth had been from old, from everlasting. After the appointed travail and birth, the remnant of his brethren would return unto the children of Israel.

This ruler would not feed his flock through the borrowed glory of men. He would stand in the strength of the LORD and in the majesty of the name of the LORD his God. The people would abide, because his greatness would reach unto the ends of the earth. He would be their peace. When the Assyrian came into the land, shepherds and principal men would be raised against him, and the ruler would deliver his people when the invader entered their borders.

Micah gave two very different pictures of the remnant of Jacob among many people. The remnant would be as dew from the LORD and showers upon grass, blessings which tarried for no man. It would also be as a lion among the beasts of the forest and among flocks, mighty against opposition. The prophecy called for their hand to be lifted above their adversaries and for their enemies to be cut off.

Yet the LORD did not remove only the enemies outside Israel. His cleansing word came against the false confidence and corrupt practices within the land. He would cut off horses and destroy chariots. Cities and strongholds would be thrown down. Witchcraft and soothsayers would cease. Graven images, standing images, and groves would be rooted out, so that the people would no longer worship the work of their hands. The LORD would execute vengeance in anger and fury upon the heathen, such as they had not heard.

Peace would rest not upon weapons, cities, strongholds, or idols, but upon the man who stood in the strength of the LORD.
