Micah’s prophecy moved through promise, anguish, deliverance, and cleansing. In the latter days, the mountain of Yahweh’s house would stand above the hills, and peoples would stream toward it. Many nations would urge one another to go up to the house of the God of Jacob. They wanted him to teach them his ways so they could walk in his paths, for instruction would go out from Zion and Yahweh’s word from Jerusalem.

Yahweh would judge between distant and powerful nations. Under his rule, swords became plowshares and spears became pruning hooks. Nation no longer raised sword against nation, and warfare ceased to be their study. Each person sat beneath vine and fig tree without anyone making them afraid. The certainty of that peace rested on the mouth of Yahweh of Armies. Other peoples might walk in the names of their gods, but Yahweh’s people declared that they would walk in his name forever.

The coming kingdom was also a gathering of those who had been cast aside. Yahweh would assemble the lame, collect the driven away, and bring together those he had afflicted. From the lame he would make a remnant and from the scattered a strong nation. He himself would reign over them on Mount Zion. The former dominion would come again to the daughter of Jerusalem.

That promise did not erase the anguish immediately before her. Zion cried like a woman in labor. She would leave the city, dwell in the field, and come as far as Babylon. Yet Babylon was named not only as a destination of distress but as the place from which Yahweh would rescue and redeem her. Likewise, many nations gathered against Zion and wished to see her defiled. They did not know Yahweh’s thoughts or understand his counsel. He had gathered them as sheaves are gathered to a threshing floor. Zion was summoned to rise, and the nations’ gain would be devoted to the Lord of the whole earth.

The vision then held siege and promise together. Troops gathered while the judge of Israel was struck on the cheek. But Bethlehem Ephrathah, small among Judah’s clans, received a word of extraordinary hope. From Bethlehem would come for Yahweh one who would rule in Israel, whose origins reached back to ancient days. After the appointed labor and birth, his brothers would return to Israel.

This ruler would not shepherd through borrowed human glory. He would stand in Yahweh’s strength and in the majesty of Yahweh his God. The people would dwell securely, because his greatness would extend to the ends of the earth. He would be their peace. When the Assyrian entered the land, leaders would be raised against him, and the promised ruler would deliver his people from the invader within their borders.

Micah used two very different images for Jacob’s remnant among many peoples. The remnant would be like dew from Yahweh and showers upon grass, blessings that waited for no human permission. It would also be like a lion among forest animals and among flocks, powerful against opposition. The prophecy asked that their hand be lifted over their adversaries and their enemies cut off.

Yet Yahweh did not merely remove enemies outside Israel. His cleansing word turned against the false securities and corrupt practices within the land. He would cut off horses and destroy chariots. Fortified cities and strongholds would fall. Witchcraft and soothsayers would cease. Carved images, sacred pillars, and Asherah poles would be uprooted, so that the people would no longer bow to the work of their hands. The nations that had refused to listen would face his anger.

Peace would rest not on weapons, fortresses, or idols, but on the ruler who stood in Yahweh’s strength.
