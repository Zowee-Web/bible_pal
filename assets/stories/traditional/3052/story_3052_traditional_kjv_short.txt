Micah looked toward the last days and saw the mountain of the house of the LORD established above the hills. Peoples flowed unto the house of the God of Jacob, desiring to learn his ways and walk in his paths. The LORD judged among nations, and weapons were fashioned into tools for husbandry. Men learned war no more. Each one sat beneath his vine and fig tree without fear, for the mouth of the LORD of hosts had spoken it.

The LORD also promised to gather the lame and those driven out. From the afflicted and scattered he would make a strong remnant, and he would reign over them in mount Zion. “Now why dost thou cry out aloud?” the prophecy asked as Zion’s travail began. She would go forth from the city and come even unto Babylon; there the LORD would redeem her. Nations assembled to look upon Zion with pleasure, not knowing that the LORD had gathered them as sheaves unto the floor. Their apparent victory remained beneath his counsel.

Siege was laid against the judge of Israel. Over against that shame stood the promise concerning little Bethlehem Ephratah. Out of that small place would come a ruler in Israel, whose goings forth were from old. He would stand and feed his flock in the strength and majesty of the name of the LORD. His people would abide, his greatness would reach unto the ends of the earth, and he would be their peace.

Micah pictured the remnant among many people in two ways. It would be as dew and showers from the LORD, tarrying for no man. It would also be as a lion among the beasts of the forest, strong before its adversaries. Deliverance from the Assyrian and victory over enemies belonged within the purpose declared by the LORD.

Yet the LORD’s cleansing reached his own people also. He would cut off their horses and chariots, throw down their strongholds, end witchcraft and soothsayers, and remove graven images, standing images, and groves. They would no longer worship the work of their hands or trust their own defences. The promised ruler, the preserved remnant, and the peace of Zion rested upon the reign of the LORD rather than the strength of men.
