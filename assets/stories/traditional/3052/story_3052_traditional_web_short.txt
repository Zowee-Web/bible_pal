Micah looked toward the latter days and saw Yahweh’s mountain raised above the hills. Peoples streamed toward the house of the God of Jacob, asking to learn his ways and walk in his paths. Yahweh judged between nations, and weapons were remade as tools for cultivation. War was no longer their study. Each person rested beneath vine and fig tree without fear, because Yahweh of Armies had spoken.

Yahweh also promised to gather the lame and the driven away. He would form a strong remnant from those who had been afflicted and scattered, and he would reign over them from Mount Zion. Yet Zion’s path passed through anguish. She would leave the city and come to Babylon; there Yahweh would rescue her. Nations gathered to gloat over Zion, unaware that Yahweh had gathered them like sheaves to a threshing floor. What looked like their triumph remained under his counsel.

Siege came against the judge of Israel. Against that humiliation stood a promise addressed to little Bethlehem Ephrathah. From that small clan would come a ruler for Israel, one whose origins were from ancient days. He would stand and shepherd in Yahweh’s strength and in the majesty of Yahweh’s name. His people would dwell securely, his greatness would reach the ends of the earth, and he would be their peace.

Micah described the remnant among many peoples with two images. It would be like dew and showers from Yahweh, depending on no human command. It would also be like a lion among forest animals, strong before its adversaries. Rescue from the invader and victory over enemies belonged within Yahweh’s declared purpose.

But Yahweh’s cleansing also reached his own people. He would remove their horses, chariots, fortified cities, witchcraft, soothsayers, carved images, sacred pillars, and Asherah poles. His people would no longer worship the work of their hands or rely on their own defenses. The promised ruler, the gathered remnant, and the peace of Zion all rested on Yahweh’s reign rather than human power.
