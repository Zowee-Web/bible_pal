In the days of his sorrow, the prophet sat alone in the broken city. The walls of Jerusalem lay in ruins around him, and the people had been carried away. He took up his pen and wrote down the words of his lament.

He said, "Remember my affliction and my misery, the wormwood and the gall. My soul still remembers them, and is bowed down within me."

He sat in silence for a time, and the dust lay heavy on the stones. The sun moved slowly across the sky, and the city was quiet around him.

Then he lifted his voice again and said, "This I recall to my mind. Therefore I have hope."

He said, "It is because of the Lord's loving kindnesses that we are not consumed, because his mercies do not fail. They are new every morning. Great is your faithfulness."

He said, "The Lord is my portion, says my soul. Therefore I will hope in him."

He said, "The Lord is good to those who wait for him, to the soul who seeks him. It is good that a man should hope, and quietly wait for the salvation of the Lord."

The wind moved across the broken walls, and the morning was still bright above the city. He folded his hands upon his knees, and he sat in the quiet of the morning until the day grew warm.

He took up his pen again and wrote the words down, that they might be read in the days that came after, and that the people who walked in shadow might find comfort in them.

The words of his lament went out into the land. The walls were still broken, but the man who wrote them sat in hope, and the morning came again as it had always come.
