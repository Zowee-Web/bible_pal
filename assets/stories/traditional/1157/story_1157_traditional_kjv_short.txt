In the days of his sorrow, the prophet sat alone in the broken city. The walls of Jerusalem lay in ruins round about him, and the people had been carried away. He took up his pen and wrote down the words of his lament.

He said, "Remembering mine affliction and my misery, the wormwood and the gall. My soul hath them still in remembrance, and is humbled in me."

He sat in silence for a season, and the dust lay heavy upon the stones. The sun passed slowly across the sky, and the city was quiet round about him.

Then he lifted up his voice again and said, "This I recall to my mind, therefore have I hope."

He said, "It is of the Lord's mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness."

He said, "The Lord is my portion, saith my soul; therefore will I hope in him."

He said, "The Lord is good unto them that wait for him, to the soul that seeketh him. It is good that a man should both hope and quietly wait for the salvation of the Lord."

The wind moved across the broken walls, and the morning was yet bright above the city. He folded his hands upon his knees, and he sat in the quiet of the morning until the day waxed warm.

He took up his pen again and wrote the words down, that they might be read in the days that came after, and that they which walked in shadow might find comfort therein.

The words of his lament went forth into the land. The walls were yet broken, but the man that wrote them sat in hope, and the morning came again, as it had ever come.
