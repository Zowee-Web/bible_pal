At wheat harvest, Samson brought a young goat to visit his wife. He asked to enter her room, but her father refused. The father said he had thought Samson hated her, so he had given her to Samson's companion. He offered her younger sister instead, calling her more beautiful.

Samson declared that he would be blameless when he harmed the Philistines. He caught three hundred foxes, paired them tail to tail, and placed a torch between each pair. With the torches burning, he released them into the Philistines' grain. The shocks, standing grain, and olive groves burned.

When the Philistines asked who had done it, they were told that Samson had acted because his wife had been given to his companion. They burned the woman and her father. Samson announced that he would take revenge and then stop. He struck them hip and thigh in a great slaughter, then went to live in the cave in Etam's rock.

The Philistines encamped in Judah and spread through Lehi. Questioned by Judah's men, they said they had come to bind Samson and repay what he had done. Three thousand men of Judah went to Samson's cave. They reminded him that the Philistines ruled them and asked what he had done to them.

Samson answered that he had treated the Philistines as they had treated him. When Judah's men said they would hand him over, he required their oath that they would not attack him themselves. They promised to bind him without killing him, fastened two new ropes around him, and brought him up from the rock.

The Philistines shouted as Samson reached Lehi. Yahweh's Spirit came mightily upon him. His ropes became like flax burned in fire, and the bonds fell from his hands. He seized a fresh donkey's jawbone and struck a thousand men with it.

Samson said, “With the jawbone of a donkey, heaps on heaps; with the jawbone of a donkey I have struck a thousand men.”

He threw the jawbone away when he finished speaking. The place was called Ramath Lehi.

Very thirsty, Samson called to Yahweh. He acknowledged the great deliverance given through his hand, then asked whether he must now die of thirst and fall into uncircumcised hands. God split the hollow place in Lehi, and water flowed out. Samson drank and revived. The spring was named En Hakkore and remained in Lehi.

Samson judged Israel for twenty years during the days of the Philistines.
