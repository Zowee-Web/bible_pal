In the wheat harvest, Samson went to his wife with a kid. He would enter her chamber, but her father would not admit him. The father declared that he had thought Samson utterly hated her and had therefore given her to his companion. He urged Samson to take her younger sister, whom he called fairer.

Samson said that he would now be more blameless than the Philistines, though he did them a displeasure. He caught three hundred foxes and turned them tail to tail, setting a firebrand between each pair. He kindled the brands and loosed the foxes among the standing corn. Shocks and corn burned, with the vineyards and olives.

The Philistines asked who had done this. They were told that Samson, the son in law of the Timnite, had done it, because his wife had been given to his companion; then they burned her and her father with fire. Samson vowed vengeance before he would cease. He smote them hip and thigh with a great slaughter, and withdrew to dwell in the top of the rock Etam.

The Philistines pitched in Judah and spread themselves in Lehi. They told the men of Judah that they sought to bind Samson and do unto him as he had done unto them. Three thousand men went down to the rock. They asked Samson whether he knew that the Philistines ruled over them, and what he had done unto them.

“As they did unto me, so have I done unto them,” Samson answered.

They had come to deliver him to the Philistines. Samson required an oath that they themselves would not fall upon him. They pledged to bind him, but not kill him. With two new cords they bound him and brought him from the rock.

At Lehi the Philistines shouted against him. The Spirit of the Lord came mightily upon Samson; the cords became as flax burnt with fire, and his bands fell loose. Taking a new jawbone of an ass, he slew a thousand men.

“With the jawbone of an ass, heaps upon heaps,” he said, “with the jaw of an ass have I slain a thousand men.” He cast away the jawbone and called the place Ramath–lehi.

Sore athirst, Samson called upon the Lord. He spoke of the great deliverance given into his hand and asked whether he should die for thirst and fall into the hands of the uncircumcised. God clave a hollow place in the jaw, and water came forth. Samson drank; his spirit returned, and he revived. He named it En–hakkore, which remained in Lehi.

For twenty years Samson judged Israel in the days of the Philistines.
