After forty years in the wilderness, Moses stood upon the plains of Moab, on the east side of Jordan, and he taught the children of Israel the words of the law. The Lord had brought them through the great wilderness — the wandering, the manna, the rock that gave water, the cloud by day and the fire by night. Their sandals had not waxed old, and their feet had not swelled, all those years. The generation which had refused to enter had fallen in the wilderness. Their children stood now, ready to pass over.

For the Lord thy God bringeth thee into a good land, a land of brooks of water, of fountains and depths that spring out of valleys and hills;

A land of wheat, and barley, and vines, and fig trees, and pomegranates; a land of oil olive, and honey;

A land wherein thou shalt eat bread without scarceness, thou shalt not lack any thing in it; a land whose stones are iron, and out of whose hills thou mayest dig brass.

When thou hast eaten and art full, then thou shalt bless the Lord thy God for the good land which he hath given thee.

Moses spake the words unto all Israel, on the plains of Moab, before the crossing. The wilderness lay behind them — the years of manna, the years of wandering, the bones of them which had not entered. The Jordan lay before them. And the good land waited beyond the river, as it had waited since the days of Abraham. And so it was, in the days before the crossing.
