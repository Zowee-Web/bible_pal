After forty years in the wilderness, Moses stood on the plains of Moab, on the eastern side of the Jordan, and he taught the children of Israel the words of the law. Yahweh had brought them through the great wilderness — the wandering, the manna, the rock that gave water, the cloud by day and the fire by night. Their sandals had not worn out, and their feet had not swelled, all those years. The generation that had refused to enter had fallen in the wilderness. Their children stood now, ready to cross.

For Yahweh your God brings you into a good land, a land of brooks of water, of springs, and underground water flowing into valleys and hills;

a land of wheat and barley, and vines and fig trees and pomegranates; a land of olive trees and honey;

a land in which you shall eat bread without scarceness, you shall not lack anything in it; a land whose stones are iron, and out of whose hills you may dig copper.

You shall eat and be full, and you shall bless Yahweh your God for the good land which he has given you.

Moses spoke the words to all Israel, on the plains of Moab, before the crossing. The wilderness lay behind them — the years of manna, the years of wandering, the bones of those who had not entered. The Jordan lay before them. And the good land waited beyond the river, as it had waited since the days of Abraham. And so it was, in the days before the crossing.
