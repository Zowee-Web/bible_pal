Jehoshaphat king of Judah returned to his house in peace at Jerusalem. Jehu the son of Hanani the seer went out to meet him. He asked, “Shouldest thou help the ungodly, and love them that hate the LORD?” Because of this, wrath was upon Jehoshaphat from before the LORD. Nevertheless, Jehu named the good things found in him: the king had taken away the groves out of the land and had prepared his heart to seek God.

Jehoshaphat dwelt at Jerusalem, yet he went out again through the people. He journeyed from Beersheba unto mount Ephraim and brought them back unto the LORD God of their fathers. Throughout the fenced cities of Judah, city by city, he set judges in the land.

The king charged the judges to take heed what they did. They judged not for man, but for the LORD, who was with them in the judgment. Therefore the fear of the LORD was to be upon them. They must act carefully, for the LORD their God hath no iniquity, respect of persons, nor taking of gifts.

Moreover, Jehoshaphat appointed Levites, priests, and chiefs of the fathers of Israel in Jerusalem. They were to judge matters of the LORD and controversies among those who returned to the city. The king commanded them to act in the fear of the LORD, faithfully, and with a perfect heart.

He named the causes that might come unto them from their brethren dwelling in the cities: matters between blood and blood, and questions concerning law, commandment, statutes, and judgments. The judges were to warn the people that they trespass not against the LORD and so bring wrath upon themselves and their brethren. By dealing faithfully, they would not trespass.

The king gave each leader a charge. Amariah the chief priest was over them in all matters of the LORD. Zebadiah the son of Ishmael, ruler of the house of Judah, was over all the king’s matters. The Levites should be officers before them.

Jehoshaphat ended with a command for courage: “Deal courageously.” The LORD would be with the good as the appointed men carried out judgment among the people.
