King Jehoshaphat of Judah returned safely to his house in Jerusalem. Jehu, the son of Hanani the seer, went out to meet him. He asked whether the king should help the wicked and love those who hated Yahweh. Because of this, wrath rested on Jehoshaphat from Yahweh. Yet Jehu also named the good found in him: the king had removed the Asheroth from the land and had set his heart to seek God.

Jehoshaphat remained at Jerusalem, but he went out again among the people. He traveled from Beersheba to the hill country of Ephraim and brought the people back to Yahweh, the God of their fathers. Throughout Judah’s fortified cities, city by city, he appointed judges.

The king charged those judges to consider what they were doing. They did not judge for man, but for Yahweh, who was with them in the judgment. Therefore the fear of Yahweh was to be upon them. They were to take care in their work, because with Yahweh their God there was no injustice, partiality, or acceptance of bribes.

Jehoshaphat also appointed Levites, priests, and heads of Israelite families in Jerusalem. They were to decide matters of Yahweh and controversies among the inhabitants who had returned to the city. The king ordered them to act in the fear of Yahweh, faithfully and with a perfect heart.

He described the disputes that could come before them from their brothers living in the cities: cases between blood and blood, and questions concerning law, commandment, statutes, and ordinances. The judges were to warn the people so that they would not become guilty toward Yahweh and bring wrath on themselves and their brothers. Faithful action in these cases would keep them from guilt.

The king set clear responsibilities. Amariah the chief priest was over them in all matters of Yahweh. Zebadiah, the son of Ishmael and ruler of the house of Judah, was over all the king’s matters. The Levites would serve as officers before them.

Jehoshaphat closed his charge with a command for courage. The appointed leaders were to deal courageously in their work, and Yahweh would be with those who were good.
