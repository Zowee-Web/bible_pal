Paul was writing from prison in Rome. He was writing to the church at Ephesus — a church of Gentiles and Jews together, gathered in a city full of temples and traders and sailors and craftsmen. And in the practical part of his letter, after the doctrine of the great mystery — the bringing together of Jew and Gentile into one body — he set down the shape of the new community's life. The walk that was worthy of the calling. The way of love. The new manner of speaking, and singing, and giving thanks.

Don't be drunken with wine, in which is dissipation, but be filled with the Spirit,

speaking to one another in psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs; singing, and making melody in your heart to the Lord;

giving thanks always concerning all things in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, to God, even the Father;

subjecting yourselves to one another in the fear of Christ.

And the letter was carried out to Ephesus, and read in the gatherings of the church. The believers heard it. The psalms were sung. The thanks were given. The days went on as the days of the gathering had been going on, and the new community lived into the new shape that the apostle had set down. And so it was in Ephesus in those days, in the early years of the church.
