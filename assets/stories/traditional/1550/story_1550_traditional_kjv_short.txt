Three crosses stood upon the hill that is called the Skull. Upon the center beam hung Jesus of Nazareth. And on either side, a malefactor was fastened with nails driven through flesh and wood. The sky of the afternoon bore down upon them, heavy and without light.

The man upon the left cross writhed against his beam. His breath came in shallow draughts, and he turned his face toward Jesus and spake through gritted teeth. "Art thou not the Christ? Deliver thyself, and deliver us also."

His voice carried over the stony ground where soldiers sat and divided the garments among themselves. A few who looked on stood at a distance, watching. And the wind stirred the dust between the stones.

Now the man upon the right cross heard those words. He shifted his weight against the wood, and a sharp agony passed through his arms. He drew breath and answered.

"Hast thou no fear of God? Thou art condemned even as we are." His voice was broken, yet it did not waver. "We suffer justly, for we receive the due reward of that which we have done."

He paused. His chest rose and fell with great labour. Blood ran in thin lines down his forearms where the iron held fast against the bone.

"But this man hath done no evil thing."

The first malefactor answered nothing more. The soldiers below did not lift their eyes. The crowd stood still and silent.

Then the man upon the right turned his head toward Jesus. And the turning cost him dearly. His neck strained against the angle of the crossbeam, and the sinews stood forth beneath his skin. He looked upon the face beside him — bruised and swollen, and crowned about with thorns.

"Lord," he said. "Remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom."

And Jesus opened his eyes. He turned toward the man. His lips were cracked and dry. Blood had dried in dark lines upon his brow where the thorns had been pressed in.

He spake. And his voice was clear, so that the man heard every word above the wind.

"Verily I say unto thee, this day shalt thou be with me in paradise."

The man upon the right cross looked upon Jesus. His hands lay open against the wood, the palms turned toward the heavens. His fingers did not move, for the nails held them fast. Below, the soldiers finished their casting of lots over the garments and sat back against the stones. The wind bore dust across the hill. And the three crosses stood against the darkening sky, and the words hung in the air between two men who were dying.
