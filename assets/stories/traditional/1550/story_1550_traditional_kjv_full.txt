The hill was naked rock and parched earth. Men called it the place of the Skull. Three upright timbers had been set into holes hewn in the stone, and three crossbeams had been fastened upon them, and three men hung from iron driven through their wrists and through their feet. The cross in the midst stood taller than those on either side by a span. And above the head of the man upon it, a writing had been fixed. It declared: This is the King of the Jews.

Jesus of Nazareth hung there. His body was rent from the scourging. Blood had dried in dark lines upon his back and across his shoulders. The crown of thorns was pressed into his scalp, and wheresoever a point had pierced the skin, a thin trail of red ran down his forehead and into his brows.

On his left hand and on his right, a condemned man hung from like timbers. Their transgressions had brought them unto this death. Their arms were stretched wide, their chests heaving with the labour of drawing breath. For every breath required that they press upward upon the nails through their feet, and then sink down again. The motion was slow and unceasing, as the rising and falling of a thing caught upon the water.

The soldiers had accomplished their work. They sat beneath the crosses upon the stony ground, casting lots for the garments they had stripped from the condemned. There was the click of small stones in a vessel of clay. The murmur of reckoning. One among them spread a robe across his knee and examined the weave thereof.

A multitude stood at a distance. Some watched and held their peace. Others had already turned away.

The man upon the left cross spake first. His voice was strained, drawn thin by the weight upon his lungs. He turned his head toward Jesus and said, "Art thou not the Christ? Deliver thyself, and deliver us also."

The words passed across the space between the beams. Jesus made no answer. The wind moved over the hilltop and stirred the dust at the foot of the crosses.

The man upon the right cross heard that which was spoken. He shifted against the wood. The movement sent a tremor through his arms that could be seen, and his face drew tight. He took breath, pressing upward upon the iron through his feet, and spake.

"Hast thou no fear of God?" His voice was rough, yet it carried. "Thou art under the selfsame condemnation."

The first criminal turned his head but said nothing.

The man upon the right continued. Each utterance came between laboured breaths. "We suffer justly. We receive what our deeds have earned." He paused. His chest rose and fell. Sweat ran from his hairline down the side of his face and fell from his jaw upon the crossbeam.

"But this man," he said, and he turned his eyes toward the center cross, "hath done no wrong."

Below, one of the soldiers lifted his gaze briefly, then looked away. The lots continued. A gust of wind took up grit from the ground and cast it against the base of the wooden beams. The sky had grown dark — not the darkness of evening, but something heavier, as though a thick cloud had settled upon the whole of the land. The light that remained was flat and grey.

The man upon the right cross was silent for a time. His fingers were curled against the wood behind his palms, and the nails held his hands open and still. Blood had gathered where the iron met the flesh, and it had dried in dark rings about each wound.

He turned his head toward Jesus. The effort showed in his neck — the sinews drawn taut beneath the skin, his jaw set against the pain of the turning. He looked upon the face of the man upon the center cross. The bruises from the beatings had swollen his features. His eyes were near closed. His lips were cracked and without colour.

The criminal spake.

"Lord," he said. "Remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom."

The words were plain. They crossed the short distance between the two crosses and reached the man for whom they were spoken.

Jesus opened his eyes. He turned his head toward the man upon his right. The thorns shifted against his scalp as he moved. A fresh line of blood traced down his temple.

He spake. His voice was clear — not loud, but it carried with such distinctness that the man beside him could hear every word above the low wind.

"Verily I say unto thee," Jesus said, "this day thou shalt be with me in paradise."

The man upon the right cross looked upon Jesus. His mouth was open slightly, drawing air. His hands remained where the nails held them, palms upward against the rough grain of the crossbeam. The blood upon his wrists had gone dark.

Below, the soldiers sat with the divided garments across their laps. The multitude stood scattered upon the lower slope, their robes pressed flat against their legs by the wind. The sky remained dark over the hill. The three crosses stood in a line against that darkness, and the only sound was the wind moving across the bare stone of the Skull.
