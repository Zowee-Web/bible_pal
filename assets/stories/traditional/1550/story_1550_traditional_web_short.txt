Three crosses stood on the hill called the Skull. The center one held Jesus of Nazareth. On either side, a criminal hung from nails driven through flesh and wood. The afternoon sky pressed down, heavy and dark.

The man on the left twisted against his beam. His breath came in shallow pulls. He turned his face toward Jesus and spoke through clenched teeth. "Are you not the Christ? Save yourself and us."

His voice carried across the stony ground where soldiers sat dividing garments. A few onlookers stood at a distance, watching. The wind moved dust between the rocks.

The man on the right cross heard those words. He shifted his weight against the wood, and pain shot through his arms. He drew a breath and answered.

"Do you not fear God? You are under the same condemnation." His voice was raw but steady. "We are here justly. We receive what our deeds deserve."

He paused. His chest rose and fell with effort. Blood ran in thin lines down his forearms where the iron held him.

"But this man has done nothing wrong."

The first criminal said nothing more. The soldiers below did not look up. The crowd stood still.

Then the man on the right turned his head toward Jesus. The movement cost him. His neck strained against the angle of the crossbeam, and the tendons stood out beneath the skin. He looked at the face beside him — bruised, swollen, crowned with thorns.

"Lord," he said. "Remember me when you come into your kingdom."

Jesus opened his eyes. He turned toward the man. His lips were cracked and dry. Blood had dried in streaks across his forehead where the thorns had pressed in.

He spoke. His voice was clear enough that the man could hear every word above the wind.

"Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in paradise."

The man on the right cross looked at Jesus. His hands were open against the wood, palms facing the sky. His fingers did not move. The nails held them in place. Below, the soldiers finished their lots over the garments and sat back against the rocks. The wind carried dust across the hill. The three crosses stood against the darkening sky, and the words hung in the air between two dying men.
