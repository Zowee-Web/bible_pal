In the thirtieth year, in the fourth month, on the fifth day of the month, Ezekiel sat among the captives by the river Chebar.

The river ran wide and slow through the land of the Chaldeans. Reeds bent at its edges. The water carried the dull color of the sky. The captives had made their settlements along that bank, far from the temple, far from the city of their fathers. Ezekiel was a priest, the son of Buzi, and he had no altar to serve. He had only the river and the foreign land and the long days of exile.

It was the fifth year of King Jehoiachin's captivity. The prophet had not been in the temple courts for many seasons. The prayers of the priest had been carried in silence over a foreign land. The Lord had seemed, to those who could no longer see the holy city, very far away.

Then the heavens were opened.

Ezekiel saw visions of God. The word of the Lord came expressly to him on that bank, and the hand of the Lord was upon him.

He looked, and a stormy wind came out of the north.

It came as a great cloud, with flashing lightning, and a brightness around it. Out of the middle of it shone something like glowing metal, out of the middle of the fire. The cloud advanced over the river. The light inside it pulsed. The prophet's eyes were drawn upward into what could not be domesticated, could not be put away with the ordinary words a man uses for the things of the field.

Out of the center of the fire came the likeness of four living creatures.

They had the likeness of a man. They had four faces. They had four wings. Their feet were straight feet. The sole of their feet was like the sole of a calf's foot, polished, and they sparkled like burnished bronze. Under their wings, on their four sides, they had the hands of a man. Their wings were joined to one another. They did not turn when they went. Each one went straight forward.

Their faces.

On the front, the face of a man. On the right side, the face of a lion. On the left side, the face of an ox. Behind, the face of an eagle. Four creatures, and each one bearing these four faces, turned every way at once. They saw before them and behind them and on either side. There was no part of the world hidden from them.

Their wings were spread out above. Two wings of each one touched another, and two covered their bodies. Each one went straight forward. Where the spirit was to go, they went. They did not turn when they went.

The appearance of the living creatures was like burning coals of fire, like the appearance of torches. The fire went up and down among them. The fire was bright, and lightning went out of the fire. The living creatures ran and returned as the appearance of a flash of lightning, swift past the prophet's seeing, gone and then before him again.

Then Ezekiel saw the wheels.

There was one wheel on the earth beside each of the living creatures, for each of the four faces of it. The appearance of the wheels and their work was like a beryl, pale and shining. The four of them had one likeness. Their appearance and their work was as it were a wheel within a wheel.

When they went, they went in their four directions. They did not turn when they went.

As for their rims, they were high and dreadful. The four of them had their rims full of eyes all around. The rims looked. The rims watched. They held in themselves a multitude of seeing.

When the living creatures went, the wheels went beside them. When the living creatures were lifted up from the earth, the wheels were lifted up beside them. Wherever the spirit was to go, they went. The spirit was to go there. The wheels were lifted up beside them, for the spirit of the living creature was in the wheels.

When the creatures went, the wheels went. When the creatures stood, the wheels stood. When the creatures were lifted up from the earth, the wheels were lifted up beside them. They moved as one. The spirit moved them, and they followed the spirit.

Above the head of the living creature was the likeness of an expanse, like an awesome crystal to look at, stretched out over their heads above. The crystal held the light of the fire below it and the light of what was above. The prophet could see through it and not through it. It was clear as ice and bright as morning, and it covered the living creatures the way a holy canopy covers a holy thing.

Under the expanse their wings were straight, one toward the other. Each one had two which covered on this side, and each one had two which covered their bodies on that side.

When the creatures went, Ezekiel heard the noise of their wings. The noise was like the noise of great waters, like the voice of the Almighty, a noise of tumult like the noise of an army. The sound filled the air over the river. It rolled past the reeds and into the foreign country. The prophet stood in it as a man stands in the spray of a falling river.

When the creatures stood, they let down their wings, and the sound fell away into a stillness that pressed against the ears.

There was a voice above the expanse that was over their heads. When the creatures stood, they let down their wings.

Above the expanse was the likeness of a throne, as the appearance of a sapphire stone.

The throne stood above all of it. Above the fire. Above the wheels. Above the creatures with their many faces and many eyes. The sapphire color of it was deep and clear, like the sea on a still day, like the heart of a stone that has been polished for a long time.

On the likeness of the throne was a likeness as the appearance of a man.

Ezekiel saw as it were glowing metal, as the appearance of fire within it all around, from the appearance of his waist upward. From the appearance of his waist downward he saw as it were the appearance of fire, and there was brightness around him. The figure on the throne was fire above and fire below, and the brightness was on every side.

As the appearance of the rainbow that is in the cloud in the day of rain, so was the appearance of the brightness all around.

The prophet had seen the rainbow before. He had seen it stand in the sky over the fields after a storm, when the sun broke and the colors bent over the wet earth. He had known then that the world was held by a promise. He saw now that the throne itself was wrapped in such a brightness, and the promise was older than the rainbow, and the colors were the hem of the glory.

This was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the Lord.

When Ezekiel saw it, he fell on his face.

The reeds bent in the wind that had brought the vision. The water moved beside him. The grass of the bank pressed against his cheek. His hands lay open at his sides. The vision continued above him, and he could not lift his eyes to it any longer. His body had become its own confession, his face to the ground, his breath in the dust of the foreign land.

He heard a voice of one that spoke, low and steady above the long silence of his exile, calling him by his name, beginning the long calling.
