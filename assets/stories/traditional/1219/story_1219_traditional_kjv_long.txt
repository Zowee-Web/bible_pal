Now it came to pass in the thirtieth year, in the fourth month, in the fifth day of the month, Ezekiel sat among the captives by the river of Chebar.

The river ran wide and slow through the land of the Chaldeans. Reeds bent at its edges. The water bore the dull colour of the sky. The captives had made their settlements along that bank, far from the temple, far from the city of their fathers. Ezekiel was a priest, the son of Buzi, and he had no altar to serve. He had only the river and the foreign land and the long days of exile.

It was the fifth year of king Jehoiachin's captivity. The prophet had not been in the temple courts for many seasons. The prayers of the priest had been borne in silence over a foreign land. The LORD had seemed, unto them that could no longer behold the holy city, very far off.

Then the heavens were opened.

Ezekiel saw visions of God. The word of the LORD came expressly unto him upon that bank, and the hand of the LORD was there upon him.

And he looked, and behold, a whirlwind came out of the north.

It came as a great cloud, and a fire infolding itself, and a brightness was about it. Out of the midst thereof shone as the colour of amber, out of the midst of the fire. The cloud drew nigh over the river. The light within it pulsed. The prophet's eyes were drawn upward unto that which could not be tamed, that which could not be laid aside with the common words a man useth for the things of the field.

Out of the midst thereof came the likeness of four living creatures.

They had the likeness of a man. Every one had four faces. Every one had four wings. Their feet were straight feet. The sole of their feet was like the sole of a calf's foot, polished, and they sparkled like the colour of burnished brass. Under their wings, on their four sides, they had the hands of a man. Their wings were joined one to another. They turned not when they went. Every one went straight forward.

Their faces.

In the front, the face of a man. On the right side, the face of a lion. On the left side, the face of an ox. Behind, the face of an eagle. Four creatures, and every one bearing these four faces, turned every way at once. They saw before them and behind them and on either side. There was no part of the world hid from them.

Their wings were stretched forth above. Two wings of every one touched another, and two covered their bodies. Every one went straight forward. Whither the spirit was to go, they went. They turned not when they went.

As for the likeness of the living creatures, their appearance was as burning coals of fire, as the appearance of lamps. The fire went up and down among them. The fire was bright, and out of the fire went forth lightning. And the living creatures ran and returned as the appearance of a flash of lightning, swift past the prophet's seeing, gone and then before him again.

Then Ezekiel beheld the wheels.

Behold one wheel upon the earth by each of the living creatures, with his four faces. The appearance of the wheels and their work was like unto the colour of a beryl, pale and shining. The four of them had one likeness. Their appearance and their work was as it were a wheel in the middle of a wheel.

When they went, they went upon their four sides. They turned not when they went.

As for their rings, they were so high that they were dreadful. The four of them had their rings full of eyes round about. The rings looked. The rings watched. They held within themselves a multitude of seeing.

When the living creatures went, the wheels went by them. When the living creatures were lifted up from the earth, the wheels were lifted up beside them. Whithersoever the spirit was to go, they went. Thither was the spirit to go. The wheels were lifted up beside them, for the spirit of the living creature was in the wheels.

When the creatures went, the wheels went. When the creatures stood, the wheels stood. When the creatures were lifted up from the earth, the wheels were lifted up beside them. They moved as one. The spirit moved them, and they followed after the spirit.

And the likeness of the firmament upon the heads of the living creature was as the colour of the terrible crystal, stretched forth over their heads above. The crystal held the light of the fire below it and the light of that which was above. The prophet could see through it and not through it. It was clear as ice and bright as the morning, and it covered the living creatures as a holy canopy covereth a holy thing.

Under the firmament their wings were straight, the one toward the other. Every one had two which covered on this side, and every one had two which covered their bodies on that side.

When the creatures went, Ezekiel heard the noise of their wings. The noise was like the noise of great waters, as the voice of the Almighty, the noise of a tumult, as the noise of an host. The sound filled the air over the river. It rolled past the reeds and into the foreign country. The prophet stood within it as a man standeth within the spray of a falling river.

When the creatures stood, they let down their wings, and the sound fell away into a stillness that pressed upon the ear.

There was a voice from the firmament that was over their heads. When the creatures stood, they let down their wings.

And above the firmament was the likeness of a throne, as the appearance of a sapphire stone.

The throne stood above all of it. Above the fire. Above the wheels. Above the creatures with their many faces and many eyes. The sapphire colour of it was deep and clear, like the sea upon a still day, like the heart of a stone that hath been polished a long time.

And upon the likeness of the throne was the likeness as the appearance of a man.

Ezekiel saw as the colour of amber, as the appearance of fire round about within it, from the appearance of his loins upward. From the appearance of his loins downward he saw as it were the appearance of fire, and there was brightness round about him. The figure upon the throne was fire above and fire below, and the brightness was on every side.

As the appearance of the bow that is in the cloud in the day of rain, so was the appearance of the brightness round about.

The prophet had beheld the bow before. He had beheld it stand in the sky over the fields after a storm, when the sun brake forth and the colours bent over the wet earth. He had known then that the world was held by a promise. He saw now that the throne itself was wrapped within such a brightness, and the promise was older than the bow, and the colours were as the hem of the glory.

This was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD.

And when Ezekiel saw it, he fell upon his face.

The reeds bent in the wind that had borne the vision. The water moved beside him. The grass of the bank pressed against his cheek. His hands lay open at his sides. The vision continued above him, and he could not lift up his eyes unto it any longer. His body had become its own confession, his face unto the ground, his breath in the dust of the foreign land.

He heard a voice of one that spake, low and steady above the long silence of his exile, calling him by his name, beginning the long calling.
