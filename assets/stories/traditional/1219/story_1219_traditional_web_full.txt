In the thirtieth year, in the fourth month, on the fifth day of the month, Ezekiel sat among the captives by the river Chebar.

The river ran wide and slow through the land of the Chaldeans. Reeds grew along its banks. The water carried the dull color of the sky. The captives had made their settlements along that water, far from the temple, far from the city of their fathers. Ezekiel was a priest, the son of Buzi, and he had no altar to serve. He had only the river and the foreign land and the long days of exile.

Then the heavens were opened.

Ezekiel saw visions of God. The word of the Lord came expressly to him on that bank, and the hand of the Lord was upon him.

He looked, and a stormy wind came out of the north.

It came as a great cloud, with flashing lightning, and a brightness around it. Out of the middle of it shone something like glowing metal, out of the middle of the fire. The cloud advanced. The light inside it pulsed. The prophet's eyes were drawn upward into what could not be domesticated.

Out of the center of the fire came the likeness of four living creatures.

They had the likeness of a man. They had four faces. They had four wings. Their feet shone like the sole of a calf's foot, polished, and they sparkled like burnished bronze. Under their wings, on their four sides, they had the hands of a man. Their wings were joined to one another. They did not turn when they went. Each one went straight forward.

Their faces.

On the front, the face of a man. On the right side, the face of a lion. On the left side, the face of an ox. Behind, the face of an eagle. Four creatures, and each one with these four faces, turned every way at once.

The appearance of them was like burning coals of fire, like the appearance of torches. The fire went up and down among them. The fire was bright, and lightning went out of the fire. The living creatures ran and returned as the appearance of a flash of lightning, swift past the prophet's seeing, gone and then before him again.

Then Ezekiel saw the wheels.

There was one wheel on the earth beside each of the living creatures. The appearance of the wheels was like a beryl, pale and shining. The four of them had one likeness. Their appearance and their work was as it were a wheel within a wheel.

As for their rims, they were high and dreadful. The four of them had their rims full of eyes all around.

When the living creatures went, the wheels went beside them. When the creatures were lifted up from the earth, the wheels were lifted up beside them. The spirit of the living creature was in the wheels. Where the spirit was to go, they went. The wheels went with the spirit.

Above the head of the living creatures was the likeness of an expanse, like an awesome crystal to look at, stretched out over their heads. The crystal held the light of the fire below it and the light of what was above. The prophet could see through it and not through it.

When the creatures went, Ezekiel heard the noise of their wings. The noise was like the noise of great waters, like the voice of the Almighty, a noise of tumult like the noise of an army. When they stood, they let down their wings, and the sound fell away into a stillness that pressed against the ears.

Above the expanse was the likeness of a throne, as the appearance of a sapphire stone.

On the throne was a likeness as the appearance of a man.

Ezekiel saw as it were glowing metal, as the appearance of fire within it all around, from the appearance of his waist upward, and from his waist downward as it were the appearance of fire, and there was brightness around him. As the appearance of the rainbow that is in the cloud in the day of rain, so was the appearance of the brightness all around.

This was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the Lord.

When Ezekiel saw it, he fell on his face by the river. The reeds bent in the wind that had brought the vision. The water moved beside him. He heard a voice of one that spoke, low and steady above the long silence of his exile, calling him by his name.
