Now it came to pass in the thirtieth year, in the fourth month, in the fifth day of the month, Ezekiel sat among the captives by the river of Chebar.

The river ran wide and slow through the land of the Chaldeans. Reeds grew along its banks. The water bore the dull colour of the sky. The captives had made their settlements along that water, far from the temple, far from the city of their fathers. Ezekiel was a priest, the son of Buzi, and he had no altar to serve. He had only the river and the foreign land and the long days of exile.

Then the heavens were opened.

Ezekiel saw visions of God. The word of the LORD came expressly unto him upon that bank, and the hand of the LORD was there upon him.

And he looked, and behold, a whirlwind came out of the north.

It came as a great cloud, and a fire infolding itself, and a brightness was about it. Out of the midst thereof shone as the colour of amber, out of the midst of the fire. The cloud drew nigh. The light within it pulsed. The prophet's eyes were drawn upward unto that which could not be tamed.

Out of the midst thereof came the likeness of four living creatures.

They had the likeness of a man. Every one had four faces. Every one had four wings. Their feet were straight feet, and the sole of their feet was like the sole of a calf's foot, polished, and they sparkled like the colour of burnished brass. Under their wings, on their four sides, they had the hands of a man. Their wings were joined one to another. They turned not when they went. Every one went straight forward.

Their faces.

In the front, the face of a man. On the right side, the face of a lion. On the left side, the face of an ox. Behind, the face of an eagle. Four creatures, and every one bearing these four faces, turned every way at once.

As for the likeness of the living creatures, their appearance was as burning coals of fire, as the appearance of lamps. The fire went up and down among them. The fire was bright, and out of the fire went forth lightning. And the living creatures ran and returned as the appearance of a flash of lightning, swift past the prophet's seeing, gone and then before him again.

Then Ezekiel beheld the wheels.

Behold one wheel upon the earth by each of the living creatures. The appearance of the wheels was like unto the colour of a beryl, pale and shining. The four of them had one likeness. Their appearance and their work was as it were a wheel in the middle of a wheel.

As for their rings, they were so high that they were dreadful. The four of them had their rings full of eyes round about.

When the living creatures went, the wheels went by them. When the creatures were lifted up from the earth, the wheels were lifted up beside them. The spirit of the living creature was in the wheels. Whither the spirit was to go, they went. The wheels went with the spirit.

Above the head of the living creatures was the likeness of a firmament, as the colour of the terrible crystal, stretched forth over their heads above. The crystal held the light of the fire below it and the light of that which was above. The prophet could see through it and not through it.

When the creatures went, Ezekiel heard the noise of their wings. The noise was like the noise of great waters, as the voice of the Almighty, the noise of a tumult, as the noise of an host. When they stood, they let down their wings, and the sound fell away into a stillness that pressed upon the ear.

And above the firmament was the likeness of a throne, as the appearance of a sapphire stone.

And upon the likeness of the throne was the likeness as the appearance of a man above upon it.

Ezekiel saw as the colour of amber, as the appearance of fire round about within it, from the appearance of his loins upward, and from the appearance of his loins downward as it were the appearance of fire, and there was brightness round about him. As the appearance of the bow that is in the cloud in the day of rain, so was the appearance of the brightness round about.

This was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD.

And when Ezekiel saw it, he fell upon his face by the river. The reeds bent in the wind that had borne the vision. The water moved beside him. And he heard a voice of one that spake, low and steady above the long silence of his exile, calling him by his name.
