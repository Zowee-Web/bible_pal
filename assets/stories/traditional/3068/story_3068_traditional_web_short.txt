Before Nehemiah returned to Jerusalem, Eliashib the priest had been appointed over the rooms of the house of God. Because he was allied with Tobiah, Eliashib prepared a large room for him in the temple courts. That room had formerly held the meal offerings, frankincense, vessels, and the tithes of grain, new wine, and oil assigned to the Levites, singers, and gatekeepers, together with the wave offerings for the priests.

Nehemiah had not been in Jerusalem when this was done. In the thirty-second year of Artaxerxes king of Babylon, he had gone to the king. After some days, Nehemiah asked permission to leave and came back to Jerusalem. There he learned of the evil Eliashib had done by preparing a room for Tobiah in the courts of God’s house.

The arrangement grieved Nehemiah greatly. He threw all Tobiah’s household goods out of the room. At his command, the rooms were cleansed. Then he brought back the vessels of God’s house with the meal offerings and frankincense.

Nehemiah also discovered that the Levites had not received their portions. The Levites and singers who performed the temple service had gone away, each to his own field. Nehemiah contended with the rulers and asked why the house of God had been forsaken. He gathered the Levites and singers together and set them again in their places.

Then all Judah brought the tithe of grain, new wine, and oil into the treasuries. Nehemiah placed responsible men over the storerooms: Shelemiah the priest, Zadok the scribe, and Pedaiah from the Levites. Hanan, the son of Zaccur and grandson of Mattaniah, served next to them. These men were counted faithful, and their responsibility was to distribute the stored portions to their brothers.

Nehemiah prayed. He asked his God to remember him concerning these deeds and not wipe out the good works he had done for the house of his God and for its observances.
