Ezekiel was a priest in exile by the river Chebar, among the people of Judah who had been carried away to Babylon. The Lord had spoken to him many times, and many times he had spoken the words of the Lord to the people, and the people had not heeded. The day came when the word came again to him; and this time the sign would be in his own house. And Ezekiel set down what was said to him.

Also Yahweh's word came to me, saying,

"Son of man, behold, I will take away from you the desire of your eyes with a stroke: yet you shall neither mourn nor weep, neither shall your tears run down.

Sigh, but not aloud. Make no mourning for the dead. Bind your headdress on you, and put your shoes on your feet. Don't cover your lips, and don't eat mourner's bread."

So I spoke to the people in the morning; and at evening my wife died. So I did in the morning as I was commanded.

And in the morning following Ezekiel went out among the people of the exile as he had been told. The wife whom he had loved had been buried in the manner that the Lord had spoken; the headdress was bound on his head as a man going about his work; and the sign was set among the people, that they might see in it the city they had loved, and how they would see it taken. And so it was in the days of Ezekiel by the river Chebar.
