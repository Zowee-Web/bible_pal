Ezekiel was a priest in exile by the river Chebar, among the people of Judah who had been carried away unto Babylon. The Lord had spoken unto him many times, and many times had he spoken the words of the Lord unto the people, and the people had not hearkened. The day came when the word came again unto him; and this time the sign should be in his own house. And Ezekiel set down what was said unto him.

Also the word of the Lord came unto me, saying,

"Son of man, behold, I take away from thee the desire of thine eyes with a stroke: yet neither shalt thou mourn nor weep, neither shall thy tears run down.

Forbear to cry, make no mourning for the dead, bind the tire of thine head upon thee, and put on thy shoes upon thy feet, and cover not thy lips, and eat not the bread of men."

So I spake unto the people in the morning: and at even my wife died; and I did in the morning as I was commanded.

And in the morning following, Ezekiel went forth among the people of the exile as he had been bidden. The wife whom he had loved was buried in the manner that the Lord had spoken; the tire was bound upon his head as a man going about his work; and the sign was set among the people, that they might behold in it the city they had loved, and how they should see it taken. And so it was in the days of Ezekiel by the river Chebar.
