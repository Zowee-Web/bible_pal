The word came to Isaiah for the people. The people were the people God had created. He named them by both names. He spoke to Jacob and to Israel together. The word was for both at once.

"Fear not," he said. "For I have redeemed thee. I have called thee by thy name. Thou art mine."

The names of the people had been written somewhere very far back. The people had been called by those names before they had stood in the wilderness, before they had crossed the sea, before the patriarchs had walked their long roads. The naming was older than the things they remembered.

The word went on. It spoke of waters. It spoke of fire.

"When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee. The rivers shall not overflow thee. When thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned. Neither shall the flame kindle upon thee."

These were waters they had passed through before. The Red Sea. The Jordan. The water that rose against them at Pharaoh's pursuit and broke open. The waters were old waters by now. The fire was new fire ahead of them, but the word was already telling them about it.

The word spoke of ransom. "I gave Egypt for thy ransom. Ethiopia and Seba for thee. Since thou wast precious in my sight, thou hast been honorable, and I have loved thee."

Then the word swept out across the earth. It went to the four directions.

"Fear not. For I am with thee. I will bring thy seed from the east. I will gather thee from the west. I will say to the north: give up. And to the south: keep not back. Bring my sons from far. Bring my daughters from the ends of the earth. Every one that is called by my name. For I have created him for my glory. I have formed him. I have made him."

Isaiah heard the four directions named. He saw, in his mind, the sons and daughters being brought back. From the east. From the west. From the north. From the south. They were coming home.

The word was finished. Isaiah held it in him a long while before he wrote it down. The country around him was still in the afternoon. The people the word was for were beyond the hills. He wrote it for them.
