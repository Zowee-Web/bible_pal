The word came unto Isaiah for the people. The people were the people God had created. He named them by both names. He spake unto Jacob and unto Israel together. The word was for both at once.

"Fear not," he said. "For I have redeemed thee. I have called thee by thy name. Thou art mine."

The names of the people had been written somewhere very far back. The people had been called by those names before they had stood in the wilderness, before they had passed through the sea, before the fathers had walked their long roads. The naming was older than the things they remembered.

The word went on. It spake of waters. It spake of fire.

"When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee. The rivers shall not overflow thee. When thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned. Neither shall the flame kindle upon thee."

These were waters they had passed through aforetime. The Red Sea. The Jordan. The water that rose against them at Pharaoh's pursuit and brake open. The waters were old waters by now. The fire was new fire ahead of them, but the word was already telling them about it.

The word spake of ransom. "I gave Egypt for thy ransom. Ethiopia and Seba for thee. Since thou wast precious in my sight, thou hast been honourable, and I have loved thee."

Then the word swept out across the earth. It went unto the four directions.

"Fear not. For I am with thee. I will bring thy seed from the east. I will gather thee from the west. I will say unto the north: give up. And unto the south: keep not back. Bring my sons from far. Bring my daughters from the ends of the earth. Even every one that is called by my name. For I have created him for my glory. I have formed him. I have made him."

Isaiah heard the four directions named. He saw, in his mind, the sons and daughters being brought back. From the east. From the west. From the north. From the south. They were coming home.

The word was finished. Isaiah held it within him a long while or ever he wrote it down. The country round about him was still in the afternoon. The people the word was for were beyond the hills. He wrote it for them.
