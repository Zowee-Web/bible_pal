Israel had gathered at Mizpeh at Samuel's word to fast and to put away the strange gods of Baal and Ashtaroth, and to pour out water before the Lord in repentance for many years of going after other gods. The lords of the Philistines had heard that Israel was assembled, and had come up against them. The people were afraid. Samuel was there with the lamb at the altar.

When the Philistines heard that the children of Israel were gathered together to Mizpeh, the lords of the Philistines went up against Israel. And when the children of Israel heard it, they were afraid of the Philistines.

And the children of Israel said unto Samuel, "Cease not to cry unto the Lord our God for us, that he will save us out of the hand of the Philistines."

And Samuel took a sucking lamb, and offered it for a burnt offering wholly unto the Lord: and Samuel cried unto the Lord for Israel; and the Lord heard him.

And as Samuel was offering up the burnt offering, the Philistines drew near to battle against Israel: but the Lord thundered with a great thunder on that day upon the Philistines, and discomfited them; and they were smitten before Israel.

And the men of Israel went out of Mizpeh, and pursued the Philistines, and smote them, until they came under Bethcar.

Then Samuel took a stone, and set it between Mizpeh and Shen, and called the name of it Ebenezer, saying, "Hitherto hath the Lord helped us."

And the stone Ebenezer stood between Mizpeh and Shen upon the slope where Samuel had set it that day. The bones of the Philistines lay scattered down toward Bethcar; the men of Israel went up again unto their houses with the swords of the fallen; and the stone with the name "Hitherto hath the Lord helped us" graven thereon stood upon the hillside, set there for them that should come after.
