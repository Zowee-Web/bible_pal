And the whole earth was of one language, and of one speech.

The generations after the flood had grown out across the country. The sons of Japheth had gone north. The sons of Shem had stayed in the midst. The sons of Ham had spread south and west. And in the midst, in the second and third generation after Noah, a great body of them journeyed from the east, and they had come together at last upon a plain in the land of Shinar, between two great rivers.

They dwelt there. The land was wide. The clay was good. The grasses grew. There were waters in both rivers. They had everything they needed. They had no cause to keep moving. And they desired not to keep moving. They had been moving too long already.

And they said one to another: go to, let us make brick, and burn them thoroughly. And they had brick for stone, and slime had they for morter. The kilns smoked along the river. The walls began to rise. The first walls were the walls of a city.

Then one said the thing the others had already been thinking. He said it openly, where the others could hear.

Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven. And let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.

The word that ran through the camp was that word: name. They had not been given one. They desired one. They were afraid of going out and being forgotten. They were afraid of the world being too great for them. They had survived the flood. They were not going to be scattered again. They should build a place to be seen from, and they should stand in it together, and the place should bear their name forward into whatever came after them.

The work began in earnest.

The tower went up. The bricks burned hot. The plain filled with the noise of work. There were levels of scaffolding. There were tools no man had thought of before. The plans grew. What had begun as a city became a city around a tower. What had been a tower became the highest tower any had built. They could see it from a day's walk away. The wind moved at the top of it, and the cloud sat upon it in the morning, and the men climbing it could look down and see the river bend and the kilns smoking and the camp spread out below.

And the Lord came down to see the city and the tower, which the children of men builded.

He said: behold, the people is one, and they have all one language; and this they begin to do: and now nothing will be restrained from them, which they have imagined to do.

Go to, he said, let us go down, and there confound their language, that they may not understand one another's speech.

And it was so.

It came not with thunder. It came not with fire. The men on the wall called down to the men below for more bricks, and the men below stood looking up at them with their hands open. The words had been their own words a moment before. They were not their own words now. The men with the morter shouted at the men with the buckets, and the men with the buckets shouted back, and neither side could tell what the other side was saying. The plans upon the table could not be read by the second man at the table. The order from the foreman could not be done by the workman who heard it.

By the end of the day, no man could give an order any other man could carry out.

The work ceased. The kilns went cold. The tower stood where it was, half builded, with the wind moving over the top of it. The men sat down where they were. They could yet see one another. They could yet know one another's faces. They simply could no longer speak to one another.

After a while, the men who could yet understand one another began to draw together. They gathered up their tools and their families and what they could bear. The camp broke up into smaller camps. The smaller camps moved off in different directions, every one bearing its own speech with it.

So the Lord scattered them abroad from thence upon the face of all the earth: and they left off to build the city.

Therefore is the name of it called Babel; because the Lord did there confound the language of all the earth: and from thence did the Lord scatter them abroad upon the face of all the earth. The tower stood empty behind them upon the plain. The name they had desired to make for themselves was the name now given unto the place — and the name meant confusion.

And so it was in the days of the children of men on the plain of Shinar, when they sought to build a name into the heavens, and the building of it was the thing that brake the gathering.
