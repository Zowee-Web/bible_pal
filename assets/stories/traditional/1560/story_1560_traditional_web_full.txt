The whole earth was of one language and one speech.

The generations after the Flood had grown out across the country. The sons of Japheth had gone north. The sons of Shem had stayed in the middle. The sons of Ham had spread south and west. And in the middle, in the second and third generation after Noah, a great body of them traveled east, and they had come together at last on a plain in the land of Shinar, between two great rivers.

They settled there. The land was wide. The clay was good. The grasses grew. There was water in both rivers. They had everything they needed. They had no reason to keep moving. And they did not want to keep moving. They had been moving for too long already.

They began to say to one another: come, let us make bricks, and burn them thoroughly. So they had bricks for stone, and they used tar for mortar. The kilns smoked along the river. The walls began to rise. The first walls were the walls of a city.

Then someone said the thing the others had already been thinking. He said it openly, where the others could hear.

Come, let us build ourselves a city, and a tower whose top reaches to the sky. And let us make a name for ourselves, lest we be scattered abroad on the surface of the whole earth.

The word that ran through the camp was that word: name. They had not been given one. They wanted one. They were afraid of going out and being forgotten. They were afraid of the world being too big for them. They had survived the Flood. They were not going to be scattered again. They would build a place to be seen from, and they would stand in it together, and the place would carry their name forward into whatever came after them.

The work began in earnest.

The tower went up. The bricks burned hot. The plain filled with the noise of work. There were levels of scaffolding. There were tools no one had thought of before. The plans grew. What had begun as a city became a city around a tower. What had been a tower became the highest tower anyone had built. They could see it from a day's walk away. The wind moved at the top of it, and the cloud sat on it in the morning, and the men climbing it could look down and see the river bend and the kilns smoking and the camp spread out below.

Yahweh came down to see the city and the tower that the children of men were building.

He said: behold, they are one people, and they all have one language. And this is only the beginning of what they will do. Nothing they intend to do will be withheld from them.

Come, he said, let us go down, and confuse their language there, that they may not understand one another's speech.

And it was so.

It did not happen with thunder. It did not happen with fire. The men on the wall called down to the men below for more bricks, and the men below stood looking up at them with their hands open. The words had been their own words a minute before. They were not their own words now. The men with the mortar shouted at the men with the buckets, and the men with the buckets shouted back, and neither side could tell what the other side was saying. The plans on the table could not be read by the second man at the table. The order from the foreman could not be carried out by the workman who heard it.

By the end of the day, no one could give an order anyone else could carry out.

The work stopped. The kilns went cold. The tower stood where it was, half built, with the wind moving over the top of it. The men sat down where they were. They could still see each other. They could still recognize each other's faces. They simply could not speak to each other anymore.

After a while, the men who could still understand one another began to draw together. They gathered up their tools and their families and what they could carry. The camp broke up into smaller camps. The smaller camps moved off in different directions, each one carrying its own speech with it, each one going toward a different horizon.

So Yahweh scattered them abroad from there over the face of all the earth. They stopped building the city.

Therefore its name was called Babel, because there Yahweh confused the language of all the earth. From there, Yahweh scattered them abroad over the face of all the earth. The tower stood empty behind them on the plain. The name they had wanted to make for themselves was the name now given to the place — and the name meant confusion.

And so it was in the days of the children of men on the plain of Shinar, when they tried to build a name into the sky, and the building of it was the thing that broke the gathering.
