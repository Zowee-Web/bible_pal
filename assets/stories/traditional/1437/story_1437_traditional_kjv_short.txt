Sisera fled away on his feet. The host was lost behind him. He came unto the tent of Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite. There was peace between Jabin the king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite. He thought he should be safe there.

Jael went out to meet him. She said unto him, "Turn in, my lord, turn in to me; fear not."

He turned in unto her into the tent. She covered him with a mantle.

He said unto her, "Give me, I pray thee, a little water to drink; for I am thirsty."

She opened a bottle of milk. She gave him drink. She covered him.

Then said he unto her, "Stand in the door of the tent, and it shall be, when any man doth come and enquire of thee, and say, Is there any man here? that thou shalt say, No."

He fell into a deep sleep. The hours of fighting had taken him.

Then Jael, Heber's wife, took a nail of the tent. She took an hammer in her hand. She went softly unto him.

She smote the nail into his temples. It fastened it into the ground.

He fainted. He died.

The tent was quiet again. The mantle lay over him.

And behold, Barak came pursuing Sisera. Jael went out to meet him at the door. "Come," said she, "and I will shew thee the man whom thou seekest."

He came unto her. He beheld.

Sisera lay dead, and the nail was in his temples.

Jael spake nothing. Barak spake nothing. The nail was in his temple, and the bottle of milk was yet upon the floor.
