Sisera fled on foot. The army was lost behind him. He came to the tent of Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite. There was peace between Jabin the king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite. He thought he would be safe there.

Jael went out to meet him. She said, "Turn in, my lord. Turn in to me. Do not be afraid."

He came in to her into the tent. She covered him with a rug.

He said, "Please give me a little water to drink. I am thirsty."

She opened a bottle of milk. She gave him a drink. She covered him.

Then he said, "Stand in the door of the tent. If any man comes and asks of you, 'Is there any man here?' you shall say, 'No.'"

He fell into a deep sleep. The hours of fighting had taken him.

Then Jael, Heber's wife, took a tent peg. She took a hammer in her hand. She went softly to him.

She struck the peg into his temple. It pierced through into the ground.

He fainted. He died.

The tent was quiet again. The rug lay over him.

Then Barak came, pursuing Sisera. Jael came out to meet him at the door. "Come," she said, "and I will show you the man whom you seek."

He came to her. He looked.

Sisera lay dead, and the tent peg was in his temples.

Jael said nothing. Barak said nothing. The peg was in his temple, and the bottle of milk was still on the floor.
