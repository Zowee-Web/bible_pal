The two angels came to Sodom at evening. Lot was sitting in the gate, and he rose and bowed his face to the earth.

"My lords, come into your servant's house. Stay the night, and go on early."

"No," they said. "We will stay in the street."

He urged them greatly, and they came in. He made them a feast, baked unleavened bread, and they ate.

Before they lay down, the men of Sodom surrounded the house, young and old.

They called to Lot, demanding he bring the men out to them.

Lot went out and shut the door behind him.

"Please, my brothers, do not act so wickedly." He offered them his own two daughters instead. "Only do nothing to these men, for they have come under the shadow of my roof."

"Stand back!" they said. "This one came here as a foreigner, and now he judges us. We will deal worse with you than with them."

They pressed hard on him and came near to break the door.

Then hands reached out of the house. They pulled Lot inside and shut the door, and struck the crowd with blindness until they wore themselves out looking for it.

"Bring out anyone else you have — sons-in-law, sons, daughters," the angels said. "We are going to destroy this place. The outcry against it has come up before Yahweh, and he has sent us."

Lot went out to the men pledged to his daughters. "Get up! Get out of this place, for Yahweh will destroy the city."

He seemed to them to be joking.

In the morning the angels hurried him. "Get up. Take your wife and your two daughters, or you will be swept away in the city's iniquity."

He lingered.

So the men took hold of his hand, his wife's hand, and his two daughters' hands, Yahweh being merciful to him, and set them outside the city.

"Escape for your life. Do not look behind you. Do not stay in the plain. Escape to the mountains."

Lot begged for a smaller thing. He could not reach the mountain, he said, but a little city was near. "Let me escape there, and my soul will live."

"I have granted this also. I will not overthrow that city. Hurry — I can do nothing till you get there."

So the city was called Zoar. The sun had risen when Lot came in.

Then Yahweh rained sulfur and fire on Sodom and Gomorrah out of the sky, and overthrew the cities and all the plain and all who lived there, and everything that grew on the ground.

Lot's wife looked back from behind him. She became a pillar of salt.

Abraham went up early to the place where he had stood before Yahweh, and looked toward Sodom and Gomorrah and the plain. The smoke went up as from a furnace.

When God destroyed the cities of the plain, God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the middle of the overthrow.
