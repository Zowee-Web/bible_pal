The sun was high in the sky over the dusty roads of Samaria. Heat shimmered off the ground, and shadows were small. Jesus and His disciples were traveling from Judea to Galilee, and as they came near a town called Sychar, Jesus grew tired from the journey. Jacob’s well was just outside the town, its stones cool in the noonday sun. Jesus sat by the well to rest, while His disciples went into the city to buy food.

At this time, a woman from Samaria came to the well to draw water. She came alone, carrying her empty jar on her shoulder. The day was bright and quiet, except for the sound of her feet on the dusty path and the faint wind blowing across the hills. Most people drew water when the sun was low, but this woman came at noon, when no one else was around.

As she neared the well, Jesus spoke. “Give me a drink,” He said. The woman looked at Him, surprised. She could see from His clothes and speech that He was a Jew, and Jews and Samaritans did not usually talk to each other or share things. She hesitated, looking at the man who had made such a simple but surprising request.

“Why do you, a Jew, ask me, a Samaritan woman, for a drink?” she asked. Her voice was gentle but unsure.

Jesus answered her, “If you knew the gift of God, and who it is who says to you, ‘Give me a drink,’ you would have asked Him, and He would have given you living water.”

The woman was puzzled. She looked at the well and then at the stranger. “Sir, you have nothing to draw with, and the well is deep. Where do you get that living water? Are you greater than our father Jacob, who gave us this well, and drank from it himself, along with his children and his cattle?”

Jesus replied, “Everyone who drinks of this water will thirst again. But whoever drinks of the water I will give him will never thirst. The water I give will become in him a well of water springing up to eternal life.”

The woman’s eyes grew wide with wonder. She said, “Sir, give me this water, so I won’t get thirsty or have to come all the way here to draw.”

Jesus, looking at her kindly, said, “Go, call your husband, and come back.”

The woman lowered her eyes. She softly replied, “I have no husband.”

Jesus said to her, “You are right. You have no husband, for you have had five husbands, and the man you have now is not your husband. You have spoken the truth.”

Hearing this, the woman realized that Jesus knew her life, even though she had never met Him before. She felt both amazed and a little afraid, for here was a man who saw her fully. She said, “Sir, I see that you are a prophet. Our fathers worshiped on this mountain, but you Jews say the place to worship is in Jerusalem.”

Jesus answered, “Woman, believe me, the time is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem. You Samaritans worship what you do not know; we worship what we know, for salvation is from the Jews. The time is coming—and has now come—when true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth. The Father is seeking such people to worship him. God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth.”

The woman thought about these words. She said, “I know that Messiah is coming. When He comes, He will tell us everything.”

Jesus looked at her and said, “I who speak to you am He.”

In the quiet noon, beside the old stone well, the woman realized she was speaking with the one everyone was waiting for. The road stayed silent, the water in the well was cool and clear, and Jesus’s words brought hope and kindness. She was known and not turned away.

For a moment, she stood very still, letting it all sink in. Then her heart lifted, the heavy weight inside her loosened just a little with hope.
