The sun was high and bright as Jesus walked through the dry land of Samaria. His journey took him unto a quiet road near a town called Sychar. There, surrounded by dust and stones, stood a deep, cool well. It was Jacob's well, and Jesus, being wearied with his journey, sat down to rest beside it.

It was the sixth hour, when the sun was hottest and most rested within. The road was quiet. From the town came a woman forth, bearing her heavy waterpot. She came alone, that none might trouble her. As she drew nigh, her feet stirred the dust. The shadow of the well fell at her side. She marvelled to see Jesus sitting there, for he was a Jew and she a Samaritan. For the Jews have no dealings with the Samaritans.

Jesus spake gently, saying, "Give me to drink." The woman was confounded. "How is it that thou, being a Jew, askest drink of me, which am a woman of Samaria?" she said. He looked upon her kindly, and said, "If thou knewest the gift of God, and who it is that saith to thee, Give me to drink; thou wouldest have asked of him, and he would have given thee living water."

She wondered. "Sir, thou hast nothing to draw with, and the well is deep: from whence hast thou that living water? Art thou greater than our father Jacob, which gave us the well?"

Jesus answered, "Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again: but whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life." Her eyes grew wide. She desired this water, that she need not draw daily. "Sir, give me this water," she said.

Jesus said unto her, "Go, call thy husband, and come hither." The woman answered, "I have no husband." Jesus saith gently, "Thou hast well said. Thou hast had five husbands; and he whom thou now hast is not thy husband. In that saidst thou truly." The woman marvelled. She perceived that Jesus knew her life, though they had never met.

She said, "Sir, I perceive that thou art a prophet. Our fathers worship in this mountain; and ye say that in Jerusalem is the place where men ought to worship." Jesus answered that the hour cometh when men should worship God in spirit and in truth, and not in one place alone.

The woman said, "I know that Messias cometh. When he is come, he will tell us all things." Jesus looked upon her and said, "I that speak unto thee am he." She felt truly known, and not turned away. She hasted forth to tell others, leaving her waterpot behind, full of hope and wonder at that which she had heard.
