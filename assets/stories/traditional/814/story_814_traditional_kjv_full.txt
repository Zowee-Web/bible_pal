The sun was high in the sky above the dusty roads of Samaria. The heat shimmered upon the ground, and the shadows were small. Jesus and his disciples journeyed from Judaea unto Galilee, and as they drew nigh unto a city called Sychar, Jesus was wearied with his journey. Jacob's well was without the city, the stones thereof cool in the noonday sun. Jesus sat thus on the well to rest, while his disciples went into the city to buy meat.

At this hour came a woman of Samaria to draw water. She came alone, bearing her empty pot upon her shoulder. The day was bright and still, save for the sound of her feet upon the dusty path and the faint wind blowing across the hills. Most folk drew water when the sun was low, but this woman came at the sixth hour, when no other was near.

As she drew nigh unto the well, Jesus spake. "Give me to drink," he said. The woman looked upon him, and marvelled. She could perceive by his garments and his speech that he was a Jew, and the Jews have no dealings with the Samaritans, neither do they speak together as friends. She paused, looking upon the man that had made so simple and yet so strange a request.

"How is it that thou, being a Jew, askest drink of me, which am a woman of Samaria?" she said unto him. Her voice was gentle, but uncertain.

Jesus answered and said unto her, "If thou knewest the gift of God, and who it is that saith to thee, Give me to drink; thou wouldest have asked of him, and he would have given thee living water."

The woman was confounded. She beheld the well, and then the stranger. "Sir, thou hast nothing to draw with, and the well is deep: from whence then hast thou that living water? Art thou greater than our father Jacob, which gave us the well, and drank thereof himself, and his children, and his cattle?"

Jesus answered, "Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again: but whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life."

The woman's eyes grew wide with marvelling. She said, "Sir, give me this water, that I thirst not, neither come hither to draw."

Jesus, looking upon her kindly, said, "Go, call thy husband, and come hither."

The woman cast down her eyes. Softly she answered, "I have no husband."

Jesus said unto her, "Thou hast well said, I have no husband: for thou hast had five husbands; and he whom thou now hast is not thy husband: in that saidst thou truly."

When she heard this, the woman perceived that Jesus knew her life, though she had never met him before. She felt both wonder and a little fear, for here was a man that saw her wholly. She said, "Sir, I perceive that thou art a prophet. Our fathers worshipped in this mountain; and ye say, that in Jerusalem is the place where men ought to worship."

Jesus answered, "Woman, believe me, the hour cometh, when ye shall neither in this mountain, nor yet at Jerusalem, worship the Father. Ye worship ye know not what: we know what we worship: for salvation is of the Jews. But the hour cometh, and now is, when the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth: for the Father seeketh such to worship him. God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth."

The woman pondered these words. She said, "I know that Messias cometh, which is called Christ: when he is come, he will tell us all things."

Jesus looked upon her and said, "I that speak unto thee am he."

In the quiet of noon, beside the old stone well, the woman perceived that she spake with him whom all had awaited. The road yet was silent, the water in the well was cool and clear, and Jesus' words brought hope and kindness. She was known, and not turned away.

For a moment she stood very still, suffering it all to sink in. Then her heart was lifted up, and the heavy weight within her loosed a little with hope.
