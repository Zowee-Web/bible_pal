The sun was high and bright as Jesus walked through the dry land of Samaria. His journey took Him to a quiet road near a little town called Sychar. There, surrounded by the dust and stones, stood a deep, cool well. It was Jacob’s well, and Jesus, feeling tired from walking, sat down to rest by its side.

It was noon, when the sun was hottest and most people were resting inside. The road was quiet. From the town, a woman came out, carrying her heavy water jar. She came alone so no one would bother her. As she stepped closer, her feet stirred the dust. The well’s shadow fell at her side. She looked surprised when she saw Jesus sitting there, because He was a Jew and she was a Samaritan. Usually, Jews and Samaritans did not talk to each other.

Jesus spoke gently, asking, “Will you give Me a drink?” The woman was puzzled. “Why do You, a Jew, ask me, a Samaritan woman, for a drink?” she asked. He looked at her kindly and said, “If you knew who I am, you would have asked Me, and I would have given you living water.”

She was curious. “Sir, You have no bucket, and the well is deep. Where can You get this living water? Are You greater than our father Jacob, who gave us the well?”

Jesus explained, “Everyone who drinks this water will get thirsty again. But anyone who drinks the water I give will never thirst. The water I give becomes a spring inside, bubbling up forever.” The woman’s eyes grew wide with wonder. She wanted this special water, so she would not have to come and draw water every day. “Sir, give me this water,” she said.

Jesus told her, “Go, call your husband, and come back.” The woman answered, “I have no husband.” Jesus nodded and gently said, “That is true. You have had five husbands, and the man you have now is not your husband. You have spoken the truth.” The woman was amazed. She realized Jesus knew about her life even though they had never met.

She said, “Sir, I see You are a prophet. Our people worship on this mountain, but Jews say we must worship in Jerusalem.” Jesus answered that a time was coming when people would worship God in spirit and truth, not just in one place.

The woman said, “I know the Messiah is coming. When He comes, He will explain everything.” Jesus looked at her and said, “I am He, the One you are waiting for.” The woman felt truly known and accepted. She hurried off to tell others, leaving her water jar behind, full of hope and wonder at what she had heard.
