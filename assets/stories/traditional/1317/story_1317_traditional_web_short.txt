Job had sat in the ashes seven days and seven nights, and his friends had sat with him, and not a word was spoken among them. The boils ran on his body. His children were dead. His herds were gone. His house lay in ruins. And on the seventh night he had opened his mouth and cursed the day of his birth, and his friends had begun to answer him, one after another, and now Job spoke again.

"Isn't a man forced to labor on earth? Aren't his days like the days of a hired hand?

As a servant who earnestly desires the shadow, as a hireling who looks for his wages,

so I am made to possess months of misery, wearisome nights are appointed to me.

When I lie down, I say, 'When will I arise, and the night be gone?' I toss and turn until the dawning of the day.

My flesh is clothed with worms and clods of dust. My skin closes up, and breaks out afresh.

My days are swifter than a weaver's shuttle, and are spent without hope."

And the dust lay on the ground. The friends sat in silence again. The wind moved across the ash. The day went on as the days had been going on, and Job spoke his weariness into the air. And so it was in the land of Uz, in those days, while Job sat among the broken things.
