After Jacob their father had been buried in the cave of Machpelah, Joseph and his brethren returned together from Canaan unto Egypt. The roads were long, and the days of mourning had been many. They came again unto their houses in the land of Goshen.

The brethren beheld that their father was now dead, and they said one to another, "Joseph will peradventure hate us, and will certainly requite us all the evil which we did unto him."

So they sent a message unto Joseph, saying, "Thy father did command before he died, saying, 'So shall ye say unto Joseph, Forgive, I pray thee now, the trespass of thy brethren, and their sin; for they did unto thee evil.' Now therefore, we pray thee, forgive the trespass of the servants of the God of thy father."

When Joseph heard their words, he wept. The chamber was silent about him, and the messengers stood waiting. He had not beheld the faces of his brethren in many days, and the message came unto him in the chamber of his house.

Then his brethren also went unto Joseph. They came before his face and fell down upon the ground, and they said, "Behold, we be thy servants." Their robes touched the floor, and none of them lifted up his eyes unto his face.

But Joseph said unto them, "Fear not: for am I in the place of God? But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive."

He said again, "Now therefore fear ye not. I will nourish you, and your little ones." So he comforted them, and spake kindly unto them. The days of fear came unto an end, and the brethren abode with him in the land of Goshen with their children and their flocks.
