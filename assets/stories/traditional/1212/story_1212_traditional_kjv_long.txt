It was the seventh month, the one and twentieth day of the month — the last day of the feast.

The people of Judah were upon the temple mount. The foundation of the second temple had been laid. The lower courses of stone had been set, and were set straight, and were holding. The work was rising around the floor of what had once been the most beautiful house in the land. New stone. New mortar. The sound of mallets against chisels carried across the mount, and the sound of men calling to one another across the courses, and the sound of a winch hauling up timber from below.

The new building was small. The men that wrought upon it could see that. The cedar that was being raised was not the cedar of Lebanon that Solomon had brought up by oxcart. The pillars were not the pillars of brass, Boaz and Jachin. The floor was not overlaid with gold.

Some of the old men had stopped working. They were leaning upon their staves at the edge of the work. They were looking at the stones that were going up, and they were looking past the stones at the empty air where Solomon's temple had stood — the great rooms, the layered courts, the doors plated with gold. The new house was not going to be that house. The old men knew it. One of them shut his eyes. One of them did not.

Then the word of the Lord came by Haggai the prophet.

Haggai walked up into the work. He found Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, with a stone-tool in his hand and dust along the front of his robe. He found Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest, who had set down his rule of measure to hear. He found the residue of the people, who had drawn near round about the prophet when they saw he was about to speak. The work ceased. The voices ceased. A length of cedar that had been swinging from the winch was set carefully down upon the stones.

The prophet lifted his face. He looked upon the old men first.

"Who is left among you that saw this house in her first glory?" he said. "and how do ye see it now? is it not in your eyes in comparison of it as nothing?"

The old men answered not. Some of them cast down their faces. Others kept looking upon the stones. The man that had shut his eyes opened them again. The man that had not shut them moved not.

"Yet now be strong, O Zerubbabel," the prophet said. "Be strong, O Joshua, son of Josedech, the high priest. Be strong, all ye people of the land. And work. For I am with you, saith the Lord of hosts."

The word "work" fell upon the still air. Zerubbabel turned his head toward the prophet. The high priest moved not.

"According to the word that I covenanted with you when ye came out of Egypt," the prophet said, "so my Spirit remaineth among you. Fear ye not."

Then the prophet's voice changed. He went on.

"For thus saith the Lord of hosts: Yet once, it is a little while, and I will shake the heavens, and the earth, and the sea, and the dry land. And I will shake all nations. The desire of all nations shall come, and I will fill this house with glory, saith the Lord of hosts."

The prophet looked across the small stones. He spake the next words slowly.

"The silver is mine, and the gold is mine, saith the Lord of hosts."

He looked upon the old men again.

"The glory of this latter house shall be greater than of the former, saith the Lord of hosts. And in this place will I give peace, saith the Lord of hosts."

The old men wept not openly. But the hand of one of them, which had been holding the staff loosely, tightened upon it until the knuckles whitened. The hand of another, which had hung at his side, came up and pressed against his chest. The new building became not Solomon's temple. The new building needed not to. Something else was being said about it.

Haggai finished speaking. The residue stirred not at once. Then Zerubbabel turned back to his stone. He took up his tool. The high priest reached for his rule of measure. The man at the winch took the rope again. The cedar that had been set down was lifted back into the air. The work began again.

Two months passed.

The walls went up another course. The cedar was raised, joint by joint. The lamps within the new rooms were lit at evening. The trumpet of the priests sounded at the appointed hours over the work, and the men hearkened unto it, and the men kept working. The old men came to the work in the mornings. They stood at the edge. They watched. They went home at evening. They came again the next day.

In the four and twentieth day of the ninth month, the word of the Lord came unto Haggai a second time. The prophet went unto Zerubbabel where he stood among the workers. The workers gathered again to hear. The work ceased, as it had ceased before.

"I will shake the heavens and the earth," the prophet said. "I will overthrow the throne of kingdoms. I will destroy the strength of the kingdoms of the heathen. I will overthrow the chariots, and those that ride in them. The horses and their riders shall come down, every one by the sword of his brother."

Zerubbabel spake not. The workers were still. A wind moved across the stones.

"In that day, saith the Lord of hosts, will I take thee, O Zerubbabel, my servant, the son of Shealtiel, saith the Lord, and will make thee as a signet. For I have chosen thee, saith the Lord of hosts."

The prophet ceased speaking. Zerubbabel cast down his face. The workers moved not for a long moment. Then the high priest took up his rule of measure. The man at the winch took the rope again. The work went on.
