David the king gathered all the congregation and set the great work before them. Solomon, the son whom God alone had chosen, was yet young and tender. The task could not be treated lightly, for the palace they would build was not for man, but for the Lord God.

David had prepared for the house of his God with all his might. He had gathered gold for things of gold, silver for things of silver, brass for things of brass, iron for things of iron, and wood for things of wood. He had also prepared onyx stones, stones for settings and inlaid work, glistering stones of divers colours, all manner of precious stones, and marble in abundance.

Yet the king's preparation did not end with the stores under his authority. Because he had set his affection upon the house of his God, David gave his own treasure over and above what he had already provided for the holy house. He gave three thousand talents of gold from Ophir and seven thousand talents of refined silver, to overlay the walls and supply the work of the artificers. Then he called for willing service: who would consecrate himself that day unto the Lord?

The answer came from throughout Israel's leadership. The chiefs of the fathers, the princes of the tribes, captains of thousands and hundreds, and rulers over the king's work all offered willingly. They gave gold, silver, brass, and iron in great quantities for the service of God's house. Those who had precious stones brought them into the treasury of the Lord by the hand of Jehiel the Gershonite. The people rejoiced because the gifts came willingly and from perfect hearts. David also rejoiced with great joy.

David did not speak as though Israel had enriched God. He humbled himself, asking who he and his people were, that they had power to offer after this sort. All things came of God; what they had given was already his own. They stood before him as strangers and sojourners, as their fathers had done. Their days upon earth were as a shadow, and there was none abiding. Even the great store prepared for the house of God's holy name came from his hand and remained all his own.

God tried the heart and had pleasure in uprightness. David had offered willingly in the uprightness of his heart, and now he had seen the people offer with joy. He asked the Lord God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel to keep this purpose in their thoughts and prepare their hearts unto himself. He also asked for Solomon to receive a perfect heart—to keep God's commandments, testimonies, and statutes, to do all that was required, and to build the palace for which David had made provision.

At David's word, the whole congregation blessed the Lord God of their fathers. They bowed their heads and worshipped the Lord and the king. On the morrow they offered sacrifices in abundance: bullocks, rams, and lambs with their drink offerings for all Israel. They ate and drank before the Lord with great gladness.

Solomon was made king the second time and anointed unto the Lord as chief governor, while Zadok was anointed priest. Solomon sat upon the throne of the Lord as king instead of David his father and prospered. Israel obeyed him; the princes, mighty men, and David's sons submitted themselves unto him. The Lord magnified Solomon in the sight of Israel and bestowed royal majesty upon him.

David the son of Jesse had reigned forty years over Israel—seven years in Hebron and thirty and three in Jerusalem. He died in a good old age, full of days, riches, and honour, and Solomon reigned in his stead. The books of Samuel, Nathan, and Gad held the record of David's reign, his might, and the times that passed over him, Israel, and the kingdoms of the countries.
