David the king gathered all the assembly and set the great work before them. Solomon, the son whom God alone had chosen, was still young and inexperienced. The task could not be treated lightly, because the house they would build was not for man, but for Yahweh God.

David had prepared for the house of his God with all his might. He had gathered gold for the gold work, silver for the silver, bronze for the bronze, iron for the iron, and wood for the wood. He had also prepared onyx, stones for settings and inlaid work, stones of many colors, every kind of precious stone, and marble in abundance.

Yet the king's preparation did not end with the stores under his authority. Because he had set his affection on the house of his God, David gave his own treasure over and above what he had already provided for the holy house. He gave three thousand talents of gold from Ophir and seven thousand talents of refined silver, enough to overlay the walls and supply the artisans' work. Then he called for willing service: who would consecrate himself that day to Yahweh?

The response came from across Israel's leadership. Heads of fathers' households, tribal princes, captains of thousands and hundreds, and rulers over the king's work all offered willingly. They gave gold, silver, bronze, and iron in great quantities for the service of God's house. Those who had precious stones brought them to Yahweh's treasury under the care of Jehiel the Gershonite. The people rejoiced because the gifts came willingly and from complete hearts. David also rejoiced with great joy.

David did not speak as though Israel had enriched God. He humbled himself, asking who he and his people were that they had strength to offer in this way. Everything came from God; what they had given was already his. They lived before him as strangers and foreigners, as their fathers had done. Their days on earth passed like a shadow, with no lasting stay. Even the great store prepared for the house of God's holy name had come from his hand and remained his own.

God tested the heart and delighted in uprightness. David had offered willingly from an upright heart, and now he had seen the people do the same with joy. He asked Yahweh, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, to preserve this purpose in their thoughts and prepare their hearts for himself. He also asked for Solomon to receive a complete heart—to keep God's commandments, testimonies, and statutes, to do what was required, and to build the house for which David had made provision.

At David's call, the whole assembly blessed Yahweh, the God of their fathers. They bowed their heads and prostrated themselves before Yahweh and the king. On the following day they offered abundant sacrifices: bulls, rams, and lambs with their drink offerings for all Israel. They ate and drank before Yahweh with great gladness.

Solomon was made king again and anointed before Yahweh as prince, while Zadok was anointed priest. Solomon sat on Yahweh's throne in place of David his father and prospered. Israel obeyed him; the princes, mighty men, and David's sons submitted to him. Yahweh magnified Solomon in Israel's sight and gave him royal majesty.

David son of Jesse had reigned forty years over Israel—seven years in Hebron and thirty-three in Jerusalem. He died at a good old age, full of days, riches, and honor, and Solomon reigned in his place. The accounts of Samuel, Nathan, and Gad held the record of David's reign, his might, and the times that passed over him, Israel, and the surrounding kingdoms.
