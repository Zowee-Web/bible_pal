David stood before the assembly and spoke of the work ahead. God had chosen Solomon, but David's son was still young and inexperienced, and the task was great. The house was not being built for man, but for Yahweh God.

David had prepared with all his might. He had gathered gold, silver, bronze, iron, wood, marble, onyx, and many precious stones for the holy house. Because he had set his affection on the house of his God, he also gave from his own treasure: gold from Ophir and refined silver, beyond everything already prepared. Then he asked who else would willingly consecrate his service to Yahweh.

The leaders of families and tribes, the captains, and the rulers of the king's work offered willingly. They brought great quantities of gold, silver, bronze, and iron. Those who possessed precious stones placed them in the treasury of Yahweh's house. The people rejoiced because they had offered with complete hearts, and David rejoiced with great joy.

David humbled himself before God. He asked who he and his people were that they could give so freely, for everything came from God, and they had only returned what was already his. Their days were like a shadow, but the whole store prepared for God's holy name had come from his hand. David prayed that God would preserve this willing purpose in the people's hearts and give Solomon a complete heart to obey and finish the work.

The assembly blessed Yahweh and bowed before him. On the next day they offered abundant sacrifices and ate and drank before Yahweh with gladness. Solomon was established as king in David's place, Zadok was appointed priest, and Israel's leaders submitted to Solomon. After forty years of rule, David died at a good old age, full of days, riches, and honor, and Solomon reigned after him.
