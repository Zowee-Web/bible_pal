David stood before all the congregation and spoke of the work that lay before them. God had chosen Solomon, yet David's son was still young and tender, and the work was great. The palace was not for man, but for the Lord God.

David had prepared with all his might. He had gathered gold, silver, brass, iron, wood, marble, onyx, and precious stones for the holy house. Because he had set his affection upon the house of his God, he also gave from his own treasure: gold of Ophir and refined silver, above all that was already prepared. Then he asked who would willingly consecrate his service that day unto the Lord.

The chiefs of the fathers, the princes of the tribes, the captains, and the rulers of the king's work offered willingly. They brought great quantities of gold, silver, brass, and iron. Those who possessed precious stones placed them in the treasury of the Lord's house. The people rejoiced because they had offered with perfect hearts, and David rejoiced with great joy.

David humbled himself before God. Who were he and his people, that they should be able to give so willingly? All things came of God, and they had given him of his own. Their days upon the earth were as a shadow, but all the store prepared for God's holy name had come from his hand. David prayed that the Lord would keep this purpose in the people's hearts and give Solomon a perfect heart to obey and finish the work.

The congregation blessed the Lord and bowed before him. On the morrow they offered sacrifices in abundance and ate and drank before the Lord with great gladness. Solomon was established as king in David's stead, and all Israel obeyed him. After forty years of rule, David died in a good old age, full of days, riches, and honour, and Solomon reigned after him.
