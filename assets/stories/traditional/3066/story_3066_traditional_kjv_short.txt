The children of Reuben, the children of Gad, and the half tribe of Manasseh came unto the borders of Jordan within the land of Canaan. There they built an altar by Jordan, a great altar to see to. Tidings came unto the other children of Israel that the eastern tribes had built it over against Canaan, near the passage where Israel crossed the river. The whole congregation gathered at Shiloh to go up to war against them.

Before battle, Israel sent a company into Gilead. Phinehas, the son of Eleazar the priest, went with ten princes, each a chief householder among the thousands of Israel. They charged the eastern tribes concerning what appeared to be a trespass against the God of Israel. Had those tribes turned away from the LORD by building an altar in rebellion? If their possession were unclean, they might pass unto the possession of the LORD, wherein his tabernacle dwelt, and take possession among their brethren. They must not rebel by making another altar beside the altar of the LORD.

The messengers recalled the iniquity of Peor and the plague upon the congregation. They also recalled Achan’s trespass in the accursed thing, when wrath fell on Israel and that man perished not alone. Thus their questions treated the new altar as a peril unto all Israel.

The children of Reuben, Gad, and the half tribe of Manasseh answered the heads of the thousands. They called upon the LORD God of gods, who knoweth their purpose, and said Israel should know it also. If they had acted in rebellion or trespass, they sought no deliverance that day. If the altar had been raised for burnt offering, meat offering, or peace offerings, let the LORD himself require it.

But they declared another cause. In time to come, the western tribes’ children might say unto their children, “What have ye to do with the LORD God of Israel?” Jordan stood as a border, and they might say the eastern tribes had no part in the LORD. Such words could make their children cease from fearing him. Therefore they had built the altar after the pattern of the LORD’s altar, not for sacrifices, but that it might be a witness between both peoples and their generations. It testified that the eastern tribes had a portion in the LORD and would serve him with offerings before his tabernacle.

They ended their defense by refusing rebellion. God forbid that they should build an altar for burnt offerings, meat offerings, or sacrifices beside the altar of the LORD their God which stood before his tabernacle.
