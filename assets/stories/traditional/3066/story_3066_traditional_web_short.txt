The Reubenites, Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh reached the region of the Jordan within the land of Canaan. There they built an altar by the river, a great altar visible from afar. Word came to the other Israelites that the eastern tribes had built it along the border of the land of Canaan, in the region around the Jordan, on the side that belongs to the children of Israel. The whole congregation gathered at Shiloh, prepared to go to war against them.

Before fighting, Israel sent a delegation into Gilead. Phinehas, the son of Eleazar the priest, led ten tribal chiefs, each a head among the thousands of Israel. They confronted the eastern tribes over what appeared to be unfaithfulness against the God of Israel. Had those tribes turned away from Yahweh by building an altar in rebellion? If their land seemed unclean, they could cross into Yahweh’s possession, where his tabernacle stood, and receive land among their brothers. They must not rebel by making another altar besides Yahweh’s altar.

The delegation recalled the iniquity at Peor and the plague that came upon the congregation. They also recalled Achan’s trespass over the devoted thing, when wrath fell on Israel and he did not perish alone. Their questions treated the new altar as a danger to the entire congregation.

Reuben, Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh answered the heads of Israel. They called on the Mighty One, God, Yahweh, as the one who knew their purpose, and said Israel should also know it. If they had acted in rebellion or unfaithfulness, they did not ask to be spared. If the altar had been built to offer burnt offerings, meal offerings, or peace offerings, Yahweh himself could require an account.

But they said they had acted from another concern. In time, the western tribes’ children might tell their children that the Jordan marked a boundary and that the eastern tribes had no portion in Yahweh. Such words could cause their descendants to stop fearing him. Therefore they had built the altar as a copy of Yahweh’s altar, not for sacrifices, but as a witness between both sides and their generations. It testified that the eastern tribes had a portion in Yahweh and would serve him with offerings at the tabernacle.

They ended their defense by rejecting rebellion. Far be it from them to build an altar for burnt offerings, meal offerings, or sacrifices besides the altar of Yahweh their God that stood before his tabernacle.
