The hills around Bethlehem were green and brown in the late afternoon. Olive trees grew in rows along the rocky slopes. Their silver-green leaves moved gently in the cool breeze. Small stone walls lined the paths between the hills. Wildflowers grew in patches near the walls. The air smelled like dry grass and warm earth.

In the town of Bethlehem, the streets were narrow and made of packed dirt. Low stone buildings sat close together. Wooden doors were propped open. The sound of people talking drifted from doorways and rooftops. Donkeys stood near water troughs, flicking their ears. Women carried clay jars on their shoulders. Children sat on stone steps, watching the busy road.

A announcement had gone out from Caesar Augustus. Every person was to go to their own town to be registered. People traveled from many places. The roads were full of families walking together. Some rode on donkeys. Some carried bundles wrapped in cloth. Dust rose from the road under their sandals.

Joseph walked along one of those roads. Mary was with him. She rode on a donkey. Its hooves clicked on the stones. Joseph held the rope that guided the animal. He walked slowly so the donkey could keep a steady pace. Mary held the wooden frame of the saddle with both hands. Her robe was dusty from the long trip. A cloth covered her head and kept the sun from her face.

They had come from Nazareth, in the region of Galilee. They were going to Bethlehem because Joseph was from the family of David. The journey was many miles. The road curved through valleys and over dry ridges. Now the town of Bethlehem was just ahead.

Other travelers moved along the road too. Some carried baskets of bread. Others led goats on short ropes. A man with a cart full of clay pots rolled past, the wheels bumping over rocks. The road was busier than usual because of the announcement. Everyone was going somewhere to be counted.

Joseph and Mary came into Bethlehem as the sun moved lower. The buildings glowed warm and golden in the late light. Joseph looked for a place to stay. He went to the inn. But the inn was full. There was no room for them there. Many people had already arrived for the registration.

So Joseph found a place where animals were kept. It was a sheltered space, quiet and out of the wind. The floor was covered with straw. A wooden manger sat against the wall. It was the kind of box used to hold food for animals. The straw inside it was dry and clean. A few animals stood nearby, chewing slowly. Their warm breath made small clouds in the cool air.

Joseph spread a blanket over the straw. Mary sat down carefully. The space was simple, but it was dry and still. The thick stone walls kept the cold out. A small oil lamp flickered on a ledge, giving a soft yellow glow. The straw rustled when anyone moved.

While they were there, the time came for Mary to have her baby. She gave birth to her first son. She wrapped him snugly in strips of cloth. The cloth held his little arms and legs close and kept him warm. Then she laid him gently in the manger. The straw cushioned him softly. He was small and new. His tiny fingers curled. His eyes blinked in the lamplight.

Joseph stood close by. Mary rested near the manger. The animals were quiet. The oil lamp glowed. Outside, the evening air was cool and still. Stars began to appear in the wide sky above Bethlehem.

Out in the countryside nearby, shepherds were living in the fields. They were keeping watch over their flocks by night. The sheep lay close together on the grass. Some of the lambs tucked themselves against their mothers. The shepherds sat near a small fire. The flames crackled softly. Orange light moved across their faces. Their wool cloaks were wrapped tight around their shoulders.

The night was calm. Crickets sang in the dry grass. A gentle breeze carried the smell of wood smoke and wool. The shepherds talked quietly among themselves. One of them held a wooden staff across his knees. Another poked the fire with a stick, sending tiny sparks upward.

Then an angel of Yahweh stood near them. The glory of Yahweh shone around them. A great, bright light filled the field. It was brighter than the fire. The shepherds could see every blade of grass, every stone, every sheep clearly in that light. Their cloaks, their sandals, even the wool of the sheep all glowed in the brightness.

The shepherds pulled their cloaks close and stared at the angel.

The angel said to them, "Do not be nervous. I bring you good news of great joy. It will be for all the people. Today in the city of David, a Savior has been born to you. He is Christ the Lord. This will be a sign to you. You will find a baby wrapped in strips of cloth, lying in a manger."

The shepherds listened. The angel's voice was clear in the quiet night.

Then suddenly, a great company of heavenly beings appeared with the angel. There were so many of them. They filled the sky above the field. Their voices rose together. They praised God, saying, "Glory to God in the highest heaven, and on earth peace among people of good will."

The sound of their voices was everywhere. It filled the hills and the valleys. It moved through the grass and over the stones. The sheep lifted their heads. The lambs stood on wobbly legs. The shepherds looked up and saw the sky full of light and heard the voices all around them.

Then the angels went away from them into heaven. The sky returned to its quiet, starry stillness. The fire crackled. The crickets began to sing again. The shepherds looked at one another. Their faces were lit by the remaining glow of the fire.

The shepherds said to one another, "Let us go now to Bethlehem. Let us see this thing that has happened, which Yahweh has made known to us."

They stood up. They gathered their staffs. They pulled their cloaks around their shoulders and began to walk. Their sandals crunched on the gravel path. The sheep stayed behind in the field, resting on the grass. The night air was cool on the shepherds' faces as they walked toward the town.

They walked quickly. The road sloped downward toward Bethlehem. Small houses appeared along the edges of the path. Oil lamps glowed in a few windows. A dog barked somewhere far off. The shepherds kept walking until they reached the place where the animals were kept.

They came in through the low doorway. They saw Mary and Joseph. And they saw the baby, lying in the manger, just as the angel had told them. The baby was wrapped in strips of cloth. He lay on the straw, small and still. The oil lamp on the ledge made a warm circle of light around the manger.

The shepherds came closer. Their rough sandals scuffed on the stone floor. They leaned their staffs against the wall. They knelt down near the manger and looked at the baby. His tiny chest rose and fell with each breath. His small hands rested on the cloth. Mary sat nearby, watching quietly.

The shepherds told Mary and Joseph everything. They told them what the angel had said about this child. They told them about the bright light in the field. They told them about the great company of heavenly beings and the words they had spoken. They told them about the sign — the baby in strips of cloth, lying in a manger.

Everyone who heard what the shepherds said was amazed. Mary listened to all these words. She kept them and treasured them.

After some time, the shepherds stood up again. They picked up their staffs. They ducked back through the low doorway and stepped into the cool night. The air smelled like wood smoke and straw.

The shepherds walked back toward their fields. As they walked, they glorified and praised God. Their voices carried across the quiet hills. They praised God for all the things they had heard and seen. Everything had been just as it was told to them.

The road stretched out ahead of them under the open sky. Their footsteps were steady on the path. Behind them, the little town of Bethlehem rested quietly. The oil lamps in the windows burned low. The animals stood still in their sheltered places.

Back in the manger, the baby slept on the soft straw. Mary rested beside him. Joseph sat close by. The oil lamp flickered gently on its ledge. The night was calm and wide. Everything was still. Everything was quiet. And the shepherds' song of praise drifted softly over the hills, growing fainter and fainter, until it blended with the sound of the wind in the olive trees.
