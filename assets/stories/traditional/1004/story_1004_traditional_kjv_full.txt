The town of Nazareth sat among the green hills. Small houses of stone lined the narrow paths. Fig trees grew near the doorways. Vines climbed along low walls. A warm breeze passed through the leaves. Donkeys stood in the shade, swishing their tails. Women bare clay jars upon their shoulders, walking unto the well. Children played beside the steps of stone. The savour of fresh bread drifted from open windows.

In this town dwelt a young woman called Mary, betrothed unto Joseph. Joseph was a carpenter. His hands were strong from the working of wood. He fashioned beams and doors and yokes for the plowing. Mary and Joseph made ready for a long journey. There went out a decree from Caesar Augustus, that all the world should be enrolled, each in the city of his lineage. Joseph was of the house of David, whose city was Bethlehem, far unto the south. So they gathered the things needful for the way.

Mary was great with child, and her time drew nigh. Joseph loaded their goods upon a donkey. He tucked a soft blanket among the bundle. Mary walked beside the donkey, and Joseph walked beside her. They followed the dusty road southward through the hills. Other travellers moved along the same road, going each unto his own city. Sandals shuffled upon the packed earth. The people called greetings one to another as they passed.

The road went through rocky valleys and past fields of grain. Olive trees were upon the hillsides. From time to time the travellers paused to rest beside a well. They drank cool water and did eat bread and dried figs. Then they continued upon their way. The journey from Nazareth unto Bethlehem was long, and took many days.

At length, Mary and Joseph came over a hill and beheld Bethlehem below them. The little town was full of people. Families had come from many places to be enrolled. Voices filled the streets. Donkeys brayed. Merchants cried out, selling food and supplies. Joseph led the donkey through the crowded ways. He sought a place wherein they might lodge. He went unto the inn, but there was no room for them there. Every guest chamber was already full.

So Joseph found a place where the beasts were kept. It was a stable, quiet and sheltered. Straw covered the ground. A manger stood against the wall, filled with hay for the cattle to eat. The air bore the savour of dry grass and warm wool. Joseph spread out blankets and made a place where Mary might rest.

And it came to pass, while they were there, the days were accomplished that she should be delivered. And she brought forth her firstborn son. She wrapped him in swaddling clothes. Then she laid him gently in the manger. The hay cushioned him. The cloth kept him warm. Joseph stood nigh. The babe rested in the manger, wrapped and still.

In the countryside near Bethlehem, shepherds abode in the field, keeping watch over their flocks by night. The quiet night stretched wide above them. The stars filled the sky, more than any man could number. The sheep lay close together upon the hillside, their wool thick and warm. Some of the lambs nestled near their mothers. The shepherds sat upon the ground or leaned upon their staves. A small fire crackled, sending up a thin curl of smoke. The air was cool and calm.

And, lo, the angel of the Lord came upon them, and the glory of the Lord shone round about them. The shepherds covered their faces and drew back, for they were sore afraid. The angel spake unto them, saying, "Fear not: for, behold, I bring you good tidings of great joy, which shall be to all people. For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord."

The angel told them how they should find the babe. "And this shall be a sign unto you," said the angel. "Ye shall find the babe wrapped in swaddling clothes, lying in a manger."

And suddenly there was with the angel a multitude of the heavenly host. There were so many of them. They praised God together, and their voices rang forth across the hills, saying, "Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men."

The shepherds stood and beheld. The sound of those voices was borne over the fields and the resting sheep. Then the angels were gone away from them into heaven. The night grew quiet again.

The shepherds looked one upon another. They said, "Let us now go even unto Bethlehem, and see this thing which is come to pass, which the Lord hath made known unto us." They went with haste. They hastened down the hillside and along the road into the town. Their sandals smote upon the stones. Their staves tapped the ground as they walked.

They found Mary and Joseph. And they beheld the babe lying in a manger, even as the angel had said. The swaddling clothes were wrapped about him. The hay was beneath him. The shepherds stood close and looked upon the little child resting there.

Then the shepherds told Mary and Joseph all things which had been told them concerning this child. They told of the angel and the bright light. They told the words which the angel had spoken. They told of the great multitude of the heavenly host praising God. And all they that heard it wondered at those things which were told them by the shepherds.

But Mary kept all these things, and pondered them in her heart.

The shepherds turned and walked back toward their fields. As they went, they glorified and praised God for all the things that they had heard and seen. Their voices were borne through the cool, still air. The sheep waited on the hillside, resting and safe. And the shepherds returned unto them, still giving thanks unto God.
