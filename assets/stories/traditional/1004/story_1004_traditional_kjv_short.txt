The town of Bethlehem sat upon a hillside. Houses of stone lined the narrow ways. Olive trees grew along the paths without the walls. The air bore the savour of bread baking and of wood smoke. A cool breeze passed through the valley below.

A man called Joseph walked slowly along the road. A young woman named Mary walked beside him. They had journeyed a long way from Nazareth. Caesar Augustus had sent forth a decree. All men were commanded to go unto their own city, that they might be enrolled. Joseph's lineage came of Bethlehem, and thither they went. Mary was great with child, and her time drew nigh. Dust clave unto their sandals from the long journey. They came into the town as the sun turned low and golden.

Joseph sought a place wherein to lodge. But the inn was already full of strangers, and there was no room for them within. So they found a stable, where the beasts of burden rested. Straw covered the ground. A donkey stood near the wall. The air was warm with the breath of the creatures. Mary and Joseph laid themselves down upon the straw.

That same night, Mary brought forth her child. She wrapped him in swaddling clothes. She laid him gently in a manger. The babe's name was Jesus. Joseph stood close beside her. The little one rested quietly upon the soft hay.

Upon the hills nearby, shepherds abode in the field, keeping watch over their flock by night. The stars filled the sky above them. The wool of the sheep shone silver in the moonlight. And lo, the angel of the Lord came upon them, and a bright light shone round about them. The angel spake unto them, saying, "Fear not: for behold, I bring you good tidings of great joy, which shall be unto all people. For unto you is born this day in Bethlehem a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord. And this shall be a sign unto you: ye shall find the babe wrapped in swaddling clothes, lying in a manger."

Then suddenly there was with the angel a multitude of the heavenly host. They praised God together, saying, "Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men." Their voices rang forth across the hills. The shepherds lifted up their eyes and hearkened.

When the angels were gone away from them into heaven, the shepherds said one to another, "Let us now go even unto Bethlehem, and see this thing which is come to pass, which the Lord hath made known unto us." They arose and made haste down the hillside. Their sheep stayed close together upon the grass.

The shepherds found Mary and Joseph in the stable. They beheld the babe lying in a manger, even as the angel had said. They told Mary and Joseph all things which they had seen and heard upon the hillside. Mary kept silent. She kept all these things, and pondered them in her heart.

The shepherds returned unto their flocks beneath the wide, quiet night. They glorified and praised God for all the things they had heard and seen. Their voices echoed softly across the hills. The sheep lifted their eyes as the shepherds returned. The stars yet shone above Bethlehem. The babe slept on in the manger, warm and safe, wrapped close in his swaddling clothes.
