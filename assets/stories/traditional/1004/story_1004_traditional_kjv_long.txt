The hills round about Bethlehem were green and brown in the late afternoon. Olive trees grew in rows along the rocky slopes. Their silver-green leaves moved gently in the cool breeze. Small walls of stone lined the paths between the hills. Wildflowers grew in patches near the walls. The air bore the savour of dry grass and warm earth.

In the town of Bethlehem, the ways were narrow and made of packed dirt. Low buildings of stone sat close together. Wooden doors were propped open. The sound of people speaking drifted from doorways and rooftops. Donkeys stood near water troughs, flicking their ears. Women bare clay jars upon their shoulders. Children sat upon steps of stone, watching the busy road.

There went out a decree from Caesar Augustus, that all the world should be enrolled, and every man go up unto his own city. The people travelled from many places. The roads were full of families journeying together. Some rode upon donkeys. Some bare bundles wrapped in cloth. The dust rose from the road beneath their sandals.

Joseph walked along one of those roads. Mary was with him. She rode upon a donkey. The hooves of the beast clicked upon the stones. Joseph held the rope that guided the animal. He walked slowly that the donkey might keep a steady pace. Mary held the wooden frame of the saddle with both hands. Her robe was dusty from the long way. A cloth covered her head and kept the sun from her face.

They had come from Nazareth, in the country of Galilee. They were going up unto Bethlehem, because Joseph was of the house and lineage of David. The journey was many miles. The road wound through valleys and over dry ridges. Now the town of Bethlehem lay just before them.

Other travellers moved along the road also. Some bare baskets of bread. Others led goats upon short ropes. A man with a cart full of clay vessels rolled past, the wheels bumping over the stones. The road was more thronged than was its wont, because of the decree. All men were going somewhere to be enrolled.

Joseph and Mary came into Bethlehem as the sun moved lower. The buildings glowed warm and golden in the late light. Joseph sought a place wherein they might lodge. He went unto the inn. But the inn was full. There was no room for them therein. Many people had already arrived for the enrolment.

So Joseph found a place where the beasts were kept. It was a sheltered space, quiet and out of the wind. The floor was covered with straw. A wooden manger stood against the wall. It was such a one as is used to hold food for the cattle. The straw within it was dry and clean. A few beasts stood nearby, chewing slowly. Their warm breath made small clouds in the cool air.

Joseph spread a blanket upon the straw. Mary sat down carefully. The place was simple, yet it was dry and still. The thick walls of stone kept the cold without. A small oil lamp flickered upon a ledge, giving a soft yellow glow. The straw rustled when any moved.

And it came to pass, while they were there, the days were accomplished that she should be delivered. And she brought forth her firstborn son. She wrapped him in swaddling clothes. The cloth held his little arms and legs close, and kept him warm. Then she laid him gently in the manger. The straw cushioned him softly. He was small and new. His tiny fingers curled. His eyes blinked in the lamplight.

Joseph stood nigh. Mary rested near the manger. The beasts were quiet. The oil lamp glowed. Without, the evening air was cool and still. The stars began to appear in the wide sky above Bethlehem.

In the countryside nearby, shepherds abode in the field, keeping watch over their flock by night. The sheep lay close together upon the grass. Some of the lambs nestled against their mothers. The shepherds sat near a small fire. The flames crackled softly. The orange light moved across their faces. Their cloaks of wool were wrapped tight about their shoulders.

The night was calm. Crickets sang in the dry grass. A gentle breeze bare the savour of wood smoke and wool. The shepherds spake quietly one to another. One of them held a wooden staff across his knees. Another stirred the fire with a stick, sending tiny sparks upward.

And, lo, the angel of the Lord came upon them, and the glory of the Lord shone round about them. A great, bright light filled the field. It was brighter than the fire. The shepherds could see every blade of grass, every stone, every sheep clearly in that light. Their cloaks, their sandals, even the wool of the sheep all shone in the brightness.

The shepherds drew their cloaks close and stared upon the angel.

And the angel said unto them, "Fear not: for, behold, I bring you good tidings of great joy, which shall be to all people. For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord. And this shall be a sign unto you: ye shall find the babe wrapped in swaddling clothes, lying in a manger."

The shepherds hearkened. The angel's voice was clear in the quiet night.

And suddenly there was with the angel a multitude of the heavenly host. There were so many of them. They filled the sky above the field. Their voices rose together. They praised God, saying, "Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men."

The sound of their voices was everywhere. It filled the hills and the valleys. It moved through the grass and over the stones. The sheep lifted their heads. The lambs stood up upon wobbly legs. The shepherds looked up, and they beheld the sky full of light and heard the voices round about them.

And it came to pass, as the angels were gone away from them into heaven, the sky returned to its quiet, starry stillness. The fire crackled. The crickets began to sing again. The shepherds looked one upon another. Their faces were lit by the remaining glow of the fire.

The shepherds said one to another, "Let us now go even unto Bethlehem, and see this thing which is come to pass, which the Lord hath made known unto us."

They arose. They gathered their staves. They drew their cloaks about their shoulders and began to walk. Their sandals crunched upon the gravel path. The sheep stayed behind in the field, resting upon the grass. The night air was cool upon the shepherds' faces as they walked toward the town.

They walked with haste. The road sloped downward toward Bethlehem. Small houses appeared along the edges of the path. Oil lamps glowed in a few windows. A dog barked somewhere afar off. The shepherds went onward until they reached the place where the beasts were kept.

They came in through the low doorway. They saw Mary and Joseph. And they beheld the babe, lying in the manger, even as the angel had told them. The babe was wrapped in swaddling clothes. He lay upon the straw, small and still. The oil lamp upon the ledge made a warm circle of light round about the manger.

The shepherds came nearer. Their rough sandals scuffed upon the floor of stone. They leaned their staves against the wall. They knelt down near the manger and looked upon the babe. His tiny chest rose and fell with every breath. His small hands rested upon the cloth. Mary sat nearby, watching silently.

The shepherds told Mary and Joseph all things. They told them what the angel had spoken concerning this child. They told them of the bright light in the field. They told them of the great multitude of the heavenly host and the words they had spoken. They told them of the sign — the babe in swaddling clothes, lying in a manger.

And all they that heard it wondered at those things which were told them by the shepherds. But Mary kept all these things, and pondered them in her heart.

After a time, the shepherds arose again. They took up their staves. They stooped back through the low doorway and stepped into the cool night. The air bore the savour of wood smoke and straw.

The shepherds walked back toward their fields. And as they went, they glorified and praised God. Their voices were borne across the quiet hills. They praised God for all the things which they had heard and seen, even as it had been told unto them.

The road stretched out before them under the open sky. Their footsteps were steady upon the path. Behind them, the little town of Bethlehem rested quietly. The oil lamps in the windows burned low. The beasts stood still in their sheltered places.

Back in the manger, the babe slept on upon the soft straw. Mary rested beside him. Joseph sat close by. The oil lamp flickered gently upon its ledge. The night was calm and wide. All was still. All was quiet. And the shepherds' song of praise drifted softly across the hills, growing fainter and fainter, until it was blended with the sound of the wind in the olive trees.
