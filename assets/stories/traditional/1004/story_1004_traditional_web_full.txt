The town of Nazareth sat among green hills. Small stone houses lined the narrow paths. Fig trees grew near doorways. Grapevines climbed along low walls. A warm breeze moved through the leaves. Donkeys stood in the shade, swishing their tails. Women carried clay jars on their shoulders, walking to the well. Children played near the stone steps. The smell of fresh bread drifted from open windows.

In this town, a young woman named Mary lived with Joseph. Joseph was a carpenter. His hands were strong from working with wood. He shaped beams and doors and yokes for plowing. Mary and Joseph were preparing for a long trip. A announcement had gone out from Caesar Augustus. Every person had to travel to the town of their family to be counted. Joseph's family came from Bethlehem, far to the south. So they packed what they needed for the journey.

Mary was going to have a baby very soon. Joseph loaded their things onto a donkey. He tucked a soft blanket into the bundle. Mary walked beside the donkey, and Joseph walked beside her. They followed the dusty road south through the hills. Other travelers moved along the same road, heading to their own family towns. Sandals shuffled on the packed dirt. People called greetings to one another as they passed.

The road went through rocky valleys and past fields of grain. Olive trees dotted the hillsides. Sometimes the travelers stopped to rest near a well. They drank cool water and ate bread and dried figs. Then they continued walking. The journey from Nazareth to Bethlehem was long. It took many days.

At last, Mary and Joseph came over a hill and saw Bethlehem below them. The little town was full of people. Families had come from many places to be counted. Voices filled the streets. Donkeys brayed. Merchants called out, selling food and supplies. Joseph led the donkey through the crowded streets. He looked for a place where they could stay. He went to the inn, but there was no room for them there. Every guest room was already full.

So Joseph found a place where animals were kept. It was a stable, quiet and sheltered. Straw covered the ground. A manger sat against the wall, filled with hay for the animals to eat. The air smelled like dry grass and warm wool. Joseph spread out blankets and made a place for Mary to rest.

And while they were there, the time came for Mary's baby to be born. She gave birth to her first son. She wrapped him snugly in strips of soft cloth. Then she laid him gently in the manger. The hay cushioned him. The cloth kept him warm. Joseph stood nearby. The baby rested in the manger, wrapped and still.

Out in the countryside near Bethlehem, shepherds were watching their flocks. The quiet night stretched wide above them. Stars filled the sky, more than anyone could count. The sheep lay close together on the hillside, their wool thick and warm. Some of the lambs tucked themselves near their mothers. The shepherds sat on the ground or leaned on their staffs. A small fire crackled, sending up a thin curl of smoke. The air was cool and calm.

Then an angel of Yahweh stood near them. A bright, glowing light shone all around. The shepherds covered their faces and drew back. The angel spoke to them. "Do not be nervous," the angel said. "I bring you good news of great joy. It will be for all the people. Today in the town of David, a Savior has been born for you. He is Christ the Lord."

The angel told them how to find the baby. "This will be a sign for you," the angel said. "You will find a baby wrapped in strips of cloth, lying in a manger."

Then suddenly, a great company of angels appeared with the first angel. There were so many of them. They praised God together, and their voices rang out across the hills. "Glory to God in the highest," they said. "And on earth, peace and good will toward people."

The shepherds stood and watched. The sound of those voices carried over the fields and the sleeping sheep. Then the angels went away from them. The night grew quiet again.

The shepherds looked at one another. "Let us go to Bethlehem," they said. "Let us see this thing that has happened, which Yahweh has told us about." They went quickly. They hurried down the hillside and along the road into the town. Their sandals slapped against the stones. Their staffs tapped the ground as they walked.

They found Mary and Joseph. And they found the baby lying in the manger, just as the angel had said. The strips of cloth were wrapped around him. The hay was beneath him. The shepherds stood close and looked at the small baby resting there.

Then the shepherds told Mary and Joseph everything. They told them about the angel and the bright light. They told them the words the angel had spoken. They told them about the great company of angels praising God. Everyone who heard what the shepherds said was amazed.

Mary listened to every word. She kept all these things and treasured them.

The shepherds turned and walked back toward their fields. As they went, they praised God. They spoke aloud about everything they had seen and heard. It was all just as the angel had told them. Their voices carried through the cool, still air. The sheep waited on the hillside, resting and safe. And the shepherds returned to them, still giving thanks to God.
