When the day of Pentecost had come, the disciples were all together in one place. They were of one accord, and the morning was quiet around them. The streets of Jerusalem outside were already filling with travelers and worshipers.

Suddenly there came from the sky a sound like the rushing of a mighty wind, and it filled the whole house where they were sitting. The doors and the lamps trembled at the sound. Then tongues like fire appeared and were distributed among them, and one sat upon each of them. They were all filled with the Holy Spirit, and they began to speak in other languages, as the Spirit gave them the ability to speak.

Now there were Jews dwelling in Jerusalem in those days, devout men from every nation under heaven. When the sound was heard, a great crowd came together and gathered at the house. People left the markets and the temple courts, and they pressed in close to listen. They were bewildered, because each one heard the disciples speaking in his own language.

They were amazed and marveled, and they said to one another, "Are not all these men who speak Galileans? How is it that we each hear them in our own native tongue?"

Among them were Parthians, Medes, Elamites, residents of Mesopotamia, Judea and Cappadocia, Pontus and Asia, Phrygia and Pamphylia, Egypt and the parts of Libya around Cyrene, visitors from Rome, both Jews and proselytes, Cretans and Arabians. They said, "We hear them speaking in our languages the mighty works of God."

The crowd pressed closer along the narrow street, and a murmur ran through the people. They were amazed and perplexed, and they said to one another, "What does this mean?" But others mocked them and said, "They are filled with new wine."
