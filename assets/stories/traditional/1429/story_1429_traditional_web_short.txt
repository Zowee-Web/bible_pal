Josiah the son of Amon was eight years old when he was made king in Jerusalem, in the days after his father had been assassinated in the palace by his servants. The boy was crowned by the people of the land in the place where his great-grandfather Hezekiah had once stood. He grew up in the palace and in the temple courts. The high places of Manasseh his grandfather had been left standing through Amon's brief reign; the Asherah poles still stood in the courts of the temple; the bones of the priests of the high places still rested in their tombs. And in the years that followed, the boy began to seek God earlier than other kings had sought.

Josiah was eight years old when he began to reign, and he reigned thirty-one years in Jerusalem.

He did that which was right in Yahweh's eyes, and walked in the ways of David his father, and didn't turn away to the right hand or to the left.

For in the eighth year of his reign, while he was yet young, he began to seek after the God of David his father; and in the twelfth year he began to purge Judah and Jerusalem from the high places, the Asherah poles, the engraved images, and the molten images.

They broke down the altars of the Baals in his presence; and he cut down the incense altars that were on high above them. He broke the Asherah poles, the engraved images, and the molten images in pieces, made dust of them, and scattered it on the graves of those who had sacrificed to them.

He burned the bones of the priests on their altars, and purged Judah and Jerusalem.

He did this in the cities of Manasseh, Ephraim, and Simeon, even to Naphtali, around in their ruins.

He broke down the altars, and beat the Asherah poles and the engraved images into powder, and cut down all the incense altars throughout all the land of Israel, then returned to Jerusalem.

And the king came back into Jerusalem after the long going-through of the country. The dust of the broken altars had been scattered over the graves of those who had sacrificed at the high places; the high places themselves were torn down; the trees of the Asherah were felled; and the young king who had begun seeking the Lord at sixteen years old returned through the gate of the city in the twelfth year of his reign, with the work of the reformation only begun in the courts above the city of Jerusalem.
