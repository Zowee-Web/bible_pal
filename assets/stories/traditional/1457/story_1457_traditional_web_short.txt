This is what Israel had been commanded to do when they came into the land that Yahweh had given them.

Each man, when he had reaped the first of his harvest, took from the first of all the fruit of the ground that he had gathered in and put it in a basket.

He carried the basket to the place where Yahweh had chosen to cause his name to dwell.

He came to the priest who was there in those days and said, "I have come to the land which Yahweh swore to our fathers to give to us."

The priest took the basket out of his hand and set it down before the altar of Yahweh his God.

And then the man, standing before the altar with his basket no longer in his hand, spoke the words that had been given for that moment.

He said, before Yahweh —

"A wandering Aramean was my father, and he went down into Egypt and lived there, few in number. There he became a great, mighty, and populous nation. The Egyptians dealt ill with us, and afflicted us, and laid on us hard bondage.

We cried to Yahweh, the God of our fathers, and Yahweh heard our voice, and saw our affliction, our toil, and our oppression.

Yahweh brought us out of Egypt with a mighty hand, and with an outstretched arm, and with great terror, and with signs, and with wonders. He has brought us into this place. He has given us this land — a land flowing with milk and honey.

And now, behold, I have brought the first of the fruit of the ground, which you, Yahweh, have given me."

He set down the basket before Yahweh his God, and he worshiped there.

Then he went from the altar to his own house. He rejoiced in all the good which Yahweh his God had given to him, and to his household, and to the Levite and the foreigner who were with him.

The basket would stay at the altar that night.

The fruit of the land was given back to the One who had brought them home.
