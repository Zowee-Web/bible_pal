This is that which Israel had been commanded to do when they came into the land which the Lord had given them.

Every man, when he had reaped the first of his harvest, took of the first of all the fruit of the earth which he had gathered in, and put it in a basket.

He bare the basket unto the place where the Lord had chosen to cause his name to dwell.

He came unto the priest that was in those days, and said, "I am come unto the land which the Lord sware unto our fathers to give us."

The priest took the basket out of his hand, and set it down before the altar of the Lord his God.

And then the man, standing before the altar with his basket no longer in his hand, spake the words that had been given for that moment.

He said, before the Lord —

"A Syrian ready to perish was my father, and he went down into Egypt, and sojourned there with a few. And there he became a nation, great, mighty, and populous. And the Egyptians evil entreated us, and afflicted us, and laid upon us hard bondage.

And when we cried unto the Lord God of our fathers, the Lord heard our voice, and looked on our affliction, and our labour, and our oppression.

And the Lord brought us forth out of Egypt with a mighty hand, and with an outstretched arm, and with great terribleness, and with signs, and with wonders. And he hath brought us into this place. And he hath given us this land, even a land that floweth with milk and honey.

And now, behold, I have brought the firstfruits of the land, which thou, O Lord, hast given me."

He set down the basket before the Lord his God, and he worshipped there.

Then went he from the altar unto his own house. He rejoiced in every good thing which the Lord his God had given unto him, and unto his house, and unto the Levite and the stranger that were among him.

The basket abode at the altar that night.

The fruit of the land was given back unto him that had brought them home.
