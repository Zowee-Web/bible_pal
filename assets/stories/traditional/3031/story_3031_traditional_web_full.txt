Yahweh's word came to Joel, the son of Pethuel, and the prophet sent its sound across a devastated land. He called first to the elders, then to all who inhabited the country. They were to hear and give close attention. Had such a thing happened in their own days? Had their fathers ever seen its like? What stood before them must be carried in memory. Fathers would tell children; those children would tell their children; the testimony would pass onward to another generation.

Joel named the ruin in successive waves. The swarming locust had left only enough for the great locust to consume. What escaped that mouth was eaten by the grasshopper. After the grasshopper, the caterpillar took the remainder. The sequence left no untouched harvest behind it.

He called the drinkers of wine from sleep and commanded them to weep. Those who sought sweet wine were to wail because it had been cut off from their mouths. A host had risen over Joel's land, strong and beyond numbering. The prophet described its teeth as a lion's teeth and its fangs as those of a lioness. It had wasted his vine and stripped his fig tree. Bark was torn away and thrown aside until the uncovered branches showed white.

The land's mourning was to be worn openly, like sackcloth wrapped around a bride mourning the husband of her youth. Worship at Yahweh's house bore the loss. Grain for the meal offering was gone, and there was no wine for the drink offering. The priests, Yahweh's own ministers, mourned because those gifts were withheld.

Beyond the house, the field lay waste and the land itself mourned. Grain was destroyed. New wine had dried up, and oil languished. Farmers were called to shame and vineyard keepers to wailing. Wheat and barley had failed together; the field's harvest had perished. The vine was dry, the fig withered, and the pomegranate, palm, and apple shared the same condition. Every tree in the field had withered. Among the people, joy had withered too.

Joel turned again to the priests and ministers of the altar. They were to put on sackcloth, mourn, and pass the night dressed for lament, because the meal offering and drink offering no longer entered the house of their God. A fast was to be set apart. A solemn assembly was to be called. Elders and every inhabitant would gather at the house of Yahweh their God, and the whole company would cry to Yahweh.

The prophet's lament named the day drawing near: the day of Yahweh was at hand, coming as destruction from the Almighty. The present emptiness lay before their eyes. Food had been cut off. Joy and gladness no longer filled the house of God.

Even what might have supplied another season was failing. Seeds rotted beneath their clods. Granaries stood desolate; barns broke down because the grain had withered. The animals gave voice to the land's distress. Herds of livestock groaned without pasture. Flocks of sheep were left in desolation.

At last Joel himself cried directly to Yahweh. Fire had devoured the wilderness pastures, and flame had burned all the trees of the field. The animals of the field panted toward God because the water brooks had dried up. Fire had consumed the remaining pastures, and the prophecy closed with land, people, priests, and animals gathered into one unrelieved cry to Yahweh.
