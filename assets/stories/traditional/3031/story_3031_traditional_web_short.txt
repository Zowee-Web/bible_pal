Yahweh's word came to Joel son of Pethuel, and it opened with a summons to the land. The elders were told to hear, and every inhabitant was called to listen. Joel asked whether anything like this had happened in their days or in the days of their fathers. The account must not end with those who saw it. They were to tell their children, their children would tell the next generation, and that generation would tell another.

Across the land, one devouring swarm followed another. What the swarming locust left, the great locust consumed. What remained after that was eaten by the grasshopper, and the caterpillar took what was left again. Drinkers of wine were called awake to weep, for sweet wine had been cut off from their mouths. A strong and numberless nation had risen over Joel's land, bearing teeth like a lion and fangs like a lioness. Vines lay ruined. The fig tree had been stripped of bark and cast bare, its branches turned white.

Mourning reached Yahweh's house. Meal and drink offerings were no longer brought, and the priests who ministered there mourned. Fields were wasted; grain was destroyed; new wine dried up; oil failed. Farmers and vineyard keepers were told to wail over wheat, barley, and a harvest that had perished. Vine, fig, pomegranate, palm, apple, and every tree of the field withered. Joy withered among the people as well.

Joel called the priests to sackcloth and a night of lament. He commanded a fast, a solemn assembly, and the gathering of elders and inhabitants at Yahweh's house. Together they were to cry to their God. The day of Yahweh was near, coming with destruction from the Almighty.

Food and gladness had vanished before their eyes. Seed rotted beneath the clods; granaries stood desolate and barns broke down. Cattle groaned without pasture, and sheep suffered with them. Joel's lament became a direct cry to Yahweh. Fire had consumed the wilderness pastures, flames had burned the trees, and the water brooks had dried. Even the animals panted toward God while the scorched land waited beneath the communal cry.
