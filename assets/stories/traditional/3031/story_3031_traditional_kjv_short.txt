The word of the Lord came unto Joel the son of Pethuel, and it began with a summons through the land. The old men were charged to hear, and every inhabitant to give ear. Joel asked whether such a thing had been seen in their days, or in the days of their fathers. The witness must not cease with them. They were to tell it unto their children, their children unto the generation following, and that generation unto another.

Throughout the land, one devourer followed upon another. What the palmerworm left, the locust consumed; what remained after the locust, the cankerworm ate; and the caterpiller finished what was left. The drinkers of wine were called from sleep unto weeping, for the new wine had been taken from their mouths. A nation mighty and without number had come upon Joel's land, having the teeth of a lion and the cheek teeth of a great lion. It hath wasted the vine. The fig tree had been barked and made bare, and the branches thereof were white.

Lamentation entered the house of the Lord. Neither meat offering nor drink offering was brought, and the priests who ministered there mourned. The field was wasted; the corn perished; the wine failed; the oil languished. Husbandmen and vinedressers were bidden to howl for wheat, barley, and the harvest that was gone. Vine and fig, pomegranate and palm, apple tree and every tree of the field withered. Joy also withered away from the sons of men.

Joel called the priests to gird themselves with sackcloth and lie all night in mourning. A fast must be sanctified, a solemn assembly called, and elders and inhabitants gathered into the Lord's house. There they would cry unto their God. The day of the Lord drew nigh, and destruction from the Almighty attended it.

Food and gladness were cut off before their eyes. Seed rotted under the clods; garners lay desolate and barns were broken. Cattle groaned for lack of pasture, and the flocks shared the desolation. Then Joel's lament became a cry unto the Lord. Fire had devoured the wilderness pastures, flame had burned the trees, and rivers of water were dry. The beasts also cried toward God while the consumed land stood beneath the lament of its people.
