The word of the Lord came unto Joel, the son of Pethuel, and the prophet sent its sound throughout a land laid waste. He called first unto the old men, and afterward unto all the inhabitants. They must hear and give diligent ear. Had such a thing been known in their own days? Had their fathers beheld the like? The witness before them must be preserved. Fathers would tell their children; their children would tell the generation following; and afterward the matter would pass unto another generation.

Joel set forth the desolation in successive companies. The palmerworm had left only that which the locust might consume. What escaped the locust was eaten by the cankerworm. After the cankerworm, the caterpiller took the remnant. Thus the swarm hath left no portion of the increase untouched.

He summoned the drinkers of wine from slumber and commanded them to weep. All who desired the new wine were bidden to howl, because it was cut off from their mouths. A nation had ascended upon Joel's land, mighty and without number. Its teeth were likened unto the teeth of a lion, and its cheek teeth unto those of a great lion. It had wasted his vine and barked his fig tree. The covering was stripped away and cast down, until the naked branches thereof were made white.

The mourning of the land must be borne openly, as sackcloth upon a virgin lamenting for the husband of her youth. The loss entered even the house of the Lord. Corn for the meat offering had failed, and wine for the drink offering was no more. Therefore the priests, who ministered before the Lord, mourned for the gifts that were withholden.

Abroad, the field was wasted and the land mourned. The corn was consumed, the new wine dried up, and the oil languished. Husbandmen were called unto shame, and vinedressers unto wailing. Wheat and barley had perished together; the harvest of the field was gone. The vine was dry, the fig tree languished, and pomegranate, palm, and apple stood withered. Every tree of the field shared the waste. Joy also was withered away from the sons of men.

Again Joel addressed the priests and servants of the altar. They must gird themselves with sackcloth, lament, and lie all night in their garments of mourning, for neither meat offering nor drink offering entered the house of their God. A fast must be sanctified. A solemn assembly must be called. The elders and every inhabitant of the land would be gathered into the house of the Lord their God, and the company would cry unto the Lord.

The prophet named the day that drew nigh: the day of the Lord was at hand, and destruction from the Almighty came with it. Their present want was manifest before their eyes. Meat had been cut off, and joy and gladness had departed from the house of God.

Even that which might have yielded another season failed beneath the ground. Seed rotted under the clods. The garners were desolate, and the barns broken down, because the corn was withered. The beasts gave sound unto the anguish of the land. Herds of cattle groaned, perplexed for want of pasture. The flocks of sheep also were made desolate.

At the last Joel himself cried unto the Lord. Fire had devoured the pastures of the wilderness, and flame had burned every tree of the field. The beasts also lifted their need toward God, for the rivers of waters were dried. Fire had consumed the pasture that remained, and the prophecy ended with land, people, priests, and beasts joined beneath one weary cry unto the Lord.
