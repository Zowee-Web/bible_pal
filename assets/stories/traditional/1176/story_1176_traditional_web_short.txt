In the city of Joppa there was a certain disciple named Tabitha, which when translated means Dorcas. This woman was full of good works and acts of mercy which she did. She had clothed the widows of the city with garments she had made, and she had cared for the needy among them.

It happened in those days that she became sick and died. When they had washed her, they laid her in an upper room.

The disciples in Joppa, hearing that Peter was in Lydda nearby, sent two men to him, imploring him not to delay in coming to them. Peter got up and went with them. When he had come, they brought him into the upper room.

All the widows stood by him weeping, and showing the coats and garments which Dorcas had made while she was with them. They held up the garments before him, one after another, and the room was full of their grief.

Peter put them all out, and knelt down and prayed. The room was quiet around him, and he turned to the body, and he said, "Tabitha, get up!"

She opened her eyes, and when she saw Peter, she sat up. He gave her his hand and raised her up. Calling the saints and widows, he presented her alive.

It became known throughout all Joppa, and many believed in the Lord. The widows wept again, but this time their tears were different. They held the coats Dorcas had made, and they did not let go of her, and they followed her down from the upper room into the street.

Peter stayed many days in Joppa with one Simon, a tanner, by the seaside. The morning sun was warm on the streets of Joppa, and the news of what had happened in the upper room went out among the houses, and the people of the city spoke of it for many days.
