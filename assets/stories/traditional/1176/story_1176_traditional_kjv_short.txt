In the city of Joppa there was a certain disciple named Tabitha, which by interpretation is called Dorcas. This woman was full of good works and almsdeeds which she did. She had clothed the widows of the city with garments which she had made, and she had cared for the needy among them.

It came to pass in those days that she fell sick and died. And when they had washed her, they laid her in an upper chamber.

The disciples in Joppa, having heard that Peter was at Lydda hard by, sent unto him two men, desiring him that he would not delay to come unto them. Peter arose and went with them. And when he was come, they brought him into the upper chamber.

All the widows stood by him weeping, and shewing the coats and garments which Dorcas made, while she was with them. They held up the garments before him, one after another, and the chamber was full of their grief.

Peter put them all forth, and kneeled down, and prayed. The chamber was quiet round about him, and turning him to the body, he said, "Tabitha, arise."

And she opened her eyes: and when she saw Peter, she sat up. He gave her his hand and lifted her up. And when he had called the saints and widows, he presented her alive.

And it was known throughout all Joppa; and many believed in the Lord. The widows wept again, but this time their tears were not the same. They held the coats which Dorcas had made, and they would not let her go, and they followed her down from the upper chamber into the street.

Peter tarried many days in Joppa with one Simon a tanner, by the sea side. The morning sun was warm upon the streets of Joppa, and the tidings of that which had come to pass in the upper chamber went forth among the houses, and the people of the city spake of it many days.
