The road from Carmel ran past Shunem, and Elisha walked it often. There was a great woman of that town who constrained him in whensoever he passed by, setting bread before him and pressing him to eat. He came to look for it. She came to watch for him on the road.

One day she spake unto her husband in the cool of the house. "Behold now, I perceive that this is an holy man of God which passeth by us continually. Let us make a little chamber, I pray thee, on the wall, and set for him there a bed, and a table, and a stool, and a candlestick. When he cometh unto us, he shall turn in thither."

So they made it. A little chamber upon the roof. They carried up a bed of carved wood. They set a table against the wall. They placed a stool beside it, and a candlestick in the corner. When Elisha next passed by, he turned aside and went up and lay there.

After a season he called unto Gehazi his servant. "What then is to be done for this woman? She hath been careful for us with all this care."

Gehazi answered, "Verily she hath no son, and her husband is old."

Elisha called her. She stood in the door of the little chamber, her hands folded against her robe. "About this season, according to the time of life," he said, "thou shalt embrace a son."

She lowered her eyes. "Nay, my lord, thou man of God, do not lie unto thine handmaid."

But the woman conceived, and bare a son at that season according to the time of life, as Elisha had said unto her.

The child grew. Upon a morning he went out to his father unto the reapers in the field. The sun rose high. He stood among the sheaves a long while, and then he said, "My head. My head."

His father turned. "Carry him unto his mother."

A lad lifted him and ran. The mother took the boy upon her knees. She held him there till noon. His breath grew small. Then it stopped.

She arose. She carried him up the steps unto the chamber of the man of God. She laid him upon the bed where Elisha had slept, and she shut the door upon him, and went out.

She called unto her husband. "Send me, I pray thee, one of the young men and one of the asses, that I may run to the man of God, and come again."

He frowned, leaning upon the doorframe. "Wherefore wilt thou go to him to day? It is neither new moon, nor sabbath."

"It shall be well," she said.

She saddled the ass herself. She turned unto the servant who would drive. "Drive, and go forward; slack not thy riding for me, except I bid thee."

The road climbed toward Carmel under a hard sun. The dust rose behind them and hung in the still air.

Elisha saw her coming afar off, and spake unto Gehazi. "Behold, yonder is that Shunammite. Run now, I pray thee, to meet her, and say unto her, Is it well with thee? is it well with thy husband? is it well with the child?"

Gehazi ran down to meet her on the road. She gave him three words, low. "It is well."

She came unto Elisha on the hill and caught him by the feet. Gehazi came near to thrust her away. Elisha lifted his hand. "Let her alone; for her soul is vexed within her: and the Lord hath hid it from me, and hath not told me."

She did not let go. "Did I desire a son of my lord? did I not say, Do not deceive me?"

Then Elisha arose. He followed her down from the hill, back along the road unto Shunem, the driver of the ass leading on before.

They came unto the house. They climbed the steps unto the little chamber. The child lay upon the bed where she had laid him, still and small.

Elisha went in, and shut the door upon them twain, and prayed unto the Lord. Then he went up, and lay upon the child. He put his mouth upon his mouth, and his eyes upon his eyes, and his hands upon his hands. He stretched himself upon him. The flesh of the child waxed warm.

Elisha arose and walked once across the chamber. Then he returned unto the bed and stretched himself upon the child again.

The child sneezed seven times. The child opened his eyes.

Elisha called for Gehazi. "Call this Shunammite." When she was come in unto the chamber, he said, "Take up thy son."

She fell at his feet, and bowed herself to the ground. Then she arose, and lifted the child from the bed, and carried him out of the little room on the wall.
