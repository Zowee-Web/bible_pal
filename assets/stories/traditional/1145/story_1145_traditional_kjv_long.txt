The road from Carmel ran past Shunem, and Elisha walked it often. There was a great woman of that town who constrained him in whensoever he passed by, setting bread before him and pressing him to eat. He came to look for it. She came to watch for him on the road.

One day she spake unto her husband in the cool of the house. "Behold now, I perceive that this is an holy man of God which passeth by us continually. Let us make a little chamber, I pray thee, on the wall, and set for him there a bed, and a table, and a stool, and a candlestick. When he cometh unto us, he shall turn in thither."

So they made it. A little chamber upon the roof. They carried up a bed of carved wood. They set a table against the wall. They placed a stool beside it, and a candlestick in the corner. When Elisha next passed by, he turned aside and went up and lay there.

After a season he called unto Gehazi his servant. "What then is to be done for this woman? She hath been careful for us with all this care. Wouldest thou be spoken for to the king, or to the captain of the host?"

Gehazi went down to ask. The woman shook her head. "I dwell among mine own people."

Elisha was quiet a moment. "What then is to be done for her?"

Gehazi answered, "Verily she hath no son, and her husband is old."

Elisha called her. She climbed the steps and stood in the door of the little chamber, her hands folded against her robe. The lamplight fell upon her face. "About this season, according to the time of life," he said, "thou shalt embrace a son."

She lowered her eyes. The promise hung in the small chamber between them. "Nay, my lord, thou man of God, do not lie unto thine handmaid."

But the woman conceived, and bare a son at that season according to the time of life, as Elisha had said unto her.

The child grew. He ran in the courtyard. He sat at the table with his father. He learned the names of the harvest and the names of the rains.

Upon a morning in the time of the reapers, he went out unto his father in the field. The sun rose high. He stood a long while among the bent backs and the rising sheaves. Then he turned. He said, "My head. My head."

His father straightened. "Carry him unto his mother."

A lad lifted him under the arms and ran the path back unto the house. The mother took the boy upon her knees. She brushed his hair from his eyes. She held him there. His breath grew small. The morning passed. At noon, his breath stopped.

She arose, the weight of him in her arms. She climbed the outer steps unto the little chamber on the wall. She laid him upon the bed where Elisha had slept. She drew the candlestick nearer to his face. She turned and shut the door upon him, and went out.

She called down unto her husband from the roof. "Send me, I pray thee, one of the young men and one of the asses, that I may run to the man of God, and come again."

He looked up from the threshing floor, his face shaded by his hand. "Wherefore wilt thou go to him to day? It is neither new moon, nor sabbath."

"It shall be well," she said.

She said no more. She saddled the ass herself, tightening the girth with both hands. She turned unto the servant who would drive. "Drive, and go forward; slack not thy riding for me, except I bid thee."

The road climbed toward Carmel under a hard sun. The dust rose behind them and hung in the still air. The driver of the ass kept the pace she had set. The mother sat straight, her eyes upon the rising ground ahead. She spake not again the whole way.

Elisha was upon the hill above Carmel when he saw the small shape on the road afar off. He spake unto Gehazi without turning. "Behold, yonder is that Shunammite. Run now, I pray thee, to meet her, and say unto her, Is it well with thee? is it well with thy husband? is it well with the child?"

Gehazi went down the path. He met her at the foot of the hill, his breath coming hard. He asked her as he had been bidden.

She gave him three words, low. "It is well."

She did not stop. She came up the hill unto where Elisha stood, and she fell to her knees, and she caught him by the feet. Gehazi came near to thrust her away. Elisha lifted his hand.

"Let her alone; for her soul is vexed within her: and the Lord hath hid it from me, and hath not told me."

She did not let go of his feet. Her voice, when it came, was steady and broken at once. "Did I desire a son of my lord? did I not say, Do not deceive me?"

Elisha turned unto Gehazi. "Gird up thy loins, and take my staff in thine hand, and go thy way: if thou meet any man, salute him not; and if any salute thee, answer him not again: and lay my staff upon the face of the child."

But the mother said, "As the Lord liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee."

So Elisha arose. He followed her down from the hill. Gehazi went on before them, the staff held across his body, running.

The road back to Shunem was the same road she had come, but longer in the returning. The sun moved down the sky. The dust rose. She slackened not, and Elisha slackened not with her.

Before they came unto the house, Gehazi came again to meet them on the road. His face was drawn. He stood before Elisha and shook his head. "I laid the staff upon the face of the child. There was neither voice, nor hearing. The child hath not awaked."

The mother did not stop walking. She passed him on the road and went on.

When Elisha was come into the house, the child lay upon the bed in the little chamber, just as she had left him. The candlestick still burned in the corner. The stool stood beside the table. The covering of the bed had not been stirred.

Elisha went in alone. He shut the door upon them twain, and prayed unto the Lord.

Then he went up, and lay upon the child. He put his mouth upon his mouth, and his eyes upon his eyes, and his hands upon his hands. He stretched himself out upon him. The flesh of the child waxed warm beneath him.

Elisha arose. He walked once across the chamber, slowly, his eyes upon the floor, his lips moving. He came to the wall and turned. He walked back. Then he went up, and stretched himself upon the child a second time.

The child sneezed seven times. The child opened his eyes.

Elisha straightened. He went unto the door and called for Gehazi. "Call this Shunammite."

Gehazi went down. The mother climbed the steps unto the chamber. She stopped in the door. The lamplight fell upon the bed. The child sat blinking in it.

Elisha said, "Take up thy son."

She fell at his feet. She bowed herself to the ground. Her shoulders shook once, and then were still. After a long moment she arose, and went unto the bed, and lifted the child into her arms. He was warm against her. She turned, and carried him out of the little room on the wall.
