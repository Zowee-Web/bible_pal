The road from Carmel ran past Shunem, and Elisha walked it often. There was a great woman of that town who urged him in whenever he passed by, setting bread before him and pressing him to eat. He came to expect it. She came to watch for him on the road.

One day she spoke to her husband in the cool of the house. "I perceive that this is a holy man of God who passes by us continually. Let us make a small chamber on the wall, and set for him a bed, a table, a chair, and a lamp. When he comes to us, he can stay there."

So they built it. A little room on the roof. They carried up a bed of carved wood. They set a table against the wall. They placed a chair beside it, and a lampstand in the corner. When Elisha next passed by, he turned aside and went up and lay there.

After some time he called Gehazi his servant. "What is to be done for this woman? She has cared for us with all this care."

Gehazi answered, "She has no son, and her husband is old."

Elisha called her. She stood in the doorway of the little chamber, her hands folded against her robe. "At this season next year," he said, "you will embrace a son."

She lowered her eyes. "No, my lord, you man of God, do not lie to your servant."

But the woman conceived, and at that season the next year she bore a son, as Elisha had said.

The child grew. One morning he went out to his father among the reapers in the field. The sun rose high. He stood among the sheaves a long while, and then he said, "My head. My head."

His father turned. "Carry him to his mother."

A servant lifted him and ran. The mother took the boy onto her lap. She held him there till noon. His breath grew small. Then it stopped.

She rose. She carried him up the steps to the man of God's chamber. She laid him on the bed where Elisha had slept, and she shut the door upon him, and she went out.

She called to her husband. "Send me one of the servants and one of the donkeys, that I may run to the man of God and come again."

He frowned, leaning on the doorframe. "Why would you go to him today? It is not a new moon or a Sabbath."

"It is well," she said.

She saddled the donkey herself. She turned to the servant who would drive. "Drive. Do not slow down for me, unless I tell you."

The road climbed toward Carmel under a hard sun. The dust rose behind them and hung in the still air.

Elisha saw her coming from far off and spoke to Gehazi. "Behold, the Shunammite. Run now to meet her. Ask her, is it well with her, with her husband, with the child?"

Gehazi ran down to meet her on the road. She gave him three words, low. "It is well."

She came to Elisha on the hill and caught hold of his feet. Gehazi reached to pull her back. Elisha lifted a hand. "Leave her alone. Her soul is troubled within her, and the Lord has hidden it from me, and has not told me."

She did not let go. "Did I ask my lord for a son? Did I not say, do not deceive me?"

Elisha rose then. He followed her down from the hill, back along the road to Shunem, the donkey driver leading on ahead.

They came to the house. They climbed the steps to the little chamber. The child lay on the bed where she had laid him, still and small.

Elisha went in, and he shut the door upon them both, and he prayed to the Lord. Then he went up onto the bed and lay upon the child. He put his mouth on his mouth, and his eyes on his eyes, and his hands on his hands. He stretched himself upon him. The flesh of the child grew warm.

Elisha rose and walked once across the room. Then he went back to the bed and stretched himself upon the child again.

The child sneezed seven times. The child opened his eyes.

Elisha called for Gehazi. "Call this Shunammite." When she came in to the chamber, he said, "Take up your son."

She fell at his feet. She bowed her face to the ground. Then she rose, and lifted the child from the bed, and carried him out of the little room on the wall.
