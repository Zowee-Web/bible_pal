The road from Carmel ran past Shunem, and Elisha walked it often. There was a great woman of that town who urged him in whenever he passed by, setting bread before him and pressing him to eat. He came to expect it. She came to watch for him on the road.

One day she spoke to her husband in the cool of the house. "I perceive that this is a holy man of God who passes by us continually. Let us make a small chamber on the wall, and set for him a bed, a table, a chair, and a lamp. When he comes to us, he can stay there."

So they built it. A little room on the roof. They carried up a bed of carved wood. They set a table against the wall. They placed a chair beside it, and a lampstand in the corner. When Elisha next passed by, he turned aside and went up and lay there.

After some time he called Gehazi his servant. "What is to be done for this woman? She has cared for us with all this care. Would she be spoken for to the king, or to the captain of the army?"

Gehazi went down to ask. The woman shook her head. "I dwell among my own people."

Elisha was quiet a moment. "What then is to be done for her?"

Gehazi answered, "She has no son, and her husband is old."

Elisha called her. She climbed the steps and stood in the doorway of the little chamber, her hands folded against her robe. The lamplight fell on her face. "At this season next year," he said, "you will embrace a son."

She lowered her eyes. The promise hung in the small room between them. "No, my lord, you man of God, do not lie to your servant."

But the woman conceived, and at that season the next year she bore a son, as Elisha had said to her.

The child grew. He ran in the courtyard. He sat at the table with his father. He learned the names of the harvest and the names of the rains.

One morning at the time of the reapers, he went out to his father in the field. The sun rose high. He stood a long while among the bent backs and the rising sheaves. Then he turned. He said, "My head. My head."

His father straightened. "Carry him to his mother."

A servant lifted him under the arms and ran the path back to the house. The mother took the boy onto her lap. She brushed his hair from his eyes. She held him there. His breath grew small. The morning passed. At noon, his breath stopped.

She rose, the weight of him in her arms. She climbed the outer steps to the little chamber on the wall. She laid him on the bed where Elisha had slept. She drew the lamp closer to his face. She turned and shut the door upon him, and she went out.

She called down to her husband from the roof. "Send me one of the servants and one of the donkeys, that I may run to the man of God and come again."

He looked up from the threshing floor, his face shaded by his hand. "Why would you go to him today? It is not a new moon or a Sabbath."

"It is well," she said.

She did not say more. She saddled the donkey herself, tightening the girth with both hands. She turned to the servant who would drive. "Drive. Do not slow down for me, unless I tell you."

The road climbed toward Carmel under a hard sun. The dust rose behind them and hung in the still air. The donkey driver kept the pace she had set. The mother sat straight, her eyes on the rising ground ahead. She did not speak again the whole way.

Elisha was on the hill above Carmel when he saw the small shape on the road far below. He spoke to Gehazi without turning. "Behold, the Shunammite. Run now to meet her. Ask her, is it well with her, is it well with her husband, is it well with the child?"

Gehazi went down the path. He met her at the foot of the hill, his breath coming hard. He asked as he had been told.

She gave him three words, low. "It is well."

She did not stop. She came up the hill to where Elisha stood, and she dropped to her knees, and she caught hold of his feet. Gehazi reached down to pull her back. Elisha lifted a hand.

"Leave her alone. Her soul is troubled within her, and the Lord has hidden it from me, and has not told me."

She did not let go of his feet. Her voice, when it came, was steady and broken at once. "Did I ask my lord for a son? Did I not say, do not deceive me?"

Elisha turned to Gehazi. "Tuck your cloak into your belt. Take my staff in your hand, and go your way. If you meet any man, do not greet him. If anyone greets you, do not answer him. Then lay my staff on the face of the child."

But the mother said, "As the Lord lives, and as your soul lives, I will not leave you."

So Elisha rose. He followed her down from the hill. Gehazi went on ahead of them, the staff held across his body, running.

The road back to Shunem was the same road she had come, but longer in returning. The sun moved down the sky. The dust rose. She did not slow, and Elisha did not slow with her.

Before they reached the house, Gehazi came back to meet them on the road. His face was drawn. He stood in front of Elisha and shook his head. "I laid the staff on the child's face. There was no voice. There was no hearing. The child has not awakened."

The mother did not stop walking. She passed him on the road and went on.

When Elisha came into the house, the child lay on the bed in the little chamber, just as she had left him. The lamp still burned in the corner. The chair stood beside the table. The blanket of the bed had not been moved.

Elisha went in alone. He shut the door upon them both, and he prayed to the Lord.

Then he went up onto the bed and lay down upon the child. He put his mouth on his mouth, and his eyes on his eyes, and his hands on his hands. He stretched himself out upon him. The flesh of the child grew warm beneath him.

Elisha rose. He walked once across the room, slowly, his eyes on the floor, his lips moving. He came to the wall and turned. He walked back. Then he went up onto the bed and stretched himself upon the child a second time.

The child sneezed seven times. The child opened his eyes.

Elisha straightened. He went to the door and called for Gehazi. "Call this Shunammite."

Gehazi went down. The mother climbed the steps to the chamber. She stopped in the doorway. The lamplight fell on the bed. The child sat blinking in it.

Elisha said, "Take up your son."

She fell at his feet. She bowed her face to the ground. Her shoulders shook once, and then were still. After a long moment she rose, and she went to the bed, and she lifted the child into her arms. He was warm against her. She turned, and she carried him out of the little room on the wall.
