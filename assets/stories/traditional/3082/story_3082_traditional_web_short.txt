Jehoshaphat succeeded his father Asa as king and strengthened himself against Israel. He stationed forces in Judah’s fortified cities and set garrisons throughout Judah and in the cities of Ephraim that Asa had taken.

Yahweh was with Jehoshaphat. The king walked in the first ways of his father David, did not seek the Baals, and sought the God of his father. He followed God’s commandments rather than the ways of Israel. Yahweh established the kingdom in his hand. All Judah brought Jehoshaphat tribute, and he possessed riches and honor in abundance. His heart was lifted up in the ways of Yahweh, and he removed the high places and Asherah poles from Judah.

In the third year of his reign, Jehoshaphat sent five princes to teach in Judah’s cities: Ben Hail, Obadiah, Zechariah, Nethanel, and Micaiah. With them went the Levites Shemaiah, Nethaniah, Zebadiah, Asahel, Shemiramoth, Jehonathan, Adonijah, Tobijah, and Tobadonijah. The priests Elishama and Jehoram also went. They carried the book of Yahweh’s law, traveled throughout Judah’s cities, and taught among the people.

The fear of Yahweh fell upon the kingdoms surrounding Judah, and they made no war against Jehoshaphat. Some Philistines brought presents and silver as tribute. Arabians brought flocks numbering seven thousand seven hundred rams and seven thousand seven hundred male goats.

Jehoshaphat grew exceedingly great. He built fortresses and store cities in Judah and had many works in Judah’s cities. Jerusalem held mighty men of valor. From Judah, Adnah commanded three hundred thousand; Jehohanan commanded two hundred eighty thousand; and Amasiah son of Zichri, who willingly offered himself to Yahweh, commanded two hundred thousand.

From Benjamin, Eliada, a mighty man of valor, had two hundred thousand armed with bow and shield. Jehozabad had one hundred eighty thousand ready and prepared for war. These men waited on the king in addition to the forces he had stationed in fortified cities throughout Judah.
