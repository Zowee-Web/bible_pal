Jehoshaphat reigned in the stead of Asa his father and strengthened himself against Israel. He placed forces in all the fenced cities of Judah. He also set garrisons in Judah and in the cities of Ephraim which Asa had taken.

The Lord was with Jehoshaphat. He walked in the first ways of his father David, sought not unto Baalim, but sought the Lord God of his father. He walked in the Lord’s commandments and not after the doings of Israel. The Lord stablished the kingdom in his hand. All Judah brought him presents, and riches and honour were his in abundance. His heart was lifted up in the ways of the Lord; moreover, he removed the high places and groves out of Judah.

In the third year of his reign, Jehoshaphat sent princes to teach in the cities of Judah: Ben-hail, Obadiah, Zechariah, Nethaneel, and Michaiah. With them he sent the Levites Shemaiah, Nethaniah, Zebadiah, Asahel, Shemiramoth, Jehonathan, Adonijah, Tobijah, and Tob-adonijah. Elishama and Jehoram, the priests, accompanied them. They had the book of the law of the Lord with them, went throughout Judah’s cities, and taught the people.

The fear of the Lord fell upon the kingdoms round about Judah, and they made no war against Jehoshaphat. Some Philistines brought presents and tribute silver. The Arabians brought seven thousand and seven hundred rams and the same number of he goats.

Jehoshaphat waxed great exceedingly. He built castles and cities of store in Judah and had much business in Judah’s cities. Mighty men of valour were in Jerusalem. Of Judah, Adnah the chief had three hundred thousand; Jehohanan had two hundred and fourscore thousand; and Amasiah the son of Zichri, who willingly offered himself unto the Lord, had two hundred thousand.

Of Benjamin, Eliada, a mighty man of valour, had two hundred thousand armed with bow and shield. Jehozabad had an hundred and fourscore thousand ready prepared for war. These waited on the king, beside those whom he placed in fenced cities throughout Judah.
