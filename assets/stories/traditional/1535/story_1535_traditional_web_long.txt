It was night. The last night before morning. In the morning Herod intended to bring Peter out before the people. Inside the prison Peter lay sleeping between two soldiers. A chain ran from his left wrist to the soldier on one side. Another chain ran from his right wrist to the soldier on the other. His breathing was steady and slow. The soldiers' breathing matched his, rising and falling in the dark. The guards at the door stood in their positions, watching the corridor. A torch burned somewhere down the passage, and the light of it fell only as far as the doorway, leaving the inner cell in shadow.

Then light filled the cell. It came all at once, pressing into every crack in the stone walls, flooding the floor, touching the faces of the sleeping soldiers. An angel of the Lord stood over Peter. The angel struck Peter on his side and raised him up. "Get up quickly," the angel said.

The chains fell from Peter's wrists. They dropped against the stone and lay there, open. The iron made a dull sound as it settled. The soldiers did not stir. Their heads remained where they had been. Their hands did not move.

"Put on your belt," the angel said. Peter fastened it around his waist. "Tie on your sandals." Peter bent and tied them, his fingers working the leather straps. "Wrap your cloak around you and follow me."

Peter pulled the cloak over his shoulders and followed the angel out of the cell. His feet moved over the cold floor. He passed the place where the soldiers slept. He did not look back.

They came to the first guard post. They passed through it. They came to the second guard post. They passed through it. No one called out. No one moved to stop them. The men stationed there stayed at their posts as if the night had not changed.

Then they reached the iron gate. It was the gate that led into the city itself — heavy, set into the wall, banded with iron straps. It opened before them on its own. No hand touched it. The hinges turned and the gate swung wide, and the cool air of the street came through, carrying the smell of stone and night and far-off smoke.

They stepped out. The stones of the street were under Peter's sandals. The walls of buildings rose on either side, dark and quiet. A dog barked somewhere far off and then was still. They walked the length of one street together, the angel just ahead, and then the angel was gone. The street was empty. Peter stood alone.

The air moved against his face. He looked down at his wrists. The skin where the iron had pressed was bare. He looked at the street around him, the walls, the sky overhead with its scattering of stars. He was outside the prison. He was standing in the city.

"Now I know for certain," Peter said. "The Lord has sent his angel and rescued me from the hand of Herod and from everything the Jewish people were expecting."

He moved quickly through the streets. He kept close to the walls. He came to the house of Mary, the mother of John who was also called Mark. Inside, many had gathered. They were praying. The room was full of low voices and the smell of oil lamps burning. The sound of the prayers drifted out into the courtyard, a steady murmur in the dark.

Peter knocked at the door of the outer gate. The sound was small at first. He waited. A servant girl came to listen. Her name was Rhoda. She pressed close to the gate to hear, and when she recognized Peter's voice she did not open it. She turned and ran back inside, her sandals slapping the courtyard stones. She did not stop to look behind her.

"Peter is standing at the gate," she said.

The voices in the room broke off. They looked at her. "You are out of your mind," they said.

She insisted. She said it again. She would not back down. She stood in front of them and held to what she had heard.

"It is his angel," they said.

Outside, Peter kept knocking. The sound carried through the courtyard — knuckles on wood, steady and repeated. It did not stop. After a time they went to the gate. They pulled it open.

Peter stood in the doorway. The lamplight from inside fell across his face and his cloak and his sandals. They stared at him. No one spoke. Their mouths were open. One of them took a step back. Another reached out as if to touch his arm and then did not.

He lifted his hand and gestured for them to be silent. Then he told them everything — how the Lord had brought him out of the prison. He told them about the light and the angel and the chains falling away. He told them about the first guard post and the second. He told them about the iron gate swinging open on its own and the empty street where the angel had left him.

"Go tell these things to James and to the brothers," he said.

Then he left. He went out from the house and departed to another place. The gate closed behind him, and his footsteps moved off down the street and were lost.

When morning came, the sun hit the prison walls. The light moved down the stone toward the cells. The soldiers woke to an empty space between them. They looked at the floor. Chains lay there, still locked to their wrists on one end and open on the other. They looked at one another. They looked at the place where Peter had been. They got up and searched the cell. They searched the corridor. They called to the guards at the door.

There was serious confusion among them — no small disturbance — over what had become of Peter.

Herod sent for Peter and he was not found. He sent men to search again. They came back without him. He questioned the guards who had kept the prison. He examined them closely. He asked them what they had seen and what they had heard. They had nothing to give him. Then he ordered them taken away and put to death.
