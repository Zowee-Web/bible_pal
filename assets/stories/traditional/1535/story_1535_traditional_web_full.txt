It was night. The last night. In the morning Herod intended to bring Peter out before the people. Inside the prison Peter lay sleeping between two soldiers. A chain ran from his left wrist to the soldier on one side. Another chain ran from his right wrist to the soldier on the other. His breathing was steady. The guards at the door stood in their positions, watching the corridor.

Then light filled the cell. It came all at once, pressing into every crack in the stone walls, flooding the floor, touching the faces of the sleeping soldiers. An angel of the Lord stood over Peter. The angel struck Peter on his side and raised him up. "Get up quickly," the angel said.

The chains fell from Peter's wrists. They dropped against the stone and lay there, open. The soldiers did not stir.

"Put on your belt," the angel said. Peter fastened it. "Tie on your sandals." Peter bent and tied them. "Wrap your cloak around you and follow me."

Peter pulled the cloak over his shoulders and followed the angel out of the cell. His feet moved over the cold floor. He passed the place where the soldiers slept.

They came to the first guard post. They passed through it. They came to the second guard post. They passed through it. No one called out. No one moved to stop them.

Then they reached the iron gate. It was the gate that led into the city itself — heavy, set into the wall. It opened before them on its own. No hand touched it. The hinges turned and the gate swung wide, and the cool air of the street came through.

They stepped out. The stones of the street were under Peter's sandals. The walls of buildings rose on either side, dark and quiet. They walked the length of one street together, the angel just ahead, and then the angel was gone. The street was empty. Peter stood alone.

The air moved against his face. He looked down at his wrists. The skin where the iron had pressed was bare. He looked at the street around him, the walls, the sky overhead. He was outside the prison. He was standing in the city.

"Now I know for certain," Peter said. "The Lord has sent his angel and rescued me from the hand of Herod and from everything the Jewish people were expecting."

He moved quickly through the streets. He came to the house of Mary, the mother of John who was also called Mark. Inside, many had gathered. They were praying. The room was full of low voices and the smell of oil lamps burning.

Peter knocked at the door of the outer gate. A servant girl came to listen. Her name was Rhoda. She pressed close to the gate to hear, and when she recognized Peter's voice she did not open it. She turned and ran back inside, her sandals slapping the courtyard stones.

"Peter is standing at the gate," she said.

They looked at her. "You are out of your mind," they said.

She insisted. She said it again. She would not back down.

"It is his angel," they said.

Outside, Peter kept knocking. The sound carried through the courtyard — knuckles on wood, steady and repeated. They went to the gate. They pulled it open.

Peter stood in the doorway. The lamplight from inside fell across his face and his cloak and his sandals. They stared at him. No one spoke. Their mouths were open.

He lifted his hand and gestured for them to be silent. Then he told them everything — how the Lord had brought him out of the prison. He told them about the light and the angel and the chains falling away. He told them about the iron gate swinging open on its own.

"Go tell these things to James and to the brothers," he said.

Then he left. He went out from the house and departed to another place.

When morning came, the sun hit the prison walls and the soldiers woke to an empty space between them. Chains lay on the floor, still locked to their wrists on one end and open on the other. There was serious confusion among them — no small disturbance — over what had become of Peter.

Herod sent for Peter and he was not found. He questioned the guards who had kept the prison. He examined them closely. Then he ordered them taken away and put to death.
