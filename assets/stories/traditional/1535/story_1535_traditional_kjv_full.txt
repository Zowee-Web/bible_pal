It was night. The last night. For when the morning came, Herod purposed to bring Peter forth before the people. And within the prison Peter lay sleeping between two soldiers. A chain bound his left wrist unto the one that sat beside him, and another chain bound his right wrist unto the other. His breathing was steady and slow. And the guards before the door stood at their posts, keeping watch upon the corridor.

Then did light fill the cell. It came all at once, pressing into every crack of the stone, flooding the floor, falling upon the faces of the soldiers where they slept. And an angel of the Lord stood over Peter. The angel smote him upon the side and raised him up. "Arise," he said. "Arise quickly."

And the chains fell from Peter's wrists. They dropped upon the stone and lay there, open. The soldiers did not stir.

"Gird thyself," the angel said. And Peter fastened his belt. "Bind on thy sandals." And Peter stooped and bound them. "Cast thy cloak about thee, and come after me."

And Peter drew the cloak over his shoulders and followed the angel out from the cell. His feet passed over the cold stone. He went by the place where the soldiers lay sleeping.

They came unto the first ward. They passed through it. They came unto the second ward. They passed through it also. No voice was raised against them. No man moved to stay them.

Then they came unto the iron gate — the gate that opened into the city itself, heavy and set deep within the wall. And it opened before them of its own accord. No hand touched it. The hinges turned and the gate swung wide, and the cool air of the street came through unto them.

And they went out. The stones of the street were beneath Peter's sandals. The walls of dwellings rose up on either side, dark and still. They walked together the length of one street, the angel going before him, and then the angel was gone. The street was empty. Peter stood alone.

The night air moved against his face. He looked down upon his wrists. The skin where the iron had pressed was bare. He looked upon the street about him, and upon the walls, and upon the sky above. He was without the prison. He stood in the city.

And Peter said, "Now I know of a surety that the Lord hath sent forth his angel and hath delivered me out from the hand of Herod, and from all that the people looked to see done unto me."

He went swiftly through the streets. And he came unto the house of Mary, the mother of John who was called also Mark. And within that house many were gathered together. They were in prayer. The room was full of low voices, and the smell of oil lamps burning.

And Peter knocked at the door of the outer gate. A servant girl came to hearken. Her name was Rhoda. She pressed close unto the gate to hear, and when she knew Peter's voice she opened not the gate for gladness, but turned and ran back within, her sandals striking upon the courtyard stones.

"Peter standeth at the gate," she said.

They looked upon her. "Thou art beside thyself," they said.

But she affirmed it strongly. She spoke it again, and she would not be turned aside.

"It is his angel," they said.

And without, Peter continued knocking. The sound carried through the courtyard — knuckles upon wood, steady and unhurried. They went unto the gate. They drew it open.

And Peter stood in the doorway. The lamplight from within fell upon his face, and upon his cloak, and upon his sandals. They looked upon him and were astonished. No one spake. Their mouths were opened.

He lifted his hand and beckoned unto them to hold their peace. Then he declared unto them all that had come to pass — how the Lord had brought him forth out of the prison. He told them of the light and of the angel and of the chains that fell away. He told them of the iron gate that swung open of itself.

"Go," he said. "Carry these things unto James, and unto the brethren."

Then he departed. He went out from the house and removed himself unto another place.

And when the morning was come, the sun fell upon the prison walls, and the soldiers woke to an empty place between them. Chains lay upon the floor, yet fastened unto their wrists at one end and open at the other. And there was no small stir among them — great trouble and confusion — over what had become of Peter.

Herod sent to bring Peter forth, and he was not found. He examined the guards who had kept the prison. He questioned them straitly. Then he commanded that they be led away and put to death.
