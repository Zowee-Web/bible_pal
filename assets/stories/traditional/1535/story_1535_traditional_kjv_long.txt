It was night, the last night before the morning. In the morning Herod purposed to bring Peter forth before the people. Within the prison Peter lay sleeping between two soldiers. A chain ran from his left wrist unto the soldier on the one side, and a chain from his right wrist unto the soldier on the other. His breath came slow and steady. The breath of the soldiers rose and fell with his own in the dark. At the door the guards kept their watch over the corridor. A torch burned somewhere down the passage, and the light thereof reached only unto the doorway, and the inner cell lay in shadow.

Then came the light. It filled the cell on a sudden, pressing into every crack of the stone, flooding the floor, falling upon the faces of the sleeping men. An angel of the Lord stood over Peter. He smote him on the side and raised him up. "Arise quickly," said the angel.

And behold, the chains fell from Peter's wrists. They dropped upon the stone and lay there, open. The iron made a dull sound as it settled. Yet the soldiers stirred not. Their heads remained as they had been. Their hands moved not at all.

"Gird thyself," said the angel. Peter fastened the belt about his waist. "Bind on thy sandals." Peter bent down and bound them, his fingers working at the leather. "Cast thy cloak about thee, and follow me."

Peter drew the cloak over his shoulders and followed the angel forth out of the cell. His feet passed over the cold floor. He went by the place where the soldiers slept. He looked not back.

They came unto the first ward, and passed through it. They came unto the second ward, and passed through it also. No man cried out. No man moved to stay them. The men at their stations kept their watch as though the night were unchanged.

Then came they unto the iron gate, even the gate that openeth into the city — heavy it was, set deep into the wall, bound about with bands of iron. And it opened before them of its own accord. No hand was laid upon it. The hinges turned, and the gate swung wide, and the cool air of the street came in unto them, bearing the smell of stone and of night and of smoke from afar.

They went out. The stones of the street were beneath Peter's feet. The walls of the houses rose up on either side, dark and still. A dog barked somewhere far off, and was silent again. They walked the length of one street together, the angel going on before, and then the angel was no more there. The street was empty. Peter stood alone.

The air moved upon his face. He looked down upon his wrists. The skin where the iron had pressed was bare. He looked upon the street about him, and the walls, and the sky overhead with its scattering of stars. He was without the prison. He was standing in the city.

"Now I know of a surety," said Peter, "that the Lord hath sent forth his angel, and hath delivered me out of the hand of Herod, and from all that the people of the Jews looked for."

He went quickly through the streets. He kept close unto the walls. He came unto the house of Mary, the mother of John whose surname was Mark. Within, many were gathered together, and they were praying. The room was full of low voices and the smell of oil lamps burning. The sound of their prayers drifted out into the courtyard, a steady murmur in the dark.

Peter knocked at the door of the outer gate. The sound was small at the first. He waited. A damsel came to hearken, whose name was Rhoda. She drew near unto the gate to listen, and when she knew Peter's voice she opened not the gate. She turned and ran back within, her sandals slapping upon the stones of the court. She stayed not to look behind her.

"Peter standeth at the gate," she said.

The voices in the chamber brake off. They looked upon her. "Thou art beside thyself," they said.

But she stood fast. She said it again. She would not be moved. She stood before them and held to that which she had heard.

"It is his angel," they said.

Without, Peter continued knocking. The sound carried through the courtyard — knuckles upon wood, steady and unceasing. It ceased not. After a season they went unto the gate. They drew it open.

Peter stood in the doorway. The lamplight from within fell upon his face, and upon his cloak, and upon his sandals. They beheld him. No man spake. Their mouths stood open. One of them stepped back. Another reached forth as though to touch his arm, and did not.

He lifted up his hand and beckoned unto them to hold their peace. Then he told them all — how the Lord had brought him forth out of the prison. He told them of the light, and of the angel, and of the chains falling away. He told them of the first ward and of the second. He told them of the iron gate that swung wide of itself, and of the empty street where the angel had left him.

"Go shew these things unto James, and unto the brethren," he said.

Then he departed. He went forth out of the house and went unto another place. The gate closed behind him, and the sound of his feet moved off down the street, and was lost.

When the morning was come, the sun fell upon the walls of the prison. The light moved down the stone toward the cells. The soldiers awoke unto an empty space between them. They looked upon the floor. The chains lay there, fastened still unto their wrists on the one end, and open on the other. They looked one upon another. They looked upon the place where Peter had been. They arose and searched the cell. They searched the corridor. They called unto the guards at the door.

And there was no small stir among them concerning what was become of Peter.

Then Herod sent for Peter, and he was not found. He sent men to search again. They came back without him. He called the keepers of the prison before him and questioned them closely. He demanded of them what they had seen and what they had heard. They had nothing to render unto him. Then commanded he that they should be led away and put to death.
