It was night. Peter lay sleeping between two soldiers, a chain on each wrist binding him to the men on either side. Guards stood at the door of the cell. Beyond the prison walls, Herod waited for morning to bring Peter out before the people.

A light filled the cell. An angel of the Lord stood there, and the darkness pulled back from every corner. The angel struck Peter on the side and raised him up. "Get up quickly," the angel said. The chains slid off Peter's wrists and hit the stone floor.

"Put on your belt," the angel said. "Tie on your sandals. Wrap your cloak around you and follow me."

Peter bound his belt, tied on his sandals, and wrapped his cloak around himself. He followed the angel out of the cell, moving slowly, his eyes wide and unfocused, like a man walking in a dream.

They passed the first guard post. They passed the second. They came to the iron gate that opened onto the city, and it swung open by itself. No hand touched it. They walked through and continued down one street, and then the angel was gone.

Peter stood alone in the dark. The night air was on his face. He looked at his hands, at the empty places where the chains had been. "Now I know for certain," he said. "The Lord has sent his angel and rescued me from the hand of Herod and from everything the Jewish people were expecting."

He went to the house of Mary, the mother of John. Many were gathered inside, praying. Peter knocked at the outer gate. A servant girl named Rhoda came to answer. When she recognized his voice, she did not open the gate. She ran back inside and told them Peter was standing outside.

"You are out of your mind," they said. She kept insisting. "It is his angel," they said.

Peter kept knocking. They opened the gate and saw him. Their faces went still with shock. He raised his hand and motioned for them to be quiet. He told them how the Lord had brought him out of the prison. "Go tell these things to James and to the brothers," he said. Then he left and went to another place.

When daylight came, there was serious confusion among the soldiers over what had become of Peter. Herod searched for him and did not find him. He questioned the guards who had kept the prison. Then he ordered them put to death.
