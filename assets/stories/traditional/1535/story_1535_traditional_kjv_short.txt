It was night. And Peter lay sleeping between two soldiers, bound unto them by chains upon each wrist, one chain to the man at his left hand and one to the man at his right. Guards were set at the door of the cell. And beyond the prison walls, Herod waited for the morning, that he might bring Peter forth before the people.

And behold, a light filled the cell. An angel of the Lord stood in that place, and the darkness drew back from every corner. The angel smote Peter upon his side and raised him up. "Rise quickly," he said unto him. And the chains loosed themselves from Peter's wrists and fell upon the stone floor.

"Gird thyself," the angel said unto him. "Bind on thy sandals. Take thy cloak about thee and follow me."

And Peter girded himself, and bound on his sandals, and drew his cloak about him. He followed the angel out of the cell, moving slowly, his eyes wide and seeing nothing clearly, as a man that walketh in a dream.

They passed the first ward. They passed the second. And they came unto the great gate of iron which openeth upon the city, and it swung wide of itself. No hand touched it. They went forth together and passed down one street, and then the angel was gone from him.

And Peter stood alone in the darkness. The night air was upon his face. He looked down upon his hands, upon the bare places where the chains had lain. "Now I know of a surety," he said, "that the Lord hath sent his angel and hath delivered me from the hand of Herod, and from all that the people looked to see done unto me."

And he went unto the house of Mary, who was mother of John. Many were gathered there within, and they prayed. Peter knocked at the outer gate. And there came a servant girl whose name was Rhoda, to hearken at the door. When she knew his voice, she opened not the gate for joy, but ran back within and told them that Peter stood without.

"Thou art beside thyself," they said unto her. But she held firm in her word. "It is his angel," they said.

And Peter continued knocking. They opened the gate and beheld him, and their faces were stricken still with astonishment. He lifted his hand and bade them hold their peace. And he declared unto them how the Lord had brought him forth out of the prison. "Go," he said, "and tell these things unto James and unto the brethren." Then he departed and went unto another place.

And when the day came, there was no small trouble among the soldiers concerning what had become of Peter. Herod sought for him and found him not. He examined the guards who had kept the prison. Then he commanded that they be put to death.
