The messenger came in apace through the court doors. His sandals were dusted from the road, and his face was streaked where the sweat had dried. The officials stepped aside to let him through. Jehoshaphat rose from his seat as the man fell upon one knee before him.

"A great multitude cometh against thee from beyond the sea from Syria," the messenger said. His breath was still uneven. "Behold, they be in Hazazon Tamar."

Jehoshaphat answered not at once. His hand went unto the edge of his robe and rested there. The colour departed from his face. Round about him the chamber had grown still, and the stillness pressed in upon him.

He feared.

He sat not down again. He stood for a long moment with his hand upon the cloth. The officials watched him. None of them spake. They knew the name Hazazon Tamar. They knew it was nigh. They knew the road that came up from there ran into the heart of Judah.

Then the king straightened. He gave the order before fear could take him further. A fast was to be proclaimed throughout all Judah. Messengers went forth from his court that same hour. They rode out through the city gates and on into the hill country, crying at the gates of every town, summoning Judah unto Jerusalem.

They came. Out of all the cities of Judah they came. The roads filled by the morning of the morrow. Mothers carried infants wrapped in cloth. Fathers walked with their boys at their sides. Old men leaned upon their staves and would not be left behind. Some had come from the high country and were yet brushing the dust from their cloaks as they entered the gates. Others had walked through the night with their children half-asleep against their shoulders. None of them spake much upon the road. They had heard what was coming.

The temple courts filled. By the time the sun was over the city, the assembly was standing shoulder to shoulder before the new court of the house of the LORD, and the lamps of the temple were burning before them. The lamps had been kindled since first light. They burned now in full daylight, low and steady, before the gathered people.

Jehoshaphat stepped forth before the congregation. He stood where they all could behold him, at the place before the new court, with the temple behind him. He lifted up his face. His hands rested at his sides.

"O LORD God of our fathers, art not thou God in heaven? and rulest not thou over all the kingdoms of the heathen? and in thine hand is there not power and might, so that none is able to withstand thee?"

His voice did not rise. It carried as a voice carrieth in a great chamber when no man stirreth.

"Thou drovest out the inhabitants of this land before thy people Israel. Thou gavest it to the seed of Abraham thy friend for ever. They dwelt in it, and have built thee a sanctuary therein for thy name. They said, 'If evil come upon us — the sword, judgment, pestilence, or famine — we will stand before this house, and in thy presence, for thy name is in this house, and cry unto thee in our affliction, and thou wilt hear and help.'"

He drew a breath. He went on.

"Now, behold, the children of Ammon and Moab and mount Seir, whom thou wouldest not let Israel invade when they came out of the land of Egypt — behold, how they reward us. They come to cast us out of thy possession, which thou hast given us to inherit."

The lamps burned. The people stood with their little ones, their wives, and their children. No man moved from his place. A child somewhere in the back of the court began to cry, and a mother hushed him against her shoulder, and the cry ceased, and the silence came again.

Then Jehoshaphat spake the words that would be remembered.

"O our God, wilt thou not judge them? for we have no might against this great company that cometh against us; neither know we what to do: but our eyes are upon thee."

He said no more. He had no more to say.

The silence after the prayer settled deep into the courts. No man stirred. The lamps flickered without a sound. All Judah was yet standing before the LORD, holding their children unto their robes, when the Spirit of the LORD came upon Jahaziel.

He was a Levite, of the sons of Asaph. He stood in the midst of the congregation. The eyes of the people turned toward him.

"Hearken ye, all Judah," he said, "and ye inhabitants of Jerusalem, and thou king Jehoshaphat. Thus saith the LORD unto you, 'Be not afraid nor dismayed by reason of this great multitude; for the battle is not yours, but God's. To morrow go ye down against them. Behold, they come up by the cliff of Ziz. Ye shall find them at the end of the brook, before the wilderness of Jeruel. Ye shall not need to fight in this battle. Set yourselves, stand ye still, and see the salvation of the LORD with you, O Judah and Jerusalem. Fear not, nor be dismayed. Go ye out against them on the morrow, for the LORD is with you.'"

His words came to an end. They hung in the temple courts.

Jehoshaphat bowed his head. He put his face to the ground. He bowed not in stages. He went down at once, in the place where he stood. The king of Judah lay face down upon the stones before the new court, and the people beheld him there.

All Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem fell down before the LORD, worshipping him. Whole families went down together, the children kneeling beside their fathers, the mothers folded over their infants. The sound of the assembly was the sound of cloth upon stone.

Then the Levites of the children of the Kohathites and of the Korahites rose up. They stood up to praise the LORD God of Israel with a loud voice on high. The sound climbed the walls of the new court and went up into the morning, and the lamps of the temple burned on beneath that great rising sound.

They rose early. They went forth into the wilderness of Tekoa. The road climbed under the morning light, and Jehoshaphat stood at the edge of the column before they moved on. He lifted up his voice unto them.

"Hear me, O Judah, and ye inhabitants of Jerusalem; Believe in the LORD your God, so shall ye be established; believe his prophets, so shall ye prosper."

Then he appointed singers at the front of the column, in their holy array, before the army. They walked before every spear. They lifted the song as they went: "Praise the LORD; for his mercy endureth for ever." The song moved out before the army along the road, and the road bore it on into the wilderness.

And when they began to sing and to praise, the LORD set ambushments against the children of Ammon, Moab, and mount Seir, which were come against Judah. The army of Judah saw it not. They walked. They sang. The valley before them was hidden by the slope of the land.

When Judah came toward the watch tower in the wilderness, they came over the rise, and they looked.

The singing ceased.

The valley lay open before them under the morning sun. The dead bodies were fallen to the earth. They lay where the army of the children of Ammon and Moab had stood. There were none that escaped. No sword had been lifted in Judah's hand.

The men of Judah stood at the watch tower a long while. No man spake. The wind moved across the wilderness and across them. Then they went down.

They were three days in gathering of the spoil, the riches and the precious jewels, more than they could carry. On the fourth day they assembled themselves in the valley of Berachah, and there they blessed the LORD. Wherefore the name of the same place was called the valley of Berachah unto this day.

They returned to Jerusalem with Jehoshaphat in the forefront of them, with psalteries and harps and trumpets, unto the house of the LORD. And the fear of God was on all the kingdoms of those countries, when they had heard that the LORD fought against the enemies of Israel. So the realm of Jehoshaphat was quiet: for his God gave him rest round about him.
