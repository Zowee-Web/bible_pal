The messenger came in apace through the court doors. His sandals were dusted from the road, and his face was streaked where the sweat had dried. The officials stepped aside to let him through. Jehoshaphat rose from his seat as the man fell upon one knee before him.

"A great multitude cometh against thee from beyond the sea from Syria," the messenger said. His breath was still uneven. "Behold, they be in Hazazon Tamar."

Jehoshaphat answered not at once. His hand went unto the edge of his robe and rested there. The colour departed from his face. Round about him the chamber had grown still, and the stillness pressed in upon him.

He feared.

Then he straightened. He gave the order before fear could take him further. A fast was to be proclaimed throughout all Judah. Messengers went forth from his court that same hour. They rode out through the city gates and on into the hill country, crying at the gates of every town, summoning Judah unto Jerusalem.

They came. Out of all the cities of Judah they came. Mothers carried infants wrapped in cloth. Fathers walked with their boys at their sides. Old men leaned upon their staves and would not be left behind. The roads filled. The temple courts filled. By the time the sun was over the city, the assembly was standing shoulder to shoulder before the new court of the house of the LORD, and the lamps of the temple were burning before them.

Jehoshaphat stepped forth before the people. He stood where they all could behold him. He lifted up his face.

"O LORD God of our fathers, art not thou God in heaven? and rulest not thou over all the kingdoms of the heathen? and in thine hand is there not power and might, so that none is able to withstand thee?"

His voice did not rise. It carried as a voice carrieth in a great chamber when no man stirreth.

"Thou drovest out the inhabitants of this land before thy people Israel. Thou gavest it to the seed of Abraham thy friend for ever. They dwelt in it, and have built thee a sanctuary therein for thy name."

He drew a breath. He went on.

"Now, behold, the children of Ammon and Moab and mount Seir, whom thou wouldest not let Israel invade when they came out of the land of Egypt — behold, how they reward us. They come to cast us out of thy possession, which thou hast given us to inherit."

The lamps burned. The people stood with their little ones, their wives, and their children. No man moved from his place.

Then Jehoshaphat spake the words that would be remembered.

"O our God, wilt thou not judge them? for we have no might against this great company that cometh against us; neither know we what to do: but our eyes are upon thee."

The silence after the prayer settled deep into the courts. No man stirred. The lamps flickered without a sound. All Judah was yet standing before the LORD, holding their children unto their robes, when the Spirit of the LORD came upon Jahaziel.

He stood up in the midst of the congregation. The eyes of the people turned toward him.

"Hearken ye, all Judah," he said, "and ye inhabitants of Jerusalem, and thou king Jehoshaphat. Thus saith the LORD unto you, 'Be not afraid nor dismayed by reason of this great multitude; for the battle is not yours, but God's. Ye shall not need to fight in this battle. Set yourselves, stand ye still, and see the salvation of the LORD with you, O Judah and Jerusalem. Go ye out against them on the morrow, for the LORD is with you.'"

Jehoshaphat bowed his head. He put his face to the ground. All Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem fell down before the LORD, worshipping him. The Levites of the children of the Kohathites and of the Korahites stood up to praise the LORD God of Israel with a loud voice on high. The sound climbed the walls of the new court and went up into the morning.

They rose early. They went forth into the wilderness of Tekoa. The road climbed under the morning light, and the singers walked at the front of the column in their holy array, lifting the song before the army: "Praise the LORD; for his mercy endureth for ever." The song moved out before them along the road.

When Judah came toward the watch tower in the wilderness, they came over the rise, and they looked. The valley lay open before them. The dead bodies were fallen to the earth, and none escaped. No sword had been lifted in Judah's hand.

They went down. They blessed the LORD in the valley of Berachah. They returned to Jerusalem with psalteries and harps and trumpets unto the house of the LORD. So the realm of Jehoshaphat was quiet: for his God gave him rest round about him.
