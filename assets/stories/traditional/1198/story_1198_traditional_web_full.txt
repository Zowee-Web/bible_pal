The messenger came in fast through the court doors. His sandals were dusted from the road, and his face was streaked where the sweat had dried. The officials stepped aside to let him through. Jehoshaphat rose from his seat as the man dropped to one knee before him.

"A great multitude is coming against you from beyond the sea from Syria," the messenger said. His breath was still uneven. "They are in Hazazon Tamar."

Jehoshaphat did not answer at once. His hand went to the edge of his robe and stayed there. The color left his face. Around him the room had grown quiet, and the quiet pressed in on him.

He was afraid.

Then he straightened. He gave the order before fear could take him further. A fast was to be proclaimed throughout all Judah. Messengers went from his court that same hour. They rode out through the city gates and on into the hill country, calling at the gates of every town, summoning Judah to Jerusalem.

They came. Out of all the cities of Judah they came. Mothers carried infants wrapped in cloth. Fathers walked with their boys at their sides. Old men leaned on their staffs and would not be left behind. The roads filled. The temple courts filled. By the time the sun was over the city, the assembly was standing shoulder to shoulder before the new court of the house of the Lord, and the lamps of the temple were burning before them.

Jehoshaphat stepped out before the people. He stood where they all could see him. He lifted his face.

"Lord, the God of our fathers, aren't you God in heaven? Aren't you ruler over all the kingdoms of the nations? Power and might are in your hand, so that no one is able to withstand you."

His voice did not rise. It carried the way a voice carries in a great room when no one moves.

"You drove out the inhabitants of this land before your people Israel. You gave it to the offspring of Abraham your friend forever. They lived in it, and built you a sanctuary in it for your name."

He drew a breath. He went on.

"Now, behold, the children of Ammon and Moab and Mount Seir, whom you would not let Israel invade when they came out of the land of Egypt — behold, how they reward us. They come to cast us out of your possession, which you have given us to inherit."

The lamps burned. The people stood with their little ones, their wives, and their children. No one moved from his place.

Then Jehoshaphat said the words that would be remembered.

"Our God, will you not judge them? For we have no might against this great company that comes against us. Neither do we know what to do, but our eyes are on you."

The silence after the prayer settled deep into the courts. No one moved. The lamps flickered without a sound. All Judah was still standing before the Lord, holding their children against their robes, when the Spirit of the Lord came on Jahaziel.

He stood up in the middle of the assembly. The eyes of the people turned toward him.

"Listen, all Judah," he said, "and you inhabitants of Jerusalem, and you, King Jehoshaphat. The Lord says to you, 'Don't be afraid, neither be dismayed by reason of this great multitude. For the battle is not yours, but God's. You will not need to fight this battle. Set yourselves, stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord with you, O Judah and Jerusalem. Go out against them tomorrow, for the Lord is with you.'"

Jehoshaphat bowed his head. He put his face to the ground. All Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem fell down before the Lord, worshiping him. The Levites of the children of the Kohathites and of the Korahites rose to praise the Lord, the God of Israel, with an exceedingly loud voice. The sound climbed the walls of the new court and went up into the morning.

They rose early. They went out into the wilderness of Tekoa. The road climbed under the morning light, and the singers walked at the front of the column in their holy array, lifting the song before the army: "Give thanks to the Lord, for his loving kindness endures forever." The song moved out ahead of them along the road.

When Judah came to the place looking out toward the wilderness, they came over the rise, and they looked. The valley lay open before them. The dead bodies were fallen to the earth, and there were none who escaped. No sword had been lifted in Judah's hand.

They went down. They blessed the Lord in the Beracah Valley. They returned to Jerusalem with stringed instruments and harps and trumpets to the house of the Lord. And the realm of Jehoshaphat was quiet, for his God gave him rest all around him.
