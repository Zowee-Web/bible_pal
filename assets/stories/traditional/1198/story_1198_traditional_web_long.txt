The messenger came in fast through the court doors. His sandals were dusted from the road, and his face was streaked where the sweat had dried. The officials stepped aside to let him through. Jehoshaphat rose from his seat as the man dropped to one knee before him.

"A great multitude is coming against you from beyond the sea from Syria," the messenger said. His breath was still uneven. "They are in Hazazon Tamar."

Jehoshaphat did not answer at once. His hand went to the edge of his robe and stayed there. The color left his face. Around him the room had grown quiet, and the quiet pressed in on him.

He was afraid.

He did not sit back down. He stood for a long moment with his hand on the cloth. The officials watched him. None of them spoke. They knew the name Hazazon Tamar. They knew it was close. They knew the road that came up from there ran into the heart of Judah.

Then the king straightened. He gave the order before fear could take him further. A fast was to be proclaimed throughout all Judah. Messengers went from his court that same hour. They rode out through the city gates and on into the hill country, calling at the gates of every town, summoning Judah to Jerusalem.

They came. Out of all the cities of Judah they came. The roads filled by morning of the next day. Mothers carried infants wrapped in cloth. Fathers walked with their boys at their sides. Old men leaned on their staffs and would not be left behind. Some had come from the high country and were still brushing the dust from their cloaks as they entered the gates. Others had walked through the night with their children half-asleep against their shoulders. None of them spoke much on the road. They had heard what was coming.

The temple courts filled. By the time the sun was over the city, the assembly was standing shoulder to shoulder before the new court of the house of the Lord, and the lamps of the temple were burning before them. The lamps had been lit since first light. They burned now in full daylight, low and steady, before the gathered people.

Jehoshaphat stepped out before the assembly. He stood where they all could see him, at the place before the new court, with the temple behind him. He lifted his face. His hands rested at his sides.

"Lord, the God of our fathers, aren't you God in heaven? Aren't you ruler over all the kingdoms of the nations? Power and might are in your hand, so that no one is able to withstand you."

His voice did not rise. It carried the way a voice carries in a great room when no one moves.

"You drove out the inhabitants of this land before your people Israel. You gave it to the offspring of Abraham your friend forever. They lived in it, and built you a sanctuary in it for your name. They said, 'If evil comes on us — the sword, judgment, pestilence, or famine — we will stand before this house, and before you, for your name is in this house, and cry to you in our affliction, and you will hear and save.'"

He drew a breath. He went on.

"Now, behold, the children of Ammon and Moab and Mount Seir, whom you would not let Israel invade when they came out of the land of Egypt — behold, how they reward us. They come to cast us out of your possession, which you have given us to inherit."

The lamps burned. The people stood with their little ones, their wives, and their children. No one moved from his place. A child somewhere in the back of the court began to cry, and a mother hushed him against her shoulder, and the cry stopped, and the silence came back.

Then Jehoshaphat said the words that would be remembered.

"Our God, will you not judge them? For we have no might against this great company that comes against us. Neither do we know what to do, but our eyes are on you."

He did not say more. He had no more to say.

The silence after the prayer settled deep into the courts. No one moved. The lamps flickered without a sound. All Judah was still standing before the Lord, holding their children against their robes, when the Spirit of the Lord came on Jahaziel.

He was a Levite, of the sons of Asaph. He stood in the middle of the assembly. The eyes of the people turned toward him.

"Listen, all Judah," he said, "and you inhabitants of Jerusalem, and you, King Jehoshaphat. The Lord says to you, 'Don't be afraid, neither be dismayed by reason of this great multitude. For the battle is not yours, but God's. Tomorrow, go down against them. Behold, they are coming up by the ascent of Ziz. You will find them at the end of the valley, before the wilderness of Jeruel. You will not need to fight this battle. Set yourselves, stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord with you, O Judah and Jerusalem. Don't be afraid, nor be dismayed. Go out against them tomorrow, for the Lord is with you.'"

His words ended. They hung in the temple courts.

Jehoshaphat bowed his head. He put his face to the ground. He did not bow in stages. He went down at once, in the place where he stood. The king of Judah lay face-down on the stones before the new court, and the people saw him there.

All Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem fell down before the Lord, worshiping him. Whole families went down together, the children kneeling beside their fathers, the mothers folded over their infants. The sound of the assembly was the sound of cloth on stone.

Then the Levites of the children of the Kohathites and of the Korahites rose. They stood up to praise the Lord, the God of Israel, with an exceedingly loud voice. The sound climbed the walls of the new court and went up into the morning, and the lamps of the temple burned on beneath that great rising sound.

They rose early. They went out into the wilderness of Tekoa. The road climbed under the morning light, and Jehoshaphat stood at the edge of the column before they moved on. He lifted his voice to them.

"Listen to me, Judah and you inhabitants of Jerusalem. Believe in the Lord your God, so you will be established. Believe his prophets, so you will prosper."

Then he placed the singers at the front of the column, in their holy array, before the army. They walked ahead of every spear. They lifted the song as they went: "Give thanks to the Lord, for his loving kindness endures forever." The song moved out ahead of the army along the road, and the road carried it on into the wilderness.

While they sang, the Lord set ambushes against the children of Ammon, Moab, and Mount Seir, who had come against Judah. The army of Judah did not see it happen. They walked. They sang. The valley ahead of them was hidden by the slope of the land.

When Judah came to the place looking out toward the wilderness, they came over the rise, and they looked.

The singing stopped.

The valley lay open before them under the morning sun. The dead bodies were fallen to the earth. They lay where the army of the children of Ammon and Moab had stood. There were none who escaped. No sword had been lifted in Judah's hand.

The men of Judah stood at the lookout a long time. No one spoke. The wind moved across the wilderness and across them. Then they went down.

They gathered the plunder for three days, the riches and the precious jewels, more than they could carry. On the fourth day they assembled in the Beracah Valley, and there they blessed the Lord. So the name of that place was called Beracah Valley to this day.

They returned to Jerusalem with Jehoshaphat in front of them, with stringed instruments and harps and trumpets, to the house of the Lord. The fear of God was on all the kingdoms of the countries when they heard that the Lord had fought against the enemies of Israel. And the realm of Jehoshaphat was quiet, for his God gave him rest all around him.
