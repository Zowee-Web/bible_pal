There was a man in the land of Uz, whose name was Job; and that man was perfect and upright, and one that feared God, and eschewed evil.

And there were born unto him seven sons and three daughters. His substance also was seven thousand sheep — their bleating filled the hills round about his land, the white of their wool upon every slope. Three thousand camels. Five hundred yoke of oxen. Five hundred she asses. And a very great household of servants that tended all these things, their cook fires dotting the land at eventide. So that this man was the greatest of all the men of the east. The wealthiest, the most honoured, a man whose name was spoken with reverence in every market and at every gate.

And his sons went and feasted in their houses, every one his day; and sent and called for their three sisters to eat and to drink with them. The sound of music and laughter carried from the houses on the nights of feasting, the light of lamps spilling through the doorways into the darkness. And it was so, when the days of their feasting were gone about, that Job sent and sanctified them, and rose up early in the morning, and offered burnt offerings according to the number of them all: for Job said, "It may be that my sons have sinned, and cursed God in their hearts." Thus did Job continually. Ere the sun was fully risen, whilst the dew yet clung to the grass, the smoke of the offering climbed into the sky above his dwelling.

And there came a day when the messengers appeared.

The first came running. His feet smote the dust of the road, his breast heaving, his countenance grey with terror. He stumbled through the gate and near fell in the court.

"The oxen were plowing, and the asses feeding beside them, and the Sabeans fell upon them, and took them away; yea, they have slain the servants with the edge of the sword; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee."

The words yet hung in the air when another appeared at the gate. Yet running. Yet breathless. Yet grey with dust. His garment was rent at the shoulder.

"The fire of God is fallen from heaven, and hath burned up the sheep, and the servants, and consumed them; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee."

Seven thousand sheep. Gone. The hillsides that had been full of bleating were still. The servants that tended them were but ashes upon the ground.

While he was yet speaking, there came also another through the gate. His garments were rent, his sandals worn through, the leather split at the sole. He set his hand against the doorpost to steady himself.

"The Chaldeans made out three bands, and fell upon the camels, and have carried them away, yea, and slain the servants with the edge of the sword; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee."

Three thousand camels. The wealth which had taken a lifetime to build was being pulled asunder in minutes, report upon report, messenger upon messenger, the blows falling faster than a man could bear. The court was filling with breathless, dust-covered men, each bearing tidings worse than the last.

While he was yet speaking, there came also another. This one could scarce stand upon his feet. His knees gave way as he crossed the threshold. His voice trembled.

"Thy sons and thy daughters were eating and drinking wine in their eldest brother's house: and, behold, there came a great wind from the wilderness, and smote the four corners of the house, and it fell upon the young men, and they are dead; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee."

The chamber was still. The dust of the road yet hung in the air where the messengers had entered in, lit by the afternoon light from the windows. Four men. Four reports. The oxen, the sheep, the camels, the servants — and now the children. All ten of them. In one day. In the space of a single afternoon the greatest man in all the east had lost all that he possessed, save the ground beneath his feet and the breath within his body.

Then Job arose.

He rent his mantle. The cloth tore in his hands — the sound sharp and ragged in the stillness. He shaved his head. The blade scraped against his scalp and the hair fell upon the ground about him in dark clumps. Then he fell down upon the ground, and worshipped.

And said, "Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither: the Lord gave, and the Lord hath taken away; blessed be the name of the Lord."

The torn mantle lay across the floor, its edges frayed. The shorn hair lay in the dust beside his knees. The four messengers stood in the doorway, their breath yet heavy, the light behind them. In all this Job sinned not, nor charged God foolishly.
