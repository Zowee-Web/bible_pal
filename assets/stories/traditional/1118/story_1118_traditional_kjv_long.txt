Now Jericho was straitly shut up because of the children of Israel: none went out, and none came in. The great gate was barred from within, the cross-beam dropped into its iron sockets. The wall rose high above the plain, the stones thereof fitted close, the parapets watched by day and by night. From the camp of Israel on the other side of the valley, the city seemed as a closed fist set against the morning.

And in the tent of meeting the LORD spake unto Joshua. The word came in the still hour before the dawning. "See, I have given into thine hand Jericho, and the king thereof, and the mighty men of valour. Ye shall compass the city, all ye men of war, going round about it once. Thus shall ye do six days. And seven priests shall bear before the ark seven trumpets of rams' horns. And on the seventh day ye shall compass the city seven times, and the priests shall blow with the trumpets. And it shall come to pass, when they make a long blast with the ram's horn, all the people shall shout with a great shout, and the wall of the city shall fall down flat, and the people shall go up, every man straight before him."

Joshua heard the counsel that no captain of men would have written. He bowed his face unto the floor of the tent. He rose.

And he called the priests. They came unto him in their linen, their feet bare, the dust of the camp soft beneath them. "Take up the ark of the covenant of the LORD," said he. "Let seven priests bear seven trumpets of rams' horns before the ark." The priests bowed their heads and spake not. And he called the captains of the people. "Go forward. Compass the city. Let the armed men pass on before the ark of the LORD."

Then set he the silence upon them. He stood where the camp might see him, and lifted up his voice that the hindmost ranks might hear. "Ye shall not shout, nor make any noise with your voice, neither shall any word proceed out of your mouth, until the day I bid you shout. Then shall ye shout."

And the order travelled outward through the camp. Mothers laid a finger upon the lips of their children. Old men set their staves down softly. A horse was led away from the line of march, lest its complaint break the order. The camp made itself quiet against the morning.

The procession was formed as the sun lifted. The armed men went first, rank by rank, and their sandals struck the dust in a steady rhythm. After them came the seven priests, the seven trumpets at their lips. Then came the ark of the LORD, borne upon the shoulders of the bearing priests, the acacia wood thereof polished by the touch of many hands, the cherubim above it taking the light. The rereward followed last. The trumpets sounded long and low as they went, a single note carried out over the plain.

They came unto the foot of the wall. They turned, and they walked along it. The wall rose above them, the shadow thereof falling cool upon the line of march. From the parapets above, watchers leaned between the stones. A man with an helmet of brass shaded his eyes and looked down. A woman drew her child back from the edge. A captain of the city called unto his archers to hold. There was no banner of war. There were no slings. There was only a line of men walking, and the trumpets, and the ark.

The line finished its circuit. The armed men turned away from the wall. The priests with the trumpets turned. The ark turned. The rereward followed. The procession returned unto the camp. They lodged in the camp. The first day was done.

And Joshua rose early on the second morning. The priests took up the ark. The trumpets sounded. The procession compassed the same circuit and returned. So did they the third day, and the fourth, and the fifth, and the sixth. Six mornings of silence broken only by the rams' horns. Six evenings of returning. Within Jericho the watchers grew weary of looking down upon the same line walking, and weary of waiting for the assault that came not, and the strangeness of it began to weigh more heavily upon them than a siege of arrows would have weighed.

And on the seventh day Israel rose at the first lifting of the dark. The priests took up the ark while the stars were yet showing. The procession went out.

They compassed the city once. They compassed it a second time. The trumpets sounded continually. They compassed it a third time, and a fourth, and a fifth, and a sixth. The dust they had raised on the first circuit had not yet settled when they raised it again on the second. The armed men turned not their heads. The bearers of the ark faltered not. The rereward kept pace. The wall slid past upon their right hand, course after course of its fitted stone, and the trumpets sounded on.

And on the seventh circuit, as the head of the procession came round unto the place from whence it had begun, the priests drew breath and blew a long blast upon the rams' horns. The note rolled out long over the plain, lifted, and brake against the wall.

Then Joshua lifted up his voice. He spake that the hindmost ranks might hear him over the trumpets. "Shout; for the LORD hath given you the city. The city shall be devoted, even it and all that is therein, unto the LORD. Only Rahab the harlot shall live, she and all that are with her in the house, because she hid the messengers that we sent. Keep yourselves from the accursed thing, lest ye bring trouble upon the camp. All the silver, and the gold, and the vessels of brass and of iron are holy unto the LORD. They shall come into the treasury of the LORD."

So the people shouted. From the foremost rank unto the hindmost the great shout rose up, a sound that had been held in for seven days and now came up into the morning all at once. The priests blew with the trumpets. And when the people heard the sound of the trumpet, they shouted with a great shout, and the wall of the city fell down flat. The stones came apart along their fitted courses. The parapets tilted and slid. A long ribbon of dust rose up into the morning light along the whole length of the wall, and where the wall had stood the plain showed open ground and the houses of Jericho beyond.

And Israel waited a breath. Then they went up into the city, every man straight before him.
