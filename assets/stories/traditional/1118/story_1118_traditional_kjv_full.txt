Now Jericho was straitly shut up because of the children of Israel: none went out, and none came in. The gates were barred. The wall stood high above the plain, the sun upon its stones in the morning and shadow at its foot in the evening, a city sealed against the camp on the other side of the valley.

And in the camp the LORD spake unto Joshua. "See, I have given into thine hand Jericho, and the king thereof, and the mighty men of valour. Ye shall compass the city, all ye men of war, and go round about the city once. Thus shall ye do six days. And seven priests shall bear before the ark seven trumpets of rams' horns. And on the seventh day ye shall compass the city seven times, and the priests shall blow with the trumpets. And it shall come to pass, when they make a long blast with the ram's horn, all the people shall shout with a great shout, and the wall of the city shall fall down flat."

And Joshua called the priests. "Take up the ark of the covenant of the LORD, and let seven priests bear seven trumpets of rams' horns before the ark." Then turned he unto the people, and his voice was even. "Go forward. Compass the city. Let the armed men pass on before the ark." Then added he one commandment more, and the camp went still to hear it. "Ye shall not shout, nor make any noise with your voice, neither shall any word proceed out of your mouth, until the day I bid you shout."

The procession was formed at the dawning. The armed men went first, and their sandals struck the dust in a steady rhythm. After them came the seven priests, and the seven trumpets at their lips. Then came the ark of the LORD, borne upon the shoulders of the priests, the staves thereof polished by the touch of many hands. The rereward followed last. The trumpets sounded long and low as they went, a single note carried over the plain.

And they compassed the city once. Not one of the children of Israel spake. Children walked with their hands held fast by their mothers. Old men set their staves down softly. From the wall above, watchers leaned between the parapets and looked down upon the silent line moving beneath them, and there was no sound save the rams' horns and the feet in the dust. The procession finished its circuit. It returned unto the camp. It lodged in the camp.

And Joshua rose early on the second morning. The priests took up the ark. The trumpets sounded. The procession compassed the same circuit, in the same silence, and returned. So did they the third day, and the fourth, and the fifth, and the sixth. Six mornings of marching, six evenings of returning, the only voice upon the plain the rams' horns and the only motion the slow ring of Israel about the wall.

And on the seventh day they rose at the first lifting of the dark. They compassed the city once. They compassed it a second time. A third. A fourth. A fifth. A sixth. The dust hung in the air behind them. The armed men turned not their heads. The trumpets sounded continually. And on the seventh circuit the priests drew breath and blew a long blast, and the note rolled out over the plain and against the wall.

Then Joshua lifted up his voice. "Shout; for the LORD hath given you the city. The city is devoted unto the LORD. Only Rahab the harlot shall live, she and all that are with her in the house, because she hid the messengers that were sent. The silver, and the gold, and the vessels of brass and of iron are holy unto the LORD."

So the people shouted. The priests blew with the trumpets. And when the people heard the sound of the trumpet, they shouted with a great shout, and the wall fell down flat. The stones came apart along their courses. The dust rose up into the morning light. And the people went up into the city, every man straight before him.
