Jericho was tightly shut up because of the children of Israel. The gates were barred. No one went out, and no one came in. The wall stood high above the plain, sun on its stones in the morning and shadow at its foot in the evening, a city sealed against the camp on the other side of the valley.

In the camp the Lord spoke to Joshua. "I have given Jericho into your hand, with its king and the mighty men of valor. The men of war shall march around the city once. So shall it be for six days. Seven priests shall bear seven shofars of rams' horns before the ark. On the seventh day the city shall be circled seven times, and the priests shall blow the shofars. When the long blast sounds, the people shall shout with a great shout, and the wall shall fall down flat."

Joshua called the priests. "Take up the ark of the covenant. Let seven priests bear seven shofars of rams' horns before the ark of the Lord." He turned to the people, and his voice was even. "Advance. March around the city. Let the armed men pass on before the ark." Then he added one command more, and the camp went still to hear it. "No shout shall be raised. No voice shall be heard. Let no word proceed out of any mouth until the day I tell you to shout."

The procession formed at dawn. The armed men went first, their sandals striking the dust in a steady rhythm. Behind them came the seven priests, the seven shofars at their lips. Then came the ark of the Lord, carried on the shoulders of the priests, its poles polished by the touch of many hands. The rear guard followed last. The shofars sounded long and low as they went, a single note carried over the plain.

They circled the city once. Not one Israelite spoke. Children walked with their hands held tightly by their mothers. Old men set their staffs down softly. From the wall above, watchers leaned between the parapets and stared down at the silent line moving below them, the only sound the rams' horns and the feet in the dust. The procession completed its circuit. It returned to the camp. It lodged in the camp.

On the second morning Joshua rose early. The priests took up the ark. The shofars sounded. The procession marched the same circuit, in the same silence, and returned. So it was the third day, and the fourth, and the fifth, and the sixth. Six mornings of marching, six evenings of return, the only voice on the plain the rams' horns and the only motion the slow ring of Israel around the wall.

On the seventh day they rose at the first lifting of the dark. They marched once around the city. They marched a second time. A third. A fourth. A fifth. A sixth. The dust hung in the air behind them. The armed men did not turn their heads. The shofars sounded continually. On the seventh circuit the priests drew breath and blew a long blast, and the note rolled out over the plain and against the wall.

Joshua lifted his voice. "Shout! For the Lord has given you the city. The city is devoted to the Lord. Only Rahab the prostitute shall live, she and all that are with her in her house, because she hid the messengers that were sent. The silver, the gold, the vessels of bronze and iron are holy to the Lord."

The people shouted. The priests blew the shofars. When the people heard the sound of the shofar, they shouted with a great shout, and the wall of the city fell down flat. The stones came apart along their courses. The dust rose into the morning light. And the people went up into the city, every man straight before him.
