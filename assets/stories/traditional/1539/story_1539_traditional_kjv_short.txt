Jesus spake this parable unto certain ones who trusted in their own righteousness and held all others in contempt.

Two men went up unto the temple to pray. The one was a Pharisee. The other was a publican.

The Pharisee came forward and stood where men might behold him. The stone pillars rose about him on every side. The smoke of incense hung upon the courts. He set his feet and lifted up his countenance and prayed thus within himself.

"God, I give thee thanks that I am not as other men are. Thieves. The unrighteous. Those who defile their beds." He paused. "Nor even as this publican."

His voice went out across the open court. Other worshipers passed him upon the stones.

"Twice in every week do I fast," said he. "And of all that I possess, I render tithes."

He stood upright. His garments were without spot. His phylacteries were broad upon his arm and upon his brow. His prayer was ended.

But the publican stood afar off. He would not draw nigh. He kept to the far edge of the court where shadow gathered against the wall. His head was bowed. He would not so much as lift his eyes unto heaven. And his fist rose up and smote upon his own breast, once, and heavily. The sound thereof was dull against his ribs.

"God," said he. His voice broke low. "Be thou merciful to me, a sinner."

He smote his breast again. His eyes remained upon the ground. The dust upon the stones was pale in the light of the afternoon.

And Jesus looked upon them that heard him.

"I say unto you," said he, "this man went down to his house justified, rather than the other."

He suffered the words to settle upon them.

"For whosoever exalteth himself shall be brought low. And he that humbleth himself shall be lifted up."

The crowd stood still about him. The air of the afternoon was warm and dry. And none answered him.
