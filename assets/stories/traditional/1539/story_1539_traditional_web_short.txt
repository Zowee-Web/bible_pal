Jesus spoke this parable to certain people who trusted in themselves that they were righteous and looked down on everyone else.

Two men went up to the temple to pray. One was a Pharisee. The other was a tax collector.

The Pharisee walked forward and stood where others could see him. The stone columns rose around him. The incense drifted through the courts. He planted his feet and lifted his face and prayed with himself.

"God, I thank you that I am not like other men. Extortioners. The unjust. Adulterers." He paused. "Or even like this tax collector."

His voice carried across the open court. Other worshipers moved past him on the stones.

"I fast twice every week," he said. "I give tithes of everything I possess."

He stood straight. His robes were clean. His phylacteries were broad on his arm and forehead. His prayer was finished.

The tax collector stood far off. He would not come near. He stood at the edge of the court where the shadows pooled against the wall. His head was down. He would not lift so much as his eyes toward heaven. His fist came up and struck his own chest, once, hard. The sound of it was dull against his ribs.

"God," he said. His voice broke low. "Be merciful to me. A sinner."

He struck his chest again. His eyes stayed on the ground. The dust on the stones was pale in the afternoon light.

Jesus looked at those listening to him.

"I tell you," he said, "this man went down to his house justified rather than the other."

He let the words settle.

"For everyone who exalts himself will be brought low. And the one who humbles himself will be lifted up."

The crowd stood still around him. The afternoon air was warm and dry. No one answered.
