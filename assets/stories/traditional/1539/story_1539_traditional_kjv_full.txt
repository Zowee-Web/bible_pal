And Jesus spake this parable unto certain ones who trusted in their own righteousness and held all others in contempt. They stood near him, listening, their faces sure and settled.

Two men went up into the temple to pray. The one was a Pharisee. The other was a collector of taxes.

Now the courts of the temple were broad and open to the sky. The paving stones caught the light and gave it back again. Smoke from the altar rose thin and grey, curling upward past the great pillars. Worshipers moved through the courts in small companies, and the scraping of their sandals upon the stone was heard. Pigeons stirred upon the ledges overhead.

The Pharisee entered with his head lifted high. His robes were long and well kept, the fringes hanging even at the hem. His phylacteries were broad upon his forehead and bound fast upon his left arm. He passed through the court without haste, moving beyond the others, and found him a place to stand where his voice might carry.

He planted his feet upon the stone. He drew his back straight. He lifted his face toward the heavens, which were visible between the high walls. And he prayed thus with himself.

"I give thee thanks, O God, that I am not as other men are."

His voice was clear and without trembling. Those who stood near could hear every word thereof.

"Extortioners. The unjust. Men given to adultery."

He turned his head but slightly, enough that his gaze might pass over the far edge of the court behind him.

"Or even as this publican."

Then he faced forward once more.

"Twice in every week do I fast," said he. "Of all that I possess, I render the tithe."

He stood with his hands open at his sides. His chin was lifted. The light fell upon the clean cloth of his outer garment. His prayer was finished. He had named what he was not and recounted what he had done, and he was satisfied. He lowered his hands and remained standing in that place.

But the tax collector stood afar off.

He had not come forward into the court where the others were gathered. He kept near the outer edge, hard by the wall where the shade lay deep and the stone was cool. His garments were plain. No man stood with him. No man spake unto him. He was alone at the margin of the place, and there he kept himself.

He would not so much as lift his eyes toward heaven.

His head was bowed. His shoulders were drawn forward. He looked upon the paving stones beneath his feet, upon the dust that had gathered in the cracks thereof, upon the worn surface where ten thousand feet had trodden before his own.

Then his right hand rose up and smote upon his breast. The sound was sudden and heavy, a fist against the ribs. His body rocked with the force of the blow.

"God," said he.

His voice was low and rough. It did not carry. Those who stood in the midst of the court would not have heard him.

"Be thou merciful unto me."

He smote his breast again. The same fist, the same place over his heart.

"A sinner."

That was all. He named not his deeds. He spoke not of what he had done nor of what he had left undone. He stood with his fist against his breast and his eyes upon the ground and the dust upon his sandals and the shadow of the wall falling across his back.

The two men were both within the temple. Both had come to pray. The smoke of the incense drifted between them. And the distance from the place where the Pharisee stood unto the place where the tax collector stood was thirty paces at the least, perhaps more. The Pharisee looked not back again. The tax collector lifted not his eyes.

Then Jesus paused. And the people round about him were still.

"I say unto you," spake Jesus, "this man went down unto his house justified, rather than the other."

He looked upon the faces before him. Some among them shifted. Others moved not at all.

"For whosoever doth exalt himself shall be brought low," said he. "And he that humbleth himself shall be lifted up."

The air was warm. The dust of the road hung in the light. No man among the company spake. And Jesus stood in their midst with his hands still, and the silence held.
