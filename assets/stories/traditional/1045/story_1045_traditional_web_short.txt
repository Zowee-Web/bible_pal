Jacob lived in Canaan with his twelve sons. Of all his sons, he loved Joseph the most. Joseph was seventeen and helped take care of the sheep.

Jacob made Joseph a special coat — a long robe with many colors. When the brothers saw it, their faces went hard. Their father loved Joseph more than any of them. They could not say a kind word to him.

Joseph had a dream. He told his brothers. "We were tying up bundles of grain in the field. My bundle stood up, and your bundles gathered around mine and bowed down."

The brothers stared at him. "Are you going to rule over us?" They hated him even more.

Joseph had another dream. "The sun, the moon, and eleven stars were bowing down to me."

Even his father frowned. "Will we all bow down to you?" But Jacob kept thinking about it.

One day Jacob sent Joseph to check on his brothers near Shechem. Joseph put on his colorful coat and set out. A man told him they had gone to Dothan. Joseph kept walking.

The brothers saw him from far away. They recognized the coat. "Here comes the dreamer. Let us kill him and throw him into a pit."

Reuben, the oldest, said, "Do not kill him. Just throw him in the pit." He planned to rescue Joseph later.

When Joseph arrived, they grabbed him, pulled off his coat, and threw him into a deep empty pit with no water.

The brothers sat down to eat. They saw a caravan of traders heading to Egypt — camels loaded with spices.

Judah said, "Let us sell him to the traders." They pulled Joseph out and sold him for twenty pieces of silver. The traders took him toward Egypt.

The brothers killed a goat and dipped Joseph's coat in the blood. They brought it to Jacob. "We found this. Is it your son's coat?"

Jacob recognized it. "My son's coat! A wild animal has torn him to pieces!" He tore his clothes, put on sackcloth, and wept many days. No one could comfort him.

The traders arrived in Egypt and sold Joseph to Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh.
