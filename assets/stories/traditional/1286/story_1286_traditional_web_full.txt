The ark had been in the house of Abinadab for twenty years.

The men of Kiriath Jearim had come and fetched it up, and brought it into the house on the hill, and sanctified Eleazar his son to keep it. Twenty harvests had passed under that roof. Twenty rainy seasons. Eleazar's hair had gone gray at the temples. The wooden chest stood in the inner room with its cloth covering, and the lamp was kept burning beside it, and the household moved quietly past the door.

And all the house of Israel lamented after the Lord. The lamenting went on in the villages. It went on in the fields at evening. The people did not know how to come back. The Philistines pressed the borders. The high places still smoked with offerings to other gods. The lamps in the houses of Baal still burned. The shapes of Ashtaroth still stood in the household corners.

Then Samuel came among them.

He spoke to all the house of Israel. The elders gathered to hear him. He stood among them with his staff in his hand, and his voice carried into the back of the crowd.

"If you do return to the Lord with all your heart," he said, "then put away the foreign gods and the Ashtaroth from among you, and direct your hearts to the Lord, and serve him only; and he will deliver you out of the hand of the Philistines."

The people went home. They went into their houses and lifted down the shapes from the corners. They carried them out and broke them. The Baals and the Ashtaroth were gathered and destroyed. The household lamps that had burned to other gods were put out. The children of Israel served the Lord only.

Samuel said, "Gather all Israel to Mizpah, and I will pray for you to the Lord."

They came. They came up from the valleys and down from the hills. They gathered at Mizpah. And on that day they did a strange and quiet thing. They drew water from the well, and they poured it out before the Lord. The water spread on the dust at their feet and was gone. They fasted on that day. They said there, "We have sinned against the Lord." The words moved through the gathering like a single breath.

While they were at Mizpah, the lords of the Philistines heard. The Philistines went up against Israel. The children of Israel heard it, and they were afraid of the Philistines.

They came to Samuel. They said to him, "Don't cease to cry to the Lord our God for us, that he will save us out of the hand of the Philistines."

Samuel took a sucking lamb. He offered it for a whole burnt offering to the Lord. The smoke went up from the altar at Mizpah. Samuel cried to the Lord for Israel.

As he was offering up the burnt offering, the Philistines drew near. The line of them came on across the open ground. The men of Israel stood and watched. The smoke was still rising.

Then the Lord thundered with a great thunder on that day upon the Philistines.

The sky cracked open above them. The thunder rolled along the line. It confused them. They were struck down before Israel. The men of Israel went out of Mizpah and pursued them, and smote them, until they came under Beth Kar.

When the dust had settled, Samuel walked out from the camp. He went a short way from Mizpah, to a place between Mizpah and Shen. He found a stone. He lifted it. He set it upright in the open ground.

Then he gave it a name.

"Ebenezer," he said. "The Lord helped us until now."

The stone stood there in the daylight. It was not a fortress. It was not a monument of war. It was a marker, set up by a man who had cried to the Lord and been heard. The Philistines were subdued, and they did not come any more within the border of Israel. The hand of the Lord was against the Philistines all the days of Samuel. The cities they had taken were given back. There was peace.

And Samuel judged Israel all the days of his life. He went year by year in a circuit to Bethel, to Gilgal, to Mizpah. His return was always to Ramah, for there was his house, and there he built an altar to the Lord.

But the stone he had set between Mizpah and Shen kept standing. The wind moved past it. The light fell on it morning by morning. And whoever passed it knew its name.
