The ark had abode in the house of Abinadab twenty years.

The men of Kirjathjearim had come and fetched it up, and brought it into the house in the hill, and sanctified Eleazar his son to keep it. Twenty harvests had passed under that roof. Twenty rainy seasons. Eleazar's hair had gone gray at the temples. The wooden chest stood in the inner room with its cloth covering, and the lamp was kept burning beside it, and the household moved quietly past the door.

And all the house of Israel lamented after the Lord. The lamenting went on in the villages. It went on in the fields at evening. The people knew not how to return. The Philistines pressed the borders. The high places still smoked with offerings unto other gods. The lamps in the houses of Baal still burned. The shapes of Ashtaroth still stood in the household corners.

Then Samuel came among them.

He spake unto all the house of Israel. The elders gathered to hear him. He stood among them with his staff in his hand, and his voice carried into the back of the throng.

"If ye do return unto the Lord with all your hearts," he said, "then put away the strange gods and Ashtaroth from among you, and prepare your hearts unto the Lord, and serve him only: and he will deliver you out of the hand of the Philistines."

The people went home. They went into their houses and took down the shapes from the corners. They carried them out and brake them. The Baalim and the Ashtaroth were gathered and destroyed. The household lamps that had burned unto other gods were put out. The children of Israel served the Lord only.

Samuel said, "Gather all Israel to Mizpeh, and I will pray for you unto the Lord."

They came. They came up from the valleys and down from the hills. They gathered together to Mizpeh. And on that day they did a strange and quiet thing. They drew water from the well, and poured it out before the Lord. The water spread on the dust at their feet and was gone. They fasted on that day. They said there, "We have sinned against the Lord." The words moved through the gathering as a single breath.

While they were at Mizpeh, the lords of the Philistines heard. The Philistines went up against Israel. The children of Israel heard it, and were afraid of the Philistines.

They came to Samuel. They said unto him, "Cease not to cry unto the Lord our God for us, that he will save us out of the hand of the Philistines."

Samuel took a sucking lamb. He offered it for a burnt offering wholly unto the Lord. The smoke went up from the altar at Mizpeh. Samuel cried unto the Lord for Israel.

And as he was offering up the burnt offering, the Philistines drew near to battle against Israel. The line of them came on across the open ground. The men of Israel stood and watched. The smoke was still rising.

Then the Lord thundered with a great thunder on that day upon the Philistines.

The sky brake open above them. The thunder rolled along the line. It discomfited them. They were smitten down before Israel. The men of Israel went out of Mizpeh, and pursued the Philistines, and smote them, until they came under Bethcar.

When the dust had settled, Samuel walked out from the camp. He went a short way from Mizpeh, to a place between Mizpeh and Shen. He found a stone. He took it up. He set it upright in the open ground.

Then he gave it a name.

"Ebenezer," he said. "Hitherto hath the Lord helped us."

The stone stood there in the daylight. It was not a fortress. It was not a monument of war. It was a marker, set up by a man who had cried unto the Lord and been heard. The Philistines were subdued, and they came no more into the coast of Israel. The hand of the Lord was against the Philistines all the days of Samuel. The cities they had taken were restored. There was peace.

And Samuel judged Israel all the days of his life. He went from year to year in circuit to Bethel, to Gilgal, to Mizpeh. His return was to Ramah, for there was his house, and there he built an altar unto the Lord.

But the stone he had set between Mizpeh and Shen kept standing. The wind moved past it. The light fell upon it morning by morning. And whoever passed it knew its name.
