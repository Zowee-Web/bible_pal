The hillside was green and the air was still. Jesus sat among the multitude, and the people were quiet, giving ear.

He spake unto them. "Take no thought for your life, what ye shall eat, or what ye shall drink. Take no thought for your body, what ye shall put on." His voice carried across the grass. "Is not the life more than meat, and the body than raiment?"

He lifted his hand toward the sky. Birds moved above the hillside, dark shapes crossing the pale blue. "Behold the fowls of the air," he said. "They sow not. They reap not. Neither do they gather into barns." He paused. "And yet your heavenly Father feedeth them."

The multitude watched the birds pass overhead. A soft wind stirred the grass beneath them.

"Are ye not much better than they?" Jesus asked.

No one answered.

He went on. "Which of you by taking thought can add one cubit unto his stature?"

The wind moved again. The people shifted where they sat upon the slope. Some leaned forward. Some held their knees to their chests.

"And why take ye thought for raiment?" Jesus said. He gestured toward the ground beside the path, where wildflowers grew in clusters — white and violet and deep gold. "Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow. They toil not, neither do they spin."

The flowers bent slightly in the breeze, their petals thin and bright.

"And yet I say unto you," he said, "that even Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these."

A murmur ran through the multitude the way a ripple runneth through water.

"Wherefore, if God so clothe the grass of the field, which today is, and tomorrow is cast into the oven, shall he not much more clothe you?" He looked across the faces before him. "O ye of little faith."

He continued. "Therefore take no thought, saying, What shall we eat? or, What shall we drink? or, Wherewithal shall we be clothed? For after all these things do the Gentiles seek, and your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things."

His voice was steady. "But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you."

The hillside was quiet. The birds still circled above the trees.

"Take therefore no thought for the morrow," Jesus said, "for the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof."

The wildflowers along the path held still. The multitude sat upon the green slope, and the wind passed low over the grass between them.
