The hillside rose gently above the road, and the grass was thick and green from the late rains. People had gathered along the slope — men and women and children, sitting on the ground or standing at the edges, their sandals dusty from the walk. The sky was clear and wide. Below the hill, the fields stretched toward the lake where the water lay flat and silver under the sun.

Jesus sat among them, higher on the slope, and the people were quiet. His voice carried in the still air.

"Do not be anxious about your life," he said, "what you will eat or what you will drink." He looked across the crowd. "Do not be anxious about your body, what you will put on."

A child shifted in his mother's lap. A man near the front rested his elbows on his knees.

"Is not life more than food," Jesus said, "and the body more than clothing?"

He paused. Then he lifted his hand toward the open sky above them. Birds moved there — small dark shapes gliding and turning against the blue. Some rose on the wind. Some dropped low over the grass.

"Look at the birds of the air," he said. "They do not sow. They do not reap. They gather nothing into barns." His hand stayed raised for a moment, tracing the path of one bird as it swept across the hillside. "And yet your heavenly Father feeds them."

The crowd followed his gaze. The birds circled once more and disappeared beyond the ridge.

"Are you not of more value than they?" Jesus asked.

No one spoke. The wind pressed gently against the grass.

He went on. "And which of you by being anxious can add a single hour to his span of life?"

The question hung in the air. A woman near the back adjusted the cloth over her head. A young man stared at his own hands.

Jesus shifted where he sat. "And why are you anxious about clothing?" he said.

He turned and gestured toward the edge of the path that wound up the hillside. Wildflowers grew there in clusters — white and violet and deep gold, their thin stems rising above the grass. The petals caught the light and held it.

"Consider the lilies of the field," he said, "how they grow." His voice was even, unhurried. "They neither toil nor spin."

The flowers swayed in the breeze. Their colors were bright against the green slope.

"Yet I tell you," Jesus said, and his voice rose slightly, "even Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these."

A murmur passed through the crowd. Some turned to look at the flowers. Others kept their eyes on Jesus.

He continued. "But if God so clothes the grass of the field, which today is alive and tomorrow is thrown into the oven, will he not much more clothe you?"

He looked across the faces before him — the weathered fishermen, the young mothers, the old men with their staffs resting across their laps.

"O you of little faith."

The words were not sharp. They were steady. The crowd was still.

"Therefore do not be anxious," he said, "saying, 'What shall we eat?' or 'What shall we drink?' or 'What shall we wear?'" He paused between each question. The syllables fell clearly in the open air. "For the Gentiles seek after all these things."

A dry leaf tumbled across the path at the base of the hill. The sun had moved higher and the shadows beneath the few scattered trees had shortened.

"And your heavenly Father knows that you need them all," Jesus said.

He leaned forward slightly. "But seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you."

The hillside was quiet. Somewhere below, near the road, a donkey brayed and fell silent. The surface of the lake in the distance caught the light and sent it back in a single white line.

Jesus spoke again. "Therefore do not be anxious about tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself."

He paused once more. The wind moved through the wildflowers along the path.

"Sufficient for the day," he said, "is its own trouble."

The crowd sat on the green slope. The lilies held their bright faces open to the sun. The birds had returned above the ridge, turning slowly in the warm air, and the hillside was still.
