Samuel was old. From his youth had he judged Israel, and now had he anointed Saul to be king over them. A king the people had desired, and a king they had been given. Samuel was stepping aside.

He stood before the people in the open air. The multitude stretched back as far as his eye could reach. Men were there, and women, and children. The elders sat near in front. Saul stood among them, higher than the rest by the head, the newness of his kingship yet upon him.

Samuel lifted up his voice. He called them to bear witness against him.

Behold me, said he. Witness ye against me before the LORD and before his anointed. Whose ox have I taken? Whose ass have I taken? Whom have I wronged, and whom have I trodden down? Of whose hand have I received any gift to turn mine eyes aside? Speak, and I will render it again.

The people looked one upon another. A murmur passed among them, and then was still.

Thou hast not wronged us, said they. Thou hast not oppressed us. Neither hast thou taken aught from any man's hand.

The LORD is witness against you, said Samuel, and his anointed is witness this day, that ye have found nothing in mine hand.

He is witness, they answered.

Then Samuel bade them sit. The multitude lowered itself in waves to the ground, the elders first, and then the rest. The field was wide and level. In the fields round about, the wheat stood cut, bound in sheaves, and waiting. The sky above was clear. The sun was high.

Then began Samuel to tell them the long tale of the LORD.

He told them of Jacob, how he went down into Egypt. He told them of their fathers, how they cried out beneath the hand of Pharaoh. He told them of Moses and of Aaron, sent forth to bring them out. He told them how the LORD had brought them through the sea by his own hand, and had set them in this place wherein they dwelt.

He told them how they had forgotten the LORD again and yet again. How they had gone after other gods. How the LORD had sold them into the hand of Sisera, captain of the host of Hazor; and into the hand of the Philistines; and into the hand of the king of Moab. And every time they cried unto the LORD. And every time the LORD raised them up deliverers. Jerubbaal. Bedan. Jephthah. Samuel himself. The LORD had plucked them out of the hand of their enemies on every side. They had dwelt in safety. They had forgotten yet again.

The people gave ear. The sun moved above them. The little ones grew restless, and were hushed. The elders sat with their heads bowed down.

Now, said Samuel, when ye saw Nahash the king of the children of Ammon come up against you, ye said unto me, Nay, but a king shall reign over us — and the LORD your God was your king.

He let the word stand a moment.

Now therefore, said he, behold the king ye have chosen, the king ye have asked for. The LORD hath set a king over you.

He turned, and the multitude turned with him. Saul stood where Samuel had placed him, his hands at his sides.

If ye will fear the LORD and serve him, said Samuel, and hearken unto his voice, and rebel not against the commandment of the LORD, then shall ye, both ye and the king that reigneth over you, continue to follow after the LORD your God.

But if ye will not obey the voice of the LORD, but rebel against his commandment, then shall the hand of the LORD be against you, as it was against your fathers.

He held his peace. The multitude was silent. The wheat in the fields round about did not stir. There was no wind.

Now stand ye still, said Samuel, and behold this great thing which the LORD will do before your eyes. Is it not the day of wheat harvest? I will call unto the LORD, and he shall send thunder and rain, that ye may know and see how great is your wickedness, which ye have wrought in the sight of the LORD, in asking you a king.

Then Samuel cried unto the LORD.

And the sky was changed. It came quickly. A darkness moved in over the field from the hills. The light went out from the wheat. The first peal of thunder broke low and near above them. Then came the rain. Hard it came. It fell upon the sheaves lying in the fields. It fell upon the heads of the people that sat upon the ground. It fell upon Samuel where he stood. It fell upon Saul.

And the people greatly feared the LORD and Samuel.

They cried out unto Samuel through the rain. Pray, said they, for thy servants unto the LORD thy God, that we die not. For unto all our sins have we added this evil, to ask us a king.

Samuel looked upon them. The rain ran down his face and his beard. The ground beneath their feet was already dark with water. The wheat in the fields was beaten down where it lay.

Fear not, said he. Ye have done all this wickedness. Yet turn ye not aside from following the LORD. Serve the LORD with all your heart.

Turn not aside, said he again. For then would ye go after empty things, which can neither profit nor deliver, for they are empty. The LORD will not forsake his people, for his great name's sake; for it hath pleased the LORD to make you a people unto himself.

And he said: as for me, God forbid that I should sin against the LORD in ceasing to pray for you. But I will teach you the good and the right way.

Only fear ye the LORD, said he, and serve him in truth with all your heart. Consider what great things he hath done for you. But if ye shall still do wickedly, ye shall be swept away, both ye and your king.

The rain came down. The wheat lay in the fields where it had been left, soaked and beaten flat. The people stood looking upon Samuel where they had been standing. Saul stood with them. Samuel had finished that which he came to say. And the thunder of the LORD was yet going forth in the hills round about them.
