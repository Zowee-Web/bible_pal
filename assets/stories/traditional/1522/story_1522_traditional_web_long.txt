Samuel was old. He had judged Israel from his youth. He had now anointed Saul as their king. The people had wanted a king. They had been given one. Samuel was stepping aside.

He stood before the people in the open air. The crowd reached back as far as he could see. Men and women and children. The elders close in front. Saul stood among them, taller than the rest, the new crown of his kingship still fresh on him.

Samuel raised his voice. He called them to witness against him.

Here I am, he said. Witness against me before the LORD and before his anointed. Whose ox have I taken? Whose donkey have I taken? Whom have I defrauded? Whom have I oppressed? From whose hand have I taken a bribe to make me look the other way? Tell me now, and I will restore it to you.

The people looked at one another. A murmur went through them and then settled.

You have not defrauded us, they said. You have not oppressed us. You have not taken anything from any man's hand.

The LORD is witness against you, Samuel said, and his anointed is witness this day, that you have found nothing in my hand.

He is witness, they answered.

Then Samuel had them sit. The crowd lowered itself to the ground in waves, the elders first, then the rest. The field was wide and flat. The wheat stood cut in the surrounding fields, gathered in sheaves, waiting. The sky above was clear. The sun was high.

Samuel began to tell them the long story of the LORD.

He told them of Jacob going down into Egypt. He told them of their fathers crying out under the hand of Pharaoh. He told them of Moses and Aaron, sent to bring them out. He told them of the LORD bringing them through the sea by his hand and setting them in this place where they now lived.

He told them how they had forgotten the LORD again and again. How they had served other gods. How the LORD had sold them into the hand of Sisera, the captain of the army of Hazor. How he had sold them into the hand of the Philistines. How he had sold them into the hand of the king of Moab. Each time they had cried unto the LORD. Each time the LORD had sent them deliverers. Jerubbaal. Bedan. Jephthah. Samuel himself. The LORD had delivered them out of the hand of their enemies on every side. They had dwelt safe. They had forgotten again.

The people listened. The sun moved over them. Children grew restless and were quieted. The elders sat with their heads bowed.

Now, Samuel said, when you saw Nahash the king of the children of Ammon come against you, you said to me, No, a king shall reign over us — when the LORD your God was your king.

He let that sit a moment.

Now then, he said, here is the king you have chosen, the king you have asked for. The LORD has set a king over you.

He turned. The crowd turned with him. Saul stood where Samuel had placed him, his hands at his sides.

If you will fear the LORD and serve him, Samuel said, and obey his voice, and not rebel against the commandment of the LORD, then both you and the king who reigns over you will continue following the LORD your God.

But if you will not obey the voice of the LORD, but rebel against the commandment of the LORD, then the hand of the LORD will be against you, as it was against your fathers.

He paused. The crowd was silent. The wheat in the surrounding fields did not move. There was no wind.

Now stand still, Samuel said, and see this great thing which the LORD will do before your eyes. Is it not wheat harvest today? I will call on the LORD, and he will send thunder and rain, that you may know and see that your wickedness is great, which you have done in the sight of the LORD, in asking for a king.

Then Samuel called unto the LORD.

The sky changed. It happened quickly. A darkness moved in over the field from the hills. The light went out of the wheat. The first crack of thunder came down over them, low and very close. Then the rain. It came hard. It came on the sheaves lying in the fields. It came on the heads of the people sitting on the ground. It came on Samuel where he stood. It came on Saul.

The people greatly feared the LORD and Samuel.

They called out to Samuel through the rain. Pray for your servants to the LORD your God, they said, that we do not die. For we have added to all our sins this evil, to ask for a king.

Samuel looked at them. The rain ran down his face and his beard. The ground at their feet was already dark with water. The wheat in the fields was being beaten down where it lay.

Do not be afraid, he said. You have done all this wickedness. Yet do not turn aside from following the LORD. Serve the LORD with all your heart.

Do not turn aside, he said again. For then you would go after empty things which cannot profit or deliver, for they are empty. The LORD will not forsake his people, for his great name's sake, because it has pleased the LORD to make you his people.

Then he said: as for me, far be it from me that I should sin against the LORD in ceasing to pray for you. But I will teach you the good and the right way.

Only fear the LORD, he said, and serve him in truth with all your heart. Consider what great things he has done for you. But if you still do wickedly, you shall be swept away, both you and your king.

The rain came down. The wheat lay in the fields where it had been left, soaked and flat. The people stood looking at Samuel where they had been standing. Saul stood with them. Samuel had finished what he came to say. The LORD's thunder was still going off in the hills around them.
