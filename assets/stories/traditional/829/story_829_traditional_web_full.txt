Early in the morning, Hagar stood at the doorway, the light just brushing the edges of the tents. Her son, Ishmael, waited beside her, quiet and watchful. Abraham rose, bringing bread and a skin of water. He placed the provisions in her hands and looked at her, saying nothing more. The camp stood silent, its people distant. With heavy steps, Hagar set off into the wild land. Ishmael followed.

The sand beneath their feet was pale and hot, already warming as the sun rose higher. The wilderness of Beersheba sprawled before them—bare, endless, and empty. They walked beneath an open sky. The only sound was the padding of feet and the shifting of the water skin on Hagar’s shoulder.

As the hours passed, the sun pressed down, relentless and harsh. The bread was eaten, crumb by crumb, the water sipped in thin hope that it might last. Each time Hagar lifted the skin, it felt lighter.

The day burned. The air shimmered. Ishmael’s steps slowed, his face marked with thirst and fatigue. Sweat traced his cheeks, dust clung to his skin. Still, no shade, no sound of water, not even the whisper of a breeze among the stones.

Hagar uncorked the water skin and tipped it. Not a drop remained. She weighed it in her palm—it was empty, as empty as the wilderness all around. She looked at her son, seeing that he could go no farther.

A stand of bushes appeared—a small patch of thorns and scant shade in a world of glare. Hagar guided Ishmael to it and made him lie beneath. She spread what little shelter the bush offered above him. His eyes were closed, his lips parched.

She stepped away. Hagar could not bear to watch her child die. She moved a good way off, as far as a bowshot, her heart tight and sore. The sound of the boy’s crying drifted to her under that burning sky.

Sitting apart, Hagar lifted her voice. Her sorrow broke loose, a sound caught between desperation and exhaustion. Yet the wilderness gave no answer, only the distant crying of her son.

And then, in the silence, the Lord heard the voice of the boy.

The angel of God called to Hagar from heaven, breaking open the loneliness of the wilderness. “What troubles you, Hagar? Don’t be afraid, for God has heard the voice of the boy where he is. Get up! Lift up the boy and hold him fast with your hand. For I will make him a great nation.”

Hagar’s breath caught. The ache in her body faded for a moment as new hope sparked in her chest. She got to her feet, strength returning. She turned to her son and reached for him, hands trembling.

Her eyes opened to something new—a glint of sunlight on water. There, not far from where the boy lay, a well stood, hidden until this very moment. Hagar hurried over, her steps quickened by relief. She filled the skin with fresh water.

Returning to Ishmael, she knelt and lifted his head. She gave him water, drop by precious drop. His lips parted, and he drank, his strength restored with every swallow, his breath steadying in the dry air.

The wilderness held them, but it did not destroy them. The water from the well sustained them. The day’s glare lessened as hope reclaimed a place in Hagar’s heart.

The boy grew strong once more, stood, and took his place beside his mother. Days of wandering became days of living, the wilderness no longer only a place of loss but of survival and promise. Ishmael went on, surviving in the wilderness, learning its ways.

God was with the boy as he grew. Ishmael became strong in the wilderness, finding his place in the dry lands. In time, he became an archer, skilled with the bow.
