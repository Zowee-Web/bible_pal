Abraham rose up early in the morning, as the first light spread across his tents. He placed bread in Hagar's hand, and a bottle of water upon her shoulder. Quietly, Hagar and her child went forth into the wilderness of Beersheba.

The sand stretched on before them, hot and without end. The sun climbed higher, burning their faces and their backs. Hagar shielded her eyes and led Ishmael onward, though their feet were sore. With every hour, the bottle grew lighter.

Soon, the bread was spent. Then the water also, every last drop, was spent in the bottle. The wilderness shimmered with dry, glaring heat. Hagar searched the horizon, but beheld no well and no shade. Her son waxed weak, and his steps faltered. At length, neither could walk farther.

With trembling hands, Hagar cast the child under one of the shrubs. The branches cast but a little shadow, yet it was all she had. His face was pale and streaked with dust, his voice but a thin whisper. Hagar could no longer bear to behold the death of the child. She went, and sat her down over against him a good way off, as it were a bowshot. And she wept.

Across the wilderness, the voice of the lad rose, crying out in the heat — a sound borne afar over the empty sand.

And God heard the voice of the lad. The angel of God called unto Hagar out of heaven, saying, "What aileth thee, Hagar? fear not; for God hath heard the voice of the lad where he is. Arise, lift up the lad, and hold him in thine hand; for I will make him a great nation."

Hagar wiped the tears from her eyes and lifted up her face. And God opened her eyes, and she saw a well of water — a glint among the rocks and shrubs. She made haste, and filled the bottle. Returning unto her son, she gave him to drink. Strength returned unto Ishmael. The wilderness seemed no longer so empty.

And God was with the lad as he grew. He dwelt in the wilderness, and became an archer. He dwelt in the wilderness of Paran. And his mother took him a wife out of the land of Egypt.
