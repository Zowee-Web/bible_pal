Early in the morning, as first light spread across the tents of Abraham, Hagar rose. Abraham placed bread in her hand, and a heavy skin of water upon her shoulder. Quietly, Hagar and her son set out into the wilderness of Beersheba.

Sand stretched ahead, hot and endless. With every step, the sun climbed higher, burning their faces and backs. Hagar shielded her eyes and guided Ishmael, pressing forward though their feet ached. Each hour, their water skins grew lighter.

Soon, the bread was gone. Then the water, every last drop, was finished. The desert pulsed with dry, shimmering heat. Hagar searched the horizon, but saw no wells and no shade. Her son grew weak, his steps faltered. Finally, neither could walk farther.

With trembling hands, Hagar guided Ishmael to a low bush. The branches barely cast a shadow, but it was all she had. She helped her child sit. His face was pale and streaked with dust. His voice was only a thin whisper. Hagar could no longer bear to watch her son die. She moved away, about a bowshot’s distance, and sat down. She wept.

Across the wilderness, the boy’s voice rose, crying out in the heat—a sound carried far over the empty sand.

God heard the voice of the boy. From heaven, the angel of God called to Hagar, saying, “What troubles you, Hagar? Do not be afraid. God has heard the voice of the boy where he is. Rise, lift up the boy and hold his hand, for I will make him a great nation.”

Hagar wiped the tears from her eyes. She looked up. Suddenly, she saw a well of water shimmering nearby—a glint among the rocks and shrubs. She hurried. Quickly, she filled the skin with cool water. Returning to her son, she gave him drink. Strength returned to Ishmael. The desert no longer seemed so empty.

God was with the boy as he grew. He lived in the wilderness and became an archer. He lived in the wilderness of Paran. His mother got a wife for him out of the land of Egypt.
