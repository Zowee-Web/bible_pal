Michal, Saul’s daughter, loved David. When Saul heard this, he was pleased. He said, “I will give her to him, that she may be a snare to him and that the hand of the Philistines may be against him.” A second time Saul told David, “You shall today be my son-in-law.”

Saul ordered his servants to speak to David secretly. He told them what to say: “Behold, the king has delight in you, and all his servants love you. Now therefore be the king’s son-in-law.”

Saul’s servants spoke those words in the ears of David. He answered, “Does it seem to you a light thing to be the king’s son-in-law, since I am a poor man and little known?”

The servants went back to Saul and reported the answer David had given them. Saul told them to carry another message to David: “The king desires no dowry except one hundred foreskins of the Philistines, to be avenged of the king’s enemies.” Saul’s purpose was to make David fall by the Philistines’ hand.

When the servants delivered this message, David was pleased at the prospect of becoming the king’s son-in-law. The deadline had not yet passed when David rose and went out with his men. They killed two hundred men of the Philistines. David brought their foreskins, and the full number was given to Saul so that David might become the king’s son-in-law.

Saul gave him Michal his daughter as his wife. The king saw and knew that Yahweh was with David; and Michal, Saul’s daughter, loved David, the man she had married. Saul’s fear of David grew still greater, and he remained David’s enemy continually.

The princes of the Philistines went out. Whenever they did, David conducted himself more wisely than all Saul’s servants. His name came to be highly esteemed.
