Michal, the daughter of Saul, loved David. The report pleased her father, who said he would give her unto David for a snare, that the hand of the Philistines might be against him. Saul told David, “Thou shalt this day be my son in law in the one of the twain.”

Then Saul charged his servants to commune with David secretly. He gave them these words to speak: “Behold, the king hath delight in thee, and all his servants love thee: now therefore be the king’s son in law.”

The servants spoke these words in David’s ears. David answered, “Seemeth it to you a light thing to be a king’s son in law, seeing that I am a poor man, and lightly esteemed?”

Saul’s servants returned to their master and told him how David had answered them. The king sent them back to David with his demand: “The king desireth not any dowry, but an hundred foreskins of the Philistines, to be avenged of the king’s enemies.” Yet Saul thought to make David fall by the hand of the Philistines.

The message pleased David well, for he would become the king’s son in law. The appointed days had not expired when David arose and went with his men. They slew two hundred men of the Philistines. David brought their foreskins, which were given in full tale to the king, that he might become his son in law. Saul then gave Michal his daughter unto David to wife.

Saul saw and knew that the Lord was with David, and that Michal loved him. He became yet more afraid of David, and his enmity against him continued.

When the princes of the Philistines went forth, David behaved himself more wisely than all the servants of Saul. His name was much set by.
