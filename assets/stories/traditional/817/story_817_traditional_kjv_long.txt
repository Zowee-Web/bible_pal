In the great city of Babylon, King Darius ruled with care over his people. The king dwelt in a grand stone palace with tall pillars and wide, echoing halls. Daniel was one of the king's trusted helpers. He was wise and kind, and the king loved him greatly. Every morning, golden sunlight danced upon the floor of the palace. Daniel came unto the court early, ever ready to do his work. His heart was strong with courage, and he loved to pray unto God three times each day.

The other helpers and rulers beheld how much the king loved Daniel. They grew envious because Daniel was honest and did his work with diligence. They sought some fault in Daniel, that they might tell the king. But Daniel did all things with care. He neither cheated nor lied. His fellow officials walked the cool stone halls, whispering one to another. "We shall not find any occasion against Daniel except it be concerning his God," they said.

On a certain day, the officials devised a crafty plan. They hasted unto the king in the bright throne room. "O King Darius, live for ever," they said, bowing low. "All the rulers have consulted together that thou shouldest make a new statute. It shall be that for thirty days, no man may pray unto any god or man save thee, O king. And whosoever breaketh the law shall be cast into the den of lions." The king hearkened and thought it good. He perceived not that they sought to ensnare Daniel. He signed the writing with care. In the kingdom of the Medes and Persians, when the king made a law, it could not be changed.

Soon the tidings of the law spread throughout the city. Daniel heard of the new statute, but his heart was not moved. Three times a day, as he did aforetime, Daniel went up unto his chamber. His window was open toward Jerusalem. He kneeled and prayed unto God, giving thanks. Though Daniel might have feared, he was brave. His love for God was greater than any fear.

The envious officials slipped through the halls of the palace and climbed the steps unto Daniel's chamber. They peered in and beheld Daniel upon his knees, praying and speaking unto God. At once they hasted unto the king's court. "O King Darius," they said, "hast thou not signed a statute that no man may pray unto any god or man save thee for thirty days?" The king answered, "Yea, the law cannot be changed." Then the officials said, "Daniel regardeth not thee, O king. He yet prayeth three times a day unto his God!"

When King Darius heard these words, he was sore displeased. He loved Daniel and would have delivered him. All the day long, the king sought a way to deliver Daniel, but he found none. The officials gathered round about the king and put him in remembrance, "Remember, O king, the law cannot be changed." As the sun went down and the cool night came, King Darius commanded that Daniel be brought unto the den of lions. The den was a deep pit of stone, with a heavy stone lid thereon.

King Darius looked upon Daniel with kind eyes and said, "Thy God, whom thou servest so faithfully, will deliver thee." The servants led Daniel unto the den. The stone walls were cold. Down below, the lions paced to and fro, their paws soft upon the ground. Their deep growls rumbled in the chill air. Gentle moonlight spilled into the pit as Daniel was lowered therein. The king's men rolled a great stone over the top and sealed it with the king's signet. The savour of earth filled the night. It was still within the pit of stone, save for the soft breathing of the lions.

That night, King Darius could not sleep. He fasted, neither were instruments of musick brought before him. In his heart, he thought upon Daniel and hoped that God would keep him safe. Meanwhile, in the den of lions, Daniel was not alone. God sent his angel to be with Daniel. The presence of the angel was as warm light in the dark pit. The lions looked upon Daniel but hurt him not. They lay down peaceably, and Daniel was safe through the night. His heart was calm and brave.

When the first golden rays of morning shone upon the city, King Darius arose very early. He went in haste unto the den of lions. His feet trod softly upon the floors of the palace, and his royal robe swept behind him. When he came unto the den, his voice trembled as he cried out, "O Daniel, servant of the living God, is thy God, whom thou servest continually, able to deliver thee from the lions?"

From within the den, Daniel answered with a clear and joyful voice, "O king, live for ever! My God hath sent his angel, and hath shut the lions' mouths. They have not hurt me, forasmuch as innocency was found in me before God, and also before thee, O king, have I done no hurt."

At these words, King Darius was filled with gladness. He commanded that Daniel be taken up out of the den at once. The stone was rolled away, and strong hands drew Daniel up out of the cool, dark pit. Daniel's garments were not torn, neither was any hurt found upon him. When the people beheld that Daniel was safe, they marvelled greatly. The strong sunlight warmed Daniel's face as he stepped back upon the stones of the palace.

King Darius praised the God of Daniel. He sent a writing unto all peoples and nations in his vast kingdom. "Peace be multiplied unto you! I make a decree that in every part of my kingdom, men must honour and fear the God of Daniel. For he is the living God, and stedfast for ever. His kingdom shall not be destroyed. He delivereth and rescueth. He worketh signs and wonders. He hath delivered Daniel from the power of the lions."

So Daniel prospered in the days of King Darius, and through the reign of Cyrus the Persian. He remained faithful and brave, serving God with all his heart. Daniel was safe, and both he and King Darius knew that with God, there is ever hope and courage, even in the hardest of times.

The people of Babylon heard the king's words and marvelled at that which was come to pass. Whispers of Daniel's courage and God's mighty deliverance flowed through every market and house. Children pointed at Daniel with wide eyes, while neighbours greeted him with new reverence. Some, who had doubted aforetime, now prayed softly unto the God of Daniel, hoping to know such strength in their own hearts. Even those envious officials grew silent, for they beheld that true honour came not from crafty devices, but from righteous living.

As the days passed, Daniel returned unto his work, humble and gentle as ever. He yet kneeled at his window, giving thanks, as he did aforetime. King Darius oft called for Daniel's wisdom, and together they made counsel for the good of the people. And when the city was hushed at evening, Daniel found peace in the rustling of the palm leaves and the cool air that swept down from the hills. He remembered the soft breathing of the lions in the dark and smiled, for he knew he was never alone. In Babylon, hope took root quietly, and the people met each new day with steadier hearts.
