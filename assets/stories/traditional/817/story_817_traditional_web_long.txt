In the great city of Babylon, King Darius ruled with care over his people. The king lived in a grand stone palace with tall pillars and wide, echoing halls. Daniel was one of the king’s trusted helpers. He was wise and kind, and the king liked him very much. Every morning, golden sunlight danced across the palace floor. Daniel came to the court early, always ready to do his work. His heart was strong with courage, and he loved to pray to God three times each day.

The other helpers and leaders saw how much the king liked Daniel. They grew jealous because Daniel was honest and did his best. They looked for something Daniel did wrong so they could tell the king. But Daniel did everything with care. He never cheated or lied. His fellow officials walked the cool stone halls, whispering together. “We will not find any fault with Daniel unless it is about his God,” they decided.

One day, the officials made a sneaky plan. They hurried to the king in the bright throne room. “O King Darius, live forever,” they said bowing low. “All the leaders have agreed that you should make a new law. It should say that for thirty days, no one may pray to any god or man except you, O King. Anyone who breaks the law will be put into the lions’ den.” The king listened and thought it sounded good. He did not know they wanted to trap Daniel. He carefully signed the law. In the kingdom of the Medes and Persians, once the king made a law, it could not be changed.

Soon news of the law spread across the city. Daniel knew about the new law, but his heart did not change. Three times a day, as always, Daniel went up to his room. His window was open toward Jerusalem. He knelt and prayed to God, thanking Him. Even though Daniel could have been afraid, he was brave. His love for God was bigger than any fear.

The jealous officials slipped through the palace halls and climbed the steps to Daniel’s room. They peeked in and saw Daniel on his knees, praying and talking to God. At once, they rushed to the king’s court. “O King Darius,” they said, “did you not sign a law saying no one may pray to any god or man except you for thirty days?” The king answered, “Yes, the law cannot be changed.” Then the officials said, “Daniel pays no attention to you, O King. He still prays three times a day to his God!”

When King Darius heard this, he was very sad. He liked Daniel and wanted to help him. All day long, the king tried to find a way to save Daniel, but he found none. The officials gathered around the king and reminded him, “Remember, O King, the law cannot be changed.” As the sun set and the cool night came, King Darius ordered Daniel to be brought to the lions’ den. The den was a deep, stone pit with a heavy stone lid.

King Darius looked at Daniel with kind eyes and said, “Your God, whom you serve so faithfully, will deliver you.” Servants led Daniel to the den. The stone walls were cold. Down below, the lions paced back and forth, their paws soft on the ground. Their deep growls rumbled in the chilly air. Gentle moonlight spilled into the pit as Daniel was lowered inside. The king’s men rolled a heavy stone over the top and sealed it with the king’s ring. The smell of earth filled the night. It was quiet in the stone pit, except for the soft breathing of the lions.

That night, King Darius could not sleep. He fasted and would not let music be played. In his mind, he thought of Daniel and hoped that God would keep him safe. Meanwhile, in the lions’ den, Daniel was not alone. God sent His angel to be with Daniel. The angel’s presence was like warm light in the dark pit. The lions looked at Daniel but did not harm him. They lay down peacefully, and Daniel was safe through the night. His heart was calm and brave.

When the first golden rays of morning shone over the city, King Darius got up quickly. He hurried to the lions’ den. His feet padded softly on the palace floors, and his royal robe swished behind him. When he reached the den, his voice shook as he called out, “Daniel, servant of the living God, has your God, whom you serve faithfully, saved you from the lions?”

From inside the den, Daniel answered with a clear and joyful voice, “O King, live forever! My God sent His angel and shut the lions’ mouths. They did not hurt me because I was found innocent before God, and I have done no wrong before you, O King.”

At these words, King Darius was filled with happiness. He ordered that Daniel be lifted out of the den at once. The stone was rolled away, and strong hands pulled Daniel up from the cool, dark pit. Daniel’s clothes were not torn, and he had not a single scratch. When the people saw that Daniel was safe, they were amazed. The strong sunlight warmed Daniel’s face as he stepped back onto the palace stones.

King Darius praised the God of Daniel. He sent a message to all the people and nations in his vast kingdom. “May you prosper greatly! I make a decree that in every part of my kingdom, people must respect and honor the God of Daniel. For He is the living God and endures forever. His kingdom will not be destroyed. He saves and rescues. He performs signs and wonders. He rescued Daniel from the power of the lions.”

So Daniel continued to do well during the days of King Darius and through the rule of Cyrus the Persian. He stayed faithful and brave, serving God with all his heart. Daniel was safe, and both he and King Darius knew that with God, there was always hope and courage, even in the hardest times.

The people of Babylon heard the king’s words and marveled at what had happened. Whispers of Daniel’s courage and God’s mighty rescue flowed through every market and home. Children pointed at Daniel with wide eyes, while neighbors greeted him with new respect. Some, who had doubted before, now prayed softly to Daniel’s God, hoping to know such strength in their own hearts. Even those jealous officials grew quiet, for they saw that true honor did not come from clever schemes, but from living rightly.

As the days passed, Daniel returned to his work, humble and gentle as ever. He still knelt at his window, giving thanks, just as before. King Darius often called for Daniel’s wisdom, and together they planned for the good of the people. And when the city hushed at evening, Daniel found peace in the rustle of palm leaves and the cool air that swept down from the hills. He remembered the lions’ soft breathing in the dark and smiled—because he knew he was never alone. In Babylon, hope quietly took root, and the people faced each new day with steadier hearts.
