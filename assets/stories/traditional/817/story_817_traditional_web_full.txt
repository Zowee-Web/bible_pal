In the tall, sparkling palace of Babylon, a wise man named Daniel lived and worked. Daniel was different from many others because he loved God and followed Him in everything. He was honest, kind, and brave. King Darius liked Daniel very much and made him an important helper. Daniel helped the king take care of the kingdom and the people. He worked harder than all the other helpers, and soon the king thought about making Daniel the most important helper in the whole land.

But not everyone liked this. Some royal officials were jealous of Daniel, because he was so good and the king wanted him to lead. They watched Daniel carefully, hoping to catch him doing something wrong, but Daniel never did wrong things. They saw that Daniel always prayed to God. Every day, three times, Daniel went to his room, opened his window facing Jerusalem, and prayed, thanking God. The men knew this was the one thing that made Daniel different.

The officials made a plan. They went to King Darius, pretending to care about him. “King Darius, you are so great! We think you should make a new rule. For thirty days, everyone must only pray to you, O king. If anyone prays to someone else, they should be sent to the den of lions.” King Darius did not think this rule would hurt Daniel. He agreed and made the law. The new law was written down and stamped so that it could not be changed, not even by the king himself.

Daniel soon heard about the new law. But Daniel was not afraid. He went to his room just as always, opened his window, and prayed to God. He thanked God with gladness. The officials rushed to Daniel’s room and saw him praying. Quickly, they went back to the king. “O King Darius,” they said, “did you not make a rule that no one may pray to anyone but you for thirty days?” The king answered, “Yes, that is the law.” They told him, “Daniel does not obey the law. He is praying to his God three times every day.”

King Darius was very sad when he heard this. He loved Daniel and did not want him to be punished. All day he tried to find a way to save him, but the law could not be changed. When evening came, the king gave the command. Daniel would have to go to the lions’ den. Daniel was brought to the place where the lions stayed. The den was dark and deep. The lions walked around inside, rumbling and growling. The king said to Daniel, “May your God, whom you serve always, save you.” Daniel was put into the den, and a big stone was rolled across the entrance. The king’s seal was pressed into the clay on the stone so that no one could move it.

That night, the palace was quiet, but King Darius could not sleep. He worried about Daniel and did not eat or listen to music. His bed felt cold and hard. As soon as the sun rose, the king hurried to the lions’ den. He called out, “Daniel, servant of the living God, was your God able to save you from the lions?”

From inside the den came Daniel’s voice. “O king, live forever. My God sent his angel and shut the lions’ mouths. The lions did not hurt me because I was innocent before God, and I have done no wrong to you, O king.”

The king was filled with joy. He ordered that Daniel be lifted out of the den. When Daniel came out, everyone could see that he was safe and the lions had not even scratched him. The king knew then that Daniel’s God watched over him.

King Darius made a new order. He told everyone in his whole kingdom to show respect to Daniel’s God. “He is the living God. His kingdom will never be destroyed. He saves and does miracles. He saved Daniel from the lions.” From that day, Daniel was safe in the palace, and he continued to serve the king and his God with a happy heart.

Peace returned, and hope blossomed quietly within Daniel’s heart as golden morning light filled the palace halls.
