Joshua called for the priests and gave them their orders. Seven of them were to carry seven trumpets made from rams' horns. They would walk before the ark of the LORD. Armed men would go ahead of the priests. A rear guard would follow behind the ark. The trumpets were to sound without stopping.

They assembled at the edge of the camp and moved toward the city.

Jericho stood closed up tight. Its walls were stone and mud brick, broad enough for a man to walk on top. No one went out. No one came in. The gates were shut and barred.

The armed men went first. Their sandals struck the packed ground in steady rhythm. Behind them came the seven priests, each one holding a ram's horn trumpet to his lips. The sound was low and constant, a raw drone that carried across the dry plain. Behind the priests came the ark of the LORD, borne on poles across the shoulders of its carriers. Behind the ark walked the rear guard, weapons ready, faces forward.

They circled the city once. The trumpets never stopped. The people of Jericho watched from the walls above. No one in the marching column spoke. When the circuit was complete, they returned to camp.

They did this on the second day. And the third. And the fourth, the fifth, and the sixth. One circuit each day. The trumpets sounding. The column moving in order. Then back to camp.

On the seventh day they rose at dawn, when the sky was still pale and the air still cool. They formed the same column. Armed men, then priests with trumpets, then the ark, then the rear guard. They marched around the city. Once. Twice. Three times. Four. Five. Six times around. The trumpets blew without ceasing. The dust rose under their feet and hung in the morning air.

On the seventh circuit, the priests blew a long blast on the trumpets.

Joshua's voice broke through the sound. "Shout! For the LORD has given you the city!"

The people opened their mouths and shouted. The sound that came from them was enormous, a roar from every throat at once, rising over the trumpet blast.

The walls shuddered. The broad stone and brick face of Jericho cracked and buckled and fell straight down, collapsing flat against the ground in a cloud of dust and broken rubble. Every man went straight ahead, up over the fallen stone, into the city. They took it.
