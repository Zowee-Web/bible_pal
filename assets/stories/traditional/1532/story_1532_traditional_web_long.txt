Joshua called for the priests. He gave them their orders in plain terms. Seven of them were to carry seven trumpets made from rams' horns. They would march before the ark of the LORD. Armed men would go ahead of the priests. A rear guard would follow behind the ark. The trumpets were to sound without stopping.

The priests took up the horns. They held them carefully, checking the mouthpieces, settling the weight against their forearms. The armed men gathered at the front of the column. Their shields hung at their sides. Their spear shafts caught the morning light. The bearers stepped beneath the poles of the ark and lifted it onto their shoulders, gripping the poles with both hands. They tested the balance, shifted their footing, steadied the load. The rear guard fell into position behind them. The formation stretched out along the camp road, hundreds of men in ordered ranks, the line so long that the men at the back could barely make out the men at the front.

Jericho stood ahead of them across the open ground.

The city walls were stone and mud brick, thick and tall, broad enough for dwellings built along the top. Smoke from cooking fires inside the city rose in thin lines above the parapet. The gates were shut and barred. No one went out. No one came in. The city had sealed itself against Israel. From the walls, sentries watched the column form. Their figures were small against the sky, leaning forward over the stones.

The armed men moved first. Their sandals struck the packed ground in steady rhythm, weapons held close, faces turned toward the city. Behind them came the seven priests. Each one raised a ram's horn trumpet to his lips and blew. The sound was low and constant, a raw droning note that pushed out across the dry plain and struck the stone walls and came back. It did not stop. Behind the priests came the ark of the LORD, carried on its poles, the wood worn smooth where the bearers' hands gripped it. The gold of the ark caught the sun in dull flashes between the men's shoulders. Behind the ark came the rear guard, armed and silent, eyes forward.

They circled the city. The dust rose beneath their feet and drifted in the still air. It settled on their tunics and on the priests' beards and on the poles of the ark. The trumpets sounded the whole way around. The people on the walls above looked down at the column passing below them. Some pointed. Some called out to one another. No one in the marching line spoke a word. One full circuit. When they completed it, the column turned and marched back to camp.

The sun set. The camp settled. Cookfires were lit. The trumpets were laid down.

On the second day, they did the same thing. The armed men went first. The priests blew the trumpets. The ark followed. The rear guard came last. One circuit around the city. Then back to camp.

On the third day, the same. On the fourth. On the fifth. On the sixth day, the column circled Jericho once more, the trumpets droning, the feet beating the ground flat, the dust rising and falling. The walls of the city still stood. The gates remained shut. A path was wearing itself into the earth where the column passed, a wide track of trampled ground that ran the full circumference of the city.

On the seventh day, they rose at dawn.

The sky was pale. The air was cool and dry. Light was just reaching the tops of the walls when the column formed again. Armed men at the front. Seven priests with seven trumpets behind them. The ark behind the priests. The rear guard at the end. The men checked their footing on the worn path and waited for the order.

They began to march.

The trumpets sounded. The column moved around the city. First circuit. The horn blasts echoed off the walls and rolled back across the plain. The armed men's feet hit the ground in rhythm. The bearers carried the ark steady on their shoulders, their forearms tight on the poles.

Second circuit. The sun climbed higher. The dust thickened along the path they were wearing into the earth. The priests' cheeks were tight against the horns, the sound unwavering. Their breath came in measured pulls between the long notes.

Third circuit. The sentries on the walls watched the same column pass beneath them again. They did not move. They did not call out. Fourth circuit. The shadow of the wall slid across the marching men as the sun moved. The temperature climbed. Fifth circuit. Sweat darkened the tunics of the bearers under the weight of the ark. Their footsteps stayed even. Sixth circuit. The trumpets had not stopped since dawn. The drone had become the sound of the air itself.

They began the seventh circuit.

The column moved along the familiar path. Every man kept his place. The priests blew the rams' horns. The sound was the same low drone that had filled the air for seven days. The armed men at the front rounded the final corner. The bearers followed. The rear guard came behind. The whole column completed the seventh pass around Jericho.

The priests blew a long blast. The sound swelled and held. It stretched out longer than any note they had blown before, the seven horns sustaining together.

Joshua raised his voice above the horns.

"Shout! For the LORD has given you the city!"

The people shouted. Every voice at once. The sound erupted from the column, from the ranks, from every throat — a single massive roar that rose over the trumpet blasts and struck the walls of Jericho.

The walls moved. The stone and brick face shuddered, cracked along its length, and fell. It did not topple inward or outward in pieces. It went straight down, collapsing flat against the ground. The impact sent dust and rubble outward in a thick cloud that rolled across the plain and over the marching column. Stone ground against stone as the weight settled. Where the wall had stood, there was open ground, broken stone lying flat, the city exposed.

Every man went straight ahead. They climbed over the fallen rubble, up and through the dust, into the city from every side at once, each man moving directly forward from where he stood. They took Jericho.
