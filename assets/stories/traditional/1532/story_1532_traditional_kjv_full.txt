Joshua summoned the priests and gave unto them their charge in plain speech. Seven among them should bear seven trumpets of rams' horns, and they should go before the ark of the LORD. Armed men should march in the forefront of the priests, and a rear guard should come after the ark. The trumpets, said he, shall not cease their sounding.

The priests took up the horns. The armed men gathered at the head of the column. The bearers raised the ark upon their shoulders, gripping the staves with both hands, and the rear guard fell in behind. The order of them stretched along the camp road — hundreds of men in ranks appointed.

And Jericho stood before them across the open plain.

The walls thereof were of stone and brick, thick and high, broad enough that dwellings were builded upon them. The gates were shut and barred. None went out. None came in. The city had sealed itself against Israel. From the walls the watchmen looked down upon the column as it gathered.

The armed men went forth first. Their sandals smote the packed ground in steady measure, weapons held close, their faces set toward the city. After them came the seven priests. Each man lifted a ram's horn unto his lips and blew, and the sound thereof was low and unbroken — a raw droning note that went out across the dry plain and smote the stone walls and returned again. It ceased not. Behind the priests came the ark of the LORD, borne upon its staves, the wood worn smooth where the bearers' hands had gripped it. And behind the ark came the rear guard, armed and silent, their eyes set forward.

They compassed the city. The dust rose up beneath their feet and hung in the still air. The trumpets sounded the whole way round. The people upon the walls looked down upon the column passing beneath. No man in the marching line spake a word. One full circuit. And when they had finished it, the column turned and marched back to the camp.

The sun went down. The camp lay still.

On the second day, even so did they. The armed men went first. The priests blew the trumpets. The ark followed after. The rear guard came last. One circuit about the city. Then back unto the camp.

On the third day, the same. On the fourth. On the fifth. On the sixth day the column compassed Jericho yet again, the trumpets droning, the feet beating the ground flat, the dust rising and falling. The walls of the city stood yet. The gates remained shut.

On the seventh day, they rose at the dawning.

The sky was pale. The air was cool and dry. The light was but newly come upon the tops of the walls when the column formed once more. Armed men in the forefront. Seven priests with seven trumpets behind them. The ark behind the priests. The rear guard at the end.

And they began to march.

The trumpets sounded. The column moved about the city. First circuit. The blasts of the horns rang back from the walls. The feet of the armed men smote the ground in measure. The bearers carried the ark steady upon their shoulders.

Second circuit. The sun climbed higher. The dust thickened along the path they were wearing into the earth. The priests' cheeks were drawn tight against the horns, and the sound wavered not.

Third circuit. The watchmen upon the walls beheld the same column passing beneath them yet again. Fourth circuit. The shadow of the wall slid across the marching men as the sun moved on. Fifth circuit. Sweat darkened the tunics of the bearers beneath the burden of the ark. Sixth circuit. The trumpets had not ceased since the dawn.

And they began the seventh circuit.

The column moved along the familiar way. Every man kept his appointed place. The priests blew the rams' horns. The sound was that same low drone which had filled the air seven days together. The armed men at the front rounded the final corner. The rear guard followed after. And the whole column finished the seventh compassing of Jericho.

Then blew the priests a long blast, and the sound swelled and held.

And Joshua lifted up his voice above the horns.

"Shout! For the LORD hath given you the city!"

And the people shouted. Every voice as one. The sound burst forth from the column, from the ranks, from every throat — a single mighty roar that rose above the blasts of the trumpets and smote the walls of Jericho.

And the walls moved. The face of stone and brick shuddered, and cracked along its length, and fell. It toppled not inward, neither outward in pieces. It went straight down, and lay flat against the ground. The shock of it sent dust and rubble outward in a thick cloud. Where the wall had stood, there was open ground, broken stone lying flat, the city laid bare.

And every man went straight before him. They clambered over the fallen rubble, up and through the dust, into the city from every side at once, each man pressing forward from the place where he stood. And they took Jericho.
