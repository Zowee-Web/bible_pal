Joshua called forth the priests, and he set their charge before them in plain speech. Seven among them should bear seven trumpets fashioned of rams' horns. Before the ark of the LORD they should go. Armed men should march at the head, and a rear guard should come behind the ark. And the trumpets — they should sound, and not cease.

The priests took up the horns. Carefully they handled them, trying the mouthpieces, settling the weight along the forearm. The armed men gathered at the front of the column, their shields hanging at their sides, their spear shafts catching the light of the morning. The bearers stepped beneath the staves of the ark and lifted it upon their shoulders, gripping the poles with both hands. They tried the balance, shifted their footing, steadied the load. The rear guard fell in behind. Along the camp road the formation stretched out, hundreds of men in ordered ranks, the line so long that they that were hindmost could scarce discern them that were foremost.

And Jericho stood before them across the open ground.

The walls of the city were of stone and of mud brick, thick and high, broad enough that dwellings were built along the top thereof. Smoke from the cooking fires within rose in thin lines above the parapet. The gates were shut and barred. None went out. None came in. The city had sealed itself against Israel. From the walls the sentries watched the column gather, their figures small against the sky, leaning forward over the stones.

The armed men moved first. Their sandals struck the packed earth in steady measure, weapons held close, faces set toward the city. And behind them came the seven priests. Each man lifted a ram's horn unto his lips and blew. Low was the sound, and unceasing, a raw droning note that went out across the dry plain and smote the stone walls and returned. It did not cease. Behind the priests came the ark of the LORD, borne upon its staves, the wood worn smooth where the bearers' hands gripped it. The gold of the ark caught the sun in dull flashes between the shoulders of the men. And behind the ark came the rear guard, armed and silent, their eyes set forward.

They compassed the city. The dust rose beneath their feet and drifted in the still air. It settled upon their tunics, and upon the beards of the priests, and upon the staves of the ark. The trumpets sounded the whole way round. They that stood upon the walls looked down upon the column passing beneath them. Some pointed. Some called one to another. But in the marching line not a word was spoken. One full circuit. And when it was finished, the column turned and went back unto the camp.

The sun went down. The camp settled. Cookfires were kindled. The trumpets were laid down.

On the second day, even so they did. The armed men went first. The priests blew the trumpets. The ark followed. The rear guard came last. One circuit about the city. Then back to the camp.

On the third day, the same. On the fourth. On the fifth. On the sixth day, the column compassed Jericho once more, the trumpets droning, the feet beating the ground flat, the dust rising and falling. Yet the walls of the city stood. The gates remained shut. A path was wearing itself into the earth where the column passed, a wide track of trampled ground that ran the full compass of the city.

On the seventh day, they rose at the dawning.

Pale was the sky. The air was cool and dry. The light was but reaching the tops of the walls when the column formed again. Armed men at the front. Seven priests with seven trumpets behind them. The ark behind the priests. The rear guard at the end. The men tried their footing upon the worn path, and they waited for the word.

They began to march.

The trumpets sounded. The column moved about the city. First circuit. The blasts of the horns rang back off the walls and rolled across the plain. The feet of the armed men struck the ground in measure. The bearers carried the ark steady upon their shoulders, their forearms tight upon the staves.

Second circuit. The sun climbed higher. The dust thickened along the path they were wearing into the earth. The cheeks of the priests were drawn tight against the horns, and the sound did not waver. Their breath came in measured pulls between the long notes.

Third circuit. The sentries upon the walls watched the same column pass beneath them yet again. They moved not. They cried not out. Fourth circuit. The shadow of the wall slid across the marching men as the sun went higher. The heat rose. Fifth circuit. Sweat darkened the tunics of the bearers beneath the weight of the ark. Yet their footsteps held even. Sixth circuit. The trumpets had not ceased since the dawning. The drone had become as the sound of the air itself.

And they began the seventh circuit.

Along the familiar path the column moved. Every man kept his place. The priests blew the rams' horns. The sound was the same low drone that had filled the air seven days. The armed men at the head rounded the final corner. The bearers followed after. The rear guard came behind. And the whole column finished the seventh pass about Jericho.

Then the priests blew a long blast. The sound swelled, and held. Longer it stretched than any note they had blown before, the seven horns sustaining as one.

And Joshua lifted up his voice above the horns.

"Shout! For the LORD hath given you the city!"

And the people shouted. Every voice at once. The sound brake forth from the column, from the ranks, from every throat — one great roar that rose above the trumpets and smote the walls of Jericho.

And the walls moved. The face of stone and brick shuddered, and cracked along the length thereof, and fell. It toppled not inward nor outward in pieces. Straight down it went, collapsing flat against the ground. The impact cast forth dust and rubble in a thick cloud that rolled across the plain and over the marching column. Stone ground against stone as the weight settled. Where the wall had stood, there was open ground, broken stone lying flat, the city laid bare.

And every man went straight before him. They climbed over the fallen rubble, up and through the dust, into the city from every side at once, each man moving directly forward from where he stood. And they took Jericho.
