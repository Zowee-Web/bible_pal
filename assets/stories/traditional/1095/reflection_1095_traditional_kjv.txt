He was altogether certain he was right. That is the most troubling part of the account — not the violence, not the zeal, but the certainty. Saul believed he did the work of God. He had the learning, the authority, the conviction. And he was utterly mistaken.

The light upon the road gave him no new knowledge. It gave him a new nature. The man who arose in Damascus was not the man who fell upon the road. Something at the very root had changed — not his learning, not his training, but the direction of his life.

Sometimes the most needful moment is not when we learn a new thing. It is when we discover that the thing we were most sure of was the very thing we most needed to release.
