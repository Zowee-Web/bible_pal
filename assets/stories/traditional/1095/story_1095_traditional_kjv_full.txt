And Saul, yet breathing out threatenings and slaughter against the disciples of the Lord, went unto the high priest and desired of him letters to Damascus to the synagogues, that if he found any of this way, whether men or women, he might bring them bound unto Jerusalem. He had stood by at the stoning of Stephen, holding the garments of them that slew him, and it had not satisfied him. These followers of Jesus were, to his mind, a pestilence — and he meant to root them out.

The letters were granted. Saul set forth upon the road to Damascus with companions. The road ran northward and eastward through dry, hilly country — several days of walking beneath the hot sun, through rocky terrain, past small villages and long stretches of empty road. Saul walked at the fore, his face set, his mind fixed upon the work at hand.

And as he journeyed, he came near Damascus: and suddenly there shined round about him a light from heaven. It was exceeding bright — brighter than the noonday sun. It engulfed him utterly. He fell to the earth upon his face, his arms about his head, his eyes shut against the unbearable glory.

A voice spake unto him out of the light.

"Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?"

He lay upon the ground, trembling. The voice was clear, direct, personal. "Who art thou, Lord?" he said.

"I am Jesus whom thou persecutest. It is hard for thee to kick against the pricks. Arise, and go into the city, and it shall be told thee what thou must do."

The men that journeyed with him stood speechless. They heard a voice but saw no man. When the light faded and Saul arose from the earth and opened his eyes, he saw no man. The glory had taken his sight. His companions led him by the hand and brought him into Damascus.

For three days he was without sight. He neither did eat nor drink. He sat in darkness in a house upon the street called Straight, and he prayed.

Now there was a certain disciple at Damascus, named Ananias. The Lord spake unto him in a vision. "Arise, and go into the street which is called Straight, and enquire in the house of Judas for one called Saul, of Tarsus: for, behold, he prayeth, and hath seen in a vision a man named Ananias coming in, and putting his hand on him, that he might receive his sight."

Ananias was afraid. "Lord," said he, "I have heard by many of this man, how much evil he hath done to thy saints at Jerusalem: and here he hath authority from the chief priests to bind all that call on thy name."

But the Lord said unto him, "Go thy way: for he is a chosen vessel unto me, to bear my name before the Gentiles, and kings, and the children of Israel: for I will shew him how great things he must suffer for my name's sake."

And Ananias went his way. He entered into the house and found Saul sitting in the darkness, his eyes open but seeing nothing, his hands folded in his lap.

He put his hands upon him. "Brother Saul," said he, "the Lord, even Jesus, that appeared unto thee in the way as thou camest, hath sent me, that thou mightest receive thy sight, and be filled with the Holy Ghost."

And immediately there fell from his eyes as it had been scales, and he received sight forthwith. Light poured in upon him. He beheld the room, the walls, the face of Ananias looking down upon him.

He arose, and was baptized. And when he had received meat, he was strengthened.
