Saul was breathing threats and murder against the disciples of the Lord. He went to the high priest and asked for letters to the synagogues in Damascus, so that if he found anyone who belonged to the Way — men or women — he could bring them bound to Jerusalem.

He set out on the road to Damascus with a group of companions. The road ran north through dry, hilly country. The sun was bright overhead. Saul walked with purpose, his authorization folded and sealed, his mind fixed on the work ahead.

Then, as he came near Damascus, a light from heaven suddenly flashed around him. It was brighter than the sun — brighter than anything he had ever seen. It struck him to the ground. He fell face-first into the dust, his arms over his head, blinded by the brilliance.

A voice spoke to him out of the light.

"Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?"

He lay on the ground, trembling. "Who are you, Lord?" he said.

"I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting. Rise and go into the city, and you will be told what you must do."

The men traveling with Saul stood speechless. They had heard the voice but saw no one. When Saul opened his eyes, he could see nothing. The light had taken his sight. His companions took him by the hand and led him into Damascus like a child.

For three days, Saul could not see. He did not eat. He did not drink. He sat in darkness in a house on the street called Straight.

In the same city, there was a disciple named Ananias. The Lord spoke to him in a vision. "Go to the street called Straight," the Lord said, "and ask for a man from Tarsus named Saul. He is praying. I have shown him a vision of a man named Ananias coming to lay hands on him so that he might regain his sight."

Ananias hesitated. "Lord, I have heard about this man — how much harm he has done to your people in Jerusalem. He has authority from the chief priests to arrest everyone who calls on your name."

The Lord said to him, "Go, for he is a chosen instrument of mine to carry my name before the Gentiles and kings and the children of Israel."

So Ananias went. He found the house. He laid his hands on Saul. "Brother Saul," he said, "the Lord Jesus, who appeared to you on the road, has sent me so that you may regain your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit."

Immediately something like scales fell from Saul's eyes, and he could see again. He rose and was baptized. Then he ate, and his strength returned.
