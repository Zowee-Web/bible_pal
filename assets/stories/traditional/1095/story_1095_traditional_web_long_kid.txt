Saul was a man who was completely, totally, one hundred percent sure he was right.

He had studied the Bible since he was a kid. He had the best teachers. He followed every single rule perfectly. And he believed — with all his heart — that the people who followed Jesus were dangerous and wrong.

So Saul decided to stop them. He went to the most powerful religious leaders in Jerusalem and said, "Give me official letters saying I can go to the city of Damascus and arrest anyone who believes in Jesus."

They gave him the letters. Saul set off on the road with some helpers. He was going to march into Damascus and drag the believers back to Jerusalem in chains.

The road to Damascus was long — it took several days of walking through dusty hills under a hot sun. Saul walked at the front of the group, his mind set, his jaw tight, completely focused on what he was going to do.

And then, when he was almost to Damascus, everything in his life changed in a single moment.

A LIGHT from the sky — brighter than the sun, brighter than anything Saul had ever seen — suddenly blazed all around him. It was so bright and so sudden that he fell flat on his face in the dirt. He couldn't see. He couldn't move. He just lay there in the dust.

Then he heard a voice.

"Saul, Saul, why are you hurting my people?"

Saul was shaking. His whole body trembled. "Who... who are you?" he whispered.

"I am Jesus. The one you are trying to stop."

JESUS! The very person whose followers Saul had been chasing and arresting and putting in prison. The person Saul believed was wrong about everything. That Jesus was speaking to him RIGHT NOW from a light brighter than the sun.

"Get up," Jesus said. "Go into the city. Someone will tell you what to do next."

The light faded. Saul lay on the road for a moment, breathing hard. Then he opened his eyes.

And he couldn't see ANYTHING.

The incredible light had made him totally blind. Everything was dark — not nighttime dark, but NOTHING dark. He stretched out his hands and felt only air.

His helpers didn't know what to do. They had heard the voice but didn't see anyone. They looked at Saul lying in the dust, and then at each other, and then at the empty road.

They took Saul by the hand — the man who had been so powerful and confident just minutes ago — and led him into Damascus like a lost little kid.

They brought him to a house on a street called Straight. Saul went inside, sat down, and... waited. In the dark.

For three whole days, Saul sat in that dark room. He didn't eat. He didn't drink. He just sat there and prayed. Everything he had been so sure about was now upside down. The person he'd been fighting against had spoken to him from heaven.

Meanwhile, in another part of the city, God spoke to a believer named Ananias.

"Ananias, go to the street called Straight. Find a man named Saul from the city of Tarsus. He's praying right now."

Ananias was NOT excited about this. "But God!" he said. "That's SAUL! The guy who's been throwing believers in JAIL! He came here to ARREST us!"

"Go," God said firmly. "I have special plans for him. He's going to carry my name to kings and countries and people all over the world."

So Ananias took a deep breath and went. He walked to the house on Straight Street. He went inside. He found Saul sitting in the dark — the great persecutor, now blind, helpless, and afraid.

Ananias put his hands gently on Saul's shoulders.

"Brother Saul," he said. And that word — brother — was probably the last thing Saul expected to hear from someone he had come to arrest. "The Lord Jesus, who appeared to you on the road, sent me here. He wants you to see again and to be filled with the Holy Spirit."

And just like that — something like scales fell off of Saul's eyes. Light came flooding back in. He could see the room. He could see the walls. He could see the face of Ananias looking down at him — not with anger, not with fear, but with kindness.

Saul blinked. He could see.

He stood up. He was baptized — which is what people did to show they believed in Jesus. Then he ate some food and got his strength back.

And here's the most amazing part of the whole story:

The man who had come to Damascus to DESTROY the followers of Jesus walked out of that house as one of them. He spent the rest of his life traveling the world, telling everyone he met about Jesus.

He had been so sure he was right. And then he discovered the truth. And the truth changed everything.
