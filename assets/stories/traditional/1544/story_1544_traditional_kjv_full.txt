Six days were passed since Jesus had spoken unto them of the things which were to come. And on the morning of the seventh day, he chose out three from among them. Peter. James. John. He bade them follow, and led them apart from the rest, toward a mountain that was high and set away from all habitation.

The way grew narrow as they ascended. Loose stone gave way beneath their sandals. The air thinned and the warmth of the lowlands fell behind them. No other soul journeyed with them. The villages below diminished to small markings upon the floor of the valley, and the noise of men and of beasts grew faint, until naught remained save the wind and the sound of their own feet upon the rock.

They came unto the summit. The sky opened wide about them on every side. And Jesus went apart from them and stood alone.

Then was his appearance changed before them.

It came to pass in the sight of their eyes. His garments became white — not as linen that hath been beaten clean, nor as cloth laid out under the sun to whiten, but a brightness fierce and pure, such as no fuller upon the earth could bring forth. The very fabric shone as though a fire burned within it. His whole form was made other than it had been, and the three men stood fast in their places and gazed upon him.

And there appeared beside him two figures. Moses stood there. Elijah stood there. And both spake with Jesus, and their voices carried over the open ground of the high place. The disciples could behold their faces plainly, and they heard the low and steady manner of the speech, though the wind moved about them all.

Then Peter opened his mouth. And when his voice came forth, it trembled.

"Master, it is good that we are come to this place. Let us build three tabernacles — one for thee, and one for Moses, and one for Elijah."

He knew not what words he uttered. They poured forth because a great fear had taken hold of him, and James and John were afraid beside him, their shoulders drawn tight, their breath coming short.

Then descended a cloud upon them.

It was not the slow turning of weather that drifteth along a ridge. It came, and it covered them, thick and near, and the sky and the light were shut out. Shadow pressed upon them from every quarter. The air grew heavy about them. The three disciples could not see past their own hands.

And a voice spake from the midst of the cloud.

"This is my Son, whom I love. Give ear unto him."

The sound filled the whole of the mountaintop. It passed through the cloud and through the bodies of the men and down into the very stone beneath their feet. Peter cast his eyes to the ground. James became as one who cannot move. John stirred not.

Then, of a sudden, the cloud was taken up. The light returned. And the wind blew clean across the summit once more.

They looked about them. Moses was gone. Elijah was gone. No other soul stood upon that mountain. Only Jesus remained, he alone with them, his garments no longer burning with brightness, and the common dust of the trail upon his sandals. He looked upon them, and they looked back.

He turned, and began to go down.

The way of descent was steep. The rocks lay dry and loose beneath their feet. The sun was warm upon their necks. And as they made their way along the path, Jesus spake unto them.

He charged them that they should tell no man the things which they had seen. Not yet. Not until the Son of Man were risen from among the dead.

They heard his words and held them close. But the three looked one upon another as they walked, and quietly, between themselves, they turned the saying over in their hearts. Rising from among the dead. They questioned one another what this thing might mean. And none among them had an answer. They kept on in their walking.

Then they put the matter before Jesus plainly.

"Wherefore do the scribes say that Elijah must needs come first?"

Jesus answered them, and his feet did not slow upon the downward way, and his voice rang clear against the open air. He told them that Elijah doth indeed come first, and setteth all things in order. Then he stayed his speech a moment. And he asked them how it was written concerning the Son of Man — was it not written that he must endure many afflictions, and be counted as nothing?

The path turned about an outcrop of pale stone. A hawk turned upon the air far beneath them.

Then Jesus spake again. He told them that Elijah had already come. And men had done unto him whatsoever they desired, even as it was written of him.

The three disciples walked behind him, one following another upon the narrow way. The mountain rose steep and silent at their backs. Below them the valley opened wide, and the road led on toward the towns and the multitudes and the dust. Jesus walked before them, and they followed him down.
