Six days were accomplished. And Jesus took with him Peter and James and John, and led them apart unto a high mountain, where no other might follow. The way was steep beneath their feet, and the air grew thin as they ascended. They went alone.

And when they had come unto the summit, a thing came to pass upon Jesus. His countenance was changed before their eyes. His garments became white exceedingly, shining with a brightness such as no fuller upon the earth could render. The very cloth blazed with light. And the three stood still and looked upon him.

Then appeared beside him two figures. Moses and Elijah, standing there, and they spake with Jesus. The disciples beheld their faces, and the sound of their discourse carried upon that high ground.

And Peter opened his mouth. His voice came forth trembling. "Master, it is well that we are come to this place. Let us raise up three tabernacles — one for thee, and one for Moses, and one for Elijah."

He knew not what words he uttered. For they were sore afraid.

And a cloud descended upon them and wrapped them in shadow. The light of the sun was taken from them. The air drew close and heavy round about. And from within the cloud there came a voice.

"This is my Son, whom I love. Give ear unto him."

And those words filled all the place where they stood. Peter and James and John turned about, looking upon every side. Moses was departed. Elijah was departed. The cloud lifted from them. And there stood Jesus only, alone with them upon the mountain.

Then they began their descent. The stones were rough beneath their feet, and the wind rose against their faces. And as they came down, Jesus charged them that they should tell no man the things they had witnessed, until the Son of Man should be risen from the dead.

And they kept his saying among themselves. But they turned the matter over, one to another, asking quietly what this rising from the dead should signify.

Then they asked him plainly. "Wherefore say the scribes that Elijah must needs come first?"

And Jesus answered them. "Elijah cometh first indeed, and setteth all things in their right order. And yet it is written that the Son of Man shall endure many things, and be accounted as nothing." He walked on as he spake, his footing sure upon the downward path. "But I say unto you that Elijah hath already come, and they dealt with him according to their own will, even as it was written concerning him."

The wind passed over the slope. And the three disciples followed after him, the mountain rising at their backs, and the valley opening wide below.
