Six days had passed. Jesus took Peter and James and John and led them up a high mountain, away from the others. The trail was steep and the air thinned as they climbed. They went alone.

At the top, something happened to Jesus. His appearance changed before their eyes. His clothes became intensely white, brighter than any bleach on earth could make them. The fabric shone. The three men stood and watched.

Then two figures appeared beside him. Moses and Elijah, standing there, talking with Jesus. The disciples could see their faces, could hear the sound of conversation on that high ground.

Peter spoke. His voice came out unsteady. "Master, it is good for us to be here. Let us make three shelters — one for you, one for Moses, one for Elijah."

He did not know what he was saying. They were deeply afraid.

A cloud came and covered them all in shadow. The sunlight disappeared. The air went thick and close. And out of that cloud came a voice.

"This is my beloved Son. Hear him."

The words filled the space around them. Peter and James and John looked in every direction. Moses was gone. Elijah was gone. The cloud lifted. Only Jesus stood with them on the mountain, alone.

They started down. The path was rocky under their feet, the wind picking up against their faces. As they descended, Jesus told them not to speak of what they had seen until the Son of Man had risen from the dead.

They kept his words to themselves. But they turned the phrase over between them, asking each other quietly what rising from the dead could mean.

Then they asked him directly. "Why do the scribes say that Elijah must come first?"

Jesus answered them. "Elijah does come first and restores all things. And it is written that the Son of Man must suffer many things and be treated as nothing." He kept walking as he spoke, his feet steady on the downward trail. "But I tell you that Elijah has already come, and they did to him whatever they wanted, just as it is written of him."

The wind moved across the slope. The three disciples walked behind him, the mountain rising at their backs, the valley opening below.
