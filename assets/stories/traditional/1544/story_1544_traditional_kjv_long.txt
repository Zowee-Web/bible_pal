Six days were fulfilled since Jesus had spoken unto them of the things which were to come. And on the morning of the seventh day, he chose three from among them. Peter. James. John. He bade them come with him, and he led them apart from the others toward a high mountain.

The way grew narrow as they ascended. Loose stones shifted beneath their sandals. The path bent between low scrub and pale rock, and the slope sharpened as they went up. The air grew thin. It grew cold. No man else was with them. The villages below were made small, mere shapes upon the floor of the valley. The voices of men and the sound of beasts faded away, until there remained only the wind, and the scrape of their own feet upon the rock.

They went up in silence. Their garments were wet with sweat. The sun moved overhead. The trail turned once, and again, doubling back along the face of the mountain. Stones rolled out from beneath their feet and clattered down the slope behind them. Peter wiped his brow with the back of his hand. James and John spake not.

They came unto the summit. The heavens stretched wide and open about them. The wind passed across the bare ground in steady, unbroken sweeps. And Jesus stood apart from them.

Then was his appearance changed.

It came to pass before their very eyes. His garments became white — not the white of washen linen, nor of cloth made bright by any fuller, but a burning, clean brightness which no craftsman upon the earth could fashion. The cloth shone as though kindled from within. The light went forth from him and lay upon the stones at his feet. His whole form was made other, transfigured before them, and the three stood where they were and beheld him.

And, lo, two appeared beside him. Moses stood there. Elias stood there. And both spake with Jesus, their voices carrying across the open ground of that high place. The disciples saw their faces clearly. They heard the low steady sound of the speech between them, though the wind moved round about them all. The three drew not nearer. They cried out not. They watched.

Peter opened his mouth. His voice trembled when it came.

"Master, it is good that we are here. Let us build three tabernacles — one for thee, one for Moses, one for Elias."

He knew not what he said. The words came tumbling out of him because great fear was upon him, and upon James and John also, their shoulders stiff, their breath short. The wind ceased not. The light ceased not, burning round about Jesus and the two who stood with him.

Then came a cloud down upon them.

It was no slow drift of weather along a ridge. It came, and it overshadowed them, thick and near, hiding the sky and the light. Shadow pressed in on every side. The air grew heavy upon them. The disciples could not see beyond their own hands. The voice of the wind was taken away. The brightness was gone. They stood in the gray weight of the cloud, and they waited.

And a voice spake out of the cloud.

"This is my beloved Son. Hear him."

The sound filled all the mountaintop. It moved through the cloud, and through their chests, and into the stone beneath their feet. Peter cast down his eyes. James grew still. John moved not. The voice spake no more.

Then, suddenly, the cloud was lifted. Light returned unto them. The wind blew clean across the summit again. The sky opened above them, wide and pale.

They looked round about. Moses was not there. Elias was not there. No man else stood upon the mountain. Only Jesus, alone with them, his garments no longer blazing, the common dust of the path upon his sandals. He looked upon them, and they looked back upon him. Peter's hands hung at his sides. James swallowed. John drew in a long breath.

And Jesus turned, and began to go down.

The descent was steep. The rocks were dry and loose underfoot. Small stones went skittering down the path before them. The sun lay warm upon their necks now, no longer above them, but bending toward the west. And as they went down, Jesus spake unto them.

He charged them to tell no man of the things which they had seen. Not yet. Not until the Son of man were risen from the dead.

They heard his words, and they kept them. But the three of them looked one upon another as they walked, and quietly, between themselves, they turned the saying over. Rising from the dead. They asked one another what manner of thing this might be. Not one of them had an answer. They went on. The path leveled briefly across a shelf of stone, then dropped again.

Then they put the question to Jesus himself.

"Why say the scribes that Elias must first come?"

And Jesus answered without halting his steps. His feet were steady upon the downward way. His voice was clear in the open air. "Elias verily cometh first," he said, "and restoreth all things." He paused. The wind passed by them, bearing the faint, dry scent of the slope. "And how is it written of the Son of man? That he must suffer many things, and be set at nought."

The trail bent round an outcrop of pale stone. A hawk wheeled upon the air far below, drifting slow upon a current of warm air rising out of the valley.

"But I say unto you," said Jesus, "that Elias is come already. And they have done unto him whatsoever they listed, even as it is written of him."

And the three disciples followed after him, single file along the narrow way. Their sandals struck the stone in soft and broken rhythm. No man spake. The mountain rose steep and silent at their backs. Below them the valley opened wide, the road bearing on toward the towns and the multitudes and the dust. Jesus went on before, and they followed him down.
