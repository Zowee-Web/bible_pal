John saw a new heaven and a new earth.

The first heaven was gone. The first earth was gone. They had passed away completely, like a page turned and removed. And the sea — the vast, churning, borderless sea — was no more. There was no dark water. No distant horizon where waves met cloud. The space where all of it had been was simply open and clean and still.

Then John looked and saw the holy city.

New Jerusalem came down out of heaven from God. It descended through the sky — whole, finished, radiant. It came the way a bride comes toward her husband, prepared and adorned. Every surface caught light. Every line was exact. The city moved downward through clear air toward the new earth, and nothing about it was broken or partial or dimmed.

John watched it come.

Then a great voice spoke out of heaven. It was not distant. It was not muffled. It filled every direction at once, strong and near, the kind of voice that settles into the chest of anyone who hears it.

"Look. The tabernacle of God is with men."

The voice was steady and unhurried.

"He will dwell with them. They will be his people. God himself will be with them and be their God."

Each sentence landed clean. Each one stood on its own, solid as stone laid in a foundation.

The voice continued.

"God will wipe away all tears from their eyes."

John heard the words in that wide, open space where the old earth and the old sky had been. Where grief had once lived. Where mourning had been as common as breathing.

"There will be no more death."

The voice did not pause for emphasis. It did not need to. The words carried their own weight.

"No sorrow. No crying. No more pain. The former things have passed away."

All of it — the sickness, the funerals, the weeping in the night, the ache in the body, the breaking of the heart — all of it was named and then dismissed. Former things. Passed away.

Then he who sat upon the throne spoke.

His voice came from the very center of everything, from the high seat of all authority, and it was different from the great voice that had spoken before. This was the voice of the one who held the beginning and the end in his hands.

"Look," he said. "I make all things new."

Not some things. All things.

The new heaven stretched overhead. The new earth spread below. The holy city descended between them, shining, and the one on the throne spoke again.

"Write this down."

He was speaking to John directly now.

"These words are true and faithful."

John heard the command. He was to record it — every syllable, every promise, exactly as it was given. Not decorated. Not interpreted. Written.

Then the one on the throne said, "It is done."

Two words. Final and complete.

"I am the Alpha and the Omega. The beginning and the end."

His voice did not waver. It did not rise or fall for effect. It was the voice of someone stating what simply is.

"To anyone who thirsts," he said, "I will give from the fountain of the water of life. Freely."

No cost. No condition beyond the thirst itself. The water of life, offered like a hand held open.

"The one who overcomes will inherit all things. I will be his God, and he will be my son."

The throne stood immovable at the center. The holy city, bright as a bride adorned for her husband, hung in the air above the new earth, still descending. The voice from the throne carried across that open, tearless, deathless world where the former things were gone and everything — sky, ground, city, light — was new.
