And John beheld a new heaven and a new earth.

For the first heaven had departed. The first earth had departed. They were passed away utterly, as a scroll that is rolled up and taken from sight. And the sea — that vast and restless deep, that water without border or end — was no more. No dark waters remained. No far horizon where wave met cloud. The place wherein all these things had been was become open and clean and still.

Then John looked, and he saw the holy city.

New Jerusalem came down out of heaven from God. It descended through the firmament — whole, and finished, and full of light. It came as a bride cometh toward her bridegroom, adorned and made ready. Every surface thereof took hold of the light. Every line was true. The city moved downward through clear air toward the new earth, and nothing upon it was broken, nor partial, nor dimmed.

And John watched it come.

Then a great voice spake out of heaven. It was not far off. It was not muffled. It filled every quarter at once, strong and near — such a voice as settleth deep in the breast of all who hear it.

"Behold. The dwelling place of God is among men."

The voice was steady and did not hasten.

"He shall dwell with them, and they shall be his people. And God himself shall be with them, and shall be their God."

Each utterance fell clean upon the hearing. Each stood of its own weight, sure as stone laid in a foundation.

And the voice continued.

"God shall wipe away every tear from their eyes."

John heard the words in that wide and open place where the former earth and the former sky had been. Where grief had once made its habitation. Where mourning had been as common unto men as the drawing of breath.

"Neither shall there be death any more."

The voice did not pause to give force unto itself. It had no need. The words bore their own weight within them.

"Neither sorrow, nor weeping, nor any manner of pain. For the former things are passed away."

All of it — the sickness, the burials, the weeping that cometh in the night, the ache of the body, the rending of the heart — all of it was named, and then put away. Former things. Passed away.

Then he that sat upon the throne spake.

His voice came forth from the very midst of all things, from the high seat of all dominion, and it was other than the great voice which had spoken before. This was the voice of him who held the beginning and the ending in his hands.

"Behold," he said. "I make all things new."

Not certain things only. All things.

The new heaven was stretched overhead. The new earth was spread abroad beneath. The holy city descended between them, shining, and he that sat upon the throne spake again.

"Write these things."

He spake now unto John directly.

"For these words are faithful and true."

And John heard the commandment. He was to set it down — every word, every promise, even as it was given unto him. Not adorned. Not interpreted. Written.

Then he that sat upon the throne said, "It is finished."

Two words. Final and complete.

"I am the Alpha and the Omega. The beginning and the end."

His voice did not waver. It did not rise nor fall for the sake of effect. It was the voice of one who declareth that which simply is.

"Unto him that thirsteth," he said, "I shall give of the fountain of the water of life. Freely."

No price. No condition beyond the thirst itself. The water of life, offered as a hand held open.

"He that overcometh shall inherit all these things. I shall be his God, and he shall be my son."

The throne stood immovable at the centre. The holy city, bright as a bride adorned for her bridegroom, hung in the air above the new earth, yet descending. And the voice from the throne carried across that open world where no tear remained and death was no more — where the former things were gone, and all things, heaven and earth, city and light, were made new.
