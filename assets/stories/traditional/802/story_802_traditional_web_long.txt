Truly, the twenty-third Psalm is a song of David. Its melody flows as gentle as still waters, its substance as sure as the staff in a shepherd's hand. Gather close and listen to the words which David, the son of Jesse, spoke in the pastures of Israel, when evening's gold spilled soft upon the hills, and lambs played upon meadows clothed in green.

The Lord is my shepherd. I lack nothing. So he says, and so it is — for nothing is lacking to those whom the Lord tends, for his hand provides with sureness and with kindness. See then the pastures of Bethlehem, green and lush under the gaze of the newly risen sun. There upon the hillsides the sheep are scattered, each marked with care, each numbered, beloved of the shepherd's watchful eye. Over all these is David himself, staff in hand, yet he knows: even as he guards his sheep, so his soul is tended by the Lord, Sovereign Shepherd over all.

He makes me lie down in green pastures. See now, for sheep do not lie down at random, neither in wastelands nor in scorched barrenness, but where the grass is thick, and the earth soft and gentle beneath the fleece of their bodies. The Lord guides his servant there, to fields alive with the dew of the morning, where the light falls fair, and the shadows slumber silent at the hedge. There is rest in these green pastures, and safety. No wolf prowls, nor enemy approaches, for the shepherd is near, his staff and his watchful gaze a sure protection to the flock.

He leads me beside still waters. See the streams that run clear between banks starred with lilies and reeds. Here the torrent does not rage, nor do troubled currents roar. Noisy waters frighten sheep, but these are placid, gentle as the voice of mildest wind. The sheep come here without fear, bending their lips low to drink, their reflection rippling in quiet pools. Even so, the Lord brings the weary soul to places of refreshment and peace, drawing with gentle hand to the fountains of his calm. The waters are still, unmarred by storm or careless trampling.

He restores my soul. As the sheep, when thirsty or wearied by the burdens of the sun, find strength renewed with the cool drink, so the Lord tends those that belong to him. When the night brings vexation or the noontide scorches, then the shepherd does not stay far off, but comes near. He pours oil on the wounded, and speaks softly to the heart troubled by shadow. So the soul is restored, its weariness lifted, its fears set at nothing.

He leads me in paths of righteousness for his name's sake. See now, O listener, the winding ways across the hills, where sheep might wander and be lost. The shepherd knows the path. His eye discerns the way that is safe, right, and pure. He calls, his staff guides, and none stray that heed his voice. The sheep are led by more than instinct. They follow for his name's sake — the name which is steadfast, sure, and true. The Lord's glory is bound up in his care, and his fame is upon the lips of the delivered.

Yes, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil. For even as the sun falls and the hills darken, there are valleys where the cloak of night is heavy, and the threat of danger lurks in the gloom. In such paths, the heart might tremble, and the hand falter. Yet David, and all in the Lord's flock, have no cause for despair. Fear finds no foothold, because the shepherd's presence remains. The valley of the shadow is only the ground of his might, and the place of his nearness.

For you are with me. The Lord is not far off, nor heedless of danger, but near — as close as breath, as constant as the beating of the heart. The sheep may not see him always, for the dusk hides much, yet his vigilance does not fail. His tread is often soundless, yet sure. So courage is born in the frailest, for his presence is a shield no darkness can breach.

Your rod and your staff, they comfort me. See the rod, sturdy and straight, for chastisement and protection both. With the rod the shepherd defends the flock from wolf and lion, striking swiftly, sparing not the foe. The staff also, with its crook, draws the wandering lamb back to safety, and guides the whole company along the right path. In these instruments is the shepherd's care made plain — in discipline, in rescue, in warding off every peril. The flock rests in comfort, for these tokens of the shepherd's authority and guardianship are ever near.

You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies. What wonder, that provision is made though enemies camp nearby. Whether in the valley or upon the high plain, the flock feeds, untroubled, though wild beasts watch from afar, though evil eyes covet from the shadows. Yet the Lord kindles the fire, sets bread and cup, bringing forth bounty even where threat and want lie in wait. The sheep are anointed in his safekeeping, and none can deny them their feast, for all is ordained by the shepherd's hand.

You anoint my head with oil. In days of old, the anointing oil signified blessing, healing, and favor. The shepherd pours it upon the wounded sheep's head to soothe the cut, to drive away the fly, to show forth lovingkindness. Even so, the Lord bestows blessing upon those he keeps, pouring forth his favor as a balm to every ill. The sheep are marked not only by safety, but by special honor, their heads glistening with the token of the shepherd's grace.

My cup overflows. O the abundance of the Lord's provision — not scant, not measured with grudging hand, but pressed down, running over, generous beyond all reckoning. The sheep lack nothing. The child of the Most High is satisfied. All that is needful, the Lord bestows, and more. Gladness overflows, like wine that fills to the brim and spills in glad excess. Where the Lord is shepherd, there is always abundance of goodness, a table overflowing with his gifts.

Surely goodness and mercy will follow me all the days of my life. The days stretch before, long as the numberless steps through the hills and valleys, yet not one passes but the twin companions, goodness and mercy, draw close behind. As the shadow follows the body in the sun's bright gaze, as the river seeks the sea without fail, so the Lord's bounty trails his chosen. Goodness, that brings all that blesses; mercy, which forgives and shelters — these ride ever at the believer's heel, never to depart.

And I will dwell in the house of the Lord forever. The journey leads not to exile nor desolation, but to a dwelling place most sure. The sheep, having wandered over hill and hollow, find their rest in the house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens. The Lord's presence is their home. His courts are their habitation. Not for the season that passes, nor for a summer's day, but forever — the gates of his love thrown wide, the invitation of his house enduring without end.

So is the twenty-third Psalm: the song of the shepherd and his sheep, of confidence in the Lord Most High. Picture David among the pastures, his harp held gently, the flock about his feet. Hear the hush of the still waters, the sigh of the breeze in the grasses. Behold the shadowed valley, and fear not, for the Lord is present, his rod strong, his staff guiding true. There, by the oil and the table and the overflowing cup, stand in reverent awe, for the mercy and goodness of the Shepherd know no measure, and his house endures forevermore. To these truths and comforts all the children of men may cling, resting under the watchful care of him who neither slumbers nor sleeps.

So ended the song, sung beneath ancient stars and beneath the blue canopy of the day — cherished in sorrow and in joy, lifted in times of need and in the quiet of the soul's peace. So fare they who say with David, "The Lord is my shepherd. I shall not want."
