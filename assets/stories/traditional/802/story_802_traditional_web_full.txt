The Lord is my Shepherd. I lack nothing. So it is written, and so it came to pass in the ordering of the days of David, the sweet psalmist of Israel. Listen, children of men, to the song of the shepherded soul, and see in the word the quiet pasture, the stillness of cool waters, and the path made straight by the guiding hand of the Most High.

The Lord — the Keeper of Israel — tends the sheep of his pasture as a good shepherd. He stays with them. He watches over them by day, and in the watches of the night. He knows each by name. None is hidden from his eyes, neither the strong ram nor the feeble lamb, neither the wanderer nor the meek. All are his — the flock of his hand.

Because the Lord lives, and because his rod and staff comfort, no want remains in the soul that trusts in him. Though the land seems lean, and though the burning sun beats upon the hills, the sheep of the Lord want nothing. He provides for them daily, according to his goodness and lovingkindness.

He makes me lie down in green pastures. So he leads, so he gives rest. See now the sheep lying down, the grass beneath them dewy and fresh, each blade appointed to the nourishing of their life. They do not hurry. The Shepherd, with wise voice and gentle crook, settles them to rest in safety. The wolf may lurk nearby, and the winds may rise, yet there, in the fold of the Lord's choosing, the soul has sweet repose.

He leads me beside still waters. No torrent rages, nor is the stream troubled. The brook appointed by God is calm and clear, flowing softly, reflecting light from above. The sheep drink and are satisfied. Their thirst is quenched in the peace given by the hand that made heaven and earth. In the noonday glare or in the hush of dawn, the Shepherd knows where the waters run calm, and he brings his own to the place of cool refreshment.

He restores my soul. When the weak fall, he lifts them. When one wanders and grows faint, he seeks and upholds. His hand binds up the broken. His balm heals the wounded heart. Where shadows gather, and the spirit is cast down, the Shepherd's presence gives life again. Fear is overcome, courage is restored, and the soul finds her joy.

He leads me in paths of righteousness for his name's sake. Not in the cunning byways of deceit, nor in the dark wood of sin, but in the fair and open way, the Shepherd guides. He counts it for his praise to keep his flock in holiness. His law shines as a light on the path, and his promise fills the air with hope. For his name — holy and mighty — he leads his sheep, so that none forget whose they are and whom they serve.

Yes, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil. See the descent, the hollow beneath the mountain, where gloom settles upon the earth and the air is thick with dread. Yet the sheep go forward, for the Shepherd goes before them. His presence is their shield, his voice their assurance. There is no danger, no lurking foe, no shadow so deep, that the rod and staff of the Lord do not comfort and defend. The valley is only a passage. Beyond, the sun of righteousness rises.

You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies. See how the Lord sets out food and bounty for his beloved, even while adversaries look on. Dangers press close, snares are laid in the secret places, but the Shepherd feeds his own with the bread of peace and the cup of blessing. None can hinder, none can take away, for the table is spread by the hand of the Almighty.

You anoint my head with oil. My cup overflows. The oil of gladness, the sign of favor, the blessing that comes down as dew upon the flock — so the Lord pours forth abundance. The cup is not meager nor scant, but presses to the brim and overflows. And the soul made whole lifts her voice in thanksgiving.

Surely goodness and mercy will follow me all the days of my life. Mark, O soul, the pursuers that are patient and kind: goodness, that shelters; mercy, that pities. They follow the steps of the faithful, from the dawn of youth until the evening of age. In every field, in every storm, goodness leads, mercy upholds.

And I will dwell in the house of the Lord forever. The way does not lead to a withered end, nor to a lonely field. As the Shepherd abides, so will I abide — to behold the beauty of the Lord, to ask in his temple, world without end.

So speaks the Psalm, and so rests the soul, content and secure in the Shepherd's keeping, from everlasting to everlasting.
