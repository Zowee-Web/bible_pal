Yea, verily, the twenty-third Psalm is a song of David, and its melody floweth gentle as still waters, its substance sure as the staff in a shepherd’s hand. Gather close and hearken to the words which David, the son of Jesse, did utter in the pastures of Israel, when evening’s gold spilled soft upon the hills, and lambs did frolic upon meadows clad in green.

The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want. So saith he, and so it is: for naught is lacking unto them whom the Lord tendeth, for His hand provideth with sureness and with kindness. Behold then the pastures of Bethlehem, green and lush under the gaze of the sun newly risen. There upon the hill-sides the sheep are scattered, each marked with care, each numbered, beloved of the shepherd’s watchful eye. Over all these is David himself, staff in hand, yet he knoweth: even as he guardeth his sheep, so his soul is tended by the LORD, Sovereign Shepherd over all.

He maketh me to lie down in green pastures. See now, for sheep do not lie at random, neither in wastelands nor in scorched barrenness, but where the grass is thick, and the earth soft and gentle ‘neath the fleece of their bodies. The Lord guideth His servant thither, to fields alive with the dew of the morning, where the light falleth fair, and the shadows slumber silent at the hedge. There is rest in these green pastures, and safety. No wolf prowleth, nor foe approacheth, for the shepherd is near, his staff and his vigilant gaze a sure bulwark to the flock.

He leadeth me beside the still waters. O behold the streams that run clear between banks starred with lilies and reeds. Here the torrent doth not rage, nor troubled currents roar. Noisome waters frighten sheep, but these are placid, gentle as the voice of mildest wind. The sheep come hither without fear, bending their lips low to drink, their reflection rippling in quiet pools. Even so, the Lord bringeth the weary soul unto places of quenching and peace, drawing with gentle hand to the fountains of His calm. The waters are still, unmarred by storm or heedless trampling.

He restoreth my soul. As the sheep, when thirsty or wearied by the burdens of the sun, find strength renewed with the cool draught, so doth the Lord tend those that belong to Him. When the night bringeth vexation or the noontide scorcheth, then the shepherd tarrieth not afar, but cometh near. He poureth oil on the wounded, and speaketh soft to the heart troubled by shadow. Thus is the soul restored, its weariness lifted, its fears set at naught.

He leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name’s sake. See now, O listener, the winding ways across the hills, where sheep might wander and be lost. The shepherd knoweth the path; his eye discerneth the way that is safe, right, and pure. He calleth, his staff doth guide, and none stray that heed his voice. The sheep are led by more than instinct; they follow for his name’s sake, the name which is steadfast, sure and true. The Lord’s glory is bound up in His care, and His fame upon the lips of the delivered.

Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil. For even as the sun falleth and the hills darken, there are valleys where the cloak of night is heavy, and the threat of peril lurketh in the gloom. In such paths, the heart might tremble, and the hand falter. Yet David, and all in the Lord’s flock, have no cause for despair; fear findeth no foothold, because the shepherd’s presence abideth. The valley of the shadow is but the ground of His might, and the place of His nearness.

For thou art with me. Not afar off, nor heedless of peril, is the Lord, but near, as close as breath, as constant as the beating of the heart. The sheep may not see Him always, for the dusk hideth much, yet His vigilance faileth not. His tread is soundless oft, yet sure. Thus is courage born in the frailest, for His presence is a shield no darkness can breach.

Thy rod and thy staff they comfort me. Behold the rod, sturdy and straight, for chastisement and protection both. With the rod the shepherd defendeth the flock from wolf and lion, striking swiftly, sparing not the foe. The staff also, with its crook, draweth the wandering lamb back to safety, and guideth the whole company along the right path. In these instruments is the shepherd’s care made manifest: in discipline, in rescue, in warding off every peril. The flock resteth in comfort, for these tokens of the shepherd’s authority and guardianship are ever near.

Thou preparest a table before me in the presence of mine enemies. O what wonder, that provision is made though foes encamp. Whether in valley or upon the high plain, the flock doth feed, untroubled, though wild beasts watch from afar, though evil eyes covet from the shadows. Yet the Lord kindleth the fire, setteth bread and cup, bringing forth bounty even where threat and want lie in wait. The sheep are anointed in His safekeeping, and none can deny them their feast, for all is ordained by the shepherd’s hand.

Thou anointest my head with oil. In days of old, the anointing oil signifieth blessing, healing, and favor. The shepherd poureth upon the wounded sheep’s head to soothe the cut, to drive away the fly, to show forth lovingkindness. Even so, the Lord bestoweth blessing upon those He keepeth, pouring forth His favor as a balm to every ill. The sheep are marked not only by safety, but by special honour, their heads glistening with the token of the shepherd’s grace.

My cup runneth over. O the abundance of the Lord’s provision! Not scant, not measured with grudging hand, but pressed down, running over, generous beyond all reckoning. The sheep lack nothing, the child of the Most High is satisfied. All that is needful, the Lord doth bestow, and more; gladness overflows, like wine that filleth to the brim and spillest in glad excess. Where the Lord is shepherd, there is ever surfeit of goodness, a table overflowing with His gifts.

Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life. The days stretch before, long as the numberless steps through the hills and valleys, yet not one passeth but the twin companions, goodness and mercy, draw close behind. As the shadow followeth the body in the sun’s bright gaze, as the river seeketh the sea without fail, so doth the Lord’s bounty trail His chosen. Goodness, that bringeth all that blesseth; mercy, which forgiveth and sheltereth—these ride ever at the believer’s heel, never to depart.

And I will dwell in the house of the Lord for ever. Lo, the journey leadeth not to exile nor desolation, but to a dwelling-place most sure. The sheep, having wandered over hill and hollow, find their rest in the house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens. The Lord’s presence is their home; His courts their habitation. Not for the season that passeth, nor for a summer’s day, but for ever—the gates of His love flung wide, the invitation of His house enduring without end.

Thus is the twenty-third Psalm: the song of the shepherd and his sheep, of confidence in the Lord Most High. Picture David among the pastures, his harp cradled gentle, the flock about his feet. Hear the hush of the still waters, the sigh of the breeze in the grasses. Behold the shadowed vale, and fear not, for the Lord is present, His rod strong, His staff guiding true. There, by the oil and the table and the overflowing cup, stand in reverent awe, for the mercy and goodness of the Shepherd know no measure, and His house endureth for evermore. And to these truths and comforts all the children of men may cleave, resting under the watchful care of Him who neither slumbereth nor sleepeth.

So ended the song, sung beneath ancient stars and beneath the blue canopy of the day; cherished in sorrow and in joy; lifted in times of need and in the quiet of the soul’s peace. Thus fare they who say with David, “The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want.”
