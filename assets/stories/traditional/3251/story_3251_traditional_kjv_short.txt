Abimelech had reigned over Israel three years when God sent an evil spirit between him and the men of Shechem. They dealt treacherously with him, that the blood of Jerubbaal's threescore and ten sons might be laid upon their brother Abimelech, who slew them, and upon the men of Shechem, who had aided him.

The men of Shechem set an ambush for Abimelech upon the mountaintops and robbed all who passed that way. Tidings of it reached him.

Then Gaal, Ebed's son, came to Shechem with his brethren, and the men of the city put their confidence in him. They gathered their vineyards, trode the grapes, and made merry. Within their god's house they ate, drank, and cursed Abimelech.

Gaal questioned whom they served. Who was Abimelech, and who was Shechem, that they should serve him? Was he not Jerubbaal's son, with Zebul as his officer? Gaal called them to serve the men of Hamor, Shechem's father, instead. Would to God the people were under his hand, he said; then he would remove Abimelech. To Abimelech he said, “Increase thine army, and come out.”

Zebul, ruler of the city, heard Gaal's words and was angry. He secretly sent messengers to tell Abimelech that Gaal and his brethren were fortifying the city against him. Zebul counselled Abimelech to bring his people by night and lie in wait in the field. At sunrise he should set upon the city; when Gaal's company came out, Abimelech could act as occasion offered.

Abimelech and all his men rose by night and lay against Shechem in four companies.

Gaal stood at the city's entrance as Abimelech's men rose from ambush. He told Zebul he saw people coming down from the mountaintops. Zebul answered that Gaal was seeing mountain shadows as though they were men.

Gaal spoke again: people were coming through the middle of the land, and another company approached along the plain of Meonenim.

Zebul now challenged Gaal's boast. Where was the mouth that had asked who Abimelech was? These were the people Gaal had despised, Zebul said; let him go out and fight them.

Gaal went before Shechem's men to fight Abimelech. Abimelech pursued him, and Gaal fled. Many were overthrown and wounded, even to the gateway.

Abimelech dwelt at Arumah. Zebul thrust Gaal and his brethren out of Shechem, so they could no longer dwell there.
