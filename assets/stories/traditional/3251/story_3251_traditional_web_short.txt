Abimelech had been prince over Israel for three years when God sent an evil spirit between him and the men of Shechem. They turned treacherously against him, so that the blood of Jerubbaal's seventy sons would fall upon their brother Abimelech, who killed them, and upon the men of Shechem, who had helped him.

The men of Shechem set an ambush for Abimelech on the mountaintops and robbed everyone who passed that way. News of it reached him.

Then Gaal, Ebed's son, arrived in Shechem with his brothers, and the men of the city trusted him. They harvested their vineyards, trod the grapes, and celebrated. In their god's house they ate, drank, and cursed Abimelech.

Gaal challenged their allegiance. Who was Abimelech, and who was Shechem, that they should serve him? Was he not Jerubbaal's son, with Zebul as his officer? Gaal urged them to serve the men of Hamor, Shechem's father, instead. He wished the people were under his own command; then, he said, he would remove Abimelech. His challenge to Abimelech was, “Increase your army and come out!”

Zebul, the city's ruler, heard and became angry. He craftily sent messengers to tell Abimelech that Gaal and his brothers were inciting the city against him. Zebul advised Abimelech to bring his men by night and hide in the field. At sunrise, he should rush the city; when Gaal's people came out, Abimelech could strike as opportunity allowed.

Abimelech and all his men rose by night and waited against Shechem in four companies.

Gaal stood in the gateway as Abimelech's men rose from hiding. He told Zebul he could see people descending from the mountaintops. Zebul answered that Gaal was mistaking mountain shadows for men.

Gaal spoke again: people were coming through the middle of the land, and one company approached by the oak of Meonenim.

Now Zebul taunted him about his boast. Where was his mouth that had asked who Abimelech was? These were the people Gaal had despised, Zebul said; now he should go and fight them.

Gaal led Shechem's men out against Abimelech. Abimelech pursued him, and Gaal fled. Many fell wounded, right up to the gateway.

Abimelech lived at Arumah. Zebul drove Gaal and his brothers out of Shechem, so they could no longer dwell there.
