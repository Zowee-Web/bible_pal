For sixteen years under Ahaz the king the doors of the temple of the Lord had been shut. The vessels had been broken or taken away to other altars; the lamps had not been lit; the morning and evening sacrifice had not been offered; and the Levitical singers had grown old without their instruments in their hands. Now Hezekiah was king in his father's place, and he had opened the doors of the temple of the Lord in the first month of his first year. The priests and the Levites had cleansed the house. The vessels had been brought back. And now they stood in their stations.

He set the Levites in Yahweh's house with cymbals, with stringed instruments, and with harps, according to the commandment of David, of Gad the king's seer, and Nathan the prophet; for the commandment was from Yahweh by his prophets.

The Levites stood with David's instruments, and the priests with the trumpets.

Hezekiah commanded them to offer the burnt offering on the altar. When the burnt offering began, Yahweh's song also began, along with the trumpets and David king of Israel's instruments.

All the assembly worshiped, the singers sang, and the trumpeters sounded. All this continued until the burnt offering was finished.

When they had finished offering, the king and all who were present with him bowed themselves and worshiped.

Moreover Hezekiah the king and the princes commanded the Levites to sing praises to Yahweh with the words of David, and of Asaph the seer. They sang praises with gladness, and they bowed their heads and worshiped.

And the smoke of the burnt offering rose up over the temple of the Lord for the first time in many years. The cymbals of the Levites struck against the silence of the courts; the trumpets of the priests answered them; the harps and stringed instruments came in beneath the trumpets; and the song of David that had not been sung at the altar in sixteen years was sung again, in the courts above the city of Jerusalem.
