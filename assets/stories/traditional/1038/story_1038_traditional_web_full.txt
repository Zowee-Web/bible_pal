In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth. The earth was formless and empty. Darkness covered the surface of the deep, and the Spirit of God moved over the face of the waters. There was nothing yet. Only the dark, and the water, and the hovering presence above it.

God said, "Let there be light."

There was light. It broke across the formless void in a single instant, pouring over the surface of the waters. God saw the light, that it was good. He divided the light from the darkness. He called the light Day and the darkness he called Night.

There was evening and there was morning. The first day.

God said, "Let there be an expanse in the middle of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters." He made the expanse and separated the waters which were below the expanse from the waters which were above the expanse. He called the expanse Sky. The sky stretched wide and blue and endless above the waters.

There was evening and there was morning. The second day.

God said, "Let the waters under the sky be gathered together to one place, and let the dry land appear." It was so. The waters drew back, churning and sliding, and the land rose — dark and wet and bare. He called the dry land Earth. The gathered waters he called Seas. God saw that it was good.

Then God said, "Let the earth put forth vegetation — plants yielding seed and fruit trees bearing fruit after their kind, with their seed in them." The ground split open. Green pushed upward everywhere at once. Grasses spread across the bare soil. Herbs rose with their stems and leaves. Trees climbed into the air, their roots gripping the new earth, their branches opening wide. Fruit formed on the limbs — heavy, ripe, bending the wood. God saw that it was good.

There was evening and there was morning. The third day.

God said, "Let there be lights in the expanse of the sky to divide the day from the night. Let them be for signs and for seasons and for days and years." He made two great lights — the greater light to rule the day and the lesser light to rule the night. He made the stars also. He set them in the expanse of the sky. The sun burned gold and the moon hung pale above the earth and the stars scattered outward, countless, flung across the dark. God saw that it was good.

There was evening and there was morning. The fourth day.

God said, "Let the waters swarm with living creatures, and let birds fly above the earth in the open expanse of the sky." He created the great sea creatures and every living thing that moves, with which the waters swarmed, after their kind. He created every winged bird after its kind. Things moved in the deep — vast shapes and small ones, silver-sided and dark-backed, quick and slow. Birds rose over the water, their wings beating against the new air. God saw that it was good. He blessed them. "Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas. Let birds multiply on the earth."

There was evening and there was morning. The fifth day.

God said, "Let the earth produce living creatures after their kind — livestock, creeping things, and animals of the earth after their kind." It was so. Cattle walked across the open land. Things crept through the grass and over the warm stones. Beasts of the earth moved in the fields and among the trees — each according to its kind. God saw that it was good.

Then God said, "Let's make man in our image, after our likeness. Let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, over the birds of the sky, over the livestock, over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creeps on the earth."

God created man in his own image. In the image of God he created him. Male and female he created them.

God blessed them. He said to them, "Be fruitful, multiply, fill the earth, and subdue it. Have dominion over the fish of the sea, over the birds of the sky, and over every living thing that moves on the earth."

God said, "I have given you every plant yielding seed which is on the surface of all the earth, and every tree which has fruit yielding seed. It will be your food. To every animal of the earth, and to every bird of the sky, and to everything that creeps on the earth, in which there is life, I have given every green herb for food." It was so.

God saw everything that he had made, and it was very good.

There was evening and there was morning. The sixth day.
