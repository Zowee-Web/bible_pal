Six times God looked at what he had made, and six times the text says he saw that it was good. The seventh time — after the man and the woman, after everything — the word changes. Very good. Not merely functional or orderly, but delighted in.

There is something striking about a Creator who pauses to look. The making itself is swift — God speaks and it is so. But the seeing is deliberate. Light exists, and then God sees the light. The act of creation and the act of appreciation are separate moments.

Joy lives in that gap. Between the making and the noticing. Between the work of a day and the moment you stop to see what has come of it.
