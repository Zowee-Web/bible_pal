In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth. The earth was formless and empty. Darkness was on the surface of the deep. The Spirit of God hovered over the face of the waters. There was nothing yet that could be named. There was no day. There was no shore. Only the dark, and the deep, and the hovering presence above it.

Into that dark, the word went out.

God said, "Let there be light."

There was light. It broke across the formless void in a single instant, and it poured over the surface of the waters. The deep that had held no edge now held a shining one. God saw the light, that it was good. He divided the light from the darkness. He called the light Day. The darkness he called Night.

There was evening and there was morning, the first day.

God said, "Let there be an expanse in the middle of the waters. Let it divide the waters from the waters." He made the expanse. He separated the waters which were below from the waters which were above. He called the expanse Sky. The sky stretched wide above the deep, blue and endless, and the waters lay quiet beneath it.

There was evening and there was morning, a second day.

God said, "Let the waters under the sky be gathered together to one place. Let the dry land appear." It was so. The waters drew back, churning and sliding, and the land rose — dark and wet and bare. He called the dry land Earth. The gathered waters he called Seas. God saw that it was good.

Then God said, "Let the earth put forth vegetation — plants yielding seed and fruit trees bearing fruit after their kind, with their seed in them." The ground split open. Green pushed upward everywhere at once. Grasses spread across the bare soil. Herbs rose with their stems and leaves. Trees climbed into the new air, their roots gripping the earth, their branches opening wide. Fruit formed on the limbs — heavy, ripe, bending the wood. God saw that it was good.

There was evening and there was morning, a third day.

Now the word went out again, and this time it went out to the sky itself.

God said, "Let there be lights in the expanse of the sky to divide the day from the night. Let them be for signs and for seasons and for days and years. Let them be for lights in the expanse of the sky to give light on the earth." It was so.

He made the two great lights. The greater light he made to rule the day. The lesser light he made to rule the night. He made the stars also. He set them in the expanse of the sky. The sun burned gold. The moon hung pale above the earth. The stars scattered outward, countless, flung across the dark in a pattern so wide that no edge could be seen. They wheeled in their courses. They began to mark the seasons. The light that had broken on the first day now had its bearers, and the heavens themselves carried the hours. God saw that it was good.

There was evening and there was morning, a fourth day.

God said, "Let the waters swarm with living creatures. Let birds fly above the earth in the open expanse of the sky." He created the great sea creatures. He created every living thing that moves, with which the waters swarmed, after their kind. He created every winged bird after its kind. Things moved in the deep — vast shapes and small ones, silver-sided and dark-backed, quick and slow. Schools turned together in the green. Wings rose over the water, beating against the new air. The seas, which had been only seas, became seas full of life. The sky, which had been only sky, became a sky crossed by flight. God saw that it was good. He blessed them. "Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas. Let birds multiply on the earth."

There was evening and there was morning, a fifth day.

God said, "Let the earth produce living creatures after their kind — livestock, creeping things, and animals of the earth after their kind." It was so. Cattle walked across the open land. Things crept through the grass and over the warm stones. Beasts of the earth moved in the fields and among the trees. Each came according to its kind. The land that had brought forth grasses now brought forth breath. God saw that it was good.

Then the speech changed.

It had been "Let there be." It had been "Let the earth bring forth." Now it became something else.

Then God said, "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness. Let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, over the birds of the sky, over the livestock, over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creeps on the earth."

God created man in his own image. In the image of God he created him. Male and female he created them. The earth that bore them was the same earth that bore the cattle and the trees, but the breath that filled them carried the image of the One who spoke. They stood upon the ground. They looked out upon the seas and the sky and the herds and the green. The world that had been made for them was waiting.

God blessed them. He said to them, "Be fruitful, multiply, fill the earth, and subdue it. Have dominion over the fish of the sea, over the birds of the sky, and over every living thing that moves on the earth."

God said, "Behold, I have given you every herb yielding seed, which is on the surface of all the earth, and every tree which has fruit yielding seed. It will be your food. To every animal of the earth, and to every bird of the sky, and to everything that creeps on the earth, in which there is life, I have given every green herb for food." It was so.

The fruit hung on the trees. The herds grazed across the open land. The fish moved in the seas. The birds turned in the sky. The lights wheeled above. The first man and the first woman stood among it all, breathing the air of a world that had been spoken into being for them.

God saw everything that he had made, and behold, it was very good.

There was evening and there was morning, a sixth day.
