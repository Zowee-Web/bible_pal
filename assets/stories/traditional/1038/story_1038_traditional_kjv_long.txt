In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth. And the earth was without form, and void. And darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters. There was nothing yet that could be named. There was no day. There was no shore. Only the dark, and the deep, and the hovering presence above it.

Into that dark, the word went forth.

And God said, "Let there be light."

And there was light. It broke across the formless void in a single instant, and it poured over the face of the waters. The deep that had held no edge now held a shining one. And God saw the light, that it was good. He divided the light from the darkness. He called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night.

And the evening and the morning were the first day.

And God said, "Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters." And God made the firmament, and divided the waters which were under the firmament from the waters which were above the firmament. He called the firmament Heaven. The heaven stretched wide above the deep, vast and endless, and the waters lay quiet beneath it.

And the evening and the morning were the second day.

And God said, "Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear." And it was so. The waters drew back, churning and sliding, and the land rose — dark and wet and bare. He called the dry land Earth, and the gathering together of the waters called he Seas. And God saw that it was good.

And God said, "Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind, whose seed is in itself, upon the earth." The ground split open. Green pushed upward everywhere at once. Grass spread across the bare soil. Herbs rose with their stems and leaves. Trees climbed into the new air, their roots gripping the earth, their branches opening wide. Fruit formed on the limbs — heavy, ripe, bending the wood. And God saw that it was good.

And the evening and the morning were the third day.

Now the word went forth again, and this time it went forth unto the heaven itself.

And God said, "Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years. And let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth." And it was so.

And God made two great lights — the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night. He made the stars also. He set them in the firmament of the heaven. The sun burned gold. The moon hung pale above the earth. The stars scattered outward, countless, flung across the dark in a pattern so wide that no edge could be seen. They wheeled in their courses. They began to mark the seasons. The light that had broken on the first day now had its bearers, and the heavens themselves carried the hours. And God saw that it was good.

And the evening and the morning were the fourth day.

And God said, "Let the waters bring forth abundantly the moving creature that hath life, and fowl that may fly above the earth in the open firmament of heaven." And God created great whales, and every living creature that moveth, which the waters brought forth abundantly, after their kind, and every winged fowl after his kind. Things moved in the deep — vast shapes and small ones, silver-sided and dark-backed, quick and slow. Schools turned together in the green. Wings rose over the water, beating against the new air. The seas, which had been only seas, became seas full of life. The heaven, which had been only heaven, became a heaven crossed by flight. And God saw that it was good. And God blessed them, saying, "Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let fowl multiply in the earth."

And the evening and the morning were the fifth day.

And God said, "Let the earth bring forth the living creature after his kind, cattle, and creeping thing, and beast of the earth after his kind." And it was so. Cattle walked across the open land. Things crept through the grass and over the warm stones. Beasts of the earth moved in the fields and among the trees. Each came after his kind. The land that had brought forth grass now brought forth breath. And God saw that it was good.

Then the speech changed.

It had been "Let there be." It had been "Let the earth bring forth." Now it became something else.

And God said, "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth."

So God created man in his own image. In the image of God created he him. Male and female created he them. The earth that bore them was the same earth that bore the cattle and the trees, but the breath that filled them carried the image of the One who spake. They stood upon the ground. They looked out upon the seas and the heaven and the herds and the green. The world that had been made for them was waiting.

And God blessed them, and God said unto them, "Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it; and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth."

And God said, "Behold, I have given you every herb bearing seed, which is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree, in the which is the fruit of a tree yielding seed; to you it shall be for meat. And to every beast of the earth, and to every fowl of the air, and to every thing that creepeth upon the earth, wherein there is life, I have given every green herb for meat." And it was so.

The fruit hung upon the trees. The herds grazed across the open land. The fish moved in the seas. The fowl turned in the heaven. The lights wheeled above. The first man and the first woman stood among it all, breathing the air of a world that had been spoken into being for them.

And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good.

And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.
