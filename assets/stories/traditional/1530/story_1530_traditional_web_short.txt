Job had been sitting a long time. The ash heap beneath him had gone cold in places and warm in others. His sores were open. His friends had spoken. He had spoken. The words had gone back and forth for days, and now the air around them had gone still in a way that felt heavy, like the sky before a storm.

Then the wind came.

It did not build slowly. It struck from the east in a single gust that threw dust against their faces and bent the scrub brush flat to the ground. The sky darkened. The wind circled and tightened and rose, pulling dirt and debris upward into a column that twisted against the clouds. The robes of the three friends whipped against their bodies. They shielded their eyes.

Out of the whirlwind, a voice spoke.

It was not like thunder. It was not like any human voice. It filled the space around them the way water fills a vessel, pressing into every corner. The ground vibrated under their feet.

"Who is this that darkens counsel by words without knowledge?" the voice said.

Job did not answer. The wind pressed against his chest.

"Gird up your loins like a man. I will demand of you, and you will answer me."

The whirlwind held its shape, spinning, roaring at the edges but clear at the center where the voice came from.

"Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth? Declare it, if you have understanding. Who set its measurements, if you know? Or who stretched the line upon it? What are its foundations fastened to? Or who laid its cornerstone, when the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy?"

The voice did not pause for breath. It did not wait.

"Or who shut up the sea with doors, when it broke forth as if it had come out of the womb? When I made the cloud its garment, and thick darkness a swaddling cloth for it, and broke up for it my decreed place, and set bars and doors, and said, 'This far you will come, but no further. Here your proud waves will be stopped.'"

The whirlwind turned. Dust and ash rose in spirals around Job where he sat. His torn robe pressed flat against his chest. His hands were open on his knees. The voice hung in the air like the last ring of a struck bell, and the wind kept turning.
