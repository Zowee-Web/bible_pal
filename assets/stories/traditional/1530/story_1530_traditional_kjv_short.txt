Job had sat a long while upon the ashes. The heap beneath him had grown cold in some places, and in others it yet held warmth. His sores lay open upon his flesh. His friends had spoken, and he had answered them, and they had answered him again. The words had passed between them many days, and now the air about them had gone still — heavy and close, as the sky groweth heavy ere the storm breaketh.

Then came the wind.

It did not gather by degrees. It smote from the east in a single great gust that cast dust against their faces and beat the scrub flat upon the ground. The heavens darkened. The wind turned upon itself and drew tighter and rose up, pulling earth and broken things upward into a pillar that twisted against the underside of the clouds. The robes of the three friends were driven hard against their bodies. They covered their eyes.

And out of the whirlwind, a voice spake.

It was not as thunder. Neither was it as any voice of man. It filled the place about them as water filleth a vessel, pressing into every corner thereof. The ground trembled beneath their feet.

"Who is this that darkeneth counsel with words and hath no knowledge?" the voice said.

Job answered not. The wind pressed hard upon his breast.

"Gird up now thy loins as a man, for I shall require of thee, and thou shalt answer me."

The whirlwind held its form, turning, roaring at the edges, yet clear at the center from whence the voice came forth.

"Where wast thou when I set the foundations of the earth? Speak, if thou hast understanding. Who appointed the measures thereof — dost thou know? Who hath stretched the line upon it? Upon what were the pillars thereof sunk, and who laid the stone at the corner, when the stars of the morning lifted up their song together, and all the sons of God cried aloud for gladness?"

The voice did not stay for breath. It did not tarry.

"Who shut in the sea with doors when it burst forth, as one coming out of the womb? When I set the cloud for a garment over it, and wrapped thick darkness about it as a swaddling band, and appointed unto it a bound, and set the bar and the gate, and said, Hitherto shalt thou come, and no further — here shall thy proud waters be stayed."

The whirlwind turned. Dust and ash rose in spirals about Job where he sat. His torn robe was pressed flat against his breast. His hands lay open upon his knees. The voice hung yet in the air, as the last ringing of a bell that hath been struck, and the wind kept turning.
