Job had been sitting a long time. Days. The ash heap beneath him had gone cold in some places and warm in others where the sun had touched it. His skin was broken with sores from his scalp to the soles of his feet. Dust had settled into the creases of his face. His robe was torn. His head was shorn.

Three men sat near him. Eliphaz. Bildad. Zophar. They had come from far places and had spoken many words to him. He had spoken many words back. The arguments had gone on for days, round after round, voice after voice, until the air between them felt used up. Like a field plowed too many times in one season.

Now the talking had stopped. The silence sat on them like weight.

Then the wind came.

It did not build the way a normal wind builds, starting soft and growing. This one struck all at once from the east, a wall of moving air that hit the ash heap and threw a curtain of gray dust into their faces. The scrub brush around them bent flat. Loose stones rolled. The hems of their robes snapped and twisted.

The sky changed. Clouds gathered from directions that did not make sense, pulling together overhead into a dark mass that blocked the sun. The temperature dropped. The light turned the color of old bronze.

The wind tightened. It began to circle. It pulled ash and dirt upward in a spiraling column, and the column grew until it stretched from the ground to the base of the clouds. The sound was enormous — a deep, sustained roar, like the voice of a river magnified a hundred times. The whirlwind held its shape and turned.

The three friends rose to their feet. They pressed their robes against their bodies and shielded their eyes with their forearms. Their beards blew sideways. They stepped back.

Job did not move. He sat in the ash with his hands on his knees and the wind pressing against his open sores.

Then a voice spoke out of the whirlwind.

It was not thunder. It was not the sound of any man. It came from the center of the spinning column and filled the space around them completely, pressing into their ears, their chests, the ground beneath them. The dirt vibrated.

"Who is this that darkens counsel by words without knowledge?"

The words were clear despite the roaring wind. They cut through it the way a blade cuts through cloth. No one answered. No one breathed.

"Gird up your loins like a man," the voice said. "I will demand of you, and you will answer me."

The whirlwind turned. Its base scraped the ground and sent fresh plumes of ash into the air. The column was dark at its edges and darker at its center, and the voice came from that center as if the darkness itself were speaking.

"Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth? Declare it, if you have understanding."

Job's mouth did not open. His hands stayed on his knees. The ash around him lifted in small spirals.

"Who set its measurements, if you know? Or who stretched the line upon it?"

The voice did not pause for Job to respond. It pressed forward, question after question, each one landing like a stone dropped into still water.

"What are its foundations fastened to? Or who laid its cornerstone?"

A shift in the wind. The roar softened for a moment, just enough for the next words to come through with a different quality — not quieter, but wider, as though the sky itself had opened to let them pass.

"When the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy."

The words hung in the air. Then the wind surged again, and the voice returned to its full force.

"Or who shut up the sea with doors, when it broke forth as if it had come out of the womb?"

The three friends had backed further away. Eliphaz had dropped to his knees. Bildad stood with both hands over his face. Zophar had turned his back to the wind and crouched low to the ground. None of them spoke.

"When I made the cloud its garment," the voice continued, "and thick darkness a swaddling cloth for it. And broke up for it my decreed place, and set bars and doors."

The whirlwind tightened. The column of spinning air drew narrower and the roar deepened. The ash heap shifted under Job. Small stones tumbled past his legs.

"And said, 'This far you will come, but no further. Here your proud waves will be stopped.'"

The voice held on the last word. The whirlwind kept turning. Dust and ash rose in wide spirals around the place where Job sat. His torn robe pressed flat against his ribs. His hands were open on his knees. The wind pulled at the edges of everything — the cloth, the ash, the dry brush — and the dark column spun against the dark sky, and the sound of it filled the valley from ridge to ridge.
