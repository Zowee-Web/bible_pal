Job had been sitting a long time. Days had passed. The ash heap beneath him had gone cold in some places and warm in others where the sun had touched it. His skin was broken with sores from his scalp to the soles of his feet. The sores had crusted in places and split open in others. Dust had settled into the creases of his face and packed into the corners of his eyes. His robe was torn at the chest where he had ripped it. His head was shorn, and the stubble had begun to grow back uneven.

A piece of broken pottery lay near his right hand. He had been using it to scrape his skin. He had set it down some time ago and had not picked it up again.

Three men sat near him. Eliphaz. Bildad. Zophar. They had come from far places and had spoken many words to him. He had spoken many words back. The arguments had gone on for days, round after round, voice after voice, until the air between them felt used up. Like a field plowed too many times in one season. Their robes were dusty at the hems from sitting on the ground. Their faces were drawn.

Now the talking had stopped. The silence sat on them like weight. A fly moved across the ash near Job's foot. Somewhere down the slope a goat called once and then was quiet.

Then the wind came.

It did not build the way a normal wind builds, starting soft and growing. This one struck all at once from the east, a wall of moving air that hit the ash heap and threw a curtain of gray dust into their faces. The scrub brush around them bent flat against the dirt. Loose stones rolled down the slope. The hems of their robes snapped and twisted around their legs. A clay water jar tipped over and rolled until it caught against a rock.

The sky changed. Clouds gathered from directions that did not make sense, pulling together overhead into a dark mass that blocked the sun. The temperature dropped. The light turned the color of old bronze. The shadows under the men's eyes deepened. The dry grass on the ridge above them lay flat and stayed flat.

The wind tightened. It began to circle. It pulled ash and dirt upward in a spiraling column, and the column grew until it stretched from the ground to the base of the clouds. The sound was enormous — a deep, sustained roar, like the voice of a river magnified a hundred times. The whirlwind held its shape and turned. Bits of dry brush spun in its lower edges. The smell of the ash rose with it, sharp and old.

The three friends rose to their feet. They pressed their robes against their bodies and shielded their eyes with their forearms. Their beards blew sideways. They stepped back, one slow step and then another, until they were several paces from where they had been sitting.

Job did not move. He sat in the ash with his hands on his knees and the wind pressing against his open sores. His robe pulled tight against his ribs. His eyes were open.

Then a voice spoke out of the whirlwind.

It was not thunder. It was not the sound of any man. It came from the center of the spinning column and filled the space around them completely, pressing into their ears, their chests, the ground beneath them. The dirt vibrated. Small pebbles near Job's feet moved as if they were being shaken.

"Who is this that darkens counsel by words without knowledge?"

The words were clear despite the roaring wind. They cut through it the way a blade cuts through cloth. No one answered. No one breathed. Eliphaz's mouth was open. Bildad's hands had gone to his ears and then dropped to his sides again.

"Gird up your loins like a man," the voice said. "I will demand of you, and you will answer me."

The whirlwind turned. Its base scraped the ground and sent fresh plumes of ash into the air. The column was dark at its edges and darker at its center, and the voice came from that center as if the darkness itself were speaking. The clouds above kept moving, low and fast, but the column beneath them stayed in place.

"Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth? Declare it, if you have understanding."

Job's mouth did not open. His hands stayed on his knees. The ash around him lifted in small spirals and settled and lifted again. A streak of blood from one of his sores had dried along his arm. The wind did not move it.

"Who set its measurements, if you know? Or who stretched the line upon it?"

The voice did not pause for Job to respond. It pressed forward, question after question, each one landing like a stone dropped into still water. The rhythm of it was steady. The pressure of it did not let up.

"What are its foundations fastened to? Or who laid its cornerstone?"

A shift in the wind. The roar softened for a moment, just enough for the next words to come through with a different quality — not quieter, but wider, as though the sky itself had opened to let them pass.

"When the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy."

The words hung in the air. For a breath the column seemed to slow. Then the wind surged again, and the voice returned to its full force, and the roar climbed back up to where it had been.

"Or who shut up the sea with doors, when it broke forth as if it had come out of the womb?"

The three friends had backed further away. Eliphaz had dropped to his knees in the dirt, his palms pressed flat against the ground. Bildad stood with both hands over his face, his fingers spread, his shoulders hunched forward. Zophar had turned his back to the wind and crouched low to the ground with his forehead nearly touching his knees. None of them spoke. None of them looked up.

"When I made the cloud its garment," the voice continued, "and thick darkness a swaddling cloth for it. And broke up for it my decreed place, and set bars and doors."

The whirlwind tightened. The column of spinning air drew narrower and the roar deepened. The ash heap shifted under Job. Small stones tumbled past his legs and came to rest against his feet. The piece of broken pottery slid a hand's width and stopped.

"And said, 'This far you will come, but no further. Here your proud waves will be stopped.'"

The voice held on the last word. The whirlwind kept turning. Dust and ash rose in wide spirals around the place where Job sat. His torn robe pressed flat against his ribs. His hands were open on his knees. The wind pulled at the edges of everything — the cloth, the ash, the dry brush, the loose hair at the back of his head — and the dark column spun against the dark sky, and the sound of it filled the valley from ridge to ridge.
