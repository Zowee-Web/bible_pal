The eleven disciples went into Galilee, to the mountain where Jesus had sent them.

They had walked north from Jerusalem in the days after the empty tomb. The country was quieter now than it had been at Passover. The roads were not crowded. The talk in the towns they passed through was not about them. Most of those towns did not yet know what had happened at the cross, or what had happened on the third day.

The mountain Jesus had named was the one they had walked together before. They knew the slope of it. They had eaten there, on the grass, that day with the loaves and the fishes. They climbed it now in silence, in pairs, the way men climb when they are not sure what is at the top.

He was there when they arrived. They saw him standing in the open place near the summit, with the lake below in the distance.

They bowed down. Some of them did so without thinking. Some of them did so because they could not have done anything else. And some of them stood at the edge of the group with their hands at their sides and looked at him and were not sure. The text does not name those ones. It says only: some doubted.

He did not separate them. He did not call them out. He did not ask them to declare themselves one way or the other before he spoke.

He came to them, and he stood among them, and he spoke.

"All authority has been given to me in heaven and on earth. Go, then, and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all things that I commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the age."

He had not asked for their certainty. He had asked for their feet.

The wind moved over the mountain. The light moved on the lake far below. The eleven looked at one another. They did not all look the same as they had looked before he spoke. Some of them were no longer at the edge of the group. Some of them still were. He had not changed that. He had only added the promise that he would be with them all the way home. And so it was on the day Jesus sent his disciples out into the world, on the mountain in Galilee, where he had told them he would meet them.
