The eleven disciples went away into Galilee, into a mountain where Jesus had appointed them.

They had walked north from Jerusalem in the days after the empty tomb. The country was quieter now than it had been at the passover. The roads were not thronged. The talk in the towns they passed through was not of them. Most of those towns knew not yet what had come to pass at the cross, or what had come to pass on the third day.

The mountain Jesus had named was the one they had walked together before. They knew the slope of it. They had eaten there, upon the grass, that day with the loaves and the fishes. They climbed it now in silence, in pairs, the way men climb when they are not sure what is at the top.

He was there when they came. They saw him standing in the open place near the summit, with the sea below in the distance.

And when they saw him, they worshipped him. Some of them did so without thinking. Some of them did so because they could not have done otherwise. And some doubted. The text nameth them not. It saith only: some doubted.

He set them not apart. He called them not out. He asked them not to declare themselves before he spake.

And Jesus came and spake unto them, saying:

"All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth. Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost; teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you. And, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world."

He had not asked their certainty. He had asked their feet.

The wind moved over the mountain. The light moved upon the sea far below. The eleven looked at one another. They did not all look the same as they had looked before he spake. Some of them were no longer at the edge of the company. Some of them yet were. He had not changed that. He had only added the promise that he should be with them all the way home. And so it was on the day Jesus sent his disciples out into the world, on the mountain in Galilee, where he had told them he should meet them.
