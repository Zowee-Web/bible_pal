For forty years the hand of the Philistines had been heavy upon the land. The children of Israel had again done evil in the sight of the LORD, and the LORD had given them over. The Philistine garrisons sat in the lowlands. The roads bore their tribute. The villages along the hill country bent under it and went on bending. In the town of Zorah, in the tribe of Dan, a man named Manoah dwelt in a small house on the edge of the fields. His wife was barren, and bare not. She had carried that quiet weight for years.

She was alone when the angel of the LORD first came unto her.

There was no fanfare. There was no warning. She had gone about her work as she did each morning, and then a man was standing where no man had been, and the air round about him was different. He spake, and his voice was even.

"Behold now, thou art barren, and bearest not: but thou shalt conceive, and bear a son. Drink not wine nor strong drink, and eat not any unclean thing. The child shall be a Nazarite unto God from the womb. No razor shall come on his head. He shall begin to deliver Israel out of the hand of the Philistines."

She asked not his name. She asked not whence he was. She hearkened until he was done. Then he was no longer there.

She ran.

She went up across the field and through the gate of the house, and the words came out of her in one breath when she found Manoah. "A man of God came unto me. His countenance was like the countenance of an angel of God, very terrible. I asked him not whence he was, neither told he me his name. He said that I should conceive and bear a son, and that the child shall be a Nazarite unto God from the womb to the day of his death."

Manoah set down what he was holding. He looked upon his wife. He had heard her voice steady, and her voice weary, and her voice low, but he had not heard it as this. He went out into the open and entreated the LORD.

"O my Lord, let the man of God which thou didst send come again unto us, and teach us what we shall do unto the child that shall be born."

He waited there a long time. The wind moved across the field. The flocks of the village went out and came in. The sun lowered. He went back into the house. He waited the next day, and the day after.

The LORD hearkened to the voice of Manoah.

And the angel of God came again. He came again unto the woman, and again she was alone. She was sitting in the field when she lifted her eyes and saw him standing there. Her breath caught. She rose up and turned and ran a second time across the same ground, and her sandals beat the dust on the path.

She found Manoah and pulled him by the sleeve. "Behold, the man hath appeared unto me that came unto me the other day." She tarried not for an answer. She turned again and began to go back, and Manoah arose and went after her.

He came up unto the man where he stood waiting in the field. He stopped a few paces off. He looked upon his face. "Art thou the man that spakest unto my wife?"

"I am."

Manoah came nearer. The words he had prepared faltered. "Now let thy words come to pass. What shall be the child's manner of life? What shall be his work?"

The angel lengthened not the answer. "Let her observe all that I commanded the woman. She may not eat of any thing that cometh of the vine. Let her drink no wine nor strong drink. Let her eat no unclean thing. Let her observe all that I commanded her."

Manoah heard it. He nodded, slowly. He had asked, and he had been told that what was for the woman was for the woman. He felt the duty of a host rise in him. He could not let the man depart without a meal.

"I pray thee, let us detain thee," he said, "that we may make ready a kid for thee."

The angel's answer came back without delay. "Though thou detain me, I will not eat of thy bread. If thou wilt offer a burnt offering, thou must offer it unto the LORD."

Manoah understood not yet who stood before him. He pressed once more. "What is thy name, that when thy sayings come to pass we may do thee honour?"

The angel looked upon him. The look was not unkind. "Wherefore askest thou thus after my name, seeing it is secret?"

So Manoah went and took the kid with the meat offering, and he laid them upon the rock there in the field. He set the fire beneath them. He stepped back. His wife stood beside him. The wood caught. The flame went up from the rock toward the sky.

And the angel of the LORD ascended in the flame.

Manoah saw it before he understood it. The figure that had stood beside them rose into the rising fire — lit from within it, lifting with it, going up as the flame went up. The flame climbed. The angel climbed with it. The light reached high above the rock. Then the flame thinned. Then the flame was gone. And the rock stood empty, smoking, in the bare field.

Manoah and his wife fell on their faces to the ground.

They abode there a long time. The earth was warm against them. The smell of the burning wood drifted on the air and faded. Somewhere upon the hill a single bird called twice and was still. Neither of them moved.

The angel appeared no more unto them.

When at last Manoah lifted his face, the light had changed. He looked upon the empty rock and then upon his wife. His voice came forth thin. "We shall surely die, because we have seen God."

But his wife was already sitting up. She looked not upon the rock. She looked upon him. Her countenance was streaked with the dust of the ground, and she was not afraid.

"If the LORD were pleased to kill us," she said, "he would not have received a burnt offering and a meat offering at our hands, neither would he have shewed us all these things, nor would he have told us such things as these at this time."

She said it as one saith what is plainly true. She raised not her voice. She softened it not. She set the words down between them and let them rest.

Manoah hearkened. The fear in his breast departed not at once, but his wife's words moved through it and held still within it. He looked upon her across the cooling ash on the rock and saw that she had heard the angel before he had, and she had heard him better. He nodded, once. He helped her unto her feet. They walked together back across the field as the evening came on.

The child came in its season. She bare a son and called his name Samson. The boy grew in the small house at Zorah. He waxed strong in the hills above it. The LORD blessed him.

And in time the Spirit of the LORD began to move him at times in the camp of Dan, between Zorah and Eshtaol, where the long evenings settled over the open hills and the wind ran low across the ground his mother had once crossed at a run to tell her husband of a voice she could not name.
