The land had been under the hand of the Philistines forty years. The children of Israel had again done evil in the sight of the LORD, and the LORD had given them over. In the hill country of Dan, in the town of Zorah, a man named Manoah dwelt with his wife. She was barren, and bare not. She had carried that quiet weight a long time.

Then the angel of the LORD came unto her.

She was alone when he came. He stood before her and spake. "Behold now, thou art barren, and bearest not: but thou shalt conceive, and bear a son. Drink not wine nor strong drink, and eat not any unclean thing. The child shall be a Nazarite unto God from the womb. No razor shall come on his head. He shall begin to deliver Israel out of the hand of the Philistines."

She asked not his name. She asked not whence he was. She hearkened. Then she ran.

She found Manoah, and the words came out of her in one breath. "A man of God came unto me. His countenance was like the countenance of an angel of God, very terrible. I asked him not whence he was, neither told he me his name." Her hands were yet trembling against the cloth of her sleeves.

Manoah went out and entreated the LORD. "O my Lord, let the man of God which thou didst send come again unto us, and teach us what we shall do unto the child that shall be born."

The LORD hearkened to the voice of Manoah. And the angel of God came again — unto the woman, in the field, while she sat alone. She rose up at once and ran a second time. Manoah followed her back across the open ground.

He came unto the man. He looked upon him. "Art thou the man that spakest unto my wife?"

"I am."

Manoah let out a slow breath. "Now let thy words come to pass. What shall be the child's manner of life?"

The angel answered without taking his eyes from him. "Let her observe all that I commanded the woman."

Manoah heard the answer and knew not what else to ask. He felt the duty of a host rise in him. "I pray thee, let us detain thee, that we may make ready a kid for thee."

"Though thou detain me, I will not eat of thy bread. If thou wilt offer a burnt offering, thou must offer it unto the LORD."

Manoah pressed once more. "What is thy name, that when thy sayings come to pass we may do thee honour?"

The angel's voice did not lift. "Wherefore askest thou thus after my name, seeing it is secret?"

So Manoah took the kid with the meat offering and laid them upon the rock. He set the fire beneath them. The wood caught. The flame rose.

And the angel of the LORD ascended in the flame.

Manoah and his wife saw it. They watched the figure rise within the rising fire, lit from within it, lifting with it. The flame went up toward the sky from the rock. The angel went up with the flame. Then the flame thinned. Then the flame was gone.

They fell on their faces to the ground. The rock was warm beneath their hands. The smell of burning wood drifted on the air. Neither of them spake for a long while.

The angel appeared no more unto them.

Manoah lifted his face at last. His voice was thin. "We shall surely die, because we have seen God."

But his wife was already sitting up. She looked at him steadily across the cooling ash. "If the LORD were pleased to kill us, he would not have received a burnt offering at our hands, neither would he have shewed us all these things, nor would he have told us such things as these at this time."

Manoah hearkened. The fear in his breast departed not at once, but his wife's words sat down within it and held still. He looked upon her and saw that she was not afraid. He had not seen that in her countenance before.

The child came in its season. She bare a son and called his name Samson. The boy grew in the small house at Zorah. The LORD blessed him. And in time the Spirit of the LORD began to move him at times in the camp of Dan, between Zorah and Eshtaol, where the long evenings settled over the open hills and the wind ran low across them as he came of age in the land where his mother had once stood alone and hearkened unto a voice she could not name.
