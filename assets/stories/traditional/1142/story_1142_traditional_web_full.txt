The land had been under the hand of the Philistines for forty years. The children of Israel had again done evil in the sight of the Lord, and the Lord had given them over. In the hill country of Dan, in the town of Zorah, a man named Manoah lived with his wife. She was barren. She had carried that quiet weight a long time.

Then the angel of the Lord came to her.

She was alone when he came. He stood before her and spoke. "Behold, you shall conceive and bear a son. Drink no wine or strong drink. Eat no unclean thing. The child shall be a Nazirite to God from the womb. No razor shall come on his head. He shall begin to save Israel out of the hand of the Philistines."

She did not ask his name. She did not ask where he was from. She listened. Then she ran.

She found Manoah and the words came out of her in one breath. "A man of God came to me. His face was like the face of the angel of God, very awesome. I did not ask him where he was from. He did not tell me his name." Her hands were still trembling against the cloth of her sleeves.

Manoah went out and prayed. "Oh, Lord, please let the man of God whom you sent come again to us, and teach us what we should do for the child to come."

The Lord heard the voice of Manoah. And the angel of God came again — to the woman, in the field, while she sat alone. She rose up at once and ran a second time. Manoah followed her back across the open ground.

He came to the man. He looked at him. "Are you the man who spoke to my wife?"

"I am."

Manoah let out a slow breath. "Now let your words come to pass. What shall be the child's way of life?"

The angel answered without taking his eyes from him. "Let her observe all that I commanded the woman."

Manoah heard the answer and did not know what else to ask. He felt the duty of a host rise in him. "Please stay with us. We will prepare a young goat for you."

"Though you detain me, I will not eat of your bread. If you prepare a burnt offering, you must offer it to the Lord."

Manoah pressed once more. "What is your name? When your words come to pass, we will honor you."

The angel's voice did not lift. "Why do you ask my name? It is wonderful."

So Manoah took the young goat and the meal offering and laid them on the rock. He set the fire beneath them. The wood caught. The flame rose.

And the angel of the Lord ascended in the flame.

Manoah and his wife saw it. They watched the figure rise inside the rising fire, lit from within it, lifting with it. The flame went up toward the sky from the rock. The angel went up with the flame. Then the flame thinned. Then the flame was gone.

They fell on their faces to the ground. The rock was warm beneath their hands. The smell of burning wood drifted on the air. Neither of them spoke for a long while.

The angel did not appear to them again.

Manoah lifted his face at last. His voice was thin. "We shall surely die, because we have seen God."

But his wife was already sitting up. She looked at him steadily across the cooling ash. "If the Lord were pleased to kill us, he would not have received a burnt offering at our hand. He would not have shown us all these things. He would not have told us such things at this time."

Manoah listened. The fear in his chest did not leave at once, but his wife's words sat down inside it and held still. He looked at her and saw that she was not afraid. He had not seen that in her face before.

The child came in its season. She bore a son and named him Samson. The boy grew in the small house at Zorah. The Lord blessed him. And in time the Spirit of the Lord began to move on him, in the camp of Dan, between Zorah and Eshtaol, where the long evenings settled over the open hills and the wind ran low across them as he came of age in the land where his mother had once stood alone and listened to a voice she could not name.
