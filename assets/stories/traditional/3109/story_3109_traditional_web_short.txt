Jesus called to himself his twelve disciples and gave them authority over unclean spirits, to cast them out, and to heal every disease and every sickness.

These are the names of the twelve apostles: the first, Simon, who is called Peter; Andrew, his brother; James the son of Zebedee; John, his brother; Philip; Bartholomew; Thomas; Matthew the tax collector; James the son of Alphaeus; Lebbaeus, who was also called Thaddaeus; Simon the Zealot; and Judas Iscariot, who also betrayed him.

Jesus sent these twelve out and commanded them. They were not to go among the Gentiles or enter into any city of the Samaritans, but rather to go to the lost sheep of the house of Israel. “As you go, preach, saying, ‘The Kingdom of Heaven is at hand!’” he told them. “Heal the sick, cleanse the lepers, and cast out demons. Freely you received, so freely give.”

They were to take no gold, silver, or brass in their money belts, no bag for the journey, neither two coats, nor sandals, nor staff, for the laborer is worthy of his food. Into whatever city or village they entered, they were to find out who in it was worthy and stay there until they went on. “As you enter into the household, greet it,” Jesus said. If the household was worthy, their peace would come on it; but if it was not worthy, their peace would return to them.

For any who would not receive them or hear their words, the command was plain: “Whoever doesn’t receive you or hear your words, as you go out of that house or that city, shake the dust off your feet.” Then Jesus said, “Most certainly I tell you, it will be more tolerable for the land of Sodom and Gomorrah in the day of judgment than for that city.”
