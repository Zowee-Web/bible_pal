When Jesus had called unto him his twelve disciples, he gave them power against unclean spirits, to cast them out, and to heal all manner of sickness and all manner of disease.

Now the names of the twelve apostles are these: the first, Simon, who is called Peter, and Andrew his brother; James the son of Zebedee, and John his brother; Philip, and Bartholomew; Thomas, and Matthew the publican; James the son of Alpheus, and Lebbeus, whose surname was Thaddeus; Simon the Canaanite, and Judas Iscariot, who also betrayed him.

These twelve Jesus sent forth and commanded them. They were not to go into the way of the Gentiles, nor enter into any city of the Samaritans, but to go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Israel. “And as ye go, preach, saying, The kingdom of heaven is at hand,” he told them. “Heal the sick, cleanse the lepers, raise the dead, cast out devils: freely ye have received, freely give.”

They were to provide neither gold, nor silver, nor brass in their purses, nor scrip for the journey, neither two coats, neither shoes, nor yet staves, for the workman is worthy of his meat. Into whatsoever city or town they entered, they were to enquire who in it was worthy, and there abide till they went thence. “And when ye come into an house, salute it,” Jesus said. If the house was worthy, their peace would come upon it; but if it was not worthy, their peace would return to them.

For any that would not receive them nor hear their words, the command was plain: “Whosoever shall not receive you, nor hear your words, when ye depart out of that house or city, shake off the dust of your feet.” Then Jesus said, “Verily I say unto you, It shall be more tolerable for the land of Sodom and Gomorrha in the day of judgment, than for that city.”
