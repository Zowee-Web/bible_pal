In the first year of Darius, Daniel sat in his chamber with the scroll of Jeremiah open before him.

The lamps were lit. The light fell upon the parchment in a low warm wash, and Daniel followed the line of the prophet's writing with his finger. He counted. He read again, and counted again. Seventy years. The word of the Lord had set a number unto the desolation of Jerusalem, and the number was nigh unto its end.

Daniel looked up from the scroll. He understood what time it was.

He set his face unto the Lord his God. He laid the scroll down and he rose and he made ready in the manner of a man who hath read what he hath read. He took off the garments of the court and put on sackcloth. He went forth from the chamber and gathered ashes from the dead coals of the fire, and he carried them in his hands and let them down upon his head, and they marked his hair and his face and his shoulders. He sat. He set his face toward Jerusalem.

He did not eat. The hours moved through the chamber and the hours moved through his body, and he kept his fast, and he opened his mouth in prayer.

"O Lord," he said, "the great and dreadful God, keeping the covenant and mercy to them that love him and keep his commandments."

He paused. He took a breath. The confession came next, and he did not hurry it.

"We have sinned," he said. "We have committed iniquity. We have done wickedly. We have rebelled, even by departing from thy precepts and from thy judgments."

The cadence of the confession was the cadence of a man laying down stones in a wall. One verb. Then the next verb. Then the next. He did not soften it. He named it four times because the thing was a fourfold thing.

"Neither have we hearkened," he said. "Neither have we hearkened unto thy servants the prophets, which spake in thy name to our kings, our princes, our fathers, and to all the people of the land. O Lord, righteousness belongeth unto thee, but unto us confusion of face."

The prayer moved on, and Daniel moved with it. He named the mercy that belonged unto the Lord. He named the rebellion that belonged unto the people. He held both in his mouth at once. He did not separate them.

"To the Lord our God belong mercies and forgivenesses," he said. "Though we have rebelled against him."

The lamps burned. The ashes lay upon his shoulders. The fast had thinned his voice, and his voice grew quieter as it went lower into the prayer, but it did not cease.

Then his voice broke open, and he cried out.

"O Lord, hear; O Lord, forgive; O Lord, hearken and do; defer not, for thine own sake, O my God: for thy city and thy people are called by thy name."

The four cries went up. Hear. Forgive. Hearken. Do. Each one short. Each one carrying the weight of seventy years.

Whiles Daniel was speaking — whiles he was praying and confessing his sin and the sin of his people and presenting his supplication for the holy mountain of his God — the angel Gabriel came.

He came in swift flight. He came about the time of the evening oblation, which was the hour the priests in another country, in a temple that no longer stood, had once lifted the lamb upon the altar. The hour had not been forgotten. Daniel had kept it in his body and the Lord had kept it in heaven.

Gabriel touched him.

Daniel felt the touch upon his shoulder. He felt the hand of the messenger come down where the ashes lay, and he stopped his words, and he bowed his face to the ground.

"O Daniel," Gabriel said. "I am now come forth to give thee skill and understanding. At the beginning of thy supplications the commandment came forth, and I am come to shew thee; for thou art greatly beloved."

The lamps burned low in the chamber, and the evening was come, and Daniel kept his face to the ground, and he heard the words of the messenger settle around him the way the ashes had settled upon his hair, and he hearkened, and he held still.
