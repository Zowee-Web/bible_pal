Long ago, a man named David wrote a song unto God. It was not a song for battle drums nor for dancing. It was a quiet song, such as a man singeth when he is alone and pondering in his heart.

David began with simple words.

"O Lord, thou hast searched me," he sang. "And known me."

He sang that the Lord knew his downsitting and his uprising. Every single time. Whether David sat upon a stone in the field or upon a seat in his chamber, the Lord beheld it. Whether he arose at dawn or in the middle of the night, the Lord took notice.

The Lord understood his thoughts afar off. Before a single word was framed upon David's tongue, the Lord already knew it — the whole saying, from beginning unto end. The Lord knew his going out and his lying down. Every road David walked, every path his sandals touched, every place he laid his body by night — the Lord was acquainted with all of it.

"Thou hast beset me behind and before," David sang. "And laid thine hand upon me."

This knowledge was too wonderful for David. He could not take it all in. It was high. He could not attain unto it.

Then David sang a question.

"Whither shall I go from thy spirit? Or whither shall I flee from thy presence?"

He sang the answer himself. If he ascended up into heaven, the Lord was already there. If he made his bed in the deep places beneath the earth, the Lord was there also. If he took the wings of the morning, and flew across the sky to the uttermost parts of the sea — the farthest place David could imagine — even there should the Lord's hand lead him. The Lord's right hand should hold him.

Then David sang of darkness. He imagined the thickest, blackest night. If David said, "Surely the darkness shall cover me; even the night shall be light about me," it should not be so. Yea, the darkness hideth not from the Lord; but the night shineth as the day. The darkness and the light were both alike unto him. Neither could hide a man from the Lord's seeing.

Then David's song changed. He sang of how he was made.

The Lord had possessed his reins. The Lord had covered him in his mother's womb, carefully and closely, as one that worketh thread upon a loom. Every bone. Every piece of skin. Every small part that no man could behold from without. All of it had been wrought by the Lord in that secret place.

"I will praise thee," David sang, "for I am fearfully and wonderfully made."

He sang that the Lord's works were marvellous. David knew this in his soul. His substance was not hid from the Lord, when he was made in secret. When he was curiously wrought in the lowest parts of the earth, the Lord beheld every part of it. The Lord's eyes had seen his substance, yet being unperfect. Before David had a shape that any man would recognize, the Lord was there, beholding him grow.

Then David sang somewhat that made a man pause and consider. He sang that all his days were written in the Lord's book before even one of them came to pass. Every single day — already there, already set down, like words upon a scroll written beforehand.

David's song turned again. He sang of God's thoughts.

"How precious also are thy thoughts unto me, O God," he sang. "How great is the sum of them!"

David knelt and took up a handful of sand, watching the grains fall between his fingers. He reached for another handful, and another, but the sand kept pouring through. He opened his hands and looked upon the shore stretching beyond him in every direction.

"If I should count them," he sang, "they are more in number than the sand."

He shut his eyes. He opened them again.

"When I awake," he sang, "I am still with thee."

The last note faded. The air was still. David sat with his hands resting open, the song finished, the silence round about him as wide and close as sand upon a shore that stretched farther than his eyes could see.
