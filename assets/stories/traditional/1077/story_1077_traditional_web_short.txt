Long ago, a man named David wrote a song to God.

He sang it quietly, the way a person talks to someone very close. The words rose like breath in cool air.

"O Lord, you have searched me," David sang. "You know me."

He sang that God knew when he sat down and when he stood up. Every single time. God understood his thoughts from far away. Before David even opened his mouth, God already knew the words he would say.

David sang about walking and lying down. God was familiar with all of it. Every path David walked, every place he rested his head at night — God knew.

"Where can I go from your Spirit?" David sang. "Where can I flee from your presence?"

He sang that if he went up to the heavens, God was there. If he made his bed in the depths below, God was there too. If he flew on the wings of the morning to the far side of the sea, even there God's hand would hold him. God's right hand would guide him.

David sang about darkness. If he said, "Surely the darkness will hide me," even the night would shine like day. Darkness and light were the same to God. Neither one could block God's sight.

Then David sang about how he was made. God had created his innermost parts. God had knit him together inside his mother's body, the way someone carefully weaves thread on a loom. Bone and skin and breath — all formed in the hidden, secret place.

"I am fearfully and wonderfully made," David sang.

He sang that God's eyes had seen him before he was finished. All his days were written in God's book before even one of them came to be. Every single day, already written down.

Then David sang about God's thoughts. How precious they were. How many there were. He said if he tried to count them, they would outnumber the grains of sand — every tiny grain on every beach and every desert and every riverbed in the whole wide world.

David sang that if he could somehow count all those thoughts and reach the end, he would still be with God.

David lowered his hands from the strings. The last note faded over the hills like smoke thinning into a wide, empty sky.
