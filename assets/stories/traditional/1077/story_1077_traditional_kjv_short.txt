Long ago, a man named David wrote a song unto God.

He sang it softly, the way a man speaketh unto one very near. The words rose like breath in cool air.

"O Lord, thou hast searched me," David sang. "And known me."

He sang that God knew his downsitting and his uprising. Every single time. God understood his thoughts afar off. Before David opened his mouth, the Lord already knew the words he would speak.

David sang of his walking and his lying down. The Lord was acquainted with all of it. Every path David trod, every place wherein he laid his head by night — the Lord knew.

"Whither shall I go from thy spirit?" David sang. "Or whither shall I flee from thy presence?"

He sang that if he ascended up into heaven, the Lord was there. If he made his bed in hell, the Lord was there also. If he took the wings of the morning, and dwelt in the uttermost parts of the sea, even there should the Lord's hand lead him. The Lord's right hand should hold him.

David sang of darkness. If he said, "Surely the darkness shall cover me," even the night would shine as the day. The darkness and the light were both alike unto the Lord. Neither could hide him from the Lord's sight.

Then David sang of how he was made. The Lord had possessed his reins. The Lord had covered him in his mother's womb, as one that weaveth thread carefully upon a loom. Bone and skin and breath — all wrought in the secret place.

"I am fearfully and wonderfully made," David sang.

He sang that the Lord's eyes had seen his substance, yet being unperfect. All his days were written in the Lord's book, when as yet there was none of them. Every single day, already set down.

Then David sang of God's thoughts. How precious they were. How many there were. He said if he should count them, they would be more in number than the sand — every tiny grain on every shore and every desert and every river bed in all the wide world.

David sang that if he could somehow number all those thoughts unto the end, when he awaked he would still be with the Lord.

David lowered his hands from the strings. The last note faded over the hills like smoke thinning into a wide and empty sky.
