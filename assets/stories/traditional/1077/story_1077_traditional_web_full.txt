Long ago, a man named David wrote a song to God. It was not a song for battle drums or dancing. It was a quiet song, the kind a person sings when they are alone and thinking.

David began with simple words.

"O Lord, you have searched me," he sang. "And you know me."

He sang that God knew when he sat down and when he stood up. Every single time. Whether David sat on a stone in the field or on a chair in his room, God saw it. Whether he rose at dawn or in the middle of the night, God noticed.

God understood David's thoughts from far away. Before a single word formed on David's tongue, God already knew it — the whole sentence, beginning to end. God knew his going out and his lying down. Every road David walked, every path his sandals touched, every place he rested his body at night — God was familiar with all of it.

"You hem me in behind and before," David sang. "You lay your hand upon me."

This knowledge was too great for David. He could not take it all in. It was too high. He could not reach it.

Then David sang a question.

"Where can I go from your Spirit? Where can I flee from your presence?"

He sang the answer himself. If he climbed up to the heavens, God was already there. If he made his bed in the deep places below the earth, God was there too. If he rose on the wings of the dawn and flew across the sky to the far side of the sea — the farthest place David could imagine — even there God's hand would hold him. God's right hand would guide him forward.

David sang about darkness next. He imagined the thickest, blackest night. If David said, "Surely the darkness will hide me, and the light around me will become night," it would not work. Even the darkness was not dark to God. The night shone like daytime. Darkness and light were the same to him. Neither one could block God from seeing.

Then David's song changed. He sang about how he was made.

God had created his innermost parts. God had knit him together inside his mother's body, carefully and closely, the way someone works thread on a loom. Every bone. Every piece of skin. Every small part that no one could see from the outside. All of it had been shaped by God in that hidden, secret place.

"I praise you," David sang, "because I am fearfully and wonderfully made."

He sang that God's works were wonderful. David knew this deeply. His frame had not been hidden from God when he was being formed. When he was being woven together in the dark, God saw every part of it. God's eyes had watched him when he was not yet finished. Before David had a shape anyone would recognize, God was there, watching him grow.

Then David sang something that made a person stop and think. He sang that all his days were written in God's book before even one of them happened. Every single day — already there, already recorded, like words on a scroll written in advance.

David's song turned again. He sang about God's thoughts.

"How precious to me are your thoughts, O God," he sang. "How vast is the sum of them."

David knelt and scooped up a handful of sand, watching the grains spill between his fingers. He reached for another handful, and another, but the sand kept pouring through. He opened his hands and looked at the shore stretching out beyond him in every direction.

"If I should count them," he sang, "they are more in number than the sand."

He closed his eyes. He opened them again.

"When I awake," he sang, "I am still with you."

The last note faded. The air was still. David sat with his hands resting open, the song finished, the silence around him as wide and close as sand on a shore that stretched farther than his eyes could see.
