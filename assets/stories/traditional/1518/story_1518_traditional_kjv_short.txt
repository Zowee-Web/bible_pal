The captives sat down by the rivers of Babylon. The rivers were great canals that the Babylonians had cut across their country. They were wider than any river in Judah. The water was slow.

The willows grew along the banks. The captives had brought their harps with them out of Jerusalem. They had carried them through the long road across the wilderness. Now they were upon the bank of a river that was not theirs, in a country that was not theirs, and they knew not what to do with the harps.

They sat them down. They wept.

They remembered Zion. They remembered the temple. They remembered the city as it had been before the wall came down, before the burning, before the long road unto Babylon.

After a while they reached for the harps. They played them not. They lifted them up unto the willows. They hanged them upon the branches by the straps. The willows held them. The harps swung gently in the moving air above the canal.

The captors came along the bank wherein they sat. They that had wasted them stood there and required songs. They said: sing us one of the songs of Zion. They desired that the captives should sing the songs of the temple, here, upon the bank of the foreign water, for their entertainment.

The captives looked at the harps in the willows.

One of them spake. He spake for all of them. He said: how shall we sing the LORD's song in a strange land?

The captors went their way. The harps abode where they were.

The same man made a vow. He spake quietly. "If I forget thee, O Jerusalem, let my right hand forget her cunning. If I do not remember thee, let my tongue cleave to the roof of my mouth, if I prefer not Jerusalem above my chief joy."

The other captives hearkened. They made the vow with him in their own hearts. The river went on past the bank. The willows held the harps. The day moved on into evening, and the songs of Zion were not sung in the strange land, but neither were they forgotten.
