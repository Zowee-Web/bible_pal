The captives sat down by the rivers of Babylon. The rivers were great canals that the Babylonians had cut across their country. They were wider than any river in Judah. The water was slow.

The willows grew along the banks. The captives had brought their harps with them out of Jerusalem. They had carried them through the long road across the wilderness. Now they were on the bank of a river that was not theirs, in a country that was not theirs, and they did not know what to do with the harps.

They sat down. They wept.

They remembered Zion. They remembered the temple. They remembered the city as it had been before the wall came down, before the burning, before the long road to Babylon.

After a while they reached for the harps. They did not play them. They lifted them up to the willows. They hung them on the branches by the straps. The willows held them. The harps swung gently in the moving air over the canal.

The captors came along the bank where they sat. Those who had wasted them stood there and asked for songs. They said: sing us one of the songs of Zion. They wanted the captives to sing the songs of the temple, here, on the bank of the foreign water, for their entertainment.

The captives looked at the harps in the willows.

One of them spoke. He spoke for all of them. He said: how shall we sing the LORD's song in a strange land?

The captors went away. The harps stayed where they were.

The same man made a vow. He spoke quietly. "If I forget thee, O Jerusalem, let my right hand forget her cunning. If I do not remember thee, let my tongue cleave to the roof of my mouth. If I prefer not Jerusalem above my chief joy."

The other captives listened. They made the vow with him in their own hearts. The river went on past the bank. The willows held the harps. The day moved on into evening, and the songs of Zion were not sung in the strange land, but they were not forgotten either.
