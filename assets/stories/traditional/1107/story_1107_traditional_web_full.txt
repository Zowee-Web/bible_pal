The day of Unleavened Bread came, the day on which the Passover lamb must be sacrificed. The city was full. Smoke rose from cooking fires across Jerusalem. Families moved through the streets carrying food, herbs, wine.

Jesus sent Peter and John ahead. "Go and prepare the Passover for us, that we may eat."

They asked, "Where do you want us to prepare it?"

"When you enter the city," Jesus said, "a man carrying a pitcher of water will meet you. Follow him into the house where he goes. Tell the master of the house, 'The Teacher says to you, "Where is the guest room, where I may eat the Passover with my disciples?"' He will show you a large, furnished upper room. Make preparations there."

Peter and John went into the city. They entered through the gate with the crowd. They found the man carrying the water pitcher, walking ahead of them on the street. They followed him. He turned into a doorway. They spoke to the owner of the house. He led them up a stone stairway to a large upper room — furnished with a low table, cushions arranged around it, lamps set along the walls.

They prepared the Passover. They set out the lamb, the unleavened bread, the bitter herbs, the wine. The room filled with the smell of roasted meat and the warm smoke of oil lamps.

When the hour came, Jesus arrived with the rest of the twelve. They reclined at the table. The room was warm. The lamps cast moving shadows on the walls.

Jesus spoke. "I have earnestly desired to eat this Passover with you before I suffer. For I tell you, I will not eat of it again until it is fulfilled in the Kingdom of God."

He took a cup from the table. He gave thanks over it. "Take this, and divide it among yourselves. For I tell you, I will not drink from the fruit of the vine from now on, until the Kingdom of God comes."

The cup was passed. Each man drank and passed it to the next. The wine was dark in the lamplight.

Then Jesus took bread from the table. He gave thanks. He broke it — the sound sharp in the quiet room. He gave pieces to each of them.

"This is my body which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me."

They ate. The bread was dry and unleavened, flat on their tongues.

After supper, he took the cup again. "This cup is the new covenant in my blood, which is poured out for you."

The cup went around the table a second time. The men drank. The room was still. The lamps burned low. Outside, the sounds of the city carried through the window — voices, footsteps, the distant bleating of lambs.

Then Jesus spoke again. His tone did not shift. His hands remained on the table.

"But behold, the hand of him who betrays me is with me on the table. For the Son of Man indeed goes as it has been determined, but woe to that man through whom he is betrayed."

The room changed. The disciples looked at one another across the table. They began to question among themselves which of them it could be who would do this thing. Voices overlapped. Hands gestured. Some leaned forward. Some looked down.

The bread lay broken on the table between them. The cup sat among the dishes, still wet on its rim. The lamps flickered against the walls. The room held them all in silence.
