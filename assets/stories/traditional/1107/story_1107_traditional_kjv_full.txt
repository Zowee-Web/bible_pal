The day of unleavened bread came, the day when the passover must be killed. The city was full. Smoke rose from cooking fires across Jerusalem. Families moved through the streets bearing food, herbs, wine.

Jesus sent Peter and John before him. "Go and prepare us the passover, that we may eat."

They asked, "Where wilt thou that we prepare?"

"Behold," Jesus said, "when ye are entered into the city, there shall a man meet you, bearing a pitcher of water; follow him into the house where he entereth in. And ye shall say unto the goodman of the house, 'The Master saith unto thee, Where is the guestchamber, where I shall eat the passover with my disciples?' And he shall shew you a large upper room furnished: there make ready."

Peter and John went into the city. They entered through the gate with the multitude. They found the man bearing the pitcher of water, walking before them upon the street. They followed him. He turned into a doorway. They spake unto the master of the house. He led them up a stone stairway unto a large upper room — furnished with a low table, cushions set about it, lamps along the walls.

They made ready the passover. They set forth the lamb, the unleavened bread, the bitter herbs, the wine. The room filled with the smell of roasted meat and the warm smoke of oil lamps.

When the hour was come, Jesus arrived with the rest of the twelve. They sat down at table. The room was warm. The lamps cast moving shadows upon the walls.

Jesus spake. "With desire I have desired to eat this passover with you before I suffer: for I say unto you, I will not any more eat thereof, until it be fulfilled in the kingdom of God."

He took a cup from the table. He gave thanks over it. "Take this, and divide it among yourselves: for I say unto you, I will not drink of the fruit of the vine, until the kingdom of God shall come."

The cup was passed. Each man drank and gave it to the next. The wine was dark in the lamplight.

Then Jesus took bread from the table. He gave thanks. He brake it — the sound sharp in the quiet room. He gave pieces unto each of them.

"This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me."

They ate. The bread was dry and unleavened, flat upon their tongues.

After supper, he took the cup again. "This cup is the new testament in my blood, which is shed for you."

The cup went round the table a second time. The men drank. The room was still. The lamps burned low. Without, the sounds of the city carried through the window — voices, footsteps, the distant bleating of lambs.

Then Jesus spake again. His tone shifted not. His hands remained upon the table.

"But, behold, the hand of him that betrayeth me is with me on the table. And truly the Son of man goeth, as it was determined: but woe unto that man by whom he is betrayed!"

The room changed. The disciples looked one upon another across the table. They began to enquire among themselves which of them it was that should do this thing. Voices fell one upon another. Hands gestured. Some leaned forward. Some looked down.

The bread lay broken upon the table between them. The cup sat among the dishes, still wet upon its rim. The lamps flickered against the walls. The upper room held all of them — he that would betray, they that would scatter, and he that had just given thanks over the bread and the cup.
