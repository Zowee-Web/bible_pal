Jesus was teaching in one of the synagogues on the sabbath day. The light came through the high windows of the synagogue, and the people sat upon the benches along the walls.

And behold, there was a woman which had a spirit of infirmity eighteen years. She was bowed together, and could in no wise lift up herself. She had come into the synagogue with the others, and she stood at the hinder part of the room.

When Jesus beheld her, he called her unto him, and saith unto her, "Woman, thou art loosed from thine infirmity."

He laid his hands on her. And immediately she arose and stood upright, and glorified God. The bowing of her back was gone, and she lifted up her countenance toward the windows of the synagogue, and praised the Lord.

The ruler of the synagogue answered with indignation, because that Jesus had healed on the sabbath day, and saith unto the multitude, "There are six days in which men ought to work. In them therefore come and be healed, and not on the sabbath day."

Then the Lord answered him, and said, "Thou hypocrite, doth not each one of you on the sabbath loose his ox or his ass from the stall, and lead him away to watering? And ought not this woman, being a daughter of Abraham, whom Satan hath bound, lo, these eighteen years, be loosed from this bond on the sabbath day?"

And when he had said these things, all his adversaries were ashamed. And all the multitude rejoiced for all the glorious things that were done by him.

The woman stood among the people, and she sat down no more. She walked through the synagogue with her back made straight, and the people made way for her. She went forth into the courts, and lifted up her eyes unto the sky as she walked.

The sabbath continued in the synagogue, and the teachers sat down again upon their benches. The multitude tarried for a long season, and the voice of the woman which had been bowed eighteen years was heard again and again, praising God.

The morning sun was warm above the city, and the woman walked unto her house with her head lifted up toward the open sky.
