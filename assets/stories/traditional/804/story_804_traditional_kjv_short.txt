The apostle Paul sat among the believers in Rome, writing with careful thought. He looked upon the company — some pressed together with hope in their eyes, others troubled by hardship and the weight of uncertainty. Paul's voice was steady and reassuring as he spake the words which he had written down.

"We know," said Paul, "that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose."

A gentle stillness filled the room as the believers hearkened. Without, the world went on with all its unpredictability and suffering, but within, Paul's message took shape between them. He spake plainly and firmly, without hidden riddles or secret meanings — only the assurance he had learned while following Jesus.

Paul said not that every day was easy. The early church faced threats and losses: some had left their homes, some had parted with families, and some had seen friends arrested or unfairly judged. He acknowledged, with a glance at every face, that life often held pain and questions without quick answers.

But now, the words took hold. "We know," he repeated, "that whatsoever cometh to pass — good or evil, joyful or hard — God weaveth it all together with purpose. Unto them that love him, unto them that belong to him, there is direction and care, even when it is not yet clear."

One in the company shifted, thinking upon troubles left unspoken. A widow thought upon her absence at the morning table. A merchant remembered the sharp words of an angry customer. A young servant felt the sting of a harsh master. Yet Paul's message stayed gentle and unwavering.

He offered not a list of reasons or detailed explanations. Instead, he delivered the sure promise that, in God's hands, every moment — sorrowful or sweet — becometh part of a larger plan, meant for good. For God's people — them that loved him and had answered his call — there was hope beneath every trial and every joy.

Paul laid down his pen. He set his hand gently upon a friend's shoulder, and the room felt lighter. The believers arose to go back unto their own daily troubles and trusts, bearing with them Paul's message: that in all things, to them that love God, there is purpose, and God is working all things together for good.
