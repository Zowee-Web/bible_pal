Here followeth a Bible PAL retelling of Romans 8:28, expanded carefully with only the verse's ideas and words, not inventing new events or doctrine. This version useth warm, clear language, details, and pacing, while staying true to the message and meaning of the passage and only what may be observed therein.

---

In the time when believers gathered in houses and shared letters among distant friends, there were followers of Christ that often faced trials. Some found themselves anxious for their safety. Others wondered how to continue living in a world sometimes unkind unto their faith. Yet among them was a word that brought quiet comfort.

One would often remind the others with certainty, "We know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose."

This promise, spoken simply, settled over them as gentle sunlight after a storm. It was not spoken uncertainly or as a hope alone, but as a truth they knew. "We know" — not "we wish" or "we think" — but "we know". There was an assurance herein, a firm confidence. The hearers would nod, slowly taking courage to meet whatsoever waited beyond the walls.

The message said not that only good things came to pass. Some sat there with heavy hearts, knowing pain, loss, and sorrow. But the saying was that *all things* — every event, the hard moments and the glad — were gathered up into something good, not by chance, but by purpose. Nothing was without this promise.

The hope was not for every man in all places, but for a certain people — them that love God, whose hearts reach for him, that trust him. Among them that believed, this promise belonged. They were a people called of God, chosen for a purpose he designed long before. Their lives, though varied, were swept together in God's great plan.

So when they faced afflictions and hardships — when they were laughed at for their faith, misunderstood by kindred, or pressed by daily needs — they quietly called to remembrance the message: *All things work together for good.*

It was not that pain became pleasant or grief disappeared; rather, the promise was threaded through every circumstance. The events of their lives, however scattered or hard, were not random or without meaning. They trusted that God, whom they loved, was working even in the dark times. He was arranging every moment into something finally good.

Among them, this message was repeated in whispered prayer and shared quietly during meals or after a long day. When doubt shadowed them, they held unto the truth: God was shaping their days according to his purpose. Nothing was wasted. Even small things — a kindness given, a burden borne, a tear wiped away — were gathered together for good.

As the followers remembered this, they found a deep calm. Not all things would be easy or gentle, but all was in God's hands. This brought courage to face new mornings. They faced every day with hope, secure in their love for God, knowing that he was arranging every moment, every joy, every struggle into a greater good than they could see.

When times were bright, and laughter filled the room, some would look up and call to remembrance: "Even this is weaving into his purpose for us." When shadows fell and hopes seemed far, the same words would steady them. "All things — yea, even now — are working together for good."

Thus the days continued. The believers lived with this promise in their hearts. With every season, every sorrow, every gladness, they trusted God's purpose unfolding. They loved him, and they were sure he would not leave them alone. With this steadfast hope, they pressed onward — knowing that all things, truly all, were being formed together for good, because they loved God and were the called according to his purpose.
