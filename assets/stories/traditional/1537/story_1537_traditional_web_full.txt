Jesus came to a city of Samaria called Sychar. It was near the piece of land that Jacob had given to his son Joseph a long time before. Jacob's well was there, cut deep into the rock, its mouth ringed with stone worn smooth by hands and ropes over many generations.

Jesus was tired from his journey. He sat down beside the well. The dust of the road was on his feet and on the hem of his garment. It was about noon. The sun was high and the shadows were short. The air was still.

His disciples had gone away into the city to buy food. He was alone.

A woman of Samaria came to the well to draw water. She carried a jar. Her sandals scraped against the dry ground as she approached. Jesus said to her, "Give me a drink."

The woman stopped. She looked at him. She said, "How is it that you, a Jew, ask a drink from me, a woman of Samaria?" The Jews had no dealings with the Samaritans. A Jewish man did not speak to a Samaritan woman at a well in the middle of the day. Yet here he sat, asking.

Jesus answered her. He said, "If you knew the gift of God, and who it is that says to you, Give me a drink, you would have asked him, and he would have given you living water."

The woman looked at the well. She looked at his hands. He had no rope. He had no bucket. She said, "Sir, you have nothing to draw with, and the well is deep. Where then do you get that living water? Are you greater than our father Jacob, who gave us the well, and drank from it himself, and his children, and his livestock?"

The stone rim of the well was warm under the sun. The water was far below, cool and dark in the shaft.

Jesus said to her, "Whoever drinks of this water will thirst again. But whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never thirst. The water that I will give him will become in him a well of water springing up to everlasting life."

The woman set her jar down on the ground beside the well. She said, "Sir, give me this water, so that I do not thirst, and do not have to come here to draw."

Jesus said to her, "Go, call your husband, and come here."

The woman was quiet for a moment. Then she answered. "I have no husband."

Jesus said, "You have said well, I have no husband. For you have had five husbands, and the one you now have is not your husband. In that you spoke truly."

The woman's hands hung at her sides. She stood in the bright midday light by the mouth of the well, looking at the man who sat on the stone. She said, "Sir, I see that you are a prophet."

She pointed toward the mountain that rose above them. "Our fathers worshipped on this mountain. And you say that in Jerusalem is the place where people ought to worship."

The dry grass on the hillside did not move. There was no wind.

Jesus said to her, "Woman, believe me, the hour is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem." He paused. "You worship what you do not know. We know what we worship, for salvation is from the Jews."

He continued. "But the hour is coming, and now is, when the true worshippers will worship the Father in spirit and in truth. For the Father seeks such to worship him. God is Spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and in truth."

The woman stood still. The jar sat on the ground at her feet. The stone well rose between them, its opening dark. She said, "I know that Messiah is coming, the one who is called Christ. When he comes, he will tell us all things."

The road into Sychar was empty. The disciples had not yet returned. It was just the two of them at Jacob's well, the sun overhead, the water deep below. Jesus looked at her and said, "I who speak to you am he."
