And it came to pass that Jesus came unto a city of Samaria, which is called Sychar, hard by the parcel of ground that Jacob gave unto his son Joseph in days long past. There was the well of Jacob, hewn deep into the rock, the mouth of it ringed about with stone worn smooth by hands and by ropes through many generations. The grooves in the stone bore witness where the ropes had passed, day upon day, and year upon year.

Jesus, being wearied with his journey, sat thus upon the well. The dust of the way was upon his feet and upon the hem of his garment. It was about the sixth hour. The sun stood high in the heavens, and the shadows beneath the few scattered trees lay short and tight against their trunks. The air was still. No bird gave voice. No leaf was stirred. The fields upon the hillside lay silent under the heat.

His disciples were gone away into the city to buy meat. He was left alone. The path that came down from the village was empty, and the path that came up from the well was empty. There was only the stone, and the sun, and the deep water beneath.

Then came a woman of Samaria to draw water. She bare a jar balanced upon her shoulder. Her sandals scraped against the dry ground as she drew near, and little clouds of dust rose about her feet with every step. She looked not upon the man who sat upon the stone until she was come nigh. Then said Jesus unto her, "Give me to drink."

The woman stayed her steps. She set one hand against the jar to steady it. She looked upon him. She marked his raiment, and the cut of his beard, and the fringes upon the corners of his garment. And she said, "How is it that thou, being a Jew, askest drink of me, which am a woman of Samaria?" For the Jews had no dealings with the Samaritans. A man of the Jews did not speak unto a Samaritan woman at a well in the heat of the day. Yet there he sat, and there he asked.

Jesus answered and said unto her, "If thou knewest the gift of God, and who it is that saith unto thee, Give me to drink, thou wouldest have asked of him, and he should have given thee living water."

The woman looked upon the well. She looked upon his hands. They were empty. He had no rope. He had no vessel. And she said, "Sir, thou hast nothing wherewith to draw, and the well is deep. From whence then hast thou that living water? Art thou greater than our father Jacob, which gave us the well, and drank thereof himself, and his children, and his cattle?"

The stone of the well was warm beneath the sun. The water lay far below, cool and dark in the shaft. A small pebble rested upon the rim where some hand had set it down and forgotten it.

Then said Jesus unto her, "Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again. But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst. The water that I shall give him shall be in him a well, springing up unto life everlasting."

The woman lowered the jar from her shoulder. She set it upon the ground beside the well. The clay made a soft sound against the packed earth. And she said, "Sir, give me this water, that I thirst not, neither come hither to draw."

Jesus saith unto her, "Go, call thy husband, and come hither."

The woman held her peace a moment. She looked away, and then again upon him. Then answered she and said, "I have no husband."

Jesus said, "Well hast thou said, I have no husband. For thou hast had five husbands, and he whom thou now hast is not thy husband. Therein hast thou spoken truly."

The woman's hands hung at her sides. She reached not for the jar. She stepped not back. She stood in the bright noon light by the mouth of the well, looking upon the man who sat upon the stone. And after a space she said, "Sir, I perceive that thou art a prophet."

She turned a little, and pointed toward the mountain that rose above them. Its slopes were dry and pale in the noon light. "Our fathers worshipped in this mountain; and ye say, that in Jerusalem is the place where men ought to worship."

The dry grass upon the hillside stirred not. There was no wind. The sound of the village was far off, muffled by distance and by heat.

Jesus said unto her, "Woman, believe me, the hour cometh, when ye shall neither in this mountain, nor yet at Jerusalem, worship the Father." He paused. He looked toward the mountain, and then again upon her. "Ye worship ye know not what; we know what we worship: for salvation is of the Jews."

And he went on, saying, "But the hour cometh, and now is, when the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth. For such doth the Father seek to worship him. God is a Spirit, and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth."

The woman stood still. The jar lay upon the ground at her feet, the mouth of it open unto the sky. The stone well rose between them, the opening dark, the depth thereof unseen. And she said, "I know that Messias cometh, which is called Christ. When he is come, he will tell us all things."

The road into Sychar was empty. The disciples were not yet returned. The hillside lay silent. The sun stood directly overhead, and the well cast scarce a shadow upon the ground. There were but the two of them at Jacob's well, the sun above, the water deep beneath. Jesus looked upon her, and said, "I that speak unto thee am he."
