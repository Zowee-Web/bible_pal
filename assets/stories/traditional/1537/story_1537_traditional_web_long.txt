Jesus came to a city of Samaria called Sychar. It was near the piece of land that Jacob had given to his son Joseph a long time before. Jacob's well was there, cut deep into the rock, its mouth ringed with stone worn smooth by hands and ropes over many generations. The grooves in the stone showed where the ropes had passed, day after day, year after year.

Jesus was tired from his journey. He sat down beside the well. The dust of the road was on his feet and on the hem of his garment. It was about noon. The sun was high overhead and the shadows beneath the few scattered trees were short and tight against their trunks. The air was still. No bird called. No leaf moved. The fields on the hillside lay quiet under the heat.

His disciples had gone away into the city to buy food. He was alone. The path leading down from the village was empty. The path leading up from the well was empty. Only the stone and the sun and the deep water below.

A woman of Samaria came to the well to draw water. She carried a jar balanced on her shoulder. Her sandals scraped against the dry ground as she approached, and small clouds of dust rose around her feet with each step. She did not look at the man sitting on the stone until she came close. Jesus said to her, "Give me a drink."

The woman stopped. She set one hand against the jar to steady it. She looked at him. She saw his clothes, the cut of his beard, the tassels at the corners of his garment. She said, "How is it that you, a Jew, ask a drink from me, a woman of Samaria?" The Jews had no dealings with the Samaritans. A Jewish man did not speak to a Samaritan woman at a well in the middle of the day. Yet here he sat, asking.

Jesus answered her. He said, "If you knew the gift of God, and who it is that says to you, Give me a drink, you would have asked him, and he would have given you living water."

The woman looked at the well. She looked at his hands. They were empty. He had no rope. He had no bucket. She said, "Sir, you have nothing to draw with, and the well is deep. Where then do you get that living water? Are you greater than our father Jacob, who gave us the well, and drank from it himself, and his children, and his livestock?"

The stone rim of the well was warm under the sun. The water was far below, cool and dark in the shaft. A small pebble lay on the rim where someone had set it down and forgotten it.

Jesus said to her, "Whoever drinks of this water will thirst again. But whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never thirst. The water that I will give him will become in him a well of water springing up to everlasting life."

The woman lowered the jar from her shoulder. She set it down on the ground beside the well. The clay made a soft sound against the packed earth. She said, "Sir, give me this water, so that I do not thirst, and do not have to come here to draw."

Jesus said to her, "Go, call your husband, and come here."

The woman was quiet for a moment. She looked away, then back at him. Then she answered. "I have no husband."

Jesus said, "You have said well, I have no husband. For you have had five husbands, and the one you now have is not your husband. In that you spoke truly."

The woman's hands hung at her sides. She did not reach for the jar. She did not step back. She stood in the bright midday light by the mouth of the well, looking at the man who sat on the stone. After a moment she said, "Sir, I see that you are a prophet."

She turned slightly and pointed toward the mountain that rose above them. Its slopes were dry and pale in the noon light. "Our fathers worshipped on this mountain. And you say that in Jerusalem is the place where people ought to worship."

The dry grass on the hillside did not move. There was no wind. The sound of the village was far off, muffled by distance and heat.

Jesus said to her, "Woman, believe me, the hour is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem." He paused. He looked toward the mountain, and then back at her. "You worship what you do not know. We know what we worship, for salvation is from the Jews."

He continued. "But the hour is coming, and now is, when the true worshippers will worship the Father in spirit and in truth. For the Father seeks such to worship him. God is Spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and in truth."

The woman stood still. The jar sat on the ground at her feet, its mouth open to the sky. The stone well rose between them, its opening dark, the depth of it unseen. She said, "I know that Messiah is coming, the one who is called Christ. When he comes, he will tell us all things."

The road into Sychar was empty. The disciples had not yet returned. The hillside was quiet. The sun was directly overhead and the well cast almost no shadow on the ground. It was just the two of them at Jacob's well, the sun overhead, the water deep below. Jesus looked at her and said, "I who speak to you am he."
