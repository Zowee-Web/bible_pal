Jesus came unto a city of Samaria which is called Sychar. It was nigh unto the parcel of ground that Jacob had given unto his son Joseph in the days of old. And Jacob's well was there, hewn deep into the rock, and the mouth thereof ringed about with stone made smooth by the hands and ropes of many generations.

And Jesus was wearied from his journey. He sat himself down beside the well. The dust of the road lay upon his feet and upon the hem of his garment. It was about the sixth hour. The sun stood high and the shadows drew close beneath all things. And the air was still.

Now his disciples were gone away into the city to buy meat. And he was alone.

And there came a woman of Samaria to draw water. She bore a jar upon her, and her sandals scraped against the dry ground as she drew near. Jesus saith unto her, "Give me to drink."

The woman stayed her step. She looked upon him. And she said, "How is it that thou, being a Jew, dost ask drink of me, who am a woman of Samaria?" For the Jews kept no company with the Samaritans. A man of Judea did not speak unto a Samaritan woman at a well in the heat of the day. Yet there he sat, and asked.

Jesus answered and said unto her, "If thou knewest the gift of God, and who it is that saith unto thee, Give me to drink, thou wouldest have asked of him, and he would have given thee living water."

The woman looked upon the well. She looked upon his hands. He had no rope. He had no vessel. She said unto him, "Sir, thou hast nothing to draw with, and the well is deep. From whence then hast thou this living water? Art thou greater than our father Jacob, who gave us this well, and drank thereof himself, he and his children and his cattle?"

The stone rim of the well was warm beneath the sun. The water lay far below, cool and dark within the shaft.

Jesus said unto her, "Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again. But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst. The water that I shall give him shall become in him a fountain of water, springing up unto life everlasting."

The woman set her jar down upon the ground beside the well. She said unto him, "Sir, give me this water, that I thirst not, neither come hither to draw."

Jesus said unto her, "Go, call thy husband, and come hither."

The woman was silent a moment. Then she answered. "I have no husband."

Jesus said, "Thou hast well said, I have no husband. For thou hast had five husbands, and he whom thou now hast is not thy husband. This thou hast spoken truly."

The woman's hands hung at her sides. She stood in the bright noontide light beside the mouth of the well, looking upon the man who sat upon the stone. She said, "Sir, I perceive that thou art a prophet."

She lifted her hand toward the mountain that rose above them. "Our fathers worshipped in this mountain. Yet ye say that in Jerusalem is the place where men ought to worship."

The dry grass upon the hillside stirred not. There was no wind.

Jesus said unto her, "Woman, believe me, the hour cometh when ye shall worship the Father neither in this mountain nor yet at Jerusalem." He was silent a moment. "Ye worship that which ye know not. We know that which we worship, for salvation is of the Jews."

And he continued. "But the hour cometh, and now is, when they that truly worship shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth. For the Father seeketh such to worship him. God is a Spirit, and they that worship him must worship in spirit and in truth."

The woman stood still. The jar sat upon the ground at her feet. The stone well rose between them, and the mouth thereof was dark. She said, "I know that Messiah cometh, he who is called the Anointed One. When he is come, he shall declare unto us all things."

The road into Sychar lay empty. The disciples had not yet returned. It was but the two of them at Jacob's well, the sun above, the water deep below. And Jesus looked upon her and said, "I that speak unto thee am he."
