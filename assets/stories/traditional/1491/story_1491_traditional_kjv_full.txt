And the Lord sent Nathan unto David.

The king was in his house in Jerusalem. The matter of Uriah the Hittite and Bathsheba his wife had been finished some time before. Uriah's body had been buried. The mourning days for the dead man had ended. Bathsheba had been brought into the king's house, and David had taken her to be his wife. The child she had conceived was growing in her.

David had returned to the business of being king. He had heard reports from the messengers. He had received them that came unto him with petitions. He had ruled in the gate of his city.

Then Nathan came unto him and stood before him.

He said —

"There were two men in one city. The one rich, and the other poor.

The rich man had exceeding many flocks and herds.

But the poor man had nothing, save one little ewe lamb, which he had bought and nourished up. And it grew up together with him, and with his children. It did eat of his own meat, and drank of his own cup, and lay in his bosom, and was unto him as a daughter.

And there came a traveller unto the rich man. And he spared to take of his own flock and of his own herd, to dress for the wayfaring man that was come unto him. But he took the poor man's lamb, and dressed it for the man that was come to him."

And David's anger was greatly kindled against the man.

He spake from where he was seated, with the heat of the matter in his voice.

He said unto Nathan, "As the Lord liveth, the man that hath done this thing shall surely die. And he shall restore the lamb fourfold, because he did this thing, and because he had no pity."

And Nathan looked at David.

He raised not his voice. He pointed not his hand. He stood where he had been standing.

He said, "Thou art the man."

He waited a moment in the chamber. Then he gave the rest of the word of the Lord unto him.

He said, "Thus saith the Lord God of Israel: I anointed thee king over Israel, and I delivered thee out of the hand of Saul."

The chamber was very quiet.

The king was yet seated where he had been seated when Nathan came in. The judgment he had pronounced against the man of the parable was yet hanging in the air. The prophet was yet standing before him.

The king spake not. The chamber held its silence round about him.
