Yahweh sent Nathan to David.

The king was in his house in Jerusalem. The matter of Uriah the Hittite and Bathsheba his wife had been finished some time before. Uriah's body had been buried. The mourning days for the dead man had ended. Bathsheba had been brought into the king's house, and David had taken her to be his wife. The child she had conceived was growing in her.

David had returned to the business of being king. He had heard reports from the messengers. He had received those who had come to him with petitions. He had ruled in the gate of his city.

Then Nathan came in to David and stood before him.

He said —

"There were two men in one city. The one was rich. The other was poor.

The rich man had very many flocks and herds.

But the poor man had nothing — except one little ewe lamb, which he had bought and raised. It grew up together with him, and with his children. It ate of his own food. It drank of his own cup. It lay in his bosom. It was like a daughter to him.

A traveler came to the rich man. The rich man spared to take of his own flock and of his own herd, to prepare for the wayfaring man who had come to him. He took the poor man's lamb, and prepared it for the man who had come to him."

David's anger burned hot against the man.

He spoke from where he was seated, with the heat of the matter in his voice.

He said to Nathan, "As Yahweh lives, the man who has done this deserves to die. He must restore the lamb fourfold, because he did this thing, and because he had no pity."

Nathan looked at David.

He did not raise his voice. He did not point his hand. He stood where he had been standing.

He said, "You are the man."

He waited for a moment in the room. Then he gave the rest of Yahweh's word to him.

He said, "This is what Yahweh, the God of Israel, says: I anointed you king over Israel, and I delivered you out of the hand of Saul."

The room was very quiet.

The king was still seated where he had been seated when Nathan came in. The judgment he had pronounced against the man of the parable was still hanging in the air. The prophet was still standing before him.

The king did not speak. The chamber held its silence around him.
