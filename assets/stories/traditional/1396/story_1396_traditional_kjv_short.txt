The siege had gone on for many months now. The famine within the walls of Samaria had reached the place where an ass's head was sold for fourscore pieces of silver, and the women of the city had begun to do terrible things to keep their own children alive. Without the gate of the city, in the dust of the road whither the lepers were not suffered to come in past, four men sat together at the entering in. They beheld the closed gate, and they beheld the country between them and the host of the Syrians, and they spake one to another.

Now there were four leprous men at the entering in of the gate. They said one to another, "Why sit we here until we die?

If we say, 'We will enter into the city,' then the famine is in the city, and we shall die there. If we sit still here, we die also. Now therefore come, and let us fall unto the host of the Syrians. If they save us alive, we shall live; and if they kill us, we shall but die."

They arose up in the twilight, to go unto the camp of the Syrians. And when they were come to the uttermost part of the camp of the Syrians, behold, there was no man there.

For the Lord had made the host of the Syrians to hear a noise of chariots, and a noise of horses, even the noise of a great host; and they said one to another, "Lo, the king of Israel hath hired against us the kings of the Hittites, and the kings of the Egyptians, to come upon us."

Wherefore they arose and fled in the twilight, and left their tents, and their horses, and their asses, even the camp as it was, and fled for their life.

And when these lepers came to the uttermost part of the camp, they went into one tent, and did eat and drink, and carried thence silver, and gold, and raiment, and went and hid it. Then they came again, and entered into another tent, and carried thence also, and went and hid it.

Then they said one to another, "We do not well. This day is a day of good tidings, and we hold our peace. If we tarry till the morning light, some mischief will come upon us. Now therefore come, that we may go and tell the king's household."

And the four men turned back from the camp of the Syrians toward the closed gate of the city. The empty tents stood in the field behind them; the horses they had left tied unto the lines; the silver and the raiment they had hid under the dust of the open ground; and the tidings of the empty camp went with them up the road in the last hour of the twilight, unto the porters of famine-stricken Samaria.
