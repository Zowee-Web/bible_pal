The word of the LORD came unto Elijah. Arise, and go unto Zarephath, which belongeth to Sidon, and dwell there. Behold, I have appointed a widow in that place to sustain thee.

So Elijah arose and went. The land was parched and without mercy. Dust hung upon the roads like a veil. The brook Cherith had failed behind him, and the stones thereof lay bare and white beneath the sun. There was no rain in all Israel, nor any token that rain should come. He journeyed northward toward the coast, toward Sidon, toward a town unknown to him and a woman he had never looked upon.

And he came unto Zarephath. The town sat nigh unto the sea, yet the drought had reached even unto that place. The ground about the gate was hard and pale as bone. Brittle weeds grew in clusters along the walls, bent and without life. And Elijah passed through the gate.

There was a woman there. She was stooped low, gathering sticks from the ground. Her garments hung loose upon her frame. She took up one stick and then another, and pressed them to her breast with one arm. Two sticks. These alone did she seek. Her movements were slow and measured, as one who moveth carefully, having no strength to waste.

And Elijah called unto her. "Fetch me, I pray thee, a little water in a vessel, that I may drink."

The woman rose up and looked upon him — a stranger, covered in the dust of the road, standing within the gate. She spake nothing. She turned and set forth toward the well.

But before she had reached it, Elijah called again. "Bring me also, I pray thee, a morsel of bread in thine hand."

She stopped. She stood still a moment with her back turned unto him. Then she turned about. The sticks were yet pressed against her side. Her face was drawn, the skin tight upon her jaw.

"As the LORD thy God liveth," she said, "I have not so much as a cake of bread. There is but a handful of meal in the jar, and a little oil in the cruse." She lifted the two sticks slightly before him. "I am gathering these that I may go in and prepare it for me and for my son. We shall eat thereof. And after that, we shall die."

She spake it plainly. There was no petition in her words, nor any weeping. She was a woman declaring the end of her provision and the end of all things thereafter. The drought had consumed every portion. A handful of meal. A swallow of oil. One last morsel for two souls. She had come forth to gather the fuel for it.

And Elijah looked upon her. "Fear not," he said.

She waited.

"Go and do according as thou hast spoken. But first, make me thereof a small cake and bring it forth unto me. Afterward, make for thyself and for thy son."

The woman did not move.

"For thus saith the LORD God of Israel," Elijah continued. "The jar of meal shall not be spent. The cruse of oil shall not fail. Not until the day when the LORD sendeth rain upon the face of the earth."

The gate was still. Wind came through the opening and drove dust along the ground. The woman held her two sticks. She looked upon the face of Elijah. She looked down upon the sticks. And she turned and walked toward her house.

She went within. She set the sticks down. She took the jar of meal from its place and turned it forth. A handful of meal slid out into the bowl. She took the cruse of oil and poured. A thin thread of oil ran over the meal. She mixed it. She formed the dough with her hands and laid the small cake upon the fire. The sticks burned beneath it.

When it was finished she brought the cake forth unto Elijah. And he ate.

Then she returned within and prepared food for herself and for her son. And they ate.

On the morrow the woman went unto the jar and reached within. Her hand came up and there was meal in it. She tilted the cruse, and oil poured forth. She baked again. They ate again.

And so it continued. Day upon day. She and Elijah and her household ate for many days. The drought endured. The sky remained empty and white as linen. No rain fell upon the fields about Zarephath, nor upon any land. But the jar of meal was not spent. The cruse of oil did not fail.

Each morning the woman reached into the jar. Each morning there was meal against her fingers. Each time she lifted the cruse, oil ran forth in a slow and steady line. The jar sat in its place upon the shelf. The cruse sat beside it. And both of them held what they had held the day before, according to the word of the LORD which he had spoken through Elijah.
