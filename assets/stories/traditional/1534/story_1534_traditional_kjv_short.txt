The word of the LORD came unto Elijah. Go unto Zarephath, which belongeth to Sidon, and dwell there. I have appointed a widow in that place to sustain thee.

So Elijah departed. The road was long and without water, and the land lay broken beneath the drought. He came at last unto Zarephath and drew nigh unto the gate of the city. And there was a woman, stooped low upon the ground, gathering sticks. Her hands were thin. She took up two sticks together and laid them in the bend of her arm.

And Elijah called unto her. "Fetch me, I pray thee, a little water in a vessel, that I may drink."

She turned and set out toward the well. But before she had gone far, he called again. "Bring me also, I pray thee, a morsel of bread."

The woman stood still. She held her few sticks and looked upon him. "As the LORD thy God liveth," she said, "I have not so much as a cake. There remaineth but a handful of meal in a jar and a small portion of oil in a cruse. I am gathering these two sticks that I may go in and prepare it for my son and for myself. We shall eat of it. And after, we shall die."

Her voice did not waver when she spake these words. She sought no pity from him. She declared unto him that which was so.

Then Elijah said unto her, "Fear not. Go and do even as thou hast spoken. But make for me first a small cake thereof and bring it out unto me. And afterward, prepare for thyself and for thy son. For thus saith the LORD, the God of Israel: the jar of meal shall not be spent, and the cruse of oil shall not fail, until the day the LORD sendeth rain upon the earth."

The woman looked upon him. She looked upon the sticks in her arms. And she turned and went into her house.

She did according to that which Elijah had spoken.

And she and Elijah and her household did eat many days. Each morning the jar yet held meal. Each day the cruse yet held oil. The meal was not spent, neither did the oil fail. It was according to the word of the LORD which he had spoken by the mouth of Elijah.

The woman reached into the jar, and her hand came forth full. She poured from the cruse, and the oil ran out in a thin and steady stream. The meal was white and fine against her palm. And the oil caught the light from the doorway.
