The word of the LORD came to Elijah. Go to Zarephath, which belongs to Sidon, and stay there. I have commanded a widow there to feed you.

So Elijah went. The road was long and dry, the land cracked from drought. He came to Zarephath and approached the gate of the city. A woman was there, bending low, picking up sticks from the ground. Her hands were thin. She gathered two sticks at a time, stacking them in the crook of her arm.

Elijah called to her. "Please bring me a little water in a cup, so I can drink."

She turned and started toward the well. Before she had gone far, he called again. "Please bring me a piece of bread too."

The woman stopped. She stood holding her few sticks and looked at him. "As the LORD your God lives," she said, "I do not have a single cake. I have only a handful of flour in a jar and a little oil in a jug. I am gathering these two sticks so I can go home and prepare it for my son and myself. We will eat it. Then we will die."

Her voice was steady when she said this. She was not asking for pity. She was telling him what was true.

Elijah said to her, "Do not be afraid. Go and do as you have said. But first make me a small cake from it and bring it out to me. Afterward, make something for yourself and your son. For the LORD, the God of Israel, says this: the jar of flour will not run out, and the jug of oil will not go dry, until the day the LORD sends rain on the land."

The woman looked at him. She looked at the sticks in her arms. She turned and went into her house.

She did what Elijah told her.

And she and Elijah and her household ate for many days. Each morning the jar still held flour. Each day the jug still held oil. The jar of flour did not run out. The jug of oil did not go dry. It was according to the word of the LORD that he had spoken through Elijah.

The woman reached into the jar and her hand came up full. She poured from the jug and oil ran out in a thin steady stream. The flour was white and fine against her palm. The oil caught the light from the doorway.
