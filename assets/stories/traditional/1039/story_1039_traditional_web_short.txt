Elijah the Tishbite said to Ahab, "As Yahweh, the God of Israel, lives, before whom I stand, there shall not be dew nor rain these years, except by my word."

The word of Yahweh came to him. "Go east. Hide yourself by the brook Cherith, before the Jordan. You shall drink from the brook. I have commanded the ravens to feed you there."

He went and dwelt by the brook Cherith. The water ran clear over the stones. The ravens brought him bread and meat in the morning, bread and meat in the evening. He drank from the brook and ate what the birds brought.

After a while the brook dried up. The water slowed to a trickle, then stopped. The bed turned to cracked mud. There was no rain in the land.

The word of Yahweh came to him. "Arise, go to Zarephath, which belongs to Sidon. I have commanded a widow there to sustain you."

He arose and went to Zarephath. At the gate of the city, a widow was gathering sticks. Her hands were thin. The sticks were few and dry.

He called to her. "Please bring me a little water in a vessel, that I may drink." As she went, he called again. "Please bring me a morsel of bread."

She stopped. "As Yahweh your God lives, I don't have a cake, but only a handful of meal in a jar and a little oil in a jug. I am gathering two sticks, that I may prepare it for me and my son, that we may eat it and die."

Elijah said, "Don't be afraid. Go and do as you have said, but make me a little cake first. Afterward make for you and your son. For Yahweh says: 'The jar of meal will not run out, and the jug of oil will not fail, until Yahweh sends rain on the earth.'"

She went and did according to his word. She made the cake and brought it to him first. Then she made for herself and her son.

They ate that day. And the next. And the day after.

The jar of meal was not used up. The jug of oil did not fail. Each morning she reached into the jar and found meal. Each morning she tilted the jug and oil ran out. Not more than enough. But enough.
