Elijah the Tishbite, who was of the inhabitants of Gilead, said unto Ahab, "As the Lord God of Israel liveth, before whom I stand, there shall not be dew nor rain these years, but according to my word."

He spake it and turned and left the king standing in his court. The sky above Samaria was blue and cloudless.

And the word of the Lord came unto him, saying, "Get thee hence, and turn thee eastward, and hide thyself by the brook Cherith, that is before Jordan. And it shall be, that thou shalt drink of the brook; and I have commanded the ravens to feed thee there."

So he went and did according unto the word of the Lord. He went and dwelt by the brook Cherith, that is before Jordan. The water ran clear and cold over smooth stones. The banks were narrow and the shade of the trees fell across the current.

The ravens brought him bread and flesh in the morning, and bread and flesh in the evening. He drank of the brook. The days were quiet and long. The birds came at dawn and at dusk, dark against the sky, carrying food in their beaks.

And it came to pass after a while, that the brook dried up, because there had been no rain in the land. The water thinned to a thread. The stones grew dry. The mud cracked and pulled apart and the bed of the brook turned to dust.

And the word of the Lord came unto him, saying, "Arise, get thee to Zarephath, which belongeth to Zidon, and dwell there. Behold, I have commanded a widow woman there to sustain thee."

So he arose and went to Zarephath. The road was dry. The fields on either side were brown. He came to the gate of the city, and, behold, the widow woman was there gathering of sticks. Her hands were thin. Her garment hung loose upon her frame. The sticks were few and dry and she gathered them slowly, one by one, bending to pluck them from the hard ground.

He called unto her. "Fetch me, I pray thee, a little water in a vessel, that I may drink."

She turned toward him. She set down the sticks and went to fetch it. As she was going, he called unto her again. "Bring me, I pray thee, a morsel of bread in thine hand."

She stopped. She stood still. Then she turned fully and looked upon him. "As the Lord thy God liveth, I have not a cake, but an handful of meal in a barrel, and a little oil in a cruse; and, behold, I am gathering two sticks, that I may go in and dress it for me and my son, that we may eat it, and die."

The words came forth flat. She was not weeping. She had passed beyond weeping.

And Elijah said unto her, "Fear not; go and do as thou hast said; but make me thereof a little cake first, and bring it unto me, and after make for thee and for thy son. For thus saith the Lord God of Israel, The barrel of meal shall not waste, neither shall the cruse of oil fail, until the day that the Lord sendeth rain upon the earth."

She looked upon him. She looked upon the sticks in her hand. She looked upon the gate of the city behind her.

And she went and did according to the saying of Elijah. She took the handful of meal from the barrel. She poured the little oil from the cruse. She mixed and shaped the cake over her small fire. She brought it unto him first. Then she made for herself and for her son.

They did eat that day.

The next morning she went unto the barrel. She reached within. There was meal. She tilted the cruse. Oil ran forth. She made bread.

The day after was the same. And the day after that.

And the barrel of meal wasted not, neither did the cruse of oil fail. Day after day, her hand found what should not have been there — an handful of meal at the bottom of the barrel, a thin stream of oil from the cruse. Not more than the day required. But enough.

According to the word of the Lord, which he spake by Elijah. The drought continued. The heaven stayed empty. But in that house in Zarephath, the three of them did eat. The barrel wasted not. The oil did not fail.
