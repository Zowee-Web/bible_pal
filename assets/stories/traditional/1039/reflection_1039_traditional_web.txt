The widow did not receive a storehouse. She received a handful of meal each morning. The jar never filled to the brim. The jug never overflowed. There was only enough for the day, and then the day ended, and she had to reach into the jar again the next morning not knowing what her hand would find.

Provision that arrives one day at a time asks something specific of the one who receives it. It asks for the willingness to go to sleep without certainty about tomorrow. The widow had already reached the end of her resources when Elijah arrived. She was gathering two sticks. She had a plan: eat, and die.

What changed was not the size of her supply. It was that the supply did not stop. Weariness and provision existed in the same house, at the same table. The drought did not end. The sky stayed empty. But the jar had meal in the morning, and that was enough to make bread.
