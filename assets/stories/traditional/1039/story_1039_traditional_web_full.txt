Elijah the Tishbite, who was of the settlers of Gilead, said to Ahab, "As Yahweh, the God of Israel, lives, before whom I stand, there shall not be dew nor rain these years, except by my word."

He said it and turned and left the king standing in his court. The sky above Samaria was blue and cloudless.

Then the word of Yahweh came to Elijah. "Go from here. Turn east and hide yourself by the brook Cherith, which is before the Jordan. It shall be that you will drink from the brook. I have commanded the ravens to feed you there."

He went. He did according to the word of Yahweh. He went and lived by the brook Cherith, which is before the Jordan. The water ran clear and cold over smooth stones. The banks were narrow and the shade of the trees fell across the current.

The ravens brought him bread and meat in the morning. They brought him bread and meat in the evening. He drank from the brook. The days were quiet and long. The birds came at dawn and at dusk, dark against the sky, carrying food in their beaks.

After a while the brook dried up, because there was no rain in the land. The water thinned to a thread. The stones grew dry. The mud cracked and pulled apart and the bed of the brook turned to dust.

The word of Yahweh came to him. "Arise, go to Zarephath, which belongs to Sidon, and live there. I have commanded a widow there to provide for you."

He arose and went to Zarephath. The road was dry. The fields on either side were brown. He came to the gate of the city. A widow was there, gathering sticks at the base of the wall. Her hands were thin. Her garment hung loose on her frame. The sticks were few and dry and she gathered them slowly, one at a time, bending to pick them from the hard ground.

He called to her. "Please bring me a little water in a vessel, that I may drink."

She turned toward him. She set down the sticks and went to get water. As she was going, he called to her again. "Please bring me a morsel of bread in your hand."

She stopped. She stood still. Then she turned fully and looked at him. "As Yahweh your God lives, I don't have a cake, but only a handful of meal in a jar and a little oil in a jug. Look, I am gathering two sticks, that I may go in and prepare it for me and my son, that we may eat it, and die."

The words came out flat. She was not weeping. She had passed beyond weeping.

Elijah said to her, "Don't be afraid. Go and do as you have said. But make me a little cake from it first and bring it out to me. Afterward make some for you and for your son. For Yahweh, the God of Israel, says: 'The jar of meal will not run out, and the jug of oil will not fail, until the day that Yahweh sends rain on the earth.'"

She looked at him. She looked at the sticks in her hand. She looked at the gate of the city behind her.

She went and did according to the word of Elijah. She took the handful of meal from the jar. She poured the little oil from the jug. She mixed and shaped the cake over her small fire. She brought it to him first. Then she made for herself and for her son.

They ate that day.

The next morning she went to the jar. She reached in. There was meal. She tilted the jug. Oil ran out. She made bread.

The day after was the same. And the day after that.

The jar of meal was not used up. The jug of oil did not fail. Day after day, her hand found what should not have been there — a handful of meal at the bottom of the jar, a thin stream of oil from the jug. Not more than the day required. But enough.

It was according to the word of Yahweh, which he spoke by Elijah. The drought continued. The sky stayed empty. But in that house in Zarephath, the three of them ate. The jar did not run dry. The oil did not fail.
