Elijah the Tishbite said unto Ahab, "As the Lord God of Israel liveth, before whom I stand, there shall not be dew nor rain these years, but according to my word."

The word of the Lord came unto him. "Get thee hence, and turn thee eastward, and hide thyself by the brook Cherith, before Jordan. Thou shalt drink of the brook. I have commanded the ravens to feed thee there."

He went and dwelt by the brook Cherith. The water ran clear over the stones. The ravens brought him bread and flesh in the morning, bread and flesh in the evening. He drank of the brook and ate what the birds brought.

After a while the brook dried up. The water slowed to a trickle, then ceased. The bed turned to cracked mud. There was no rain in the land.

The word of the Lord came unto him. "Arise, get thee to Zarephath, which belongeth to Zidon. I have commanded a widow woman there to sustain thee."

He arose and went to Zarephath. At the gate of the city, a widow was gathering sticks. Her hands were thin. The sticks were few and dry.

He called unto her. "Fetch me, I pray thee, a little water in a vessel, that I may drink." As she went, he called again. "Bring me a morsel of bread in thine hand."

She stopped. "As the Lord thy God liveth, I have not a cake, but an handful of meal in a barrel and a little oil in a cruse. I am gathering two sticks, that I may dress it for me and my son, that we may eat it and die."

Elijah said, "Fear not. Go and do as thou hast said, but make me thereof a little cake first. Afterward make for thee and thy son. For thus saith the Lord: The barrel of meal shall not waste, neither shall the cruse of oil fail, until the Lord sendeth rain upon the earth."

She went and did according to the saying of Elijah. She made the cake and brought it unto him first. Then she made for herself and her son.

They did eat that day. And the next. And the day after.

The barrel of meal wasted not, neither did the cruse of oil fail. Each morning she reached into the barrel and found meal. Each morning she tilted the cruse and oil ran forth. Not more than enough. But enough.
