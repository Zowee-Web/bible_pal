There were standing by the cross of Jesus his mother, and his mother's sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene. They had followed him from Galilee, and they stood at a distance and watched the things that were done.

The afternoon had grown dark over the city of Jerusalem. The voices of the soldiers rose along the hill, and the lots had been cast for the garments of Jesus.

Mary Magdalene stood with the women, and she did not turn away. She had walked behind him in the days of Galilee, and she had been delivered of seven demons by his hand. Now she stood at the place of the cross, and the wood was rough above her, and the sky was darkening.

Jesus saw his mother, and the disciple whom he loved standing there. He said to his mother, "Woman, behold your son."

Then he said to the disciple, "Behold your mother."

From that hour, the disciple took her to his own home.

Mary Magdalene remained with the other women near the cross. The hours passed, and the daylight did not return for a long time. The voices of the priests had quieted, and the soldiers stood watch.

When evening came, Joseph of Arimathaea came and asked Pilate for the body of Jesus. He took it down and wrapped it in fine linen, and laid it in a tomb that had been hewn out of the rock. He rolled a stone against the door of the tomb and went away.

Mary Magdalene and the other Mary sat opposite the tomb. They watched the place where he had been laid, and they did not leave until the Sabbath drew near.

The evening was cool over Jerusalem, and the women rose from their place. They went to prepare spices and ointments, and they kept the Sabbath according to the commandment.

Mary Magdalene returned to the place where she was staying. The lamps were burning low along the streets, and the city was quiet, and she did not sleep that night for the things she had seen.
