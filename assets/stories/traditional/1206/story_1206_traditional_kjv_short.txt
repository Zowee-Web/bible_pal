There stood by the cross of Jesus his mother, and his mother's sister, Mary the wife of Cleophas, and Mary Magdalene. They had followed him from Galilee, and they stood afar off, and beheld the things that were done.

The afternoon had waxed dark over the city of Jerusalem. The voices of the soldiers arose along the hill, and the lots had been cast for the garments of Jesus.

Mary Magdalene stood with the women, and she turned not away. She had walked behind him in the days of Galilee, and out of her had been cast seven devils by his hand. Now stood she at the place of the cross, and the wood was rough above her, and the heavens were waxing dark.

When Jesus therefore saw his mother, and the disciple standing by, whom he loved, he saith unto his mother, "Woman, behold thy son!"

Then saith he to the disciple, "Behold thy mother!"

And from that hour, that disciple took her unto his own home.

Mary Magdalene tarried with the other women near the cross. The hours passed, and the daylight returned not for a long season. The voices of the priests had grown still, and the soldiers kept watch.

When the even was come, Joseph of Arimathaea came and besought Pilate that he might take away the body of Jesus. He took it down and wound it in fine linen, and laid it in a sepulchre that had been hewn out of the rock. He rolled a stone against the door of the sepulchre, and went his way.

Mary Magdalene and the other Mary sat over against the sepulchre. They watched the place where he was laid, and they departed not until the sabbath drew nigh.

The even was cool over Jerusalem, and the women arose from their place. They went to prepare spices and ointments, and they kept the sabbath day according to the commandment.

Mary Magdalene returned unto the place where she abode. The lamps were burning low along the streets, and the city was still, and she slept not that night for the things which she had seen.
