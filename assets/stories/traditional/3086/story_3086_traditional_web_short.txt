Word came to David that the Philistines were fighting against Keilah and robbing the threshing floors. David asked Yahweh whether he should go and strike them. Yahweh told him to go, defeat the Philistines, and save Keilah.

David's men answered that they were already afraid while staying in Judah. Going to Keilah against the armies of the Philistines seemed still more dangerous. David therefore asked Yahweh again. Yahweh told him to arise and go down to Keilah, for the Philistines would be delivered into his hand.

David went with his men. They fought the Philistines, drove away their livestock, and struck them with a great slaughter. In this way David saved the inhabitants of Keilah. When Abiathar son of Ahimelech had fled to David there, he had brought the ephod with him.

Saul was told that David had entered Keilah. Hearing that David was inside a town with gates and bars, Saul summoned all the people to war and planned to besiege the town. David learned that Saul was devising harm against him, and he told Abiathar the priest to bring the ephod near.

David prayed to Yahweh, the God of Israel. He had heard that Saul intended to come and destroy the city because David was there. He asked whether Saul would come down. Yahweh answered that Saul would come. David then asked whether the men of Keilah would deliver him and his men into Saul's hand. Yahweh answered that they would deliver him.

David and his men, about six hundred, arose and left Keilah. They went wherever they could go. When Saul heard that David had escaped, he stopped the expedition. David stayed in the wilderness strongholds and in the hill country of the wilderness of Ziph. Saul sought him every day, but God did not deliver David into his hand.
