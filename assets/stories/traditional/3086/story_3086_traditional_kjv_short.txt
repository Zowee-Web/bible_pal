Tidings came unto David that the Philistines fought against Keilah and robbed the threshingfloors. David enquired of the Lord whether he should go and smite them. The Lord commanded him to go, smite the Philistines, and save Keilah.

But David's men said that they were afraid even while they abode in Judah. If they went down to Keilah against the armies of the Philistines, their peril would be greater. David therefore enquired of the Lord yet again. The Lord bade him arise and go down, for he would deliver the Philistines into David's hand.

David and his men went unto Keilah. They fought with the Philistines, brought away their cattle, and smote them with a great slaughter. So David saved the inhabitants of Keilah. When Abiathar the son of Ahimelech fled unto David there, he came down with an ephod in his hand.

It was told Saul that David had entered Keilah. Because David was within a town that had gates and bars, Saul called all the people together to war and purposed to besiege the city. David knew that Saul secretly practised mischief against him, and he commanded Abiathar the priest to bring near the ephod.

David called upon the Lord God of Israel. He had surely heard that Saul sought to come unto Keilah and destroy the city for his sake. He asked whether Saul would indeed come down, and the Lord answered that he would come. David then asked whether the men of Keilah would deliver him and his men into Saul's hand. The Lord answered that they would deliver him.

Then David and his men, about six hundred, arose and departed out of Keilah. They went whithersoever they could go. When Saul heard that David had escaped, he forbare to go forth. David abode in strong holds in the wilderness and in the mountain of the wilderness of Ziph. Saul sought him every day, but God delivered him not into his hand.
