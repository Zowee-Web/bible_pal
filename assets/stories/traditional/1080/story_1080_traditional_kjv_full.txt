The road through the village was thick with people. Dust rose in low clouds beneath the shuffle of their feet, and the sun stood high above the pale stone walls. At the midst of the press walked a man called Jesus. His garment was plain and dark with the dust of the road. The air about him was warm and full of voices — people calling his name, reaching toward him, pressing nigh from every side. Children moved between the legs of the grown. Women held their bundles close. Men pushed forward from the edges. Whithersoever he went, the people followed.

Among that multitude, pressed between strangers, was a certain woman. She had an issue of blood twelve years. She spake of it to no man. She lifted not her face. But the affliction had shaped her life, narrowing it to a single, wearing burden. She had suffered many things of many physicians — treatments that brought suffering but no healing, remedies that promised much and gave nothing. She had spent all that she had, every coin, every possession, and was nothing bettered. She was rather grown worse.

She had heard the things which were spoken of Jesus. Healings. Wonders. The sick made whole. Such tidings passed from village to village along the roads, and she had hearkened. Something in those stories yet reached her.

That day she followed the crowd. She moved quietly, her head bowed low, pressing through the bodies about her. She came behind Jesus, step by step, until she was near enough to reach.

She said within herself, "If I may touch but his clothes, I shall be whole."

She stretched forth her hand. Her fingers touched the border of his garment — the fringe, the very hem.

And straightway the fountain of her blood was dried up. She felt it in her body — a stillness where there had been none, a quieting she had not known in twelve years. She knew that she was healed of that plague.

And immediately Jesus, knowing in himself that virtue had gone out of him, turned him about in the press. He looked upon the faces round about him.

"Who touched my clothes?" he asked.

His disciples said unto him, "Thou seest the multitude thronging thee, and sayest thou, Who touched me?"

But he was not satisfied. He looked round about to see her that had done this thing. His eyes moved from face to face across the crowd.

The woman stood very still. She was trembling now. She knew what was done in her. The crowd shifted, but she moved not. Then she came forward. She fell down before him in the dust. It rose about her knees.

She lifted her eyes unto his and told him all the truth. The twelve years. The physicians. The money spent. The hope that had grown thin. The day she heard of him. The reaching. The touch. She told him all.

Jesus looked upon her. His countenance was gentle. He spake softly.

"Daughter," he said, "thy faith hath made thee whole. Go in peace, and be whole of thy plague."
