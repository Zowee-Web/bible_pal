The road through the village was thick with people. Dust rose beneath their feet and hung in the warm air. At the midst of the crowd walked a man called Jesus. His garment was dark with the dust of the road. The people pressed upon him from every side — reaching toward him, calling his name, following wherever he went.

Among them was a certain woman, which had an issue of blood twelve years. She had suffered many things of many physicians, and had spent all that she had, and was nothing bettered, but rather grew worse. Twelve years she had borne this affliction, and none could make her whole.

She had heard of the things concerning Jesus. Healings. Wonders. The sick made well.

That day she followed the crowd. She spake to no one. She lifted not her face. She came behind him in the press, step by step, drawing near.

She said within herself, "If I may touch but his clothes, I shall be whole."

She stretched forth her hand. Her fingers touched the hem of his garment.

And straightway the fountain of her blood was dried up. She felt it in her body — a stillness where there had been none. She knew that she was healed of that plague.

And immediately Jesus, knowing in himself that virtue had gone out of him, turned him about in the press and said, "Who touched my clothes?"

His disciples said unto him, "Thou seest the multitude thronging thee, and sayest thou, Who touched me?"

But he looked round about to see her that had done this thing.

The woman, fearing and trembling, knowing what was done in her, came and fell down before him. The dust rose about her knees. She lifted her eyes unto his and told him all the truth.

He looked upon her with kindness. He spoke gently.

"Daughter," he said, "thy faith hath made thee whole. Go in peace, and be whole of thy plague."
