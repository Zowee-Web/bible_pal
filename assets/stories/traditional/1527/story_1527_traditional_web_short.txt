The people of Israel stood at the edge of the sea with the Egyptian army behind them, the sound of chariots growing louder across the flat ground.

Moses stretched out his hand over the water.

The Lord sent a strong east wind. It came hard and steady across the surface, and it did not stop. All through the night it blew. The water moved. It pulled back from the center, slow at first, then faster. The seabed appeared — dark sand, then pale stone, then long stretches of ground drying under the wind. The water rose on the right side and rose on the left side, standing up like walls, and between them lay a path wide enough for a nation to walk through.

Israel went forward. They stepped onto dry ground where hours before there had been deep water. Families walked together. Livestock moved between them. The wind pressed against their faces and robes. On either side, the water stood high and still, held in place, and the people passed between those walls with the night sky above them.

The Egyptians followed. Pharaoh's chariots and horsemen drove onto the dry seabed after them. Horses, riders, wheels turning on the hard ground — the full army came in pursuit between the walls of water.

Then the Lord looked down on the Egyptian army and threw them into confusion. Chariot wheels locked and broke away. Horses stumbled. The heavy frames dragged against the ground. The Egyptians fought to keep moving, but every wheel pulled sideways or came off entirely. The chariots ground to a halt.

The Egyptians said, "Let us flee from the face of Israel, for the Lord fights for them against the Egyptians."

The Lord said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the sea, that the waters may come back upon the Egyptians, upon their chariots, and upon their horsemen."

Moses stretched out his hand over the sea.

The dawn was coming. The water moved. The walls fell inward and the sea returned to its normal flow, rushing across the ground where the army stood. The water covered the chariots. It covered the horsemen. It covered every soldier of Pharaoh's army that had followed Israel onto the seabed. Not one of them remained.

Israel stood on the far shore. The wind had dropped. The sea lay flat and wide and still in the early light. Along the waterline, the bodies of Egyptian soldiers washed against the sand. The people of Israel looked out across the water at the great work the Lord had done against Egypt. They dropped to their knees on the wet sand.
