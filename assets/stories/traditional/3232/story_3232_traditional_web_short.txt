That day, King Ahasuerus gave Queen Esther the house of Haman, the Jews’ enemy. Esther had disclosed her relationship to Mordecai, and Mordecai came before the king. The ring taken from Haman passed from the king’s hand to Mordecai’s. Esther placed Mordecai over Haman’s house.

But Esther came before the king again. Falling at his feet in tears, she begged him to remove Haman the Agagite’s evil plan against the Jews. Ahasuerus extended the golden scepter, and she rose to stand before him.

If it pleased the king, if she had found favor with him, if the thing seemed right and she was pleasing in his eyes, she asked for letters reversing Haman’s letters. Haman, son of Hammedatha the Agagite, had written to destroy the Jews throughout the king’s provinces.

“How can I endure to see the evil that would come to my people?” she asked. “How can I endure to see the destruction of my relatives?”

Ahasuerus answered Esther and Mordecai together. He had given her Haman’s house, he said, and Haman had been hanged for laying his hand on the Jews. They could write to the Jews as they wished in the king’s name and seal it with his ring. Yet a writing issued under that name and seal could not be reversed.

On Sivan’s twenty-third day, in the third month, the royal scribes were summoned. They wrote all that Mordecai commanded to the Jews and to the local governors, governors, and princes throughout the hundred twenty-seven provinces from India to Ethiopia. Each province received its own writing, each people its own language, and the Jews theirs.

Mordecai issued the letters in Ahasuerus’s name and sealed them with the royal ring. Couriers carried them on royal horses bred from swift steeds.

The letters permitted the Jews in every city to gather and defend their lives. They could destroy, kill, and cause to perish the forces of any people or province assaulting them, their little ones and women, and plunder their possessions. This permission applied throughout the provinces on one day: the thirteenth of Adar, the twelfth month.

Copies were published to all peoples in every province, so the Jews would be ready that day to avenge themselves on their enemies. Urged on by the king’s command, the couriers rode out. The decree was issued in the citadel of Susa.
