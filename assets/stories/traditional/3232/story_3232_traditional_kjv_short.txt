On that day Ahasuerus bestowed upon Esther the house of Haman, the enemy of the Jews. Mordecai appeared before the king, for Esther had made known what he was unto her. Ahasuerus gave Mordecai the ring he had taken from Haman, and Esther appointed Mordecai over Haman’s house.

Once more the queen fell at the king’s feet. With tears she besought him to put away Haman the Agagite’s mischief and his device against the Jews. The golden sceptre was stretched toward her. Esther arose and stood before Ahasuerus.

She sought a written reversal of the letters Haman, son of Hammedatha the Agagite, had devised for the Jews’ destruction in every province. She asked it if the king were pleased, if she had found favour, if the matter seemed right before him, and if she were pleasing in his eyes.

“How can I endure to see the evil that shall come unto my people?” Esther asked. “How can I endure to see the destruction of my kindred?”

The king answered both Esther and Mordecai. He had given her Haman’s house, and Haman had been hanged because he laid his hand upon the Jews. Let them write for the Jews as they pleased, using the king’s name and seal. No man could reverse a writing bearing that name and the seal of his ring.

The scribes were called on the three and twentieth day of Sivan, the third month. At Mordecai’s command they wrote to the Jews, lieutenants, deputies, and provincial rulers, from India unto Ethiopia: an hundred twenty and seven provinces. Each province received its own writing and each people its own language, as did the Jews.

In the king’s name Mordecai wrote and sealed the letters. They went by posts on horseback and riders on mules, camels, and young dromedaries. The king granted the Jews in every city leave to assemble and stand for their lives: to destroy, slay, and cause to perish all the power of any people and province that assaulted them, both little ones and women, and take their spoil for a prey.

One day was appointed throughout Ahasuerus’s provinces, the thirteenth day of Adar, the twelfth month. The writing was published to all people in every province, that the Jews might prepare to avenge themselves upon their enemies that day.

Pressed by the royal command, the posts upon mules and camels went forth. At Shushan the palace, the decree was given.
