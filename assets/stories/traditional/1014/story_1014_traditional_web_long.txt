Morning light spread across the lake called Gennesaret. The water was wide and still, colored soft blue and silver where the early sun touched it. Along the shore, smooth round stones lay in rows where the waves had pushed them. Small ripples moved in and out, making a quiet sound against the rocks. The air smelled like lake water and damp wood. A few birds flew low over the surface, dipping and rising again.

Two wooden boats sat at the water's edge. Their hulls rested on the wet stones, tilted slightly to one side. Ropes hung over the edges, coiled and tangled. The boats were empty. No one sat inside them. Puddles of water gathered in the bottom of each one, catching the light.

Nearby, fishermen knelt on the flat rocks close to the water. Their clothes were damp from a long night on the lake. Their sleeves were rolled up past their elbows. They held wide nets across their laps and pulled at them slowly, picking out bits of seaweed and small sticks caught in the ropes. Their fingers worked through knot after knot. One man shook a section of net, and drops of water scattered across the stones. Another man stretched his net out flat and folded it carefully, laying it in a pile beside him. The morning was quiet. Only the lapping water and the soft rustle of rope broke the stillness.

A crowd of people began walking along the shore toward a man named Jesus. They came from the direction of the nearby town, moving in small groups along the sandy path. Some carried bundles. Some walked quickly. Others moved more slowly, looking ahead to see where Jesus stood. More and more people arrived. The group grew larger and pressed closer together. People stepped around each other, trying to find a place to stand. Some moved to the front. Others stood on their toes to see over the heads in front of them. The crowd pushed nearer and nearer to the water's edge, and the space around Jesus became very small.

Jesus looked at the two boats resting on the shore. He stepped toward one of them. It belonged to a fisherman named Simon. Jesus climbed into the boat, setting one foot on the side and stepping down onto the wooden floor. The boat rocked under his weight. He sat down on the bench near the back. Then he turned to Simon and asked him to push the boat out a little way from the shore.

Simon set down the net he had been washing. He stood up and walked to the boat. He placed both hands on the rough wooden edge and leaned forward. His sandals pressed into the wet stones. He pushed, and the boat slid across the rocks with a low scraping sound. It moved into the shallow water. Small waves bumped against the hull. The boat floated gently, just a short distance from the beach.

Jesus sat in the boat, facing the crowd. All the people stood along the shore, looking out at him. Some sat down on the rocks. A few children stood near the front, close to the water. Jesus began to speak. His voice carried clearly across the open water. The crowd listened. No one moved. The only other sound was the soft lapping of the lake against the side of the boat.

When Jesus finished speaking, the people on the shore stayed still for a moment. Then Jesus turned to Simon. He looked right at him. "Push out into the deep water," Jesus said. "Let down your nets for a catch."

Simon looked at Jesus. His hands rested on the edge of the boat. His clothes were still damp from the night before. "Teacher," Simon said, "we worked hard all through the night and caught nothing." He paused. His fingers curled around the wooden rim. Then he said, "But because you say so, I will let down the nets."

Simon picked up the oars and set them into the water. He pulled back, and the boat began to move away from the shore. The crowd on the beach grew smaller behind them. The shallow green water near the shore gave way to deeper blue. Simon rowed steadily. The oars dipped in and out, leaving small circles on the surface. The boat glided farther and farther from land. The water underneath turned deep and dark blue.

Simon reached for the heavy nets piled in the bottom of the boat. He gathered them in his arms, the wet rope pressing against his chest. He lifted them up and swung them over the side. The nets spread open in the air for just a moment, then dropped down into the lake. They sank below the surface, disappearing into the deep water. The rope trailed over the edge of the boat. Simon held the end and waited.

Then something wonderful happened. The rope in Simon's hands began to pull. It tugged and stretched. The net below the water grew heavy. Simon leaned back, gripping the rope tighter. The net pulled harder. He braced his feet against the floor of the boat. Fish filled the net — so many fish that the ropes began to strain. The woven threads stretched and started to come apart at the seams. Simon's arms shook with the weight.

Simon looked toward the shore. The second boat was still there, near the rocks. He waved his arms high above his head. "Come and help us!" he called out. His voice echoed across the water.

James and John, the sons of Zebedee, were Simon's partners. They stood on the shore and saw him waving. They hurried to the second boat and pushed it into the water. They grabbed the oars and rowed out quickly, pulling hard with each stroke. The boat cut through the small waves, sending sprays of water to each side. They pulled up alongside Simon's boat. The two hulls bumped together gently.

James leaned over and grabbed a section of the net. John reached in from the other side. Together, they began pulling fish up and into both boats. Fish spilled over the edges of the net. They flopped and slid across the wet wooden floors. Silver scales caught the sunlight. More and more fish came up from the water. The men scooped them with both hands. Fish piled up around their feet and ankles. Water splashed everywhere.

Both boats sat lower and lower in the lake. The weight of all those fish pushed the sides of the boats down until the edges nearly touched the surface of the water. Small waves lapped over the rims. The men stopped pulling and looked at the catch all around them. Fish lay in heaps across every part of both boats. There was barely room to stand.

Simon stepped carefully between the piles of fish. His sandals slipped on the wet scales. He fell down on his knees in the boat, right there among all those fish. He looked up at Jesus. Water dripped from his hands and arms. "Go away from me, Lord," Simon said, "for I am a sinful man."

Everyone around him stared at the enormous catch. James stood still in the second boat, his hands dripping with lake water. John stood beside him, his mouth open. No one spoke for a long moment. The boats rocked gently side by side. Water lapped softly between them.

Jesus looked at Simon with a steady, gentle gaze. His voice was calm and clear. "Don't be nervous," Jesus said. "From now on, you will be catching people."

Simon stayed on his knees for a moment. Then he slowly stood up. He looked at Jesus. He looked back at the fish, the nets, the two boats heavy with their catch. James and John looked at each other across the water between the boats.

The men picked up their oars. They turned both boats toward the shore. The rowing was slow because of the weight. The oars dipped deep into the water with each pull. Bit by bit, the shore grew closer. The smooth round stones along the beach came back into view. The shallow green water appeared beneath them again.

The hulls scraped softly against the sand. Water dripped from the nets piled high with fish. The boats settled onto the shore with a low, heavy sound. Simon stepped out first. His feet landed on the wet stones. James and John climbed out of the second boat. Their clothes were soaked. Their arms hung at their sides.

They pulled the boats up onto the beach. The wooden hulls left long marks in the sand. Fish still lay in heaps inside both boats. The nets hung over the edges, heavy and torn. Water pooled around the stones beneath the boats.

Simon, James, and John stood together on the shore. They looked at the boats, the nets, and the fish. Then they turned away from all of it. They left everything right there on the beach — the fish, the nets, the boats, the oars. All of it stayed behind.

They walked away with Jesus, side by side, along the sunlit path that led away from the lake. Their sandals crunched softly on the dry ground. Warm morning light rested on their shoulders. The wide, still lake sparkled quietly behind them.
