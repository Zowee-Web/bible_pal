Morning light touched the edge of the lake called Gennesaret. Small waves lapped against the rocky shore. The water stretched out wide and calm, mirroring the pale blue above. Fishermen stood near two ships drawn up upon the sand. Their nets lay spread across the ground, still dripping. Hands moved slowly, fingers working through the tangles. The savour of lake water and wet rope filled the air. A few seagulls called out overhead, circling in easy loops.

Simon and his partners James and John crouched beside the nets. Their garments were damp from a long night upon the water. They had toiled through all the quiet hours, casting nets again and again. But the nets had come up empty every time. Now they washed the nets and folded them carefully. Their hands moved with steady practice. Water dripped from the woven cords upon the warm sand.

A multitude began to gather along the shore. People walked down the dusty paths toward the lake, more and more of them. They came to hear Jesus, who was walking nigh the water's edge. His sandals pressed into the soft ground. Voices rose as the multitude grew greater. People leaned in close, seeking to hear every word he spake. The company pressed forward, and soon there was but little room left along the shore.

Jesus stepped toward the two ships resting upon the sand. He went up into Simon's ship and sat down. Then he prayed Simon that he would thrust it out a little from the land. Simon set his hands against the wooden side and thrust. The ship slid into the shallow water with a soft scrape. Simon stepped in and steadied the oars. The ship drifted but a short distance from shore.

Jesus sat in the ship and taught the people from thence. His voice carried across the still water. The people stood along the shoreline and gave ear. Some sat down upon the rocks. Children stood near their parents. The morning sun warmed their shoulders. Jesus taught for a good while, and the multitude tarried close, hearing every word that travelled over the lake.

When he had left speaking, Jesus turned unto Simon. He said, "Launch out into the deep, and let down your nets for a draught." Simon beheld him. He answered, "Master, we have toiled all the night, and have taken nothing." He paused. Then he said, "Nevertheless at thy word I will let down the net."

Simon took up the oars and rowed the ship farther out. The water grew deeper beneath them. The colour of the lake changed from pale green unto a rich, dark blue. James and John beheld from the shore. Simon gathered the nets in his arms. He lifted them up and cast them over the side of the ship. The nets spread wide and sank below the surface.

Almost straightway, the nets pulled tight. The ropes drew taut in Simon's hands. Fishes filled the nets so full that the cords began to stretch and strain. Simon gripped the ropes and pulled, but there were so many fishes that the nets began to break. He called out toward the shore, beckoning with his arm.

James and John ran unto the second ship. They thrust it into the water and rowed forth with haste. Their oars splashed with every stroke. When they came unto Simon, they laid hold upon the nets also. Together, the men hauled the fishes up and into both ships. Fishes tumbled and flopped across the wooden planks. Silver scales caught the sunlight. The ships sat lower and lower in the water as more fishes came aboard. Both ships were so full that the water began to come over the sides.

Simon set down the net. He stepped forward and fell upon his knees before Jesus. Water sloshed round about him. Fishes lay piled on every side. He said, "Depart from me, Lord, for I am a sinful man." His voice was rough. His hands rested upon the wet boards of the ship.

James and John stared at the draught all round about them. Their mouths hung open. They beheld one another, then back at the fishes heaped high in both ships. Every one upon the shore could see the ships sitting low, packed full. The multitude murmured and pointed.

Jesus beheld Simon. His voice was steady and kind. He said, "Fear not. From henceforth thou shalt catch men."

The men rowed the heavy ships back toward the shore. The oars dipped slowly under the added weight. Water dripped from the hulls as the ships scraped against the sand. Simon climbed out first. His feet sank into the wet ground. James and John followed, stepping over the piles of fishes.

The three men drew the ships up upon the shore. They set the oars aside. Nets lay in heaps, still full of fishes. The morning sun was higher now, warming the sand and the stones along the water's edge. Seagulls circled above the catch, calling one to another.

Simon stood up straight. He brushed the water from his hands. He looked unto Jesus. James and John came and stood beside him. Then, even there upon the shore, all three of them forsook all. They forsook the ships. They forsook the nets. They forsook the fishes piled high and glistening in the sun.

And they walked with Jesus, side by side, along the bright, sandy path that led from the lake. The sound of the water grew softer behind them. Warm morning air moved through the grass beside the way. Their footsteps fell together upon the dry ground, steady and unhurried, going forth into the wide, open day.
