Morning light touched the edge of the lake called Gennesaret. Small waves lapped against the rocky shore. The water stretched out wide and calm, reflecting the pale blue above. Fishermen stood near two boats pulled up onto the sand. Their nets lay spread across the ground, still dripping. Hands moved slowly, fingers working through the tangles. The smell of lake water and wet rope filled the air. A few gulls called out overhead, circling in easy loops.

Simon and his partners James and John crouched beside the nets. Their clothes were damp from a long night on the water. They had worked all through the quiet hours, casting nets again and again. But the nets had come up empty every time. Now they rinsed the nets and folded them carefully. Their hands moved with steady practice. Water dripped from the woven cords onto the warm sand.

A crowd began to gather along the shore. People walked down the dusty paths toward the lake, more and more of them. They came to hear Jesus, who was walking near the water's edge. His sandals pressed into the soft ground. Voices rose as the crowd grew larger. People leaned in close, trying to hear every word he spoke. The group pressed forward, and soon there was little room left along the shore.

Jesus stepped toward the two boats resting on the sand. He climbed into Simon's boat and sat down. Then he asked Simon to push the boat out a little way from the land. Simon set his hands against the wooden side and pushed. The boat slid into the shallow water with a soft scrape. Simon stepped in and steadied the oars. The boat drifted just a short distance from shore.

Jesus sat in the boat and spoke to the crowd from there. His voice carried across the still water. The people stood along the shoreline and listened. Some sat down on the rocks. Children stood near their parents. The morning sun warmed their shoulders. Jesus taught for a good while, and the crowd stayed close, listening to every word that traveled over the lake.

When he finished speaking, Jesus turned to Simon. He said, "Put out into the deep water and let down your nets for a catch." Simon looked at him. He answered, "Master, we worked hard all night and caught nothing." He paused. Then he said, "But at your word, I will let down the nets."

Simon picked up the oars and rowed the boat farther out. The water grew deeper beneath them. The color of the lake changed from pale green to a rich, dark blue. James and John watched from the shore. Simon gathered the nets in his arms. He lifted them high, then cast them out over the side of the boat. The nets spread wide and sank below the surface.

Almost right away, the nets pulled tight. The ropes went taut in Simon's hands. Fish filled the nets so full that the cords began to stretch and strain. Simon gripped the ropes and pulled, but there were so many fish that the nets started to tear. He called out toward the shore, waving his arm.

James and John ran to the second boat. They pushed it into the water and rowed out quickly. Their oars splashed with each stroke. When they reached Simon, they grabbed hold of the nets too. Together, the men hauled the fish up and into both boats. Fish tumbled and flopped across the wooden planks. Silver scales caught the sunlight. The boats sat lower and lower in the water as more fish came aboard. Both boats were so full that water began to come over the sides.

Simon set down the net. He stepped forward and dropped to his knees in front of Jesus. Water sloshed around him. Fish lay piled on every side. He said, "Go away from me, Lord, for I am a sinful man." His voice was rough. His hands rested on the wet boards of the boat.

James and John stared at the catch all around them. Their mouths hung open. They looked at each other, then back at the fish heaped high in both boats. Everyone on shore could see the boats sitting low, packed full. The crowd murmured and pointed.

Jesus looked at Simon. His voice was steady and kind. He said, "Don't be nervous. From now on, you will be catching people."

The men rowed the heavy boats back toward shore. The oars dipped slowly under the extra weight. Water dripped from the hulls as the boats scraped against the sand. Simon climbed out first. His feet sank into the wet ground. James and John followed, stepping over the piles of fish.

The three men pulled the boats up onto the shore. They set the oars aside. Nets lay in heaps, still full of fish. The morning sun was higher now, warming the sand and the stones along the water's edge. Gulls circled above the catch, calling to one another.

Simon stood up straight. He brushed the water from his hands. He looked at Jesus. James and John came and stood beside him. Then, right there on the shore, all three of them left everything. They left the boats. They left the nets. They left the fish piled high and glistening in the sun.

And they walked with Jesus, side by side, along the bright, sandy path that led away from the lake. The sound of the water grew softer behind them. Warm morning air moved through the grass beside the road. Their footsteps fell together on the dry ground, steady and unhurried, heading forward into the wide, open day.
