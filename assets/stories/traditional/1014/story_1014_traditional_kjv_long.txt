Morning light spread across the lake called Gennesaret. The water was wide and still, coloured soft blue and silver where the early sun touched it. Along the shore, smooth round stones lay in rows where the waves had pushed them. Small ripples moved in and out, making a quiet sound against the rocks. The air bore the savour of lake water and damp wood. A few birds flew low over the surface, dipping and rising again.

Two wooden ships sat at the water's edge. Their hulls rested upon the wet stones, tilted slightly to one side. Ropes hung over the sides, coiled and tangled. The ships were empty. No man sat within them. Pools of water gathered in the bottom of each one, catching the light.

Nigh unto them, fishermen knelt upon the flat rocks close to the water. Their garments were damp from a long night upon the lake. Their sleeves were rolled up past their elbows. They held wide nets across their laps and plucked at them slowly, picking out bits of seaweed and small sticks caught in the ropes. Their fingers worked through knot after knot. One man shook a section of net, and drops of water scattered across the stones. Another man stretched his net out flat and folded it carefully, laying it in a heap beside him. The morning was quiet. Only the lapping water and the soft rustle of rope broke the stillness.

A multitude of people began to walk along the shore unto a man called Jesus. They came from the way of the nigh town, moving in small companies along the sandy path. Some bare bundles. Some walked swiftly. Others moved more slowly, looking ahead to see where Jesus stood. More and more people arrived. The company grew greater and pressed closer together. People stepped round one another, seeking a place to stand. Some went to the front. Others stood upon their toes to see over the heads before them. The multitude pressed nigher and nigher unto the water's edge, and the space around Jesus became very small.

Jesus beheld the two ships resting upon the shore. He stepped toward one of them. It belonged unto a fisherman named Simon. Jesus went up into the ship, setting one foot upon the side and stepping down upon the wooden floor. The ship rocked under his weight. He sat down upon the bench near the back. Then he turned unto Simon and prayed him that he would thrust the ship out a little from the land.

Simon set down the net he had been washing. He stood up and went unto the ship. He laid both hands upon the rough wooden edge and leaned forward. His sandals pressed into the wet stones. He thrust, and the ship slid across the rocks with a low scraping sound. It moved into the shallow water. Small waves bumped against the hull. The ship floated gently, but a short distance from the beach.

Jesus sat in the ship, facing the multitude. All the people stood along the shore, looking out at him. Some sat down upon the rocks. A few children stood near the front, close to the water. Jesus began to speak. His voice carried clearly across the open water. The multitude gave ear. No man moved. The only other sound was the soft lapping of the lake against the side of the ship.

When Jesus had left speaking, the people upon the shore tarried still for a moment. Then Jesus turned unto Simon. He looked right at him. "Launch out into the deep," Jesus said, "and let down your nets for a draught."

Simon beheld Jesus. His hands rested upon the side of the ship. His garments were yet damp from the night before. "Master," Simon said, "we have toiled all the night, and have taken nothing." He paused. His fingers curled around the wooden rim. Then he said, "Nevertheless at thy word I will let down the net."

Simon took up the oars and set them into the water. He pulled back, and the ship began to move away from the shore. The multitude upon the beach grew smaller behind them. The shallow green water near the shore gave way to deeper blue. Simon rowed steadily. The oars dipped in and out, leaving small circles upon the surface. The ship glided farther and farther from land. The water beneath turned deep and dark blue.

Simon reached for the heavy nets piled in the bottom of the ship. He gathered them in his arms, the wet rope pressing against his breast. He lifted them up and swung them over the side. The nets spread open in the air for but a moment, then dropped down into the lake. They sank below the surface, vanishing into the deep water. The rope trailed over the edge of the ship. Simon held the end and waited.

Then a wondrous thing came to pass. The rope in Simon's hands began to pull. It tugged and stretched. The net below the water grew heavy. Simon leaned back, gripping the rope tighter. The net pulled harder. He braced his feet against the floor of the ship. Fishes filled the net — so many fishes that the ropes began to strain. The woven threads stretched and started to break at the seams. The arms of Simon shook with the weight.

Simon looked toward the shore. The second ship was yet there, near the rocks. He waved his arms high above his head. "Come and help us!" he called out. His voice echoed across the water.

James and John, the sons of Zebedee, were Simon's partners. They stood upon the shore and saw him beckoning. They went with haste unto the second ship and thrust it into the water. They took up the oars and rowed forth with haste, pulling hard with every stroke. The ship cut through the small waves, sending sprays of water to each side. They came alongside Simon's ship. The two hulls bumped together gently.

James leaned over and laid hold upon a section of the net. John reached in from the other side. Together, they began drawing fishes up and into both ships. Fishes spilled over the edges of the net. They flopped and slid across the wet wooden floors. Silver scales caught the sunlight. More and more fishes came up from the water. The men gathered them with both hands. Fishes piled up around their feet and ankles. Water splashed everywhere.

Both ships sat lower and lower in the lake. The weight of all those fishes pressed the sides of the ships down until the edges almost touched the surface of the water. Small waves lapped over the rims. The men ceased pulling and beheld the draught all round about them. Fishes lay in heaps across every part of both ships. There was scarce room to stand.

Simon stepped carefully between the piles of fishes. His sandals slipped upon the wet scales. He fell down upon his knees in the ship, there among all those fishes. He looked up unto Jesus. Water dripped from his hands and arms. "Depart from me, Lord," Simon said, "for I am a sinful man."

Every one round about him beheld the great draught. James stood still in the second ship, his hands dripping with lake water. John stood beside him, his mouth open. No man spake for a long moment. The ships rocked gently side by side. Water lapped softly between them.

Jesus beheld Simon with a steady, gentle gaze. His voice was calm and clear. "Fear not," Jesus said. "From henceforth thou shalt catch men."

Simon tarried upon his knees for a moment. Then he slowly arose. He looked unto Jesus. He looked back upon the fishes, the nets, the two ships heavy with their draught. James and John looked one upon another across the water between the ships.

The men took up their oars. They turned both ships toward the shore. The rowing was slow because of the weight. The oars dipped deep into the water with every pull. Little by little, the shore drew closer. The smooth round stones along the beach came back into view. The shallow green water appeared beneath them again.

The hulls scraped softly against the sand. Water dripped from the nets piled high with fishes. The ships settled upon the shore with a low, heavy sound. Simon stepped out first. His feet landed upon the wet stones. James and John climbed out of the second ship. Their garments were soaked. Their arms hung at their sides.

They drew the ships up upon the beach. The wooden hulls left long marks in the sand. Fishes yet lay in heaps within both ships. The nets hung over the edges, heavy and torn. Water pooled around the stones beneath the ships.

Simon, James, and John stood together upon the shore. They beheld the ships, the nets, and the fishes. Then they turned away from all of it. They forsook all there upon the beach — the fishes, the nets, the ships, the oars. All of it was left behind.

They walked away with Jesus, side by side, along the sunlit path that led from the lake. Their sandals crunched softly upon the dry ground. Warm morning light rested upon their shoulders. The wide, still lake glistened quietly behind them.
