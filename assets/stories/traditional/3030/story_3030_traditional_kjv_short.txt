At the ending, the Preacher took up once more the saying that had sounded throughout his account: vanity of vanities; all is vanity. Yet the last testimony ceased not with that refrain. Because the Preacher was wise, he still instructed the people in knowledge. He gave heed, searched diligently, and ordered many proverbs. His labour among words was careful. He sought speech that was acceptable, and writing that was upright and true.

The closing witness then declared the strength of wise sayings, and it set forth two figures. The words of the wise are as goads. A goad moveth that which would otherwise stand still, and wise sayings work upon the hearer after that manner. Gathered words abide as nails fastened well. A nail driven fast slippeth not from its place, and that which it holdeth remaineth.

These sayings came by many voices. The masters of the assemblies gathered them and delivered them unto such as would hear. Yet the giving was not traced unto the many who spake. It is given from one shepherd.

Then was the son warned concerning the abundance of writings, and by these very words was he admonished. The making of books cometh to no stopping place. More may ever be added, and the adding endeth not. Much study is a weariness of the flesh, and he that studieth long beareth that weariness in his body. The conclusion required not another book laid upon the heap. The whole matter had been heard.

The charge was plain: fear God, and keep his commandments, for herein standeth the duty of man. The Preacher's final word bound reverence unto obedience. Whatsoever is hidden from mortal sight shall not therefore vanish. God shall bring every work unto judgment, and every secret thing therewith. The Preacher named both: whether it be good, or whether it be evil.
