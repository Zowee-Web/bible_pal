At the close, the Preacher returned to the word that had sounded through his account: vanity of vanities; all is vanity. Yet his final record did not stop at that refrain. Because the Preacher was wise, he continued teaching the people knowledge. He weighed matters, searched them out, and arranged many proverbs. His labor with words was deliberate. He looked for words that would be acceptable, and what he wrote was upright and true.

The epilogue then described the force of wise words, and it used two images to do it. The words of the wise press like goads. A goad moves what would otherwise stand still, and wise words work on the hearer in that way. Collected sayings hold like nails driven well. A nail set fast does not slip, and what it fastens stays in place.

The sayings came through many voices. Masters of the assemblies gathered them and delivered them to those who listened. Yet the giving was not traced to the many who spoke. It came from one shepherd.

Then came a warning to the son who received them, and he was admonished by these very words. The making of books reaches no stopping point. More can always be added, and the adding does not come to an end. Much study is a weariness of the flesh, and the one who studies long carries that weariness in the body. The answer was not another volume added to the pile. The account had reached its conclusion. Everything had been heard.

The final charge was plain: fear God and keep his commandments, for this belongs to the whole duty of humanity. The Preacher's last word joined humble reverence to faithful obedience. What human eyes overlook does not disappear. God will bring every work into judgment, and every hidden thing with it. The Preacher named both: whether the work was good, or whether it was evil.
