Habakkuk stood upon his watch and set himself upon the tower. He watched to see what the Lord would say unto him and what he should answer when reproved. The Lord commanded him to write the vision plainly upon tables, that he might run that read it. The vision was for an appointed time. Though it tarried, Habakkuk was to wait for it, because it would surely come. The lifted-up soul was not upright, but the just would live by his faith.

Then the vision took up a taunting proverb against the proud man whose desire could not be satisfied. They that should bite him would rise suddenly, they that should vex him would awake, and he would be for booties unto them. Because he had spoiled many nations, the remnant of the people would spoil him for men’s blood and for violence against the land, the city, and all that dwelt therein. He coveted an evil covetousness to his house and consulted shame to it by cutting off many people. The stone would cry out of the wall, and the beam out of the timber would answer it.

The woe against him that built a town with blood and established a city by iniquity declared that the people should labour in the very fire and the people should weary themselves for very vanity. Yet the earth would be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the Lord, as the waters cover the sea.

Another woe came upon him that gave his neighbour drink and made him drunken to look upon his nakedness. He was filled with shame instead of glory, and the cup of the Lord’s right hand would be turned unto him. The violence of Lebanon, the spoil of beasts, men’s blood, and violence against land and city would cover him.

At last the vision declared the emptiness of idols. The graven image and the molten image were teachers of lies. Wood could not awake, nor could a dumb stone arise and teach. Though overlaid with gold and silver, there was no breath within it. The Lord was in his holy temple. The vision ended with a command unto all the earth: keep silence before him.
