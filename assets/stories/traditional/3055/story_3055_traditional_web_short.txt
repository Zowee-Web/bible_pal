Habakkuk took his place at the watch and set himself on the ramparts. He looked out to see what Yahweh would say to him and what he would answer concerning his complaint. Yahweh answered him and commanded him to write the vision plainly on tablets, that he who runs may read it. The vision was for the appointed time and hurried toward the end. Though it took time, Habakkuk was to wait, because it would surely come. The proud soul was not upright, but the righteous would live by faith.

The vision then raised a taunting proverb against the arrogant man whose desire could not be satisfied. His debtors would rise suddenly, those who made him tremble would wake, and he would become their victim. Because he had plundered many nations, the remnant of the peoples would plunder him for human blood and for violence done to land, city, and inhabitants. The one who got evil gain for his house devised shame for it by cutting off many peoples. The stone in the wall would cry out, and the beam in the woodwork would answer it.

The woe against one who built a town with blood and established a city by iniquity declared that the peoples labored for fire and the nations wearied themselves for vanity. Yet the earth would be filled with the knowledge of Yahweh’s glory as the waters cover the sea.

Another woe addressed the one who gave his neighbor drink so that he might gaze on nakedness. Shame, not glory, would cover him, and the cup of Yahweh’s right hand would come around to him. The violence done to Lebanon, the destruction of animals, the bloodshed, and the violence against land and cities would overwhelm him.

The vision finally exposed the emptiness of idols. An engraved or molten image was a teacher of lies. Wood could not awaken, and a mute stone overlaid with gold and silver could not arise or teach; there was no breath within it. Yahweh remained in his holy temple. The vision ended with a command to all the earth: be silent before him.
