Word came unto Jehoshaphat. The children of Moab, and the children of Ammon, and others with them beside the Ammonites, had gathered themselves together, and were come against him to battle.

The messengers came in unto the palace breathing hard, the dust upon their feet, their robes streaked with the marks of the road. A long way had they run. They told the king plainly: a great multitude was already moving toward him from beyond the sea, from the country of Syria. They were at Hazazon-tamar, which is En-gedi — the high rocky place east of Judah, where the cliffs fall away to the salt water beneath. Already had they crossed into the land. Already were they near.

And Jehoshaphat feared.

He sent not for generals. He counted not his chariots. He called not the captains of his hosts to muster the men of Judah. He set himself to seek the LORD. And he proclaimed a fast throughout all Judah. And the word went forth — riders through the hill country, criers in the gates of every city, the tidings passing from town to town and from village to village. Out of all Judah they came. Men left their fields, the grain yet standing. Women came bearing their infants upon their hips. Whole households walked the roads unto Jerusalem, the dust rising behind them, the sun climbing as they came.

And they gathered themselves together to ask help of the LORD.

The house of the LORD was filled. The new court was thronged with people standing shoulder to shoulder, and back unto chest, till there was no place to set the foot. The air was warm and close with the press of bodies. The smell of road dust and of wool hung in the court. Children held the hands of their mothers and pressed their faces into the folds of their mothers' robes. Old men leaned upon their staves and breathed slowly through dry mouths. None had eaten. None spake above a murmur. From the hinder part of the multitude could be heard nothing but the soft shuffle of feet upon stone, and now and again the cry of an infant quickly hushed.

Then stood Jehoshaphat before them all.

He stood in the congregation of Judah and Jerusalem, in the house of the LORD, before the new court, and he lifted up his voice that every soul in that multitude might hear.

"O LORD God of our fathers, art thou not God in heaven? Dost thou not rule over all the kingdoms of the nations? In thine hand is power and might, and none is able to stand against thee."

His voice rang against the stones. They that stood far off leaned forward to catch the words.

"Art thou not our God, who didst drive out the dwellers of this land before thy people Israel, and gavest it unto the seed of Abraham thy friend for ever? They have dwelt therein. They have builded thee a sanctuary therein for thy name, saying: If evil come upon us — the sword, judgment, pestilence, or famine — we will stand before this house, and before thee, for thy name is in this house, and we will cry unto thee in our affliction, and thou wilt hear and help."

The multitude was still. Not a child made a sound. A woman near the front laid her hand upon her mouth.

"And now — behold. The children of Ammon and Moab and mount Seir, whom thou wouldest not suffer Israel to invade when they came up out of the land of Egypt — Israel turned aside from them, and destroyed them not. And see how they reward us. They come to cast us out of thy possession, which thou hast given us to inherit."

The hands of Jehoshaphat were spread open before him, his palms upward, his fingers parted.

"O our God, wilt thou not judge them? For we have no might against this great company that cometh against us. Neither know we what to do."

He paused. The court was still. The dust hung in the shafts of light that fell from the high openings, slow and visible in the air above the people.

"But our eyes are upon thee."

All Judah stood before the LORD. Their little ones were with them. Their wives. Their children. Whole households standing together in the court, faces lifted toward the front, waiting. The fasting had left them hollow of cheek and dry of lip. Lips were cracked. Eyes wide. The enemy was near — so near that some among them had heard the tidings only that morning. None stirred.

Then came the Spirit of the LORD upon Jahaziel.

Jahaziel, the son of Zechariah, the son of Benaiah, the son of Jeiel, the son of Mattaniah — a Levite of the sons of Asaph — stood in the midst of the congregation. He had not been at the front. Among the people he was standing. His voice rose sudden and clear, cutting through the silence as a trumpet blast.

"Hearken — all Judah, and ye inhabitants of Jerusalem, and thou, King Jehoshaphat."

Every head turned toward him. The press about him shifted, and a small space opened.

"Thus saith the LORD unto you: Be not afraid. Be not dismayed by reason of this great multitude. For the battle is not yours, but God's."

The words fell upon the multitude. They took hold one of another by the arm. A man at the side lifted his hand unto his face. Mothers drew their little ones closer into their robes.

"To morrow go ye down against them. Behold, they come up by the cliff of Ziz. Ye shall find them at the end of the brook, before the wilderness of Jeruel."

Jahaziel's voice wavered not. His eye departed not from the king.

"Ye shall not need to fight in this battle. Set yourselves. Stand ye still. And see the salvation of the LORD with you, O Judah and Jerusalem. Fear not. Be not dismayed. To morrow go out against them, for the LORD will be with you."

And Jehoshaphat bowed himself. He cast his head down toward the ground, his face pressed unto the cool stone of the floor. His crown slid forward. His robes folded about him. And behind him, all Judah — all Jerusalem — fell down before the LORD. The sound thereof was as the wind that moveth through standing corn, hundreds going down together, robes folding against stone, foreheads touching the pavement, sandals scraping as they knelt. They worshipped the LORD there upon the ground, with their faces toward the earth, the whole court filled with people upon their knees. The small hand of a child lay flat beside the hand of her father. The staff of an old man lay upon the stone beside him. None lifted up his head.

Then rose the Levites. The children of the Kohathites stood up. The children of the Korhites stood up with them. They opened their mouths, and praised the LORD God of Israel with a loud voice on high, their singing rising above the bowed multitude, filling the court, and passing beyond the walls into the open air above the city.
