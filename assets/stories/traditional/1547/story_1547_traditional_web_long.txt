Word came to Jehoshaphat. The children of Moab, the children of Ammon, and others beside the Ammonites had joined together and were coming against him to battle.

The messengers arrived at the palace, breathing hard, dust on their feet and their robes streaked from the road. They had run a long way. They told the king plainly: a great multitude was already moving toward him from beyond the sea, from the direction of Syria. They were at Hazazon-tamar — the place called En-gedi — the rocky plateau east of Judah where the cliffs dropped to the salt water below. Already they had crossed into the land. Already they were close.

Jehoshaphat was afraid.

He did not send for generals. He did not count chariots. He did not call for the captains of his armies to muster the men of Judah. He set himself to seek the LORD. He proclaimed a fast throughout all of Judah. And the word went out — riders moving through the hill country, criers standing in the gates of every city, the message passed from town to town and village to village. From all of Judah they came. Men left their fields with the grain still standing. Women came carrying infants on their hips. Whole families walked the roads toward Jerusalem, the dust rising behind them, the sun climbing as they came.

They gathered to ask help of the LORD.

The house of the LORD filled. The new court was packed with people standing shoulder to shoulder, then back to chest, until there was no more room to step. The air was warm and close with the press of bodies. The smell of road dust and wool hung in the court. Children held their mothers' hands and pressed their faces against their mothers' robes. Old men leaned on staffs and breathed slowly through dry mouths. No one had eaten. No one spoke above a murmur. From the back of the crowd you could hear nothing but the soft shuffle of feet on stone and the occasional cry of an infant quickly hushed.

Then Jehoshaphat stood before them all.

He stood in the congregation of Judah and Jerusalem, in the house of the LORD, before the new court, and he lifted his voice so that every person in that crowd could hear.

"O LORD God of our fathers, are you not God in heaven? Do you not rule over all the kingdoms of the nations? In your hand is power and might, so that none is able to withstand you."

His voice echoed off the stone walls. The crowd at the back leaned forward to catch the words.

"Are you not our God, who drove out the inhabitants of this land before your people Israel and gave it to the descendants of Abraham your friend forever? They have lived in it. They have built you a sanctuary in it for your name, saying: If evil comes upon us — the sword, judgment, pestilence, or famine — we will stand before this house and before you, for your name is in this house, and we will cry out to you in our affliction, and you will hear and help."

The crowd was silent. Not a child made a sound. A woman near the front held her hand over her mouth.

"And now — look. The children of Ammon and Moab and Mount Seir, whom you would not let Israel invade when they came out of the land of Egypt — Israel turned away from them and did not destroy them. And see how they repay us. They come to cast us out of your possession, which you gave us to inherit."

Jehoshaphat's hands were open before him, palms upward, fingers spread.

"O our God, will you not judge them? For we have no might against this great company that comes against us. We do not know what to do."

He paused. The court was still. Dust hung in the shafts of light that fell through the high openings, slow and visible in the air above the people.

"But our eyes are on you."

All Judah stood before the LORD. Their little ones were with them. Their wives. Their children. Whole families standing together in the courtyard, faces turned toward the front, waiting. The fasting had left them hollow-cheeked and dry-lipped. Lips cracked. Eyes wide. The enemy was close — close enough that some of them had heard the news only that morning. No one moved.

Then the Spirit of the LORD came upon Jahaziel.

Jahaziel the son of Zechariah, the son of Benaiah, the son of Jeiel, the son of Mattaniah — a Levite of the sons of Asaph — stood in the middle of the congregation. He had not been at the front. He was standing among the people. His voice rose sudden and clear, cutting through the silence like a horn blast.

"Listen — all Judah, and you inhabitants of Jerusalem, and you, King Jehoshaphat."

Every head turned toward him. The crowd around him shifted, opening a small space.

"This is what the LORD says to you: Do not be afraid. Do not be dismayed because of this great multitude. For the battle is not yours, but God's."

The words fell across the crowd. People gripped each other's arms. A man near the side put his hand to his face. Mothers pulled their children closer against their robes.

"Tomorrow go down against them. They come up by the cliff of Ziz. You will find them at the end of the brook, before the wilderness of Jeruel."

Jahaziel's voice did not waver. His eyes did not move from the king.

"You will not need to fight in this battle. Set yourselves. Stand still. And see the salvation of the LORD with you, O Judah and Jerusalem. Do not fear. Do not be dismayed. Tomorrow go out against them, for the LORD will be with you."

Jehoshaphat bowed. He dropped his head toward the ground, his face pressing against the cool stone floor. His crown slid forward. His robes folded around him. Behind him, all Judah — all of Jerusalem — fell before the LORD. The sound of it was like wind moving through grain, hundreds of bodies going down at once, robes folding against stone, foreheads touching the pavement, sandals scraping as people knelt. They worshipped the LORD there on the ground, faces down, the entire court filled with people on their knees. A child's small hand pressed flat beside her father's. An old man's staff lay on the stone beside him. No one lifted a head.

Then the Levites rose. The children of the Kohathites stood. The children of the Korhites stood beside them. They opened their mouths and praised the LORD God of Israel with a loud voice on high, their singing rising over the bowed crowd, filling the court, carrying past the walls into the open air above the city.
