Word came unto Jehoshaphat. The children of Moab, the children of Ammon, and certain others beside the Ammonites had gathered themselves together, and were come against him to make war.

And messengers arrived at the palace, their breath heavy and the dust of the road upon their feet. They spake plainly unto the king: a great multitude was already moving toward him from beyond the sea, from the way of Syria. They were come as far as Hazazon-tamar, which is En-gedi — that rocky height east of Judah where the cliffs fall away unto the salt waters below.

And Jehoshaphat was afraid.

He sent not for his captains. He numbered not his chariots. He set his face to seek the LORD. And he proclaimed a fast throughout all Judah, and the word went forth unto every city, every town, every village in the hill country and in the lowlands. And from all Judah they came — men departing their fields, women bearing infants at the breast, whole families walking the roads toward Jerusalem.

They gathered themselves together to seek help of the LORD.

And the house of the LORD was filled. The new court was thronged with people standing shoulder unto shoulder. The air was warm and close with the press of bodies. Children held fast to their mothers' hands. Old men leaned upon their staffs. None had eaten. None spake above a murmur.

Then Jehoshaphat stood before them all.

He stood in the congregation of Judah and Jerusalem, in the house of the LORD, before the new court, and he lifted up his voice that every soul in that multitude might hear.

"O LORD God of our fathers, art thou not God in the heavens? Dost thou not rule over all the kingdoms of the nations? In thine hand is power and might, and none may stand against thee."

His voice returned from the stone walls.

"Art thou not our God, who didst drive out the inhabitants of this land from before thy people Israel, and gavest it unto the seed of Abraham thy friend for ever? And they have dwelt therein, and have built thee a sanctuary for thy name, saying: If evil fall upon us — whether the sword, or judgment, or pestilence, or famine — we shall stand before this house and before thee, for thy name abideth in this house, and we shall cry unto thee in our affliction, and thou wilt hear, and thou wilt deliver."

The multitude held their peace. Not a child made a sound.

"And now — behold. The children of Ammon and Moab and Mount Seir, whom thou wouldest not suffer Israel to touch when they came forth out of Egypt — Israel turned aside from them and destroyed them not. And see how they do requite us. They are come to thrust us out from thy possession, which thou hast given us to inherit."

The hands of Jehoshaphat were open before him, the palms turned upward.

"O our God, wilt thou not execute judgment upon them? For we have no strength against this great host that cometh against us. Neither know we what to do."

He paused. The court was still. Dust hung in the light that fell through the high openings.

"But our eyes are upon thee."

And all Judah stood before the LORD. Their little ones were with them, and their wives, and their children. Whole families standing together in the court, their faces turned toward the front, waiting. The fast had left them hollow of cheek and dry of lip. The enemy was near. None stirred.

Then came the Spirit of the LORD upon Jahaziel.

Jahaziel the son of Zechariah, the son of Benaiah, the son of Jeiel, the son of Mattaniah — a Levite of the sons of Asaph — stood in the midst of the congregation. And his voice rose sudden and clear, cutting through the silence as the blast of a horn.

"Hearken — all Judah, and ye inhabitants of Jerusalem, and thou, King Jehoshaphat."

Every head turned unto him.

"Thus saith the LORD unto you: Fear not, neither be dismayed by reason of this great multitude. For the battle is not yours. It is God's."

And the words fell upon the assembly. Men took hold of one another's arms. Mothers drew their children near.

"Tomorrow go ye down against them. They ascend by the pass of Ziz. Ye shall find them at the end of the valley, before the wilderness of Jeruel."

The voice of Jahaziel did not waver.

"Ye shall have no need to fight this battle. Take your positions. Stand ye still. And behold the deliverance of the LORD, who is with you, O Judah and Jerusalem. Fear not. Be not dismayed. Go forth against them on the morrow, for the LORD goeth with you."

And Jehoshaphat bowed himself. He brought his head low toward the ground, his face pressed against the cool stone of the floor. And behind him, all Judah — all Jerusalem — fell down before the LORD. The sound of it was as wind passing through standing grain, hundreds of bodies going down together, robes folding against stone, foreheads touching the pavement. And they worshipped the LORD there upon the ground, faces downward, the whole of the court filled with people upon their knees.

Then the Levites arose. The children of the Kohathites stood. The children of the Korhites stood beside them. And they opened their mouths and praised the LORD God of Israel with a great voice on high, and their singing rose above the bowed multitude, filling the court, carrying beyond the walls into the open air above the city.
