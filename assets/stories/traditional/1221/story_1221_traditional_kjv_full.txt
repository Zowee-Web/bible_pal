Thirty thousand men gathered unto Baale of Judah. They came on foot from every tribe, the chosen men of Israel, dust upon their sandals and bright cloth upon their shoulders. They had come for the ark. The ark of God had sat for years in the house of Abinadab upon the hill, quiet under its covering, and David had said, this day.

They brought it forth. The ark, which is called by the Name, even the name of the Lord of hosts that dwelleth between the cherubims. They set it upon a new cart. Uzzah and Ahio walked beside the oxen, Ahio going before, and the long road to Jerusalem opened before them.

David went with the people. And the people made music.

There were harps. There were psalteries and timbrels. There were cornets and the bright clash of cymbals. The whole house of Israel played before the Lord on all manner of instruments made of fir wood, and the sound of them rose along the road and out across the fields. Children ran ahead. Women clapped. The oxen plodded. The cart wheels turned in the dust. It was a procession that felt as a feast, and the air over the column shimmered with the noise of joy.

They came unto Nachon's threshingfloor.

The oxen shook it. The cart pitched. The ark moved.

Uzzah put forth his hand. He took hold of it.

The music went on for one heartbeat longer than the man. Then the line behind him saw, and the line behind that saw, and the harps fell silent in waves down the road. Uzzah lay where he had stood. The anger of the Lord had been kindled against him, and God had smitten him there for his error, and he had died by the ark of God.

David was displeased. The breach in the procession was a breach in him. He called the name of that place Perezuzzah, and the name held — the breaking-out — and it hath held unto this day.

David was afraid of the Lord that day. He stood upon the road before thirty thousand men and felt the thing he had not felt until that moment. The ark was holy. The ark was not a thing he could carry upon a cart with timbrels. He said, "How shall the ark of the Lord come unto me?"

He would not remove it further. He carried the cart aside into the house of Obededom the Gittite, and he left it there, and he went up to Jerusalem without it.

Three months passed.

In those three months the ark abode in the house of Obededom, and the house was blessed. The fields were blessed. The flocks were blessed. The household, all of it, prospered under the presence that had slain Uzzah. Word came unto David. They came and told him, saying, "The Lord hath blessed the house of Obededom, and all that pertaineth unto him, because of the ark of God."

David heard it. And he went back.

This time they that bare the ark carried it. When they had gone six paces, he sacrificed an ox and a fatling. The blood ran upon the road. Then they walked, and after a while he made them stay, and he sacrificed again. The procession crawled. The procession was reverent. And then, slowly, the gladness came back into it.

David put off his royal robes. He girded himself with a linen ephod, the plain garment of a priest, and he stepped forth from the front of the column, and he danced.

He danced before the Lord with all his might. He leaped. He turned about. The trumpet sounded and the people shouted and the whole house of Israel brought up the ark with shouting and with the sound of the trumpet. The road into the city of David was loud with it.

As the ark came in through the gate, Michal the daughter of Saul looked through a window. She saw the king leaping. She saw the king dancing before the Lord. And she despised him in her heart.

The procession did not see her. They brought in the ark and set it in his place, in the midst of the tabernacle that David had pitched for it, and David offered burnt offerings and peace offerings before the Lord.

When he had made an end, he blessed the people in the name of the Lord of hosts. And he dealt among all the people, even among the whole multitude of Israel, as well to the women as men, to every one a cake of bread, and a good piece of flesh, and a flagon of wine. They went home with food in their hands. They went home full.

So all the people departed, every one to his house. And in an upper window in the city, Michal stood watching them go.
