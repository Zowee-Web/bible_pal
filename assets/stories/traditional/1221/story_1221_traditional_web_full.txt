Thirty thousand men gathered to Baale Judah. They came on foot from every tribe, the chosen men of Israel, dust on their sandals and bright cloth on their shoulders. They had come for the ark. The ark of God had sat for years in the house of Abinadab on the hill, quiet under its covering, and David had said, today.

They brought it out. The ark, called by the Name, the name of the Lord of Armies who sits above the cherubim. They set it on a new cart. Uzzah and Ahio walked beside the oxen, Ahio going before, and the long road to Jerusalem opened ahead of them.

David walked with the people. And the people made music.

There were harps. There were stringed instruments and tambourines. There were castanets and the bright clash of cymbals. The whole house of Israel played before the Lord with all kinds of instruments made of cypress wood, and the sound of them rose along the road and out across the fields. Children ran ahead. Women clapped. The oxen plodded. The cart wheels turned in the dust. It was a procession that felt like a festival, and the air over the column shimmered with the noise of joy.

They came to the threshing floor of Nacon.

The oxen stumbled. The cart pitched. The ark moved.

Uzzah put out his hand. He took hold of it.

The music kept going for one heartbeat longer than the man. Then the line behind him saw, and the line behind that saw, and the harps fell silent in waves down the road. Uzzah lay where he had stood. The Lord's anger had burned against him, and God had struck him there for his error, and he had died by the ark of God.

David was displeased. The breach in the procession was a breach in him. He called that place Perez Uzzah, and the name held — the breaking-out — and it has held to this day.

David was afraid of the Lord that day. He stood on the road in front of thirty thousand men and felt the thing he had not felt until that moment. The ark was holy. The ark was not a thing he could carry on a cart with castanets. He said, "How could the Lord's ark come to me?"

He would not move it further. He turned the cart aside into the house of Obed-Edom the Gittite, and he left it there, and he went up to Jerusalem without it.

Three months passed.

In those three months the ark sat in Obed-Edom's house, and the house was blessed. The fields were blessed. The flocks were blessed. The household, all of it, prospered under the presence that had killed Uzzah. Word reached David. They came and told him, "The Lord has blessed the house of Obed-Edom, and all that belongs to him, because of God's ark."

David heard it. And he went back.

This time the bearers carried the ark. When they had gone six paces, he sacrificed an ox and a fattened calf. The blood ran on the road. Then they walked, and after a while he made them stop, and he sacrificed again. The procession crawled. The procession was reverent. And then, slowly, the joy came back into it.

David put off his royal robes. He girded himself with a linen ephod, the plain garment of a priest, and he stepped out from the front of the column, and he danced.

He danced before the Lord with all his might. He leaped. He spun. The trumpet sounded and the people shouted and the whole house of Israel brought up the ark with shouting and with the sound of the trumpet. The road into the city of David was loud with it.

As the ark came in through the gate, Michal the daughter of Saul looked out through the window. She saw the king leaping. She saw the king dancing before the Lord. And she despised him in her heart.

The procession did not see her. They brought in the ark and set it in its place, in the middle of the tent that David had pitched for it, and David offered burnt offerings and peace offerings before the Lord.

When he had finished, he blessed the people in the name of the Lord of Armies. And he gave to all the people, to men and to women alike, to every one of the whole multitude of Israel, a cake of bread and a portion of meat and a cake of raisins. They went home with food in their hands. They went home full.

So all the people departed, each to his own house. And in an upper window in the city, Michal stood watching them go.
