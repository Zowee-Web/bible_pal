The banquet hall of Belshazzar was loud at the high hour of the night. A thousand lords reclined along the tables. Music carried from the corners of the hall, the horn and the lyre and the harp, and the wine went around among them without pause. The king sat at the head of his lords. He lifted his cup. He drank wine before them all.

While he tasted the wine, a thought came into him. He gave the command. Servants ran from the hall and down into the treasury beneath the palace. They returned carrying the gold and silver vessels of the house of God, which Nebuchadnezzar his father had taken out of the temple in Jerusalem. The vessels were set upon the king's table. The lamplight caught them. The cups of the sanctuary stood among the cups of the feast.

The king lifted one of the holy vessels. His lords lifted theirs. They drank wine from them. And as they drank they praised the gods of gold and of silver, of bronze, of iron, of wood, and of stone.

In the same hour the fingers of a man's hand came forth. They came out beside the lampstand, against the plaster of the wall of the king's palace. The hand wrote. The king saw the part of the hand that wrote.

The cup stopped at his lip. He could not look away. Across the wall the fingers moved, deliberate, forming letters in the light. His face was changed in him. The flush of the wine drained away. His thoughts troubled him. The joints of his hips were loosed. His knees struck one against another beneath his robe.

He cried aloud. His voice cracked across the hall and the music broke off. "Bring in the wise men. Bring in the astrologers and the soothsayers." Servants ran. The lords sat with their cups still in their hands and their eyes on the wall.

The wise men of Babylon came in. The king stood from his seat. "Whoever shall read this writing, and show me its interpretation, shall be clothed with purple, and have a chain of gold around his neck. He shall be the third ruler in the kingdom."

The wise men gathered before the wall. They studied the letters. They turned to one another. They turned back. None of them could read it. None of them could make the interpretation known to the king. The king was greatly troubled, and his face was changed in him, and his lords were perplexed.

Then the queen came into the banquet house. She had heard the cry from her own rooms, and she crossed the hall and stood before the king. Her voice was steady. "O king, live forever. Do not let your thoughts trouble you. There is a man in your kingdom in whom is the spirit of the holy gods. In the days of your father, light and understanding and wisdom were found in him. Let Daniel be called. He will show the interpretation."

They sent for him. He came up through the hall, an old man among the bright tables, brought through the wine-stained lords until he stood before the young king. The king looked at him and spoke. "If you can read the writing and make known to me its interpretation, you shall be clothed with purple."

Daniel did not move toward the gifts. He answered before the king. "Let your gifts be to yourself, and give your rewards to another. Yet I will read the writing to the king, and make known to him the interpretation."

He looked at the cups upon the table. He looked at the king. His voice did not rise. "The Most High God gave your father the kingdom. But you have not humbled your heart. You have lifted yourself up against the Lord of heaven. They have brought the vessels of his house before you, and you and your lords have drunk wine from them. You have praised the gods of silver and gold which do not see, nor hear, nor know. The God in whose hand your breath is, you have not glorified. Therefore this writing was sent from before him."

Daniel turned to the wall. The lamplight fell across the letters. He read them aloud, one by one, into the hush of the hall. "Mene. Mene. Tekel. Upharsin."

He turned back to the king. "This is the interpretation. Mene: God has numbered your kingdom, and brought it to an end. Tekel: you are weighed in the balances, and are found wanting. Peres: your kingdom is divided, and given to the Medes and Persians."

The king made the proclamation. They clothed Daniel with purple. They put a chain of gold around his neck.

In that night the kingdom passed from the hand of Belshazzar, and Darius the Mede received it before the morning.
