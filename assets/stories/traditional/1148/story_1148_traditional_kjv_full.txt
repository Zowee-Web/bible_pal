The banquet hall of Belshazzar was loud at the high hour of the night. A thousand lords reclined along the tables. Music carried from the corners of the hall, the horn and the lyre and the harp, and the wine went round about among them without pause. The king sat at the head of his lords. He lifted his cup. He drank wine before them all.

While he tasted the wine, a thought came into him. He gave the command. Servants ran from the hall and down into the treasury beneath the palace. They returned bearing the golden and silver vessels of the house of God, which Nebuchadnezzar his father had taken out of the temple in Jerusalem. The vessels were set upon the king's table. The lamplight caught them. The cups of the sanctuary stood among the cups of the feast.

The king lifted one of the holy vessels. His lords lifted theirs. They drank wine therein. And as they drank they praised the gods of gold, and of silver, of brass, of iron, of wood, and of stone.

In the same hour came forth fingers of a man's hand. They came out beside the candlestick, against the plaister of the wall of the king's palace. The hand wrote. The king saw the part of the hand that wrote.

The cup stopped at his lip. He could not look away. Across the wall the fingers moved, deliberate, forming letters in the light. His countenance was changed in him. The flush of the wine drained away. His thoughts troubled him. The joints of his loins were loosed. His knees smote one against another beneath his robe.

He cried aloud. His voice cracked across the hall and the music brake off. "Bring in the wise men. Bring in the astrologers and the soothsayers." Servants ran. The lords sat with their cups yet in their hands and their eyes upon the wall.

The wise men of Babylon came in. The king stood up from his seat. "Whosoever shall read this writing, and shew me the interpretation thereof, shall be clothed with scarlet, and have a chain of gold about his neck. He shall be the third ruler in the kingdom."

The wise men gathered before the wall. They studied the letters. They turned one to another. They turned back again. None of them could read it. None of them could make known the interpretation unto the king. Then was the king greatly troubled, and his countenance was changed in him, and his lords were astonied.

Then came the queen into the banquet house. She had heard the cry from her own chambers, and she crossed the hall and stood before the king. Her voice was steady. "O king, live for ever. Let not thy thoughts trouble thee. There is a man in thy kingdom in whom is the spirit of the holy gods. In the days of thy father, light and understanding and wisdom were found in him. Let Daniel be called. He will shew the interpretation."

They sent for him. He came up through the hall, an old man among the bright tables, brought through the wine-stained lords until he stood before the young king. The king looked upon him and spake. "If thou canst read the writing, and make known unto me the interpretation thereof, thou shalt be clothed with scarlet."

Daniel moved not toward the gifts. He answered before the king. "Let thy gifts be to thyself, and give thy rewards to another. Yet I will read the writing unto the king, and make known unto him the interpretation."

He looked upon the cups upon the table. He looked upon the king. His voice rose not. "The most high God gave thy father the kingdom. But thou hast not humbled thine heart. Thou hast lifted up thyself against the Lord of heaven. They have brought the vessels of his house before thee, and thou and thy lords have drunk wine therein. Thou hast praised the gods of silver and gold which see not, nor hear, nor know. And the God in whose hand thy breath is, hast thou not glorified. Then was this writing sent from before him."

Daniel turned unto the wall. The lamplight fell across the letters. He read them aloud, one by one, into the hush of the hall. "Mene. Mene. Tekel. Upharsin."

He turned again unto the king. "This is the interpretation thereof. Mene: God hath numbered thy kingdom, and finished it. Tekel: thou art weighed in the balances, and art found wanting. Peres: thy kingdom is divided, and given to the Medes and Persians."

The king made the proclamation. They clothed Daniel with scarlet. They put a chain of gold about his neck.

In that night the kingdom passed from the hand of Belshazzar, and Darius the Median received it before the morning.
