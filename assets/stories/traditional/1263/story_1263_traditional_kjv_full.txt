The voices of Peter and John were yet in the air when the footsteps reached them. The priests came first, their robes brushing the stones of the temple court. Behind them walked the captain of the temple, and after him the Sadducees, their faces set. The crowd that had been hearkening drew back to let them through.

The temple guards laid their hands on Peter and on John. They took them by the arms. The sun was already low against the western wall, and the long shadow of the colonnade reached across the pavement.

"It is now eventide," the captain said. "Put them in hold unto the next day."

They were led away. The doors of the holding chamber closed behind them. But the word they had been speaking did not close with the doors. Out in the court, men which had heard them turned one to another. They spake the name they had heard. Howbeit by nightfall the number of the men which believed was come to be about five thousand.

On the morrow the council was gathered. Annas the high priest sat at the head. Caiaphas was at his right hand. John and Alexander were on the bench. As many as were of the kindred of the high priest filled the seats along the curve. The chamber was wide and stone-floored, and the lamps were already lit against the early grey of the day.

The guards brought Peter and John in. They set them in the midst of the half-circle. The two men stood there in the rough robes they had worn the day before, the dust of the holding chamber still upon them. Their hands were empty. Their faces were lifted.

Annas spake first. "By what power, or by what name, have ye done this?"

The chamber waited. The scribes at the wall had their styluses ready.

Then Peter, filled with the Holy Ghost, lifted up his voice.

"Ye rulers of the people, and elders of Israel," he said. "If we this day be examined of the good deed done to the impotent man, by what means he is made whole — be it known unto you all, and to all the people of Israel, that by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom ye crucified, whom God raised from the dead, even by him doth this man stand here before you whole."

A councilman at the far end shifted on his bench. Another laid his palm against the carven arm of his seat.

"This is the stone," Peter said, "which was set at nought of you builders, which is become the head of the corner. Neither is there salvation in any other. For there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved."

The chamber did not answer at once. The councilmen beheld Peter. They beheld John beside him. They saw the boldness in two men which could not read their law in the tongue wherein it was given. They marvelled, and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus.

Then they looked further. The man which had been impotent — the man whom every passer-by at the gate called Beautiful had known by name, the man who was above forty years old — stood among the witnesses. He stood on the two feet that had never borne him before. The councilmen beheld him standing with them. They could say nothing against it.

Annas raised his hand. "Take them aside."

The guards led Peter and John out of the chamber. The man which was healed went with them. The doors closed. The council was left to itself.

"What shall we do to these men?" one said.

"A notable miracle hath been done by them," said another. "It is manifest unto all them that dwell in Jerusalem. We cannot deny it."

A third voice, lower. "But that it spread no further among the people — let us straitly threaten them, that they speak henceforth to no man in this name."

The council agreed. They called the two men back in. They commanded them not to speak at all nor teach in the name of Jesus.

Peter and John stood again in the midst of them. They did not lower their eyes.

"Whether it be right in the sight of God to hearken unto you more than unto God, judge ye," they said. "For we cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard."

The council threatened them further. They could find nothing how they might punish them, because of the people. For all men glorified God for that which was done. The man was above forty years old, on whom this miracle of healing was shewed.

The doors were opened. Peter and John walked out into the morning. The healed man walked with them on his own two feet. The city was beginning its long day.
