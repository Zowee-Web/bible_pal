The voices of Peter and John were still in the air when the footsteps reached them. The priests came first, robes brushing the stones of the temple court. Behind them walked the captain of the temple, and after him the Sadducees, their faces set. The crowd that had been listening drew back to let them through.

The temple guards laid their hands on Peter and on John. They took them by the arms. The sun was already low against the western wall, and the long shadow of the colonnade reached across the pavement.

"It is evening," the captain said. "Hold them until morning."

They were led away. The doors of the holding room closed behind them. But the words they had been speaking did not close with the doors. Out in the court, men who had heard them turned to one another. They spoke the name they had heard. By nightfall the number of those who believed had come to be about five thousand.

In the morning the council was gathered. Annas the high priest sat at the head. Caiaphas was at his right. John and Alexander were on the bench. The relatives of the high priest filled the seats along the curve. The chamber was wide and stone-floored, and the lamps were already lit against the early grey of the day.

The guards brought Peter and John in. They set them in the middle of the half-circle. The two men stood there in the rough robes they had worn the day before, the dust of the holding room still on them. Their hands were empty. Their faces were lifted.

Annas spoke first. "By what power, or in what name, have ye done this?"

The chamber waited. The scribes at the wall had their styluses ready.

Then Peter, filled with the Holy Ghost, lifted his voice.

"Ye rulers of the people, and elders of Israel," he said. "If we this day be examined of the good deed done to the impotent man, by what means he is made whole — be it known unto you all, and to all the people of Israel, that by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom ye crucified, whom God raised from the dead, even by him doth this man stand here before you whole."

A councilman at the far end shifted on his bench. Another laid his palm against the carved arm of his seat.

"This is the stone," Peter said, "which was set at nought of you builders, which is become the head of the corner. Neither is there salvation in any other. For there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved."

The room did not answer at once. The councilmen looked at Peter. They looked at John beside him. They saw the boldness in two men who could not read their law in its original tongue. They marvelled, and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus.

Then they looked further. The man who had been impotent — the man whom every passer-by at the Beautiful gate had known by name, the man who was above forty years old — stood among the witnesses. He stood on the two feet that had never carried him before. The councilmen could see him. They could say nothing against it.

Annas raised a hand. "Take them aside."

The guards led Peter and John out of the chamber. The man who had been healed went with them. The doors closed. The council was left to itself.

"What shall we do to these men?" one said.

"A notable miracle hath been done by them," said another. "It is manifest to all them that dwell in Jerusalem. We cannot deny it."

A third voice, lower. "But that it spread no further among the people — let us straitly threaten them, that they speak henceforth to no man in this name."

The council agreed. They called the two men back in. They commanded them not to speak at all nor teach in the name of Jesus.

Peter and John stood again in the middle of them. They did not lower their eyes.

"Whether it be right in the sight of God to hearken unto you more than unto God, judge ye," they said. "For we cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard."

The council threatened them further. They could find nothing how they might punish them, because of the people. For all men glorified God for that which was done. The man was above forty years old, on whom this miracle of healing was shewed.

The doors were opened. Peter and John walked out into the morning. The healed man walked with them on his own two feet. The city was beginning its long day.
