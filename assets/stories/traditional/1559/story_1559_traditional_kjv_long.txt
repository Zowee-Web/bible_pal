John had been upon the isle of Patmos when the door was opened in heaven.

He had been a prisoner there for the word of God and the testimony of Jesus. He had not expected to see anything but the rocks and the sea and the small huts of the other men exiled with him. He had been an old man a long time now. He had outlived the others. He had outlived Peter, who had been crucified head downward in Rome. He had outlived James, with whom he had grown up at his father Zebedee's nets. He had outlived all of them, and he had been left alone with the writing, and now he had been sent to this isle that the writing also might be silenced.

On the Lord's day he had heard a voice behind him as the sound of a trumpet, and he had turned, and the door had been opened, and the voice had said: come up hither.

He had been carried in the Spirit unto a throne whereof he had no language. He had seen the throne. He had seen him that sat upon it, whose appearance was as a jasper and a sardine stone. He had seen the rainbow round about the throne, in sight like unto an emerald. He had seen the four beasts, full of eyes before and behind, each with six wings, resting not. The first was like a lion. The second was like a calf. The third had the face of a man. The fourth was like a flying eagle. He had seen the four and twenty elders sitting upon their own thrones round about the great throne, clothed in white raiment, and they had crowns of gold upon their heads, as a council of fathers about a king.

The whole place sang. Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty, which was, and is, and is to come. The elders cast their crowns down before the throne, and said: thou art worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and honour and power. For thou hast created all things, and for thy pleasure they are and were created.

He had seen all of this. He thought he had seen the most that could be seen. He stood in the place where he had been brought, and tried not to fall.

Then he saw a book.

It lay in the right hand of him that sat upon the throne. It was written within and on the backside, and sealed with seven seals. The seals were of red wax. The book moved not. He understood, without being told, that the book held what was to come — every page of what God had set in motion and should yet bring to its end. Not a list of dates. Not a calendar. Something greater. The whole shape of how the story finished.

A strong angel proclaimed with a loud voice that filled the throne room: who is worthy to open the book, and to loose the seals thereof?

And no man answered.

No man in heaven. No man in earth. No man under the earth. None among the angels. None among the elders. None among the saints that had come up before. The throne room was silent. The four beasts moved not. The voice of the angel hung in the air, and the question stayed open, and no man stepped forward.

And John wept.

He wept much. He could not forbear. He had been carried up to see the throne, and the throne had a book, and no man was found worthy to open it. The whole long unfolding of what God had begun, all the way from the first light in Genesis, was held in a hand that was holding it forth — and no man was found who could read it. No man was found worthy even to look thereon.

He wept the way an old man weeps. Quietly. With his shoulders. The four beasts saw, and the elders saw, and the angels saw. They let him weep.

After a while, one of the elders came unto him. He laid his hand upon John's shoulder.

"Weep not," he said. "Behold, the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, hath prevailed to open the book, and to loose the seven seals thereof."

John looked up. He had been bidden to look for a Lion. He had been bidden to look for the Root of David, the lion-king of Judah promised in the dying words of Jacob, promised again in the writings of Isaiah. He turned his eyes back toward the throne.

He beheld a Lamb.

It stood in the midst of the throne and of the four beasts, and in the midst of the elders, as it had been slain. The marks were upon it. It had seven horns and seven eyes — the seven Spirits of God, sent forth into all the earth. It was the strangest sight in the throne room, and the most familiar. He knew the Lamb. He had stood at the foot of the cross. He had washed and wrapped the body. He had walked away on the third day with the empty linen in his hands.

The Lamb came forward, into the open place before the throne. It took the book out of the right hand of him that sat upon the throne. The book moved from one hand to the other without a sound.

And when it had taken the book, the four beasts and four and twenty elders fell down before the Lamb. Every one of them had a harp, and golden vials full of odours, which are the prayers of saints. The harps and the vials touched the floor together. The whole council of fathers went down at once.

They sung a new song. Not one of the old songs. Not the one Moses had sung at the sea. Not the one David had sung when the ark came up to Jerusalem. A new one. A song that had not been sung before, for what it was for had not come to pass before.

"Thou art worthy to take the book, and to open the seals thereof: for thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation; and hast made us unto our God kings and priests: and we shall reign on the earth."

Then John beheld, and he heard the voice of many angels round about the throne and the beasts and the elders. The number of them was ten thousand times ten thousand, and thousands of thousands. He could not count them. He could but hear them. They said with a loud voice:

"Worthy is the Lamb that was slain to receive power, and riches, and wisdom, and strength, and honour, and glory, and blessing."

And then the song was answered.

Every creature which is in heaven, and on the earth, and under the earth, and such as are in the sea, and all that are in them, sang it back. The whole creation sang it back together. The fish in the deep. The birds in the air. The lions upon the hills. The men in the fields. The old prisoners on isles like Patmos who should never have known. The whole creation sang.

"Blessing, and honour, and glory, and power, be unto him that sitteth upon the throne, and unto the Lamb, for ever and ever."

And the four beasts said: Amen.

And the four and twenty elders fell down and worshipped him that liveth for ever and ever.

And John stood where he had been standing. The Lion he had been told of, and the Lamb he had been shown, were the same. The book could be opened now. The wounds were the cause it could be. And so it was on the day John was shown the book, and the One who could open it, in the throne room of heaven.
