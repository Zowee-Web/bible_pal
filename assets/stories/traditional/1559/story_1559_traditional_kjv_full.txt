John had been upon the isle of Patmos when the door was opened in heaven. He had been a prisoner there for the word of God and the testimony of Jesus. He had not expected to see anything but the rocks and the sea and the small huts of the other men exiled with him. But on the Lord's day he had heard a voice behind him as the sound of a trumpet, and he had turned, and the door had been opened, and the voice had said: come up hither.

He had been carried in the Spirit unto a throne whereof he had no language. He had seen the throne. He had seen him that sat upon it, whose appearance was as a jasper and a sardine stone. He had seen the rainbow round about the throne, in sight like unto an emerald. He had seen the four beasts, full of eyes before and behind, each with six wings, resting not. He had seen the four and twenty elders sitting upon their own thrones round about the great throne, clothed in white raiment, and they had crowns of gold upon their heads.

The whole place sang. Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty, which was, and is, and is to come. The elders cast their crowns down before the throne, and said: thou art worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and honour and power.

He had seen all of this. He thought he had seen the most that could be seen.

Then he saw a book.

It lay in the right hand of him that sat upon the throne. It was written within and on the backside, and sealed with seven seals. A strong angel proclaimed with a loud voice that filled the throne room: who is worthy to open the book, and to loose the seals thereof?

And no man answered. No man in heaven. No man in earth. No man under the earth. None among the angels. None among the elders. None among the saints that had come up before. The throne room was silent.

And John wept.

He wept much. He could not forbear. The whole long unfolding of what God had set in motion, was held in a hand that was holding it forth — and no man was found who could read it. No man was found worthy to open the book, neither to look thereon.

And one of the elders came unto him. He laid his hand upon John's shoulder.

"Weep not," he said. "Behold, the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, hath prevailed to open the book, and to loose the seven seals thereof."

John looked up. He had been bidden to look for a Lion. He turned his eyes back toward the throne.

He beheld a Lamb.

It stood in the midst of the throne and of the four beasts, and in the midst of the elders, as it had been slain. The marks were upon it. It had seven horns and seven eyes — the seven Spirits of God, sent forth into all the earth. And it came and took the book out of the right hand of him that sat upon the throne. The book moved from one hand to the other without a sound.

And when it had taken the book, the four beasts and four and twenty elders fell down before the Lamb. Every one of them had a harp, and golden vials full of odours, which are the prayers of saints. The harps and the vials touched the floor together.

They sung a new song, saying:

"Thou art worthy to take the book, and to open the seals thereof: for thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation; and hast made us unto our God kings and priests: and we shall reign on the earth."

Then John beheld, and he heard the voice of many angels round about the throne and the beasts and the elders. The number of them was ten thousand times ten thousand, and thousands of thousands. He could not count them. He could but hear them. They said with a loud voice:

"Worthy is the Lamb that was slain to receive power, and riches, and wisdom, and strength, and honour, and glory, and blessing."

And then the song was answered.

Every creature which is in heaven, and on the earth, and under the earth, and such as are in the sea, and all that are in them, heard he saying:

"Blessing, and honour, and glory, and power, be unto him that sitteth upon the throne, and unto the Lamb, for ever and ever."

And the four beasts said: Amen.

And the four and twenty elders fell down and worshipped him that liveth for ever and ever.

And John stood where he had been standing. The Lion he had been told of, and the Lamb he had been shown, were the same. The book could be opened now. The wounds were the cause it could be. And so it was on the day John was shown the book, and the One who could open it, in the throne room of heaven.
