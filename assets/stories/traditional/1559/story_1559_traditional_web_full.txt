John had been on the island of Patmos when the door was opened in heaven. He had been a prisoner there for the word of God and the testimony of Jesus. He had not expected to see anything but the rocks and the sea and the small huts of the other men exiled with him. But on the Lord's day he had heard a voice behind him like the sound of a trumpet, and he had turned, and the door had been opened, and the voice had said: come up here.

He had been carried in the Spirit to a throne room he had no language for. He had seen the throne. He had seen the One who sat on it, whose appearance was like jasper and carnelian. He had seen the rainbow around the throne, like an emerald. He had seen the four living creatures, full of eyes before and behind, each with six wings, never resting. He had seen the twenty-four elders seated on their own thrones around the great throne, dressed in white, with crowns of gold on their heads.

The whole place sang. Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God Almighty, who was and who is and who is to come. The elders cast their crowns down before the throne, and said: you are worthy, our Lord and our God, to receive the glory and the honor and the power.

He had seen all of this. He thought he had seen the most that could be seen.

Then he saw a scroll.

It lay in the right hand of him who sat on the throne. It was written on the inside and on the outside, and it was sealed shut with seven seals. A mighty angel proclaimed with a loud voice that filled the throne room: who is worthy to open the scroll, and to break its seals?

No one answered. No one in heaven above. No one on the earth. No one under the earth. None among the angels. None among the elders. None among the saints who had come up before. The throne room was silent.

And John wept.

He wept much. He could not help it. The history of the world, the long unfolding of what God had set in motion and would yet bring to its end, was held in a hand that was holding it out — and no one was found who could read it. No one was found worthy to open the scroll, or even to look in it.

One of the elders came to him. He laid his hand on John's shoulder.

"Don't weep," he said. "Behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has overcome. He will open the scroll and its seven seals."

John looked up. He had been told to look for a Lion. He turned his eyes back toward the throne.

He saw a Lamb.

It stood in the middle of the throne and of the four living creatures, and in the middle of the elders. It looked as though it had been slain. The marks were on it. It had seven horns and seven eyes — the seven Spirits of God, sent out into all the earth. It walked forward, into the open place before the throne. It took the scroll out of the right hand of him who sat on the throne. The scroll moved from one hand to the other without a sound.

When the Lamb had taken the scroll, the throne room moved.

The four living creatures and the twenty-four elders fell down before the Lamb. Each elder held a harp, and a golden bowl full of incense, which the text said were the prayers of the saints. The harps and the bowls touched the floor together.

They sang a new song:

"You are worthy to take the scroll and to open its seals, for you were slain, and bought us for God with your blood out of every tribe and language and people and nation. You have made us a kingdom and priests to our God, and we will reign on the earth."

Then John heard another voice rising. It was the voice of many angels around the throne and the living creatures and the elders. The number of them was ten thousand times ten thousand, and thousands of thousands. He could not count them. He could only hear them. They sang with a loud voice:

"Worthy is the Lamb who has been slain, to receive the power, and wealth, and wisdom, and strength, and honor, and glory, and blessing."

And then the song was answered.

Every created thing — every creature in heaven, every creature on the earth, every creature under the earth, every creature on the sea, and everything in them — sang back. The whole creation sang it back together. Blessing and honor and glory and dominion to him who sits on the throne, and to the Lamb, forever and ever.

The four living creatures said: Amen.

The elders fell down and worshiped.

And John stood where he had been standing. The Lion he had been told about, and the Lamb he had been shown, were the same. The scroll could be opened now. The wounds were the reason it could be. And so it was on the day John was shown the scroll, and the One who could open it, in the throne room of heaven.
