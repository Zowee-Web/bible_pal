Moses was a hundred and twenty years old, and the day of his death was nigh. He had set down the songs and was speaking now in the plain prose of a man who knew the end. The people stood upon the plains of Moab, on the near side of the Jordan, in sight of the hills of the land they had been told they should enter and that he had been told he should not.

He had spoken many days. He had recounted the way the Lord had brought them out of Egypt, and the giving of the law at Horeb, and the wandering in the wilderness. He had warned them of the gods of the nations whose land they were about to take. He had named the curses that would come if they turned away, and the blessings that would come if they obeyed.

And now, on the last of those days, he came to the part that mattered most to him.

"This commandment which I command thee this day is not hidden from thee, neither is it far off. It is not in heaven, that thou shouldest say, Who shall go up for us to heaven, and bring it unto us, that we may hear it, and do it? Neither is it beyond the sea. But the word is very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart, that thou mayest do it.

"See, I have set before thee this day life and good, and death and evil. I call heaven and earth to record this day against you, that I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing. Therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live. Love the Lord thy God, obey his voice, and cleave unto him. For he is thy life, and the length of thy days."

And the old man finished what he had to say. The plains stretched out around them. The Jordan ran behind him. The hills of the land they should enter rose in front of them in the afternoon light. He had said it as plainly as he knew how. The choice was theirs now to make. And so it was on the day Moses set life and death before the people of Israel, in the last year of his pilgrimage, on the plains of Moab.
