Moses was a hundred and twenty years old, and the day of his death was close. He had set down the songs and was speaking now in the plain prose of a man who knew the end. The people stood on the plains of Moab, on the near side of the Jordan, in sight of the hills of the land they had been told they would enter and that he had been told he would not.

He had spoken many days. He had recounted the way Yahweh had brought them out of Egypt, and the giving of the law at Horeb, and the wandering in the wilderness. He had warned them of the gods of the nations whose land they were about to take. He had named the curses that would come if they turned away, and the blessings that would come if they obeyed.

And now, on the last of those days, he came to the part that mattered most to him.

"This commandment which I command you today is not too hard for you, neither is it far off. It is not in heaven, that you should say, who shall go up for us to heaven, and bring it down to us, that we may hear it, and do it? Nor is it beyond the sea. The word is very near you, in your mouth and in your heart, that you may do it.

"See, I have set before you today life and good, and death and evil. I call heaven and earth to witness against you today, that I have set before you life and death, blessing and curse. Therefore choose life, that you and your descendants may live. Love Yahweh your God, obey his voice, and cling to him. For he is your life, and the length of your days."

And the old man finished what he had to say. The plains stretched out around them. The Jordan ran behind him. The hills of the land they would enter rose in front of them in the afternoon light. He had said it as plainly as he knew how. The choice was theirs now to make. And so it was on the day Moses set life and death before the people of Israel, in the last year of his pilgrimage, on the plains of Moab.
