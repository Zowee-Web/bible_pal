Moses was a hundred and twenty years old. His eye had not grown dim and his natural force had not abated, but the day of his death was close enough that he had stopped writing songs and was now speaking in the plain prose of a man who knew the end. He had climbed Mount Nebo only once already, in his thoughts. He would climb it once more in his body before this week was out. The people stood gathered before him on the plains of Moab, on the near side of the Jordan, in sight of the hills of the land they had been told they would enter and that he had been told he would not.

He had spoken many days. He had recounted the way Yahweh had brought them out of Egypt — the plagues, the sea, the song of Miriam beside the bones of the chariots. He had recounted the giving of the law at Horeb, the trembling mountain and the fire on its top. He had recounted the wandering in the wilderness, the manna, the water from the rock, the forty years that had buried a generation and grown a new one. He had warned them of the gods of the nations whose land they were about to take — the high places, the carved images, the names that would come whispering through the doorways of their houses. He had named the curses that would come if they turned away. He had named the blessings that would come if they obeyed.

The people had listened. The elders had nodded slowly, remembering. The children stared up at him with the open faces of those who had not been there for any of it, and who would be there for everything that came after.

And now, on the last of those days, he came to the part that mattered most to him. The part he had circled around for forty chapters and was finally ready to say plain.

"This commandment which I command you today is not too hard for you, neither is it far off. It is not in heaven, that you should say, who shall go up for us to heaven, and bring it down to us, that we may hear it, and do it? Nor is it beyond the sea, that you should say, who shall go over the sea for us, and bring it to us? The word is very near you, in your mouth and in your heart, that you may do it.

"See, I have set before you today life and good, and death and evil. If you obey the commandments of Yahweh your God which I command you today, to love Yahweh your God, to walk in his ways and to keep his commandments, then you will live and multiply, and Yahweh your God will bless you in the land where you are going in to possess it. But if your heart turns away, and you will not hear, but are drawn away and worship other gods, you will surely perish. You will not prolong your days.

"I call heaven and earth to witness against you today, that I have set before you life and death, blessing and curse. Therefore choose life, that you and your descendants may live. Love Yahweh your God, obey his voice, and cling to him. For he is your life, and the length of your days, that you may dwell in the land which Yahweh swore to your fathers, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, to give them."

And the old man finished what he had to say. He looked out at them and they looked back at him. Joshua stood somewhere in the assembly, the man who would lead them across the Jordan in the morning, his arms crossed, his face composed. The elders looked at one another. No one spoke. There was nothing more to add.

The plains stretched out around them. The Jordan ran behind him. The hills of the land rose in front of them in the late afternoon light. He had said it as plainly as he knew how. The choice was theirs now to make. And so it was on the day Moses set life and death before the people of Israel, in the last year of his pilgrimage, on the plains of Moab.
