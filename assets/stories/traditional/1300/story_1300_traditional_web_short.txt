It was the first month, in the spring of the third year of Cyrus the king of Persia. The waters of the great river Hiddekel were running high with melt from the mountains. Daniel was an old man. He had been by the river many days. He had not gone home. He had not sat at the king's table. The men who had come with him stayed at a distance.

In the third year of Cyrus king of Persia a thing was revealed to Daniel, whose name was called Belteshazzar; and the thing was true, even a great warfare. He understood the thing, and had understanding of the vision.

In those days I, Daniel, was mourning three whole weeks.

I ate no pleasant food, neither came flesh nor wine into my mouth, neither did I anoint myself at all, until three whole weeks were fulfilled.

The morning came and went. The evening came and went. The men with him went to their houses. Daniel stayed by the river. The wind moved across the water. The reed beds bent and rose again. The first month passed. The river ran on.
