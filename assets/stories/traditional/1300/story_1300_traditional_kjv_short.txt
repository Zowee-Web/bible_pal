It was the first month, in the spring of the third year of Cyrus the king of Persia. The waters of the great river Hiddekel were running high with melt from the mountains. Daniel was an old man. He had been by the river many days. He had not gone home. He had not sat at the king's table. The men which were come with him stood afar off.

In the third year of Cyrus king of Persia a thing was revealed unto Daniel, whose name was called Belteshazzar; and the thing was true, but the time appointed was long: and he understood the thing, and had understanding of the vision.

In those days I Daniel was mourning three full weeks.

I ate no pleasant bread, neither came flesh nor wine in my mouth, neither did I anoint myself at all, till three whole weeks were fulfilled.

The morning came and went. The evening came and went. The men with him went unto their houses. Daniel abode by the river. The wind moved across the water. The reed beds bowed and rose again. The first month passed. The river ran on.
