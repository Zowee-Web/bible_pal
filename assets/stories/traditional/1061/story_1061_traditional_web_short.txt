Naaman was one of the most important soldiers in all of Syria. He was the commander of the whole army. The king trusted him. Other soldiers looked up to him. He was brave and strong and had won many battles.

But Naaman had a problem that nobody could fix. He had a terrible skin disease called leprosy. It made his skin turn white in patches and lose all feeling. No matter how many battles he won, no matter how important he was, the disease stayed.

In Naaman's house there was a little girl from Israel. She had been taken from her home and brought to Syria to be a servant. She was far from her family and far from everything she knew. But she still cared about people.

One day she said to Naaman's wife, "I wish my master could go see the prophet in Israel. He could heal him."

Those words changed everything. Naaman traveled all the way to Israel with horses and chariots and bags of gold and silver. He was used to being treated like a very important person.

He arrived at the prophet Elisha's house and stood at the door. But Elisha didn't even come outside. He just sent a servant with a message: "Go wash in the Jordan River seven times, and you'll be healed."

Naaman was furious. "I thought he would come out, wave his hands, call on God, and do something impressive! The rivers back home are nicer than the Jordan. Why can't I wash there?"

He turned to leave. But his servants spoke up gently. "Sir, if the prophet had asked you to do something hard, you would have done it. This is easy. Just try it."

Naaman thought about it. Then he went to the river.

He walked into the water and dipped himself under. Once. Twice. Three times. Four. Five. Six. Nothing changed.

Seven.

He came up out of the water and looked at his arms. The white patches were gone. His skin was smooth and soft — like a little child's skin. Clean. Completely healed.

Naaman stood in the middle of the Jordan with water dripping from his beard, turning his arms over and over in the sunlight.
