Early on the first day of the week, while it was still dark, Mary Magdalene made her way to the tomb. The garden was quiet. The air was cool and heavy with dew. The first gray light of morning had not yet reached the tops of the olive trees that lined the path. Her sandals scraped softly against the stone. She carried spices she had prepared — oils and fragrant herbs wrapped in cloth. Her eyes were swollen from days of weeping.

When she reached the tomb, she stopped. The great stone — the one they had watched the men roll into place two days before — had been moved. The entrance stood open, dark and empty.

She turned and ran. She found Simon Peter and the other disciple, the one Jesus loved. "They have taken the Lord out of the tomb," she said, her voice breaking. "We don't know where they have laid him."

Both disciples ran to the tomb. The other disciple was faster. He reached the entrance first, bent down, and peered inside. He could see the linen wrappings lying on the stone shelf — but he did not go in. Peter arrived a moment later, breathing hard, and went straight inside. He saw the linen cloths. He saw the face cloth — the one that had covered Jesus' head — not lying with the rest of the linens, but folded neatly and placed to one side by itself.

Then the other disciple stepped inside. He saw the same things. And he believed — though they did not yet understand the scripture, that Jesus must rise from the dead.

The two disciples went back to their homes. But Mary stayed.

She stood outside the tomb, weeping. The garden was still. The birds had not yet begun to sing. After a while, she bent down and looked inside the tomb one more time.

Two angels sat where the body of Jesus had been — one at the head, one at the feet. They were dressed in white, and they looked at her with calm, steady eyes.

"Woman, why are you weeping?" they asked.

"Because they have taken away my Lord," she said, "and I don't know where they have laid him."

She turned around. A man was standing behind her in the garden. The light was still gray. Her eyes were blurred with tears. She did not recognize him.

"Woman, why are you weeping?" the man asked. "Who are you looking for?"

She thought he was the gardener — someone who worked in this place, who might have moved the body. "Sir," she said, "if you have carried him away, tell me where you have laid him, and I will take him."

Then he spoke her name.

"Mary."

One word. Her name, spoken the way only he had ever spoken it.

She turned fully toward him. Everything in her shifted — grief breaking open into recognition, disbelief dissolving into joy so sudden it stopped her breath.

"Rabboni!" she cried. Teacher.

She reached for him. But Jesus said to her, "Do not cling to me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father. But go to my brothers and tell them, 'I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God.'"

Mary Magdalene left the garden. She walked through the streets of Jerusalem in the early morning light, her face streaked with tears but her eyes bright and clear. She found the disciples and told them.

"I have seen the Lord," she said. And she told them everything he had said to her.
