Early on the first day of the week, while it was still dark, Mary Magdalene came to the tomb. The garden was quiet. The air was cool and damp. The first gray light of morning had not yet reached the tops of the trees. She walked along the path between the stones, her breath visible in the cold air, carrying spices she had prepared.

When she reached the tomb, she stopped. The stone had been rolled away from the entrance.

She turned and ran. She found Simon Peter and the other disciple, the one Jesus loved, and said to them, "They have taken the Lord out of the tomb, and we don't know where they have laid him."

Peter and the other disciple ran to the tomb. The other disciple outran Peter and reached it first. He bent down and looked in — he saw the linen cloths lying there, but he did not go in. Peter arrived and went straight inside. He saw the linen cloths, and the face cloth that had been on Jesus' head, folded neatly and set aside by itself. Then the other disciple went in too. He saw, and he believed. They did not yet understand the scripture, that Jesus must rise from the dead. The two disciples went back to their homes.

But Mary stayed.

She stood outside the tomb, weeping. After a while, she bent down and looked inside. Two angels in white were sitting where the body of Jesus had been — one at the head, one at the feet.

"Woman, why are you weeping?" they asked.

"Because they have taken away my Lord," she said, "and I don't know where they have laid him."

She turned around. A man was standing behind her in the garden. She did not recognize him. The light was still dim. Her eyes were blurred with tears.

"Woman, why are you weeping?" the man said. "Who are you looking for?"

She thought he was the gardener. "Sir," she said, "if you have carried him away, tell me where you have laid him, and I will take him."

Then he spoke her name.

"Mary."

She turned fully toward him. Something broke open in her — recognition, disbelief, joy so sudden it took her breath.

"Rabboni!" she said. Teacher.

Jesus said to her, "Do not cling to me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father. But go to my brothers and say to them, 'I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God.'"

Mary Magdalene went to the disciples. Her face was streaked with tears, but her eyes were bright. She told them, "I have seen the Lord." And she told them what he had said to her.
