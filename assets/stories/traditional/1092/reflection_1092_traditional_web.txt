She came to care for a body. She came carrying spices, expecting death, expecting silence, expecting the worst thing to still be true.

And then someone spoke her name.

Not a title. Not an explanation. Her name — spoken the way only one person had ever spoken it. And in that single word, everything she thought she knew about what was possible broke open.

There is something about being known — truly known, by name, by someone you thought was gone — that no argument or evidence can replicate. It is not proof. It is presence.

Sometimes the thing that changes everything is not a dramatic event. It is a familiar voice, saying your name, in a place where you did not expect to hear it.
