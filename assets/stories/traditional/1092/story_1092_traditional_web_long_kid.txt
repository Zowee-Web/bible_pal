It was very early in the morning — so early that the sky was still dark. A woman named Mary Magdalene walked through a quiet garden, all by herself. Her eyes were red and puffy from crying. She had been sad for days, ever since Jesus died on the cross.

She was carrying special spices wrapped in cloth. Back then, when someone you loved died, you would put nice-smelling oils and spices on their body to show respect. That's what Mary was coming to do.

But when she got to the tomb — a kind of cave carved into rock with a big, heavy stone across the entrance — she stopped.

The stone was GONE. The entrance was wide open.

Mary's heart started pounding. She turned around and RAN.

She found two of Jesus' closest friends — Peter and another disciple. "They've taken Jesus out of the tomb!" she cried. "We don't know where he is!"

Peter and the other disciple took off running toward the tomb. The other disciple was faster and got there first. He bent down and peeked inside. He could see the cloths that had been wrapped around Jesus' body, lying there on the stone shelf. But no Jesus.

Peter came running up behind him and went straight inside. He saw the same thing — the cloths were there, and the special cloth that had been over Jesus' face was folded up neatly and set off to the side all by itself.

Then the other disciple went in too. He looked around. And he believed — even though he didn't fully understand yet what had happened.

The two disciples went home. But Mary couldn't leave. She stood outside the tomb, crying and crying.

After a while, she bent down and looked inside one more time.

This time, two angels in white clothes were sitting where Jesus' body had been! One was sitting at the head, and one at the feet.

"Why are you crying?" they asked her gently.

"Because someone took my Lord," Mary said through her tears, "and I don't know where he is."

Then she turned around. A man was standing right behind her in the garden. It was still kind of dark, and her eyes were blurry from crying. She didn't recognize him at all.

"Why are you crying?" the man asked. "Who are you looking for?"

Mary figured he must be the gardener — who else would be out here this early? "Sir," she said, "if you moved him somewhere, please just tell me where, and I'll go get him."

Then the man said just one word.

"Mary."

That's all it took. ONE WORD. Her name. But she knew that voice. She had heard it so many times — teaching, laughing, calling her name — and she never, ever thought she'd hear it again.

She spun around. Her breath caught. Her tears stopped.

"TEACHER!" she cried out.

It was Jesus. Really, truly, actually Jesus. He was ALIVE.

She wanted to grab onto him and never let go. But Jesus said, "Don't hold onto me right now. I need you to go tell the others something important. Tell them I'm going to be with my Father — my Father and your Father, my God and your God."

Mary wiped her eyes. She stood there in the garden for just a moment. The sky was getting lighter. Birds were starting to sing. The first rays of sunlight were touching the tops of the trees.

Then she turned and ran — not because she was sad this time, but because she had the greatest news anyone has ever had to share.

She found the disciples. Her face was still wet with tears, but she was SMILING.

"I have seen the Lord!" she said.

And she told them everything.
