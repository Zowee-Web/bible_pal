The first day of the week cometh Mary Magdalene early, when it was yet dark, unto the sepulchre. The garden was still. The air was cold and heavy with dew. The first grey light of morning had not reached the tops of the olive trees that lined the path. Her sandals scraped softly upon the stone. She bore spices — oils and sweet herbs wrapped in cloth. Her eyes were swollen with days of weeping.

When she came unto the sepulchre, she stayed her steps. The great stone — the stone they had watched the men roll into place two days before — had been taken away. The entrance stood open, dark and empty.

She turned and ran. She found Simon Peter and the other disciple, whom Jesus loved. "They have taken away the Lord out of the sepulchre," she said, her voice breaking, "and we know not where they have laid him."

Both disciples ran to the tomb. The other disciple outran Peter and came first. He stooped down and looked in — he saw the linen clothes lying, but he went not in. Then cometh Simon Peter, and went into the sepulchre. He seeth the linen clothes lie, and the napkin that was about his head, not lying with the linen clothes, but wrapped together in a place by itself.

Then went the other disciple in also. He saw, and believed — though as yet they knew not the scripture, that he must rise again from the dead.

The disciples went away unto their own homes. But Mary stood without, weeping.

As she wept, she stooped down and looked into the sepulchre. Two angels in white sat where the body of Jesus had lain — one at the head, one at the feet.

"Woman, why weepest thou?" they said.

"Because they have taken away my Lord," she said, "and I know not where they have laid him."

She turned back. A man stood behind her in the garden. The light was dim. Her eyes were blurred with tears. She knew him not.

"Woman, why weepest thou?" the man said. "Whom seekest thou?"

She, supposing him to be the gardener, saith, "Sir, if thou have borne him hence, tell me where thou hast laid him, and I will take him away."

Jesus saith unto her, "Mary."

One word. Spoken as only he had ever spoken it.

She turned fully toward him. All within her broke open — grief giving way unto recognition, sorrow dissolving into joy so sudden it took her breath.

"Rabboni!" she cried.

She reached for him. But Jesus saith unto her, "Touch me not; for I am not yet ascended to my Father: but go to my brethren, and say unto them, I ascend unto my Father, and your Father; and to my God, and your God."

Mary Magdalene departed from the garden. She walked through the streets of Jerusalem in the early morning light, her face wet with tears, her eyes bright and clear. She came unto the disciples.

"I have seen the Lord," she said. And she told them all things which he had spoken unto her.
