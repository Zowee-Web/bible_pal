Jesus sent two disciples before him as they drew nigh unto Jerusalem, come to Bethphage at the Mount of Olives. "Go into the village over against you," he said, "and straightway ye shall find an ass tied, and a colt with her: loose them, and bring them unto me. And if any man say ought unto you, ye shall say, 'The Lord hath need of them,' and straightway he will send them."

The two went forth. They found the ass and the colt tied by a doorway upon the street, even as he had said. They loosed them. One standing nearby asked what they did. They answered as Jesus had bidden them. The man let them go.

They brought the beasts unto Jesus. The disciples put on them their garments. Jesus sat thereon.

The multitude began to move. Many spread their garments in the way. Others cut down branches from the trees — palm fronds and leafy boughs — and strawed them in the way. The road from the Mount of Olives descended toward the city. Jerusalem sat below them, the temple walls catching the morning light.

The multitude that went before began to cry out. They that followed joined their voices.

"Hosanna to the son of David! Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord! Hosanna in the highest!"

The sound carried down the slope. The colt walked steadily through the branches and spread garments. Dust rose from the road. The palm fronds rustled beneath its hooves.

When he was come into Jerusalem, all the city was moved. People came forth from doorways. They looked down from housetops.

"Who is this?" they asked.

The multitude answered: "This is Jesus the prophet of Nazareth of Galilee."

The procession passed through the streets. The cries continued. The branches kept coming. The road into the city was covered with garments and cut greenery for as far as the eye could see.
