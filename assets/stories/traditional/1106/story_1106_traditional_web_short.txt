Jesus sent two disciples ahead as they drew near to Jerusalem, approaching Bethphage at the Mount of Olives. "Go into the village opposite you," he said, "and immediately you will find a donkey tied, and a colt with her. Untie them and bring them to me. If anyone says anything to you, say, 'The Lord needs them,' and he will send them immediately."

The two went. They found the donkey and the colt tied near a doorway on the street, exactly as he had said. They untied them. Someone standing nearby asked what they were doing. They answered as Jesus had told them. The man let them go.

They brought the animals to Jesus. The disciples laid their garments on the colt. Jesus sat on it.

The crowd began to move. People spread their cloaks on the road ahead of him. Others cut branches from the trees — palm fronds and leafy limbs — and laid them on the packed earth. The road from the Mount of Olives descended toward the city. Jerusalem sat below them, the temple walls catching the morning light.

The crowd walking ahead began to shout. Those following behind joined.

"Hosanna to the son of David! Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord! Hosanna in the highest!"

The sound carried down the slope. The colt walked steadily through the laid branches and spread garments. Dust rose from the road. The palm fronds rustled under its hooves.

When they entered Jerusalem, the whole city was stirred. People came out of doorways. They looked down from rooftops.

"Who is this?" they asked.

The crowds answered: "This is the prophet, Jesus, from Nazareth of Galilee."

The procession moved through the streets. The shouts continued. The branches kept coming. The road into the city was covered with garments and cut greenery for as far as the eye could see.
