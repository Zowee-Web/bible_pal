The city of Jericho was straitly shut up. None went out. None came in. The walls were high. The gates were barred. The country round about the city was empty.

The LORD spake unto Joshua. He said: I have given into thine hand Jericho. Ye shall compass the city. Let all the men of war go round about the city once. Thus shalt thou do six days. Seven priests shall bear before the ark seven trumpets of rams' horns. On the seventh day ye shall compass the city seven times. The priests shall blow with the trumpets. When ye hear the long blast of the ram's horn, all the people shall shout with a great shout. The wall of the city shall fall down flat. The people shall ascend up every man straight before him.

Joshua called the priests. He gave them the trumpets. He set the order of the procession. The armed men first. Seven priests with the trumpets. The ark of the LORD borne by the Levites. The rereward last. He spake unto the people. "Ye shall not shout. Let not your voice be heard. Let no word go out of your mouth until the day I bid you shout. Then shall ye shout."

The first day the procession came out of the camp. The trumpets blew continually as they went. The ark followed. They went once round the city. The people shouted not. They returned into the camp. The watchmen on the walls of Jericho looked down at them all the while and understood not what they saw.

The second day they did so. The third. The fourth. The fifth. The sixth. Each day the procession went forth at the morning and returned into the camp or ever the evening. Each day the trumpets blew. Each day the people held their tongues. The city watched from the walls. Nothing was said. The walls were not touched. The men on the watch within the city had been looking for an assault. The assault came not.

The seventh day they rose early about the dawning of the day. They compassed the city seven times. After the seventh time the priests blew with the trumpets with the long blast. Joshua said unto the people: shout. For the LORD hath given you the city.

The people shouted with a great shout when the priests blew with the trumpets. The wall fell down flat where they stood. The wall fell along the whole circuit they had walked. The people went up into the city, every man straight before him. The streets lay open unto them.

Only Rahab and her household were spared, because she had hid the messengers that were come to spy. The procession that had been silent for six days was become a great noise on the seventh. The walls that had been high were a heap of stones. The morning went on across the plain and over the broken walls, and Israel was within the city for the first time.
