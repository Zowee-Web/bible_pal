The city of Jericho was straitly shut up because of the children of Israel. None went out. None came in. The walls were high and thick. The gates were barred and double-barred. The country round about the city was empty. The fields had not been worked. The flocks had been brought within the walls. The men on the walls watched the camp of Israel in the plain below. They could see the smoke of the cooking fires.

The LORD spake unto Joshua. He said: I have given into thine hand Jericho, and the king thereof, and the mighty men of valour. Ye shall compass the city. Let all the men of war go round about the city once. Thus shalt thou do six days. Seven priests shall bear before the ark seven trumpets of rams' horns. On the seventh day ye shall compass the city seven times. The priests shall blow with the trumpets. When ye hear the long blast of the ram's horn, all the people shall shout with a great shout. The wall of the city shall fall down flat. The people shall ascend up every man straight before him.

Joshua called the priests. He gave them the trumpets. He set the order of the procession. The armed men first. Seven priests with the trumpets. The ark of the LORD borne by the Levites. The rereward last. He spake unto the people. "Ye shall not shout. Let not your voice be heard. Let no word go out of your mouth until the day I bid you shout. Then shall ye shout."

The first day the procession came out of the camp. The armed men went before. The seven priests bearing the seven trumpets of rams' horns passed on before the ark. The trumpets blew continually as they went. The ark of the LORD followed. The rereward came after. They went once round the city. The people shouted not. They returned into the camp. The watchmen on the walls of Jericho looked down at them all the while and understood not what they saw. The day ended. The sun went down behind the camp. The walls were not touched.

The second day they did so. The armed men. The seven priests. The trumpets. The ark. The rereward. Once round. Silence. They returned into the camp.

The third day was the same. The fourth. The fifth. The sixth. Each day the procession went forth at the morning and returned into the camp or ever the evening. Each day the trumpets blew. Each day the people held their tongues. The city watched from above. Nothing was said. The walls were not touched. Within the city the men on the watch knew not what to make of it. They had been looking for an assault. The assault came not. Only the slow circuit, day after day, with the trumpets.

The seventh day they rose early about the dawning of the day. They went forth out of the camp as they had gone forth on every day before. They compassed the city the same manner they had compassed it six times. After the first circuit they returned not into the camp. They compassed it a second time. A third. A fourth. A fifth. A sixth. The watchmen on the walls saw them coming round again and again, and the silence held. The morning wore on. The sun rose higher. The procession kept walking. The trumpets kept blowing. The people kept their mouths shut.

The seventh time they compassed the city the priests blew with the trumpets with the long blast. The blast carried over the walls into the city. Joshua said unto the people: shout. For the LORD hath given you the city.

The people shouted with a great shout when the priests blew with the trumpets. The shout came up out of the host. The wall of the city fell down flat where they stood. The wall fell along the whole circuit they had walked. It cracked not first. It leaned not. It went down at once. The dust of the stones rose up over the country.

The people went up into the city, every man straight before him. They went up where the wall had been. They needed not ladders. They needed not ropes. The streets lay open unto them. The houses stood with their doors broken. The watchmen had been on the walls and now were not. The shock of the falling had thrown them down.

The city was given over according to the word of the LORD. Only Rahab and her household were spared, because she had hid the messengers that were come to spy. The scarlet cord yet hung from her window in the part of the wall that had been her house. Joshua had given the commandment or ever the shout that her house should be kept.

The procession that had been silent for six days was become a great noise on the seventh. The walls that had been high were a heap of stones. The country round about Jericho was as it had been before, save that the smoke now rose from the city itself, whence it had not risen for many days. The camp of Israel was come up close unto the place where the gates had been. The trumpets had ceased blowing. The morning went on across the plain and over the broken walls, and Israel was within the city for the first time.
