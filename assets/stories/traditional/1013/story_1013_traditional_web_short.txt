Early morning light touched the hills near the Jordan River. Tall grass bent gently in the breeze along the riverbank. Birds called to one another from the branches of willow trees. The water moved quickly, full and wide, stretching far from bank to bank. It was springtime, and the river had risen high with melting snow from the mountains.

Joshua stood among the people of Israel near the edge of the water. Thousands of families had gathered there with their children, their bundles, and their animals. Cooking fires sent thin lines of smoke into the cool air. For three days they had camped in this place, watching the wide river flow past.

Officers walked through the camp, speaking to each family. "When you see the priests carrying the ark of the covenant of the Lord your God," they said, "then follow after it." They told the people to leave space between themselves and the ark. Joshua spoke to the people too. "Come here and listen to the words of the Lord your God," he called out. He told them the Lord would do something wonderful among them. The living God was with them. Joshua said the ark of the covenant would go ahead of them into the Jordan.

The priests lifted the ark onto their shoulders. It was a wooden chest overlaid with gold. They carried it carefully, walking in steady steps toward the river. The people watched from behind, standing still. Dust rose softly under the priests' sandals as they walked the dry path down to the water's edge.

Then the priests' feet touched the river. The water was cold and moving fast. But as soon as their feet stepped in, the water began to change. Upstream, the flowing river stopped. It rose up and gathered in a heap, far away near a town called Adam. Downstream, the water kept flowing away toward the sea. And right where the priests stood, the riverbed appeared — dry ground, firm and solid beneath their feet.

The priests walked to the middle of the river and stood there, holding the ark. They did not move. The ground under them was dry.

Then the people began to cross. Families stepped onto the dry riverbed, one group after another. Children walked beside their parents. Sandals pressed into the smooth, firm ground where water had been only moments before. Goats and donkeys walked steadily across. Bundles of cloth and jars of grain were carried on shoulders and in carts. The sound of many footsteps filled the quiet riverbed.

All the people of Israel crossed over on dry ground. Every family, every child, every animal made it to the other side. The priests still stood in the middle, steady and still, the ark resting on their shoulders.

On the far bank, families set down their bundles. Children sat on the warm stones by the water's edge. The afternoon sun rested on their shoulders. Voices carried gently across the open air as people gathered together on the new side of the Jordan. The priests stood firm in the dry riverbed, and the people were safe on solid ground.
