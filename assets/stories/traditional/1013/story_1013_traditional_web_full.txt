Early morning light spread across the wide camp beside the Jordan River. Cooking fires sent thin lines of smoke into the cool air. Families sat together on blankets, sharing bread and dried fruit. Children ran between the tents, their sandals patting softly on the dusty ground. Oxen stood nearby, chewing slowly. Bundles of clothing and tools lay stacked beside wooden carts. The whole camp of Israel stretched out across the flat land, tent after tent after tent. Birds called from the bushes along the riverbank. A gentle breeze carried the smell of wood smoke and warm earth.

Joshua rose early that morning. He walked from Shittim through the rows of tents toward the river. The people followed, carrying their belongings on their backs and in their arms. Little ones held onto their parents' robes. Older children helped carry water jars. The whole group moved together, step by step, until they reached the edge of the Jordan. There they set up camp again and stayed for three days. During those three days, the people rested and waited. They cooked meals, mended sandals, and looked out at the wide river flowing past.

The Jordan was full. It was harvest time, and the water ran high and fast, spreading beyond its usual banks. The river rushed and tumbled, brown and swirling, wider than anyone could jump or wade across. Water splashed against rocks and pushed through the low bushes at the edges. The people could hear the steady sound of it moving, day and night.

After three days, officers walked through the camp. They moved from tent to tent, speaking clearly so everyone could hear. "When you see the ark of the covenant of the Lord your God," they said, "and the Levitical priests carrying it, then you are to move out from where you are and follow it." The officers told the people to stay about two thousand cubits behind the ark. "Do not come near it," they said, "so that you may know the way to go, since you have not passed this way before."

Joshua stood before the people. He spoke in a clear, steady voice. "Sanctify yourselves," he said, "for tomorrow the Lord will do wonders among you." The people listened. They prepared themselves and waited through the quiet night. Stars filled the wide sky above the camp. Crickets sang in the grass.

When morning came, Joshua spoke to the priests. "Take up the ark of the covenant," he said, "and pass over before the people." So the priests lifted the ark onto their shoulders. They walked forward, and the people watched them go, step by step, toward the riverbank.

Then the Lord spoke to Joshua. "Today I will begin to make you great in the sight of all Israel," the Lord said, "that they may know that as I was with Moses, so I will be with you." The Lord told Joshua what to say next. Joshua gathered the people close and spoke again. "Come here," he called, "and listen to the words of the Lord your God."

Joshua told them that the living God was among them. "By this you shall know," Joshua said, "that the living God is among you." He told them that the ark of the covenant of the Lord of all the earth would pass over ahead of them into the Jordan. Joshua asked twelve men to be chosen, one from each tribe of Israel.

Then Joshua said something wonderful. He told the people that when the feet of the priests carrying the ark touched the water of the Jordan, the waters flowing down from above would be cut off. They would stand up in one heap.

The people moved out from their tents. They walked forward, carrying their bundles and their children. The priests went ahead, the ark resting on their shoulders. Their robes swayed as they walked. Their sandals pressed into the soft ground near the river. The sound of rushing water grew louder with every step.

Then the priests' feet touched the edge of the water. Their toes dipped into the cold, flowing river. And the waters that came down from upstream stood still. They rose up in a heap, far away, near a town called Adam, beside Zarethan. The waters flowing down toward the sea of the Arabah were completely cut off. The riverbed appeared — wide, open ground, right there in front of everyone.

The priests carrying the ark walked forward into the middle of the riverbed. They stood still on dry ground, right in the center of the Jordan. Their feet rested on firm earth where water had been rushing just moments before. They held the ark steady on their shoulders and waited.

The people began to cross. Families walked together across the dry riverbed. Fathers carried small children on their shoulders. Mothers held bundles close. Older children walked beside their grandparents. Oxen pulled carts over the smooth ground. Sandals crunched on the dry river bottom. Voices murmured and called out softly. The whole nation of Israel passed by, one family after another, while the priests stood firm in the middle.

Above them, the sky was bright and open. On both sides, the banks of the Jordan rose gently. Ahead, the land of promise waited, green and wide. Every single person crossed over on dry ground. Not one foot splashed in water. The priests remained standing in the center of the riverbed, holding the ark, until every last person had walked safely to the other side.

On the far bank, families set down their bundles. Children sat on the warm ground. Parents stood together, looking back at the dry riverbed and the priests still standing there, steady and still. The sound of so many footsteps had faded into quiet. Dust settled softly in the air. A gentle wind moved across the open land. The people of Israel stood together on new ground, all of them safe and warm on the other side of the Jordan.
