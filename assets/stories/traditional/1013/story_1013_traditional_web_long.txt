Early morning light touched the hills near the Jordan River. Dew clung to the tall grass along the bank. Each blade bent gently under the weight of small water drops. Birds called to one another from the branches of willow trees. Their songs moved back and forth across the water. A soft breeze carried the smell of wet earth and green leaves. The river moved quickly, full and wide, stretching far from bank to bank. It was springtime, and melting snow from the mountains had raised the water high. Muddy currents pushed along sticks and leaves. The sound of the river was steady, like a low hum that never stopped.

Joshua stood among the people of Israel near the edge of the water. Thousands of families had gathered there with their children, their bundles, and their animals. Tents of brown and gray cloth dotted the hillside in every direction. Cooking fires sent thin lines of smoke into the cool air. The smell of bread baking on flat stones drifted between the tents. Children sat near the fires, eating small pieces of flatbread. Some of them watched the river from where they sat, chewing quietly. Dogs lay curled near tent poles. Goats pulled at patches of dry grass between the camp paths. Donkeys stood together in small groups, flicking their ears at flies. For three days the people had camped in this place, watching the wide river flow past.

Bundles of clothing and rolled mats leaned against tent walls. Clay jars of grain and oil sat in neat rows near each family's fire. Ropes held tent corners tight against the ground. Women folded blankets and stacked them. Men checked the straps on leather bags. Everyone moved with purpose, but no one was in a hurry. The camp was full of quiet, steady activity. Somewhere nearby, a baby made small sounds, and a mother's voice hummed softly in reply.

Officers walked through the camp that morning, stepping carefully between tent lines and fire circles. They stopped at each family group and spoke clearly. "When you see the priests carrying the ark of the covenant of the Lord your God," they said, "then follow after it." They told the people to leave space between themselves and the ark. "Do not come near it," they added. "Keep a distance between you and the ark." The officers repeated these words at every cluster of tents. People stood up from their morning meals to listen. They nodded and looked toward the center of the camp where the priests waited.

Joshua walked to a place where many people could see and hear him. He stood on a slight rise in the ground. His robe moved gently in the breeze. Dust covered his sandals from the dry paths of the camp. He lifted his voice so it carried across the crowd. "Come here and listen to the words of the Lord your God," he called out. People moved closer. Families gathered in wide half-circles around him. Parents held small children on their hips. Older children stood on their toes to see over the heads of grown-ups.

Joshua spoke steadily. He told them the living God was with them. He said the Lord would do something wonderful among them. The ark of the covenant would go ahead of them into the Jordan. Joshua's voice was clear and even. He did not rush his words. When he finished speaking, the people stood quietly for a moment. Then they began to move back toward their families and their belongings, preparing to walk.

The priests came forward from the center of the camp. There were several of them, walking together in a line. On their shoulders they carried the ark. It was a wooden chest overlaid with gold. The gold surface caught the morning light. Wooden poles ran along each side, resting on the priests' shoulders. Their hands gripped the poles firmly. They walked in steady, careful steps, keeping the ark level between them. Their robes brushed the tops of their sandals with each step.

The people watched from behind, standing still. No one walked ahead of the ark. A wide space opened between the priests and the crowd. Dust rose softly under the priests' sandals as they walked the dry path down toward the water's edge. Small stones shifted under their feet. The ground sloped gently downward. Grass grew thinner as they came closer to the muddy bank. The sound of the river grew louder with each step.

Then the priests reached the water. The first priest's foot pressed into the wet mud at the edge. Cold water moved over his toes and around his ankles. The current pushed against his legs. The second priest stepped in beside him. Their robes darkened where the water touched the cloth.

As soon as their feet entered the river, the water began to change. Upstream, the flowing current slowed. Then it stopped. The water gathered and rose up in a heap, far away near a town called Adam, beside a place called Zarethan. It piled up and held still, as if something unseen pressed against it. Downstream, the remaining water kept flowing away toward the Salt Sea. It drained and thinned and moved on. And right where the priests stood, the riverbed appeared. Wet stones showed first. Then patches of firm sand. Then wide stretches of dry ground, solid and smooth beneath their feet. The mud dried quickly. The ground became firm enough to walk on without slipping.

The priests walked forward into the middle of the riverbed. Their sandals left clear prints in the dry ground. They reached the center and stopped. They stood there, holding the ark on their shoulders. Their arms were steady. Their feet were planted. They did not move. The gold surface of the ark shone in the growing sunlight. On either side of the priests, the empty riverbed stretched wide and open. Smooth stones lay scattered across the bottom. Small puddles sat in low places but did not spread. The air smelled like wet rock and river clay.

Then the people began to cross. The first families stepped down from the bank onto the dry riverbed. Their sandals pressed into the smooth, firm ground where water had been only moments before. Parents walked with children close beside them. A mother carried a small child on one hip and a cloth bundle on the other. A father led a donkey by a rope. The donkey's hooves clicked softly on the flat stones. Goats walked in uneven lines, their small hooves tapping the ground. An older boy guided three goats with a stick, tapping the earth beside them to keep them moving forward.

More families followed. The line of people stretched far back up the bank and into the camp. Group after group stepped down onto the riverbed. Grandmothers walked slowly, steadied by the arms of younger women. Grandfathers carried bundles of rolled cloth on their backs. Jars of grain and oil were balanced on heads and shoulders. Wooden carts with solid wheels rolled across the dry ground, pulled by oxen. The wheels left shallow tracks in the firm sand. Children looked around as they walked. Some of them reached down and touched the dry stones with their fingers. The stones were cool and still damp in places.

The sound of many footsteps filled the quiet riverbed. Sandals shuffled. Hooves tapped. Cart wheels creaked. Voices murmured softly as families called to one another. "Stay close." "This way." "Hold my hand." A young girl walked beside her grandmother, matching her short steps. A boy carried a small clay pot carefully in both hands, watching his feet as he crossed. A woman shifted a sleeping baby from one arm to the other without stopping.

All the people of Israel crossed over on dry ground. Every family, every child, every animal made it to the other side. The line of people kept moving steadily, group after group, until the last family stepped off the riverbed and onto the far bank. No one stumbled. No one was left behind. The priests still stood in the middle, steady and still, the ark resting on their shoulders. They had not moved from that spot the entire time.

On the far bank, families set down their bundles on the warm ground. Women unrolled mats and spread them flat. Men stacked jars of grain beside the mats. Children sat on smooth stones near the water's edge. Some of them dangled their feet over the bank. A boy picked up a small round stone and turned it over in his hands. A girl leaned against her mother's side. Goats wandered between the resting families, nibbling at tufts of new grass. Donkeys stood still, their heads low, resting after the crossing.

The afternoon sun rested on their shoulders. Warm air carried the smell of dry grass and river clay. Voices drifted gently across the open space as people gathered together on the new side of the Jordan. Someone laughed softly. A child called out to a friend. Cooking fires began to appear again, small and bright, with thin smoke rising straight up in the still air.

The priests stood firm in the dry riverbed behind them, the ark steady on their shoulders. And all the people were safe on solid ground, together on the far side of the river.
