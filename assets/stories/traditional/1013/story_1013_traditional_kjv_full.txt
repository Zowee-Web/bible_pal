Early morning light spread across the wide camp beside the river Jordan. Cooking fires sent up thin lines of smoke into the cool air. Households sat together upon their blankets, sharing bread and dried fruits. Little ones ran between the tents, their sandals patting softly upon the dusty ground. Oxen stood nigh, chewing slowly. Bundles of raiment and tools lay heaped beside wooden carts. The whole camp of Israel stretched out across the level land, tent after tent after tent. Birds called from the bushes along the riverbank. A gentle breeze bare the savour of wood smoke and warm earth.

Joshua arose early that morning. He went from Shittim through the rows of tents toward the river. The people followed after, bearing their goods upon their backs and in their arms. The little ones held fast unto the robes of their parents. The elder children helped to bear the water jars. The whole company moved together, step by step, until they came unto the brink of Jordan. There they pitched their tents again and abode for three days. In those three days the people rested and tarried. They prepared their meals, mended their sandals, and beheld the wide river flowing past.

Jordan was overflowed. It was the time of harvest, and the waters ran high and swift, spreading beyond their accustomed banks. The river rushed and tumbled, brown and swirling, wider than any man could leap over or wade across. Waters dashed against the stones and pressed through the low bushes at the brink. The people could hear the steady sound thereof moving, day and night.

After three days, the officers went through the camp. They moved from tent to tent, speaking plainly that every man might hear. "When ye see the ark of the covenant of the Lord your God," they said, "and the priests the Levites bearing it, then ye shall remove from your place, and go after it." The officers charged the people to keep about two thousand cubits behind the ark. "Come not near unto it," they said, "that ye may know the way by which ye must go: for ye have not passed this way heretofore."

Joshua stood before the people. He spake with a clear and steady voice. "Sanctify yourselves," he said, "for to morrow the Lord will do wonders among you." The people gave ear. They prepared themselves and waited through the quiet night. Stars filled the wide sky above the camp. Crickets sang in the grass.

When the morning came, Joshua spake unto the priests. "Take up the ark of the covenant," he said, "and pass over before the people." So the priests took up the ark upon their shoulders. They went forward, and the people beheld them as they went, step by step, toward the riverbank.

Then the Lord spake unto Joshua. "This day will I begin to magnify thee in the sight of all Israel," the Lord said, "that they may know that as I was with Moses, so I will be with thee." The Lord told Joshua what he should say next. Joshua gathered the people close and spake again. "Come hither," he called, "and hear the words of the Lord your God."

Joshua told them that the living God was among them. "Hereby ye shall know," Joshua said, "that the living God is among you." He told them that the ark of the covenant of the Lord of all the earth should pass over before them into Jordan. Joshua bade twelve men be chosen, of every tribe of Israel a man.

Then Joshua said a wondrous thing. He told the people that when the soles of the feet of the priests bearing the ark should rest in the waters of Jordan, the waters of Jordan should be cut off from the waters that came down from above. They should stand upon an heap.

The people removed from their tents. They went forward, bearing their bundles and their little ones. The priests went before, the ark resting upon their shoulders. Their robes swayed as they walked. Their sandals pressed into the soft ground nigh the river. The sound of the rushing waters grew louder with every step.

Then the feet of the priests were dipped in the brim of the water. Their toes touched the cold, flowing river. And the waters that came down from above stood still. They rose up upon an heap, very far from the city Adam, beside Zaretan. The waters that came down toward the sea of the plain were wholly cut off. The riverbed appeared — wide, open ground, right there before all the people.

The priests bearing the ark went forward into the midst of the riverbed. They stood firm on dry ground, even in the midst of Jordan. Their feet rested upon firm earth where the waters had been rushing but moments before. They held the ark steady upon their shoulders and tarried there.

The people began to pass over. Households walked together across the dry riverbed. Fathers bare little ones upon their shoulders. Mothers held bundles close. The elder children walked beside their grandsires. Oxen drew carts over the smooth ground. Sandals crunched upon the dry bottom of the river. Voices murmured and called softly one to another. The whole nation of Israel passed by, household after household, while the priests stood firm in the midst.

Above them, the heavens were bright and open. On both sides, the banks of Jordan rose gently. Before them, the land of promise tarried, green and wide. Every soul passed clean over upon dry ground. Not one foot was dipped in the waters. The priests remained standing in the midst of the riverbed, bearing the ark, until every last man had passed safely unto the other side.

Upon the far bank, the households laid down their bundles. Little ones sat upon the warm ground. Parents stood together, looking back upon the dry riverbed and the priests yet standing there, steady and still. The sound of so many footsteps had faded into quiet. Dust settled softly in the air. A gentle wind passed across the open land. The people of Israel stood together upon new ground, all of them safe and warm upon the other side of Jordan.
