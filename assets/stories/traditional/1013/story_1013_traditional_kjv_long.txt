Early morning light touched the hills nigh unto the river Jordan. The dew clave unto the tall grass along the bank. Each blade bent gently under the weight of small drops of water. Birds called one to another from the branches of the willow trees. Their songs passed to and fro across the water. A soft breeze bare the savour of wet earth and green leaves. The river ran swiftly, full and wide, stretching far from bank to bank. It was springtime, and the melting snows of the mountains had lifted the waters high. Muddy currents pressed along sticks and leaves. The sound of the river was steady, as a low hum that never ceased.

Joshua stood among the people of Israel nigh unto the edge of the water. Thousands of households were gathered there with their little ones, their bundles, and their cattle. Tents of brown and gray cloth were spread across the hillside in every direction. Cooking fires sent up thin lines of smoke into the cool air. The savour of bread baking upon flat stones drifted between the tents. Children sat near the fires, eating small pieces of flatbread. Some of them beheld the river from where they sat, chewing quietly. Dogs lay curled near the tent poles. Goats pulled at patches of dry grass between the paths of the camp. Asses stood together in small companies, flicking their ears at the flies. For three days had the people encamped in that place, beholding the wide river as it flowed past.

Bundles of raiment and rolled mats leaned against the tent walls. Vessels of clay full of grain and oil sat in neat rows nigh each household's fire. Cords held the tent corners fast against the ground. The women folded blankets and stacked them. The men examined the straps upon the leathern bags. Every one moved with purpose, but no man made haste. The camp was full of quiet, steady labour. Somewhere nigh, a babe made small sounds, and the voice of a mother hummed softly in answer.

The officers passed through the camp that morning, stepping carefully between the tent lines and fire circles. They stayed at every household and spake plainly. "When ye behold the priests bearing the ark of the covenant of the Lord your God," they said, "then ye shall remove from your place, and go after it." They charged the people to leave a space betwixt themselves and the ark. "Come not near unto it," they added. "Keep a distance betwixt you and the ark." The officers repeated these words at every cluster of tents. The people arose from their morning meals to give ear. They nodded and looked toward the midst of the camp where the priests waited.

Joshua walked unto a place where many people might see and hear him. He stood upon a slight rise in the ground. His robe moved gently in the breeze. Dust covered his sandals from the dry paths of the camp. He lifted up his voice that it might carry across the multitude. "Come hither," he called out, "and hear the words of the Lord your God." The people drew nearer. Households gathered in wide half-circles round about him. Parents held little ones upon their hips. The elder children stood upon their toes that they might see over the heads of the grown.

Joshua spake steadily. He told them the living God was among them. He said the Lord would do wonders among them. The ark of the covenant should go before them into Jordan. The voice of Joshua was clear and even. He hastened not his words. When he had made an end of speaking, the people stood quietly for a moment. Then they began to turn back unto their households and their goods, preparing to walk.

The priests came forth from the midst of the camp. There were several of them, walking together in a line. Upon their shoulders they bare the ark. It was a chest of wood overlaid with gold. The gold caught the morning light. Staves of wood ran along each side, resting upon the shoulders of the priests. Their hands held the staves firmly. They walked with steady, careful steps, keeping the ark level betwixt them. Their robes brushed the tops of their sandals with every step.

The people beheld from behind, standing still. No man went before the ark. A wide space opened betwixt the priests and the multitude. Dust rose softly beneath the sandals of the priests as they walked the dry path down toward the water's edge. Small stones shifted under their feet. The ground sloped gently downward. The grass grew thinner as they came nigher to the muddy bank. The sound of the river grew louder with every step.

Then the priests came unto the water. The foot of the first priest pressed into the wet mire at the brink. Cold water passed over his toes and round his ankles. The current pressed against his legs. The second priest stepped in beside him. Their robes were darkened where the water touched the cloth.

As soon as their feet were dipped in the brim, the waters began to change. From above, the flowing current slowed. Then it stayed. The waters gathered and rose up upon an heap, very far from the city Adam, beside the place called Zaretan. They piled up and held still, as though some unseen hand pressed against them. From beneath, the remaining waters went down toward the salt sea. They drained and thinned and passed on. And there where the priests stood, the riverbed appeared. Wet stones showed first. Then patches of firm sand. Then wide stretches of dry ground, solid and smooth beneath their feet. The mire dried quickly. The ground became firm enough to walk upon without slipping.

The priests went forward into the midst of the riverbed. Their sandals left clear prints in the dry ground. They came unto the midst and stood still. They tarried there, bearing the ark upon their shoulders. Their arms were steady. Their feet were planted. They moved not. The gold of the ark shone in the growing sunlight. On either side of the priests, the empty riverbed stretched wide and open. Smooth stones lay scattered across the bottom. Small pools sat in the low places but spread not. The air bore the savour of wet rock and river clay.

Then the people began to pass over. The first households stepped down from the bank upon the dry riverbed. Their sandals pressed into the smooth, firm ground where waters had been but moments before. Parents walked with little ones close beside them. A mother bare a small child upon one hip and a cloth bundle upon the other. A father led an ass by a cord. The hoofs of the ass clicked softly upon the flat stones. Goats walked in uneven lines, their small hoofs tapping the ground. An elder boy guided three goats with a staff, tapping the earth beside them to keep them moving forward.

More households followed. The line of people stretched far back up the bank and into the camp. Company after company stepped down upon the riverbed. Grandmothers walked slowly, steadied by the arms of the younger women. Grandsires bare bundles of rolled cloth upon their backs. Jars of grain and oil were balanced upon heads and shoulders. Wooden carts with solid wheels rolled across the dry ground, drawn by oxen. The wheels left shallow tracks in the firm sand. Children looked about as they walked. Some of them reached down and touched the dry stones with their fingers. The stones were cool and yet damp in places.

The sound of many footsteps filled the quiet riverbed. Sandals shuffled. Hoofs tapped. Cart wheels creaked. Voices murmured softly as households called one to another. "Keep nigh." "This way." "Take my hand." A young maiden walked beside her grandmother, matching her short steps. A boy bare a small pot of clay carefully in both hands, watching his feet as he passed over. A woman shifted a sleeping babe from one arm to the other without stopping.

All the people of Israel passed clean over upon dry ground. Every household, every child, every beast came unto the other side. The line of people kept moving steadily, company after company, until the last household stepped off the riverbed and upon the far bank. No man stumbled. No man was left behind. The priests stood yet in the midst, steady and still, the ark resting upon their shoulders. They had not moved from that place the whole time.

Upon the far bank, the households laid down their bundles upon the warm ground. The women unrolled mats and spread them flat. The men stacked jars of grain beside the mats. Children sat upon smooth stones near the water's edge. Some of them dangled their feet over the bank. A boy picked up a small round stone and turned it over in his hands. A maiden leaned against the side of her mother. Goats wandered between the resting households, nibbling at tufts of new grass. Asses stood still, their heads low, resting after the crossing.

The afternoon sun rested upon their shoulders. Warm air bare the savour of dry grass and river clay. Voices drifted gently across the open space as the people gathered together upon the new side of Jordan. Some one laughed softly. A child called out unto a friend. Cooking fires began to appear again, small and bright, with thin smoke rising straight up in the still air.

The priests stood firm in the dry riverbed behind them, the ark steady upon their shoulders. And all the people were safe upon solid ground, together upon the far side of the river.
