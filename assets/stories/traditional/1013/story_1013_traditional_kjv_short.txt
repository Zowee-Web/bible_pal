Early morning light touched the hills nigh unto the river Jordan. Tall grass bent gently in the breeze along the riverbank. Birds called one to another from the branches of the willow trees. The waters ran swiftly, full and wide, stretching far from bank to bank. It was springtime, and the river was risen high with the melting snows of the mountains.

Joshua stood among the people of Israel nigh unto the edge of the water. Thousands of households were gathered there with their little ones, their bundles, and their cattle. Cooking fires sent up thin lines of smoke into the cool air. For three days had they encamped in that place, beholding the wide river as it flowed past.

The officers passed through the camp, speaking unto every household. "When ye behold the priests bearing the ark of the covenant of the Lord your God," they said, "then ye shall remove from your place, and go after it." They charged the people to leave a space betwixt themselves and the ark. Joshua also spake unto the people. "Come hither," he called out, "and hear the words of the Lord your God." He told them that the Lord would do wonders among them. The living God was in the midst of them. Joshua said that the ark of the covenant should go before them into Jordan.

The priests lifted up the ark upon their shoulders. It was a chest of wood overlaid with gold. They bare it carefully, walking with steady steps unto the river. The people beheld from behind, standing still. Dust rose softly from beneath the priests' sandals as they walked the dry path down unto the water's edge.

Then the feet of the priests touched the river. The water was cold and ran swiftly. But as soon as their feet were dipped in the brim, the waters began to change. From above, the flowing river stayed. It rose up upon an heap, very far from the city Adam. From beneath, the waters went down toward the sea. And there where the priests stood, the riverbed appeared — dry ground, firm and solid beneath their feet.

The priests went forth into the midst of Jordan and stood there, bearing the ark. They moved not. The ground beneath them was dry.

Then the people began to pass over. Households stepped down upon the dry riverbed, one company after another. Little ones walked beside their parents. Sandals pressed into the smooth, firm ground where waters had been but moments before. Goats and asses passed steadily over. Bundles of cloth and jars of grain were borne upon shoulders and in carts. The sound of many footsteps filled the quiet riverbed.

All the people of Israel passed clean over upon dry ground. Every household, every child, every beast came unto the other side. The priests stood yet in the midst, steady and still, the ark resting upon their shoulders.

Upon the far bank, the households laid down their bundles. Children sat upon the warm stones by the water's edge. The afternoon sun rested upon their shoulders. Voices were borne gently across the open air as the people gathered together upon the new side of Jordan. The priests stood firm in the dry riverbed, and the people were safe upon solid ground.
