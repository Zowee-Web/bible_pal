David and his men came up to Ziklag on the third day from whence they had been turned back, and Ziklag was burned with fire. The Amalekites had invaded the south while the men had been away. The houses had been put to the flame. The smoke was yet rising from certain of the timbers as David and his men entered into what had been their city.

The wives were gone. The sons and the daughters had been taken. There was none of the slain left in the ashes — neither man nor woman wounded — only the silence of a place from whence the people had been carried away.

David lifted up his voice and wept. The men with him wept also. They wept until they had no more power to weep, and they sat down upon the broken ground in the ashes, and they could not stir.

After a while one of them spake against David. Another took up the word. A bitterness was rising. They began to speak of stoning him, every man because of his sons and his daughters that had been carried away. David heard them speaking. He sat alone among them in the ashes.

He encouraged himself in the LORD his God.

That is what is written — and the writing is sparing of what it meaneth. He arose not at once. He called not unto the men. He sat where he had sat. Something steadied within him in the silence between the words spoken against him and the words he should speak back.

When he had encouraged himself, he called for the ephod. The priest brought it unto him. David inquired: "Shall I pursue after this troop? shall I overtake them?"

The answer came: "Pursue: for thou shalt surely overtake them, and without fail recover all."

David arose and went forth. The men that were with him arose and followed him. The country before them was the country whither the troop had gone.
