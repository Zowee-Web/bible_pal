David and his men came up to Ziklag on the third day from where they had been turned back, and Ziklag had been burned. The Amalekites had come up against the south country while the men had been gone. The houses had been put to the fire. The smoke was still rising from some of the timbers as David and his men walked into what had been their city.

The wives were gone. The sons and the daughters had been taken. Not a body was left in the ash — no slain, no one wounded — only the silence of a place where the people had been carried away.

David lifted up his voice and wept. The men around him wept also. They wept until they had no more strength to weep, and they sat down on the broken ground in the ash, and they could not move.

After a while one of them spoke against David. Another took up the word. A bitterness was rising. They began to talk of stoning him, every man for his sons and his daughters that had been carried away. David heard them speaking. He sat alone among them in the ashes.

He encouraged himself in the LORD his God.

That is what the chronicle says — and the chronicle is sparing on what it means. He did not get up at once. He did not call to the men. He sat where he had sat. Something steadied in him in the silence between the words spoken against him and the words he would speak back.

When he had encouraged himself, he called for the ephod. The priest brought it to him. David asked: "Shall I pursue after this troop? Shall I overtake them?"

The answer came: "Pursue: for you shall surely overtake them, and without fail recover all."

David got up and went out. The men with him got up and followed. The country before them was the country into which the troop had gone.
