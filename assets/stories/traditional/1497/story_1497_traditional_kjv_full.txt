David and his men were three days on the road back from whence Achish had sent them. They were turned out of the army of the Philistines at Aphek. The lords of the Philistines had not trusted David among them when the battle was setting in array. He had reasoned with Achish. Achish had let him go. They marched south, six hundred men with their weapons and their dust.

They came up to Ziklag on the third day. The city was their city. David had been given it by Achish a year and four months before. Their wives and their children dwelt there. Their goods were there.

Ziklag was burned with fire.

The Amalekites had invaded the south while the men had been away. They had come into Ziklag and they had taken it. They had not slain a man. They had carried the women and the children away. The houses had been put to the flame. The smoke was yet rising from certain of the timbers as David and his men entered into what had been their city.

The wives were gone. The sons and the daughters had been taken. There was none of the slain left in the ashes — neither man nor woman wounded — only the silence of a place from whence the people had been carried away.

David lifted up his voice and wept. The men with him wept also. They wept until they had no more power to weep. They sat down upon the broken ground in the ashes. They could not stir. The sun went over them as they sat.

After a while one of them spake against David. Another took up the word. A bitterness was rising among them. They began to speak of stoning him, every man because of his sons and his daughters that had been carried away. They had been with him in the wilderness for years. They had eaten what he had eaten. They had hidden in caves with him. Now they could not bear the weight of what had befallen, and the bitter word turned in them and turned again.

David heard them speaking. He sat alone among them in the ashes. He answered them not. He arose not.

He encouraged himself in the LORD his God.

That is what is written — and the writing is sparing of what it meaneth. He arose not at once. He called not unto the men. He sat where he had sat. Something steadied within him in the silence between the words spoken against him and the words he should speak back.

When he had encouraged himself, he called for the ephod. He called for Abiathar the priest. Abiathar brought the ephod unto him. David inquired: "Shall I pursue after this troop? Shall I overtake them?"

The answer came: "Pursue. For thou shalt surely overtake them. Without fail thou shalt recover all."

David arose. He went forth. The men that were with him arose and followed him. The country before them was the country whither the troop had gone. The ashes of the city were behind them. The day was wearing on. They went after the Amalekites with the strength that was come back into them, and the bitterness that had been about to break the company was put away.
