Early in the wilderness, under the hot sun, the Israelites drew near to Moses. Day after day, from morning until evening, they stood in long lines, their faces marked by waiting and worry. The dust hung low in the air, and voices murmured as each person hoped to have their case heard. At the center of this camp, Moses sat, listening to every dispute, his voice growing tired as he judged between neighbor and neighbor.

All this, Jethro observed. Moses’ father-in-law had come to the camp and watched as the leader of the people wore himself thin. Jethro saw the waiting crowds and the unbroken labor of Moses, whose shoulders sagged under the weight of so many burdens. When evening settled, and the last seekers found answers, Moses finally rose, drained by the work.

The next morning, Jethro approached Moses. He saw again the endless stream of people standing before his son-in-law, the air already growing warm. Jethro spoke plainly. “What is this thing that you are doing for the people? Why do you sit alone, while all the people stand around you from morning until evening?”

Moses answered, his voice carrying exhaustion. “Because the people come to me to inquire of God. When they have a matter, they come to me, and I judge between a man and his neighbor. I make them know the statutes of God, and his laws.”

Jethro listened. Then he gave counsel. “What you are doing is not good. You will surely wear yourself out, both you and these people with you. The task is too heavy for you. You cannot do it alone.”

He did not rebuke, but spoke as an elder who had walked many hard roads. “Listen now to my voice, and I will give you advice, and God be with you. You represent the people before God, and you bring their causes to Him. You teach them the statutes and laws, and show them the way they must walk. But select capable men from all the people—men who fear God, trustworthy men who hate unjust gain—and set them as officials over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens. Let them judge the people at all times. Every great matter they shall bring to you, but every small matter they shall judge themselves. So it will be easier for you, and they will share the load with you. If you do this, as God commands, you will endure, and all these people also will go to their place in peace.”

Moses listened. The weight of the day seemed lighter as he heard wise counsel. He chose able men from all Israel and set them as heads over the people—leaders of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens. The long lines grew shorter. The cases were shared among many, and relief passed through the camp.

They judged the people at all times. The hard cases they brought to Moses, but every small matter they judged themselves.

Moses let his father-in-law depart, and he went his way into his own land.
