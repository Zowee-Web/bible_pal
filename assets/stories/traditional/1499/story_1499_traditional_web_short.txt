Hanani came up from Judah in the winter, in the ninth month, and Nehemiah received him in the citadel of Shushan where Nehemiah served the king. Nehemiah asked him about the Jews that had escaped and were left of the captivity, and about Jerusalem.

Hanani answered him. The remnant in the province were in great affliction and reproach. The wall of Jerusalem was broken down. The gates were burned with fire.

When Nehemiah heard these words, he sat down. He did not get up. He wept where he was. He mourned for days, and he could not eat in the way he had eaten, and he could not lift the cup to the king with the steadiness he had carried before he heard. The wine was set out. He set it back. He turned his face to the wall.

He prayed to the God of heaven. "O LORD God of heaven, the great and terrible God who keeps covenant and lovingkindness with those who love him and keep his commandments — let your ear now be attentive, and your eyes open, that you may hear the prayer of your servant which I pray before you at this time, day and night."

He confessed the sins of his people. He spoke of the word the LORD had commanded to Moses: that if they returned, the LORD would bring them again. He named the city he had not yet seen, the city to which his fathers had belonged.

"O Lord, I beseech you," he said at the end of it. "Let your ear now be attentive to the prayer of your servant, and to the prayer of your servants who delight to fear your name. Prosper your servant this day. Grant him mercy in the sight of this man."

He did not say the name of the man. He did not need to. He was the king's cupbearer. The cup he had set aside still stood on the table. The wine had gone dim in the bowl while he wept.

He rose at last from where he had been sitting. The window of the chamber looked out across the citadel toward where Jerusalem lay, far west, beyond the rivers, in the place he could not see.
