Hanani came up out of Judah in the winter, in the ninth month, in the cold of Chisleu. He had been on the road many days. The dust of the road was upon his sandals. He came into the palace of Shushan wherein Nehemiah served the king. Nehemiah received him in his own chamber, apart from the noise of the court.

Nehemiah asked him first concerning the Jews that had escaped, which were left of the captivity. He asked next concerning the city.

Hanani answered him. The remnant that were left of the captivity there in the province were in great affliction and reproach. The wall of Jerusalem was broken down. The gates thereof were burned with fire. Many years had passed. Nothing had been built up again.

When Nehemiah heard these words, he sat him down. He arose not. He wept where he was. He covered his face with the sleeve of his garment. He spake not.

Hanani tarried with him a while. Then he went out and left Nehemiah alone in the chamber.

Nehemiah mourned certain days. He could not eat as he had eaten. He could not lift the cup unto the king with the steadiness he had carried before he heard. The wine was set forth at the king's table. He put it back. He turned his face unto the wall when the others were speaking. He sat alone in his own room in the evenings and could not be reached.

He prayed unto the God of heaven. "O LORD God of heaven, the great and terrible God, that keepeth covenant and mercy for them that love him and observe his commandments — let thine ear now be attentive, and thine eyes open, that thou mayest hear the prayer of thy servant, which I pray before thee now, day and night, for the children of Israel thy servants."

He confessed the sins of the children of Israel. He confessed his own sin and the sin of his father's house. He named what they had done against the LORD. He named the commandments that had not been kept.

Then he remembered the word which the LORD had commanded his servant Moses. If they transgressed, the LORD had said, he would scatter them among the nations. But if they turned unto him, and kept his commandments, the LORD would gather them from the uttermost parts of heaven, and bring them unto the place that he had chosen to set his name there. Nehemiah said this word back unto the LORD.

"O Lord, I beseech thee," he said at the end of it. "Let thine ear now be attentive to the prayer of thy servant, and to the prayer of thy servants, who desire to fear thy name. Prosper, I pray thee, thy servant this day. Grant him mercy in the sight of this man."

He named not the man. He needed not. He was the king's cupbearer. The cup he had set aside stood yet upon the table beside him. The wine was grown dim in the bowl while he wept and while he prayed.

He arose at last from where he had been sitting. The window of the chamber looked out across the palace toward where Jerusalem lay, far west, beyond the rivers. The land he could not see was very dark in his mind. The wall that was broken down was very vivid in him. He stood at the window a long while.

Then he turned and went unto the king's chamber for the work of the morning.
