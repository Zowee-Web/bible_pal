Hanani came up out of Judah in the winter, in the ninth month, and Nehemiah received him in the palace of Shushan wherein Nehemiah served the king. Nehemiah asked him concerning the Jews that had escaped, which were left of the captivity, and concerning Jerusalem.

Hanani answered him. The remnant that were left of the captivity there in the province were in great affliction and reproach. The wall of Jerusalem was broken down. The gates thereof were burned with fire.

When Nehemiah heard these words, he sat him down. He arose not. He wept where he was. He mourned certain days, and he could not eat as he had eaten, and he could not lift the cup unto the king with the steadiness he had carried before he heard. The wine was set forth. He put it back. He turned his face unto the wall.

He prayed unto the God of heaven. "O LORD God of heaven, the great and terrible God, that keepeth covenant and mercy for them that love him and observe his commandments — let thine ear now be attentive, and thine eyes open, that thou mayest hear the prayer of thy servant, which I pray before thee now, day and night."

He confessed the sins of his people. He spake of the word which the LORD had commanded his servant Moses, that if they returned, the LORD would gather them again. He named the city he had not yet seen, the city wherein his fathers had dwelt.

"O Lord, I beseech thee," he said at the end of it. "Let thine ear now be attentive to the prayer of thy servant, and to the prayer of thy servants, who desire to fear thy name. Prosper, I pray thee, thy servant this day. Grant him mercy in the sight of this man."

He named not the man. He needed not. He was the king's cupbearer. The cup he had set aside stood yet upon the table. The wine was grown dim in the bowl while he wept.

He arose at last from where he had been sitting. The window of the chamber looked out across the palace toward where Jerusalem lay, far west, beyond the rivers, in the place he could not see.
