Hanani came up from Judah in the winter, in the ninth month, in the cold of Chislev. He had been on the road many days. The dust of the road was on his sandals. He came into the citadel of Shushan where Nehemiah served the king. Nehemiah received him in his own chamber, away from the noise of the court.

Nehemiah asked him first about the Jews that had escaped, the ones who were left after the captivity. He asked next about the city.

Hanani answered him. The remnant in the province were in great affliction and reproach. The wall of Jerusalem was broken down. The gates were burned with fire. Many years had passed. Nothing had been built up again.

When Nehemiah heard these words, he sat down. He did not get up. He wept where he was. He covered his face with the sleeve of his garment. He did not speak.

Hanani stayed with him for a while. Then he went out and left Nehemiah alone in the chamber.

Nehemiah mourned for days. He could not eat in the way he had eaten. He could not lift the cup to the king with the steadiness he had carried before he heard. The wine was set out at the king's table. He set it back. He turned his face to the wall when the others were speaking. He sat alone in his own room in the evenings and could not be reached.

He prayed to the God of heaven. "O LORD God of heaven, the great and terrible God who keeps covenant and lovingkindness with those who love him and keep his commandments — let your ear now be attentive, and your eyes open, that you may hear the prayer of your servant which I pray before you at this time, day and night, for the children of Israel your servants."

He confessed the sins of the children of Israel. He confessed his own sin and the sin of his father's house. He named what they had done against the LORD. He named the commandments that had not been kept.

Then he remembered the word the LORD had spoken to Moses. If they transgressed, the LORD had said, he would scatter them among the nations. But if they turned unto him, and kept his commandments, the LORD would gather them from the uttermost parts of heaven, and bring them to the place where he had set his name. Nehemiah said this word back to the LORD.

"O Lord, I beseech you," he said at the end of it. "Let your ear be attentive to the prayer of your servant, and to the prayer of your servants who delight to fear your name. Prosper your servant this day. Grant him mercy in the sight of this man."

He did not say the name of the man. He did not need to. He was the king's cupbearer. The cup he had set aside still stood on the table beside him. The wine had gone dim in the bowl while he wept and while he prayed.

He rose at last from where he had been sitting. The window of the chamber looked out across the citadel toward where Jerusalem lay, far west, beyond the rivers. The country he could not see was very dark in his mind. The wall that was broken down was very vivid in him. He stood at the window a long while.

Then he turned and went to the king's chamber for the work of the morning.
