The people had gathered in the open square before the Water Gate. Ezra had stood upon a high pulpit of wood made for the purpose, and the Levites had stood around him; and Ezra had opened the book of the Law of Moses, and the men and women and all the children who could understand had stood up. He had read from the morning until midday. The Levites had given the sense of it as it was read, so that the people understood. And when the people heard the words of the Law — words that they had not heard read aloud since before the carrying away to Babylon — they had begun to weep.

Nehemiah, who was the governor, and Ezra the priest and scribe, and the Levites who taught the people, said to all the people, "Today is holy to Yahweh your God. Don't mourn, nor weep." For all the people wept when they heard the words of the law.

Then he said to them, "Go your way. Eat the fat, drink the sweet, and send portions to him for whom nothing is prepared, for today is holy to our Lord. Don't be grieved, for the joy of Yahweh is your strength."

So the Levites calmed all the people, saying, "Hold your peace, for the day is holy. Don't be grieved."

All the people went their way to eat, to drink, to send portions, and to celebrate, because they had understood the words that were declared to them.

And the square before the Water Gate began to empty. The men and women went up into their houses and brought down the food they had been keeping; the portions for those who had nothing were carried through the streets to the doors of the poor; and the day that had begun with weeping went on into the evening with the sound of eating and the calling out of neighbor to neighbor across the courts. The joy of the Lord had been declared to them as their strength, and they had taken it for themselves.
