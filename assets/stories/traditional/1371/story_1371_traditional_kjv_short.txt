The people had gathered in the open square before the Water Gate. Ezra had stood upon a high pulpit of wood made for the purpose, and the Levites had stood about him; and Ezra had opened the book of the Law of Moses, and the men and women and all the children who could understand had arisen. He had read from the morning until midday. The Levites had given the sense of it as it was read, that the people might understand. And when the people heard the words of the Law — words that they had not heard read aloud since before the carrying away unto Babylon — they had begun to weep.

Nehemiah, which is the Tirshatha, and Ezra the priest the scribe, and the Levites that taught the people, said unto all the people, "This day is holy unto the Lord your God; mourn not, nor weep." For all the people wept, when they heard the words of the law.

Then he said unto them, "Go your way, eat the fat, and drink the sweet, and send portions unto them for whom nothing is prepared: for this day is holy unto our Lord: neither be ye sorry; for the joy of the Lord is your strength."

So the Levites stilled all the people, saying, "Hold your peace, for the day is holy; neither be ye grieved."

And all the people went their way to eat, and to drink, and to send portions, and to make great mirth, because they had understood the words that were declared unto them.

And the square before the Water Gate began to empty. The men and women went up unto their houses and brought down the food they had been keeping; the portions for them that had nothing were carried through the streets unto the doors of the poor; and the day that had begun with weeping went on into the evening with the sound of eating and the calling out of neighbour unto neighbour across the courts. The joy of the Lord had been declared unto them as their strength, and they had taken it unto themselves.
