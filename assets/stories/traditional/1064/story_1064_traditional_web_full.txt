The king of Syria was making war against Israel. He counseled with his servants and chose his positions carefully, setting ambushes along the roads where Israelite troops would pass.

But each time, before the trap could close, word came to the king of Israel from the prophet Elisha. "Beware. Do not pass by that place. The Syrians have come down there."

The king of Israel sent men to check. It was true. He avoided the place.

This happened not once or twice. It happened again and again.

The king of Syria was greatly troubled. He gathered his servants. His voice was sharp. "Will you not show me which of us is for the king of Israel? Who is the spy?"

One of his servants answered. "None, my lord, O king. But Elisha, the prophet who is in Israel, tells the king of Israel the words that you speak in your bedroom."

The king said, "Go and find where he is, that I may send and seize him."

The report came back. "He is in Dothan."

So the king sent horses and chariots and a great army. They traveled by night. By the time the stars began to fade, they had circled the city of Dothan. The ring was complete. No one could leave.

Elisha's servant rose early. The air was still cool when he stepped outside. He stopped. All around the city walls, the Syrian army waited. Horses stamped in the dust. Iron-rimmed chariot wheels caught the first light. Spear points bristled above the ranks of soldiers.

The servant ran back. His voice shook. "Alas, my master! What shall we do?"

Elisha's voice was steady. "Do not be afraid. Those who are with us are more than those who are with them."

The servant stared at him. Two men in a house. A foreign army outside the walls.

Then Elisha prayed. "O Lord, please open his eyes that he may see."

The Lord opened the eyes of the young man. He looked out again at the hills surrounding Dothan. His breath caught. The mountains were covered — horses and chariots of fire, blazing in ranks across the slopes, filling every ridge, stationed all around Elisha. The light from them pulsed against the morning sky.

The Syrian soldiers began to advance toward the city. Elisha walked out to meet them. He prayed again, quietly. "Please strike this people with blindness."

The Lord struck them. Their eyes went dark. They stumbled, reaching for each other, calling out. Their formation broke.

Elisha walked among them. His voice carried clearly. "This is not the way, and this is not the city. Follow me, and I will bring you to the man whom you seek."

They followed the sound of his voice. He led them down the road. Dust rose under hundreds of feet, the clatter of horses and chariots behind a single man walking. Mile after mile. The blinded army followed, hands on each other's shoulders, gripping reins, stepping carefully.

He led them to Samaria.

They passed through the gates of the capital city of Israel. Soldiers on the walls watched the strange procession — a prophet leading a blind army into the heart of their kingdom.

When they stood inside the city, Elisha prayed once more. "O Lord, open the eyes of these men, that they may see."

The Lord opened their eyes.

The Syrian soldiers blinked. They looked around. They were not in Dothan. They stood in the streets of Samaria, surrounded by the army of Israel. The walls rose high on every side. The gates stood behind them, guarded.

The king of Israel came down to see. His eyes went wide at the sight of the captured army. He turned to Elisha. "My father, shall I strike them down? Shall I strike them down?"

Elisha said, "You shall not strike them down. Would you strike down those whom you have taken captive with your sword and your bow? Set bread and water before them, that they may eat and drink and go to their master."

The king gave the order. His servants prepared a great feast. Bread was brought out. Water and wine. Meat. The tables were spread in the open streets of Samaria.

The Syrian soldiers sat down among their enemies. They ate. They drank. No one raised a weapon. No one spoke a threat.

When the meal was finished, the soldiers rose. The king of Israel sent them away. They walked back through the gates of Samaria and returned to their master in Syria.

And the bands of Syria came no more on raids into the land of Israel.
