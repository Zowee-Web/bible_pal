The king of Syria made war against Israel. He took counsel with his servants, and chose his encampments with great care, setting ambushes along the roads where the hosts of Israel should pass.

But each time, ere the snare could be shut, word came unto the king of Israel from the prophet Elisha. "Beware that thou pass not by that place, for the Syrians are come down thither."

And the king of Israel sent men to search it out. And it was so. He turned aside from that place.

This came to pass not once nor twice. It came to pass again and again.

And the heart of the king of Syria was sore troubled thereby. He gathered his servants unto him. His voice was sharp. "Will ye not shew me which of us is for the king of Israel? Who among us is the spy?"

And one of his servants answered and said, "None, my lord, O king. But Elisha, the prophet that is in Israel, telleth the king of Israel the words that thou speakest in thy bedchamber."

And he said, "Go and spy where he is, that I may send and fetch him."

And it was told him, saying, "Behold, he is in Dothan."

Therefore sent he thither horses, and chariots, and a great host. And they went by night. And by the time the stars began to fade, they had compassed the city of Dothan round about. The ring was complete. None could go forth.

And the servant of Elisha rose early. The air was yet cool when he went forth. And he stood still. For round about the city walls, the army of Syria waited. Horses stamped in the dust. The iron rims of chariot wheels caught the first light. Spear points bristled above the ranks of the soldiers.

And the servant ran back within. His voice trembled. "Alas, my master! How shall we do?"

And Elisha spake, and his voice was steady. "Fear not, for they that be with us are more than they that be with them."

And the servant looked upon him. Two men in a house. A strange host without the walls.

And Elisha prayed, and said, "Lord, I pray thee, open his eyes, that he may see."

And the Lord opened the eyes of the young man, and he looked. And behold, the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire, blazing in ranks across the slopes, filling every ridge, set round about Elisha on every side. And the light thereof pulsed against the morning sky.

And the soldiers of Syria began to come down against the city. And Elisha went forth to meet them. And he prayed again, quietly. "Smite this people, I pray thee, with blindness."

And the Lord smote them with blindness. And their eyes were darkened. They stumbled, and laid hold one upon another, and cried out. Their ranks were broken.

And Elisha walked among them. His voice carried clearly. "This is not the way, neither is this the city. Follow me, and I will bring you to the man whom ye seek."

And they followed after the sound of his voice. And he led them down the road. Dust rose beneath the feet of hundreds, the clatter of horses and chariots following after one man that walked before them. Mile upon mile. The blinded host followed, with hands upon one another's shoulders, gripping fast the reins, treading carefully.

And he led them to Samaria.

And they passed through the gates of the capital city of Israel. And soldiers upon the walls beheld the strange procession — a prophet leading a blind army into the very heart of their kingdom.

And when they were come within the city, Elisha prayed once more. "Lord, open the eyes of these men, that they may see."

And the Lord opened their eyes, and they saw.

And the soldiers of Syria blinked. And they looked about them. They were not in Dothan. They stood in the streets of Samaria, compassed about by the host of Israel. The walls rose high on every side. The gates stood behind them, and were guarded.

And the king of Israel came down to see them. And his eyes were wide at the sight of the captive host. And he turned unto Elisha, and said, "My father, shall I smite them? Shall I smite them?"

And Elisha said, "Thou shalt not smite them. Wouldest thou smite those whom thou hast taken captive with thy sword and with thy bow? Set bread and water before them, that they may eat and drink, and go to their master."

And the king gave commandment. And his servants prepared a great feast. Bread was brought forth. Water and wine. Flesh. And the tables were spread in the open streets of Samaria.

And the soldiers of Syria sat down among their enemies. And they did eat. And they did drink. No man raised a weapon. No man spake a threat.

And when the feast was ended, the soldiers rose up. And the king of Israel sent them away. And they went out through the gates of Samaria, and returned unto their master in Syria.

And the bands of Syria came no more into the land of Israel.
