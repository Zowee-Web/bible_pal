The king of Syria made war against Israel. He took counsel with care and great deliberation. He gathered unto him his servants, his captains, and those men in whom he placed his chiefest trust, and he chose the places where his troops should lie in wait. A road hard by a certain pass. A valley through which the soldiers of Israel must needs march. He gave his commands privily and set his forces in secret.

But ere the snare could be sprung, word was brought unto the king of Israel. It came from Elisha, the prophet. "Beware that thou pass not by this place, for the Syrians are come down thither."

The king of Israel sent men to search out the matter. It was even so. He turned aside his men. The ambush came to nought.

It came to pass again. The king of Syria chose a new position. A new place. And again the word came from Elisha unto the king of Israel, and again the Israelites turned aside from the snare.

Not once. Not twice. Many times.

And the heart of the king of Syria was sore troubled by this thing. He called his servants before him. His jaw was set fast. "Will ye not shew me which of us is for the king of Israel?"

There was silence in that place. The accusation hung in the air as smoke.

One of his servants spake. "None, my lord, O king." He paused. "But Elisha, the prophet that is in Israel, telleth the king of Israel the words that thou speakest in thy bedchamber."

The king's eyes grew narrow. A prophet. Not a spy amongst his own men, but a prophet who heard that which could not be heard.

"Go and spy where he is," the king saith, "that I may send and fetch him."

His men searched. The report came back with haste. "Behold, he is in Dothan."

Dothan was a small city. The king of Syria sent not a small company. He sent horses and chariots and a great host. They went by night, their wheels muffled in the darkness, their torches unlit. By the time the last stars were faded, they had compassed the city round about. A ring of iron and sinew. Horses standing shoulder to shoulder. Chariots set at every road. None could go in or come out of Dothan.

The servant of Elisha rose early that morning. The sky was but beginning to grow pale. He went forth without to begin the labour of the day, and he stayed himself in the doorway.

Soldiers. On every side.

He could see the line of them stretched along the ridge to the east, dark shapes against the brightening sky. Horses stamped and snorted in the cool of the air. Chariot wheels gleamed. To the north, yet more. To the south, yet more. To the west, the road was stopped with armed men standing three ranks deep.

The servant turned and ran back within. His voice brake. "Alas, my master! How shall we do?"

Elisha looked upon him. His voice did not waver. "Fear not, for they that be with us are more than they that be with them."

The eyes of the servant moved from Elisha unto the open door. Two of them. Two men in a house. Without, hundreds of Syrian soldiers, peradventure thousands, armed and waiting.

Then Elisha prayed. He spake plainly, and he lifted not his voice. "O Lord, I pray thee, open his eyes, that he may see."

And the Lord opened the eyes of the young man.

He looked again. The hills round about Dothan had not changed their form. The rocks were the same. The scrub clung to the slopes as before. But now the mountains were full. Horses and chariots of fire covered the ridges, rank upon rank, blazing with a light that was not the light of the sun. They filled every slope round about Elisha. The fire consumed not the ground. It held steady, rippling and bright, and the chariots stood ready, and the horses stood still, and the heat thereof pressed against the morning air.

The servant stared. His mouth was open. He spake not.

Below, the Syrian host began to move. Officers called out their commands. Soldiers formed their ranks and advanced toward the walls of the city.

Elisha went forth to meet them. He made no haste. When he was come nigh enough that the foremost rank of soldiers might hear him, he stayed himself and prayed. His words were quiet. "I pray thee, smite this people with blindness."

And the Lord smote them.

The foremost rank stumbled. Men reached for their eyes. Horses tossed their heads. The close formation brake asunder as soldiers lost their footing, groping in sudden darkness. Voices rose up—confused, sharp, calling one for another. Hands laid hold upon arms. Men stumbled against chariots and fell over spear shafts cast down in the dust.

Elisha walked toward them. His sandals did tread upon the dry ground. He lifted up his voice that they might hear. "This is not the way, neither is this the city. Follow me, and I will bring you to the man whom ye seek."

They were come for Elisha. They knew not that they hearkened unto him now.

They followed. The blind host set itself in order by slow degrees, soldiers laying hold upon one another's shoulders, horses led by their bridles. The chariots rolled forward, guided by the sound of the prophet's footsteps. Elisha walked before them upon the road, a single man leading an entire army.

The road from Dothan to Samaria was not short. Dust rose up in a long pale pillar behind the procession. The sun climbed higher. The soldiers shuffled forward, unable to see whither they went, trusting the voice that went before them. Their weapons hung useless at their sides.

They came unto Samaria.

The gates of the chief city of Israel stood open. The watchmen upon the walls looked down upon the strange sight beneath them—an army stumbling forward behind one man, their eyes void, their hands stretched forth. Whispering ran along the walls. Soldiers gripped their spears and looked unto their captains.

Elisha led the Syrian host through the gates and into the city.

When they stood in the streets of Samaria, compassed on every side by the walls and the soldiers of Israel, Elisha stayed himself. He prayed yet once more. "O Lord, open the eyes of these men, that they may see."

And the Lord opened their eyes.

The Syrians blinked. Light brake in upon them. The first thing they beheld was not Dothan. Not the hills. Not the open road. They stood in a city which they knew not. Walls of stone rose on every side. Israelite soldiers lined the housetops, armed and watching. The gates behind them were kept. They were within Samaria.

Their hands went unto their weapons. But they were compassed about, outnumbered, and shut up within a fenced city. There was no place whither they might flee.

The king of Israel came down from the upper city. He saw the Syrian soldiers thronged in his streets, blinking and confounded. His countenance was flushed. He turned unto Elisha. The words came swift and eager.

"My father, shall I smite them? Shall I smite them?"

He asked it twice. His hand was nigh unto his sword.

Elisha answered. "Thou shalt not smite them. Wouldest thou smite those whom thou hast taken captive with thy sword and with thy bow?" He paused. "Set bread and water before them, that they may eat and drink, and go to their master."

The king stared upon Elisha. Then he turned unto his servants and gave the commandment.

They prepared a great feast. Not bread and water alone. Tables were brought forth into the streets. Flesh was roasted. Bread was heaped up high. Vessels of water and wine were borne out from the storehouses. The savour of food drifted through the open streets of Samaria.

The Syrian soldiers sat down. Their weapons lay beside them upon the ground. Israelite servants set meat before them. They did eat. They did eat until they were filled. They drank. None spake threats. None drew a blade. The clatter of vessels and the murmur of voices filled the streets where, but moments before, an host had stood ready to give battle.

When the meal was ended, the Syrian soldiers arose. The king of Israel sent them away. They went forth through the gates of Samaria, back down the road which they had travelled blind, back unto their master the king of Syria.

And the bands of Syria came no more on raids into the land of Israel.
