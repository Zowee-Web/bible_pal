The king of Syria was making war against Israel. He would set his camp in secret places, planning ambushes along the roads. But each time, the prophet Elisha sent word to the king of Israel. "Do not pass by that place," the message would say. "The Syrians are waiting there."

The king of Israel sent men to check. Every time, Elisha was right.

This happened again and again. The king of Syria grew furious. He called his servants together. "Tell me which of you is a spy for the king of Israel."

One of his servants spoke. "None of us, my lord. It is Elisha, the prophet in Israel. He tells the king of Israel the words you speak in your own bedroom."

The king said, "Go and find where he is, so I may send and seize him."

Word came back. "He is in Dothan."

So the king of Syria sent horses and chariots and a great army. They came by night and surrounded the city.

When Elisha's servant rose early the next morning, he went outside. Soldiers ringed the city. Horses. Chariots. Everywhere he looked.

He ran back inside. "My master, what shall we do?"

Elisha said, "Do not be afraid. Those who are with us are more than those who are with them."

Then Elisha prayed. "Lord, open his eyes that he may see."

The Lord opened the servant's eyes. He looked again at the hills around Dothan. The mountains were full of horses and chariots of fire, blazing all around Elisha.

The Syrian army moved closer. Elisha prayed again. "Strike this people with blindness." The Lord struck them blind.

Elisha walked out to them. "This is not the way, and this is not the city. Follow me, and I will bring you to the man you seek." He led them to Samaria.

When they entered the city, Elisha prayed. "Lord, open the eyes of these men that they may see." Their eyes opened. They stood inside Samaria, surrounded by Israel's army.

The king of Israel saw them. "My father, shall I strike them down? Shall I strike them down?"

Elisha said, "You shall not strike them. Set bread and water before them, that they may eat and drink and go to their master."

The king prepared a great feast. The Syrian soldiers ate and drank. Then they rose, and he sent them away to their master.

The raiding bands of Syria came no more into the land of Israel.
