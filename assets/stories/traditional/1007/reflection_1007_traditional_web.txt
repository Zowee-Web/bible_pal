The sea does not always wait for us to be ready. Sometimes the storm arrives precisely when we are running, when the hold of the ship feels safest, when sleep seems like the kindest mercy we can offer ourselves.

There is something worth noticing in how the sailors responded — men who worshiped different gods, who had no particular reason to care for one Hebrew prophet asleep below deck. They rowed harder first. They tried everything within their own strength before asking the difficult question. And when the answer came from the very man who had caused the turmoil, they still hesitated to act. Even knowing what needed to happen, they grieved it.

Storms have a way of revealing what is hidden — not always as punishment, but sometimes as invitation. The lots fell. The truth surfaced. And the calm came not through escape but through surrender.

It is a curious thing, how peace sometimes waits on the other side of the very thing we most want to avoid. The sailors feared the LORD that day, not because they were told to, but because they witnessed something unmistakable in the sudden stillness of the water.
