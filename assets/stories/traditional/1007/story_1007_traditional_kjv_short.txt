The ship cut westward through open water, bound for Tarshish, and the prophet lay below deck in the hold, his body pressed against the planking, asleep among the cargo and the coiled rope.

Then the Lord hurled a great wind upon the sea.

The surface broke apart. Waves rose and struck the hull broadside, and the timbers groaned beneath the force. Spray erupted over the bow and poured across the deck in white sheets. The vessel pitched so violently that the mast swayed in wide arcs against the darkened sky, and the mariners staggered where they stood.

Each man cried out to his own god. Their voices carried and scattered in the gale. They seized the stores and the wares lashed to the deck and cast them overboard, barrel and bundle and crate, heaving them into the churning deep to lighten the ship. Still the waves mounted.

The master of the vessel descended into the hold, bracing himself against the walls. He found Jonah lying fast asleep. He shook him and spoke sharply, commanding him to rise and call upon his God, that perhaps this God might give thought unto them and spare their lives.

Jonah rose. He climbed the ladder to the deck, where the wind tore at his garments and the sea flung itself against the rails.

The mariners gathered and spoke among themselves, declaring they should cast lots to learn on whose account this evil had come upon them. They cast the lots. The lot fell upon Jonah.

They pressed close around him, their hair matted with salt water, their hands gripping the rigging to keep upright. They demanded he tell them his occupation, whence he came, what people he belonged to, and why this calamity had fallen.

Jonah answered them plainly. He declared himself a Hebrew, and said he feared the Lord God of heaven, who hath made the sea and the dry land. He told them he had fled from the presence of the Lord.

The men were exceedingly afraid. They asked him what they ought to do, for the sea grew more tempestuous with every passing moment.

He told them to take him up and cast him forth into the sea, and the waters would be calm, for he declared that this great tempest had come upon them for his sake.

Yet the men would not do it. They rowed hard toward the land, pulling against the oars with all their strength. The sea would not yield. The waves drove them back and rose higher still.

Then they cried unto the Lord, beseeching Him not to lay upon them innocent blood, and acknowledging that He had done as it pleased Him. They took up Jonah. They cast him into the sea.

The wind ceased. The waves flattened and grew still. The surface of the water spread out smooth and dark beneath the hull, and the ship rocked gently in the sudden quiet.
