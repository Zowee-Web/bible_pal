The ship moved out from Joppa into open water, its wooden hull cutting through the waves under a broad sail. Sailors climbed the rigging and worked the ropes, their bare feet steady on the deck planks. The coast shrank behind them, and the Mediterranean stretched wide in every direction. Below deck, in the lowest part of the hold, Jonah lay down among the cargo and fell into a deep sleep.

Then Yahweh hurled a great wind across the sea.

The surface of the water, moments before rolling in long, even swells, broke apart into steep, churning waves. The wind struck the sail with a sound like a crack of thunder, and the mast groaned under the sudden pressure. Spray burst over the bow and drenched the forward deck. The ship pitched sharply, timbers creaking and straining as the hull rose on one wave and plunged into the trough of the next. Water poured across the deck in sheets, rushing from side to side as the vessel rolled.

The sailors shouted to one another over the roar of the storm. Each man cried out to his own god, his voice rising into the wind. Some dropped to their knees on the slick planking, hands raised, mouths open in desperate prayer. Others seized the cargo — jars of oil, bales of cloth, crates of goods — and heaved them over the railing into the churning sea, trying to lighten the ship. Barrel after barrel tumbled into the dark water and vanished beneath the foam. The deck tilted again, and men grabbed at ropes and railings to keep from sliding.

The shipmaster made his way below, bracing himself against the walls of the narrow passage as the ship lurched. He found Jonah lying in the hold, asleep. The shipmaster stood over him and spoke sharply: "What do you mean, sleeper? Get up! Call on your God! Perhaps your God will notice us, so that we won't perish."

Jonah rose.

Up on deck, the sailors gathered together, rain streaming down their faces, their clothing soaked through and clinging to their bodies. The wind tore at their hair and beards. One man called out above the noise: "Come! Let us cast lots, so we can find out who is responsible for this disaster." They cast lots, and the lot fell on Jonah.

Every face turned toward him. The men pressed close, water running off their shoulders, the ship bucking beneath their feet. They spoke to him: "Tell us, please, on whose account is this evil on us? What is your occupation? Where do you come from? What is your country? Of what people are you?"

Jonah answered them. "I am a Hebrew. I fear Yahweh, the God of heaven, who has made the sea and the dry land."

The men drew back from him. Their voices rose, and they said to him, "What have you done?" For the men knew that he was fleeing from the presence of Yahweh, because he had told them.

The waves climbed higher. Green water crashed over the sides and pooled around their ankles. The ship groaned as if its joints were pulling apart. The sailors shouted again to Jonah: "What shall we do to you, that the sea may be calm for us?" The storm only grew worse. The mast swayed, ropes snapped taut, and the deck shuddered under each impact.

Jonah said to them, "Pick me up and throw me into the sea. Then the sea will be calm for you, for I know that because of me this great storm is on you."

But the men did not do it. Instead, they seized the oars and rowed hard, trying to bring the ship back toward land. They pulled with their full weight, arms straining, backs bent, feet braced against the deck. The oars bit into the water, but the sea drove them back. Wave after wave struck the hull broadside, and the ship spun and wallowed. They could not reach the shore. The storm would not relent.

Then the sailors cried out to Yahweh. Their voices carried through the wind and the spray: "We beg you, Yahweh, we beg you, don't let us die for this man's life, and don't lay on us innocent blood; for you, Yahweh, have done as it pleased you."

They took hold of Jonah. Hands gripped his arms and his clothing. They lifted him up and carried him to the rail. The sea roared below, white and churning, waves slamming against the hull. They threw Jonah into the sea.

The wind stopped.

The waves flattened. The surface of the water, which moments before had been a chaos of foam and surging crests, went still. The ship steadied. The mast stood upright. Water drained from the deck through the scuppers, and the hull rocked gently on a calm sea. The only sound was the creak of wet wood and the dripping of water from the rigging.

The sailors stood on the deck, drenched and breathing hard. They offered a sacrifice to Yahweh and made vows, there on that ship, with the quiet water stretching around them in every direction.
