The ship had scarcely gained the open water when the wind changed. It did not shift gently, as winds do when the season turns. It fell upon the sea as a thing hurled from above, and the deep answered with waves that rose higher than the mast-heads. The timbers groaned beneath the force of it, and the vessel pitched so violently that the deck could not be walked upon without a man clinging to rope or rail.

The mariners, men seasoned by many voyages, staggered and cried aloud. Each called upon his own god, lifting his voice above the roar of water and wind. Their words were swallowed by the gale, scattered like spray. They labored with their hands, hauling at the rigging, lashing what could be lashed, and when the ship listed further still, they took hold of the wares stored below and cast them into the sea. Bales and jars and casks tumbled over the side, swallowed by the churning deep. The vessel rose a little with each load shed, but the storm did not relent.

Below deck, in the innermost hold where the sound of the tempest was muffled by planking and pitch, Jonah lay upon his side. He was fast asleep. The rolling of the ship did not wake him, nor the shuddering of the hull, nor the shouts of the crew above.

The shipmaster came down. He descended the narrow way with one hand braced against the wall, water streaming from his garments. He found Jonah there in the dark hold, motionless. The shipmaster shook him and spoke sharply, demanding to know what manner of sleeper he was. He bade Jonah rise and call upon his God, saying that perhaps this God would give thought unto them, that they perish not.

Jonah rose. He climbed the steps into the full fury of the storm. Rain drove sideways across the deck. The horizon had vanished behind walls of grey water, and the ship rode each swell as though it would not come down again.

The mariners gathered together, bracing themselves against the pitch and roll. They spoke among themselves and determined to cast lots, that they might discover for whose cause this evil had come upon them. The lots were cast. They fell upon Jonah.

Every face turned toward him. The wind tore at their garments and salt water ran down their beards. They pressed him with questions, speaking one upon another. They asked him to tell them for whose cause this evil had come, what his occupation was, and whence he came. They asked what country and what people were his.

Jonah answered them. He declared that he was a Hebrew, and that he feared the LORD, the God of heaven, who had made the sea and the dry land.

At these words the men were exceedingly afraid. The ship lurched, and several seized the ropes to keep their footing. They asked him what he had done, for he had told them already that he fled from the presence of the LORD. Their voices carried the sharp edge of men who see death close at hand.

They asked him what they should do unto him, that the sea might be calm. The waves struck the hull broadside now, and water poured across the deck in sheets. The vessel shuddered and dipped so low on one side that the sea nearly reached the rail.

Jonah told them plainly. He bade them take him up and cast him forth into the sea, and the sea would be calm unto them. He declared that he knew it was for his sake that this great tempest had come upon them.

But the men did not obey at once. Instead they took up the oars and rowed hard, straining to bring the ship back toward dry land. Their arms pulled and their backs bent and the oars bit into the water, but the sea wrought against them and grew yet more tempestuous. The waves would not yield. The wind bore down harder. Foam flew from the crests and the sky pressed low and dark above them.

At last they ceased their rowing. They cried unto the LORD, beseeching Him not to let them perish for this man's life, and not to lay upon them innocent blood. They declared aloud that the LORD had done as it pleased Him.

Then they took Jonah up. Several hands gripped him, arms straining against the roll of the deck. They carried him to the side of the ship. The sea churned below, black and white with fury. They cast him forth.

He went over the rail and fell into the deep.

The wind ceased. The waves, which had risen like mountains, sank low. The surface of the water flattened and grew still. The ship steadied beneath their feet, and the timbers no longer groaned. Where there had been roaring, there was silence, broken only by the creak of wet rope and the dripping of water from the mast.

The mariners stood upon the deck and looked out over the calm sea. They feared the LORD exceedingly. They offered a sacrifice there upon the ship, and they made vows, their voices carrying now across water that lay quiet and unbroken beneath the open sky.
