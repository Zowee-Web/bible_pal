The ship cut through open water, its sails stretched tight, the crew working the ropes and calling to one another across the deck. Then Yahweh hurled a great wind across the sea. The surface broke apart into walls of churning water. Waves crashed over the bow, sending spray across the planks. The timbers groaned, and the vessel pitched so sharply that jars and cargo slid across the hold.

The mariners cried out, each man to his own god. They seized crates and bundles and heaved them over the railing, lightening the ship as the storm battered them from every side. Ropes snapped. The mast shuddered. Water pooled ankle-deep on the lower deck.

Below, in the innermost part of the ship, Jonah lay fast asleep. The shipmaster found him there, bracing himself against the doorframe as the vessel rolled. He shook Jonah and shouted over the roar of wind and waves, "What do you mean, sleeper? Get up! Call on your God! Maybe your God will notice us, so that we won't perish."

Back on deck, the sailors gathered in the driving rain. They said to one another, "Come, let's cast lots, so we can know who is responsible for this evil that is on us." They cast lots, and the lot fell on Jonah.

They pressed close around him, gripping the rigging to keep their footing. "Tell us! On whose account is this evil on us? What is your occupation? Where do you come from? What is your country? Of what people are you?"

Jonah stood among them, rain streaming down his face. He said, "I am a Hebrew, and I fear Yahweh, the God of heaven, who has made the sea and the dry land."

The men drew back. Their voices rose against the storm. They said to him, "What is this that you have done?" For they knew he was fleeing from the presence of Yahweh, because he had told them.

The sea grew worse. Waves towered above the deck and broke across the hull. The men shouted to Jonah, "What shall we do to you, that the sea may be calm for us?"

He answered them, "Pick me up and throw me into the sea. Then the sea will be calm for you, for I know that because of me this great storm is on you."

Instead, the men rowed hard to bring the ship back to land, but they could not. The sea grew more and more violent against them. So they cried out to Yahweh, "We beg you, Yahweh, we beg you, don't let us die for this man's life, and don't lay on us innocent blood; for you, Yahweh, have done as it pleased you."

They took Jonah and threw him into the sea. His body struck the water and disappeared beneath the surface. The wind dropped. The waves flattened. The sea became still.
