The letter came down from Jerusalem in the winter, in the second year of the captivity. Two men carried it across the long road. Elasah, the son of Shaphan, and Gemariah, the son of Hilkiah, were sent by Zedekiah king of Judah. They went unto Nebuchadnezzar. The letter went with them.

They delivered it unto the elders of the captivity when they were come to Babylon. The elders opened it in the house wherein the captives were gathered to hear what was said. The reading was done by lamplight in the evening. The men leaned forward to hear.

It was not what the people had hoped for. They had hoped to hear that the time should be short. They had hoped that the prophet should speak the word that would bring them home or ever another planting season had made the country green again. They had hoped, in the secret of their hearts, that the LORD had not in truth meant to leave them in this place.

What he said unto them was that they should build.

Build ye houses, the letter said. Dwell in them. Plant gardens. Eat the fruit thereof. Take ye wives. Give your sons unto wives. Give your daughters unto husbands. Multiply ye there. Be not diminished. Seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away captive. Pray unto the LORD for that city. In the peace thereof shall ye have peace.

Then he said: When seventy years be accomplished, I will visit you, and bring you again unto this place.

The elders were silent when the reading was finished. The letter lay open upon the table. The lamp upon the wall threw light across the words. None of them spake for a while. Without, the land of Babylon went on with its evening. The dust of the day was settling upon the housetops. The carts were coming in from the gardens. The lamps were lighting in the houses where the exiles were already, in part, beginning to dwell.

In the rest of the letter, the LORD said: For I know the thoughts that I think toward you. Thoughts of peace, and not of evil. Thoughts to give you an expected end. When ye shall call upon me, I will hearken. When ye shall pray, I will hear. When ye shall search for me with all your heart, I will be found of you.

The elders folded the letter. They laid it where it might be read again. They went forth unto their houses one by one. The night was come on while they sat. The stars were out above the land of Babylon.

One of them, walking, looked down upon the street where his neighbour had begun to lay the foundation of a wall. The man was setting his stones in the dust. He was using the small light from his own door. He worked slowly. He set one stone. He set another. He laid the third level with the first two.

The wall should be a long time in the building. The man building it knew it. His sons should help him with it. His daughters should carry water unto him at midday. The neighbours on either side should set their walls against his when their time came. By the seventieth year, when the deliverance should come, the wall should be old.

The elder stood and watched him a while. Then he went on home through the streets the captives had been building for two years already, and the streets that they should be building for a long time still.
