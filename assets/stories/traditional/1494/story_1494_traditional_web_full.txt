The letter came down from Jerusalem in the winter, in the second year of the captivity. Two men carried it across the long road. Elasah, the son of Shaphan, and Gemariah, the son of Hilkiah, had been sent by Zedekiah the king of Judah. They went to Nebuchadnezzar. The letter went with them.

They handed it over to the elders of the captivity when they reached Babylon. The elders opened it in the house where the captives gathered to hear what had been said. The reading was done by lamplight in the evening. The men leaned forward to listen.

It was not what the people had hoped for. They had hoped to hear that the time would be short. They had hoped that the prophet would speak the word that would bring them home before another planting season had turned the country green. They had hoped, in the back of their hearts, that the LORD had not really meant to leave them in this place.

What he said instead was that they should build.

Build houses, the letter said. Live in them. Plant gardens. Eat the fruit. Take wives. Give your sons in marriage. Let your daughters be given. Multiply there. Do not be made few. Seek the peace of the city where I have caused you to be carried away captive. Pray to Yahweh for that city. In its peace, you will have peace.

Then he said: When seventy years are accomplished, I will come and bring you again to this place.

The elders were quiet when the reading was done. The letter lay open on the table. The lamp on the wall threw light across the words. None of them spoke for a while. Outside, the country of Babylon went on with its evening. The dust of the day was settling on the roofs. The carts were coming in from the gardens. The lamps were lighting in the houses where the exiles had begun, already, to live.

In the rest of the letter, Yahweh said: For I know the thoughts I think toward you. They are thoughts of peace, not of evil. They are thoughts of a future and a hope. When you call upon me, I will hearken. When you pray, I will hear. When you search for me with all your heart, you will find me.

The elders folded the letter. They put it where it could be read again. They went out to their houses one at a time. The night had come on while they sat. The stars were out above the country of Babylon.

One of them, walking, looked down at the street where his neighbor had begun to lay the foundation of a wall. The man was setting his stones in the dust. He was using the small light from his own door. He worked slowly. He set one stone. He set another. He laid the third level with the first two.

The wall would be a long time in the building. The man building it knew that. His sons would help him with it. His daughters would carry water to him at midday. The neighbors on either side would set their walls against his when their time came. By the seventieth year, when the deliverance came, the wall would be old.

The elder stood and watched him for a while. Then he went on home through the streets the captives had been building for two years already, and the streets that they would be building for a long time still.
