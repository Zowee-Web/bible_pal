The letter came down from Jerusalem in the hands of two men who had been sent across the long road, and the elders in Babylon opened it in the house where the captives gathered to hear what had been said.

It was not what the people had hoped for. They had hoped to hear that the time would be short. They had hoped that the prophet would speak the word that would bring them home before another planting season had turned the country green.

What he said instead was that they should build.

Build houses, the letter said, and live in them. Plant gardens, and eat the fruit. Take wives. Give your sons in marriage. Let your daughters be given. Multiply there. Do not be made few. Seek the peace of the city where I have caused you to be carried away captive, and pray to Yahweh for it, for in the peace of that city you will have peace.

Then he said: When seventy years are accomplished, I will come and bring you again to this place.

The elders were quiet when the reading was done. The letter lay open on the table, and the lamp on the wall threw light across the words. Outside, the country of Babylon went on with its evening — the dust of the day settling on the roofs, the carts coming in from the gardens, the lamps lighting in the houses where the exiles had begun, already, to live.

In the rest of the letter, Yahweh said: For I know the thoughts I think toward you. They are thoughts of peace, not of evil. They are thoughts of a future, and a hope. When you call upon me and pray, I will hearken. When you search for me with all your heart, you will find me.

The elders folded the letter and put it where it could be read again. They went out to their houses. One of them, walking, looked down at the street where his neighbor had begun to lay the foundation of a wall.

The man was setting his stones in the dust. The wall would be a long time in the building.
