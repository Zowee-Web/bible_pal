The letter came down from Jerusalem in the hands of two men that had been sent across the long road, and the elders in Babylon opened it in the house where the captives were gathered to hear what had been said.

It was not what the people had hoped for. They had hoped to hear that the time should be short. They had hoped that the prophet should speak the word that would bring them home or ever another planting season had made the country green again.

What he said unto them was that they should build.

Build ye houses, the letter said, and dwell in them. Plant gardens, and eat the fruit thereof. Take ye wives. Give your sons unto wives. Give your daughters unto husbands. Multiply ye there. Be not diminished. Seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away captive, and pray unto the LORD for it, for in the peace thereof shall ye have peace.

Then he said: When seventy years be accomplished, I will visit you, and bring you again unto this place.

The elders were silent when the reading was finished. The letter lay open upon the table, and the lamp upon the wall threw light across the words. Without, the land of Babylon went on with its evening — the dust of the day settling upon the housetops, the carts coming in from the gardens, the lamps lighting in the houses where the exiles were already, in part, beginning to live.

In the rest of the letter, the LORD said: For I know the thoughts that I think toward you. Thoughts of peace, and not of evil. Thoughts to give you an expected end. When ye shall call upon me, and pray, I will hearken unto you. And when ye shall search for me with all your heart, I will be found of you.

The elders folded the letter and laid it where it might be read again. They went forth unto their houses. One of them, walking, looked down upon the street where his neighbour had begun to lay the foundation of a wall.

The man was setting his stones in the dust. The wall would be a long time in the building.
