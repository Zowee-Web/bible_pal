Nehemiah had a place of honour. He was the man that brought the king his wine. Every day he stood within the palace, poured the wine, and made sure it was safe to drink. It was a place of great trust.

But Nehemiah was sorrowful. He had heard that in Jerusalem — the city whence his fathers came — the walls were broken down and the gates were burned with fire. The city lay waste. None had set them up.

On a day, the king beheld Nehemiah's countenance. "Why is thy countenance sad?" the king asked. "Thou art not sick. This is nothing else but sorrow of heart."

Nehemiah was sore afraid. It was not meet that one should look sad before the king. But he spake the truth. "The city of my fathers' sepulchres lieth waste. The walls are broken down, and the gates thereof are consumed with fire."

The king said, "For what dost thou make request?"

Nehemiah's heart was pounding. Even there, standing before the mightiest man in all the earth, he prayed in his mind a swift prayer. Then he spake.

"Send me, I pray thee, unto Jerusalem, that I may build it."

And the king said yea. He gave Nehemiah letters for safe passage and leave to take timber from the king's forest for the building.

When Nehemiah came unto Jerusalem, he told no man his purpose straightway. First, he went forth by night with but a few men and one beast. He rode about the broken walls in the darkness, viewing the damage. Stones lay scattered all about. Thorns grew through the rubbish. In some places the way was so blocked that the beast could not pass through, and Nehemiah must needs climb over the fallen stones afoot.

After he had seen all things, Nehemiah gathered the people of the city together.

"Ye see the distress that we are in," said he. "Jerusalem lieth waste. The gates are burned. But the good hand of my God hath been upon me, and the king hath granted us our need. Let us rise up and build!"

The people looked one on another. Then they said:

"Let us rise up and build!"

And they did so. They girded up their loins, took up the stones, and began to work.
