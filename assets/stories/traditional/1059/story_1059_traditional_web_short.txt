Nehemiah had an important job. He was the person who brought the king his wine. Every day he stood in the palace, poured the wine, and made sure it was safe to drink. It was a very trusted position.

But Nehemiah was sad. He had heard that back in Jerusalem — the city where his family came from — the walls were broken down and the gates were burned. The city was a mess. Nobody had fixed it.

One day, the king noticed Nehemiah's face. "Why do you look so sad?" the king asked. "You're not sick. Something is bothering your heart."

Nehemiah was afraid. You weren't supposed to look sad in front of the king. But he told the truth. "The city of my ancestors is ruined. The walls are broken and the gates are burned up."

The king said, "What do you want?"

Nehemiah's heart was pounding. Right there, standing in front of the most powerful man in the world, he said a quick prayer in his mind. Then he spoke.

"Please send me to Jerusalem so I can rebuild it."

And the king said yes. He gave Nehemiah letters for safe travel and permission to take wood from the royal forest for building.

When Nehemiah arrived in Jerusalem, he didn't tell anyone his plan right away. First, he went out at night with just a few men and one donkey. He rode around the broken walls in the dark, looking at the damage. Stones were scattered everywhere. Thorns grew through the rubble. Some places were so blocked that the donkey couldn't get through, and Nehemiah had to climb over the fallen stones on foot.

After he had seen everything, Nehemiah gathered the people of the city together.

"Look at the trouble we're in," he said. "Jerusalem is a ruin. The gates are burned. But God has been good to me, and the king has given us what we need. Let's rebuild the wall!"

The people looked at each other. Then they said the best words Nehemiah could have heard:

"Let us get up and build!"

And they did. They rolled up their sleeves, picked up stones, and started to work.
