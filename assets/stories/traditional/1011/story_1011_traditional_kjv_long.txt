A warm afternoon sun lit the hillside. Green grass covered the long slopes that rolled down toward a valley below. Wildflowers grew in patches of yellow and purple between the rocks. Some of the blossoms were small and round. Others stretched tall upon thin stems, nodding gently when the air moved. A soft breeze bore the savour of earth and herbs across the open land.

Olive trees stood farther down the hill. Their silvery leaves turned and flickered in the wind. A few flat stones lay scattered along the upper slope, half buried in the dirt. Insects buzzed low over the grass, drifting from one flower to the next. The sky above was wide and blue, with only a few thin clouds stretched out near the horizon.

Jesus walked up the hillside. His sandals pressed into the soft ground. Dust clave unto the hem of his robe. He found a place to sit where the grass was thick and the ground was level. He settled down and rested his hands upon his knees.

His disciples followed him up the slope. They came in small companies, talking quietly one to another. Some of them carried leathern bags upon their shoulders. Others held staves which they set down in the grass beside them. One by one, they found places to sit nigh unto Jesus. Some leaned upon their elbows. Others sat cross-legged with their dusty sandals tucked beneath their robes. A few stretched their legs out before them and tilted their faces toward the sun.

The breeze moved through the company. It lifted the edges of their cloaks and ruffled their hair. One disciple drew a water skin from his bag and took a drink. Another wiped his forehead with the back of his hand. The walk had been long, and the road had been dry. Now they rested together upon the open hillside.

A few birds lighted nigh. They were dark ravens with glossy feathers. Their small black eyes looked this way and that. They hopped across the bare patches of dirt, pecking at seeds. One bird found somewhat in the soil and tapped at it with its beak. Another bird lighted beside it and did the same. Their feet made tiny scratching sounds upon the ground.

Jesus beheld the birds. He watched them hop and peck for a moment. Then he pointed unto them and spake. "Consider the ravens," he said. His voice was calm and carried easily in the open air. "They sow not. They reap not. They have neither storehouse nor barn." He paused. The birds kept moving across the dirt, untroubled by the people sitting nigh. One raven tilted its small head to one side, then pecked at the earth again. "And God feedeth them," Jesus said.

The disciples watched the birds. A few of them turned their heads to follow the ravens as they hopped along. The breeze stirred the grass between them.

"How much more are ye better than the fowls," Jesus said.

He opened his hands as he spake. His palms faced upward, and his fingers spread wide. "Which of you with taking thought can add to his stature one cubit?" he asked. No man answered. The wind moved through the tall grass round about them. A grasshopper leapt from one blade to another with a quiet click. Jesus let the question rest in the air for a moment. "If ye then be not able to do that thing which is least," he said, "why take ye thought for the rest?"

Then Jesus turned and pointed unto the flowers growing along the hillside. Bright petals swayed in the breeze. Yellow blossoms clustered together near a low stone wall. Purple flowers dotted the spaces between the rocks. Some of the petals were thin and delicate. Others were broad and open, catching the full light of the afternoon sun.

"Consider the lilies how they grow," Jesus said. "They toil not. They spin not." He leaned forward and touched one of the stems gently. The flower bobbed beneath his hand and then straightened again. Its petals were smooth and bright. "And yet I say unto you," he said, "that Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these."

A few of the disciples looked down upon the flowers near their feet. Small purple blossoms grew between the stones, open and still. One man reached down and cupped a flower lightly in his hand, beholding its petals. Another disciple brushed his fingers across the top of the grass, where tiny white flowers grew close to the ground. The hillside was covered with colour in every direction.

A yellow butterfly drifted past, circling one of the taller stems before lighting upon a leaf. Its wings opened and closed slowly. The disciples watched it rest there, bright against the green.

"If then God so clothe the grass of the field," Jesus continued, "which is to day, and to morrow is cast into the oven — how much more will he clothe you?" His voice was steady and clear. He looked round about upon the company. Some of the men met his eyes. Others looked out at the flowers and the wide hillside stretching before them.

Jesus paused. The breeze picked up again, bending the tall grass in a slow wave that moved across the slope. A few loose petals lifted from the ground and tumbled gently through the air before settling again in the dirt.

He spake again, his hands resting together in his lap. "And seek not ye what ye shall eat, or what ye shall drink," he said. "Neither be ye of doubtful mind." He folded his hands. His fingers laced together. "For all these things do the nations of the world seek after." He looked round about upon each of them, one by one, turning slowly where he sat. "But your Father knoweth that ye have need of these things."

One of the ravens lifted off the ground with a quick beat of its wings. It flew low over the wildflowers, and its shadow passed across the grass for but a moment. Then it lighted again a few paces away and went back to pecking at the dirt. Another bird joined it, and the two of them moved together across the open ground.

A disciple near the back of the company shifted his weight and pulled his cloak tighter about his shoulders. The man beside him set down his water skin and folded his arms across his knees. The company was quiet. The only sounds were the wind, the birds, and the soft rustling of grass.

Jesus spake again. "But rather seek ye the kingdom of God," he said, "and all these things shall be added unto you."

His words hung in the still air. The breeze had settled. The hillside was quiet. Sunlight rested upon the grass and upon the disciples sitting close together. A warm glow spread across the slope. The stones were warm to the touch. The flowers held their bright colours in the steady light.

A few of the disciples brushed dust from their hands. One man drew forth a piece of bread from his leathern bag. He brake it slowly, and the soft sound of the crust cracking carried in the quiet. He handed half unto the man sitting next him. That man nodded and took it. They did eat together without speaking.

Another disciple stretched his arms above his head and then rested them behind him, leaning back in the grass. He looked up at the wide sky. The thin clouds had scarce moved since they had all sat down.

Down the slope, the olive trees swayed in a gentle gust. Their leaves flashed silver and green, over and over. A small brown bird lighted upon one of the lower branches and sang a short, clear note. It sang again, then tucked its head and sat still.

The wildflowers kept swaying. The ravens hopped and pecked at the ground nigh, full and unhurried. One of them ruffled its feathers and settled into a patch of warm dirt. The butterfly had moved to a new flower, its wings still opening and closing in the sun.

Jesus sat among his friends upon the warm hillside. The bread was shared. The water was passed. The afternoon stretched on, gentle and bright. The hillside held them all together, and the air bore the savour of grass and herbs and warm earth.
