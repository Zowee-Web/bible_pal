A warm afternoon stretched over the hillside near the Sea of Galilee. Tall grass bent gently in the breeze. Wild mustard plants dotted the slope with tiny yellow blossoms. The air carried the smell of dry earth and green leaves.

People sat in small groups across the hillside. Some leaned against smooth rocks. Others rested on the grass with their sandals tucked beside them. Mothers held little children on their laps. Older men crossed their legs and settled their hands on their knees. A few young women shaded their eyes and looked toward the front of the gathering.

Jesus stood where everyone could see him. His robe moved softly in the wind. His disciples sat close, just a few steps away. Some of them had their cloaks folded beneath them. Others sat with their arms resting on their knees. The whole hillside was quiet, and people leaned forward to listen.

Jesus lifted his voice so the crowd could hear. He spoke to his disciples, but his words carried across the grass to everyone gathered there.

"Don't worry about your life," he said, "about what you will eat." He paused. A bird called out somewhere behind him. "Don't worry about your body, about what you will wear."

A few people shifted where they sat. Some tilted their heads. A woman near the back adjusted her head covering and kept listening.

"Life is more than food," Jesus continued. "The body is more than clothing."

He raised one hand and pointed toward the open air above the hillside. Several ravens glided overhead, their dark wings spread wide. They circled slowly, riding the warm air that rose from the ground below.

"Think about the ravens," Jesus said. His voice was steady and clear. "They don't plant seeds. They don't gather a harvest. They have no storeroom and no barn."

The birds drifted in a wide, easy loop. Their wings caught the sunlight.

"Yet God feeds them," Jesus said. He brought his hand back down. "How much more valuable are you than birds?"

A child near the front pointed up at the ravens and tugged on his father's sleeve. The father smiled and gently guided the boy's hand back down.

Jesus went on speaking. "Which of you, by worrying, can add a single moment to your life?"

No one answered. The breeze rustled through the grass. A few heads shook slowly from side to side.

"If you can't do even the smallest thing," he said, "why worry about the rest?"

He turned slightly and gestured toward the hillside itself. Flowers grew in patches among the rocks and grass. Red anemones, small white blossoms, and those bright yellow mustard flowers all swayed together in the moving air.

"Think about how the lilies grow," Jesus said. His hand swept gently over the scene. "They don't work hard spinning thread. They don't weave cloth."

He let the words settle for a moment. A woman near the middle of the crowd looked down at the simple fabric of her own robe, then looked back up.

"Yet I tell you," Jesus said, "even Solomon, in all his fine robes, was not dressed as beautifully as one of these."

A few people glanced at the flowers nearby. The petals moved lightly in the breeze. Each blossom was delicate and bright, open to the afternoon sun.

Jesus stepped forward slightly. His sandals pressed into the soft ground. "If God clothes the grass of the field like this — grass that is here today and tomorrow is thrown into the oven — how much more will he clothe you?"

He looked across the faces of his listeners. Some of them had rough, patched clothing. Some had dust on their hands from long days of work. A young man near the edge of the group rubbed his calloused palms together and sat very still.

"You have such little faith," Jesus said. His voice was not sharp. It was warm and direct, like someone speaking to people he cared about.

He continued without stopping. "Don't spend your time looking for what you will eat or what you will drink. Don't keep worrying."

An older woman rocked a sleeping baby in her arms. The baby's small fingers curled against the woman's shoulder.

"All the nations of the world go running after these things," Jesus said. He moved his hands outward, as if showing something far away. "But your Father already knows that you need them."

A few of the disciples nodded. One of them adjusted his cloak and folded his arms across his chest. Another rested his chin in his hand and kept his eyes on Jesus.

"Instead," Jesus said, and his voice lifted just a little, "seek God's purposes. And all these things will be given to you as well."

The breeze picked up for a moment. It moved through the crowd like a long, slow breath. Cloaks rippled. Hair lifted from foreheads. The yellow and red flowers along the hillside bobbed and swayed.

Jesus stood still. The afternoon light rested on his face and on the people gathered around him. No one got up to leave. The hillside was full and quiet.

A raven passed overhead once more, gliding without effort. Wildflowers held their bright faces open to the sky. The people sat together on the warm grass, close to one another, close to the one who spoke to them. The breeze carried the smell of earth and blossoms across the whole hillside, and the afternoon went on, gentle and unhurried.
