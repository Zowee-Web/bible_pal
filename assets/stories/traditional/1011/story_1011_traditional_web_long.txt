A warm afternoon sun lit the hillside. Green grass covered the long slopes that rolled down toward a valley below. Wildflowers grew in patches of yellow and purple between the rocks. Some of the blossoms were small and round. Others stretched tall on thin stems, nodding gently when the air moved. A soft breeze carried the smell of earth and herbs across the open land.

Olive trees stood farther down the hill. Their silvery leaves turned and flickered in the wind. A few flat stones lay scattered along the upper slope, half buried in the dirt. Insects buzzed low over the grass, drifting from one flower to the next. The sky above was wide and blue, with only a few thin clouds stretched out near the horizon.

Jesus walked up the hillside. His sandals pressed into the soft ground. Dust clung to the hem of his robe. He found a place to sit where the grass was thick and the ground was level. He settled down and rested his hands on his knees.

His disciples followed him up the slope. They came in small groups, talking quietly to one another. Some of them carried leather bags over their shoulders. Others held walking sticks that they set down in the grass beside them. One by one, they found places to sit near Jesus. Some leaned on their elbows. Others sat cross-legged with their dusty sandals tucked beneath their robes. A few stretched their legs out in front of them and tilted their faces toward the sun.

The breeze moved through the group. It lifted the edges of their cloaks and ruffled their hair. One disciple pulled a water skin from his bag and took a drink. Another wiped his forehead with the back of his hand. The walk had been long, and the road had been dry. Now they rested together on the open hillside.

A few birds landed nearby. They were dark ravens with glossy feathers. Their small black eyes looked this way and that. They hopped across the bare patches of dirt, pecking at seeds. One bird found something in the soil and tapped at it with its beak. Another bird landed beside it and did the same. Their feet made tiny scratching sounds on the ground.

Jesus looked at the birds. He watched them hop and peck for a moment. Then he pointed toward them and spoke. "Look at the ravens," he said. His voice was calm and carried easily in the open air. "They don't plant seeds. They don't harvest grain. They don't have a storeroom or a barn." He paused. The birds kept moving across the dirt, unbothered by the people sitting nearby. One raven tilted its small head to one side, then pecked at the earth again. "God feeds them," Jesus said.

The disciples watched the birds. A few of them turned their heads to follow the ravens as they hopped along. The breeze stirred the grass between them.

"How much more valuable you are than birds," Jesus said.

He opened his hands as he talked. His palms faced upward, and his fingers spread wide. "Which of you, by worrying, can add one moment to your life?" he asked. No one answered. The wind moved through the tall grass around them. A grasshopper jumped from one blade to another with a quiet click. Jesus let the question sit in the air for a moment. "If you cannot do even such a small thing," he said, "why do you worry about the rest?"

Then Jesus turned and pointed to the flowers growing along the hillside. Bright petals swayed in the breeze. Yellow blossoms clustered together near a low stone wall. Purple flowers dotted the spaces between the rocks. Some of the petals were thin and delicate. Others were broad and open, catching the full light of the afternoon sun.

"Think about how the lilies grow," Jesus said. "They don't work. They don't spin thread." He leaned forward and touched one of the stems gently. The flower bobbed under his hand and then straightened again. Its petals were smooth and bright. "But I tell you," he said, "even Solomon in all his fine robes was not dressed as beautifully as one of these."

A few of the disciples looked down at the flowers near their feet. Small purple blossoms grew between the stones, open and still. One man reached down and cupped a flower lightly in his hand, looking at its petals. Another disciple brushed his fingers across the top of the grass, where tiny white flowers grew close to the ground. The hillside was covered with color in every direction.

A yellow butterfly drifted past, circling one of the taller stems before landing on a leaf. Its wings opened and closed slowly. The disciples watched it rest there, bright against the green.

"If God clothes the grass of the field this way," Jesus continued, "grass that is here today and tomorrow is tossed into an oven — how much more will he clothe you?" His voice was steady and clear. He looked around at the group. Some of the men met his eyes. Others looked out at the flowers and the wide hillside stretching before them.

Jesus paused. The breeze picked up again, bending the tall grass in a slow wave that moved across the slope. A few loose petals lifted from the ground and tumbled gently through the air before settling again in the dirt.

He spoke again, his hands resting together in his lap. "Don't keep striving for what you will eat or what you will drink," he said. "Don't worry." He folded his hands. His fingers laced together. "The nations of the world seek after all these things." He looked around at each of them, one by one, turning slowly where he sat. "But your Father knows that you need them."

One of the ravens lifted off the ground with a quick beat of its wings. It flew low over the wildflowers, and its shade passed across the grass for just a moment. Then it landed again a few paces away and went back to pecking at the dirt. Another bird joined it, and the two of them moved together across the open ground.

A disciple near the back of the group shifted his weight and pulled his cloak tighter around his shoulders. The man beside him set down his water skin and folded his arms across his knees. The group was quiet. The only sounds were the wind, the birds, and the soft rustling of grass.

Jesus spoke again. "Instead, seek God's care," he said, "and these things will be given to you as well."

His words hung in the still air. The breeze had settled. The hillside was quiet. Sunlight rested on the grass and on the disciples sitting close together. A warm glow spread across the slope. The stones were warm to the touch. The flowers held their bright colors in the steady light.

A few of the disciples brushed dust from their hands. One man pulled a piece of bread from his leather bag. He broke it slowly, and the soft sound of the crust cracking carried in the quiet. He handed half to the man sitting next to him. That man nodded and took it. They ate together without speaking.

Another disciple stretched his arms above his head and then rested them behind him, leaning back in the grass. He looked up at the wide sky. The thin clouds had barely moved since they had all sat down.

Down the slope, the olive trees swayed in a gentle gust. Their leaves flashed silver and green, over and over. A small brown bird landed on one of the lower branches and sang a short, clear note. It sang again, then tucked its head and sat still.

The wildflowers kept swaying. The ravens hopped and pecked at the ground nearby, full and unhurried. One of them ruffled its feathers and settled into a patch of warm dirt. The butterfly had moved to a new flower, its wings still opening and closing in the sun.

Jesus sat among his friends on the warm hillside. The bread was shared. The water was passed. The afternoon stretched on, gentle and bright. The hillside held them all together, and the air smelled of grass and herbs and warm earth.
