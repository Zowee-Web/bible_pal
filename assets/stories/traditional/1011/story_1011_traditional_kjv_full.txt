A warm afternoon stretched over the hillside near the Sea of Galilee. Tall grass bent gently in the breeze. Wild mustard plants dotted the slope with tiny yellow blossoms. The air bore the savour of dry earth and green leaves.

People sat in small companies across the hillside. Some leaned against smooth rocks. Others rested upon the grass with their sandals tucked beside them. Mothers held little children upon their laps. Older men crossed their legs and settled their hands upon their knees. A few young women shaded their eyes and looked toward the front of the gathering.

Jesus stood where every man could see him. His robe moved softly in the wind. His disciples sat close, but a few steps away. Some of them had their cloaks folded beneath them. Others sat with their arms resting upon their knees. The whole hillside was quiet, and the people leaned forward to hearken.

Jesus lifted up his voice so the multitude could hear. He spake unto his disciples, but his words carried across the grass unto every one gathered there.

"Take no thought for your life," he said, "what ye shall eat." He paused. A bird called out somewhere behind him. "Neither for the body, what ye shall put on."

A few people shifted where they sat. Some tilted their heads. A woman near the back adjusted her head covering and kept listening.

"The life is more than meat," Jesus continued. "And the body is more than raiment."

He raised one hand and pointed unto the open air above the hillside. Several ravens glided overhead, their dark wings spread wide. They circled slowly, riding the warm air that rose from the ground below.

"Consider the ravens," Jesus said. His voice was steady and clear. "For they neither sow nor reap. They have neither storehouse nor barn."

The birds drifted in a wide, easy loop. Their wings caught the sunlight.

"And God feedeth them," Jesus said. He brought his hand back down. "How much more are ye better than the fowls?"

A child near the front pointed up at the ravens and tugged on his father's sleeve. The father smiled and gently guided the boy's hand back down.

Jesus went on speaking. "And which of you with taking thought can add to his stature one cubit?"

No man answered. The breeze rustled through the grass. A few heads shook slowly from side to side.

"If ye then be not able to do that thing which is least," he said, "why take ye thought for the rest?"

He turned slightly and gestured toward the hillside itself. Flowers grew in patches among the rocks and grass. Red anemones, small white blossoms, and those bright yellow mustard flowers all swayed together in the moving air.

"Consider the lilies how they grow," Jesus said. His hand swept gently over the scene. "They toil not, they spin not."

He let the words settle for a moment. A woman in the midst of the crowd looked down at the simple cloth of her own robe, then looked back up.

"And yet I say unto you," Jesus said, "that Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these."

A few people glanced at the flowers nigh. The petals moved lightly in the breeze. Each blossom was delicate and bright, open to the afternoon sun.

Jesus stepped forward slightly. His sandals pressed into the soft ground. "If then God so clothe the grass of the field, which is to day, and to morrow is cast into the oven — how much more will he clothe you?"

He looked across the faces of them that hearkened. Some of them had rough, patched garments. Some had dust upon their hands from long days of work. A young man near the edge of the company rubbed his calloused palms together and sat very still.

"O ye of little faith," Jesus said. His voice was not sharp. It was warm and direct, like one speaking to those for whom he cared.

He continued without ceasing. "And seek not ye what ye shall eat, or what ye shall drink. Neither be ye of doubtful mind."

An older woman rocked a sleeping babe in her arms. The babe's small fingers curled against the woman's shoulder.

"For all these things do the nations of the world seek after," Jesus said. He moved his hands outward, as if showing something afar off. "But your Father knoweth that ye have need of these things."

A few of the disciples nodded. One of them adjusted his cloak and folded his arms across his breast. Another rested his chin in his hand and kept his eyes upon Jesus.

"But rather," Jesus said, and his voice lifted just a little, "seek ye the kingdom of God. And all these things shall be added unto you."

The breeze picked up for a moment. It moved through the multitude like a long, slow breath. Cloaks rippled. Hair lifted from foreheads. The yellow and red flowers along the hillside bobbed and swayed.

Jesus stood still. The afternoon light rested upon his face and upon the people gathered round about him. No man arose to depart. The hillside was full and quiet.

A raven passed overhead once more, gliding without effort. Wildflowers held their bright faces open unto the sky. The people sat together upon the warm grass, close to one another, close to the one who spake unto them. The breeze bore the savour of earth and blossoms across the whole hillside, and the afternoon went on, gentle and unhurried.
