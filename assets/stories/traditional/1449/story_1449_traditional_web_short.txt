David and his men came up to the walls of Jerusalem.

The Jebusites stood on the wall above them. They were the inhabitants of the land. They had held the stronghold of Zion since the days before David was born.

They called down to him.

"You will not come in here," they said. "Take away the blind and the lame, and even they will turn you back. David cannot come in here."

The wall stood high above the slope. The gate was shut and barred. The slope below was steep and broken with stones.

David sat down before the stronghold with his men. He looked at the walls. He looked at the watercourse that came down out of the rock under the wall — the channel where the water of the spring ran into the city.

He said to his men, "Whoever strikes the Jebusites — let him go up by the watercourse."

His men went to the watercourse.

They came to the mouth of the shaft where it ran out beneath the wall. The water was running through it from the spring above. Their feet got wet at the entrance.

They climbed up into the rock in the dark. Their feet pressed against the wet stone. Their hands found the holds above them. They could hear the water running beside them in the channel. They could not see the man in front of them. They went up by feel, one stone at a time.

They came out at the top of the shaft.

They came up into the city through the channel. They struck the wall from the inside. They opened the gate to David below.

David came up into the stronghold of Zion.

He took the city. He called it the city of David. He built around it from the Millo and inward. He dwelt in the stronghold he had taken.

The wall the Jebusites had held stood now around the house of David. Yahweh the God of Armies was with him.
