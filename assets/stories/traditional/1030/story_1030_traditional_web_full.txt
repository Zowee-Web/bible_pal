The Lord said to Samuel, "How long will you mourn for Saul, since I have rejected him as king over Israel? Fill your horn with oil and go. I am sending you to Jesse of Bethlehem. I have provided for myself a king among his sons."

Samuel had a problem. "How can I go?" he asked. "If Saul hears about it, he will kill me."

The Lord said, "Take a heifer with you and say, 'I have come to sacrifice to the Lord.' Invite Jesse to the sacrifice, and I will show you what to do. You shall anoint for me the one I point out to you."

So Samuel did what the Lord told him. He took a young cow and traveled to Bethlehem. It was a small town set among gentle hills. Olive trees grew on the slopes. Sheep dotted the pastures around it.

When Samuel arrived, the elders of the town came out to meet him. They were shaking with nervousness. Samuel was an important man — a prophet of God. When a prophet showed up unexpectedly, people worried. They asked, "Do you come in peace?"

Samuel answered, "In peace. I have come to sacrifice to the Lord. Consecrate yourselves and come with me to the sacrifice." He went to Jesse's family especially and invited them.

When they came, Samuel looked at Jesse's oldest son, Eliab. He was tall. He was strong. He stood straight and confident. He looked like a king. Samuel's heart jumped. "Surely this is the Lord's anointed," he thought.

But the Lord spoke to Samuel. "Do not look at his appearance or the height of his stature, because I have rejected him."

Then God said something Samuel would never forget. "For the Lord does not see as man sees. Man looks at the outward appearance, but the Lord looks at the heart."

The heart. Not the height. Not the muscles. Not the strong jaw or the broad shoulders. God was looking at something no one else could see.

Samuel turned back to Jesse. Jesse called his second son, Abinadab, and had him walk before Samuel. Samuel watched carefully, listening for God's voice. "The Lord has not chosen this one," Samuel said.

Jesse called Shammah, his third son. He walked before Samuel. "The Lord has not chosen this one either."

Then Jesse called another son. And another. And another. Seven sons in all passed before Samuel, each one standing tall, each one hoping to be chosen. Seven times Samuel listened. Seven times the answer was no.

Samuel looked at Jesse. His brow was creased with concern. "Are these all the sons you have?"

Jesse hesitated. "Well," he said, "there is still the youngest. But he is out keeping the sheep."

The youngest. Out in the fields. Not even important enough, it seemed, to be brought in for this occasion. Nobody had thought to call him.

"Send for him," Samuel said. His voice was firm. "We will not sit down until he arrives here."

Jesse sent a servant running to the pastures. The group waited. The sacrifice was ready. The oil was in Samuel's horn. But they could not begin until the last son came.

Time passed. Then footsteps on the path. A boy appeared, coming in from the fields. His skin was ruddy — tanned and warm from being outside in the sun and wind. His eyes were bright and clear. He was young, probably still a teenager. His clothes were simple shepherd's clothes, and he smelled like grass and sheep.

His name was David.

The Lord spoke to Samuel. The words were clear and sure. "Arise. Anoint him. This is the one."

Samuel stood. He took the horn of oil — a curved animal horn filled with rich, golden olive oil. He stepped toward David. The boy's older brothers watched. Jesse watched. The elders of Bethlehem watched.

Samuel tipped the horn over David's head. The oil flowed out, golden and thick. It ran over David's hair and down his forehead. It ran along his cheeks and dripped from his chin. The sweet smell of olive oil filled the air.

David stood still while the oil ran down. His bright eyes looked up at Samuel. His brothers stood around him — all seven of them, taller, older, stronger. But God had chosen the youngest. The shepherd boy. The one nobody had thought to call in from the fields.

From that day forward, the Spirit of the Lord came upon David in power. It rushed over him like a strong wind and stayed with him. The boy who had been watching sheep in the pasture that morning was now the one God had chosen to be the next king of Israel.

Samuel finished and stepped back. The oil glistened on David's head in the sunlight. The sacrifice was offered. The day went on. But everything had changed. God had looked at the hearts of eight brothers and chosen the one everyone else had overlooked — the youngest, the smallest, the one out in the field with the sheep.
