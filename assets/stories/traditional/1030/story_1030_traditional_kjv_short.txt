The Lord spake unto Samuel. "How long wilt thou mourn for Saul? I have rejected him from being king over Israel. Fill thine horn with oil, and go. I will send thee to Jesse the Bethlehemite. I have chosen me a king among his sons."

Samuel was sore troubled. "If Saul hear it, he will kill me," he said.

The Lord told Samuel to take an heifer with him and say he was come to sacrifice unto the Lord. The Lord would shew him what he should do.

So Samuel went unto Bethlehem. When he was come thither, the elders of the town came forth to meet him. They trembled at his coming. "Comest thou peaceably?" they said.

"Peaceably," Samuel said. "I am come to sacrifice unto the Lord." He bade Jesse and his sons come.

When they were come, Samuel beheld the eldest son, Eliab. He was tall and strong. Samuel thought, "Surely the Lord's anointed is before him."

But the Lord said unto Samuel, "Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature. I have refused him. The Lord seeth not as man seeth. Man looketh on the outward appearance, but the Lord looketh on the heart."

Then Jesse called Abinadab, his second son, and made him pass before Samuel. "Neither hath the Lord chosen this," Samuel said. Then came Shammah. The same answer. One by one, seven of Jesse's sons passed before Samuel. The Lord said nay to every one.

Samuel said unto Jesse, "Are here all thy children?"

Jesse paused. "There remaineth yet the youngest," he said. "And behold, he keepeth the sheep."

"Send and fetch him," Samuel said. "We will not sit down till he come hither."

Jesse sent and brought him in. The lad came in from the fields. He was ruddy, and of a fair countenance, and goodly to look to. His name was David.

The Lord said unto Samuel, "Arise, anoint him: for this is he."

Samuel took the horn of oil and poured it upon David's head in the midst of his brethren. The oil ran down upon his hair and his face. And the Spirit of the Lord came mightily upon David from that day forward.
