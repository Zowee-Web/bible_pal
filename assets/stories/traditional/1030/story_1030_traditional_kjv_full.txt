The Lord said unto Samuel, "How long wilt thou mourn for Saul, seeing I have rejected him from reigning over Israel? Fill thine horn with oil, and go. I will send thee to Jesse the Bethlehemite. I have provided me a king among his sons."

Samuel had a trouble. "How can I go?" he said. "If Saul hear it, he will kill me."

The Lord said, "Take an heifer with thee, and say, 'I am come to sacrifice to the Lord.' Call Jesse to the sacrifice, and I will shew thee what thou shalt do. Thou shalt anoint unto me him whom I name unto thee."

So Samuel did that which the Lord spake. He took a young heifer and journeyed unto Bethlehem. It was a small town set among gentle hills. Olive trees grew upon the slopes. Sheep dotted the pastures round about it.

When Samuel was come, the elders of the town came forth to meet him. They trembled at his coming. Samuel was a great man — a prophet of the Lord. When a prophet came on a sudden, the people were afraid. "Comest thou peaceably?" they said.

"Peaceably," Samuel answered. "I am come to sacrifice unto the Lord. Sanctify yourselves, and come with me to the sacrifice." He went especially unto Jesse's house and called them.

When they were come, Samuel beheld Jesse's eldest son, Eliab. He was tall. He was strong. He stood upright and full of confidence. He had the look of a king. Samuel's heart leapt up. "Surely the Lord's anointed is before him," he thought.

But the Lord spake unto Samuel. "Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him."

Then God said somewhat Samuel would never forget. "For the Lord seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the Lord looketh on the heart."

The heart. Not the height. Not the strength of arm. Not the strong jaw nor the broad shoulders. The Lord was looking upon somewhat no man else could see.

Samuel turned again to Jesse. Jesse called Abinadab, his second son, and made him pass before Samuel. Samuel beheld him carefully, hearkening for the voice of the Lord. "Neither hath the Lord chosen this," Samuel said.

Jesse called Shammah, his third son. He passed before Samuel. "Neither hath the Lord chosen this."

Then Jesse called yet another son. And another. And another. Seven sons in all passed before Samuel, each one standing tall, each one hoping to be chosen. Seven times Samuel hearkened. Seven times the answer was nay.

Samuel beheld Jesse. His brow was creased with concern. "Are here all thy children?"

Jesse paused. "Well," he said, "there remaineth yet the youngest. And behold, he keepeth the sheep."

The youngest. Out in the fields. Not even deemed worthy, it seemed, to be brought in for this hour. None had thought to call him.

"Send and fetch him," Samuel said. His voice was firm. "We will not sit down till he come hither."

Jesse sent a servant running to the pastures. The company waited. The sacrifice was made ready. The oil was in Samuel's horn. But they could not begin until the last son was come.

Time passed. Then footsteps upon the path. A lad appeared, coming in from the fields. He was ruddy — tanned and warm from being without in the sun and the wind. His eyes were bright and clear. He was young, scarce more than a youth. His garments were simple shepherd's garments, and he bore the savour of grass and sheep.

His name was David.

The Lord spake unto Samuel. The words were clear and sure. "Arise, anoint him: for this is he."

Samuel arose. He took the horn of oil — a curved horn filled with rich, golden olive oil. He stepped toward David. The lad's elder brethren beheld. Jesse beheld. The elders of Bethlehem beheld.

Samuel tipped the horn over David's head. The oil flowed forth, golden and thick. It ran down upon his hair and upon his forehead. It ran along his cheeks and dropped from his chin. The sweet savour of olive oil filled the air.

David stood still as the oil ran down. His bright eyes looked up unto Samuel. His brethren stood about him — all seven of them, taller, elder, stronger. But the Lord had chosen the youngest. The shepherd lad. The one none had thought to call in from the fields.

From that day forward, the Spirit of the Lord came mightily upon David. It rushed over him like a strong wind and abode with him. The lad who had been keeping sheep in the pasture that morning was now the one God had chosen to be the next king of Israel.

Samuel made an end and stepped back. The oil glistened upon David's head in the sunlight. The sacrifice was offered. The day went on. But every thing was changed. The Lord had looked upon the hearts of eight brethren and chosen the one whom all other men had overlooked — the youngest, the smallest, the one in the field with the sheep.
