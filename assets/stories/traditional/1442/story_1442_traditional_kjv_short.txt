They were walking together when it came.

Elijah and Elisha had crossed the Jordan that morning. They had spoken on the way, master and disciple, prophet and prophet's son. They were going on still. They were talking.

Then it came down between them.

A chariot of fire and horses of fire — the wheels of the chariot turning, the flank of each horse burning, the air round about them hot. The fire parted them asunder. Elisha could no more behold his master. The wind thereof tare at his robe. The sound of the hooves filled his ears.

Then Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven.

Elisha saw it. The chariot turned upward. The horses climbed. The mantle was caught in the wind, falling. Elijah was rising.

He cried out, "My father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof!"

And he saw him no more.

He stood in the place wherein they had been walking. He stood there alone. He took hold of his own clothes, and he rent them in two pieces, and he stood there with his garments rent open in the field.

The mantle of Elijah had fallen to the ground. It lay there in the dust. He stooped down and took it up. He turned with it back the way that he had come.

He went down unto the Jordan again. He stood by the bank thereof. The water was running south.

He took the mantle of Elijah that fell from him. He smote the waters. He said, "Where is the Lord God of Elijah?"

He smote the waters a second time.

They parted hither and thither.

And Elisha went over unto the other side. The mantle was in his hand. The bank was empty behind him. The sky over Jordan was open, and Elijah was there no more.
