They were walking together when it came.

Elijah and Elisha had crossed the Jordan that morning. They had spoken on the road, master and disciple, prophet and prophet's son. They were continuing on. They were talking.

Then it came down between them.

A chariot of fire and horses of fire — the wheels of the chariot turning, the flank of each horse burning, the air around them hot. The fire separated them. Elisha could no longer see his master. The wind of it tore at his robe. The sound of the hooves filled his ears.

Then Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven.

Elisha saw it. The chariot turned upward. The horses climbed. The cloak was caught in the wind, falling. Elijah was rising.

He cried out, "My father, my father, the chariots of Israel and its horsemen!"

He saw him no more.

He stood in the place where they had been walking. He stood there alone. He took hold of his own clothes, and he tore them in two pieces, and he stood there with his garments torn open in the field.

The cloak of Elijah had fallen to the ground. It lay there in the dust. He bent down and took it up. He turned with it back the way he had come.

He went down to the Jordan again. He stood by the bank of it. The water was running south.

He took the cloak of Elijah that had fallen from him. He struck the waters. He said, "Where is Yahweh, the God of Elijah?"

He struck the waters a second time.

They divided apart.

Elisha went over to the other side. The cloak was in his hand. The bank was empty behind him. The sky over the Jordan was open, and Elijah was not there anymore.
