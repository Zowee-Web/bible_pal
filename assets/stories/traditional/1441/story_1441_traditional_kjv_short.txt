By faith, Abraham, when he was called, obeyed to go out unto the place which he should after receive for an inheritance. He went out, not knowing whither he went.

He took his household. He took his tents, his cattle, his goods. He came unto the land of Canaan and dwelt there. The land was not his. The wells therein had been digged by other hands. The fields had been wrought by other feet.

But he had heard the voice of God, and he was come.

By faith he sojourned in the land of promise, as in a strange country, dwelling in tabernacles with Isaac and Jacob, the heirs with him of the same promise.

The tent became not a house in his lifetime. His son's tent became not a house. His grandson's tent became not a house. They removed when the rains failed. They removed when the seasons turned. They were strangers in the land that had been promised unto them.

For he looked for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God.

He looked. He waited. He set his eye upon the city he could not see.

By faith also Sara herself received strength to conceive seed, and was delivered of a child when she was past age, because she judged him faithful who had promised.

The promise had come unto her at the door of the tent. The laughter had gone forth out of her. The years had passed by. The years had passed by again.

And the child was born unto her in her old age. And the laughter became another kind.

Therefore sprang there even of one, and him as good as dead, so many as the stars of the sky in multitude, and as the sand which is by the sea shore innumerable.

The stars stood over the tent by night. The sand stretched along the shore at evening. The promise had been made, and the promise had been kept, in the line of one man that waited for the city he could not see.
