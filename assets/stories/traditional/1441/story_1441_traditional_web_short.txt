By faith, Abraham, when he was called, obeyed to go out to the place which he was to receive for an inheritance. He went out, not knowing where he went.

He took his household. He took his tents, his cattle, his goods. He came to the land of Canaan and dwelt there. The land was not his. The wells in it had been dug by other hands. The fields had been worked by other feet.

But he had heard the voice of God, and he had come.

By faith, he lived as an alien in the land of promise, as in a land not his own, dwelling in tents, with Isaac and Jacob, the heirs with him of the same promise.

The tent did not become a house in his lifetime. His son's tent did not become a house. His grandson's tent did not become a house. They moved when the rains failed. They moved when the seasons turned. They were strangers in the land that had been promised to them.

For he looked for the city which has the foundations, whose builder and maker is God.

He looked. He waited. He set his eye upon the city he could not see.

By faith, even Sarah herself received power to conceive, and she bore a child when she was past age, since she counted him faithful who had promised.

The promise had come to her at the door of the tent. The laughter had come out of her. The years had gone by. The years had gone by again.

And the child was born to her in her old age. And the laughter became another kind.

Therefore as many as the stars of the sky in multitude, and as innumerable as the sand which is by the sea shore, were fathered by one man, and him as good as dead.

The stars stood over the tent at night. The sand stretched along the shore at evening. The promise had been made, and the promise had been kept, in the line of one man who waited for the city he could not see.
