A long time without rain had turned the land brown and dry. King Ahab ruled Israel, and the people had turned away from God to worship a false god called Baal. So the prophet Elijah sent word unto the king: gather all the people and all four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal unto Mount Carmel.

They came. Hundreds and hundreds of people climbed the mountain. The prophets of Baal came in a great multitude. King Ahab came also.

Elijah stood before them all. "How long halt ye between two opinions?" he said. "If the Lord be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him."

The people answered him not a word.

Elijah spake again. "I, even I only, remain a prophet of the Lord. But Baal's prophets are four hundred and fifty men." He bade them bring two bullocks. Baal's prophets would prepare one bullock upon their altar. Elijah would prepare the other upon his. No man would put fire under. "The God that answereth by fire," Elijah said, "let him be God."

And all the people answered and said, It is well spoken.

The prophets of Baal went first. They cut up their bullock and laid it upon the wood. Then they began to call. "O Baal, hear us!" they cried. They called from morning until noon. They leaped upon the altar. Nothing came to pass. No voice. No fire. None that answered.

At noon Elijah mocked them. "Cry aloud: for he is a god. Either he is talking, or he is pursuing, or he is in a journey, or peradventure he sleepeth, and must be awaked."

They cried aloud. They leaped upon the altar all afternoon. Yet nothing. The altar sat cold and still.

Then Elijah called the people near. He repaired the altar of the Lord with twelve stones, one for every tribe of Israel. He made a deep trench round about it. He laid the wood on top and put the bullock upon the wood.

"Fill four barrels with water," he said. "Pour it on the burnt sacrifice, and on the wood."

They did. "Do it the second time," he said. They did. "Do it the third time." They poured again. The water ran about the altar and filled the trench unto the brim.

Elijah came near. "Lord God of Abraham, Isaac, and of Israel," he prayed, "let it be known this day that thou art God in Israel, and that I am thy servant."

Then the fire of the Lord fell. It consumed the burnt sacrifice. It consumed the wood and the stones and the dust. It licked up the water that was in the trench.

And when all the people saw it, they fell on their faces: and they said, "The Lord, he is the God! The Lord, he is the God!"
