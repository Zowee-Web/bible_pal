A long time without rain had turned the land brown and dry. King Ahab ruled Israel, and the people had turned away from God to worship a pretend god called Baal. So the prophet Elijah sent word to the king: bring all the people and all four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal to Mount Carmel.

They came. Hundreds and hundreds of people climbed the mountain. The prophets of Baal came in a great crowd. King Ahab came too.

Elijah stood before them all. "How long will you go back and forth between two sides?" he said. "If the Lord is God, follow Him. If Baal is god, follow him."

The people said nothing. Not one word.

Elijah spoke again. "I am the only prophet of the Lord left here. But Baal has four hundred and fifty prophets." He told them to bring two bulls. Baal's prophets would prepare one bull on their altar. Elijah would prepare the other on his. No one would light a fire. "The god who answers by fire," Elijah said, "he is God."

All the people agreed.

The prophets of Baal went first. They cut up their bull and laid it on the wood. Then they began to call out. "O Baal, answer us!" they shouted. They called from morning until noon. They danced around the altar. Nothing happened. No voice. No fire. No answer.

At noon Elijah called to them. "Shout louder! Maybe your god is thinking. Maybe he is busy. Maybe he is sleeping and needs to wake up."

They shouted louder. They leaped around the altar all afternoon. Still nothing. The altar sat cold and quiet.

Then Elijah called the people close. He rebuilt the altar of the Lord with twelve stones, one for each tribe of Israel. He dug a deep trench around it. He laid the wood on top and placed the bull on the wood.

"Fill four large jars with water," he said. "Pour it over the offering and the wood."

They did. "Do it again," he said. They did. "Do it a third time." They poured again. Water ran down the altar and filled the trench to the brim.

Elijah stepped forward. "Lord, God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel," he prayed, "let it be known today that You are God in Israel, and that I am Your servant."

Then fire fell from heaven. It burned up the bull. It burned the wood and the stones and the dust. It licked up every drop of water in the trench.

All the people fell on their faces to the ground. "The Lord, He is God!" they cried. "The Lord, He is God!"
