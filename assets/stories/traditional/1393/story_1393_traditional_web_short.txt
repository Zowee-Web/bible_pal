The wall around Jerusalem had been rising for some weeks now. The builders of Nehemiah's household — the priests, the goldsmiths, the perfumers, the rulers of the districts of Tekoa and Beth Haccherem and Mizpah — had set the stones from the Sheep Gate at the north all the way around toward the Tower of the Furnaces at the west; the rubble of the old walls lay still in heaps along the inside of the line; the men carried loads with one hand and held a weapon in the other. And the strength of the bearers was failing.

Judah said, "The strength of the bearers of burdens is fading, and there is much rubble; so that we are not able to build the wall."

Our adversaries said, "They will not know or see, until we come in among them and kill them, and cause the work to cease."

When the Jews who lived by them came, they said to us ten times from all places, "Wherever you turn, they will attack us."

Therefore I set guards in the lowest parts of the space behind the wall, in the open places. I set the people by family groups with their swords, their spears, and their bows.

I looked, and rose up, and said to the nobles, to the rulers, and to the rest of the people, "Don't be afraid of them! Remember the Lord, who is great and awesome, and fight for your brothers, your sons, your daughters, your wives, and your houses."

And the people set their hands to the wall again in the morning. The bearers of burdens went back to the heaps of rubble; the masons set up their lines along the next length of stone; the watchmen took their positions in the lowest places behind the wall; and the great cry — "Don't be afraid of them; remember the Lord" — stayed in the air over the work, all the way around the unfinished circuit of Jerusalem.
