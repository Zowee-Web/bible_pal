The wall about Jerusalem had been rising for some weeks now. The builders of Nehemiah's household — the priests, the goldsmiths, the apothecaries, the rulers of the districts of Tekoa and Beth-haccerem and Mizpah — had set the stones from the sheep gate at the north all the way round toward the tower of the furnaces at the west; the rubbish of the old walls lay yet in heaps within the line; the men bare burdens with one hand, and with the other hand held a weapon. And the strength of the bearers was decayed.

And Judah said, "The strength of the bearers of burdens is decayed, and there is much rubbish; so that we are not able to build the wall."

And our adversaries said, "They shall not know, neither see, till we come in the midst among them, and slay them, and cause the work to cease."

And it came to pass, that when the Jews which dwelt by them came, they said unto us ten times, "From all places whence ye shall return unto us they will be upon you."

Therefore set I in the lower places behind the wall, and on the higher places, I even set the people after their families with their swords, their spears, and their bows.

And I looked, and rose up, and said unto the nobles, and to the rulers, and to the rest of the people, "Be not ye afraid of them: remember the Lord, which is great and terrible, and fight for your brethren, your sons, and your daughters, your wives, and your houses."

And the people set their hands unto the wall again in the morning. The bearers of burdens went back unto the heaps of rubbish; the masons set their lines along the next length of stone; the watchmen took their places in the lower spaces behind the wall; and the great cry — "be not ye afraid of them: remember the Lord" — abode in the air over the work, all the way round the unfinished compass of Jerusalem.
