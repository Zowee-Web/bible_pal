Jesus left Judaea, and departed again into Galilee. And he must needs go through Samaria.

The road through Samaria climbed and fell across pale hills. The disciples walked behind him, their sandals raising small puffs of dust. The sun rose to the top of the sky and stayed there. By the time they came unto a city of Samaria called Sychar, near to the parcel of ground that Jacob gave to his son Joseph, the heat was full upon the day.

Now Jacob's well was there. The well was deep. The stone rim was worn smooth by the hands of generations.

Jesus therefore, being wearied with his journey, sat thus on the well. He set his hands upon the warm stone. He let his head bow for a moment, then lifted it. And it was about the sixth hour. The sun stood directly overhead. The shadows were short. His disciples were gone away unto the city to buy meat, and he was alone by the well.

The plain about him was still. A few goats moved in the distance. The city shewed its low walls and white roofs through the heat shimmer. No man was out upon the path. At the sixth hour, the women of the town came at dawn or in the cool of the evening. The well stood quiet beneath the sun.

Then footsteps came along the path.

There cometh a woman of Samaria to draw water. She walked alone. Her waterpot rode on her shoulder, balanced with one hand. Her sandals scuffed the dust. She came at noon because at noon no other came, and she had learned, year by year, to choose the heat above the eyes of other women. She lifted not her face until she reached the well.

When she looked up, she saw him.

Jesus saith unto her, "Give me to drink."

The woman went still. She set the waterpot down upon the rim with both hands. The clay tapped against the stone. She looked at him, then past him, then at him again.

"How is it that thou, being a Jew, askest drink of me, which am a woman of Samaria?"

For the Jews have no dealings with the Samaritans.

Jesus answered and said unto her, "If thou knewest the gift of God, and who it is that saith to thee, 'Give me to drink,' thou wouldest have asked of him, and he would have given thee living water."

The woman looked upon his hands. They were empty. She looked upon the dark mouth of the well.

"Sir, thou hast nothing to draw with, and the well is deep. From whence then hast thou that living water? Art thou greater than our father Jacob, which gave us the well, and drank thereof himself, and his children, and his cattle?"

Jesus moved not from the rim. He spake quietly, and his voice carried in the still air.

"Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again. But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst. But the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life."

The woman's hands rested upon the lip of the waterpot. She looked not away from him.

"Sir, give me this water, that I thirst not, neither come hither to draw."

There was a small pause. The light fell white upon the stones. A breath of wind moved across the open ground and was gone.

Jesus saith unto her, "Go, call thy husband, and come hither."

The woman's eyes lowered.

"I have no husband."

Jesus said unto her, "Thou hast well said, 'I have no husband,' for thou hast had five husbands, and he whom thou now hast is not thy husband. In that saidst thou truly."

The wind moved not. Her hand on the waterpot tightened, then loosened. She stepped not back. She gathered not the waterpot to leave. She stood in the place she had stood, and she looked upon the man on the rim, and her face changed.

"Sir, I perceive that thou art a prophet."

Her voice was lower now. She turned her face toward the hills behind him, where Mount Gerizim rose above the plain.

"Our fathers worshipped in this mountain, and ye say, that in Jerusalem is the place where men ought to worship."

Jesus saith unto her, "Woman, believe me, the hour cometh, when ye shall neither in this mountain, nor yet at Jerusalem, worship the Father. Ye worship ye know not what. We know what we worship, for salvation is of the Jews. But the hour cometh, and now is, when the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth, for the Father seeketh such to worship him. God is a Spirit, and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth."

The words settled into the noon. The woman answered not at once. She looked upon the well. She looked upon him. The light upon the stones was very bright.

"I know that Messias cometh, which is called Christ. When he is come, he will tell us all things."

Jesus saith unto her, "I that speak unto thee am he."

And upon this came his disciples back along the path. They had bread in cloth and a skin of food slung between them, and they slowed as they neared the well. They marvelled that he talked with the woman. They brake not the silence. No man said, "What seekest thou?" No man said, "Why talkest thou with her?" They stood at a small distance and waited.

The woman then left her waterpot.

She took it not up. She drew not the water she had come for. She turned from the well and walked back along the path she had come, and her steps were quicker now, and she looked not back. The waterpot sat where she had set it, full of nothing, balanced upon the worn stone.

She went her way into the city. The first she met were in the street near the gate, and her voice carried.

"Come, see a man, which told me all things that ever I did. Is not this the Christ?"

Heads turned. A man stopped where he was. Another set down that which he carried. The woman which had walked unto the well alone at noon now spake in the open street, and the people hearkened.

They went out of the city. They came unto him. A line of men and women began along the path toward Jacob's well, the city behind them, the noon sun still high, the stones of the well still warm.

The waterpot sat upon the stone rim of the well.
