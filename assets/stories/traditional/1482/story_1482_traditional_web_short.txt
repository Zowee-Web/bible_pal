It was the first day of the week, in the upper room at Troas.

The disciples had gathered together to break bread. Paul was with them, and would be leaving on the next day for the road south. He spoke to them, and the speech went on into the night.

There were many lights in the upper room. The room was on the third floor of the house, and the windows stood open in the heat.

A certain young man was sitting in one of the windows. His name was Eutychus.

The hour grew late. Paul went on speaking. Eutychus was weighed down with deep sleep where he sat in the window, and the more Paul spoke, the more the sleep weighed him down, until at last he went forward into it and fell out the window.

He fell from the third floor.

He was taken up dead.

Paul went down out of the upper room. He came to where the boy lay on the ground. He fell upon him. He embraced him.

He said to those who had come down with him, "Don't be troubled. His life is in him."

He came back up to the upper room.

He broke bread. He ate. He talked with them a long while longer — until the break of day. Then he departed, the way he had said he would.

The boy was brought into the upper room alive, before Paul had gone, and the disciples were greatly comforted.

The lights in the upper room burned low into the morning. The empty window was open behind them, and the sound of the city beginning the day was rising up from below.
