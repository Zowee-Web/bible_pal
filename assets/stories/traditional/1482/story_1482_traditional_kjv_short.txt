It was the first day of the week, in the upper chamber at Troas.

The disciples had come together to break bread. Paul was with them, ready to depart on the morrow for the road south. He preached unto them, and his speech continued until midnight.

There were many lights in the upper chamber. The chamber was on the third loft of the house, and the windows stood open in the heat.

There sat in a window a certain young man named Eutychus.

The hour grew late. Paul went on preaching. Eutychus was fallen into a deep sleep where he sat in the window, and as Paul was long preaching, he sunk down with sleep, and fell down from the third loft.

He was taken up dead.

Paul went down out of the upper chamber. He came to where the young man lay on the ground. He fell on him. He embraced him.

He said unto them that had come down with him, "Trouble not yourselves; for his life is in him."

He came back up to the upper chamber.

He had broken bread. He had eaten. He talked with them a long while longer — even till break of day. Then he departed, as he had said he would.

They brought the young man alive into the upper chamber, before Paul had gone, and they were not a little comforted.

The lights in the upper chamber burned low into the morning. The empty window was open behind them, and the sound of the city beginning the day was rising up from below.
