The sons of the prophets said to Elisha, "See now, the place where we dwell and meet with you is too small for us. Please let us go to the Jordan, and each man take a beam from there, and let us make a place there, where we may dwell."

Elisha answered, "Go."

One of them said, "Please be content, and go with your servants." Elisha answered, "I will go." So he went with them.

When they came to the Jordan, they cut down trees. The morning was warm above the river, and the men labored together by the bank. The sound of the axes rose along the water, and the chips of wood lay scattered upon the ground.

But as one was cutting down a beam, the axe head fell into the water. He cried out, "Alas, my master, for it was borrowed."

The man of God said, "Where did it fall?" He showed him the place. Elisha cut down a stick, threw it in there, and made the iron float.

He said, "Take it." So the man put out his hand and took it. The water rose up around his arm, and he lifted the axe head from where it had been lost.

The work continued by the river. The men gathered the beams and laid them upon the ground, and they prepared to build the place where they would dwell. The Jordan moved slowly past them, and the morning grew warm above the trees.

The sons of the prophets had seen what Elisha had done. They went on with their labor, and they did not speak of it for a long time, but each man kept the wonder of it in his heart.

When the day was ended, they returned to their place upstream. The axe heads were carried with them, and the borrowed iron was returned to the one who had lent it.

The river ran on past the place where they had cut the trees. The trees stood quiet along the banks, and the water moved on toward the sea.
