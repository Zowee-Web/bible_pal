The sons of the prophets said unto Elisha, "Behold now, the place where we dwell with thee is too strait for us. Let us go, we pray thee, unto Jordan, and take thence every man a beam, and let us make us a place there, where we may dwell."

Elisha answered, "Go ye."

And one of them said, "Be content, I pray thee, and go with thy servants." Elisha answered, "I will go." So he went with them.

When they came to Jordan, they cut down trees. The morning was warm above the river, and the men laboured together by the bank. The sound of the axes rose along the water, and the chips of wood lay scattered upon the ground.

But as one was felling a beam, the axe head fell into the water. He cried, and said, "Alas, master! for it was borrowed."

And the man of God said, "Where fell it?" And he shewed him the place. And he cut down a stick, and cast it in thither; and the iron did swim.

He said, "Take it up to thee." And he put out his hand, and took it. The water rose up about his arm, and he lifted the axe head from the place where it had been lost.

The work continued by the river. The men gathered the beams and laid them upon the ground, and they made ready to build the place where they should dwell. Jordan moved slowly past them, and the morning grew warm above the trees.

The sons of the prophets had beheld what Elisha had done. They went on with their labour, and they spake not of it for a long season, but each man kept the wonder of it in his heart.

When the day was ended, they returned unto their place upstream. The axe heads were carried with them, and the borrowed iron was rendered unto him that had lent it.

The river ran on past the place where they had cut the trees. The trees stood quiet along the banks, and the water moved on toward the sea.
