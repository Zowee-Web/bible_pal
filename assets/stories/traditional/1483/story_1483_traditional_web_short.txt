This was one of the songs the pilgrims sang as they walked up to Jerusalem.

They sang it on the roads that crossed the dry hills. They sang it when the sandals were worn thin and the wells were far apart between one and the next. They sang it as they came down through the valley of Weeping and up onto the rise where the city first came into view.

The song was this —

"How lovely are your dwellings, Yahweh of Armies.

My soul longs, and even faints for the courts of Yahweh. My heart and my flesh cry out for the living God.

Yes, the sparrow has found a home, and the swallow a nest for herself, where she may have her young, near your altars, Yahweh of Armies, my King, and my God.

Blessed are those who dwell in your house. They are always praising you.

Blessed are those whose strength is in you, who have set their hearts on a pilgrimage.

Passing through the valley of Weeping, they make it a place of springs. Yes, the autumn rain covers it with blessings.

They go from strength to strength. Every one of them appears before God in Zion."

The pilgrims sang it as they climbed. The dust of the road rose around their feet. The valley of Weeping was behind them, or below them, or still to come — it differed for each pilgrim by where they were in the journey.

But the song was the same.

And the sparrows that had made nests in the eaves of the temple did not know that they had been sung of. They flew their small flights from the altars to the cedars and back, and the song went up from the road below, and the road climbed steadily toward Zion in the heat of the day.
