This was one of the songs the pilgrims sang as they came up to Jerusalem.

They sang it on the roads that crossed the dry hills. They sang it when the sandals were worn thin and the wells were far apart between one and the next. They sang it as they came down through the valley of Baca and up onto the rise where the city first came into view.

The song was this —

"How amiable are thy tabernacles, O Lord of hosts.

My soul longeth, yea, even fainteth for the courts of the Lord. My heart and my flesh crieth out for the living God.

Yea, the sparrow hath found an house, and the swallow a nest for herself, where she may lay her young, even thine altars, O Lord of hosts, my King, and my God.

Blessed are they that dwell in thy house. They will be still praising thee.

Blessed is the man whose strength is in thee, in whose heart are the ways of them.

Who passing through the valley of Baca make it a well. The rain also filleth the pools.

They go from strength to strength. Every one of them in Zion appeareth before God."

The pilgrims sang it as they climbed. The dust of the road rose round about their feet. The valley of Baca was behind them, or below them, or yet to come — it differed for each pilgrim by where they were in the journey.

But the song was the same.

And the sparrows that had built their nests in the eaves of the temple knew not that they had been sung of. They flew their small flights from the altars to the cedars and back, and the song went up from the road below, and the road climbed steadily toward Zion in the heat of the day.
