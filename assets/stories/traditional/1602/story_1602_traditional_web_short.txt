In the reign of Artaxerxes king of Persia, a scribe named Ezra came up from Babylon to Jerusalem. He was a descendant of Aaron the chief priest, and he was skilled in the law of Moses, which Yahweh the God of Israel had given. The king granted him everything he asked, for the good hand of Yahweh his God was on him. With him went up priests, Levites, singers, gatekeepers, and temple servants. The journey took him from the first month to the fifth, and he came to Jerusalem according to the good hand of his God on him. For Ezra had set his heart to study the law of Yahweh, to do it, and to teach its statutes and ordinances in Israel.

The king had sent a letter with him. "Artaxerxes, king of kings, to Ezra the priest, scribe of the law of the God of heaven: I decree that any of Israel in my kingdom who wish to go to Jerusalem may go with you. You are sent to carry the silver and gold that I and my counselors have freely offered to the God of Israel, and to buy with it bulls, rams, and lambs to offer on his altar. Whatever more the house of your God needs, take it from the royal treasury. None of the priests or temple servants shall be taxed. And you, Ezra, appoint magistrates and judges who know the law of your God, and teach those who do not know it."

Then Ezra said, "Blessed be Yahweh, the God of our fathers, who has put such a thing as this in the king's heart, to beautify the house of Yahweh in Jerusalem, and who has extended his loving kindness to me before the king and his counselors. I was strengthened, for the hand of Yahweh my God was on me, and I gathered leaders out of Israel to go up with me."
