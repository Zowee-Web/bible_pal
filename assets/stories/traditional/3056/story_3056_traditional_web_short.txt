Paul remembered the believers with thanksgiving. In every request for them, he prayed with joy because of their partnership in the Good News from the first day until then. He was confident that God, who had begun a good work in them, would complete it until the day of Jesus Christ. They shared grace with him both in his bonds and in the defense and confirmation of the Good News. He prayed that their love would abound in knowledge and discernment, so they might approve excellent things and be filled with the fruits of righteousness through Jesus Christ.

Paul wanted them to understand what his confinement had produced. His bonds in Christ had become evident throughout the palace guard and to others. Many believers had grown more confident through his bonds and spoke God’s word without fear. Some proclaimed Christ from envy and selfish ambition, hoping to add affliction to his chains; others preached from goodwill and love. Paul rejoiced that, whether in pretense or in truth, Christ was proclaimed, and he said he would continue to rejoice.

Through their prayers and the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ, Paul expected that Christ would be magnified in his body, whether by life or by death. To live was Christ, and to die was gain. He was hard pressed between departing to be with Christ, which was far better, and remaining in the flesh, which was more necessary for their sake. Confident that he would remain, he looked toward their progress and joy in the faith.

Paul then charged them to live in a way worthy of the Good News of Christ. Whether he came to see them or remained absent, he wanted to hear that they stood firm in one spirit, striving together with one soul for the faith of the Good News. They were not to be frightened by adversaries. They had been granted not only to believe in Christ but also to suffer on his behalf, sharing the same conflict they had seen in Paul and now heard was still his.
