Paul gave thanks unto God upon every remembrance of the believers. In every prayer for them all, he made request with joy because of their fellowship in the gospel from the first day until then. He was confident that God, who had begun a good work in them, would perform it until the day of Jesus Christ. They were partakers of his grace both in his bonds and in the defence and confirmation of the gospel. He prayed that their love might abound in knowledge and judgment, that they might approve excellent things and be filled with the fruits of righteousness by Jesus Christ.

Paul desired that they should understand what had come through his confinement. His bonds in Christ were manifest throughout the palace and in other places. Many brethren had waxed confident through his bonds and were more bold to speak the word without fear. Some preached Christ from envy, strife, and contention, supposing to add affliction to his bonds; others preached of good will and love. Paul rejoiced that, whether in pretence or in truth, Christ was preached, and he said that he would rejoice.

Through their prayer and the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ, Paul expected that Christ would be magnified in his body, whether by life or by death. To him, to live was Christ, and to die was gain. He was in a strait betwixt departing to be with Christ, which was far better, and abiding in the flesh, which was more needful for them. Confident that he would abide, he looked toward their furtherance and joy of faith.

Paul then charged them to let their conduct become the gospel of Christ. Whether he came and saw them or remained absent, he desired to hear that they stood fast in one spirit, with one mind striving together for the faith of the gospel. They were not to be terrified by their adversaries. It had been given unto them not only to believe on Christ, but also to suffer for his sake, having the same conflict which they had seen in Paul and now heard to be in him.
