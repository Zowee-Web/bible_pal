In the days of his sorrow, the psalmist went up to the watch of the night. The lamps were low in the city, and the streets were quiet. He poured out his soul before the Lord, and he lifted up his voice from the depths.

He said, "Out of the depths I have cried to you, O Lord."

He cried out, "Lord, hear my voice. Let your ears be attentive to the voice of my petitions."

He said, "If you, O Lord, kept a record of sins, who could stand? But there is forgiveness with you. Therefore you are feared."

He prayed, "I wait for the Lord. My soul waits. I hope in his word."

He said, "My soul longs for the Lord more than watchmen long for the morning, more than watchmen long for the morning."

Then he turned toward the people of Israel, and he said, "Israel, hope in the Lord. For there is loving kindness with the Lord. With him is abundant redemption. He will redeem Israel from all their sins."

The night was deep around him, and the lamps of the city had grown dim. He sat in the silence and waited for the morning.

The watchmen stood at their stations along the wall. They looked toward the east, and they kept their watch. The psalmist remained where he was, and he waited as the watchmen waited.

Then the morning came over the hills of Jerusalem. The sun rose above the eastern wall, and the light came down into the streets of the city. The psalmist rose from where he had sat, and he went his way into the day.

The words of his prayer were written down, that they might be read in the days that came after, and that the souls of the people might find hope in them.
