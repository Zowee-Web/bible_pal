In the days of his sorrow, the psalmist went up unto the watch of the night. The lamps were low in the city, and the streets were quiet. He poured out his soul before the Lord, and he lifted up his voice from the depths.

He said, "Out of the depths have I cried unto thee, O Lord."

He cried out, "Lord, hear my voice. Let thine ears be attentive to the voice of my supplications."

He said, "If thou, Lord, shouldest mark iniquities, O Lord, who shall stand? But there is forgiveness with thee, that thou mayest be feared."

He prayed, "I wait for the Lord, my soul doth wait, and in his word do I hope."

He said, "My soul waiteth for the Lord more than they that watch for the morning: I say, more than they that watch for the morning."

Then he turned unto the people of Israel, and he said, "Let Israel hope in the Lord: for with the Lord there is mercy, and with him is plenteous redemption. And he shall redeem Israel from all his iniquities."

The night was deep around him, and the lamps of the city had grown dim. He sat in the silence and waited for the morning.

The watchmen stood at their stations along the wall. They looked toward the east, and they kept their watch. The psalmist remained where he was, and he waited as the watchmen waited.

Then the morning came over the hills of Jerusalem. The sun arose above the eastern wall, and the light came down into the streets of the city. The psalmist arose from where he had sat, and he went his way into the day.

The words of his prayer were written down, that they might be read in the days that came after, and that the souls of the people might find hope therein.
