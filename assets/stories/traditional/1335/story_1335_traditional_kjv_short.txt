Paul had come unto the end of eleven chapters of writing. He had set down the gospel from beginning to end — the wrath of God against ungodliness, the righteousness of God revealed in Christ, the justification of the believer, the freedom from sin, the work of the Spirit, the security of them that are beloved of God, the long and difficult question of his own people Israel, the gathering in of the Gentiles, and the mercy that should come at last to all. And when he had set it all down, and could behold the whole pattern of it laid out — what God had done, was doing, and would do — something rose up in him that no theology could hold, and he set down the doxology.

O the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and knowledge of God! how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past finding out!

For who hath known the mind of the Lord? or who hath been his counsellor?

Or who hath first given to him, and it shall be recompensed unto him again?

For of him, and through him, and to him, are all things: to whom be glory for ever. Amen.

And the apostle set down his pen. The scroll lay before him. The lamp burned low. The days went on as the days of his writing had been going on, and the doxology was sealed into the epistle that should go unto Rome, to be read in the gathering of the believers there. And so it was in the days when the apostle wrote the epistle to the Romans.
