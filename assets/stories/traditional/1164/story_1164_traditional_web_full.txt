The council chamber held its breath. Stephen stood at the center of the half-circle of benches, his hands loose at his sides, his robe still bearing the dust of the streets where he had been seized. The elders watched him from the curved tiers above. The high priest sat at the head, his fingers laid flat along the arm of his seat.

The high priest spoke. "Are these things so?"

Stephen lifted his face. The witnesses against him had said he spoke against the temple and against Moses. He drew a breath. The room waited.

"Brothers and fathers, listen. The God of glory appeared to our father Abraham, when he was in Mesopotamia, before he lived in Haran. He said to him, 'Get out of your land, and from your relatives, and come into a land which I will show you.'"

The eldest on the bench gave a slow nod. The story they all knew. The story of the beginning. A scribe near the wall lowered his stylus and listened.

Stephen carried Abraham forward. The promise of a land for a people not yet born. The covenant. Isaac. Jacob. The twelve patriarchs. He let the names fall in order. The councilmen nodded again. Familiar ground.

Then he turned to Joseph.

"The patriarchs, moved with jealousy against Joseph, sold him into Egypt. God was with him."

The word jealousy moved through the room. A man in the second row shifted in his seat. Stephen carried it onward. The famine. The brothers brought low before the brother they had cast out. Jacob and his household going down into Egypt. The bones brought back to lie in the tomb.

He moved to Moses. He spoke of the new king who did not know Joseph, of the infants cast into the river, of the child drawn from the water and raised in Pharaoh's house. He spoke of the day Moses went out to his brothers and struck the Egyptian, and how his brothers did not understand that God was giving deliverance by his hand. He spoke of Midian, and of the bush that burned and was not consumed, and the voice that called him back.

The room had grown quieter. The shift was small. The scribe forgot his tablet altogether.

"This Moses whom they refused," Stephen said, "saying, 'Who made you a ruler and a judge?' — God has sent him as both a ruler and a deliverer."

The eldest leaned forward.

Stephen spoke of the wilderness. Of the people who said to Aaron, "Make us gods that will go before us." Of the calf they made, and the sacrifice they offered to the work of their own hands, and of how God turned away.

The high priest's fingers closed on the arm of his seat.

Stephen spoke of the tabernacle in the wilderness. Of David asking to build a house. Of Solomon building it. Then his voice settled lower, and the room settled with it.

"However, the Most High does not dwell in temples made with hands."

A murmur ran along the back tier. It died quickly. The elders waited for what was coming, because they had begun to feel where the story was bending.

Stephen lifted his eyes from the floor and let them travel along the curved benches. He looked at each face.

"Brothers and fathers" was gone. What came next came at them.

"You stiff-necked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, you always resist the Holy Spirit! As your fathers did, so do you! Which of the prophets did your fathers not persecute? They killed those who foretold the coming of the Righteous One, of whom you have now become betrayers and murderers. You received the law as it was ordained by angels, and did not keep it."

The room broke.

Men were on their feet. Robes swung. The eldest pressed his palms against his ears. The high priest was rising. Teeth showed. Hands shook. The murmur became a roar. And in the middle of it Stephen, full of the Holy Spirit, looked up into the rafters of the chamber as if the roof were not there.

His face changed. It grew bright. It became as the face of an angel. And he saw what they could not. He saw the heavens opened, and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God.

They covered their ears. They cried out with a loud voice. They rushed upon him with one accord. They cast him out of the city, and they stoned him. And the witnesses laid down their cloaks at the feet of a young man named Saul.

Stephen knelt. He cried with a loud voice, "Lord, do not hold this sin against them."

And when he had said this, he fell asleep. The stones lay where they had fallen. The morning held still.
