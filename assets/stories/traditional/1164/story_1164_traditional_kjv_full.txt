The council chamber held its breath. Stephen stood at the centre of the half-circle of benches, his hands loose at his sides, his robe still bearing the dust of the streets where he had been seized. The elders watched him from the curved tiers above. The high priest sat at the head, his fingers laid flat along the arm of his seat.

The high priest spake. "Are these things so?"

Stephen lifted his face. The witnesses against him had said he spake against the temple and against Moses. He drew a breath. The room waited.

"Men, brethren, and fathers, hearken. The God of glory appeared unto our father Abraham, when he was in Mesopotamia, before he dwelt in Charran. He said unto him, 'Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and come into the land which I shall shew thee.'"

The eldest on the bench gave a slow nod. The story they all knew. The story of the beginning. A scribe near the wall lowered his stylus and listened.

Stephen carried Abraham forward. The promise of a land for a people not yet born. The covenant. Isaac. Jacob. The twelve patriarchs. He let the names fall in order. The councilmen nodded again. Familiar ground.

Then he turned unto Joseph.

"The patriarchs, moved with envy, sold Joseph into Egypt. But God was with him."

The word envy moved through the room. A man in the second row shifted in his seat. Stephen carried it onward. The famine. The brethren brought low before the brother they had cast out. Jacob and his household going down into Egypt. The bones brought back to lie in the tomb.

He moved unto Moses. He spake of the new king which knew not Joseph, of the infants cast into the river, of the child drawn from the water and raised in Pharaoh's house. He spake of the day Moses went out unto his brethren and smote the Egyptian, and how his brethren understood not that God by his hand would deliver them. He spake of Midian, and of the bush that burned and was not consumed, and the voice that called him back.

The room had grown quieter. The shift was small. The scribe forgot his tablet altogether.

"This Moses whom they refused," Stephen said, "saying, 'Who made thee a ruler and a judge?' — the same did God send to be a ruler and a deliverer."

The eldest leaned forward.

Stephen spake of the wilderness. Of the people who said unto Aaron, "Make us gods to go before us." Of the calf they made, and the sacrifice they offered unto the work of their own hands, and of how God turned away.

The high priest's fingers closed on the arm of his seat.

Stephen spake of the tabernacle in the wilderness. Of David asking to build an house. Of Solomon building it. Then his voice settled lower, and the room settled with it.

"Howbeit the most High dwelleth not in temples made with hands."

A murmur ran along the back tier. It died quickly. The elders waited for what was coming, because they had begun to feel where the story was bending.

Stephen lifted his eyes from the floor and let them travel along the curved benches. He looked at each face.

"Men, brethren, and fathers" was gone. What came next came at them.

"Ye stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, ye do always resist the Holy Ghost! As your fathers did, so do ye! Which of the prophets have not your fathers persecuted? They have slain them which shewed before of the coming of the Just One, of whom ye have been now the betrayers and murderers. Ye who have received the law by the disposition of angels, and have not kept it."

The room broke.

Men were on their feet. Robes swung. The eldest pressed his palms against his ears. The high priest was rising. Teeth shewed. Hands shook. The murmur became a roar. And in the midst of it Stephen, full of the Holy Ghost, looked up into the rafters of the chamber as if the roof were not there.

His face changed. It grew bright. It became as the face of an angel. And he saw what they could not. He saw the heavens opened, and the Son of man standing on the right hand of God.

They stopped their ears. They cried out with a loud voice. They ran upon him with one accord. They cast him out of the city, and stoned him. And the witnesses laid down their cloaks at the feet of a young man named Saul.

Stephen kneeled down. He cried with a loud voice, "Lord, lay not this sin to their charge."

And when he had said this, he fell asleep. The stones lay where they had fallen. The morning held still.
