Jesus was on the hillside. The people had gathered round about him. He had been teaching them many things. The day had come on warm. The country opened round about them, with the sea below and the hills behind. The grass on the hillside was green from the rains that had come earlier in the season.

He came to a part of his teaching wherein he willed them not to be afraid for their lives.

"Take no thought for your life," he said. "What ye shall eat. What ye shall drink. Nor for your body, what ye shall put on. Is not the life more than meat, and the body than raiment?"

A bird went over their heads. It was looking for somewhat to eat. He pointed at it.

"Behold the fowls of the air. They sow not. Neither do they reap. Nor gather into barns. Your heavenly Father feedeth them. Are ye not much better than they?"

He paused. He looked round at the faces of the people. None of them by taking thought could add one cubit unto his stature. None of them by thinking could grow themselves taller. He said so.

Then he came to the lilies.

The lilies grew in the grass where the people sat. They were white and small. They had opened themselves up unto the morning. He pointed at them with the same hand he had pointed at the bird.

"And why take ye thought for raiment? Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow. They toil not. Neither do they spin. And yet I say unto you, that even Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these."

Solomon's robes had been the richest in the country. The whole world had come to see his throne. The lilies in the grass were arrayed better than he had been on his best day. The people had heard of Solomon their whole lives. They had not heard the lilies named before.

His hand moved toward the lilies as he spake.

"Wherefore, if God so clothe the grass of the field, which to day is, and to morrow is cast into the oven, shall he not much more clothe you, O ye of little faith?"

He let his hand fall back unto his side. He went on.

"Therefore take no thought, saying: what shall we eat? Or, what shall we drink? Or, wherewithal shall we be clothed? After all these things do the Gentiles seek. For your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things."

He named the one thing they should seek.

"But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness. And all these things shall be added unto you."

Then he came to the close.

"Take therefore no thought for the morrow. For the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof."

The bird had passed over and gone away. The lilies stayed in the grass where they had been. The people sat still. The country opened round about them. Jesus went on with his teaching.
