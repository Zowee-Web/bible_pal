Jesus was on the hillside. The people had gathered around him. He had been teaching them many things. The day had come on warm. The country opened around them, with the sea below and the hills behind.

He came to a part of his teaching where he wanted them not to be afraid for their lives.

"Take no thought for your life," he said. "What ye shall eat. What ye shall drink. Nor for your body, what ye shall put on. Is not the life more than food, and the body than clothing?"

A bird went over their heads. It was looking for something to eat. He pointed at it.

"Behold the fowls of the air. They sow not. They reap not. They gather not into barns. Your heavenly Father feeds them. Are ye not much better than they?"

He paused. He looked around at the faces of the people. None of them by taking thought could add one cubit to their stature. None of them by thinking could grow themselves taller. He said so.

Then he came to the lilies.

The lilies were growing in the grass where the people were sitting. They were white and small. They had opened themselves up to the morning. He pointed at them with the same hand he had pointed at the bird.

"And why take ye thought for clothing? Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow. They toil not. Neither do they spin. And yet I say unto you, that even Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these."

His hand moved toward the lilies as he spoke.

"If God so clothe the grass of the field, which today is, and tomorrow is cast into the oven, shall he not much more clothe you, O ye of little faith?"

He let his hand fall back to his side. He came to the close.

"Take therefore no thought for the morrow. For the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof."

The bird had passed over and gone away. The lilies stayed in the grass where they had been. The people sat still. The country opened around them. Jesus went on with his teaching.
