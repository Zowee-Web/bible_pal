The king of Aram was at war with Israel. He would make secret counsel with his officers concerning whither he should move his army. But every time, the prophet Elisha sent word unto the king of Israel, warning him. Pass not by that place, Elisha would say. The Aramean army arrived and found no man.

This came to pass again and again.

The king of Aram waxed wroth. He called his officers together. "Which one of you doth betray my secrets unto the king of Israel?"

One of his servants spake. "None of us, my lord. It is Elisha, the prophet in Israel. He knoweth the very words which thou speakest in thy bedchamber."

The king's countenance darkened. "Go and find out where he is."

Word came back. Elisha sojourned in the city of Dothan.

So the king sent horses and chariots and a great host. They journeyed through the night. By morning they had compassed the city. Soldiers filled the ways. Chariots lined the hills. There was no way out.

Elisha's servant arose early. He stepped without and stood still. Everywhere he beheld — horses, chariots, soldiers with spears. The army encompassed the whole city like a wall of iron and dust.

He ran back within. "Alas, my master!" he cried. "What shall we do?"

Elisha's voice was calm. "Fear not: for they that be with us are more than they that be with them."

The servant stared. It was but the two of them.

Then Elisha prayed. "Lord, I pray thee, open his eyes, that he may see."

The Lord opened the servant's eyes. He beheld again the hills round about Dothan. The slopes were covered — horses and chariots of fire, blazing bright, filling every ridge and hollow between the trees. They encompassed the Aramean army on every side.

As the soldiers moved toward them, Elisha prayed once more. "Smite this people, I pray thee, with blindness." And the Lord smote them with blindness.

Elisha went forth unto the bewildered soldiers stumbling in the way. "This is not the way," he said unto them. "Neither is this the city. Follow me, and I will bring you unto the man whom ye seek."

He led them all the way to Samaria, the capital of Israel.

When they were within the city walls, Elisha prayed again. "Lord, open the eyes of these men, that they may see." Their sight returned. They looked round about. They were standing in the midst of Samaria, compassed about by Israel's army.

The king of Israel beheld them and ran unto Elisha. "My father, shall I smite them? shall I smite them?"

"Thou shalt not smite them," Elisha said. "Set bread and water before them, that they may eat and drink, and then send them back unto their master."

So the king prepared a great feast. The Aramean soldiers sat and did eat bread and drink water. Then they arose, and the king sent them back unto the king of Aram.

The bands of Aram came no more into the land of Israel.
