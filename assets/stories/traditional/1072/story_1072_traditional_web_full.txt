The king of Aram was at war with Israel. He held councils with his officers in private rooms behind heavy doors. Together they chose the places where they would set up camp and lay their traps.

But every time, the prophet Elisha sent a message to the king of Israel. "Do not pass near that place," Elisha warned, "because the Arameans are going down there."

The king of Israel sent men to check. They found it was true. He kept his soldiers away, and the Aramean traps caught nothing. This happened not once or twice but again and again.

The king of Aram was furious. He paced before his officers, his robes sweeping the stone floor. "Tell me!" he demanded. "Which one of you is betraying our plans to the king of Israel?"

The room went quiet. The officers looked at one another.

One servant stepped forward. "None of us, my lord the king. Elisha, the prophet who is in Israel, tells the king of Israel the very words you speak in your bedroom."

The king's jaw tightened. "Go, find out where he is," he ordered, "so I can send men and capture him."

His scouts returned. "He is in Dothan," they reported.

Dothan was a small city set among rolling hills, its walls low and pale in the sun. The king of Aram did not send a handful of soldiers. He sent horses. He sent chariots. He sent a great army. They marched through the dark hours of the night, their wheels grinding over the dry ground, their armor clinking softly. By the time the first gray light touched the sky, they had surrounded the city completely.

Elisha's servant rose early that morning. He pushed open the door and stepped into the cool air. Then he froze.

Soldiers everywhere. Horses stamping in lines along the road. Chariots parked on every hill. Spear points catching the early light. The army wrapped around Dothan like a fist.

He stumbled back inside, his breath coming fast. "Oh, my master!" he cried. "What shall we do?"

Elisha looked at him. His voice was steady and unhurried. "Don't be afraid. Those who are with us are more than those who are with them."

The servant blinked. He looked behind him. He looked at Elisha. It was just the two of them standing in that room, the morning light slanting through the window.

Elisha bowed his head. "O Lord," he prayed, "open his eyes so he may see."

And the Lord opened the servant's eyes.

He looked out again at the hills around Dothan. His hands gripped the doorframe. The slopes were covered. Horses and chariots of fire stood in blazing rows across every ridge, bright as the sun on bronze, filling the spaces between the trees, stretching along the hilltops in every direction. They surrounded the Aramean army on all sides.

The servant stood in the doorway, staring.

Then the Aramean soldiers began their advance. Their boots crunched on the gravel road. Their horses snorted and pulled forward.

Elisha prayed again. "Strike this army with blindness."

And the Lord struck them. Every soldier, every horseman. They stumbled. They reached out with empty hands. They called to one another, confused, turning in circles on the road.

Elisha walked out of the city toward them. Dust rose around his sandals. He stopped before the blinded army.

"This is not the right road," he said to them, his voice clear and plain. "This is not the right city. Follow me, and I will lead you to the man you are looking for."

The soldiers turned toward his voice. They followed. The whole army — horses, chariots, foot soldiers — followed the prophet's footsteps along the dirt road. They walked for miles, through open country, past fields and low stone walls. The sun rose higher and warmed their backs.

Elisha led them all the way to Samaria, the capital city of Israel. He brought them through the gates, into the heart of the city, where the walls rose high on every side and the king of Israel's own soldiers stood armed and watchful along the ramparts.

When they were well inside, Elisha prayed once more. "Lord, open the eyes of these men so they may see."

The Lord opened their eyes. The soldiers blinked. They looked around. Stone walls. Armed guards. The palace of the king of Israel rising before them. They were standing in the middle of Samaria, surrounded.

The king of Israel saw the entire Aramean army standing helpless in his city. He rushed to Elisha, barely able to contain himself. "My father, shall I kill them?" he asked. "Shall I kill them?"

"Do not kill them," Elisha said. "Would you kill those you have captured with your own sword and bow? Set food and water before them so that they may eat and drink, and then let them go back to their master."

The king obeyed. He ordered a great feast prepared. Servants brought out bread and meat and jars of water. The Aramean soldiers sat down on the ground in the open square of Samaria. They ate. They drank. The bread was warm and the water was cool.

When they had finished, they stood and brushed the crumbs from their clothes. The king of Israel sent them away, back to the king of Aram.

After that, the raiding bands from Aram stopped coming into the land of Israel.
