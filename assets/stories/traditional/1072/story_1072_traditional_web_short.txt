The king of Aram was at war with Israel. He would make secret plans with his officers about where to move his army. But every time, the prophet Elisha sent word to the king of Israel, warning him. Stay away from that place, Elisha would say. The Aramean army arrived and found no one.

This happened again and again.

The king of Aram grew furious. He called his officers together. "Which one of you is telling my secrets to the king of Israel?"

One of his servants spoke up. "None of us, my lord. It is Elisha, the prophet in Israel. He knows the very words you speak in your bedroom."

The king's face darkened. "Go find where he is."

Word came back. Elisha was staying in the city of Dothan.

So the king sent horses and chariots and a great army. They traveled through the night. By morning they had surrounded the city. Soldiers filled the roads. Chariots lined the hills. There was no way out.

Elisha's servant woke early. He stepped outside and stopped. Everywhere he looked — horses, chariots, soldiers with spears. The army circled the entire city like a wall of iron and dust.

He ran back inside. "Oh, my master!" he cried. "What shall we do?"

Elisha's voice was calm. "Don't be afraid. Those who are with us are more than those who are with them."

The servant stared. It was just the two of them.

Then Elisha prayed. "O Lord, open his eyes so he may see."

The Lord opened the servant's eyes. He looked again at the hills around Dothan. The slopes were covered — horses and chariots of fire, blazing bright, filling every ridge and hollow between the trees. They surrounded the Aramean army on every side.

As the soldiers moved toward them, Elisha prayed once more. "Strike this army with blindness." And the Lord struck them blind.

Elisha walked out to the confused soldiers stumbling in the road. "This is not the right road," he told them. "This is not the right city. Follow me, and I will lead you to the man you are looking for."

He led them all the way to Samaria, the capital of Israel.

When they were inside the city walls, Elisha prayed again. "Lord, open the eyes of these men so they may see." Their sight returned. They looked around. They were standing in the middle of Samaria, surrounded by Israel's army.

The king of Israel saw them and rushed to Elisha. "Shall I kill them, my father? Shall I kill them?"

"Do not kill them," Elisha said. "Set food and water before them so they may eat and drink, and then send them back to their master."

So the king prepared a great feast. The Aramean soldiers sat and ate bread and drank water. Then they rose, and the king sent them back to the king of Aram.

The bands from Aram stopped raiding Israel's land.
