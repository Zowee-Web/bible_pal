The room was small and the lamps were low. Thirty or forty people sat on benches along the walls, some leaning forward with their elbows on their knees, some with their heads tipped back against the stone. Their faces were drawn. Their hands were still. The air smelled of oil smoke and sweat and damp wool.

A man stood near the far wall with a letter in his hands. The parchment was unrolled to the final section. He had been reading for some time. His voice was steady but not loud. He read the way a man reads who knows the people in front of him are tired.

He found his place and spoke.

"Therefore, seeing we also are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses—"

He paused. A woman near the door shifted on the bench. A child slept against her side. The man's eyes moved over the room, over the tired faces, and then back to the parchment.

"—let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which so easily entangles us."

A few of them straightened. One man uncrossed his arms. The lamp nearest the reader flickered and the shadow on the wall behind him moved.

"And let us run with patience the race that is set before us."

His voice carried through the room like something handed from one pair of hands to another. Some of them looked at the floor. Some looked at him. One older woman near the back pressed her lips together and nodded once, barely visible.

"Looking to Jesus, the author and finisher of our faith."

He drew a breath. The parchment trembled slightly in his grip.

"Who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is seated at the right hand of the throne of God."

The room was quiet. No one coughed. No one moved. The child against the woman's side breathed softly.

The reader looked up from the letter one more time. His voice came lower now, slower, as though he were placing each word on the bench beside each person.

"For consider him who endured such hostility from sinners against himself, so that you do not grow weary and faint in your minds."

He held the parchment still. The oil lamps burned. The thirty or forty faces looked back at him in the low yellow light, their shoulders heavy, their eyes open.
