Jesus set another parable before them.

The kingdom of heaven, he said, is likened unto a man which sowed good seed in his field. Then, while men slept, his enemy came into that field. The enemy sowed tares among the wheat, and went his way.

The blade sprang up and brought forth fruit. Then the tares appeared also.

So the servants came to the householder who had sown the field.

"Sir, didst not thou sow good seed in thy field?" they said. "From whence then hath it tares?"

"An enemy hath done this," he said.

"Wilt thou then that we go and gather them up?" they asked.

"Nay," he answered. "For while ye gathered up the tares, ye would root up the wheat also. Let both grow together until the harvest. Then, in the time of harvest, I will say to the reapers: gather ye together first the tares, and bind them in bundles to burn them. But the wheat, gather into my barn."

Afterward Jesus sent the multitude away and went into the house. His disciples came unto him there, and asked him to declare unto them the parable of the tares of the field.

He answered them. He that soweth the good seed, he said, is the Son of man. The field is the world. The good seed are the children of the kingdom; the tares are the children of the wicked one, and the enemy that sowed them is the devil.

The harvest is the end of the world, and the reapers are the angels. Then he said unto them:

"As therefore the tares are gathered and burned in the fire, so shall it be in the end of this world. The Son of man shall send forth his angels. They shall gather out of his kingdom all things that offend, and them which do iniquity. These they shall cast into a furnace of fire. And there shall be wailing and gnashing of teeth.

"Then shall the righteous shine forth as the sun in the kingdom of their Father.

"Who hath ears to hear, let him hear."
