The assembly at Antioch included prophets and teachers: Barnabas, Simeon called Niger, Lucius of Cyrene, Manaen the foster brother of Herod the tetrarch, and Saul. While they served the Lord and fasted, the Holy Spirit told them to separate Barnabas and Saul for the work to which he had called them. After further fasting and prayer, the believers laid their hands on the two men and sent them away.

Sent out by the Holy Spirit, Barnabas and Saul went down to Seleucia and sailed from there to Cyprus. At Salamis they proclaimed God’s word in the Jewish synagogues. John accompanied them as their attendant. They traveled across the island until they reached Paphos.

There they met Bar Jesus, a Jewish sorcerer and false prophet. He was with Sergius Paulus, the proconsul, who was a man of understanding. The proconsul summoned Barnabas and Saul because he wanted to hear the word of God. Elymas the sorcerer, as his name was interpreted, opposed them and tried to turn Sergius Paulus away from the faith.

Saul, who was also called Paul, was filled with the Holy Spirit and fixed his eyes on Elymas. He called him a son of the devil, full of deceit and cunning, an enemy of all righteousness who kept perverting the right ways of the Lord. Paul declared that the hand of the Lord was upon him and that he would be blind, unable to see the sun for a season.

Immediately mist and darkness fell upon Elymas. He moved about seeking someone to lead him by the hand. When the proconsul saw what had happened, he believed. He was astonished at the teaching of the Lord. The mission that began with prayer and the Spirit’s call continued through opposition, and the proconsul responded to what he saw and heard.
