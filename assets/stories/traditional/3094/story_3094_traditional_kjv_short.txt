In the church at Antioch were certain prophets and teachers: Barnabas, Simeon called Niger, Lucius of Cyrene, Manaen, which had been brought up with Herod the tetrarch, and Saul. As they ministered to the Lord and fasted, the Holy Ghost said, “Separate me Barnabas and Saul for the work whereunto I have called them.” After fasting and prayer, the church laid their hands on them and sent them away.

Being sent forth by the Holy Ghost, Barnabas and Saul departed unto Seleucia and sailed from thence to Cyprus. At Salamis they preached the word of God in the synagogues of the Jews. John was also with them as their minister. They passed through the isle until they came unto Paphos.

There they found Bar-jesus, a Jewish sorcerer and false prophet. He was with Sergius Paulus, the deputy of the country, a prudent man who called for Barnabas and Saul and desired to hear the word of God. Elymas the sorcerer, as his name was interpreted, withstood them and sought to turn the deputy away from the faith.

Saul, who also was called Paul, was filled with the Holy Ghost and set his eyes on Elymas. He called him a child of the devil, full of subtilty and mischief, an enemy of all righteousness. “Wilt thou not cease to pervert the right ways of the Lord?” Paul declared, “Behold, the hand of the Lord is upon thee,” and said Elymas would be blind, not seeing the sun for a season.

Immediately a mist and darkness fell on Elymas. He went about seeking some to lead him by the hand. When the deputy saw what was done, he believed and was astonished at the doctrine of the Lord. The work begun through prayer and the call of the Holy Ghost continued despite opposition, and the deputy believed after seeing what came to pass.
