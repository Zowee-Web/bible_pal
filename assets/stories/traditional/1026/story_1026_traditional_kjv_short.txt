Jacob departed from Beersheba and went toward a place called Haran. The road was long and lonely. He journeyed until the sun began to set, painting the sky with orange and rose. When it waxed dark, he stayed to rest for the night.

He found a stone and laid it under his head for a pillow. The ground was hard beneath him. Stars came forth one by one overhead, filling the sky. Jacob closed his eyes and slept.

Then he dreamed. And behold, in his dream a great ladder was set up upon the earth. The top of it reached even unto heaven. Angels were ascending and descending upon the ladder, their robes shining softly. It was the most wondrous thing Jacob had ever beheld.

And lo, at the very top stood the Lord. God spake unto Jacob. "I am the Lord, the God of Abraham thy grandfather, and the God of Isaac thy father. The land whereon thou liest — to thee will I give it, and to thy seed."

God promised that Jacob's seed should spread abroad in every direction, like the dust spreading across the earth. And through his seed all the families of the earth should be blessed. Then God said something exceeding kind. "I am with thee. I will keep thee whithersoever thou goest. I will bring thee again unto this land. I will not leave thee."

Jacob awoke out of his sleep. The night was quiet. Stars yet burned above him. He sat up and whispered, "Surely the Lord is in this place, and I knew it not." His heart beat fast. "How dreadful is this place! This is the gate of heaven."

Early in the morning, Jacob took the stone he had used for a pillow and set it up for a pillar. He poured oil upon the top of it. Then he called the name of that place Bethel, which meaneth "house of God."

Jacob vowed a vow. "If God will be with me, and will keep me safe in this way that I go, and will bring me again to my father's house in peace, then shall the Lord be my God. And this stone shall be God's house."
