Jacob left Beersheba and set out on the road toward Haran. It was a long journey — many days of walking across dry, open country. Hills rolled away on every side, brown and golden under the wide sky. The path wound between rocky ridges where scrubby bushes clung to the thin soil.

Jacob walked alone. His sandals scuffed the dusty ground. His shadow stretched longer and longer as the sun dipped toward the hills in the west. The air began to cool. Birds settled into their nests for the night. The first stars appeared, faint and pale in the deepening blue.

When the sun was fully gone, Jacob stopped. He could not walk in the dark. He looked around at the rocky ground and found a smooth stone. He placed it under his head as a pillow. The earth was hard and cool beneath his back. He pulled his cloak tight around his shoulders.

Above him the sky opened wide, filled with more stars than he could count. The night was quiet. No voices, no campfire, no sound except the wind whispering across the stones. Jacob closed his eyes and slept.

Then he dreamed.

In the dream, a great ladder stood on the ground right where Jacob lay. It rose up and up, higher than the tallest tree, higher than the highest hill, all the way to heaven. The ladder blazed with light. And on it, angels were going up and coming down. Their feet moved steadily on the rungs, up and down, up and down, a constant stream of shining figures moving between earth and sky.

At the very top of the ladder stood the Lord.

God spoke. "I am the Lord, the God of Abraham your father, and the God of Isaac." His voice was clear and strong, filling the whole dream. "The land on which you lie, I will give to you and to your offspring."

God continued. "Your offspring will be like the dust of the earth. You will spread abroad to the west and to the east, to the north and to the south. In you and in your offspring, all the families of the earth will be blessed."

Then God spoke words that were just for Jacob. "I am with you. I will keep you wherever you go. I will bring you back to this land. I will not leave you until I have done what I have promised you."

Jacob woke up.

The night was still dark. Stars burned overhead. The stone was still under his head. The ground was still hard beneath him. Everything looked the same. But everything felt different.

Jacob sat up slowly. His breath came quick and shallow. He looked around at the dark, rocky ground, the same ground he had thought was just an ordinary stopping place for the night.

"Surely the Lord is in this place," Jacob whispered, "and I did not know it."

A shiver passed through him. Not from cold. From wonder. From the knowledge that God had been right there — right where he lay sleeping on the hard ground with a stone for a pillow.

"How awesome is this place," Jacob said. His voice shook a little. "This is none other than God's house. This is the gate of heaven."

Early in the morning, before the sun was fully up, Jacob stood and took the stone from under his head. He set it upright in the ground like a pillar, standing tall against the pale sky. Then he took a small flask of oil and poured it over the top of the stone. The oil ran down the sides, dark and shining.

He named the place Bethel. That name means "house of God." Before, the town nearby had been called Luz. But from this day forward, it was Bethel.

Then Jacob made a vow, a solemn promise. "If God will be with me and will keep me on this journey that I am going, and will give me bread to eat and clothing to wear, so that I come again to my father's house in peace, then the Lord will be my God. This stone that I have set up as a pillar will be God's house. And of all that God gives me, I will give a tenth back to him."

The morning light spread across the rocky ground. Jacob gathered his things and stood beside the stone pillar he had raised. The oil still glistened on its surface. Then he turned and continued on his way toward Haran, carrying with him the memory of the ladder, the angels, and the voice of God speaking in the night.
