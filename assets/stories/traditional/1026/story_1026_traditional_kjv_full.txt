Jacob went out from Beersheba and set forth upon the road toward Haran. It was a long journey — many days of walking across dry and open country. Hills rolled away on every side, brown and golden under the wide sky. The path wound between rocky ridges where scrubby bushes clave to the thin soil.

Jacob walked alone. His sandals scuffed the dusty ground. His shadow stretched longer and longer as the sun went down toward the hills in the west. The air began to grow cool. Birds settled into their nests for the night. The first stars appeared, faint and pale in the deepening blue.

When the sun was fully gone, Jacob stayed. He could not walk in the dark. He looked round about upon the rocky ground and found a smooth stone. He laid it under his head for a pillow. The earth was hard and cool beneath his back. He drew his cloak tight about his shoulders.

Above him the sky opened wide, filled with more stars than he could number. The night was quiet. No voices, no campfire, no sound save the wind whispering across the stones. Jacob closed his eyes and slept.

Then he dreamed.

And behold, in his dream a great ladder was set up upon the ground right where Jacob lay. It rose up and up, higher than the tallest tree, higher than the highest hill, even unto heaven. The ladder blazed with light. And lo, the angels of God were ascending and descending on it. Their feet moved steadily upon the rungs, up and down, up and down, a constant stream of shining figures moving between earth and heaven.

At the very top of the ladder stood the Lord.

God spake. "I am the Lord, the God of Abraham thy father, and the God of Isaac." His voice was clear and strong, filling the whole dream. "The land whereon thou liest, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed."

God continued. "Thy seed shall be as the dust of the earth. Thou shalt spread abroad to the west, and to the east, and to the north, and to the south. In thee and in thy seed shall all the families of the earth be blessed."

Then God spake words that were for Jacob alone. "And, behold, I am with thee, and will keep thee in all places whither thou goest. I will bring thee again into this land. I will not leave thee, until I have done that which I have spoken to thee of."

Jacob awoke out of his sleep.

The night was still dark. Stars burned overhead. The stone was still under his head. The ground was still hard beneath him. All things looked the same. But all things felt different.

Jacob sat up slowly. His breath came quick and shallow. He beheld the dark, rocky ground, the same ground he had thought to be but an ordinary stopping place for the night.

"Surely the Lord is in this place," Jacob whispered, "and I knew it not."

A trembling passed through him. Not of cold. Of wonder. Of the knowledge that God had been right there — right where he lay sleeping on the hard ground with a stone for his pillow.

"How dreadful is this place," Jacob said. His voice shook a little. "This is none other but the house of God. This is the gate of heaven."

Early in the morning, before the sun was fully risen, Jacob arose and took the stone that he had put for his pillow. He set it up for a pillar, standing tall against the pale sky. Then he took a small flask of oil and poured it upon the top of the stone. The oil ran down the sides, dark and shining.

He called the name of that place Bethel. That name meaneth "house of God." Before, the city nigh thereto had been called Luz. But from this day forward, it was Bethel.

Then Jacob vowed a vow, a solemn promise. "If God will be with me, and will keep me in this way that I go, and will give me bread to eat, and raiment to put on, so that I come again to my father's house in peace, then shall the Lord be my God. And this stone, which I have set for a pillar, shall be God's house. And of all that thou shalt give me I will surely give the tenth unto thee."

The morning light spread across the rocky ground. Jacob gathered his things and stood beside the pillar he had raised. The oil yet glistened upon its surface. Then he turned and went on his way toward Haran, bearing with him the memory of the ladder, the angels, and the voice of God speaking in the night.
