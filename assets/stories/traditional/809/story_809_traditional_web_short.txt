As evening fell on the shores of the Sea of Galilee, Jesus said to his disciples, “Let us go over to the other side.” Leaving the crowd behind, they took him along, just as he was, in the boat. Other boats were also with them. The sky grew dim, the water’s surface shifting from gentle to uneasy, as the wind began to stir. The disciples, tired from the day, felt a creeping unease as darkness settled around them.

Suddenly, a furious storm arose. The wind howled across the surface of the lake, and mighty waves crashed against the sides of the boat. Water surged over the edge, filling the vessel faster than they could empty it. The disciples’ voices rose in alarm as the boat rocked and groaned, the wood straining under the relentless assault of waves. Cold spray stung their faces, their robes clinging to their bodies as they fought to keep afloat. Above the roar of the wind, their hearts raced with fear, for the storm threatened to capsize the boat and drown them all.

Amid the chaos, Jesus lay in the stern, his head on a cushion. He was sleeping despite the danger, untroubled by the storm. The disciples, frantic and afraid, hurried to him and woke him. “Teacher, don’t you care that we are dying?” they cried, voices trembling with anxiety and doubt. The sound of water sloshing in the boat and the tempest’s fury pressed upon them with every passing moment.

Jesus rose and stood in the battered boat, calm amid the terror. With authority, he rebuked the wind and spoke to the sea, “Peace! Be still!” Immediately, the wind ceased its howling, and the waves grew gentle. The air, once filled with the chaotic noise of the storm, settled into an unnatural stillness. The sudden quiet pressed around them—the boat resting on calm water, the clouds parting overhead.

Jesus turned to his disciples and said, “Why are you so afraid? Do you still have no faith?” The disciples, now filled with awe and a deeper kind of fear, looked at one another and asked, “Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?”
