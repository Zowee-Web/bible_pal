As the evening came down upon the land, Jesus said unto his disciples, "Let us pass over unto the other side." And leaving the multitude, they took him with them in the ship, even as he was. There were also with them other little ships, drifting upon the waters of the sea of Galilee. The sky deepened and the shadows grew long, yet the air was uneasy and restless, a chill beneath the quiet soon shewing itself.

The little company set forth across the water, and though the journey began in the soft fading light, a brooding unease clung unto the air above the lake. The water, calm at the first, shifted beneath the rising wind. Then, without warning, there arose a great storm of wind. Dark clouds rolled across the sky, hiding the last of the daylight. The wind waxed strong, crying out over the face of the deep, and sudden, heavy waves beat against the sides of their ship. The wild waters tossed them up and down, casting cold and stinging drops upon the disciples. The ship creaked and groaned, pitched and rocked by the unceasing waves. The water came in over the low sides, threatening to overwhelm them.

In the midst of the tumult, Jesus was in the hinder part of the ship, asleep on a pillow. Even as the thunder rolled and the lightning flashed in the clouds above, he was not troubled. But the disciples, filled with fear and dismay, laboured to bail out the water with their hands. Every wave that smote the ship threatened to overturn it. The ship shuddered wildly, lifting and plunging beneath their feet without warning. The disciples cried out above the roar of the storm, their voices well-nigh lost in the howling of the wind and the water.

Fear grew in their hearts as the danger waxed more grievous. The sea of Galilee was known for storms that arose suddenly with great violence, but this one seemed to have no mercy, as though set upon sweeping them away. Every flash of lightning shewed pale faces and trembling hands. The sound of splintering wood and the battering of the waves drowned out their words. In that desperate moment, they beheld that Jesus yet slept, untroubled in the midst of the chaos.

At length, one of the disciples came unto him in alarm. They awaked him, and they cried out, saying, "Master, carest thou not that we perish?" Their voices trembled, and their fear was plain to see. The storm raged on, the wind lashing and the spray blinding their eyes as they besought him.

And he arose, and stood within the unsteady ship. His presence brought a quiet authority into the midst of the confusion and dread. He spake unto the wind, and rebuked the raging waves, his voice cutting through the storm. "Peace, be still." And straightway the wind ceased. The wild waters were quieted unto a gentle ripple across the lake. The ship ceased to pitch and to roll, and a sudden and entire stillness settled upon the sea. The only sounds now were the laboured breathing of the disciples and the slow drip of water from their garments and from the sides of the ship.

Then he turned unto them, and beheld their troubled faces. "Why are ye so fearful?" he asked. "How is it that ye have no faith?" The disciples stood in silence, their anxious hearts now overcome by awe and wonder. The power of the storm had vanished at the word of Jesus, leaving the night air quiet and the lake smooth beneath their feet. In that moment, a different kind of fear took hold of them — not the terror of the wind nor of the waves, but a sense of mighty awe.

And they spake one to another, with hushed and uncertain voices, saying, "What manner of man is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?"

For a long moment, the disciples suffered the words of Jesus to linger between them and the hush of the peaceful waters. They drew deep breaths, their hearts beating hard with that which they had beheld. The stars, hidden during the tempest, began to come forth as the clouds drifted away, their pale glow reflected upon the rippling face of the lake. Quiet returned unto the world round about, yet within their minds a thousand questions stirred, as though the storm now rolled within.

Peter, never one to keep silent, wiped the water from his brow and beheld the others. "Saw ye it? He spake only, and the wind fled at his word." Andrew bowed his head, his hands yet trembling. "I feared for my life," he said in the low voice of one shaken but safe. "And yet now I know not what to feel." Round about, the other disciples murmured their agreement, their fear giving way unto awe, a swelling sense of the authority Jesus wielded.

Their ship drifted upon the softly rocking water, the marks of the storm reminding them how nigh they had come unto destruction. James leaned closer unto John. "He commanded the sea — as in the stories we heard as children — yet here before us, in our little ship." John, ever thoughtful, watched Jesus as he settled again in the hinder part. "Who are we, that we should behold this?" John whispered, humbled and amazed.

Jesus, beholding their wonder, met every man's gaze with a compassion that steadied their hearts. "Ye have seen much," he said gently. "And there is yet more to understand." The least of a smile touched his lips, and bade them come forward into greater mysteries.

As the ship glided quietly toward the further shore, none among them returned unto sleep. Their minds and their hearts were awake as never before, bearing the memory of the Master's voice long after the storm had passed.
