The kings on this side Jordan gathered with one accord to fight against Joshua. But the inhabitants of Gibeon, who had heard what Israel did to Jericho and Ai, wrought wilily instead. They took old sacks and rent wine bottles upon their asses, put on clouted shoes and old garments, took dry and mouldy bread, and came to the camp at Gilgal. We be come from a far country, they said. Make ye a covenant with us.

The men of Israel put the question to them — Peradventure ye dwell among us; how then shall we make a covenant with you? — but the Gibeonites pressed their tale: they were come from a very far land because of the name of the Lord their God; and here was the proof, they said — bread gone dry, bottles rent, garments and shoes worn by the long way. The men took of their victuals. But they asked not counsel at the mouth of the Lord. So Joshua made peace and a covenant to let them live, and the princes sware unto them.

At the end of three days the truth came out: these were their neighbours. Israel came to their cities but smote them not, because the princes had sworn by the Lord. The congregation murmured, but the princes held to the oath. We have sworn unto them by the Lord; we may not touch them, they said, lest wrath be upon us.

Then Joshua confronted them. Wherefore have ye beguiled us, saying ye were far off, when ye dwell among us? Now ye are cursed — bondmen shall ye ever be for the house of my God. They answered that they had heard the Lord had given Israel all the land and commanded the destruction of its peoples, and so were sore afraid for their lives. Now we are in thine hand; do as seemeth good unto thee. And Joshua spared them, and made them hewers of wood and drawers of water for the congregation, and for the altar of the Lord.
