When the kings west of the Jordan — the Hittites and Amorites and Canaanites and the rest — heard what Israel had done, they gathered together with one accord to fight against Joshua. But the people of Gibeon took another way. They had heard what Joshua had done to Jericho and to Ai, and they resorted to a ruse. They loaded their donkeys with worn-out sacks and cracked, mended wineskins, put patched sandals on their feet and old clothes on their backs, and took bread that was dry and crumbling; and so they came to the camp at Gilgal.

"We have come from a far country," they told Joshua and the men of Israel. "Now make a covenant with us."

The men of Israel answered them, "Perhaps you live right among us. How then could we make a covenant with you?" But the Gibeonites turned to Joshua. "We are your servants," they said. When he asked who they were and where they had come from, they answered with a story: they had come from a very far country because of the fame of Yahweh their God, having heard all that he did in Egypt and to the two kings of the Amorites beyond the Jordan. Their elders had sent them with provisions for the road. And they pointed to the proof — this bread was taken hot from their houses the day they set out, they said, and now it was dry and moldy; these wineskins were new when filled, and now they were cracked; their clothes and sandals were worn out by the very long journey.

The men of Israel sampled their provisions. But they did not ask counsel from the mouth of Yahweh. So Joshua made peace with them, and made a covenant to let them live, and the leaders of the congregation swore an oath to them.

Three days later the truth came out: these people were their neighbors, living right among them. Israel came to their cities — Gibeon, Chephirah, Beeroth, and Kiriath Jearim — but did not strike them, because the leaders had sworn to them by Yahweh, the God of Israel. The whole congregation murmured against the leaders. But the leaders held to the oath. "We have sworn to them by Yahweh," they said. "Now we cannot touch them. This we will do: we will let them live, so that no wrath comes on us for the oath we swore." So the Gibeonites were spared, and made woodcutters and water-carriers for the congregation.

Then Joshua called them and confronted them. "Why did you deceive us," he said, "telling us you were far away, when you live right among us? Now you are cursed. You will never cease to be slaves — woodcutters and water-carriers for the house of my God."

They answered plainly. "Your servants were told for certain that Yahweh your God had commanded his servant Moses to give you all this land, and to destroy all its inhabitants before you. So we feared greatly for our lives, and did this thing. Now we are in your hand. Do to us as it seems good and right to you." And Joshua did so, and delivered them out of the hand of Israel, so that they did not kill them. That day he made them woodcutters and water-carriers for the congregation and for the altar of Yahweh, in the place he would choose.
