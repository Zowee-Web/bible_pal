Early in the morning, Jerubbaal—who is Gideon—and all the people that were with him rose up early, and pitched beside the well of Harod.

Below them, in the valley, the host of Midian was spread across the plain like locusts. Their camels were as the sand by the sea side for multitude—beyond counting.

At Gideon's side stood an host of thousands.

Then the Lord spake:
"The people that are with thee are too many for me to give the Midianites into their hands, lest Israel vaunt themselves against me, saying, 'Mine own hand hath saved me.'"

Gideon stepped forth into the crisp dawn air. His voice carried over the camp:
"Whosoever is fearful and afraid, let him return and depart early from mount Gilead."

A murmur passed through the men. Then movement.

Twenty and two thousand turned back.

Ten thousand remained.

But the Lord said again:
"The people are yet too many; bring them down unto the water, and I will try them for thee there."

So Gideon led them down.

By the edge of the rippling water, he stood and beheld.

Some men bowed down upon their knees, and put their faces to the stream.
Others took up water in their hands, bringing it quickly unto their mouths—lapping as they drank.

Then the Lord said:
"By the three hundred men that lapped will I save you, and deliver the Midianites into thine hand."

Gideon set apart the three hundred.

The rest he sent away.

The men looked one upon another.
In their hands were trumpets… and empty pitchers.

No swords.

That night, the host of Midian lay below, swallowed in darkness.

The Lord said unto Gideon:
"Arise, get thee down unto the host; for I have delivered it into thine hand.
But if thou fear to go down, go thou with Phurah thy servant."

So Gideon went down.

He and Phurah crept unto the edge of the enemy camp. Shadows stretched long beneath the moon.

Voices drifted through the darkness.

A man was speaking:
"Behold, I dreamed a dream, and, lo, a cake of barley bread tumbled into the host of Midian. It came unto a tent, and smote it that it fell."

Another answered:
"This is nothing else save the sword of Gideon the son of Joash: for into his hand hath God delivered Midian."

Gideon hearkened.

Then he turned and went again.

⸻

He gathered the three hundred men.

He divided them into three companies.

Into every man's hand he put a trumpet… and an empty pitcher, with a lamp hid within.

The hillside was still. The air was tight with anticipation.

Gideon spake softly:
"Look on me, and do likewise.

When I come to the outside of the camp and blow the trumpet, then blow ye the trumpets also on every side of all the camp, and say, 'The sword of the Lord, and of Gideon.'"

⸻

They moved into position under cover of night, compassing the camp.

Gideon lifted up his trumpet.

He blew.

Forthwith, the three hundred men blew the trumpets.

They brake the pitchers that were in their hands.

Clay shattered.
Lamps burst into flame.

Light flared across the darkness.

Voices rose together:
"The sword of the Lord, and of Gideon!"

The host of Midian woke in confusion.

Men cried out.
Lanterns flickered over panicked faces.

They seized their swords—
and turned every man against his fellow.

⸻

The three hundred blew the trumpets, and the Lord set every man's sword against his fellow, even throughout all the host.

The camp brake.

They fled—
unto Bethshittah toward Zererath,
and to the border of Abelmeholah, unto Tabbath.
