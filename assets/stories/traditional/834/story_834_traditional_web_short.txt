Early in the morning, Jerubbaal—who is Gideon—and all the people with him gathered by the spring of Harod.

Below them, in the valley, the camp of Midian spread across the plain like locusts. Their camels were as the sand on the seashore—beyond counting.

At Gideon's side stood an army of thousands.

Then Yahweh spoke:
"The people who are with you are too many for me to give the Midianites into their hand, lest Israel boast against me, saying, 'My own hand has saved me.'"

Gideon stepped forward into the crisp dawn air. His voice carried over the camp:
"Whoever is fearful and trembling, let him return and depart from Mount Gilead."

A murmur passed through the men. Then movement.

Twenty-two thousand turned back.

Ten thousand remained.

But Yahweh said again:
"The people are still too many. Bring them down to the water, and I will test them there."

So Gideon led them down.

By the edge of the rippling water, he stood and watched.

Some men knelt, lowering their faces to the stream.
Others scooped water in their hands, bringing it quickly to their mouths—lapping as they drank.

Then Yahweh said:
"By the three hundred who lapped I will save you, and deliver the Midianites into your hand."

Gideon separated the three hundred.

The rest he sent away.

The men looked at one another.
In their hands were trumpets… and empty jars.

No swords.

⸻

That night, the camp of Midian lay below, swallowed in darkness.

Yahweh said to Gideon:
"Go down against the camp, for I have delivered it into your hand.
But if you are afraid, go down with your servant Purah."

So Gideon went down.

He and Purah crept to the edge of the enemy camp. Shadows stretched long beneath the moon.

Voices drifted through the darkness.

A man was speaking:
"Behold, I dreamed a dream. A cake of barley bread tumbled into the camp of Midian. It struck a tent and overturned it."

Another answered:
"This is nothing other than the sword of Gideon, son of Joash. God has delivered Midian into his hand."

Gideon listened.

Then he turned and went back.

⸻

He gathered the three hundred men.

He divided them into three companies.

Into each man's hand he placed a trumpet… and a clay jar, with a torch hidden inside.

The hillside was still. The air was tight with anticipation.

Gideon spoke quietly:
"Watch me. Do what I do.

When I come to the edge of the camp and blow the trumpet, you also blow the trumpets around the whole camp and shout, 'For Yahweh and for Gideon.'"

⸻

They moved into position under cover of night, surrounding the camp.

Gideon lifted his trumpet.

He blew.

At once, the three hundred men blew their trumpets.

They smashed the jars in their hands.

Clay shattered.
Torches burst into flame.

Light flared across the darkness.

Voices rose together:
"A sword for Yahweh and for Gideon!"

The camp of Midian woke in chaos.

Men shouted.
Lanterns flickered over panicked faces.

They seized their swords—
and turned against one another.

⸻

They blew the three hundred trumpets, and Yahweh set every man's sword against his fellow and against all the army.

The camp broke.

They fled—
as far as Beth Shittah toward Zererah,
as far as the border of Abel Meholah, by Tabbath.
