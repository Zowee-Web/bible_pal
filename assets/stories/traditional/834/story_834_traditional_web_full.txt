Early in the morning, while dew still lay on the ground, Jerubbaal—who is Gideon—rose and gathered the people with him.

They encamped by the spring of Harod.

Across the valley, on the northern side, the camp of Midian stretched wide beneath the hill of Moreh.

The men of Israel looked out.

The Midianites covered the land. Their camels were without number—like the sand by the sea.

⸻

Yahweh spoke to Gideon:
"The people who are with you are too many for me to give the Midianites into their hand, lest Israel claim glory for themselves and say, 'My own hand has saved me.'"

Gideon called out:
"Whoever is fearful and trembling, let him return and depart from Mount Gilead."

For a moment, no one moved.

Then the line broke.

Twenty-two thousand men turned back, their footsteps fading across the hillside.

Ten thousand remained.

⸻

Again Yahweh said:
"The people are still too many. Bring them down to the water, and I will test them there."

Gideon led them down the rocky slope to the spring.

He stood and watched.

Some knelt and drank directly from the stream.
Others scooped water in their hands, bringing it quickly to their mouths, lapping as they drank.

Yahweh said:
"By the three hundred who lapped I will save you. Let all the others return to their homes."

Gideon set apart the three hundred.

The rest he sent away.

In their hands were trumpets… and empty jars.

No swords.

⸻

That night, the camp of Midian lay below, spread across the valley.

Yahweh said:
"Arise, go down against the camp, for I have delivered it into your hand.
But if you are afraid, go down with your servant Purah and listen to what they say."

So Gideon went down with Purah.

They crept through the darkness toward the edge of the camp.

Voices drifted through the night air.

A man was speaking:
"Behold, I dreamed a dream. A loaf of barley bread tumbled into the camp of Midian. It struck a tent and overturned it."

Another answered:
"This is nothing else but the sword of Gideon, son of Joash, a man of Israel. Into his hand God has delivered Midian and all the camp."

Gideon listened.

Then he turned and went back.

⸻

He gathered the three hundred men.

"Arise," he said, his voice low and steady,
"for Yahweh has delivered the camp of Midian into your hand."

He divided them into three companies.

Into each man's hand he placed a trumpet… and an empty clay jar, with a torch hidden inside.

The hillside was still.

The night pressed in.

Gideon said:
"Watch me. Do as I do.

When I come to the edge of the camp and blow the trumpet, you also blow your trumpets on every side of the camp and shout, 'For Yahweh and for Gideon!'"

⸻

They moved into position under cover of darkness.

The camp was surrounded.

It was just after the middle watch, when the night is deepest and the guards had just taken their place.

Gideon raised his trumpet.

He blew.

At once, the three hundred men blew their trumpets.

They smashed the jars in their hands.

Clay shattered.
Torches flared.

Light burst across the hills.

Voices rose together:
"A sword for Yahweh and for Gideon!"

⸻

Panic swept through the camp.

Men stumbled from their tents.
Camels cried out.

Light and shadow flickered across the valley.

The sound of trumpets echoed from every side.

⸻

Yahweh set the swords of the Midianites against one another.

Each man turned on his fellow.

The army broke.

They fled—

as far as Beth Shittah toward Zererah,
as far as the border of Abel Meholah, by Tabbath.
