Early in the morning, while dew still lay on the ground, Jerubbaal—who is Gideon—rose and gathered the people with him.

They encamped beside the spring of Harod.

Across the valley, below them, the camp of Midian stretched across the plain beneath the hill of Moreh.

The tents filled the land.

Their camels were without number—like the sand on the seashore.

⸻

Yahweh spoke to Gideon:
"The people who are with you are too many for me to give the Midianites into their hand, lest Israel boast against me, saying, 'My own hand has saved me.'

Now therefore proclaim in the ears of the people, saying, 'Whoever is fearful and trembling, let him return and depart from Mount Gilead.'"

Gideon moved among the men and gave the call:
"Whoever is fearful and trembling, let him return."

The line shifted.

Then it broke.

Men turned back in great numbers.

Twenty-two thousand departed, leaving ten thousand behind.

⸻

Yahweh said:
"The people are still too many. Bring them down to the water, and I will test them there."

Gideon led them down from the heights to the stream.

At the water's edge, the men bent to drink.

Some knelt and drank directly from the water.
Others scooped water with their hands and brought it to their mouths, lapping as they drank.

Yahweh said:
"By the three hundred who lapped I will save you and deliver the Midianites into your hand. Let all the others return."

Gideon set apart the three hundred.

The rest he sent away.

They stood on the hillside above the vast camp below.

In their hands were trumpets… and empty clay jars with torches inside.

No swords.

⸻

As evening came, the camp of Midian stirred below them.

Voices and movement rose from the valley.

That night Yahweh said to Gideon:
"Arise, go down against the camp, for I have delivered it into your hand.

But if you are afraid, go down with Purah your servant, and you shall hear what they say."

⸻

Gideon went down with Purah.

They moved quietly through the darkness toward the edge of the camp.

Near a tent, they heard men speaking.

One said:
"Behold, I dreamed a dream. A loaf of barley bread tumbled into the camp of Midian. It struck a tent and overturned it."

Another answered:
"This is nothing else but the sword of Gideon, son of Joash, a man of Israel. God has delivered Midian into his hand, and all the camp."

⸻

Gideon heard.

He bowed in worship.

Then he returned.

⸻

He gathered the three hundred men.

"Arise," he said,
"for Yahweh has delivered the camp of Midian into your hand."

He divided them into three companies.

Into each man's hand he placed a trumpet… and an empty jar, with a torch inside.

He said:
"Watch me. Do as I do.

When I come to the edge of the camp and blow the trumpet, you also blow your trumpets on every side of the camp and shout, 'For Yahweh and for Gideon!'"

⸻

They moved into position under cover of darkness.

They surrounded the camp.

It was just after the middle watch, when the night is deepest and the guards had just taken their place.

All was still.

⸻

Gideon raised his trumpet.

He blew.

⸻

At once, the three hundred men blew their trumpets.

They smashed the jars in their hands.

Clay shattered.
Torches flared.

Light burst across the hills.

Voices rose together:
"A sword for Yahweh and for Gideon!"

⸻

The camp woke in confusion.

Men ran from their tents.
Shouts filled the night.

The sound of trumpets echoed from every side.

⸻

Yahweh set every man's sword against his fellow.

The Midianites turned on one another in the darkness.

The army broke.

They fled—

toward Beth Shittah, toward Zererah,
as far as the border of Abel Meholah, by Tabbath.

⸻

They blew the three hundred trumpets, and Yahweh set every man's sword against his fellow and against all the army.
