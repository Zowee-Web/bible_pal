Dinah, the daughter Leah bare unto Jacob, went to see the daughters of the land. Shechem, son of Hamor the Hivite, prince of the country, saw her, took her, lay with her, and defiled her. His soul clave unto Dinah; he loved the damsel and spake kindly unto her. He asked Hamor to get her for his wife.

Jacob heard that his daughter had been defiled. His sons were with the cattle in the field, and he held his peace until they returned. Hamor came to speak with him. Hearing the news, Jacob's sons returned grieved and very wroth. Lying with Jacob's daughter was folly in Israel, a thing that ought not to be done.

Hamor asked for Dinah as his son's wife because Shechem longed for her. He proposed marriages between their families, inviting them to dwell, trade, and gain possessions in the land. Shechem sought grace from Dinah's father and brethren, offering whatever dowry and gift they asked, however great.

Because Shechem had defiled their sister, Jacob's sons answered deceitfully. Giving her to an uncircumcised man would be a reproach, they said. If every male were circumcised, they would exchange daughters in marriage and dwell together as one people. Otherwise, they said, “We will take our daughter, and we will be gone.”

Hamor and Shechem were pleased. Shechem, more honourable than all his father's house, did not delay, because he delighted in Dinah.

At the city gate, father and son called Jacob's people peaceable. They urged the men to let them dwell and trade in the spacious land and to exchange daughters in marriage. Circumcision for every male, they said, was the condition for becoming one people. They asked whether the newcomers' cattle, substance, and every beast would not become theirs; they urged consent so Jacob's people would stay. All the men who went through the gate hearkened and were circumcised.

On the third day, while they were sore, Dinah's brethren Simeon and Levi each took a sword. They came boldly upon the city and slew every male. They slew Hamor and Shechem with the sword, took Dinah from Shechem's house, and departed.

Jacob's sons came upon the slain and spoiled the city because their sister had been defiled. They took sheep, oxen, asses, and wealth from city and field. They captured all the little ones and wives and spoiled everything in the houses.

Jacob told Simeon and Levi they had troubled him, making him stink among the Canaanites and Perizzites. He said his people were few; the inhabitants would gather against him, slay him, and destroy him and his household.

His sons answered, “Should he deal with our sister as with an harlot?”
