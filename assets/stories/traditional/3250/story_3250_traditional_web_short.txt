Dinah, the daughter Leah bore to Jacob, went to see the daughters of the land. Shechem, son of Hamor the Hivite, prince of the land, saw her, took her, lay with her, and humbled her. His soul joined to Dinah; he loved her and spoke kindly to her. He asked Hamor to get her for his wife.

Jacob heard that his daughter had been defiled. His sons were tending livestock in the field, and he kept silent until they returned. Hamor came to speak with him. Hearing the news, Jacob's sons came home grieved and very angry. Lying with Jacob's daughter was folly in Israel, something that ought not to be done.

Hamor asked for Dinah as his son's wife because Shechem longed for her. He proposed marriages between their families, inviting them to dwell, trade, and acquire possessions in the land. Shechem sought favor from Dinah's father and brothers, offering whatever dowry they asked, however great.

Because Shechem had defiled their sister, Jacob's sons answered deceitfully. Giving her to an uncircumcised man would disgrace them, they said. If every male were circumcised, they would exchange daughters in marriage and live together as one people. Otherwise, they would take their sister and leave.

Hamor and Shechem were pleased. Shechem, honored above his father's whole household, delayed nothing because he delighted in Dinah.

At the city gate, father and son called Jacob's people peaceful. They urged the men to let them live and trade in the spacious land and to exchange daughters in marriage. Circumcision for every male, they said, was the condition for becoming one people. They asked whether the newcomers' livestock, possessions, and animals would not become theirs; they urged agreement so Jacob's people would stay. All the men who went through the gate listened and were circumcised.

On the third day, while they were sore, Dinah's brothers Simeon and Levi each took a sword. They attacked the unsuspecting city, killing every male. They killed Hamor and Shechem with the sword, took Dinah from Shechem's house, and left.

Jacob's sons came upon the dead and plundered the city because their sister had been defiled. They took flocks, herds, donkeys, and wealth from city and field. They captured all the little ones and wives and plundered everything in the houses.

Jacob told Simeon and Levi they had brought trouble on him, making him odious among the Canaanites and Perizzites. He said his people were few; the inhabitants would gather against him, strike him, and destroy him and his household.

His sons answered, “Should he deal with our sister as with a prostitute?”
