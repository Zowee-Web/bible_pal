While Apollos was at Corinth, Paul traveled through the upper country and came to Ephesus. There he found about twelve disciples. He asked whether they had received the Holy Spirit when they believed. They answered that they had not even heard that there is a Holy Spirit.

Paul asked about their baptism. They said they had received John's baptism. Paul explained that John baptized with the baptism of repentance and told the people to believe in the one coming after him, Jesus. When they heard this, they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus. Paul laid his hands on them, the Holy Spirit came upon them, and they spoke with other languages and prophesied.

Paul entered the synagogue and spoke boldly for three months, reasoning and persuading concerning God's Kingdom. When some became hardened, disobedient, and spoke evil of the Way before the multitude, Paul withdrew from them. He separated the disciples and reasoned daily in the school of Tyrannus. This continued for two years, so that all who lived in Asia heard the word of the Lord, both Jews and Greeks.

God worked special miracles by Paul's hands. Even handkerchiefs or aprons carried from him to the sick were followed by healing, and evil spirits went out. Some traveling Jewish exorcists then tried to invoke the name of the Lord Jesus over those who had evil spirits. Seven sons of Sceva, a Jewish chief priest, did this. The evil spirit answered that it knew Jesus and knew Paul, but asked who they were. The possessed man overpowered them, and they fled from the house naked and wounded.

The event became known throughout Ephesus. Fear fell on Jews and Greeks, and the name of the Lord Jesus was magnified. Many believers came confessing and declaring their deeds. Many who practiced magical arts brought their books together and burned them publicly. Their value was counted at fifty thousand pieces of silver. So the word of the Lord was growing and becoming mighty.
