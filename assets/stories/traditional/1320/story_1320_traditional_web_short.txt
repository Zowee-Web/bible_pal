The prophet stood in the courts of the kings and in the squares of Jerusalem. He had spoken of judgment. He had spoken of comfort. He had spoken of the suffering one who would come — the servant of Yahweh, the one upon whom the iniquity of all would be laid. And in the midst of those songs, he gave the song of the servant's weariness — the song of the one who was struck and did not turn away.

The Lord Yahweh has given me the tongue of those who are taught, that I may know how to sustain with words him who is weary. He wakens morning by morning, he wakens my ear to hear as those who are taught.

The Lord Yahweh has opened my ear, and I was not rebellious. I have not turned back.

I gave my back to those who struck me, and my cheeks to those who plucked off the hair. I didn't hide my face from shame and spitting.

For the Lord Yahweh will help me. Therefore I have not been confounded. Therefore I have set my face like a flint, and I know that I will not be disappointed.

He who justifies me is near. Who will bring charges against me? Let us stand up together. Who is my adversary? Let him come near to me.

Behold, the Lord Yahweh will help me. Who is he who will condemn me? Behold, all they shall grow old as a garment. The moth shall eat them up.

And the song of the servant was set down. The prophet rolled up the scroll. The day went on. The shame and the spitting were yet to come, and the help from the Lord Yahweh was yet to come, and the servant's face was set like a flint, against the day when both would meet. And so it was in the writing of the prophet.
