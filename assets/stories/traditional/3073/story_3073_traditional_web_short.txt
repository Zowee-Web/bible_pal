Saul chose three thousand men of Israel. Two thousand remained with him in Michmash and the Mount of Bethel, while one thousand were with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin. Saul sent the rest of the people back to their tents.

Jonathan struck the Philistine garrison at Geba, and the Philistines heard what had happened. Saul blew the trumpet throughout the land so the Hebrews would hear. Israel learned of the attack and that the nation had become offensive to the Philistines. The people gathered after Saul at Gilgal.

The Philistines assembled to fight Israel with thirty thousand chariots, six thousand horsemen, and a multitude like sand on the seashore. They encamped at Michmash, east of Beth Aven.

When the men of Israel saw their trouble and distress, they hid in caves, thickets, rocks, tombs, and pits. Some Hebrews crossed the Jordan into the lands of Gad and Gilead. Saul remained at Gilgal, and the people following him trembled.

Saul waited seven days, according to the time Samuel had set. Samuel did not come to Gilgal within that time, and the people began scattering from Saul. Then Saul ordered that the burnt offering and peace offerings be brought to him. Saul offered the burnt offering.

As soon as he finished, Samuel arrived. Saul went out to greet him. Samuel asked, “What have you done?”

Saul answered by naming what he had seen: the people were scattering, Samuel had not come within the appointed days, and the Philistines had assembled at Michmash. Saul said he expected the Philistines to come down against him at Gilgal before he had entreated Yahweh’s favor. He said that he had forced himself and offered the burnt offering.

Samuel told Saul that he had acted foolishly and had not kept the commandment Yahweh his God had given him. Samuel said that Yahweh would otherwise have established Saul’s kingdom over Israel forever. Now, however, Saul’s kingdom would not continue. Yahweh had sought a man after his own heart and appointed him prince over his people because Saul had not kept Yahweh’s command.

Samuel rose and went from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin. Saul counted those who remained with him. About six hundred men were present.
