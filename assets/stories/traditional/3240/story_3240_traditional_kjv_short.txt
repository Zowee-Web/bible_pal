Jacob heard the sons of Laban accuse him of taking their father’s goods and obtaining all his glory from their father’s substance. He beheld Laban’s countenance also; it was no longer toward him as before. The Lord bade Jacob return unto the land of his fathers and his kindred, and promised to be with him.

Out in the field, beside his flock, Jacob called Rachel and Leah to him. Their father’s countenance had changed, he told them, but the God of his father had been with him. They knew he had served Laban with all his power. Laban had deceived him and changed his wages ten times, yet God had suffered him no hurt.

Jacob told how the cattle bare speckled young whenever the speckled were his wages. When the ringstraked were his hire, the cattle bare ringstraked. Thus, he said, God had taken their father’s cattle and given them unto him.

Then Jacob recounted a dream from the time when the cattle conceived. He saw the rams leaping upon the cattle, ringstraked, speckled, and grisled. The angel of God called unto him, “Jacob,” and Jacob answered, “Here am I.”

The angel bade him lift his eyes to those rams, for he had seen all that Laban did unto him. He said, “I am the God of Beth–el, where thou anointedst the pillar, and where thou vowedst a vow unto me.” Jacob was commanded to arise, depart from that land, and return to the land of his kindred.

Rachel and Leah asked whether any portion or inheritance remained for them in their father’s house. Were they not counted strangers? He had sold them, they said, and quite devoured their money. All the riches God had taken from their father belonged to them and their children. “Now then, whatsoever God hath said unto thee, do.”

Jacob set his sons and wives upon camels. With all the cattle and goods he had gotten in Padan–aram, he departed to go unto Isaac his father in Canaan.

Laban had gone to shear his sheep, and Rachel had stolen her father’s images. Jacob stole away unawares to Laban the Syrian, telling him nothing of his flight. With all that he had, Jacob passed over the river and set his face toward mount Gilead.
