Jacob heard Laban’s sons accusing him of taking their father’s possessions and gaining his wealth from them. Laban’s expression toward Jacob had changed too. Then Yahweh told Jacob to return to the land of his fathers and his relatives, promising to be with him.

Jacob called Rachel and Leah out to the field where his flock was. He told them that their father no longer looked on him as before, but the God of his father had been with him. They knew how fully he had served Laban. Yet Laban had deceived him and changed his wages ten times. God had not allowed Laban to hurt him.

Jacob described how the flock bore speckled young when speckled animals were his wages, and streaked young when those were his wages. God, he said, had taken their father’s livestock and given them to him.

He also told them his dream during mating season. The male goats leaping on the flock were streaked, speckled, and grizzled. In the dream the angel of God called, “Jacob,” and he answered, “Here I am.”

The angel told him to look at those goats, for he had seen everything Laban was doing to Jacob. He declared, “I am the God of Bethel, where you anointed a pillar, where you vowed a vow to me.” He commanded Jacob to leave that land and return to the land of his birth.

Rachel and Leah answered together. Was any portion or inheritance left for them in their father’s house? He treated them as foreigners, they said. He had sold them and used up their money. The riches God had taken from their father belonged to them and their children. “Now then, whatever God has said to you, do.”

Jacob rose and put his sons and wives on camels. He took all his livestock and possessions gathered in Paddan Aram, setting out for Isaac his father in Canaan.

Laban had gone to shear his sheep. Rachel stole her father’s teraphim, and Jacob deceived Laban the Syrian by leaving without telling him he was fleeing. Jacob fled with everything he had, crossed the River, and turned toward the mountain of Gilead.
