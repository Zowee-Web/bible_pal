Jacob rose that night. He took his two wives, his two female servants, and his eleven children. He sent them across the ford of the Jabbok. The water ran shallow, cold over smooth stones. The children clung to their mothers. Jacob sent everything he had across the stream and watched the last of them disappear into the darkness.

Then Jacob was left alone.

The river murmured below the bank. The stars hung above, and the darkness pressed close. Somewhere beyond the hills, Esau was coming with four hundred men. Jacob had divided his camp and sent gifts ahead. He had done everything a man could do. Now he stood alone beside the water.

A man wrestled with him.

There was no warning. A figure came upon him in the dark, and they grappled. Jacob braced his feet in the dirt. The man pressed against him. Jacob pressed back. They locked together, straining, each trying to throw the other to the ground.

Jacob was strong. Twenty years he had worked Laban's flocks — lifting well-stones, wrestling rams. His hands were calloused. He knew how to hold on.

They wrestled through the night. Neither prevailed. The sound of the Jabbok ran beneath them. Stones shifted under their feet. Sweat soaked through Jacob's garments.

When the man saw that he did not prevail, he touched the hollow of Jacob's thigh. The hip wrenched from its socket. The pain was sudden, like fire through his side. Jacob's leg buckled.

Still he held on.

The man said, "Let me go, for the day breaks."

The eastern sky had begun to pale.

Jacob said, "I will not let you go unless you bless me."

His voice was ragged. His hip was ruined. But his grip did not open.

The man said, "What is your name?"

"Jacob."

The man said, "Your name will no longer be called Jacob, but Israel. For you have struggled with God and with men, and have prevailed."

Jacob said, "Please tell me your name."

"Why is it that you ask about my name?" He did not give it. But he blessed Jacob there, beside the Jabbok, as the dawn came on.

Jacob called the place Peniel. "For I have seen God face to face," he said, "and my life is preserved."

The sun rose on him as he passed over Peniel. He limped on his thigh. Each step cost him. The river lay behind. Jacob walked forward into the morning — renamed, broken, blessed.
