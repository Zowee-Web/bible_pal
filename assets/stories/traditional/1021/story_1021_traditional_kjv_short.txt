Jacob rose up that night. He took his two wives, his two womenservants, and his eleven sons. He sent them over the ford Jabbok. The water ran shallow, cold upon the smooth stones. The children clung to their mothers. Jacob sent over all that he had and watched the last of them vanish into the darkness.

And Jacob was left alone.

The river murmured below the bank. The stars hung above, and the darkness pressed close. Somewhere beyond the hills, Esau drew nigh with four hundred men. Jacob had divided his camp and sent gifts before him. He had done all that a man might do. Now he stood alone beside the water.

There wrestled a man with him.

There was no warning. A figure came upon him in the dark, and they strove together. Jacob set his feet in the dust. The man pressed against him. Jacob pressed back. They locked together, straining, each seeking to cast the other down.

Jacob was strong. Twenty years had he served Laban's flocks — lifting stones from wells, wrestling rams. His hands were hardened. He knew how to take hold.

They wrestled through the night. Neither prevailed. The sound of the Jabbok ran beneath them. Stones shifted under their feet. Sweat soaked through Jacob's garments.

When the man saw that he prevailed not, he touched the hollow of his thigh. The joint was wrenched from its socket. The pain was sudden, as fire through his side. Jacob's leg gave way.

Yet he held fast.

The man said, "Let me go, for the day breaketh."

The eastern sky had begun to grow pale.

Jacob said, "I will not let thee go, except thou bless me."

His voice was broken. His hip was ruined. But his grip opened not.

The man said unto him, "What is thy name?"

"Jacob."

The man said, "Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but Israel. For as a prince hast thou power with God and with men, and hast prevailed."

Jacob said, "Tell me, I pray thee, thy name."

"Wherefore is it that thou dost ask after my name?" He gave it not. But he blessed Jacob there, beside the Jabbok, as the dawn came on.

Jacob called the name of that place Peniel. "For I have seen God face to face," said he, "and my life is preserved."

The sun rose upon him as he passed over Peniel. He halted upon his thigh. Each step cost him dear. The river lay behind. Jacob went forward into the morning — renamed, broken, blessed.
