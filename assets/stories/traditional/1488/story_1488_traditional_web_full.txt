This is what Jesus said about the shepherds and the sheep.

He said it in Jerusalem, in the days after he had given sight to the man born blind. The Pharisees were still arguing about him in the courts of the temple. The man who had been blind had been thrown out from among them. Jesus had found him and spoken with him, and now Jesus was speaking again to the people in the porch where the teachers gathered.

The flocks of Judea were on the hills around the city in those days. Small flocks tended by men in rough cloaks, who slept among their sheep at night and called them each by name. Larger flocks with hired hands brought in from elsewhere when the season needed extra help — the lambing, the shearing, the long summer when grass was thin.

The people who listened to Jesus knew what shepherds were and what hirelings were. They had seen both. They had seen the difference.

He said —

"I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep."

The good shepherd. The one who owned the sheep, who knew the sheep, who had been with them since they were small. Who would not run from what threatened them.

"He who is a hired hand, and not a shepherd, who doesn't own the sheep, sees the wolf coming, leaves the sheep, and flees. The wolf snatches the sheep, and scatters them."

The hireling. The man who had been paid for the season, who would be paid again next season for some other work in some other place. Who did not own the sheep. Who would not lose what was his if the sheep were lost. Who would lose his life if he stayed against a wolf — and his life was the one thing he had not been hired to give.

"The hired hand flees because he is a hired hand. He doesn't care for the sheep."

Jesus did not condemn the hireling. He named what the hireling was. The hireling did the work the hireling was paid to do, and ran when the running was the thing his payment did not cover.

"I am the good shepherd. I know my own, and I'm known by my own — even as the Father knows me, and I know the Father.

I lay down my life for the sheep."

The words went out among the people who had been listening to him.

Some of them turned and looked toward the hills. The flocks were still grazing in the afternoon, with their shepherds walking among them. The people who had ever been shepherds themselves, or who had brothers who were shepherds, or who had lain awake at night listening for the call of a man in the field — they understood.

They could see what Jesus meant.

They could also see what he was saying about himself.

The Pharisees who were there did not turn to look at the hills.
