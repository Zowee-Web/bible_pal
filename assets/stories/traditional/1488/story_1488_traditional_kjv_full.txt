This is what Jesus said concerning the shepherds and the sheep.

He said it in Jerusalem, in the days after he had given sight unto the man born blind. The Pharisees were yet arguing about him in the courts of the temple. The man who had been blind had been cast out from among them. Jesus had found him and had spoken with him, and now Jesus was speaking again unto the people in the porch where the teachers gathered.

The flocks of Judea were on the hills round about the city in those days. Small flocks tended by men in rough cloaks, who slept among their sheep at night and called them each by name. Larger flocks with hirelings brought in from elsewhere when the season required help — the lambing, the shearing, the long summer when grass was thin.

They which listened to Jesus knew what shepherds were and what hirelings were. They had seen both. They had seen the difference.

He said —

"I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep."

The good shepherd. He that owned the sheep, that knew the sheep, that had been with them since they were small. That would not flee from what threatened them.

"But he that is an hireling, and not the shepherd, whose own the sheep are not, seeth the wolf coming, and leaveth the sheep, and fleeth. And the wolf catcheth them, and scattereth the sheep."

The hireling. The man that had been paid for the season, that would be paid again next season for some other work in some other place. That owned not the sheep. That would not lose what was his if the sheep were lost. That would lose his life if he stayed against the wolf — and his life was the one thing he had not been hired to give.

"The hireling fleeth, because he is an hireling, and careth not for the sheep."

Jesus condemned not the hireling. He named what the hireling was. The hireling did the work the hireling was paid to do, and fled when the fleeing was the thing his hire did not cover.

"I am the good shepherd, and know my sheep, and am known of mine — as the Father knoweth me, even so know I the Father.

And I lay down my life for the sheep."

The words went out among them that had been hearing him.

Some of them turned and looked toward the hills. The flocks were yet grazing in the afternoon, with their shepherds walking among them. They that had ever been shepherds themselves, or that had brothers who were shepherds, or that had lain awake at night listening for the call of a man in the field — they understood.

They could see what Jesus meant.

They could also see what he was saying about himself.

The Pharisees who were there did not turn to look at the hills.
