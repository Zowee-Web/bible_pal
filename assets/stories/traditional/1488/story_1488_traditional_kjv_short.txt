This is what Jesus said concerning the shepherds and the sheep.

He said it in the days after he had given sight unto the man born blind, when the Pharisees of Jerusalem were yet arguing about who he was. The flocks of Judea were on the hills round about the city — small flocks tended by men in cloaks, larger flocks with hirelings brought in from elsewhere when the season required help. They which heard him knew what shepherds were and what hirelings were. They had seen both. They had seen the difference.

He said —

"I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.

But he that is an hireling, and not the shepherd, whose own the sheep are not, seeth the wolf coming, and leaveth the sheep, and fleeth. And the wolf catcheth them, and scattereth the sheep.

The hireling fleeth, because he is an hireling, and careth not for the sheep.

I am the good shepherd, and know my sheep, and am known of mine — as the Father knoweth me, even so know I the Father.

And I lay down my life for the sheep."

The words went out among them that had been hearing him. Some of them turned to look at the hills, where the flocks were yet grazing in the afternoon, with their shepherds walking among them. They could see what he meant. They could also see what he was saying about himself.

The Pharisees who were there did not turn to look at the hills.
