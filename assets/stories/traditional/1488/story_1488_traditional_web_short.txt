This is what Jesus said about the shepherds and the sheep.

He said it in the days after he had given sight to the man born blind, when the Pharisees of Jerusalem were still arguing about who he was. The flocks of Judea were on the hills around the city — small flocks tended by men in cloaks, larger flocks with hired hands brought in from elsewhere when the season needed extra help. The people who listened to him knew what shepherds were and what hirelings were. They had seen both. They had seen the difference.

He said —

"I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.

He who is a hired hand, and not a shepherd, who doesn't own the sheep, sees the wolf coming, leaves the sheep, and flees. The wolf snatches the sheep, and scatters them.

The hired hand flees because he is a hired hand. He doesn't care for the sheep.

I am the good shepherd. I know my own, and I'm known by my own — even as the Father knows me, and I know the Father.

I lay down my life for the sheep."

The words went out among the people who had been listening to him. Some of them turned to look at the hills, where the flocks were still grazing in the afternoon, with their shepherds walking among them. They could see what he meant. They could also see what he was saying about himself.

The Pharisees who were there did not turn to look at the hills.
