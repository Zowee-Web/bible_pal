Yahweh spoke of a time when he would restore Judah and Jerusalem. He would gather the nations into the valley of Jehoshaphat and judge them for what they had done to his heritage. Israel had been scattered, the land divided, and the people treated as objects of trade. Boys and girls had been exchanged to satisfy the desires of their captors.

Tyre, Sidon, and the regions of Philistia had taken Yahweh’s silver, gold, and precious treasures. They had carried them into their temples and sold the children of Judah and Jerusalem far from home. Yahweh declared that he would awaken those captives from the lands where they had been sold. The payment intended for his people would return upon those who had trafficked them. Yahweh himself had spoken.

Then the nations were summoned. They were told to prepare for war, gather their warriors, and turn farm tools into weapons. Even the weak were to claim strength and hurry toward the appointed valley. Their gathering did not place Yahweh on trial; it brought them before his judgment. The harvest was ripe, the winepress full, and wickedness overflowing. Multitudes stood in the valley of decision as the day of Yahweh drew near.

The lights of heaven darkened. Yahweh roared from Zion and thundered from Jerusalem until heaven and earth shook. Yet the terror of his judgment was not the whole word. Yahweh was a refuge for his people and a stronghold for Israel. They would know him as their God dwelling in Zion, and Jerusalem would be holy.

The vision ended with abundance and settled dwelling. The mountains dripped with sweet wine, the hills flowed with milk, and Judah’s dry channels filled with water. A fountain came from Yahweh’s house. Egypt and Edom faced desolation because of violence and innocent blood, but Judah and Jerusalem endured through the generations. Yahweh would cleanse the bloodguilt not yet cleansed, for he dwelt in Zion.
