The letter to the Galatian churches was nearing its end. Paul had written of the law and the spirit. He had warned them not to fall back into bondage. He had reminded them that they were sons of the promise. He had given them the rule of love. And now, as the scroll grew short, he set down the words of perseverance — the words for those who would grow weary in the long doing of good.

Don't be deceived. God is not mocked, for whatever a man sows, that he will also reap.

For he who sows to his own flesh will from the flesh reap corruption. But he who sows to the Spirit will from the Spirit reap eternal life.

Let us not be weary in doing good, for we will reap in due season, if we don't give up.

So then, as we have opportunity, let's do what is good toward all men, and especially toward those who are of the household of the faith.

And the letter was sealed and sent. The messenger took the road to the churches in Galatia. The words traveled from town to town, and the believers heard them read aloud in their gatherings. The days went on as the days had been going on, and the seed went into the ground, and the doers of good kept doing good. And so it was in the early days of the church.
