The epistle unto the Galatian churches was nearing its end. Paul had written of the law and of the spirit. He had warned them not to fall back into bondage. He had put them in remembrance that they were sons of the promise. He had given them the rule of love. And now, as the scroll grew short, he set down the words of perseverance — the words for them which would grow weary in the long doing of good.

Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.

For he that soweth to his flesh shall of the flesh reap corruption; but he that soweth to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life everlasting.

And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not.

As we have therefore opportunity, let us do good unto all men, especially unto them who are of the household of faith.

And the epistle was sealed and sent. The messenger took the road unto the churches in Galatia. The words travelled from town to town, and the believers heard them read aloud in their gatherings. The days went on as the days had been going on, and the seed went into the ground, and the doers of good kept doing good. And so it came to pass in the early days of the church.
