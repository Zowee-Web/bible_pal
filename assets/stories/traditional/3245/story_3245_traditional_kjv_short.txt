On the third day, word reached Laban that Jacob was fled. He took his brethren and pursued seven days’ journey, overtaking Jacob in mount Gilead. By night God came to Laban the Syrian in a dream, saying, “Take heed that thou speak not to Jacob either good or bad.”

Jacob’s tent stood in the mount, and Laban with his brethren pitched there also. Laban charged Jacob with stealing away unawares and carrying his daughters off as captives taken with the sword. Why had Jacob departed secretly? Laban said he might have sent him away with mirth, songs, tabret, and harp. Jacob had not suffered him to kiss his sons and daughters. Laban called his conduct foolish.

Laban declared that he had power to do Jacob hurt, yet repeated the warning which the God of Jacob’s father had spoken yesternight. Though Jacob sorely longed for his father’s house, Laban asked, why had he stolen Laban’s gods?

Jacob answered that he feared Laban might take his daughters from him by force. Whoever possessed Laban’s gods should not live. Before their brethren, Laban should discern and take whatever was his. Jacob knew not that Rachel had stolen the gods.

Laban entered Jacob’s tent, Leah’s tent, and the two maidservants’ tents, finding nothing. From Leah’s tent he passed into Rachel’s. Rachel had hidden the images in the camel’s furniture and sat upon them. Laban searched the tent without finding them. Rachel asked her father not to be displeased because she could not rise, saying the custom of women was upon her. The images remained undiscovered.

Jacob was wroth and chode with Laban. What trespass or sin had occasioned such hot pursuit? After searching all his goods, what had Laban found of his own household stuff? Let him lay it before their brethren to judge betwixt them.

For twenty years, Jacob said, Laban’s ewes and she goats had not cast their young. He had eaten none of the flock’s rams. He had borne the loss of beasts torn, bringing them not to Laban. Whatever was stolen, whether by day or night, Laban had required at his hand.

Jacob spoke of drought consuming him by day, frost by night, and sleep departing from his eyes. He had served fourteen years for Laban’s two daughters and six for his cattle. Ten times Laban had changed his wages.

Except the God of his father, the God of Abraham and the fear of Isaac, had been with him, Jacob said, Laban would have sent him away empty. God had seen his affliction and the labour of his hands, and rebuked Laban yesternight.
