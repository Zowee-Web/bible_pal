An ephah of barley weighs roughly thirty pounds. Ruth carried it on her shoulder from the field back into the city, and the weight of it was the weight of a day spent bending, gathering, beating out grain under the open sun. She had gone out that morning with nothing. She came back with more than enough.

What stands out is the quiet accumulation of kindness. Boaz did not announce his generosity. He told his men to leave handfuls on purpose, and told them not to rebuke her. The provision was woven into the ordinary work of the day so that it looked, from the outside, like she had simply gleaned well.

Gratitude often begins in the recognition that something was given before we knew to ask for it. A field that happened to belong to a kinsman. A man who happened to notice. Handfuls left as though by accident. The geometry of care, arranged before the one who needed it arrived.
