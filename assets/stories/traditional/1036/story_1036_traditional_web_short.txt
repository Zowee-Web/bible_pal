There was a wealthy man in Bethlehem named Boaz, a relative of Elimelech. The barley harvest had come. Fields stretched golden under the morning sun.

Ruth spoke to Naomi. "Let me go to the field and glean among the ears of grain." Naomi said, "Go, my daughter."

She went out and came to a field belonging to Boaz. She did not know it was his. She bent low behind the reapers, gathering what fell. The stalks scraped her fingers. Sweat ran down her neck.

Boaz arrived from Bethlehem. "May Yahweh be with you," he called to his reapers. They answered, "May Yahweh bless you."

He noticed the young woman at the edge of the rows. "Whose young woman is this?" he asked his servant.

"The Moabite woman who came back with Naomi. She has worked since morning without rest."

Boaz went to Ruth. "Listen, my daughter. Do not glean in another field. Stay close to my young women. I have told the young men not to touch you. When you thirst, drink from the vessels."

Ruth fell on her face and bowed. "Why have I found favor in your eyes, since I am a foreigner?"

"I have heard all you did for your mother-in-law since your husband died," Boaz answered. "You left your father and mother and your homeland. May Yahweh reward your work under whose wings you have come to take refuge."

At mealtime Boaz called her. "Come. Eat bread and dip your morsel in the vinegar." She sat beside the reapers. He passed her roasted grain. She ate until satisfied.

When she rose to glean, Boaz commanded his young men. "Let her glean among the sheaves. Pull out some from the bundles for her."

She gleaned until evening. She beat out what she had gathered — about an ephah of barley. She carried it into the city and showed Naomi.

Naomi saw the grain. "Where did you glean today? Blessed be the man who noticed you."

"The man's name is Boaz," Ruth said.

Naomi's hands went still. "Blessed be he of Yahweh. This man is a close relative, one of our kinsman-redeemers."

So Ruth stayed close to the young women of Boaz through the barley harvest and the wheat harvest. She lived with her mother-in-law, and the grain did not run out.
