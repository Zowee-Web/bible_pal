Naomi had a kinsman of her husband's, a mighty man of wealth, of the family of Elimelech; and his name was Boaz. He held land outside the city walls, and his name was spoken with honour at the gate. The barley harvest had come, and the fields outside the city stood ripe and golden under the wide sky. Men with sickles walked the rows from first light. Women followed behind them, binding what was cut into sheaves. The gleaners came last — the poor, the widow, the stranger — gathering what the reapers' hands had let fall.

Ruth the Moabitess spake unto Naomi in the small house they shared. "Let me now go to the field, and glean ears of corn after him in whose sight I shall find grace." Naomi looked upon her a long moment. The morning light was grey through the doorway. "Go, my daughter."

She went forth early. The air was cool and the dew clung still to the lower stalks. The path from the city wound down past the threshing floor and out into the fields. Other gleaners were already upon the road, their cloths folded under their arms, their feet quiet in the dust. Ruth knew not any of them. She walked with her head covered and her eyes lowered. Her hap was to light on a part of the field belonging unto Boaz. She knew it not.

She bent low and gathered what fell from the reapers' hands. The stalks were sharp at the cut ends. Chaff stuck to her forearms and clung to her dress. The sun rose higher and pressed heat upon her back. She moved along the rows with the other gleaners but laboured alone, speaking unto no one. When a stalk fell within reach of two gleaners, the other waited and let her take it. She lifted not her head to see why.

Boaz came from Bethlehem. He walked along the edge of the field with the steady gait of a man who knew his land. He greeted his reapers. "The LORD be with you." They lifted their heads from their work. "The LORD bless thee."

He looked across the field and beheld the young woman gleaning at the far end. Her head covering was unfamiliar. The way she bent and rose, the way she spake not with the others, drew his eye. He turned unto his servant that was set over the reapers. "Whose damsel is this?"

The servant answered. "It is the Moabitish damsel that came back with Naomi out of the country of Moab. She said when she came, 'I pray you, let me glean and gather after the reapers among the sheaves.' She hath continued from the morning until now. She hath hardly rested in the house."

Boaz crossed the field unto the place where Ruth laboured. The dust rose where his sandals struck the ground. She straightened when she heard his step and turned. He spake first. "Hearest thou not, my daughter? Go not to glean in another field, neither go from hence, but abide here fast by my maidens. Let thine eyes be on the field that they do reap, and go thou after them. Have I not charged the young men that they shall not touch thee? And when thou art athirst, go unto the vessels, and drink of that which the young men have drawn."

Ruth fell on her face. She bowed herself to the ground. Her hands pressed into the loose soil between the cut stalks. "Why have I found grace in thine eyes, that thou shouldest take knowledge of me, seeing I am a stranger?"

Boaz answered. He raised not his voice. "It hath fully been shewed me, all that thou hast done unto thy mother in law since the death of thine husband. How thou hast left thy father and thy mother, and the land of thy nativity, and art come unto a people which thou knewest not heretofore. The LORD recompense thy work, and a full reward be given thee of the LORD God of Israel, under whose wings thou art come to trust."

She lifted up her face. "Let me find favour in thy sight, my lord; for thou hast comforted me, and for that thou hast spoken friendly unto thine handmaid, though I be not like unto one of thine handmaidens."

The sun reached its height. The reapers set down their sickles and gathered in the shade of a tree at the edge of the field. Cloths were spread upon the ground. Wineskins were brought out. Boaz said unto Ruth, "Come thou hither, and eat of the bread, and dip thy morsel in the vinegar."

She sat beside the reapers. They made room. Boaz reached her parched corn with his own hand. She did eat, and was sufficed. She had some left over, and she folded it into a cloth at her side. The bread was coarse, the vinegar sharp, the grain warm from the fire. The reapers ate and spake quietly among themselves, and now and again one of them glanced toward her and looked away.

When she was risen up to glean again, Boaz commanded his young men apart. "Let her glean even among the sheaves, and reproach her not. And let fall also some of the handfuls of purpose for her, and leave them, that she may glean them. Shame her not."

She gleaned in the field until the even. The reapers laboured steadily through the long heat. The gleaners walked their rows behind them. When the shadows began to grow long across the stubble, Ruth gathered what she had collected upon a cloth at the edge of the field. She beat out that she had gleaned, striking the stalks against the ground until the grain fell free. It was about an ephah of barley — more than a day's wages for a labouring man. She lifted the cloth upon her shoulder. It was heavy. She walked back toward the city in the slanting light.

The road into Bethlehem rose between low walls. Other gleaners were upon it now also, bearing their day's gathering. Some had little. Some had less. None had what Ruth bare. The people at the gate beheld her pass.

Naomi was at the doorway. Ruth set the grain upon the floor within and brought forth the food saved from the meal. Naomi looked upon the heap of barley. Her eyes widened. She looked at her daughter in law and back unto the grain. "Where hast thou gleaned today? And where wroughtest thou? Blessed be he that did take knowledge of thee."

Ruth told her what had befallen. She told her of the servant that had spoken for her, of Boaz crossing the field, of the meal in the shade, of the charge unto the young men. Then she said, "The man's name with whom I wrought to day is Boaz."

Naomi's hands went still upon the grain. She looked up. Her face changed. "Blessed be he of the LORD, who hath not left off his kindness to the living and to the dead." She paused, and her voice was lower. "The man is near of kin unto us, one of our next kinsmen."

Ruth said, "He said unto me also, Thou shalt keep fast by my young men, until they have ended all my harvest."

Naomi said unto Ruth her daughter in law, "It is good, my daughter, that thou go out with his maidens, that they meet thee not in any other field."

So she kept fast by the maidens of Boaz to glean unto the end of barley harvest and of wheat harvest. The mornings were many and the work was steady. Each even she returned with grain upon her shoulder and food saved from the meal at her side. The neighbours in Bethlehem grew used to the sight of her upon the road in the long light. She dwelt with her mother in law, and the house was not empty, and the grain did not fail, and the harvest came in.
