There was a kinsman of Naomi's husband, a mighty man of wealth, of the family of Elimelech. His name was Boaz. The barley harvest had come upon Bethlehem, and the fields outside the city stood ripe and golden under the wide sky.

Ruth the Moabitess said unto Naomi, "Let me now go to the field and glean ears of corn after him in whose sight I shall find grace." Naomi looked upon her. "Go, my daughter."

She went forth early. The air was cool and the dew clung still to the lower stalks. She came unto a field and began to glean behind the reapers. She knew not whose field it was. It belonged unto Boaz.

She bent low and gathered what fell from the reapers' hands. Chaff stuck to her forearms. The sun rose higher and the heat pressed upon her back. She moved among the gleaners but labored alone, speaking unto no one.

Boaz came from Bethlehem. He walked along the edge of the field. "The Lord be with you," he said unto his reapers. They lifted their heads. "The Lord bless thee."

He beheld the young woman gleaning at the far end. Her head covering was unfamiliar. He turned unto his servant. "Whose damsel is this?"

"The Moabitish damsel that came back with Naomi out of the country of Moab. She hath continued from the morning until now and hath not rested save a little."

Boaz crossed the field to where Ruth labored. She straightened at his step. "Hearest thou not, my daughter?" he said. "Go not to glean in another field. Abide here fast by my maidens. I have charged the young men that they shall not touch thee. When thou art athirst, go unto the vessels and drink."

Ruth fell on her face and bowed to the ground. "Why have I found grace in thine eyes, that thou shouldest take knowledge of me, seeing I am a stranger?"

Boaz answered. "It hath fully been shewed me all that thou hast done unto thy mother-in-law since the death of thine husband. Thou didst leave thy father and thy mother and the land of thy nativity and camest unto a people thou knewest not. The Lord recompense thy work. A full reward be given thee of the Lord God of Israel, under whose wings thou art come to trust."

She said, "Let me find favour in thy sight, my lord, for thou hast comforted me and spoken friendly unto thine handmaid."

The sun reached its height. The reapers set down their sickles and gathered in shade. Boaz said unto Ruth, "Come thou hither and eat of the bread and dip thy morsel in the vinegar."

She sat beside the reapers. He reached her parched corn with his own hand. She did eat and was sufficed. The bread was coarse, the vinegar sharp, the grain warm from the fire.

When she was risen up to glean, Boaz commanded his young men apart. "Let her glean even among the sheaves and reproach her not. Let fall some of the handfuls of purpose for her."

She gleaned until even. Shadows grew long across the stubble. She beat out what she had gleaned. The grain fell into her cloth — about an ephah of barley. She lifted it upon her shoulder and went into the city.

Naomi waited. Ruth set the grain before her and brought forth the food saved from the meal. Naomi looked upon the heap. Her eyes widened. "Where hast thou gleaned today? Blessed be he that did take knowledge of thee."

"The man's name is Boaz," Ruth said.

Naomi's hands grew still. "Blessed be he of the Lord, who hath not left off his kindness to the living and to the dead. The man is near of kin, one of our next kinsmen."

Ruth said, "He bade me keep fast by his young men until they have ended all his harvest."

Naomi said, "It is good, my daughter, that thou go out with his maidens."

So Ruth kept fast by the maidens of Boaz through barley harvest and wheat harvest. Each even she returned with grain. She dwelt with her mother-in-law, and the house was not empty.
