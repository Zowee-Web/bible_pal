There was a wealthy man in Bethlehem named Boaz, a relative of Elimelech. He was a mighty man of valor. The barley harvest had come, and the fields outside the city stood ripe and golden under the wide sky.

Ruth the Moabite spoke to Naomi. "Let me go to the field and glean among the ears of grain, behind someone in whose sight I may find favor." Naomi looked at her. "Go, my daughter."

She went out early. The air was cool and dew still clung to the lower stalks. She came to a field and began to glean behind the reapers. She did not know whose field it was. It belonged to Boaz.

She bent low and gathered what fell from the reapers' hands. Chaff stuck to her forearms. The sun rose higher and pressed heat against her back. She moved along the rows with the other gleaners but worked alone, speaking to no one.

Boaz came from Bethlehem. He walked along the edge of the field and greeted his reapers. "May Yahweh be with you." They lifted their heads. "May Yahweh bless you."

He looked across the field and saw the young woman gleaning at the far end. Her head covering was unfamiliar. He turned to his servant. "Whose young woman is this?"

The servant answered. "She is the Moabite woman who came back with Naomi from the country of Moab. She said, 'Please let me glean and gather behind the reapers.' She has continued from morning until now. She has hardly rested."

Boaz crossed the field to where Ruth worked. She straightened when she heard his step. "Listen, my daughter," he said. "Do not go to glean in another field. Do not leave this one. Stay close to my young women. I have charged the young men not to touch you. When you are thirsty, go to the vessels and drink."

Ruth fell on her face. She bowed to the ground. "Why have I found favor in your eyes, that you should notice me, since I am a foreigner?"

Boaz answered. "It has been reported to me, all that you have done for your mother-in-law since your husband died. You left your father, your mother, and the land of your birth. You came to a people you did not know. May Yahweh reward your work. May a full reward be given you by Yahweh, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge."

She said, "Let me find favor in your sight, my lord. You have comforted me and spoken kindly to your servant."

The sun reached its highest point. The reapers set down their sickles and gathered in shade. Boaz called to Ruth. "Come at mealtime. Eat bread and dip your morsel in the vinegar."

She sat beside the reapers. Boaz passed her roasted grain with his own hand. She ate and was satisfied. She had some left over. The bread was coarse, the vinegar sharp, the grain warm from the fire.

When she rose to glean again, Boaz commanded his young men privately. "Let her glean even among the sheaves. Do not rebuke her. Pull out some from the bundles on purpose and leave it for her."

She gleaned in the field until evening. Shadows grew long across the stubble. She beat out what she had gathered, striking the stalks against the ground. The grain fell into her cloth. It was about an ephah of barley — more than a day's wages. She lifted it onto her shoulder and walked back to the city.

Naomi was waiting. Ruth set the grain before her and brought out the food saved from the meal. Naomi looked at the heap of barley. Her eyes widened. "Where did you glean today? Blessed be the man who took notice of you."

Ruth told her. "The man's name is Boaz."

Naomi's hands went still upon the grain. She looked up. "Blessed be he of Yahweh, who has not left off his kindness to the living and to the dead." She paused. "This man is a close relative. He is one of our kinsman-redeemers."

Ruth said, "He told me to stay close to his workers until they have finished all his harvest."

Naomi said, "It is good, my daughter, that you go out with his young women, so that no one meets you in any other field."

So Ruth kept close to the young women of Boaz. She gleaned through the barley harvest and through the wheat harvest. Each evening she returned with grain. She lived with her mother-in-law, and the house was not empty.
