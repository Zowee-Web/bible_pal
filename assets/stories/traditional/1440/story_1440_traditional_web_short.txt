On the third day Esther put on her royal clothing.

She had been three days fasting. She had been three days knowing that to come into the king's inner court without being called was the law of death.

The morning had come up gray. The stone floor of the women's quarter was cool. Her servants stood at the door, not speaking, as she walked past them in her royal robe.

She came up through the courts of the palace, through the long colonnade where the guards stood with their spears against the wall. None of them looked at her face.

She came into the inner court of the king's house, and she stood there, next to the king's house. The king sat on his royal throne in the royal house, next to the entrance of the house.

The king looked up.

She did not move. The light came in through the upper windows. Esther waited.

When the king saw Esther the queen standing in the court, she obtained favor in his sight; and the king held out to Esther the golden scepter that was in his hand. So Esther came near, and touched the top of the scepter.

The king asked her, "What would you like, queen Esther? What is your request? It shall be given you even to the half of the kingdom."

Esther said, "If it seems good to the king, let the king and Haman come today to the banquet that I have prepared for him."

Then the king said, "Bring Haman quickly, so that it may be done as Esther has said." So the king and Haman came to the banquet that Esther had prepared.

At the banquet of wine the king said to Esther, "What is your petition? It shall be granted you. What is your request? Even to the half of the kingdom it shall be performed."

Esther answered, "My petition and my request is this. If I have found favor in the sight of the king, and if it pleases the king to grant my petition and to perform my request, let the king and Haman come to the banquet that I will prepare for them, and I will do tomorrow as the king has said."

The cups had been gathered. The wine had been poured out. Esther sat in the quiet of her own rooms when the lamps were lit, and the day of her speaking had not yet come.
