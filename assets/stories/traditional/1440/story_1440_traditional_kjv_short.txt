And it came to pass on the third day, that Esther put on her royal apparel.

She had fasted three days. She had known three days that to come into the inner court of the king without being called was the law of death.

The morning was come up gray. The stone floor of the women's quarter was cool. Her handmaidens stood at the door, and spake not, as she passed them by in her royal robe.

She came up through the courts of the palace, through the long colonnade where the guards stood with their spears against the wall. None of them looked upon her face.

She came into the inner court of the king's house, and she stood there, over against the king's house. The king sat upon his royal throne in the royal house, over against the gate of the house.

The king lifted up his eyes.

She moved not. The light came in through the upper windows. Esther waited.

And it was so, when the king saw Esther the queen standing in the court, that she obtained favour in his sight: and the king held out to Esther the golden sceptre that was in his hand. So Esther drew near, and touched the top of the sceptre.

Then said the king unto her, "What wilt thou, queen Esther? and what is thy request? it shall be even given thee to the half of the kingdom."

Esther answered, "If it seem good unto the king, let the king and Haman come this day unto the banquet that I have prepared for him."

Then the king said, "Cause Haman to make haste, that it may be done as Esther hath said." So the king and Haman came unto the banquet that Esther had prepared.

At the banquet of wine the king said unto Esther, "What is thy petition? it shall be granted thee. And what is thy request? even to the half of the kingdom it shall be performed."

Esther answered, "My petition and my request is this. If I have found favour in the sight of the king, and if it please the king to grant my petition, and to perform my request, let the king and Haman come unto the banquet that I shall prepare for them, and I will do to morrow as the king hath said."

The cups were gathered. The wine was poured out. Esther sat in the quiet of her own chambers when the lamps were lit, and the day of her speaking was not yet come.
