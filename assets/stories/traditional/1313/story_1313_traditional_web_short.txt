The ark had come to Jerusalem at last. David had brought it up from the house of Obed-Edom with shouting and the sound of horns. He had pitched a tent for it in the City of David. He had offered sacrifices before the Lord. And then he had appointed certain of the Levites to minister before the ark — to celebrate, and to thank, and to praise Yahweh, the God of Israel. Asaph was the chief, and his brothers were with him, with cymbals, and with stringed instruments, and with harps.

And on that day David first delivered the psalm to give thanks to Yahweh, into the hand of Asaph and his brothers.

Oh give thanks to Yahweh. Call on his name. Make what he has done known among the peoples.

Sing to him. Sing praises to him. Tell of all his marvelous works.

Glory in his holy name. Let the heart of those who seek Yahweh rejoice.

Seek Yahweh and his strength. Seek his face forever more.

Remember his marvelous works that he has done, his wonders, and the judgments of his mouth,

you offspring of Israel his servant, you children of Jacob, his chosen ones.

And the psalm was sung that day before the ark, and on the days after, as Asaph and his brothers ministered continually before the tent. The cymbals carried out across the City of David. The strings carried out. And so it was in the days when the ark first dwelt in Jerusalem.
