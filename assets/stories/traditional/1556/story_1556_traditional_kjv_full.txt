Ezekiel had been shown the temple. The man with the measuring line, whose appearance was as the appearance of brass, had walked with him through every gate and every chamber, and had measured the courts and the porches and the rooms of the priests, and had set down for him the shape of the house that should one day stand.

Now, at the end of all the measuring, the man brought him again unto the door of the house.

"Look," he said.

Ezekiel looked. And behold, waters issued out from under the threshold of the house eastward, for the forefront of the house stood toward the east. And the waters came down from under from the right side of the house, at the south side of the altar. It was a small flow, no wider than a hand. It moved over the smooth stone of the threshold, and ran down into the courtyard, and made its way out toward the gate.

The man brought him out of the way of the gate northward, because the south side was where the altar stood and one passed not that way. He led him about the way without unto the utter gate by the way that looketh eastward. And, behold, there ran out waters on the right side.

Then the man that had the line in his hand went forth eastward. The desert lay below, and the dead sea, and the wilderness of En-gedi, but Ezekiel could not yet see them from where he stood. The man measured a thousand cubits. He took Ezekiel by the hand, and he brought him through the waters. The waters were to the ankles.

They had grown.

He measured a thousand. He brought him through. The waters were to the knees.

He measured a thousand. The waters were to the loins.

Afterward he measured a thousand. And it was a river that he could not pass over. The waters had risen further than he would have thought possible from such a thin flow over the threshold. Waters to swim in. A river that could not be passed over.

The man turned unto him. "Son of man," he said, "hast thou seen this?"

Ezekiel said yea. He had seen it. He knew not what else to say.

The man brought him, and caused him to return to the brink of the river. Now when he had returned, behold, at the bank of the river were very many trees on the one side and on the other. They had grown up while he walked. They stood close together in the wet ground. Their leaves moved in the air that came up off the river.

"These waters issue out toward the east country," the man said, "and go down into the desert, and go into the sea. Which being brought forth into the sea, the waters shall be healed. And it shall come to pass, that every thing that liveth, which moveth, whithersoever the rivers shall come, shall live. And there shall be a very great multitude of fish, because these waters shall come thither. For they shall be healed, and every thing shall live whither the river cometh.

"And it shall come to pass, that the fishers shall stand upon it from En-gedi even unto En-eglaim. They shall be a place to spread forth nets. Their fish shall be according to their kinds, as the fish of the great sea, exceeding many."

He paused.

"But the miry places thereof and the marishes thereof shall not be healed. They shall be given to salt. Some of the land shall keep its old work. But the river — the river is not for that."

He gestured unto the trees on either bank.

"And by the river upon the bank thereof, on this side and on that side, shall grow all trees for meat, whose leaf shall not fade, neither shall the fruit thereof be consumed. It shall bring forth new fruit according to his months, because their waters they issued out of the sanctuary. And the fruit thereof shall be for meat, and the leaf thereof for medicine."

The river ran past Ezekiel's feet, going east, going down toward the dead place, bearing the trees of healing along both its banks. He stood and beheld it a long time. The water had begun as a trickle at the door of the house. It had grown four times in four thousand cubits. Where it ended in the sea, the sea should live. And so it was shown unto him, in the vision of the man with the measuring line, what the threshold of the house should one day pour forth.
