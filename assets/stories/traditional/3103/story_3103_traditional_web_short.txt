Now on the last and greatest day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried out, “If anyone is thirsty, let him come to me and drink! He who believes in me, as the Scripture has said, from within him will flow rivers of living water.” He said this about the Spirit, which those believing in him were to receive, for the Holy Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified.

The crowd that heard these words did not agree about him. Many of the multitude said, “This is truly the prophet.” Others said, “This is the Christ.” But some said, “What, does the Christ come out of Galilee? Hasn’t the Scripture said that the Christ comes of the offspring of David, and from Bethlehem, the village where David was?” So, because of him, a division arose in the multitude that had listened to his words. Some of them would even have arrested him, but no one laid hands on him.

The officers therefore came back to the chief priests and Pharisees without him, and the chief priests and Pharisees asked them, “Why didn’t you bring him?” The officers answered, “No man ever spoke like this man!”

The Pharisees answered them, “You aren’t also led astray, are you? Have any of the rulers or any of the Pharisees believed in him? But this multitude that doesn’t know the law is cursed.”

Then Nicodemus raised a question of the law among them. He was the one who had come to Jesus by night, and he was one of their own. “Does our law judge a man unless it first hears from him personally and knows what he does?” he asked the chief priests and the Pharisees.

They answered him, “Are you also from Galilee? Search and see that no prophet has arisen out of Galilee.”
