And the children of Israel did evil in the sight of the Lord. And the Lord delivered them into the hand of Midian seven years. And the Midianites came up with their cattle and their tents. They came as grasshoppers for multitude, and they wasted the land. And Israel was brought very low.

And there came an angel of the Lord, and sat under an oak which was in Ophrah, that pertained unto Joash the Abiezrite. And his son Gideon threshed wheat by the winepress, to hide it from the Midianites.

The winepress was a low stone basin hewn into the hillside. It was not made for the threshing of grain. The walls stayed the wind that would have borne the chaff away upon a proper threshing floor. But a threshing floor sat open to the sky, visible for miles in every quarter. The Midianites watched for grain. They watched for smoke of ovens. They watched for any sign that a household had gathered a thing worth the taking.

So Gideon laboured in the winepress. He swung the flail against the stalks in the cramped space, his elbows scraping the stone walls. Chaff clung to his arms and face and lodged in his hair. The grain fell slowly, handful by handful, into the bottom of the basin. It was weary, hidden, shameful work — a man threshing wheat as a fugitive in his own land.

And the angel of the Lord appeared unto him, and said unto him, "The Lord is with thee, thou mighty man of valour."

Gideon stayed his hand. He looked up from the grain dust. His hands were foul with dirt. His shoulders ached from the bent, cramped labour. Sweat made lines through the chaff upon his face.

And he said unto him, "Oh my lord, if the Lord be with us, why then is all this befallen us? And where be all his miracles which our fathers told us of, saying, Did not the Lord bring us up from Egypt? But now the Lord hath forsaken us, and delivered us into the hands of the Midianites."

It was an honest question. Not defiance, but grief pressed flat by seven years of hiding food in winepresses and watching strangers strip the fields bare.

And the Lord looked upon him, and said, "Go in this thy might, and thou shalt save Israel from the hand of the Midianites. Have not I sent thee?"

And he said unto him, "Oh my Lord, wherewith shall I save Israel? Behold, my family is poor in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house."

He stood in a winepress with grain dust upon his arms. The poorest family. The least among them. This was the man.

And the Lord said unto him, "Surely I will be with thee, and thou shalt smite the Midianites as one man."

And he said unto him, "If now I have found grace in thy sight, then shew me a sign that thou talkest with me. Depart not hence, I pray thee, until I come unto thee, and bring forth my present, and set it before thee."

And he said, "I will tarry until thou come again."

And Gideon went in. He made ready a kid, and unleavened cakes of an ephah of flour. The flesh he put in a basket, and he put the broth in a pot. He carried them forth and set them before him under the oak.

And the angel of God said unto him, "Take the flesh and the unleavened cakes, and lay them upon this rock, and pour out the broth." And he did so. He set the flesh and cakes upon the flat of the rock and poured the broth until it ran over the edges and pooled upon the stone.

Then the angel of the Lord put forth the end of the staff that was in his hand, and touched the flesh and the unleavened cakes. And there rose up fire out of the rock, and consumed the flesh and the unleavened cakes in a single rush of flame and heat. The broth hissed and vanished. The rock was dry.

And the angel of the Lord departed out of his sight.

And when Gideon perceived that he was an angel of the Lord, Gideon said, "Alas, O Lord God! For because I have seen an angel of the Lord face to face."

And the Lord said unto him, "Peace be unto thee. Fear not. Thou shalt not die."

Then Gideon built an altar there unto the Lord, and called it Jehovah-shalom — The Lord Is Peace. Unto this day it is yet in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.
