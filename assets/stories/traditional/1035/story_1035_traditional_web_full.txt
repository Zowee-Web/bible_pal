The people of Israel did what was evil in the sight of the Lord, and the Lord gave them into the hand of Midian seven years. The Midianites came up with their livestock and their tents. They came like locusts in number, and they laid waste the land. Israel was brought very low.

The angel of the Lord came and sat under the oak at Ophrah, which belonged to Joash the Abiezrite. His son Gideon was beating out wheat in the winepress to hide it from the Midianites.

The winepress was a low stone basin cut into the hillside. It was not built for threshing grain. The walls blocked the wind that would have carried the chaff away on a proper threshing floor. But a threshing floor sat open to the sky, visible for miles in every direction. The Midianites watched for grain. They watched for smoke from ovens. They watched for any sign that a family had harvested something worth taking.

So Gideon worked in the winepress. He swung the flail against the stalks in the cramped space, his elbows scraping the stone walls. Chaff stuck to his arms and face and lodged in his hair. The grain fell slowly, handful by handful, into the bottom of the basin. It was exhausting, hidden, shameful work — a man threshing wheat like a fugitive in his own land.

The angel of the Lord said to him, "The Lord is with you, O mighty man of valor."

Gideon stopped. He looked up from the grain dust. His hands were dirty. His shoulders ached from the bent, cramped swinging. Sweat tracked lines through the chaff on his face.

He said, "Please, my lord, if the Lord is with us, why then has all this happened to us? And where are all his wonderful deeds that our fathers told us about? They said, 'Did not the Lord bring us up from Egypt?' But now the Lord has forsaken us and given us into the hand of Midian."

It was an honest question. Not defiance, but grief pressed flat by seven years of hiding food in winepresses and watching strangers strip the fields.

The Lord turned to him and said, "Go in this might of yours and save Israel from the hand of Midian. Do not I send you?"

Gideon said, "Please, Lord, how can I save Israel? Behold, my clan is the weakest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house."

He stood in a winepress with grain dust on his arms. The weakest clan. The smallest member. This was the man.

The Lord said to him, "But I will be with you, and you shall strike the Midianites as one man."

Gideon said, "If now I have found favor in your eyes, then show me a sign that it is you who speaks with me. Do not depart from here, please, until I come to you and bring out my offering and set it before you."

And he said, "I will stay until you return."

So Gideon went into his house. He prepared a young goat and unleavened cakes from an ephah of flour. He put the meat in a basket and the broth in a pot. He carried them out and presented them under the oak tree.

The angel of God said to him, "Take the meat and the unleavened cakes, and lay them on this rock, and pour the broth over them." Gideon did so. He set the meat and cakes on the flat surface of the rock and poured the broth until it ran over the edges and pooled on the stone.

Then the angel of the Lord reached out the tip of the staff that was in his hand and touched the meat and the unleavened cakes. Fire sprang up from the rock. It consumed the meat and the cakes in a single rush of flame and heat. The broth hissed and vanished. The rock was dry.

And the angel of the Lord vanished from his sight.

Then Gideon perceived that he had been speaking with the angel of the Lord. He cried out, "Alas, O Lord God! For now I have seen the angel of the Lord face to face."

But the Lord said to him, "Peace be to you. Do not fear. You shall not die."

Then Gideon built an altar there to the Lord and called it "The Lord Is Peace." To this day it still stands at Ophrah, which belongs to the Abiezrites.
