At the edge of the city, near the wide plains under a sweep of bright sky, a group of people gathered. Their robes shifted in the steady breeze. Some sat on the stones, some stood. In the open sunlight, dust moved in slow spirals at their feet, shining and falling back to earth. The voices were low until a man began to speak, his words carrying clear through the air. Travelers and workers paused, faces turning toward him. The cattle nearby stood chewing, their bell-clappers softly striking now and again. The branches in a small stand of olive trees moved with the wind, tossing silvery leaves back and forth.

The man stood tall in the center, his voice gathering strength. “Haven’t you known? Haven’t you heard?” He looked around at the upturned faces, arms slightly lifted. “The everlasting God, Yahweh, the Creator of the ends of the earth, does not faint. He doesn’t grow weary.”

A woman nearby nodded, holding her child’s hand. The people pressed closer, listening. The sun moved, brightening the stones and throwing long shadows of the gathering. Overhead, a flock of birds crossed the blue sky, their wings flashing in the light.

His tone stayed firm. “There is no searching of his understanding.” The speaker’s staff tapped the ground as he shifted his weight. “He gives power to the weak. To those who have no might, he increases strength.” Two young men helped a bent old man to settle onto a flat rock, careful not to disturb the dust.

Further off, sheep grazed quietly, their voices low and rough in the dry grass. Children played near a wall of sun-warmed stones, their laughter quick and high. Horses pawed idly at the ground while distant workers raised up buckets of water at the community well.

The speaker’s voice rang out stronger. “Even the youths will faint and be weary. The young men will utterly fall.” He paused, looking from face to face, arms spreading a little with the words. “But those who wait for Yahweh will renew their strength.”

Clouds made shifting patterns over the fields, stripes of light and shade sliding over the land. A shepherd leaned on his staff at the far end of the assembly, listening.

The man’s voice rose as he said, “They will mount up with wings like eagles.” As he spoke, he turned his open palm to the sky. “They will run, and not be weary. They will walk, and not faint.”

His last words settled over the crowd. The wind stirred the robes and the olive branches again. The people remained quiet, the sounds of the land continuing around them, as sunlight shone through the gathering.
