Crowds gather in the wide valleys, where the land rises gently toward distant ridges. People stand in open places, sunlight pouring down across their shoulders. Some lean on staffs. Others draw close, children gathered at their sides. The sky above is a deep, endless blue, stretching further than any road they have walked. There is a steady stirring in the air, as if the world waits for a voice to shape the silence.

From among the people, one voice lifts. “Have you not known?” The words ring clear across the low hills. “Have you not heard?” Murmurs travel through the listeners. Some men shift, glancing at each other, as if searching for the answer in one another’s gaze. Women hush their children, tuning their ears. The breeze plays over the gathered heads, riffling cloaks and stirs the dry grasses.

“The Lord is the everlasting God,” the voice continues, rising above the noise of sandals shifting on packed earth. “He is the Creator of the ends of the earth.” People look from one horizon to another, where far-off mountains meet the sky. No one can see beyond those ridges, yet the words carry further still. A boy traces lines in the dust with his finger, pausing to look upward.

The speaker’s voice is strong, echoing along the field’s edge, “He doesn’t faint. He doesn’t grow weary.” Shoulders straighten. No sign of fatigue can be seen near the speaker. Sunlight glints from a patch of water at the stream’s bend, shimmering beside scattered stones. The crowd stands quiet, listening as wind rustles low branches and stones clatter softly beneath shifting feet.

“His understanding is unsearchable,” says the voice. The words settle into the hush, as people stand in clusters. Some shade their eyes, peering into distance. Others draw their robes tighter as the late-day breeze sweeps through. In the distance, a bird darts high in the sky, wings wide and unhurried.

The voice calls again, steady and clear, “He gives power to the weak.” Some reach out to grasp another’s shoulder. Old men straighten. Among the children, a small girl lifts herself a little higher, drawn by the promise in that voice. The speaker gestures toward the gathered crowd, making a broad arc with his arm.

“To him who has no might, he increases strength.” Women balance sleeping infants on their hips, bending their heads to catch the rest. A youth, leaning heavily on a stick, lifts his face toward the sun as if measuring the words one by one. The air holds steady, the words hanging between earth and sky.

The sun drops lower, painting gold across the valley. Sheep graze further out, their wool bright in late light. Laughter drifts up from the edges of the crowd, quiet and brief. “Even the youths will faint and be weary,” the speaker’s voice carries. “The young men will utterly fall.” Young men glance at each other, standing tall, arms folded. Even their strength, the voice states, will fail. The field is full of quiet steps, the shuffling of sandals in dust.

“But those who wait for the Lord will renew their strength.” The voice grows firm. People listen closely, straining to hear each word. The breeze quiets, dust settling around gathered feet. “They will mount up with wings like eagles.” Eyes turn upward, searching the sky, watching as a great bird circles far above the plain, dark against the sun.

“They will run, and not be weary.” Some in the crowd extend their hands as if reaching for something rising above the earth. Young children sway in place, shifting from foot to foot. Others stand steady, gripped by the sound of the promise.

“They will walk, and not faint.” The voice finishes, the echo resting over the assembly. For a long moment, people stand together in the glowing light. The day wanes, but the words hold fast above the moving people.

Children press close to their mothers. Neighbors exchange murmured greetings. A few men begin to head home, walking slowly across the field, their shadows lengthening. Staffs tap steadily against the ground. Sheep lift their heads, ears flicking. Cicadas sing in the dusk. The crowd thins gradually, the message echoing faintly as sandals tread back along the worn paths—words shared quietly, movement gentle beneath the late sky.

Night stretches in, cool and calm across the valley. The land lies still. The children rest, faces pressed to their mothers’ robes. Scattered voices repeat lines softly, while above, the first stars blink into view. The day fades and quiet surrounds those remaining in the field. The words spoken remain like the hush of wind in the grass, carried across the open land beneath the watching sky.
