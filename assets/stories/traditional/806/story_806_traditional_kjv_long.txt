The air was clear above the slopes, where the stones shone with the early morning light. The ground underfoot was dry and pale, scattered with small tufts of stubborn grass. In the valley below, low clouds moved quietly, stretching themselves out like veils, and above it all the sky stood wide and blue. Overhead, birds wheeled high. Their wings cut against the light with silent, steady strokes.

A company of men and women walked slowly along the narrow path, their shadows trailing behind them. They paused often, leaning upon their staves or crouching beside cool stones. Dust clave unto their sandals and the hems of their robes. Some were older, their hair silvered and their backs bent. Some were yet young, their steps quick, but as the way climbed the ridge, even the strongest slowed, wiped sweat from their brows, and breathed through dry lips. Each bare what his strength allowed: a bundle of blankets, a jar, or a woven bag.

At the head of the company, a shepherd turned and waited for the last of them. He stood in the sun, the folds of his outer wrap catching the morning breeze. His gaze moved from the shining horizon unto those gathered behind him. Then he lifted up his staff and spake, his voice clear in the broad space between earth and sky.

"Hast thou not known? Hast thou not heard?"

The company stood still, their feet pressed into the earth, their faces turned unto him. A child plucked at his mother's sleeve. A woman pressed her hand unto her brow to shade her eyes, peering upward. Distant sheep were bleating, their sound thin in the air, but the voice of the shepherd was stronger, rising above the wind.

He waited as the wind rattled the grasses and the sun climbed higher, painting the rocks pale gold. He spake again. "The everlasting God, the Lord, the Creator of the ends of the earth, fainteth not, neither is he weary."

The sound of his words lingered over the stones. The travellers hearkened, their eyes searching the horizon or dropping unto the ground. In the hush, a raven called from a crag above, flapping its dark wings.

Moving forward, the shepherd's feet sent pebbles clattering down the slope. He bent, drew a circle in the dust with the end of his staff, and looked up unto the sky. High above, a cloud drifted, bright and slow. The shepherd continued, his voice steady, "There is no searching of his understanding."

The company pressed closer. Cloth rustled, and a jar clunked against the rocks. A boy shifted and beheld the shepherd trace lines in the dirt at his feet, the dust rising in tiny spirals. In the silence, a breeze swept through, brushing against faces and fluttering the corners of cloaks.

The shepherd arose and opened his hands, as if to show the company the wide sky and the sun climbing ever upward. "He giveth power to the faint," he said, his voice ringing in the cool morning. "And to them that have no might he increaseth strength."

For a long moment, the words settled among them. The sun warmed their backs, and the humming of small insects grew loud along the way's edge. The older men leaned upon their staves. One flexed his hand, gnarled from years of labour, brushing sand from his fingers.

The shepherd's words drifted into the spaces between them. "Even the youths shall faint and be weary," he said, his gaze travelling unto the youngest travellers, "and the young men shall utterly fall."

At that, the boy standing by his mother's side looked away, his head bowed. Further back, a maid, tall and slim, wiped her hands upon her skirt and moved her feet, the shadows playing across her sandals.

The shepherd stepped aside, giving the company a view further down the slope, beyond the clumps of wild thyme unto the rolling hills beyond. "But they that wait upon the Lord shall renew their strength," he called.

He gestured upward, as a flock of birds wheeled higher into a pocket of wind. Their wings flashed white and gray. They moved together, rising upon invisible currents, gliding without faltering.

The shepherd pointed, letting his words ride the stillness. "They shall mount up with wings as eagles."

For a moment, all eyes followed the birds as they soared, their pinions spread wide. The travel-worn gathered closer, their shadows mingling upon the earth. A stray breeze tugged at a woven sash. Dust motes danced in the sunlight.

The shepherd's voice grew softer, yet every word was clear in the morning air: "They shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint."

One of the older women straightened herself, gripping her staff. Her veil fluttered at her shoulders. Near her, a small boy clapped his hands once, quick and light, and the sound faded into the hush that lingered over the trail.

The company remained silent, the stones yet warm beneath their feet. The valley behind them was dotted with olive trees, their leaves shimmering like tiny coins. Above, the sky stretched wide and unbroken.

The words the shepherd had spoken seemed to settle over them like the sun's warmth, filling the ridge where they paused. The birds continued to spiral above, steady and swift, their movements easy beneath the open sky.

Near the edge of a rock, a woman kneeled, rearranging the jars in her bag. The light painted her hands gold. The company made ready to continue, gathering up their bundles and lifting their staves. The narrow path followed the shoulder of the hill, curving through the grasses and pale wildflowers. The shepherd walked among them, his steps steady, and they followed him, the sounds of their movement soft against the earth and stone.

Further along the way, the air cooled in a shallow hollow. A trickle of water ran through the rocks, pooled in a hollowed stone, and slipped away beneath the grass. The oldest man cupped his hands and dipped them into the spring, letting the water glisten upon his fingers. The children beheld, wide-eyed, as the droplets gleamed in the sun.

The day brightened steadily, the sun stretched higher, pouring a white light over the hills. Shadows grew shorter. The travellers pressed on, step by slow step, their faces lit with the morning's clear glow.

At another rise, the way levelled out, and the shepherd paused again. He waited for all to catch up, lifting up his staff toward the highest point. There he told them once more, "The everlasting God, the Lord, fainteth not, neither is he weary." His words landed among them, firm as the stones beneath their feet.

Onward they walked, their line stretching along the ridge. Warm wind carried the soft murmur of words between them. The sound of their sandals stirred the dust. Every so often, a bird would cry from above, cutting the air with a sharp call, its wings wide.

A faint trail led them downward again, past tufts of grass dotted with tiny blue blooms. The shepherd led the way, and the travellers followed, moving from sunlight into shadow and back again. The world before them seemed wide, the earth steady underfoot.

In this hush, every figure, young and old, pressed onward — moving, step by step, across the hill, guided by the shepherd's steady voice and the stirring light of morning. The words he had spoken — of the God that groweth not weary, of strength given unto the faint — remained echoing in the open air, as the birds soared high upon bright wings, circling endlessly against the deep blue sky.
