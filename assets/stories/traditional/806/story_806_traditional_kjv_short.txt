At the edge of the city, hard by the wide plains beneath a sweep of bright sky, a company of people gathered. Their robes shifted in the steady breeze. Some sat upon the stones, some stood. In the open sunlight, dust moved in slow spirals at their feet, shining and falling back unto the earth. Their voices were low until a man began to speak, his words carrying clear through the air. Travellers and labourers paused, their faces turning toward him. The cattle nearby stood chewing, their bell-clappers softly striking now and again. The branches in a small stand of olive trees moved with the wind, tossing silvery leaves to and fro.

The man stood tall in the midst, his voice gathering strength. "Hast thou not known? Hast thou not heard?" He looked round about upon the upturned faces, his arms slightly lifted. "The everlasting God, the Lord, the Creator of the ends of the earth, fainteth not, neither is weary."

A woman nearby nodded, holding her child's hand. The people pressed closer, hearkening. The sun moved, brightening the stones and casting long shadows of the gathering. Overhead, a flock of birds crossed the blue sky, their wings flashing in the light.

His tone stayed firm. "There is no searching of his understanding." The speaker's staff tapped the ground as he shifted his weight. "He giveth power to the faint; and to them that have no might he increaseth strength." Two young men helped a bent old man to settle upon a flat rock, careful not to disturb the dust.

Further off, sheep grazed quietly, their voices low and rough in the dry grass. Children played near a wall of sun-warmed stones, their laughter quick and high. Horses pawed idly at the ground while distant labourers drew up buckets of water at the common well.

The speaker's voice rang out stronger. "Even the youths shall faint and be weary, and the young men shall utterly fall." He paused, looking from face to face, his arms spreading a little with the words. "But they that wait upon the Lord shall renew their strength."

Clouds made shifting patterns over the fields, stripes of light and shade sliding over the land. A shepherd leaned upon his staff at the far end of the assembly, hearkening.

The man's voice arose as he said, "They shall mount up with wings as eagles." As he spake, he turned his open palm toward the sky. "They shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint."

His last words settled over the throng. The wind stirred the robes and the olive branches again. The people remained quiet, the sounds of the land continuing round about them, as sunlight shone through the gathering.
