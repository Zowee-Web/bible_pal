The people gather in the wide valleys, where the land riseth gently toward distant ridges. They stand in open places, sunlight pouring down upon their shoulders. Some lean upon staves. Others draw close, their children gathered at their sides. The sky above is a deep, endless blue, stretching further than any road that they have walked. There is a steady stirring in the air, as if the world waited for a voice to shape the silence.

From among the people, one voice lifteth up. "Hast thou not known?" The words ring clear across the low hills. "Hast thou not heard?" Murmurs travel through the listeners. Some men shift, glancing at one another, as if searching for the answer in each other's gaze. Women hush their children, tuning their ears. The breeze playeth over the gathered heads, riffling cloaks and stirring the dry grasses.

"The Lord is the everlasting God," the voice continueth, rising above the noise of sandals shifting upon packed earth. "He is the Creator of the ends of the earth." The people look from one horizon unto another, where far-off mountains meet the sky. None can see beyond those ridges, yet the words carry further still. A boy traceth lines in the dust with his finger, pausing to look upward.

The speaker's voice is strong, echoing along the field's edge: "He fainteth not, neither is weary." Shoulders straighten. No sign of weariness can be seen nigh the speaker. Sunlight glinteth from a patch of water at the stream's bend, shimmering beside the scattered stones. The throng standeth quiet, hearkening as wind rustleth low branches and stones clatter softly beneath shifting feet.

"There is no searching of his understanding," saith the voice. The words settle into the hush, as the people stand in clusters. Some shade their eyes, peering into the distance. Others draw their robes tighter as the late-day breeze sweepeth through. In the distance, a bird darteth high in the sky, its wings wide and unhurried.

The voice calleth again, steady and clear: "He giveth power to the faint." Some reach out to grasp another's shoulder. Old men straighten themselves. Among the children, a small maid lifteth herself a little higher, drawn by the promise in that voice. The speaker gestureth toward the gathered throng, making a broad arc with his arm.

"To them that have no might he increaseth strength." Women balance sleeping babes upon their hips, bending their heads to catch the rest. A youth, leaning heavily upon a stick, lifteth his face toward the sun as if measuring the words one by one. The air holdeth steady, the words hanging between earth and sky.

The sun droppeth lower, painting gold across the valley. Sheep graze further out, their wool bright in the late light. Laughter drifteth up from the edges of the throng, quiet and brief. "Even the youths shall faint and be weary," the speaker's voice carrieth, "and the young men shall utterly fall." Young men glance at one another, standing tall, their arms folded. Even their strength, the voice declareth, shall fail. The field is full of quiet steps, of the shuffling of sandals in the dust.

"But they that wait upon the Lord shall renew their strength." The voice groweth firm. The people hearken closely, straining to catch every word. The breeze quieteth, dust settling about the gathered feet. "They shall mount up with wings as eagles." Eyes turn upward, searching the sky, watching as a great bird circleth far above the plain, dark against the sun.

"They shall run, and not be weary." Some in the throng stretch out their hands as if reaching for something rising above the earth. Young children sway in place, shifting from foot to foot. Others stand steady, gripped by the sound of the promise.

"They shall walk, and not faint." The voice finisheth, the echo resting over the assembly. For a long moment, the people stand together in the glowing light. The day waneth, but the words hold fast above the moving people.

Children press close unto their mothers. Neighbours exchange murmured greetings. A few men begin to head home, walking slowly across the field, their shadows lengthening. Staves tap steadily against the ground. Sheep lift up their heads, their ears flicking. Cicadas sing in the dusk. The throng thinneth gradually, the message echoing faintly as sandals tread back along the worn paths — words shared quietly, movement gentle beneath the late sky.

Night stretcheth in, cool and calm across the valley. The land lieth still. The children rest, their faces pressed unto their mothers' robes. Scattered voices repeat the lines softly, while above, the first stars blink into view. The day fadeth and quiet surroundeth those that remain in the field. The words spoken remain like the hush of wind in the grass, borne across the open land beneath the watching sky.
