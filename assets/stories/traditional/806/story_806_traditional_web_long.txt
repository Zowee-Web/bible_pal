The air was clear above the slopes, where the stones shone with early morning light. The ground underfoot was dry and pale, scattered with small tufts of stubborn grass. In the valley below, low clouds moved quietly, stretching themselves like veils, and above it all the sky stood wide and blue. Overhead, birds wheeled high. Their wings cut against the light with silent, steady strokes.

A group of men and women walked slowly along the narrow path, their shadows trailing behind them. They paused often, leaning on their staffs or crouching by cool stones. Dust clung to their sandals and the edges of their robes. Some were older, their hair silvered and their backs bent. Some were still young, their steps quick, but as the road climbed the ridge, even the strongest slowed, wiped sweat from their brows, and breathed through dry lips. Each carried what his strength allowed: a bundle of blankets, a jar, or a woven bag.

At the head of the group, a shepherd turned and waited for the last of them. He stood in the sun, the folds of his outer wrap catching the morning breeze. His gaze moved from the shining horizon to those gathered behind him. Then, he lifted his staff and spoke, his voice clear in the broad space between earth and sky.

“Haven’t you known? Haven’t you heard?”

The group stood still, their feet pressed into the earth, their faces turned to him. A child plucked at his mother’s sleeve. A woman pressed her hand to her brow to shade her eyes, peering upward. Distant sheep were bleating, their sound thin in the air, but the voice of the shepherd was stronger, rising above the wind.

He waited as the wind rattled the grasses and the sun climbed higher, painting the rocks pale gold. He spoke again. “The everlasting God, Yahweh, the Creator of the ends of the earth, does not faint. He is not weary.”

The sound of his words lingered over the stones. The travelers listened, eyes searching the horizon or dropping to the ground. In the hush, a raven called from a crag above, flapping its dark wings.

Moving forward, the shepherd’s feet sent pebbles clattering down the slope. He bent, drew a circle in the dust with the end of his staff, and looked up at the sky. High above, a cloud drifted, bright and slow. The shepherd continued, his voice steady, “There is no searching of his understanding.”

The group pressed closer. Cloth rustled, and a jar clunked against the rocks. A boy shifted and watched the shepherd trace lines in the dirt at his feet, the dust rising in tiny spirals. In the silence, a breeze swept through, brushing against faces and fluttering the corners of cloaks.

The shepherd straightened and opened his hands, as if to show the crowd the wide sky and the sun climbing ever upward. “He gives power to the faint,” he said, his voice ringing in the cool morning. “To him who has no might, he increases strength.”

For a long moment, the words settled among them. The sun warmed their backs, and the humming of small insects grew loud along the path’s edge. The older men leaned against their staffs. One flexed his hand, gnarled from years of work, brushing sand from his fingers.

The shepherd’s words drifted into the spaces between them. “Even the youths will faint and be weary,” he said, his gaze traveling to the youngest travelers, “and the young men will utterly fall.”

At that, the boy standing by his mother’s side looked away, head bowed. Further back, a girl, tall and slim, wiped her hands on her skirt and moved her feet, the shadows playing across her sandals.

The shepherd stepped aside, allowing the group a view further down the slope, beyond the clumps of wild thyme to the rolling hills beyond. “But those who wait for Yahweh will renew their strength,” he called.

He gestured upward, as a flock of birds wheeled higher into a pocket of wind. Their wings flashed white and gray. They moved together, rising on invisible currents, gliding without faltering.

The shepherd pointed, letting his words ride the stillness. “They will mount up with wings like eagles.”

For a moment, all eyes followed the birds as they soared, their pinions spread wide. The travel-worn gathered closer, their shadows mingling on the earth. A stray breeze tugged at a woven sash. Dust motes danced in the sunlight.

The shepherd’s voice grew softer, but every word was clear in the morning air: “They will run, and not be weary. They will walk, and not faint.”

One of the older women straightened, gripping her staff. Her veil fluttered at her shoulders. Near her, a small boy clapped his hands once, quick and light, and the sound faded into the hush that lingered over the trail.

The group remained silent, the stones still warm beneath their feet. The valley behind them was dotted with olive trees, their leaves shimmering like tiny coins. Above, the sky stretched wide and unbroken.

The words the shepherd had spoken seemed to settle over them like the sun’s warmth, filling the ridge where they paused. The birds continued to spiral above, steady and swift, their movements easy beneath the open sky.

Near the edge of a rock, a woman knelt, rearranging the jars in her bag. The light painted her hands gold. The group readied themselves to continue, gathering up their bundles and lifting their staffs. The narrow path followed the shoulder of the hill, curving through the grasses and pale wildflowers. The shepherd walked among them, his steps steady, and they followed, the sounds of their movement soft against the earth and stone.

Further along the way, the air cooled in a shallow hollow. A trickle of water ran through the rocks, pooled in a hollowed stone, and slipped away beneath the grass. The oldest man cupped his hands and dipped them into the spring, letting the water glisten on his fingers. The children watched, wide-eyed, as the droplets gleamed in the sun.

The day brightened steadily, the sun stretched higher, pouring a white light over the hills. Shadows grew shorter. The travelers pressed on, step by slow step, their faces lit with the morning’s clear glow.

At another rise, the path leveled out, and the shepherd paused again. He waited for all to catch up, lifting his staff toward the highest point. There he told them once more, “The everlasting God, Yahweh, does not faint. He is not weary.” His words landed among them, firm as the stones beneath their feet.

Onward they walked, their line stretching along the ridge. Warm wind carried the soft murmur of words between them. The sound of their sandals stirred the dust. Every so often, a bird would cry from above, cutting the air with a sharp call, its wings wide.

A faint trail led them downward again, past tufts of grass dotted with tiny blue blooms. The shepherd led the way, and the travelers followed, moving from sunlight into shadow and back again. The world before them seemed wide, the earth steady underfoot.

In this hush, each figure, young and old, pressed onward—moving, step by step, across the hill, guided by the shepherd’s steady voice and the stirring light of morning. The words he had spoken—of God who does not grow tired, of strength given to the weary—remained echoing in the open air, as the birds soared high on bright wings, circling endlessly against the deep blue sky.
