There was a man in Bethlehem whose name was Boaz. He was a kinsman of Elimelech, who had been husband unto Naomi. And Boaz was a man of great substance. His fields lay broad beyond the walls of the town, and the number of his servants was not few.

And Ruth, the woman of Moab, spake unto Naomi in the small dwelling where they had made their abode. "Suffer me to go out unto the fields," said she, "that I might gather grain behind any man in whose sight I shall find grace."

And Naomi regarded her. "Go, my daughter," she said.

So Ruth departed from the house and passed through the narrow ways of Bethlehem. The morning air was already warm upon the land. Dust hung in the light where feet had troubled the road before her. And beyond the last of the stone walls the barley fields opened wide, row upon row, golden and heavy with grain that was ripe for the cutting. Workers moved through the stalks in ordered lines, and the sound of their sickles carried over the open ground — a steady measure of blade against stalk, blade against stalk.

And Ruth came unto a certain field, and she began to gather. She stooped low and took up the loose ears of grain that the reapers had left, drawing them from among the stubble. She knew not whose field it was. But it was the field of Boaz.

The sun climbed higher. The heat bore down upon the open land. And Ruth's hands moved without ceasing. She bent and gathered, bent and gathered. Chaff clung to the sweat upon her arms. Her shadow grew short beneath her as the noon hour drew nigh. Since the early morning she had laboured, and scarcely had she paused to rest.

Then came Boaz out from Bethlehem. He walked along the margin of the field where the cut barley lay bound in sheaves, and he lifted up his voice unto his reapers as he drew near.

"The Lord be with you," said he.

And their voices rose together from the rows. "The Lord bless thee."

Boaz moved among them, and the workers parted and gave way as he passed. He looked out over the field and beheld a young woman whom he knew not. She laboured behind the reapers, gathering stalks near to the ground. Her garments were plain. She was not one of his servants.

He turned unto the young man who had charge over the reapers. "Who is this young woman?" he asked.

And the servant answered him. "She is the woman out of Moab, who returned with Naomi from the country of Moab. She came unto me this morning and sought leave to gather among the sheaves behind the reapers. And she hath been upon her feet from that hour. Scarcely hath she sat down."

Boaz looked upon Ruth a moment longer. Then he walked across the cut rows unto the place where she laboured. The stubble broke beneath his sandals. And Ruth, seeing him approach, rose up and stood straight.

"Hear me, daughter," Boaz said unto her. His voice was steady and sure. "Go not to glean in any other field. Depart not from this place. Abide here, keeping close by my young women. Mark where my reapers do cut, and follow thou after them."

He paused. A warm wind passed through the standing grain beyond the place where the reapers had not yet come.

"I have charged the young men concerning thee," said he. "None shall lay hand upon thee. And when thirst cometh upon thee, go unto the vessels of water and drink of that which the servants have drawn."

Ruth answered not at once. She went down unto the ground. She fell upon her face and bowed herself low, her forehead near unto the dirt, her palms laid flat against the rough stubble. Small pieces of broken stalk pressed into her skin.

"Wherefore have I found grace in thine eyes," she said from the ground, "that thou shouldest take notice of me? For I am a stranger."

And Boaz stood above her. Behind him the reapers continued in their labour, their sickles flashing in short arcs through the stalks. Sweat darkened the cloth upon their shoulders. The sheaves lay bound along the rows.

"All hath been declared unto me," said Boaz. "All that thou hast done for thy mother-in-law since her husband was taken from her. How thou didst leave thy father. How thou didst leave thy mother. How thou didst leave the land of thy birth and come unto a people whom thou hadst not known."

His shadow fell upon her where she knelt. And the wind stirred again, and the standing grain bowed and rose.

"May the Lord recompense thy labour," said he. "May thy reward be full and wanting nothing, given thee by the Lord, the God of Israel, beneath whose wings thou art come to shelter thyself."

And Ruth lifted her face. Dust lay upon her forehead and in the lines of her hands. She looked up at Boaz.

"Let me find grace in thy sight, my lord," she said. "For thou hast given me comfort. Thou hast spoken gently unto thy handmaid, though I am not worthy to be counted even among thy maidservants."

And the reapers moved onward through the rows. The sound of their blades did not cease. Ruth knelt in the stubble with her gathered grain about her, and Boaz stood before her in the bright and open field.
