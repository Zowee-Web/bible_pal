There was a man in Bethlehem named Boaz. He was a man of great wealth, a relative of Elimelech, who had been Naomi's husband.

Ruth, the woman from Moab, spoke to Naomi. "Let me go out to the fields," she said. "Let me gather grain behind anyone who will allow it."

Naomi told her to go.

Ruth walked out past the edge of town where the barley fields stretched wide and gold under the morning sun. She came to a field and began to gather the loose stalks left behind by the reapers. She did not know it, but the field belonged to Boaz.

The sun climbed. Ruth bent and gathered, bent and gathered, moving slowly behind the workers. Dust stuck to her arms. The morning wore on toward midday.

Then Boaz came from Bethlehem. He walked among the rows and called to his reapers. "The Lord be with you," he said.

They answered him together. "The Lord bless you."

Boaz saw Ruth working among the stalks. He turned to his servant, the one set over the reapers. "Whose young woman is this?" he asked.

The servant answered. "She is the woman from Moab who came back with Naomi. She asked if she could gather among the sheaves behind the reapers. She has been here since early morning. She has hardly rested."

Boaz walked to where Ruth was working. She straightened when he spoke.

"Listen to me, daughter," he said. "Do not go to any other field. Do not leave this place. Stay close to my young women. Watch where the reapers are cutting and follow behind them. I have told the young men not to bother you. When you are thirsty, go to the water jars and drink what the servants have drawn."

Ruth dropped to the ground. She bowed low, her face near the dirt, her hands flat against the stubble.

"Why would you notice me?" she said. "Why would you show me kindness? I am a foreigner."

Boaz stood over her. The reapers worked on behind him, their blades catching light.

"I have been told everything," he said. "What you did for your mother-in-law after her husband died. How you left your father and your mother and the land where you were born. How you came to a people you did not know before. May the Lord repay your work. May a full reward be given to you by the Lord, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take shelter."

Ruth lifted her face from the ground. Dust marked her forehead and the lines of her palms.

"Let me find favor in your sight, my lord," she said. "You have comforted me. You have spoken kindly to your servant, though I am not even like one of your maidservants."
