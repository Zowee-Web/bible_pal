There was a man in Bethlehem named Boaz. He was a relative of Elimelech, who had been Naomi's husband. Boaz was a man of great wealth. His fields spread wide outside the town, and his workers were many.

Ruth, the woman from Moab, spoke to Naomi in the small house where they had settled. "Let me go out to the fields," she said. "Let me gather grain behind anyone in whose sight I find favor."

Naomi looked at her. "Go, my daughter," she said.

Ruth left the house and walked through the narrow lanes of Bethlehem. The morning air was warm already. Dust hung in the light where feet had stirred the road. Beyond the last stone walls the barley fields opened up, row after row, gold and heavy with grain ready for cutting. Workers moved through the stalks in lines. The sound of their sickles carried across the open ground — a steady rhythm of blade and stalk.

Ruth came to a field and began to gather. She picked up the loose ears of grain that the reapers left behind, stooping low and pulling them from the stubble. She did not know whose field it was. It happened to belong to Boaz.

The sun rose higher. Heat pressed down on the open land. Ruth's hands moved steadily. She bent and gathered, bent and gathered. Chaff stuck to the sweat on her arms. Her shadow shortened beneath her as midday approached. She had been working since early morning and had barely stopped to rest.

Then Boaz came from Bethlehem. He walked along the edge of the field where the cut barley lay in sheaves, and he called out to his reapers as he drew near.

"The Lord be with you," he said.

Their voices rose together from the rows. "The Lord bless you."

Boaz moved among them. The workers parted and made way as he passed. He looked out over the field and saw a young woman he did not recognize. She was working behind the reapers, gathering stalks near the ground. Her clothes were plain. She was not one of his servants.

He turned to the young man who oversaw the reapers. "Whose young woman is this?" he asked.

The servant answered. "She is the woman from Moab. She came back with Naomi out of the country of Moab. She came to me this morning and asked permission to gather among the sheaves behind the reapers. She has been on her feet since then. She has hardly sat down."

Boaz looked at Ruth a moment longer. Then he walked across the cut rows to where she was working. The stubble crunched under his sandals. Ruth saw him coming and straightened.

"Listen to me, daughter," Boaz said. His voice was steady and clear. "Do not go to glean in another field. Do not leave this place. Stay here, close by my young women. Watch where my reapers are cutting and follow behind them."

He paused. A warm wind moved through the standing grain beyond where the reapers had not yet reached.

"I have given orders to the young men," he said. "They will not touch you. And when you are thirsty, go to the water jars and drink what the servants have drawn."

Ruth did not answer right away. She dropped to the ground. She fell on her face and bowed herself low, her forehead near the dirt, her palms flat against the rough stubble. Small pieces of broken stalk pressed into her skin.

"Why have I found favor in your eyes?" she said from the ground. "Why would you take notice of me? I am a foreigner."

Boaz stood above her. Behind him the reapers kept working, their sickles flashing in short arcs. Sweat darkened the cloth across their shoulders. Sheaves lay bound in the rows.

"It has all been told to me," Boaz said. "Everything you have done for your mother-in-law since her husband died. How you left your father. How you left your mother. How you left the land where you were born and came to a people you did not know before."

His shadow fell over her where she knelt. The wind moved again and the standing grain bent and recovered.

"May the Lord repay your work," he said. "May a full reward be given to you by the Lord, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take shelter."

Ruth lifted her face. Dust marked her forehead and the creases of her hands. She looked up at Boaz.

"Let me find favor in your sight, my lord," she said. "You have comforted me. You have spoken kindly to your servant, though I am not even like one of your maidservants."

The reapers moved on through the rows. The sound of their blades continued. Ruth knelt in the stubble with her gathered grain around her, and Boaz stood before her in the bright open field.
