There was a man in Bethlehem whose name was Boaz. He was a man of great substance, a kinsman of Elimelech, who had been husband unto Naomi.

And Ruth, the woman of Moab, spake unto Naomi. "Let me go forth into the fields," she said, "and glean the ears of grain behind any in whose sight I may find favour."

And Naomi bade her go.

So Ruth went out beyond the borders of the town, where the barley fields lay broad and golden beneath the morning sun. She came unto a field and began to gather the loose stalks that the reapers had left behind them. She knew it not, but the field was the portion of Boaz.

The sun rose high. Ruth stooped and gathered, stooped and gathered, moving slowly after the workers. Dust clung upon her arms. The morning passed toward the heat of midday.

Then came Boaz from Bethlehem. He walked among the rows and called unto his reapers. "The Lord be with you," said he.

And they answered him with one voice. "The Lord bless thee."

And Boaz looked upon Ruth where she laboured among the stalks. He turned unto his servant, him that was set over the reapers. "Who is this young woman?" he asked.

The servant answered him. "She is the woman of Moab, who returned with Naomi. She sought leave to glean among the sheaves behind the reapers. She hath been here from the early morning, and hath scarcely rested at all."

Then Boaz went unto the place where Ruth was working. She rose upright when he spake.

"Hear me, my daughter," said he. "Go not unto another field. Depart not from this place, but keep close beside my young women. Mark where the reapers cut, and follow thou behind them. I have charged the young men that none shall trouble thee. And when thou art thirsty, go unto the vessels and drink of that which the servants have drawn."

Then Ruth fell to the ground. She bowed herself low, her face nigh unto the earth, her hands pressed flat upon the stubble.

"Why hast thou taken notice of me?" she said. "Wherefore shouldest thou show kindness unto me? I am but a stranger."

And Boaz stood over her. Behind him the reapers laboured on, and the light caught upon their blades.

"All hath been told unto me," said he. "What thou didst for thy mother-in-law after her husband perished. How thou didst leave thy father and thy mother and the land of thy birth. How thou camest unto a people whom thou hadst not known." He paused, and the wind moved through the barley. "May the Lord recompense thy labour. May a full reward be granted thee from the Lord, the God of Israel, beneath whose wings thou hast come to shelter."

Ruth lifted her face from the ground. Dust was upon her forehead and in the lines of her palms.

"Let me find favour in thy sight, my lord," she said. "Thou hast comforted me. Thou hast spoken gently unto thy handmaid, though I am not so much as one of thy maidservants."
