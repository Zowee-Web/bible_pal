Elijah the prophet had come unto the widow of Zarephath in the days when the famine was upon the land. The meal in her barrel had not wasted and the oil in her cruse had not failed, for the Lord had said by the word of his prophet that they should not fail. And the widow and her son and the prophet had dwelt together in her house. And after these things her son fell sick.

It came to pass after these things, that the son of the woman, the mistress of the house, fell sick; and his sickness was so sore, that there was no breath left in him.

And she said unto Elijah, "What have I to do with thee, O thou man of God? art thou come unto me to call my sin to remembrance, and to slay my son?"

And he said unto her, "Give me thy son." And he took him out of her bosom, and carried him up into a loft, where he abode, and laid him upon his own bed.

And he cried unto the Lord, and said, "O Lord my God, hast thou also brought evil upon the widow with whom I sojourn, by slaying her son?"

And he stretched himself upon the child three times, and cried unto the Lord, and said, "O Lord my God, I pray thee, let this child's soul come into him again."

And the Lord heard the voice of Elijah; and the soul of the child came into him again, and he revived.

And Elijah took the child, and brought him down out of the chamber into the house, and delivered him unto his mother; and Elijah said, "Behold, thy son liveth."

And the woman said unto Elijah, "Now by this I know that thou art a man of God, and that the word of the Lord in thy mouth is truth."

And the child was given back into his mother's arms. The upper chamber where the prophet had cried out stood empty above them; the widow held the child against her, and the prophet went down unto the door of the house; and the meal and the oil were yet enough for the days that would follow. And so it was in the days when Elijah sojourned with the widow of Zarephath of Zidon.
