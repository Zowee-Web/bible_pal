In those days, Herod the tetrarch heard the report concerning Jesus, and he said to his servants, "This is John the Baptizer. He is risen from the dead. That is why these powers work in him."

For Herod had laid hold of John, and bound him, and put him in prison for the sake of Herodias, his brother Philip's wife. For John said to him, "It is not lawful for you to have her." When he would have put him to death, he feared the multitude, because they counted him as a prophet.

But when Herod's birthday came, the daughter of Herodias danced among them and pleased Herod. He promised with an oath to give her whatever she should ask. She, being prompted by her mother, said, "Give me here on a platter the head of John the Baptizer."

The king was grieved, but for the sake of his oaths, and of those who sat at the table with him, he commanded it to be given. He sent and beheaded John in the prison.

His head was brought on a platter, and given to the young woman; and she brought it to her mother. The lamps were burning low along the walls of the banquet hall, and the music had stopped.

His disciples came and took the body and buried it. They went and told Jesus.

When Jesus heard it, he withdrew from there in a boat to a deserted place apart. The morning was gray over the lake, and the boat moved softly upon the water. Jesus sat in the boat and looked toward the shore that he had left.

When the multitudes heard it, they followed him on foot from the cities. The people pressed along the shore of the lake, and they came to him in the deserted place where he had gone.

The day grew warm above the water. The disciples gathered around Jesus, and the crowd waited along the bank. The voice of John, which had cried in the wilderness, was silent now.

The Jordan ran on as it had always run, and the land of Judea remained quiet under the sun.
