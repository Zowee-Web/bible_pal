James began with a plain charge: the brethren were not to hold the faith of the Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory, with respect of persons. He set before them an assembly entered by two men, one with a gold ring and goodly apparel, and the other in vile raiment. To give the finely clothed man a good place whilst bidding the poor man stand or sit beneath a footstool made them partial judges of evil thoughts. God had chosen the poor of this world rich in faith and heirs of the promised kingdom, yet they had despised the poor.

James directed them to the royal law, “Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.” To fulfil it was to do well; to have respect of persons was to commit sin and be convinced of the law as transgressors. Whosoever kept the whole law and yet offended in one point was guilty of all. He that said, “Do not commit adultery,” said also, “Do not kill.” James therefore charged them so to speak and so to do as they that should be judged by the law of liberty. Judgment would be without mercy to him that had shewed no mercy, but mercy rejoiceth against judgment.

James then asked what it profited if a man said he had faith but had not works. If a brother or sister lacked clothing and daily food, words bidding them depart in peace, be warmed, and be filled supplied none of those things needful to the body. Even so, faith without works was dead, being alone. Faith could not be shewn without works, but it could be shewn by works. The devils also believed that there is one God, and trembled.

Abraham was justified by works when he offered Isaac his son upon the altar. His faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect. The Scripture saying that Abraham believed God and it was imputed unto him for righteousness was fulfilled, and he was called the Friend of God. James concluded that by works a man is justified, and not by faith only. Likewise also, Rahab the harlot was justified by works when she received the messengers and sent them out another way. As the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead also.
