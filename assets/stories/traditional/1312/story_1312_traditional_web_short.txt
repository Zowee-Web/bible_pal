Twenty-five years had passed since Yahweh had called Abraham out of Haran. Twenty-five years since the promise had been spoken under the stars — that his descendants would be as the stars themselves for number. Sarah had grown old in the long waiting. Abraham had grown old. They had lived in the land as strangers, and the tent had stood through many seasons, and the promised son had not come.

Then the visitors had come to the tent at Mamre. They had spoken of a son at the appointed time. Sarah had laughed behind the tent door, for she was old and Abraham was old. And the visitor had asked, "Why did Sarah laugh? Is anything too hard for Yahweh?"

And the time came.

Yahweh visited Sarah as he had said, and Yahweh did to Sarah as he had spoken.

Sarah conceived, and bore Abraham a son in his old age, at the set time of which God had spoken to him.

Abraham called his son who was born to him, whom Sarah bore to him, Isaac.

Abraham circumcised his son, Isaac, when he was eight days old, as God had commanded him.

Abraham was one hundred years old when his son, Isaac, was born to him.

Sarah said, "God has made me laugh. Everyone who hears will laugh with me."

She said, "Who would have said to Abraham, that Sarah should nurse children? For I have borne him a son in his old age."

And Isaac grew at his mother's breast. The tent at Mamre was filled with the sound of his small voice. The fires were lit in the evening, and the lamps in the night, and the boy slept beside Sarah and beside Abraham. And so it was at the tent of the promise, in the days after Isaac was born.
