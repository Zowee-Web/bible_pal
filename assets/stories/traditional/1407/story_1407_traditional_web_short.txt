Manasseh king of Judah had reigned fifty-five years. He had set up the idols of Baal and made an Asherah, as Ahab had done before him in Israel; he had built altars to the host of heaven in the courts of Yahweh's house; he had made his sons to pass through fire in the valley of Hinnom; and Yahweh had sent prophets to him and to the people, but they had not listened. And then Yahweh did to Manasseh what no king of Judah had ever experienced.

Yahweh spoke to Manasseh, and to his people; but they didn't listen.

Therefore Yahweh brought on them the captains of the army of the king of Assyria, who took Manasseh in chains, bound him with fetters, and carried him to Babylon.

When he was in distress, he begged Yahweh his God, and humbled himself greatly before the God of his fathers.

He prayed to him; and he was entreated by him, and heard his supplication, and brought him again to Jerusalem into his kingdom. Then Manasseh knew that Yahweh was God.

And Manasseh returned to Jerusalem an old king with the chains of Assyria gone from his wrists. The high places he had built he tore down; the altars in the courts of the temple he took away; the foreign gods he had set among the people he removed; and the king who had been carried away in hooks to Babylon walked the courts of the temple again, knowing the Lord who had heard him.
