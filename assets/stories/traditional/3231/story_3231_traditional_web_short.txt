News reached Saul that David and his men had been found. Saul sat at Gibeah beneath the tamarisk tree in Ramah, spear in hand, with his servants around him.

He challenged the Benjamites: would Jesse’s son give them fields and vineyards, or make them commanders of thousands and hundreds? He accused them all of conspiring against him. No one had disclosed his son’s treaty with David, he said; no one pitied him or reported that his son had stirred David to lie in wait against him.

Doeg the Edomite spoke from among the servants. He reported seeing David at Nob with Ahimelech, Ahitub’s son. According to Doeg, the priest had inquired of Yahweh for David, fed him, and given him Goliath the Philistine’s sword.

Saul summoned Ahimelech and his father’s entire household, the priests at Nob. They all came. When Saul called him the son of Ahitub, Ahimelech answered, “Here I am, my lord.”

Saul accused the priest and David of conspiracy. Giving David bread and a sword, and inquiring of God for him, were, Saul claimed, help for an ambush against the king.

Ahimelech answered with David’s faithfulness. David was Saul’s son-in-law, captain of his bodyguard, and honored in his house. “Have I today begun to inquire of God for him? Be it far from me!” He begged Saul to charge neither him nor his father’s household, for he knew nothing of the matter, great or small.

Saul pronounced death upon Ahimelech and his father’s house. He ordered his guards to kill Yahweh’s priests, accusing them of siding with David and concealing his flight.

The guards would not attack the priests.

Then Saul gave the order to Doeg. That day Doeg killed eighty-five people wearing linen ephods. He also put Nob, the priests’ city, to the sword: men and women, children and nursing babies, cattle, donkeys, and sheep.

Abiathar, a son of Ahimelech the son of Ahitub, escaped. He fled to David and told him that Saul had killed Yahweh’s priests.

David said that when he had seen Doeg there that day, he knew Doeg would tell Saul. He accepted responsibility for the deaths of Abiathar’s father’s household.

“Stay with me. Don’t be afraid,” David told him. The one seeking David’s life also sought Abiathar’s. David promised, “You will be safe with me.”
