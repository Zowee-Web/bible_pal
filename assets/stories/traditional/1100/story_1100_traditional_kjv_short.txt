And the word of the Lord came unto Jonah the second time, saying, "Arise, go unto Nineveh, that great city, and preach unto it the preaching that I bid thee."

This time, Jonah went.

He arose and went unto Nineveh, according to the word of the Lord. The journey was long — weeks of walking through open country, the road dusty, the days hot. But he walked on, one foot before the other, going toward the city from which he had once fled.

Now Nineveh was an exceeding great city of three days' journey. The walls were high and thick. The streets were crowded with people — merchants, soldiers, families, beggars. The noise of the city pressed in from every side.

Jonah entered into the city a day's journey. Then he stopped and cried aloud:

"Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!"

And the people of Nineveh believed God.

Word spread from street to street, from house to house, from the markets to the palace. The people proclaimed a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them even to the least.

When the tidings reached the king of Nineveh, he arose from his throne and laid his robe from him, and covered himself with sackcloth, and sat in ashes. He caused it to be proclaimed through Nineveh: "Let neither man nor beast, herd nor flock, taste any thing: let them not feed, nor drink water. Let man and beast be covered with sackcloth, and cry mightily unto God: yea, let them turn every one from his evil way, and from the violence that is in their hands. Who can tell if God will turn and repent, and turn away from his fierce anger, that we perish not?"

And God saw their works, that they turned from their evil way.

And God repented of the evil, that he had said that he would do unto them; and he did it not.
