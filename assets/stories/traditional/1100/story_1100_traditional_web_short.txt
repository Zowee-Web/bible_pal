The word of the Lord came to Jonah a second time. "Arise," God said. "Go to Nineveh, that great city, and proclaim to it the message that I tell you."

This time, Jonah went.

He rose and traveled east. The journey was long — weeks of walking through open country, across rivers, through towns he did not know. The road was dusty and the days were hot. But he kept walking, one foot in front of the other, moving toward the city he had once fled from.

Nineveh was enormous. It took three days to walk across it. The walls were high and thick. The streets were crowded with people — merchants, soldiers, families, beggars. The noise of the city pressed in from every side. It was nothing like the quiet hills of Israel.

Jonah entered the city. He walked for one day into the heart of it, past market stalls and temple gates and houses stacked close together. Then he stopped and began to speak.

"In forty days," he cried out, "Nineveh will be overthrown!"

That was the message. Short. Blunt. Terrifying.

And the people of Nineveh believed God.

It started small — a few people in the street who heard him and stopped walking. Then more. Word spread from block to block, from house to house, from the markets to the palace. The message moved through the city like fire through dry grass.

The people proclaimed a fast. Everyone — from the greatest to the least — put on sackcloth, the rough garment of mourning and repentance. They sat in ashes. They stopped eating. They stopped their business. The city, which had been loud and busy, grew quiet.

When the word reached the king of Nineveh, he rose from his throne. He took off his royal robes and put on sackcloth himself. He sat down in ashes. Then he issued a decree throughout the city: "Let neither man nor beast, herd nor flock, taste anything. Let them not eat or drink. Let everyone cry out mightily to God. Let everyone turn from his evil way and from the violence that is in his hands. Who knows? God may turn and relent and turn from his fierce anger, so that we may not perish."

God saw what they did — how they turned from their evil ways.

And God relented. He did not bring upon them the destruction he had threatened.
