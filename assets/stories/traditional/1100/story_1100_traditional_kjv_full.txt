And the word of the Lord came unto Jonah the second time. The same God. The same command. The same city.

"Arise, go unto Nineveh, that great city, and preach unto it the preaching that I bid thee."

Jonah had fled this call before. He had taken ship to Tarshish, been cast into the sea, and passed three days in the belly of a great fish. He had prayed from the darkness and been vomited upon dry land. And now the word came again, unchanged.

This time, Jonah obeyed.

He arose and went unto Nineveh, according to the word of the Lord. The journey took many weeks — through open country, across rivers, through towns where none knew his name or his God. The road was long and dusty. The days were hot. But Jonah kept walking, one foot before the other.

Now Nineveh was an exceeding great city of three days' journey. The walls were high and thick, built of brick and stone. Within the walls, the streets were crowded — merchants, soldiers, families, slaves. It was the capital of Assyria, and its scale was past all reckoning.

Jonah entered into the city and went a day's journey. He passed market squares and temple gates and houses close together along the narrow streets. He sought no permission. He gave no introduction.

He stopped and cried aloud: "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!"

That was the message. No theology. No argument. A deadline and a judgment.

And the people of Nineveh believed God.

It began with the few that heard him. Word spread from street to street, house to house, market to palace. The people proclaimed a fast. From the greatest to the least, they put on sackcloth. They sat in ashes. They ceased eating, ceased drinking, ceased working. The busy, roaring city fell still.

When the word reached the king, he arose from his throne. He laid his robe from him, covered himself with sackcloth, and sat in ashes. He proclaimed throughout the city: "Let neither man nor beast, herd nor flock, taste any thing. Let man and beast be covered with sackcloth, and cry mightily unto God: yea, let them turn every one from his evil way, and from the violence that is in their hands. Who can tell if God will turn and repent, and turn away from his fierce anger, that we perish not?"

The city obeyed. They turned from their evil.

And God saw their works, that they turned from their evil way; and God repented of the evil, that he had said that he would do unto them; and he did it not.
