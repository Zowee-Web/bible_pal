When David had finished speaking to Saul, the soul of Jonathan was knit to the soul of David. Jonathan loved him as his own soul.

David still stood before the king. His tunic was stained with grass and the sweat of the valley. The sling hung loose from his belt. Dust covered his sandals and the skin of his ankles.

Saul took David that day and would not let him return to his father's house. David entered the king's service. He stood where soldiers stood. He ate where the court ate.

And Jonathan made a covenant with David, because he loved him as his own soul.

Then Jonathan reached up and unfastened the robe he was wearing. The bronze clasp at his shoulder clicked open. He drew the purple cloth from his shoulders and held it out. David took it. The fabric was heavier than anything he had worn. He held it against his chest.

Jonathan unbuckled his armor. He loosened the leather breastpiece from his chest, unfastened the bronze guard at his side, lifted the pieces free. The straps left pale marks on his tunic where they had pressed. He set the armor in David's arms on top of the robe.

He drew his sword from the scabbard at his hip. The leather sheath was worn smooth. He laid the blade across David's open palms. The grip was polished from his hand.

He unstrung his bow and held it out — dark curved wood, slackened string. David took it and balanced it across the pile growing in his arms.

He unbuckled his belt. The wide leather strap that held sword and quiver. The buckle was warm. He pulled it free and placed it on top of the rest.

Jonathan stood in the court of his father's palace. His plain linen tunic hung loose without the belt. His arms were bare where the armor had been. David stood across from him, arms full — robe, armor, sword, bow, belt. The bronze clasp of the robe caught the light from the high windows. Neither of them spoke.
