And it came to pass, when David had made an end of speaking unto Saul, that the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul.

And Saul took him that day, and would let him go no more home to his father's house. David entered into the king's service.

And Jonathan and David made a covenant, because he loved him as his own soul.

Then Jonathan stripped himself of the robe that was upon him and gave it to David. The purple cloth passed from his hands into the hands of the shepherd lad who yet wore the dust of the valley upon his sandals.

He gave him his armour. He loosed the breastpiece of leather from his chest, unfastened the guard of bronze at his side, and set the pieces in David's arms. The leather was dark with years of wearing.

He gave him his sword. He drew the blade from the sheath upon his hip and laid it across David's open palms. The grip was worn smooth.

He gave him his bow. He unstrung it and held it forth — the curved wood, the slackened string, the weapon he had borne since he was a lad.

He gave him his girdle. The wide strap of leather which held sword and quiver. He unbuckled it, drew it free, and laid it upon the rest.

Jonathan stood in the court of his father's palace. David stood before him, his arms full — robe, armour, sword, bow, girdle. The bronze clasp of the robe caught the light from the high windows. Neither of them spake.
