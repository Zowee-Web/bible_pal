Gideon and the people that were with him rose up early. They came down through the slope of the hill and pitched beside the well of Harod. The water ran clear over stones at their feet. Across the valley, on the north side, the host of Midian spread wide along the hill of Moreh. Tents stretched into the distance. Camels grazed in the open ground. The smoke of cooking fires drifted up by the hundreds, thin and steady, into the morning air.

Gideon stood at the well and looked across.

Then came the word of the LORD unto him. "The people that are with thee are too many for me to give the Midianites into their hand, lest Israel vaunt themselves against me, saying, 'Mine own hand hath saved me.' Now therefore proclaim in the ears of the people, saying, 'Whosoever is fearful and afraid, let him return and depart early from mount Gilead.'"

Gideon turned and gave the word.

The proclamation moved through the camp. Men looked one upon another. Some looked down upon the ground. The valley below was full of Midian. Slowly, then in companies, men began to take up their packs. They set down their spears and lifted their packs upon their shoulders. They walked back the way they had come. The line of them stretched along the hillside, moving away. When the last of them was gone, the well of Harod was very still.

Two and twenty thousand had returned. Ten thousand remained.

And the LORD said unto Gideon, "The people are yet too many. Bring them down unto the water, and I will try them for thee there."

Gideon brought the people down unto the water.

And the LORD said unto him, "Every one that lappeth of the water with his tongue, as a dog lappeth, him shalt thou set by himself; likewise every one that boweth down upon his knees to drink."

Gideon watched. The men came unto the water in companies. Most of them bowed down. They put their faces down to the surface and drank long. A few did not bow. They put their hand to their mouth and lapped it standing, their eyes lifting now and again unto the valley across the way. Gideon counted these. Three hundred men.

And the LORD said unto Gideon, "By the three hundred men that lapped will I save thee, and deliver the Midianites into thine hand. Let all the rest of the people go every man unto his place."

The rest were sent away. They took up their victuals and their trumpets, and they went, and the three hundred remained at the well.

The camp of Midian lay beneath them still. From the high ground Gideon looked down upon it. The Midianites and the Amalekites and all the children of the east lay along in the valley like grasshoppers for multitude. Their camels were without number, as the sand by the sea side for multitude. A wind moved through their tents. Their watchfires marked the floor of the valley in many small flickering points.

That same night, the word of the LORD came unto Gideon again. "Arise, get thee down unto the host, for I have delivered it into thine hand. But if thou fear to go down, go thou with Phurah thy servant down unto the host. Thou shalt hear what they say; and afterward shall thine hands be strengthened to go down unto the host."

Gideon called Phurah. They went down together.

They moved slow through the dark. They left the well behind them. They left the watchfires of the three hundred behind them. The slope was loose under their feet. They went upon hands and knees over the rough places, and then upon foot again, low to the ground, until they came unto the outside of the armed men that were in the host.

A fire burned low between two tents at the edge. Two men sat near unto it. Gideon and Phurah lay flat against the slope and held still.

One of the men was speaking. He had a low, troubled voice. "Behold," said he, "I dreamed a dream. A cake of barley bread tumbled into the host of Midian. It came unto a tent, and smote it that it fell, and turned it upside down, that the tent lay along."

His fellow answered not at once. The fire snapped. Somewhere in the host a camel groaned and resettled. Then the second man answered, and his voice was lower than the first.

"This is nothing else save the sword of Gideon the son of Joash, a man of Israel. For into his hand hath God delivered Midian, and all the host."

Gideon lay in the dark with his face against the earth. He had heard it. So had Phurah beside him.

When Gideon heard the telling of the dream and the interpretation thereof, he worshipped. He bowed his head where he lay upon the slope and gave thanks unto the LORD. Then he and Phurah began the long crawl back up the slope, and when they were clear of the host they rose upon their feet and ran.

He came again unto the host of Israel. He gathered the three hundred. He said, "Arise, for the LORD hath delivered the host of Midian into your hand."

He divided them into three companies. Into the hand of every man he put a trumpet, an empty pitcher, and within the pitcher a lamp. He said, "Look on me, and do likewise. Behold, when I come to the outside of the camp, it shall be that, as I do, so shall ye do. When I blow with a trumpet, I and all that are with me, then blow ye the trumpets also on every side of all the camp, and say, 'The sword of the LORD, and of Gideon!'"

They went down by night, the three companies, round about the four sides of the valley.

The middle watch had but newly been set when Gideon and his hundred came unto the outside of the Midianite camp. The other two companies came in from the far sides. The watchmen had but newly taken their posts. The fires had burned low. Somewhere a camel shifted in the dark. The valley was full of sleeping men.

Gideon lifted his trumpet unto his mouth. He blew. He brake the pitcher in his hand against the ground, and it brake asunder. The lamp within it flared up into the night. All round about the camp, three hundred pitchers brake at once. Three hundred lamps flared. Three hundred trumpets sounded along the four sides of the valley.

The three hundred stood every man in his place, and they cried, "The sword of the LORD, and of Gideon!"

The Midianites awoke into fire and trumpets and shouting on every side. They came up out of their tents already running. They could not see who held the lamps. They could not see how many. The lamps stood still in their places along the rim of the valley, and the trumpets did not cease, and the cry went on through the dark.

The host turned upon itself. In the confusion of the night the Midianites could not tell brother from enemy. The LORD set every man's sword against his fellow throughout all the host. The cry rose up out of the valley, a cry of running men and of fear. The host of Midian brake and streamed away into the dark. They fled to Bethshittah in Zererath, and to the border of Abelmeholah, unto Tabbath.

And on the slope above the valley, the three hundred stood every man in his place, the lamps still burning in their hands, the trumpets at their mouths, and the morning began to come on.
