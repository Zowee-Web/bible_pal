Gideon and the people who were with him rose up early. They came down through the slope of the hill and camped beside the spring of Harod. The water ran clear over stones at their feet. Across the valley, on the north side, the camp of Midian spread wide along the hill of Moreh. Tents stretched into the distance. Camels grazed in the open ground. The smoke of cooking fires drifted up by the hundreds, thin and steady, into the morning air.

Gideon stood at the spring and looked across.

Then the word of the Lord came to him. "The people who are with you are too many for me to give the Midianites into their hand, lest Israel brag against me, saying, 'My own hand has saved me.' Now therefore proclaim in the ears of the people, saying, 'Whoever is fearful and trembling, let him return and depart from Mount Gilead.'"

Gideon turned and gave the word.

The proclamation moved through the camp. Men looked at one another. Some looked down at the ground. The valley below was full of Midian. Slowly, then in groups, men began to take up their packs. They set down their spears and lifted their packs onto their shoulders. They walked back the way they had come. The line of them stretched along the hillside, moving away. When the last of them was gone, the spring of Harod was very quiet.

Twenty-two thousand had returned. Ten thousand remained.

The Lord said to Gideon, "The people are still too many. Bring them down to the water, and I will test them for you there."

Gideon brought the people down to the water.

The Lord said to him, "Everyone who laps of the water with his tongue, like a dog laps, you shall set him by himself; likewise everyone who bows down on his knees to drink."

Gideon watched. The men came to the water in groups. Most of them knelt. They put their faces down to the surface and drank long. A few did not kneel. They scooped water with their hand to their mouth and lapped it standing, eyes lifting now and again to the valley across the way. Gideon counted these. Three hundred men.

The Lord said to Gideon, "I will save you by the three hundred men who lapped, and deliver the Midianites into your hand. Let all the rest of the people go, every man to his place."

The rest were sent away. They took up their food and their trumpets, and they went, and the three hundred remained at the spring.

The camp of Midian was beneath them still. From the high ground Gideon looked down on it. The Midianites and the Amalekites and all the children of the east lay along in the valley like locusts for multitude. Their camels were without number, as the sand which is on the seashore. A wind moved through their tents. Their watchfires marked the floor of the valley in many small flickering points.

That same night, the word of the Lord came to Gideon again. "Arise, go down into the camp, for I have delivered it into your hand. But if you are afraid to go down, go with Purah your servant down to the camp. You will hear what they say; and afterward your hands will be strengthened to go down into the camp."

Gideon called Purah. They went down together.

They moved slow through the dark. They left the spring behind them. They left the watchfires of the three hundred behind them. The slope was loose under their feet. They went on hands and knees over the rough places, and then on foot again, low to the ground, until they came to the outermost part of the armed men of the camp.

A fire burned low between two tents at the edge. Two men sat near it. Gideon and Purah lay flat against the slope and held still.

One of the men was speaking. He had a low, troubled voice. "Behold," he said, "I dreamed a dream. A cake of barley bread tumbled into the camp of Midian. It came to the tent, and struck it so that it fell, and turned it upside down, so that the tent lay flat."

His fellow did not answer at once. The fire snapped. Somewhere in the camp a camel groaned and resettled. Then the second man answered, and his voice was lower than the first.

"This is nothing other than the sword of Gideon the son of Joash, a man of Israel. God has delivered Midian into his hand, with all the army."

Gideon lay in the dark with his face against the earth. He had heard it. So had Purah beside him.

When Gideon heard the telling of the dream and its interpretation, he worshiped. He bowed his head where he lay on the slope and gave thanks to the Lord. Then he and Purah began the long crawl back up the slope, and when they were clear of the camp they rose to their feet and ran.

He came again to the camp of Israel. He gathered the three hundred. He said, "Arise, for the Lord has delivered the army of Midian into your hand."

He divided them into three companies. Into the hands of each man he placed a trumpet, an empty pitcher, and inside the pitcher a torch. He said, "Watch me, and do likewise. When I come to the outermost part of the camp, as I do, so you shall do. When I blow the trumpet, I and all who are with me, then blow the trumpets also on every side of all the camp, and shout, 'For the Lord and for Gideon!'"

They went down by night, the three companies, around the four sides of the valley.

The middle watch had only just been set when Gideon and his hundred reached the outer edge of the Midianite camp. The other two companies came in from the far sides. The watchmen had only newly taken their posts. The fires had burned low. Somewhere a camel shifted in the dark. The valley was full of sleeping men.

Gideon lifted his trumpet to his mouth. He blew. He brought down the pitcher in his hand against the ground, and it broke. The torch inside it flared up into the night. All around the camp, three hundred pitchers broke at once. Three hundred torches flared. Three hundred trumpets sounded along the four sides of the valley.

The three hundred stood each in his place, and they cried out, "The sword of the Lord and of Gideon!"

The Midianites awoke into fire and trumpets and shouting on every side. They came up from their tents already running. They could not see who held the torches. They could not see how many. The torches stood still in their places along the rim of the valley, and the trumpets did not stop, and the cry went on through the dark.

The host turned on itself. In the confusion of the night the Midianites could not tell brother from enemy. The Lord set every man's sword against his fellow throughout all the army. The cry rose up out of the valley, a cry of running men and of fear. The host of Midian broke and streamed away into the dark. They fled as far as Beth Shittah, toward Zererah, as far as the border of Abel Meholah, by Tabbath.

And on the slope above the valley, the three hundred stood each in his place, the torches still burning in their hands, the trumpets at their mouths, and the morning began to come on.
