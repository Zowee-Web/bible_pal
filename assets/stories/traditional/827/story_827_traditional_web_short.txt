Jesus was traveling with his disciples when he entered a village. A woman named Martha welcomed him into her house. The day was warm, and the scent of freshly baked bread moved through the air. The home was full of quiet movement and the promise of a meal.

Martha moved quickly through her kitchen, arms full, intent on preparing the best for her guest. The clatter of dishes sounded as she shaped loaves and tended pots. She wanted everything to be just right for Jesus.

Her sister Mary was also there. While Martha bustled from the hearth to the table, Mary found a place near Jesus. She sat down at his feet, listening with care to every word he spoke. The room, filled with gentle voices and the aroma of bread, grew still around them. Mary listened, her eyes fixed on Jesus.

Martha watched the scene as she hurried by, the work of serving pressing on her. She grew distracted by her many preparations. As the tasks piled up, her concern deepened. She saw Mary, seated and attentive, while she herself was left to manage alone.

Finally, Martha came to Jesus. Concern and weariness edged her voice. “Lord, don’t you care that my sister left me to serve alone? Tell her to help me.”

Jesus looked at Martha. His voice was gentle and kind. “Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things.”

The room grew quiet as his words settled over them. The warmth of the kitchen, the lingering scent of the meal, paused in that moment.

He continued, “But one thing is needed. Mary has chosen the good part, which will not be taken away from her.”
