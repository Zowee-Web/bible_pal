Jesus entered a village. A woman named Martha welcomed him into her home. The air inside was warm and filled with the aromas of baking bread and simmering food. Martha moved quickly, her steps sure on the worn floor. She worked to prepare the meal, hands busy with dishes and ingredients, the gentle clatter of pottery echoing in the house.

Mary, her sister, did not help in the kitchen. Instead, she found her place at Jesus’s feet in the quiet room, sitting close to listen. She did not move, focused only on the words that Jesus spoke. His voice was gentle, filling the room with calm. Martha’s footsteps and the hum of her work sounded from the kitchen.

Martha glanced at Mary from the doorway. The bread’s scent mingled with the quiet, the stillness of Mary’s posture different from Martha’s steady pace. Pots shifted. Spoons tapped against bowls. The preparations weighed on Martha’s mind and arms.

At last, Martha left her work and approached Jesus. There was a touch of strain in her words. “Lord, do you not care that my sister has left me to serve alone? Ask her to help me.”

The room grew quiet. The sound of clattering dishes faded away. Jesus turned his gaze to Martha, his eyes full of kindness. His words were gentle, every syllable steady.

“Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things,” he said.

The quiet room seemed to grow warmer, the air full of the peace that lingered around Jesus. Martha stood silent, listening.

Jesus continued, “But one thing is needed. Mary has chosen the good part, which will not be taken away from her.”

Mary stayed seated, her gaze steady and her hands calm in her lap. The hush in the room deepened as Jesus’s words hung there. Martha’s breathing slowed, her hands resting by her sides. The scent of the meal drifted quietly through the house, yet neither sister spoke. Jesus’s eyes remained gentle, his presence filling the home with a quiet assurance. No footsteps moved, no pots rattled, and the sunlight lay still along the floor. Mary’s focus did not waver, her heart settled on each word Jesus had spoken. The small room felt set apart, given over to the moment’s peace and the gift Mary had chosen. Jesus’s voice, soft yet sure, promised, “Mary has chosen the good part, which will not be taken away from her.”
