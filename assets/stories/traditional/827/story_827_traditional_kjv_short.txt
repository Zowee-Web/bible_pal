Jesus was journeying with his disciples when he entered into a certain village. A certain woman named Martha received him into her house. The day was warm, and the savour of fresh-baken bread moved through the air. The house was full of quiet movement and the promise of a meal.

Martha moved swiftly through her kitchen, her arms full, intent upon preparing the best for her guest. The clatter of dishes sounded as she shaped the loaves and tended the pots. She would have everything be just so for Jesus.

And she had a sister called Mary, which was also there. While Martha bustled from the hearth unto the table, Mary found a place near unto Jesus. She sat down at his feet, and heard his word, hearkening with care unto every word he spake. The room, filled with gentle voices and the savour of bread, grew still round about them. Mary listened, her eyes fixed upon Jesus.

Martha beheld the scene as she hastened by, the work of serving pressing upon her. But Martha was cumbered about much serving. As the tasks were heaped one upon another, her concern deepened. She saw Mary, seated and attentive, while she herself was left to manage alone.

At length, Martha came to him, and said, "Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone? bid her therefore that she help me."

Jesus looked upon Martha. His voice was gentle and kind. "Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things."

The room grew quiet as his words settled over them. The warmth of the kitchen, the lingering savour of the meal, paused in that moment.

And he continued, "But one thing is needful: and Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her."
