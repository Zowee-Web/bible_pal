Hezekiah sent to all Israel and Judah, with letters also to Ephraim and Manasseh, that they should come to the house of the Lord at Jerusalem, to keep the passover unto the Lord God of Israel. The king, his princes, and all the congregation took counsel to keep it in the second month, for the priests had not sanctified themselves sufficiently, and the people had not gathered. The decree went out from Beersheba even to Dan.

So the posts went with the letters throughout all Israel and Judah. “Ye children of Israel, turn again unto the Lord God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel,” it said, “and he will return to the remnant of you, that are escaped out of the hand of the kings of Assyria.” The letters urged them not to be stiffnecked as their fathers were, but to yield themselves unto the Lord, who is gracious and merciful, and will not turn away his face from them that return unto him.

Through Ephraim and Manasseh, even unto Zebulun, the people laughed the posts to scorn and mocked them. Nevertheless divers of Asher and Manasseh and of Zebulun humbled themselves and came to Jerusalem. In Judah the hand of God gave the people one heart to do the commandment of the king and the princes by the word of the Lord.

A very great congregation assembled to keep the feast of unleavened bread in the second month. They took away the altars that were in Jerusalem, with the altars for incense, and cast them into the brook Kidron. They killed the passover on the fourteenth day of the second month, and the priests and the Levites, ashamed, sanctified themselves and brought burnt offerings into the house of the Lord.

Many of the people had not cleansed themselves, yet did they eat the passover otherwise than it was written. For them Hezekiah prayed, “The good Lord pardon every one that prepareth his heart to seek God, the Lord God of his fathers, though he be not cleansed according to the purification of the sanctuary.” The Lord hearkened to Hezekiah, and healed the people.

Seven days the children of Israel kept the feast with great gladness, and the Levites and the priests praised the Lord day by day with loud instruments. Hezekiah spake comfortably unto the Levites that taught the good knowledge of the Lord. Then the whole assembly took counsel to keep other seven days, and kept them with gladness; Hezekiah gave a thousand bullocks and seven thousand sheep, and the princes a thousand bullocks and ten thousand sheep.

All the congregation of Judah rejoiced, with the priests and the Levites, all that came out of Israel, and the strangers that dwelt in Judah. There was great joy in Jerusalem; since the time of Solomon there was not the like in Jerusalem. Then the priests the Levites arose and blessed the people, and their voice was heard; their prayer came up to his holy dwelling place, even unto heaven.
