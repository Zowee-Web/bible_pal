The king could not sleep that night. He had been told all things by Esther so far, but the matter had not yet been brought to the second banquet. He turned in his bed for a long time. He commanded the book of records of the chronicles to be brought. He thought he might be made to sleep by the reading.

They brought the book. They read to him in the night. The reader's voice was steady. The lamps burned in the chamber.

They came to the part where it was written about Mordecai. Mordecai had reported a plot against the king. Two of the chamberlains had sought to lay hands on the king. Mordecai had heard of it and made it known. The men had been hanged on a tree.

The king sat up. He asked: what honor and dignity has been done to Mordecai for this?

His servants answered: there is nothing done for him.

The king said: who is in the court?

Haman had just come into the outer court of the king's house. He had come early to ask the king to hang Mordecai on the gallows he had built for him in the night. The gallows were already standing in his own courtyard, fifty cubits high. He had not yet spoken to the king when the king sent for him.

The king said: let him come in.

Haman came in. The king said to him: what shall be done unto the man whom the king delights to honor?

Haman thought in his heart: to whom would the king delight to do honor more than to me?

He said: let royal apparel be brought, the apparel the king has worn. Let the horse be brought, the horse the king has ridden, with the crown royal on its head. Let the apparel and the horse be delivered to one of the king's most noble princes. Let him array the man the king delights to honor. Let him cause the man to ride through the streets of the city. Let him proclaim before him: thus shall it be done to the man whom the king delights to honor.

The king said to Haman: make haste. Take the apparel and the horse as you have said. Do so to Mordecai the Jew that sits at the king's gate. Let nothing fail of all that you have spoken.

Haman went out. He took the apparel and the horse. The crown royal was set on the horse's head. He came to Mordecai at the king's gate. Mordecai was sitting there in sackcloth, as he had been for many days. Haman arrayed Mordecai in the king's apparel with his own hands. He set him on the horse the king had ridden. He led the horse himself through the streets of the city. The streets were full of people in the morning. The people of Shushan watched the procession go by. Haman cried before Mordecai as he walked: thus shall it be done unto the man whom the king delights to honor.
