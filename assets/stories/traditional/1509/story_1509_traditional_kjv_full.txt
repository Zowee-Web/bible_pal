The king could not sleep that night. He had been told all things by Esther so far, but the matter had not yet been brought to the second banquet. He turned in his bed a long while. He commanded to bring the book of records of the chronicles. He thought he might be made to sleep by the reading.

They brought the book. They were read before the king in the night. The reader's voice was steady. The lamps burned in the chamber.

They came to the part where it was written concerning Mordecai. Mordecai had told of the plot against the king. Two of the chamberlains had sought to lay hand on the king. Mordecai had heard of it and shewed it. The men had been hanged on a tree.

The king sat up. He said: what honour and dignity hath been done to Mordecai for this?

His servants that ministered unto him said: there is nothing done for him.

The king said: who is in the court?

Haman was come into the outward court. He was come early to speak unto the king to hang Mordecai on the gallows that he had prepared for him in the night. The gallows already stood in his own courtyard, fifty cubits high.

The king said: let him come in.

Haman came in. The king said unto him: what shall be done unto the man whom the king delighteth to honour?

Haman thought in his heart: to whom would the king delight to do honour more than to myself?

He said: let the royal apparel be brought, which the king useth to wear. Let the horse be brought, the horse that the king rideth upon, and the crown royal which is set upon his head. Let the apparel and horse be delivered to the hand of one of the king's most noble princes. Let them array the man withal whom the king delighteth to honour. Let them bring him on horseback through the street of the city. Let them proclaim before him: thus shall it be done to the man whom the king delighteth to honour.

The king said to Haman: make haste. Take the apparel and the horse, as thou hast said. Do even so to Mordecai the Jew that sitteth at the king's gate. Let nothing fail of all that thou hast spoken.

Haman went out. He took the apparel and the horse. The crown royal was set upon the horse's head. He came unto Mordecai at the king's gate. Mordecai sat there in sackcloth, as he had sat for many days. Haman arrayed Mordecai in the king's apparel with his own hands. He set him on the horse the king had ridden. He led the horse himself through the street of the city. The streets were full of people in the morning. The people of Shushan watched the procession go by. Haman proclaimed before Mordecai as he walked: thus shall it be done unto the man whom the king delighteth to honour.
