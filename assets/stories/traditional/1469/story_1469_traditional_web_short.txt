Nebuchadnezzar was full of fury.

The form of his appearance was changed against Shadrach, and Meshach, and Abednego. He spoke. He commanded.

"Heat the furnace," he said. "Heat it seven times more than it is usually heated."

The fire was made stronger. The men of his army were called.

The king commanded the strong men of his army to bind the three. They bound Shadrach. They bound Meshach. They bound Abednego. They left them in their pants, and their tunics, and their mantles, and the other garments they had worn into the king's hall.

They lifted the three men, bound, to take them to the mouth of the furnace.

The king's commandment was urgent. The furnace was exceedingly hot.

The flame of the fire killed the men who had taken up Shadrach, and Meshach, and Abednego at the mouth of the furnace.

The three fell down bound into the middle of the burning fiery furnace.

Then Nebuchadnezzar the king was astonished.

The fury was gone from him. He rose up in haste from his place. He came near to the mouth of the furnace.

He spoke to his counselors.

"Did we not cast three men bound into the middle of the fire?"

"True, O king," they answered him.

The king looked again into the furnace.

He said, "Look. I see four men loose. They are walking in the middle of the fire, and they are unharmed.

The appearance of the fourth is like a son of the gods."
