Nebuchadnezzar was full of fury.

The form of his visage was changed against Shadrach, and Meshach, and Abednego. He spake. He commanded.

"Heat the furnace," he said. "Heat it seven times more than it is wont to be heated."

The fire was made stronger. The mighty men of his army were called.

The king commanded the most mighty men of his army to bind the three. They bound Shadrach. They bound Meshach. They bound Abednego. They left them in their coats, and their hosen, and their hats, and the other garments which they had worn into the king's hall.

They lifted the three men, bound, to bear them unto the mouth of the furnace.

The king's commandment was urgent. The furnace was exceeding hot.

The flame of the fire slew the men that took up Shadrach, and Meshach, and Abednego at the mouth of the furnace.

The three fell down bound into the midst of the burning fiery furnace.

Then Nebuchadnezzar the king was astonied.

The fury was gone from him. He rose up in haste from his place. He drew nigh unto the mouth of the furnace.

He spake unto his counsellors.

"Did not we cast three men bound into the midst of the fire?"

"True, O king," they answered him.

The king looked again into the furnace.

He said, "Lo, I see four men loose. They are walking in the midst of the fire, and they have no hurt.

The form of the fourth is like the Son of God."
