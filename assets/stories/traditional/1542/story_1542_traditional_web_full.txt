David sat on a flat stone outside the cave at Adullam. The desert stretched below him, brown and dry, the hills folding into one another under a white sky. Dust settled on everything — on the rocks, on his cloak, on the wooden frame of the harp that rested across his knees. His men were scattered in small groups among the stones, sharpening blades, mending sandals, saying little. The cave mouth behind him was dark and cool, and the air that came out of it smelled of earth.

He set his fingers to the strings. The first sound was quiet, almost lost in the wind that came along the ridge. But the men nearest him looked up. One stopped what he was doing. Another turned his head.

David began to sing.

"I will bless the LORD at all times. His praise will always be in my mouth."

His voice was steady and unhurried. It was the voice of a man who had decided something and was now saying it plainly. His fingers moved across the strings in a slow pattern, each note landing clean in the open air.

"My soul will boast in the LORD. The humble will hear it and be glad."

A few of the men set down what they held. They came closer, feet scraping on the dry ground, and sat. Some crossed their legs beneath them. Others leaned against the stones. Their faces were tired. Their clothes were worn thin in places. But they were listening.

David looked out over the hills as he played. The light was warm and low, casting long shadows behind the rocks.

"Magnify the LORD with me," he sang. "Let us lift up his name together."

One of the men near the front repeated the words quietly, his lips moving just behind David's voice. Another nodded, slow, his hands resting on his knees. The harp filled the space between David's lines, each phrase given room to settle before the next one came.

"I sought the LORD, and he heard me. He delivered me from all my fears."

David's right hand struck a chord and held it. The sound hung over the ridge, clear and warm, before the wind carried it away. He let the silence sit for a moment. Then he played again.

"Those who looked to him were bright. Their faces were not ashamed."

He paused. He drew a breath. His left hand shifted on the neck of the harp, and a lower note sounded, darker, fuller.

"This poor man cried out, and the LORD heard him. He saved him out of all his troubles."

The men were still now. No one spoke. A bird moved across the sky behind David, crossing from one ridge to the next, and disappeared. The dust rose and settled in the small gusts of wind that came along the ground.

David's fingers found a new pattern on the strings. The notes were brighter, higher, spaced apart like stones across a stream.

"The angel of the LORD camps around those who fear him. He delivers them."

He let the harp ring. The sound carried out across the open ground and faded. A man near the back of the group shifted his weight and looked down at his hands. Another wiped his eyes with the heel of his palm and said nothing.

David leaned forward slightly. His voice lifted.

"Taste and see that the LORD is good. Blessed is the one who trusts in him."

He sang it again, slower the second time, and the harp followed the words, each note matching the rhythm of his voice. The men heard it twice. Some of them mouthed the words.

The wind moved across the ridge. The sun had dropped lower now, and the shadows of the rocks stretched long and thin across the dust. David's cloak shifted around his shoulders. His sandals were scuffed and cracked. His hair was thick with dust from the road.

He straightened and played a rising sequence of notes, the harp bright and sharp in the still air.

"Fear the LORD, you his holy ones. There is no lack for those who fear him."

His eyes swept across the men gathered before him. They sat on the bare ground with their worn cloaks and their dull blades and their empty water skins. He held their gaze and sang the last words.

"Young lions grow weak and go hungry. But those who seek the LORD will not lack any good thing."

The final note left the strings and rose into the air above the ridge. David's hand came down flat against the harp, and the sound stopped. The wind pressed against the group and passed. No one spoke. The men sat in the dust with their faces turned toward him, the low sun on their skin. David held the harp in his lap, both hands resting on the frame, the strings still beneath his palms.
