David sat on a flat stone outside the cave at Adullam. The desert wind moved across the dry hills and stirred the dust at his feet. His harp rested across his knees. His fingers found the strings, and a low sound rose into the open air.

He began to sing.

"I will bless the LORD at all times. His praise will always be in my mouth."

His voice was not loud. It carried the way a voice carries in open country, where there is nothing to stop it. A few men from his company sat nearby on the ground, their weapons beside them, their faces turned toward him.

"My soul will boast in the LORD. The humble will hear it and be glad."

He struck a chord and let it ring. The wind took the sound out across the rocks.

"Magnify the LORD with me. Let us lift up his name together."

Some of the men shifted where they sat. One of them repeated the words under his breath. David's fingers moved again on the strings, and the next line came.

"I sought the LORD, and he heard me. He delivered me from all my fears."

His eyes were open. He looked out across the hills as he sang, the light falling on the stone and the scrub brush and the hard ground.

"Those who looked to him were bright. Their faces were not ashamed. This poor man cried, and the LORD heard him. He saved him out of all his troubles."

The harp rang once, a single clear note that held in the dry air.

"The angel of the LORD camps around those who fear him. He delivers them."

David paused. His hand rested flat against the strings, and the sound stopped. Then he lifted his hand and played again, and his voice rose.

"Taste and see that the LORD is good. Blessed is the one who trusts in him."

The men were still. No one moved. The wind pressed against their cloaks and passed.

"Fear the LORD, you his holy ones. There is no lack for those who fear him. Young lions grow weak and go hungry. But those who seek the LORD will not lack any good thing."

The last note hung in the air over the dry hills. David's hand came to rest on the strings. The men sat where they were, dust on their sandals, the low sun on their faces. The harp was still in his lap. The wind moved, and the silence held.
