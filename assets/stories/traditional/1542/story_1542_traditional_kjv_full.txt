David sat upon a flat stone before the cave at Adullam. The wilderness lay spread beneath him, brown and parched, the hills folding one into another under a pale heaven. Dust had settled upon all things — upon the rocks, upon his mantle, upon the wooden frame of the harp that rested across his knees. His men were scattered in small companies among the stones, putting edges to their blades, mending their sandals, speaking little. The mouth of the cave behind him was dark and cool, and the air that issued forth from it carried the smell of the deep earth.

He set his fingers upon the strings. The first sound was quiet, nigh swallowed by the wind that moved along the ridge. But those men nearest unto him lifted their eyes. One ceased from his labour. Another turned his head.

And David began to sing.

"I will bless the LORD in every hour. His praise shall not depart from my lips."

His voice was steady and unhurried. It was the voice of a man who had resolved a matter within himself and now declared it plainly. His fingers passed over the strings in a slow measure, each note falling clean upon the open air.

"My soul shall glory in the LORD. Let the lowly hear thereof, and be glad."

A few among the men laid down what they held. They drew near, their feet scraping upon the dry ground, and sat themselves down. Some folded their legs beneath them. Others set their backs against the stones. Their faces were heavy with weariness. Their garments had grown thin in places. Yet they listened.

David looked out over the hills as he played. The light was warm and low, and the shadows of the rocks stretched long behind them.

"O magnify the LORD with me," he sang. "Let us exalt his name together."

One of the men near the fore repeated the words softly, his lips moving just behind the voice of David. Another nodded, slow, his hands resting upon his knees. The harp filled the space between each line, and every phrase was given room to settle ere the next was sung.

"I sought the LORD, and he answered me. He drew me forth from all my fears."

David's right hand struck a chord and held it. The sound hung above the ridge, clear and warm, until the wind bore it away. He suffered the silence to rest a moment. Then he played again.

"They that looked unto him were radiant. Shame did not touch their faces."

He paused. He drew breath. His left hand shifted upon the neck of the harp, and a lower note sounded, darker and fuller than before.

"This afflicted man lifted his cry, and the LORD heard him. Out of all his troubles he was saved."

The men were still. None spake. A bird passed across the sky behind David, crossing from one ridge unto the next, and was gone. The dust rose and settled again in the small gusts that moved along the ground.

David's fingers found a new pattern upon the strings. The notes were brighter now, higher, set apart one from another like stones laid across a stream.

"The angel of the LORD encampeth round about them that fear him. And he delivereth them."

He let the harp ring out. The sound carried across the open ground and faded into nothing. A man near the hinder part of the company shifted where he sat and looked down at his hands. Another drew the heel of his palm across his eyes and said nothing.

David leaned forward. His voice rose.

"O taste and know that the LORD is good. Blessed is he that putteth his trust in him."

He sang it once more, slower in the second rendering, and the harp followed after the words, each note fitted to the measure of his voice. The men heard it twice over. Some among them shaped the words upon their lips.

The wind moved across the ridge. The sun had fallen lower, and the shadows of the rocks lay long and thin upon the dust. David's mantle shifted about his shoulders. His sandals were worn through and cracked. The dust of the road lay thick in his hair.

He straightened himself and played a rising course of notes, the harp bright and sharp in the still air.

"Fear the LORD, ye his holy ones. They that fear him shall want for nothing."

His eyes passed over the men gathered before him. They sat upon the bare earth in their threadbare cloaks, with their dull blades and their empty water skins. He held their gaze and sang the last words.

"The young lions do grow faint and hunger. But they that seek the LORD shall not be wanting of any good thing."

The final note left the strings and rose into the air above the ridge. David brought his hand down flat against the harp, and the sound was stilled. The wind pressed upon the company and passed. No man spake. They sat in the dust with their faces turned toward him, the low sun upon their skin. David held the harp in his lap, both hands resting upon the frame, the strings quiet beneath his palms.
