David sat upon a flat stone before the mouth of the cave at Adullam. The wind of the wilderness passed over the dry hills and stirred the dust about his feet. His harp lay across his knees. His fingers found the strings, and a low sound rose up into the open air.

And he began to sing.

"I will bless the LORD in every hour. His praise shall not depart from my mouth."

His voice was not loud. It carried as a voice doth carry in open country, where there is nothing to hinder it. A number of his men sat near upon the ground, their weapons laid beside them, their faces turned toward him.

"My soul shall glory in the LORD. The lowly shall hear thereof and be glad."

He struck a chord and let it ring. And the wind bore the sound out across the rocks.

"O magnify the LORD with me. Let us together lift his name on high."

Some among the men shifted where they sat. One repeated the words beneath his breath. And David's fingers moved again upon the strings, and the next line came forth.

"I sought the LORD, and he heard me. He delivered me out of all my fears."

His eyes were open. He looked out across the hills as he sang, and the light fell upon the stone and the thorn-scrub and the hard ground.

"They that looked unto him were radiant. Their faces bore no shame. This poor man cried aloud, and the LORD heard him, and saved him out of all his troubles."

The harp rang once — a single clear note that held in the dry air.

"The angel of the LORD encampeth round about them that fear him, and delivereth them."

Then David paused. He laid his hand flat against the strings, and the sound ceased. Then he lifted his hand and played again, and his voice rose.

"O taste and see that the LORD is good. Blessed is the man that putteth his trust in him."

The men were still. None moved. The wind pressed against their cloaks and passed.

"Fear the LORD, ye his holy ones, for there is no want unto them that fear him. The young lions do languish and hunger. But they that seek the LORD shall not want for any good thing."

The last note hung in the air above the dry hills. David's hand came to rest upon the strings. The men sat where they were, dust upon their sandals, the low sun upon their faces. The harp lay still in his lap. The wind moved, and the silence held.
