The pass at Michmash lay between two crags. They rose out of the valley like the two walls of a gate, sharp and unmoving, and between them ran the narrow ground where any man would have to walk who wanted to come up against the garrison. The crag on the north was called Bozez. The crag on the south was called Seneh. Bozez stood in front of Michmash. Seneh stood in front of Geba. Between them there was no path. There was only stone, and the climb that the stone allowed.

The Philistines held the higher ground. Their watchmen sat at the top of Bozez where they could see the road and the fields and the dust of any traveler. The men of Israel had been hiding for many days in caves and in holes among the rocks. They came out at night to draw water and went back in before the sun.

In the uttermost part of Gibeah, under the pomegranate tree which is in Migron, Saul the king sat with about six hundred men. Ahijah, the son of Ahitub, the priest of Yahweh in Shiloh, stood among them wearing an ephod. They were waiting. They were not moving. The pomegranate tree gave them shade, and the morning was passing.

None of them knew that Jonathan was gone.

He had risen before light. He had taken only his armor bearer with him. He had not told his father. The two of them had walked down through the broken ground south of the pass, and they had come at first light to the foot of Seneh, where the rocks gave them cover and the garrison above them could not yet see.

Jonathan stood at the foot of the crag. He looked up. Above him the face of Bozez climbed white into the morning. The spearheads of the garrison caught the new sun at the top.

He said to the young man who bore his armor, "Come. Let us go over to the garrison of these uncircumcised. It may be that Yahweh will work for us. There is no restraint on Yahweh to save by many or by few."

The armor bearer was younger than Jonathan. He carried Jonathan's shield and his second blade. He looked up at the crag. He looked down at his own hands. He looked back at Jonathan.

"Do all that is in your heart," he said. "Turn. Behold, I am with you according to your heart."

Jonathan nodded. He set his hand against the rock. The stone was cold under his palm. He turned and looked up the line of the climb. He did not pray aloud. He worked out the way they would go.

"We will pass over to the men, and we will reveal ourselves to them," he said. "If they say this to us, 'Wait until we come to you,' then we will stand still in our place. We will not go up. But if they say this, 'Come up to us,' then we will go up. For Yahweh will have delivered them into our hand. This shall be the sign to us."

The armor bearer said nothing. He waited.

Jonathan stepped out from the cover of Seneh. He walked into the open ground at the foot of the pass. His armor bearer walked behind him. They stood there in plain sight, two men where two men were not supposed to be.

A shout came down from above. Then a second shout. The watchmen on Bozez were standing now. They were pointing. Their voices carried down the rock face, and there was laughter in their voices. They said to one another, "Behold, the Hebrews are coming out of the holes where they had hidden themselves!"

Jonathan did not move. He kept his eyes on the top of the crag.

The men of the garrison answered Jonathan and his armor bearer, and said, "Come up to us, and we will show you something!"

The word came down. The sign was given.

Jonathan turned to his armor bearer. "Come up after me," he said. "For Yahweh has delivered them into the hand of Israel."

Then he turned to the rock.

Jonathan climbed up on his hands and on his feet, and his armor bearer climbed up after him. The face of Bozez was steep. The handholds were narrow. He set his fingers in a crack of the stone and pulled. He found a ledge for his foot. He pushed up. He felt the rock with his palms and chose the next place to set his hand. The armor bearer climbed behind him in his shadow, the shield slung at his back, his eyes on the heels of Jonathan above him.

The garrison waited at the top. They had stopped laughing. They were watching the two men come up out of the gap. They did not yet move to block the climb. They were still waiting for the show that they had promised themselves.

Jonathan came over the lip of Bozez. He stood up on the level ground at the top, and the first Philistine of the garrison was within reach of him. Jonathan's short blade was already in his hand. The man fell before Jonathan. Then the next man fell. The armor bearer came up over the edge behind him, and he came in close, and what Jonathan struck down the armor bearer finished.

They moved together along the line of the garrison. The Philistines reached for their weapons too slowly. Some had set their spears down. Some had been laughing only a moment before. Jonathan and the armor bearer worked through them. That first slaughter, which Jonathan and his armor bearer made, was about twenty men, within as it were half a furrow's length in an acre of land.

A small piece of ground. Twenty men.

The men still standing in the garrison turned and ran. They ran back through the rocks toward the tents of the main camp behind them. The men in the tents saw their own watchmen running toward them. They reached for their swords. They shouted to one another. They did not know what was coming. They thought a whole army of Israel had come up over Bozez.

The raiders out in the field saw the dust of the running men and turned back toward the camp. They saw their own running. They did not know what to do. They began to run too.

And then the ground shook.

There was a trembling in the camp, in the field, and among all the people. The garrison trembled. The raiders trembled. The men in the tents trembled. The earth quaked under all of them, and there was an exceedingly great trembling. The pots on the cookfires fell. The standing spears leaned and fell. The men on their feet stumbled and did not know if it was their own legs or the ground beneath them.

Jonathan stood on the high rock above Michmash. His armor bearer stood beside him. They did not chase the running men. They stood and watched the host of the Philistines break apart and run in every direction across the valley. Two men had come up the crag. Twenty men had fallen at their feet. And the host of the enemy was running.

Below them, far behind them, under the pomegranate tree at Migron, Saul did not yet know what had happened at the pass. The six hundred men with him did not yet know. Ahijah the priest did not yet know.

Above them, on the rock called Bozez, the sun was climbing. The dust of the running Philistines moved across the valley below. The earth that had quaked grew still again under Jonathan's feet, and Jonathan was still standing.
