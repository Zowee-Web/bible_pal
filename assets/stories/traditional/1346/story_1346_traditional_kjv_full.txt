The pass at Michmash lay between two crags. One rose on the north, sheer and pale in the morning. The other rose on the south, lower but rougher, its face broken with handholds and sharp stone. The Philistines had taken the higher ground. From their garrison they could see the road, the camp at Geba, and the dust of any man who came near.

Jonathan stood on the south side at first light. His armourbearer stood beside him. They were two, and only two.

Saul, his father, was far behind them. He tarried in the uttermost part of Gibeah under the pomegranate tree which is in Migron, and the people that were with him were about six hundred men. Ahijah, the LORD's priest, stood among them wearing an ephod. And the people knew not that Jonathan was gone.

Jonathan said unto the young man that bare his armour, "Come, and let us go over unto the garrison of these uncircumcised. It may be that the LORD will work for us: for there is no restraint to the LORD to save by many or by few."

The armourbearer looked at him. He looked at the crag above them, where the spearheads of the garrison caught the new sun. He looked back at Jonathan. "Do all that is in thine heart," he said. "Turn thee; behold, I am with thee according to thy heart."

Jonathan looked up at the rock. The name of the rock above Michmash was Bozez. The name of the rock at their feet was Seneh. Between them there was no path. There was only the climb.

"Behold, we will pass over unto these men, and we will discover ourselves unto them," he said. "If they say thus unto us, 'Tarry until we come to you,' then we will stand still in our place, and will not go up unto them. But if they say thus, 'Come up unto us,' then we will go up: for the LORD hath delivered them into our hand: and this shall be a sign unto us."

He stepped out from the cover of the crag. His armourbearer stepped out behind him. The two men walked into the open at the foot of the pass, where the watchmen of the garrison could see them plainly.

A voice rose from above. Then another. The Philistines were pointing down at them. Their laughter carried down the rock. "Behold," they said, "the Hebrews come forth out of the holes where they had hid themselves!"

Jonathan did not move. He waited.

And the men of the garrison answered them, and said, "Come up to us, and we will shew you a thing!"

Jonathan turned to his armourbearer. "Come up after me: for the LORD hath delivered them into the hand of Israel."

Then Jonathan put his hands on the rock. He found the first hold. He pulled himself up. He set his feet on the next ridge. He climbed up upon his hands and upon his feet, and his armourbearer climbed after him. The crag was steep. The handholds were narrow. They moved up against the face of Bozez while the garrison waited above, still laughing, still waiting for the show.

Jonathan came over the top. He came up onto the level ground at the edge of the garrison, and the laughter stopped.

The first Philistine fell before him. Then the next. Jonathan moved through them with the short blade in his hand, and his armourbearer came up behind him and finished what fell. They went on. That first slaughter, which Jonathan and his armourbearer made, was about twenty men, within as it were an half acre of land, which a yoke of oxen might plow.

The men still standing in the garrison turned and ran. They ran into the tents behind them. The men in the tents saw their own running and reached for their swords. The spoilers in the field looked back toward the camp and saw men running in the wrong direction.

Then the ground shook.

There was trembling in the host, in the field, and among all the people. The garrison trembled. The spoilers trembled. And the earth quaked under all of them: so it was a very great trembling.

Jonathan stood on the rock above Michmash with his armourbearer beside him. Two men. Twenty fallen. A host running in every direction, and the ground itself moving beneath them. And the morning had scarce begun.
