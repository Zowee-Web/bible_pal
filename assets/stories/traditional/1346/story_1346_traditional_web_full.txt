The pass at Michmash lay between two crags. One rose on the north, sheer and pale in the morning. The other rose on the south, lower but rougher, its face broken with handholds and sharp stone. The Philistines had taken the higher ground. From their garrison they could see the road, the camp at Geba, and the dust of any man who came near.

Jonathan stood on the south side at first light. His armor bearer stood beside him. They were two, and only two.

Saul, his father, was far behind them. He had stayed in the uttermost part of Gibeah under the pomegranate tree which is in Migron, and the people who were with him were about six hundred men. Ahijah, the priest of Yahweh, stood among them wearing an ephod. None of them knew where Jonathan was.

Jonathan said to the young man who bore his armor, "Come. Let us go over to the garrison of these uncircumcised. It may be that Yahweh will work for us. There is no restraint on Yahweh to save by many or by few."

The armor bearer looked at him. He looked at the crag above them, where the spearheads of the garrison caught the new sun. He looked back at Jonathan. "Do all that is in your heart," he said. "Turn. Behold, I am with you according to your heart."

Jonathan looked up at the rock. The name of the rock above Michmash was Bozez. The name of the rock at their feet was Seneh. Between them there was no path. There was only the climb.

"We will pass over to the men, and we will reveal ourselves to them," he said. "If they say, 'Wait until we come to you,' then we will stand still in our place and will not go up. But if they say, 'Come up to us,' then we will go up. For Yahweh will have delivered them into our hand. This shall be the sign to us."

He stepped out from the cover of the crag. His armor bearer stepped out behind him. The two men walked into the open at the foot of the pass, where the watchmen of the garrison could see them plainly.

A voice rose from above. Then another. The Philistines were pointing down at them. Their laughter carried down the rock. "Behold," they said, "the Hebrews are coming out of the holes where they had hidden themselves!"

Jonathan did not move. He waited.

The men of the garrison answered them, and said, "Come up to us, and we will show you something!"

Jonathan turned to his armor bearer. "Come up after me. For Yahweh has delivered them into the hand of Israel."

Then Jonathan put his hands on the rock. He found the first hold. He pulled himself up. He set his feet on the next ridge. He climbed up on his hands and on his feet, and his armor bearer climbed after him. The crag was steep. The handholds were narrow. They moved up against the face of Bozez while the garrison waited above, still laughing, still waiting for the show.

Jonathan came over the top. He came up onto the level ground at the edge of the garrison, and the laughter stopped.

The first Philistine fell before him. Then the next. Jonathan moved through them with the short blade in his hand, and his armor bearer came up behind him and finished what fell. They went on. That first slaughter, which Jonathan and his armor bearer made, was about twenty men, within as it were half a furrow's length in an acre of land.

The men still standing in the garrison turned and ran. They ran into the tents behind them. The men in the tents saw their own running and reached for their swords. The raiders in the field looked back toward the camp and saw men running in the wrong direction.

Then the ground shook.

There was a trembling in the camp, in the field, and among all the people. The garrison trembled. The raiders trembled. And the earth quaked under all of them, and there was an exceedingly great trembling.

Jonathan stood on the rock above Michmash with his armor bearer beside him. Two men. Twenty fallen. A host running in every direction, and the ground itself moving beneath them. And the morning had barely begun.
