The five men of the king came down from the palace to the second quarter of Jerusalem.

They came to the house of Huldah the prophetess.

She was the wife of Shallum, the son of Tikvah, the son of Harhas — the keeper of the wardrobe. She lived in the second quarter of the city, where the houses of those who served the king's household were.

The five came to her door — Hilkiah the priest, and Ahikam the son of Shaphan, and Achbor the son of Micaiah, and Shaphan the scribe, and Asaiah the king's servant.

They told her what had happened.

They told her about the book of the law that had been found in the house of Yahweh. They told her about the king's tearing of his garments. They told her about his command to inquire of Yahweh for him, and for the people, and for all Judah.

Huldah heard them.

She did not stand to answer. She did not raise her voice. She gave them Yahweh's word from where she sat in her own house.

She said, "Yahweh, the God of Israel, says — tell the man who sent you to me, Yahweh says: Behold, I will bring evil on this place, and on its inhabitants, even all the words of the book which the king of Judah has read. Because they have forsaken me, and have burned incense to other gods, that they might provoke me to anger with all the work of their hands. Therefore my wrath shall be kindled against this place, and it shall not be quenched."

She paused.

Then she gave them the second word — the word for the king himself.

"But to the king of Judah, who sent you to inquire of Yahweh, you shall tell him — Yahweh, the God of Israel, says: Concerning the words which you have heard, because your heart was tender, and you humbled yourself before Yahweh when you heard what I spoke against this place and against its inhabitants — and you tore your clothes, and wept before me — I also have heard you, says Yahweh. Therefore, behold, I will gather you to your fathers, and you shall be gathered to your grave in peace. Neither shall your eyes see all the evil which I will bring on this place."

She finished.

The five men of the king stood quiet in her room for a moment. Then they went out of the second quarter of the city, and back up to the palace, and they brought the message to the king.
