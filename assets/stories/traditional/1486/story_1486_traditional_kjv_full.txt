They had been brought into Babylon from Jerusalem in the third year of the king's reign. They had been chosen out from among the children of the captives — young men without blemish, well favoured, skilful in all wisdom, such as had ability to stand in the king's palace.

Daniel was one of them. Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah were with him.

The king's meat was set before them at the appointed hour. Wine from the king's table — the same wine the king drank in his own hall. Meat from the king's table — beasts slain upon the king's altars unto the king's gods, with portions sent unto the young men who were being trained.

Three years of this. Then they should stand before the king.

But Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's meat, nor with the wine which he drank.

He went unto the prince of the eunuchs and requested that he might not defile himself.

The prince was afraid. He said, "I fear my lord the king, who hath appointed your meat and your drink. Why should he see your faces worse liking than the children which are of your sort? Then shall ye make me endanger my head to the king."

Daniel pressed him not there. He departed from the prince and waited.

Then went he to Melzar, whom the prince of the eunuchs had set over Daniel and Hananiah and Mishael and Azariah. He spake unto him in a place where no other man heard.

He said, "Prove thy servants, I beseech thee, ten days. Let them give us pulse to eat, and water to drink. Then let our countenances be looked upon before thee, and the countenance of the children that eat of the portion of the king's meat. And as thou seest, deal with thy servants."

So Melzar consented to them in this matter, and proved them ten days.

The pulse was brought morning by morning. The water was drawn from the well of the house where the young men slept. The four ate together at the appointed hour, while the others ate the king's meat at the long table down the hall.

And at the end of ten days their countenances appeared fairer and fatter in flesh than all the children which did eat the portion of the king's meat.

Thus Melzar took away the portion of their meat, and the wine that they should drink. He gave them pulse, from that day forth.

The three years passed. Daniel and the three with him grew in knowledge. As for these four children, God gave them knowledge and skill in all learning and wisdom. And Daniel had understanding in all visions and dreams — which the others did not yet know he had.

Now at the end of the days that the king had said he should bring them in, the prince of the eunuchs brought them in before Nebuchadnezzar.

The king sat upon his high seat in the great hall. The young men of the kingdom stood before him in their ranks. And the king communed with every one of them in turn.

Among them all was found none like Daniel and Hananiah and Mishael and Azariah.

Therefore stood they before the king from that day forth.

And in all matters of wisdom and understanding that the king enquired of them, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and astrologers that were in all his realm.

Daniel continued before the king from that year. He continued through the years of Nebuchadnezzar, and the years after Nebuchadnezzar, and even unto the first year of King Cyrus.

The choice he had made at the table when he was a young man held him through all those years.
