In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah, Nebuchadnezzar came up against Jerusalem and besieged it. He took part of the vessels of the house of God with him to Babylon, and he took young men of the house of the king of Judah, and of the princes — children in whom was no blemish, well favoured, skilful in all wisdom, cunning in knowledge, and understanding science, such as had ability to stand in the king's palace.

Daniel was one of them. Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah were with him.

They were lodged together in a house of the king. The prince of the eunuchs, whose name was Ashpenaz, had charge over them. They were given new names that came from the gods of Babylon — Belteshazzar, Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego — names meant to take the names of their fathers' God off their tongues.

They were given the king's portion to eat.

The portion was set before them at the appointed hour every day. Wine from the king's table, in a cup. Meat from the king's table, upon a plate. The meat had been offered unto the king's gods ere it came out of the king's kitchens. The wine had been poured in the king's banqueting hall before that which was left was sent down unto the young men who were being trained.

Three years of this was the plan. At the end of the three years, the trained ones should stand before the king himself.

Daniel was a young man. He had been in Jerusalem in the year before. He had seen the temple of Yahweh and had eaten at the table of his own father's house. He had known what was clean and what was unclean. He had been taught the law of Moses from his childhood.

But Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's meat, nor with the wine which he drank.

The decision shewed not in his face when he made it. He kept it within, where the men of Babylon could not see it. Then went he out, when the hour was right, unto the prince of the eunuchs.

He requested of the prince that he might not defile himself.

The prince was afraid.

He said, "I fear my lord the king, who hath appointed your meat and your drink. Why should he see your faces worse liking than the children which are of your sort? Then shall ye make me endanger my head to the king."

Daniel pressed him not there. He took the answer the prince had given. He waited.

After some days he went unto Melzar, whom the prince of the eunuchs had set over Daniel and Hananiah and Mishael and Azariah. He spake unto him in a place where the prince could not hear, and where the other children could not hear. He spake quietly.

He said, "Prove thy servants, I beseech thee, ten days. Let them give us pulse to eat, and water to drink. Then let our countenances be looked upon before thee, and the countenance of the children that eat of the portion of the king's meat. And as thou seest, deal with thy servants."

So Melzar consented to them in this matter, and proved them ten days.

He had the pulse brought unto them in the morning. He had the water drawn from the well of the house. The four ate together at the appointed hour, while the other children ate the king's meat at the long table down the hall.

The ten days passed.

And at the end of ten days the steward looked at the four faces. He looked at the faces of the other children. He saw that Daniel's countenance, and Hananiah's, and Mishael's, and Azariah's were fairer. He saw that they were fatter in flesh than all the children which did eat the portion of the king's meat.

Thus Melzar took away the portion of their meat, and the wine that they should drink. He gave them pulse, from that day forth, and the wine he set aside for himself.

The three years passed.

In those years, God gave the four children knowledge and skill in all learning and wisdom. They were taught the tongue of the Chaldeans, and the writings, and the science of the stars. They learned what the Babylonian teachers could teach them. And unto Daniel, beyond what the teachers gave, God gave understanding in all visions and dreams.

The others knew not yet that he had this understanding. It was a thing that would come out later, in the chambers of Nebuchadnezzar.

Now at the end of the days that the king had said he should bring them in, the prince of the eunuchs brought all the trained children in before Nebuchadnezzar.

The king sat upon his high seat in the great hall of his palace. The children stood in their ranks before him. And the king communed with every one of them in turn. He asked them what he would ask.

He came to Daniel.

He came to Hananiah, and to Mishael, and to Azariah.

Among them all was found none like these four.

Therefore stood they before the king from that day forth. They served in the king's court. They were brought in when the king enquired of them in matters of wisdom and understanding.

And in every such matter, the king found them ten times better than all the magicians and astrologers that were in all his realm.

Daniel continued before the king from that year.

He continued through the years of Nebuchadnezzar, through the years of the king's madness in the field and the king's returning to his throne, through the years of Belshazzar after Nebuchadnezzar, through the night that Belshazzar was slain, and even unto the first year of King Cyrus the Persian, when the captivity was ending and the people of God were given leave to return unto Jerusalem.

The choice he had made at the table when he was a young man held him through all those years.
