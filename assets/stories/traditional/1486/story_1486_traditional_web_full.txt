They had been brought into Babylon from Jerusalem in the third year of the king's reign. They had been selected from among the children of the captives — young men without blemish, well-favored, skillful in all wisdom, fit to stand in the king's palace.

Daniel was one of them. Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah were with him.

The king's portion was set before them at the appointed hour. Wine from the king's table — the same wine the king drank in his own hall. Meat from the king's table — animals slaughtered on the king's altars to the king's gods, with portions sent to the young men who were being trained.

Three years of this. Then they would stand before the king.

Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the king's food, nor with the wine which the king drank.

He went to the prince of the eunuchs and asked that he might not defile himself.

The prince was afraid. He said, "I fear my lord the king, who has appointed your food and your drink. Why should he see your faces worse than the other youths who are of your own age? You would endanger my head with the king."

Daniel did not press him there. He went away from the prince and waited.

Then he went to the steward whom the prince had set over him and over Hananiah and Mishael and Azariah. He spoke to him in a place where no one else heard.

He said, "Test your servants, I beg you, ten days. Let them give us vegetables to eat, and water to drink. Then let our faces be examined before you, and the faces of the youths who eat the king's portion. As you see, deal with your servants."

The steward listened to him in this matter. He tested them for ten days.

The vegetables were brought morning by morning. The water was drawn from the well of the house where the young men slept. The four ate together at the appointed hour, while the others ate the king's meat at the long table down the hall.

The ten days passed.

At the end of them, the steward looked at the four faces and at the faces of the other youths. He saw that Daniel's face, and Hananiah's, and Mishael's, and Azariah's were fairer. He saw that they were fatter in flesh than all the youths who ate of the king's food.

He took away the king's portion. He gave them vegetables, from that day on.

The three years passed. Daniel and the three with him grew in knowledge. God gave them skill in all learning and wisdom. Daniel had understanding in visions and dreams, which the others did not yet know he had.

When the days were finished that the king had appointed, the prince of the eunuchs brought them in before Nebuchadnezzar.

The king sat on his high seat in the great hall. The young men of the kingdom stood before him in rows. The king spoke with each of them in turn.

Among them all was found no one like Daniel and Hananiah and Mishael and Azariah.

Therefore they stood before the king from that day on.

In every matter of wisdom and understanding concerning which the king inquired of them, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters in his realm.

Daniel continued before the king from that year. He continued through the years of Nebuchadnezzar, and the years after Nebuchadnezzar, and into the first year of King Cyrus.

The choice he had made at the table when he was a young man held him through all those years.
