Daniel had been brought before the king. He had been brought by Arioch, the captain of the king's guard. He stood before the throne. He had been given the dream and the interpretation in a vision of the night. He had blessed God before he came in. He had said: blessed be the name of God for ever and ever, for wisdom and might are his. He stood now before the king.

The king had asked him: are you able to make known unto me the dream which I have seen, and the interpretation thereof?

Daniel had answered: the secret which the king has demanded cannot the wise men, the astrologers, the magicians, the soothsayers, show unto the king. But there is a God in heaven that reveals secrets. He has made known to the king Nebuchadnezzar what shall be in the latter days. Your dream, and the visions of your head upon your bed, are these. As for you, O king, your thoughts came into your mind upon your bed, what should come to pass hereafter. He that reveals secrets has made known to you what shall come to pass. As for me, this secret is not revealed to me for any wisdom that I have more than any living, but for the sakes of those that shall make known the interpretation to the king, and that you might know the thoughts of your heart.

He spoke first of what the king had seen. "You saw, O king, and behold, a great image. The image was very bright. Its form was terrible. It stood before you.

"The head of the image was of fine gold. The breast and the arms were of silver. The belly and the thighs were of brass. The legs were of iron. The feet were part iron and part clay.

"You saw a stone cut out of the mountain without hands. The stone struck the image on its feet of iron and clay. It broke them in pieces.

"Then the iron, the clay, the brass, the silver, and the gold were broken to pieces together. They became like the chaff of the summer threshing-floors. The wind carried them away. No place was found for them.

"The stone that struck the image became a great mountain. The mountain filled the whole earth."

He paused. The king was listening.

He gave the interpretation. "This is the dream. We will tell the interpretation thereof before the king.

"You, O king, are a king of kings. The God of heaven has given you a kingdom, power, and strength, and glory. Wherever the children of men dwell, the beasts of the field and the fowls of the heaven has he given into your hand. He has made you ruler over them all. You are this head of gold.

"After you shall arise another kingdom inferior to you. Then a third kingdom of brass which shall bear rule over all the earth.

"The fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron. As iron breaks in pieces all things, so shall it break in pieces and bruise. The feet and toes part iron and part clay: the kingdom shall be divided. Part of it shall be strong as iron. Part shall be broken. As iron is not mixed with clay, so shall they not cleave one to another.

"In the days of these kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom which shall never be destroyed. The kingdom shall not be left to other people. It shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms. It shall stand for ever.

"The stone was cut out of the mountain without hands. It broke in pieces the iron, the brass, the clay, the silver, and the gold. The great God has made known to the king what shall come to pass hereafter. The dream is certain. The interpretation thereof is sure."

The king fell upon his face. He worshipped Daniel. He said: of a truth, your God is a God of gods, and a Lord of kings, and a revealer of secrets, seeing you could reveal this secret. He commanded that offerings be brought to Daniel and sweet odors set before him. The court of the king watched. The wise men who had not been able to read the dream watched also. The king did not look at them.

He made Daniel a great man. He gave him many great gifts. He set him ruler over the whole province of Babylon. He made him chief of the governors over all the wise men of Babylon. Daniel asked of the king, and the king set Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego over the affairs of the province of Babylon. Daniel himself sat in the gate of the king. The four young men were established together in the king's house, where they had been brought up from the captivity. The dream had been read. The great image of gold and silver and brass and iron stood now only in the memory of the king as a thing he had been shown. The stone that became a mountain stood there also, in the same memory, and it would not pass away from the king for the rest of the days he was given to walk under the sun. The image had been struck on its feet of iron and clay. The mountain had filled the whole earth in the vision. Daniel had been the one to read it.
