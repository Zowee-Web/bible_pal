Ezra prayed and confessed before God’s house, weeping and casting himself down. A great assembly of Israelite men, women, and children gathered around him. They too wept bitterly.

Shecaniah, son of Jehiel, of the sons of Elam, spoke to Ezra. They had trespassed against their God by marrying foreign women, he said, yet there was still hope for Israel. He proposed a covenant with God to put away the wives and their children, following the counsel of Ezra and those who trembled at God’s commandment, according to the law. He urged Ezra to rise and act courageously: the matter belonged to him, and they would stand with him.

Ezra rose. He required the chief priests, the Levites, and all Israel to swear that they would follow those words. They swore.

Then he entered the room of Jehohanan, Eliashib’s son. He ate no bread and drank no water, mourning the exiles’ trespass.

A proclamation went throughout Judah and Jerusalem, summoning everyone from the captivity to Jerusalem within three days. By the counsel of the princes and elders, anyone absent would forfeit all possessions and be excluded from the assembly.

The men of Judah and Benjamin came within the appointed three days. On the twentieth day of the ninth month, they sat in the open place before God’s house. They trembled because of the matter before them and because of the heavy rain.

Ezra stood and charged them with increasing Israel’s guilt by marrying foreign women. He called them to confess to Yahweh, the God of their fathers, do his pleasure, and separate from the peoples of the land and the foreign women.

The assembly answered loudly that they must do as he said. But there were many people, and the rain prevented them from standing outside. So many had transgressed that the work could not take only a day or two. They proposed appointing their princes to represent the assembly. Men from each city who had married foreign women would come at appointed times with their elders and judges, until God’s fierce wrath over the matter was turned away.

Jonathan, Asahel’s son, and Jahzeiah, Tikvah’s son, opposed this. Meshullam and Shabbethai the Levite helped them.

The returned exiles proceeded with the arrangement. Ezra and named heads of ancestral households were set apart to examine the matter. They began on the first day of the tenth month. By the first day of the first month, they had finished examining all the men who had married foreign women.
