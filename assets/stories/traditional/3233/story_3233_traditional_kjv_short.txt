Before the house of God, Ezra wept, prayed, and confessed, casting himself down. Men, women, and children gathered unto him from Israel in a very great congregation. The people wept sorely.

Shechaniah, the son of Jehiel, of the sons of Elam, answered Ezra. They had sinned against their God in taking strange wives from the people of the land; yet he spoke of hope for Israel concerning this matter. He called for a covenant with God to put away the wives and their children, according to the counsel of Ezra and those who trembled at God’s commandment. Let it be done according to the law. Ezra must arise and act with courage, Shechaniah said, for the matter belonged to him and they would be with him.

Ezra stood and made the chief priests, the Levites, and all Israel swear to carry out that word. They gave their oath.

Leaving the house of God, he entered Johanan the son of Eliashib’s chamber. There he took neither bread nor water, mourning the transgression of those carried away.

A proclamation summoned the children of the captivity from Judah and Jerusalem. They were to assemble at Jerusalem within three days. By the counsel of the princes and elders, any who failed to come would forfeit all their substance and be separated from the congregation.

Within three days, all the men of Judah and Benjamin had gathered. It was the twentieth day of the ninth month. Sitting in the street before God’s house, the people trembled over this matter and under the great rain.

Ezra the priest arose and charged them with increasing Israel’s trespass by taking strange wives. He commanded confession unto the Lord God of their fathers, obedience to his pleasure, and separation from the people of the land and the strange wives.

With a loud voice the congregation agreed. But they pleaded that they were many, the rain was great, and they could not stand without. The work could not be finished in a day or two, for many had transgressed. Let the rulers act for the congregation, they proposed. Men with strange wives should come from their cities at appointed times, accompanied by each city’s elders and judges, until God’s fierce wrath for the matter was turned away.

Jonathan, Asahel’s son, and Jahaziah, Tikvah’s son, were employed about this matter; Meshullam and Shabbethai the Levite helped them.

The exiles followed the arrangement. Ezra and named heads of fathers’ houses were set apart. On the first day of the tenth month they began their examination. By the first day of the first month, they had finished with all the men who had taken strange wives.
