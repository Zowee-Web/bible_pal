Abimelech, son of Jerubbaal, came to his mother’s brethren at Shechem and spoke with all the family of her father’s house. He bade them ask Shechem’s men whether seventy sons of Jerubbaal should reign over them, or one. Let them remember that he was their bone and flesh.

His mother’s brethren carried his words to Shechem. The men’s hearts inclined toward Abimelech, for they called him their brother. They gave him threescore and ten pieces of silver from the house of Baal–berith. With these he hired vain and light persons to follow him.

He went to his father’s house at Ophrah and slew his brethren, Jerubbaal’s seventy sons, upon one stone. Yet Jotham, the youngest, remained alive, having hidden himself. All Shechem’s men and the house of Millo gathered and made Abimelech king by the plain of the pillar in Shechem.

When Jotham was told, he stood on mount Gerizim and lifted his voice. Let the men of Shechem hearken unto him, he cried, that God might hearken unto them. He spoke of trees going forth to anoint a king.

They asked the olive tree to reign. The olive tree asked whether it should leave its fatness, whereby God and man were honoured, to be promoted over the trees.

They asked the fig tree next. The fig tree asked whether it should forsake its sweetness and good fruit to be promoted over them. Then came their invitation to the vine. The vine asked whether it should leave the wine that cheered God and man to be promoted over the trees.

At last all the trees called upon the bramble to reign. If they truly anointed it king, it said, let them trust in its shadow. If not, let fire issue from the bramble and devour the cedars of Lebanon.

Jotham asked whether they had dealt truly and sincerely in making Abimelech king, and had treated Jerubbaal and his house according to the deserving of his hands. His father had fought for them, adventured his life far, and delivered them from Midian.

But they had risen against his father’s house, Jotham charged, slaying his seventy sons upon one stone. They had crowned Abimelech, the son of Jerubbaal’s maidservant, because he was their brother.

If their dealing had been true and sincere, let them rejoice in Abimelech, and he in them. Otherwise, Jotham said, let fire come from Abimelech to devour Shechem’s men and Millo’s house, and from them to devour Abimelech.

Jotham fled to Beer and dwelt there, for fear of Abimelech his brother.
