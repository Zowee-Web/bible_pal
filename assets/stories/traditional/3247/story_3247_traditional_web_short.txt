Abimelech, Jerubbaal’s son, went to his mother’s brothers in Shechem and spoke with all her father’s household. He asked them to put a choice before Shechem’s men: should all seventy sons of Jerubbaal rule them, or just one? They should remember that he was their own flesh and bone.

His mother’s brothers repeated his words. Shechem’s men inclined toward him, saying he was their brother. From Baal Berith’s house they gave him seventy pieces of silver. With it Abimelech hired vain, reckless men who followed him.

At his father’s house in Ophrah, Abimelech killed his brothers, the seventy sons of Jerubbaal, on one stone. But the youngest, Jotham, survived by hiding. Shechem’s men and all the house of Millo gathered and made Abimelech king by the oak of the pillar in Shechem.

When Jotham heard, he stood atop Mount Gerizim and cried to Shechem’s men to listen, that God might listen to them. He told of trees setting out to anoint a king.

They first asked the olive tree to reign. The olive asked whether it should stop producing the oil by which God and man were honored, to wave back and forth over the trees.

The fig tree was asked next. Should it leave its sweetness and good fruit to wave over the trees? Then the trees asked the vine, which asked whether it should leave its new wine, cheering God and man, to wave over them.

Finally all the trees asked the bramble. If they truly anointed it king, the bramble answered, they should take refuge in its shade. Otherwise, let fire come from it and devour Lebanon’s cedars.

Jotham then asked whether they had acted truly and righteously in making Abimelech king, treating Jerubbaal and his house as his deeds deserved. His father had fought for them, risked his life, and delivered them from Midian.

Yet, Jotham charged, they had risen against his father’s house that day, slain his seventy sons on one stone, and made Abimelech, the son of his female servant, king because he was their brother.

If they had dealt truly and righteously, Jotham said, let them rejoice in Abimelech, and let him rejoice in them. If not, let fire come from Abimelech to devour Shechem’s men and Millo’s house, and fire from them to devour Abimelech.

Jotham fled to Beer and lived there, afraid of his brother Abimelech.
