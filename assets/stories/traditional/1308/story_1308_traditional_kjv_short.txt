After they had laid hands on Stephen and stoned him without the city, a great persecution arose against the church which was at Jerusalem. They that had heard the word were scattered abroad — into the regions of Judaea, and into the country of the Samaritans. They went every where preaching the word. The apostles tarried in Jerusalem, but the disciples went forth, carrying the gospel with them along the ways.

Philip was one of the seven that had been chosen to serve the tables. He departed out of Jerusalem with the others. He took the road northward — into the hill country, into the territory that was no longer Jewish, into the city of the Samaritans.

Then Philip went down to the city of Samaria, and preached Christ unto them.

And the people with one accord gave heed unto those things which Philip spake, hearing and seeing the miracles which he did.

For unclean spirits, crying with loud voice, came out of many that were possessed with them: and many taken with palsies, and that were lame, were healed.

And there was great joy in that city.

And they that had been bound were loosed. They that had been silent began to speak. They that had lain down in their houses arose and walked in the streets. The word of the Christ moved through Samaria. And so it was in the days when the gospel first crossed over into the country beyond Jerusalem.
