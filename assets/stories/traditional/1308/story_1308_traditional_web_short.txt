After they had laid hands on Stephen and stoned him outside the city, a great persecution began against the church in Jerusalem. Those who had heard the word were scattered abroad — into the regions of Judea, and into the country of the Samaritans. They went everywhere preaching the word. The apostles remained in Jerusalem, but the disciples went out, carrying the gospel with them down the roads.

Philip was one of the seven who had been chosen to serve the tables. He left Jerusalem with the others. He took the road north — into the hill country, into the territory that was no longer Jewish, into the city of the Samaritans.

Philip went down to the city of Samaria, and proclaimed to them the Christ.

The multitudes listened with one accord to the things that were spoken by Philip, when they heard and saw the signs which he did.

For unclean spirits came out of many of those who had them, crying with a loud voice. Many who had been paralyzed and lame were healed.

There was great joy in that city.

And the people who had been bound were unbound. The people who had been silent began to speak. The people who had lain down in their houses got up and walked in the streets. The word of the Christ moved through Samaria. And so it was in the days when the gospel first crossed into the country beyond Jerusalem.
