Elijah the prophet stood before King Ahab. "As the Lord, the God of Israel, lives," Elijah said, "there will be no dew or rain these years except by my word." Then he turned and walked away.

God told Elijah where to go. "Go east and hide by the brook Cherith, near the Jordan River. You will drink from the brook. I have commanded the ravens to feed you there."

Elijah went. He found the brook — a small stream running over smooth stones. Every morning, ravens came with bread and meat in their beaks. Every evening, they came again. Black birds with shiny feathers, bringing food to a hungry man.

But the rain did not come. The brook got smaller. The water slowed to a trickle. Then it dried up completely. The stones lay bare and warm.

God spoke to Elijah again. "Get up and go to Zarephath. I have commanded a widow there to feed you."

Elijah walked a long way. He was tired and thirsty. When he came to the gate of Zarephath, he saw a woman gathering sticks. She was thin. Her clothes were worn.

"Please bring me a little water in a cup so I can drink," Elijah called to her.

She turned to go get it. "And please bring me a piece of bread too," he added.

The woman stopped. She looked at him with sad eyes. "As the Lord your God lives," she said, "I do not have a loaf of bread. All I have is a handful of flour in a jar and a little bit of oil in a jug. I am gathering these two sticks to make a fire and cook one last meal for my son and me. After we eat it, we will die."

Elijah looked at her kindly. "Do not be afraid," he said. "Go home and do as you have said. But first make me a small cake and bring it to me. Then make something for yourself and your son. For this is what the Lord says: the jar of flour will not run out, and the jug of oil will not become empty, until the day the Lord sends rain on the earth."

The woman looked at Elijah. She went home and did what he said. She made a small cake and brought it to him first. Then she made food for herself and her son. They ate.

The next morning, she went to the flour jar. There was flour inside. She tipped the oil jug. Oil poured out. Day after day, there was always enough.
