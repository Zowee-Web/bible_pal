Elijah the prophet stood before King Ahab. "As the Lord, the God of Israel, liveth," Elijah said, "there shall not be dew nor rain these years, but according to my word." Then he turned and went his way.

The Lord spake unto Elijah, saying, "Get thee hence, and turn thee eastward, and hide thyself by the brook Cherith, which is before Jordan. Thou shalt drink of the brook, and I have commanded the ravens to feed thee there."

Elijah went. He found the brook — a small stream running over smooth stones. Every morning the ravens came with bread and flesh in their beaks. Every evening they came again. Black birds with shining feathers, bringing food unto a hungry man.

But the rain came not. The brook grew small. The water slowed unto a trickle. Then it dried up wholly. The stones lay bare and warm.

The Lord spake unto Elijah again. "Arise, get thee to Zarephath. I have commanded a widow woman there to sustain thee."

Elijah journeyed far. He was weary and athirst. When he came unto the gate of Zarephath, he beheld a woman gathering of sticks. She was thin. Her raiment was worn.

"Fetch me, I pray thee, a little water in a vessel, that I may drink," Elijah called unto her.

She turned to go fetch it. "Bring me, I pray thee, a morsel of bread also," he added.

The woman stayed her steps. She looked upon him with sorrowful eyes. "As the Lord thy God liveth," she said, "I have not a cake. I have but an handful of meal in a barrel and a little oil in a cruse. Behold, I am gathering two sticks, that I may go in and dress it for me and my son, that we may eat it, and die."

Elijah looked upon her with kindness. "Fear not," he said. "Go and do as thou hast said. But make me thereof a little cake first, and bring it unto me. After, make for thee and for thy son. For thus saith the Lord: the barrel of meal shall not waste, neither shall the cruse of oil fail, until the day that the Lord sendeth rain upon the earth."

The woman looked upon Elijah. She went home and did according to the saying of Elijah. She made a little cake and brought it unto him first. Then she made bread for herself and her son. They did eat.

The next morning, she went unto the barrel of meal. There was meal within. She took up the cruse of oil. Oil poured forth. Day after day, there was always enough.
