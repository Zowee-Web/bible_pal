The Passover had brought Jesus up to Jerusalem, and he was staying in a house in the city in the days after the cleansing of the temple. The lamps had been lit in the upper room where he was sitting; the courtyard outside was quiet under the night; and a man came up the stone steps from the street, walking carefully, not wishing to be seen by those who watched the entrance. He was a Pharisee, a ruler of the Jews, named Nicodemus. He came to the door of the upper room where Jesus was, and they let him in.

Now there was a man of the Pharisees named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews.

The same came to him by night, and said to him, "Rabbi, we know that you are a teacher come from God, for no one can do these signs that you do, unless God is with him."

Jesus answered him,

"Most certainly, I tell you, unless one is born anew,

he can't see God's Kingdom."

Nicodemus said to him, "How can a man be born when he is old? Can he enter a second time into his mother's womb, and be born?"

Jesus answered,

"Most certainly I tell you, unless one is born of water and spirit, he can't enter into God's Kingdom.

That which is born of the flesh is flesh. That which is born of the Spirit is spirit.

Don't marvel that I said to you, 'You must be born anew.'

The wind blows where it wants to, and you hear its sound, but don't know where it comes from and where it is going. So is everyone who is born of the Spirit."

And the door of the upper room closed again behind the ruler of the Jews as he went down the stone steps into the late hours of the night, with the words about the wind that blows where it wishes still moving in him on the way back to his own house.
