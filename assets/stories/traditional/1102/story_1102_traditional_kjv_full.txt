The multitude was gathered at the shore when Jesus returned from across the water. They had waited — upon the rocks, along the waterline. When the ship came to land, they pressed forward. Voices rose. Bodies drew near.

A man came pushing through. He moved with haste, his arms working against those about him. Jairus, a ruler of the synagogue — a man known in the town. But his face was gray, and his mouth set fast. He reached Jesus and fell down at his feet. His knees struck the wet sand.

His only daughter, about twelve years of age, lay dying. He besought him that he would come to his house.

Jesus went with him. The crowd followed, pressing on every side. They moved through the narrow way — bodies jostling, feet scraping upon stones. Jesus walked among them with Jairus beside him, and the ruler's eyes were fixed before him, his steps quick, his hands clenching and opening.

And a woman came behind him. She was thin and pale. She had had an issue of blood twelve years. She had spent all her living upon physicians, and none could heal her. She came behind him and touched the border of his garment.

Immediately her issue of blood was stanched. She felt it — the cessation, sudden and complete.

Jesus stood still and said, "Who touched me?"

Peter and they that were with him said, "Master, the multitude throng thee and press thee, and sayest thou, Who touched me?"

Jesus said, "Somebody hath touched me: for I perceive that virtue is gone out of me."

The crowd about him grew still. The woman, seeing she was not hid, came trembling. She fell down before him and declared unto him before all the people the cause for which she had touched him, and how she was healed immediately.

Her voice trembled, but she spake.

He said unto her, "Daughter, be of good comfort: thy faith hath made thee whole; go in peace."

Jairus stood near. His face had not changed. While these things were spoken, his daughter lay dying in the house behind them. The moments pressed upon him. He stood rigid.

While Jesus yet spake, there came one from the ruler's house. He was out of breath. He said, "Thy daughter is dead; trouble not the Master."

The words fell between them. Jairus stood still.

But Jesus turned and said unto him, "Fear not: believe only, and she shall be made whole."

And they went on to the house. The sound of mourning came before they drew near — women wailing, the beating of hands upon their breasts.

Jesus entered the house, suffering none to go in, save Peter, and James, and John, and the father and the mother of the maiden.

"Weep not," he said. "She is not dead, but sleepeth."

And they laughed him to scorn, knowing that she was dead.

But he put them all out, and took her by the hand, and called, saying, "Maid, arise."

And her spirit came again, and she arose straightway. She sat up, as one waking from sleep, and her eyes opened, and she looked about the room.

Her parents stood behind, their hands fast one within another. The room was still, save for the child's breathing.

And he commanded that something should be given her to eat.
