The crowd was already gathered at the shore when Jesus stepped from the boat. They had been waiting — sitting on the rocks, standing along the waterline. When the hull scraped against the sand, they moved forward. Voices rose. Bodies pressed close.

A man came pushing through from the back. He moved quickly, elbows working against the bodies on either side. Jairus, a ruler of the synagogue — a man of standing in the town. But his face was gray and his mouth set tight. He reached Jesus and fell at his feet. His knees hit the wet sand. He looked up.

"Come to my house," he said. His voice was strained. His only daughter — twelve years old — was dying.

Jesus went with him. The crowd came too, pressing from every side, bodies bumping and jostling in the narrow street that led up from the shore. The movement was slow. People stumbled. Jesus walked among them with Jairus at his side, the father's eyes fixed forward, his hands opening and closing.

Behind Jesus, a woman moved through the crowd. She was thin. She had been bleeding for twelve years — a constant sickness that had taken everything from her. She had spent all she had on physicians. None had helped. She reached forward through the press of bodies and touched the fringe of Jesus' garment with her fingertips.

Immediately the flow of blood stopped. She felt it — the cessation, sudden and complete.

Jesus stopped walking. The crowd pressed on, but he stood still. "Who touched me?" he said.

Peter gestured at the bodies around them. "Master, the multitudes press and crush you on every side. How can you ask who touched you?"

"Someone touched me," Jesus said. "I perceived that power went out from me."

The crowd grew quiet. The woman saw she could not remain hidden. She came forward trembling and fell down before Jesus. She spoke. She told him everything before all the people — why she had touched him, how long she had been sick, how she had been healed in that instant. Her voice shook.

Jesus looked down at her. "Daughter," he said, "your faith has made you well. Go in peace."

Jairus stood three steps away. His face had not changed. Through the whole exchange his daughter had been dying. The delay pressed on him.

While Jesus was still speaking, a man came running from the direction of Jairus' house. He was breathing hard. He reached Jairus and spoke. "Your daughter is dead. Do not trouble the Teacher any longer."

The words fell. Jairus stood rigid.

Jesus turned to him. "Do not be afraid," he said. His voice was quiet, for Jairus alone. "Only believe, and she will be made well."

They walked on. They reached the house. Mourners were already gathered outside — women weeping, men beating their chests. Jesus allowed no one to enter except Peter, John, James, and the child's father and mother.

"Do not weep," he said to those inside. "She is not dead, but sleeping."

The mourners laughed at him. They had seen the body. They knew she was dead.

Jesus crossed the room to where the girl lay on a low mat. She was still. Her face was pale. He knelt beside her and took her hand — small, cool, motionless in his.

"Child, arise," he said.

Her chest moved. Color returned to her face. Her eyes opened. She sat up — quickly, the way a child wakes from ordinary sleep. She looked around the room.

Her parents stood behind Jesus, their mouths open. The room was silent except for the girl's breathing.

"Give her something to eat," Jesus said.
