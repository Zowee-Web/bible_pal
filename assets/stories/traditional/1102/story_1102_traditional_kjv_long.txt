The multitude was already gathered at the shore when the ship appeared upon the lake. They had waited since morning — fishermen who had drawn up their nets upon the sand, women with their vessels upon the flat rocks, older men sitting in the shadow of an overturned hull. Children ran along the water's edge. When the ship drew near and the bow scraped upon the sand, the crowd moved forward as one body. Voices rose. Hands reached forth. They pressed toward Jesus as he stepped out upon the shore, the water still upon his sandals.

A man came pushing through from the back. He moved with urgency, his arms working against the press of bodies, not pausing when any spoke unto him. Jairus, a ruler of the synagogue — known throughout the town, a man of position. But his garment was wrinkled as one who hath not slept, and his face was gray. He reached the space before Jesus and fell down — his knees striking the wet sand, his hands upon the ground.

"Come to my house," he said. His voice was strained. His only daughter — about twelve years of age — lay dying. He besought him earnestly.

Jesus went with him. The multitude came also, pressing close on every side. They moved up from the shore into the narrow way that climbed toward the town. The street was crowded. Bodies pressed and shuffled. Sandals scraped the stone pavement. Jesus walked among them with Jairus at his side, and the ruler's eyes were fixed before him, his steps quick, his hands opening and closing.

Behind Jesus, deeper within the press of the crowd, a woman moved. She was thin — her garment hung loose at her shoulders. She had had an issue of blood twelve years. A constant affliction, draining her day by day. She had spent all her living upon physicians. She had gone to one and then another, and none could heal her. She was weaker now than she had been twelve years before, and poorer, and alone. She pressed through the crowd from behind, working her way near. She reached forth and touched the border of his garment with her fingertips.

Immediately her issue of blood stanched. She felt it — the cessation, sudden and complete. Twelve years of loss, and then stillness. Her hand withdrew.

Jesus stopped. The crowd about him kept pressing forward a moment, bodies moving against his stillness. He turned.

"Who touched me?" he said.

Peter was beside him. He looked upon the crowd — scores of bodies pressed together. "Master," he said, "the multitude throng thee and press thee, and sayest thou, Who touched me?"

"Somebody hath touched me," Jesus said. "I perceive that virtue is gone out of me."

The crowd grew still. People stepped back, opening a small space. The woman saw she was not hid. She had thought to touch him and withdraw unseen. But he had perceived it. She came forward trembling. Her face was drawn. She fell upon the ground before him and spoke.

She declared before all the people the cause for which she had touched him. How long she had been afflicted. How she had spent all she had. How she had come through the press of bodies and touched the border of his garment and been healed in that instant. Her voice trembled, but she spoke plainly.

Jesus looked upon her.

"Daughter," he said, "be of good comfort: thy faith hath made thee whole; go in peace."

The woman rose and withdrew into the crowd. The people made way for her.

Jairus stood near. His countenance had not changed. Through the whole of it — the stopping, the question, the woman's confession — his daughter had been dying. The minutes bore upon him. He stood rigid, his hands at his sides, his jaw set.

While Jesus yet spake unto the woman, one came running from the ruler's house. He came swiftly through the crowd. He was short of breath. He reached Jairus and spoke unto him.

"Thy daughter is dead," he said. "Trouble not the Master."

The words struck the air. Jairus was still. The crowd about them was silent.

Jesus turned unto Jairus. He spoke quietly, the words for him alone.

"Fear not," he said. "Believe only, and she shall be made whole."

They went forward. The crowd did not follow — only Jesus, Jairus, and three of the disciples: Peter, James, and John. They came to the house. The sound of mourning reached them before they turned the way — women wailing, voices lifted in grief, the beating of hands upon breasts. Mourners had gathered in the court.

Jesus entered the house, suffering none to come in save the three disciples and the maiden's father and mother. Within it was dim — oil lamps burning, the smell of spices. People filled the front room, weeping.

"Weep not," Jesus said. "She is not dead, but sleepeth."

The weeping ceased. Several of the mourners looked upon him. Then they laughed him to scorn. They had seen the child. They had felt for breath and found none.

Jesus turned from them and passed through the house to the inner room where the child lay. Her parents followed. Peter, James, and John stood behind them in the doorway.

The room was small. A single lamp burned upon a shelf. The maiden lay upon a low mat against the wall. She was still. Her face was pale, without color. Her hands lay at her sides. She was very small — twelve years of age, thin, her hair spread upon the mat beneath her head.

Jesus crossed the room and knelt beside her. He reached forth and took her hand. It was small and cool and still within his. He held it.

"Maid, arise," he said.

The room was silent. Then the child's breast moved. Breath came into her, then another. Color returned to her face — a faint warmth. Her eyes opened. She looked upon the face above her. Then she arose straightway — without hesitation, as one waking from sleep. She looked about the room at the faces before her.

Her mother made a sound. Her father stepped forward and stopped. The disciples in the doorway spoke not.

The maiden was alive. She sat upon the edge of the mat, her feet upon the floor, looking about with clear eyes. She breathed.

Jesus commanded that something should be given her to eat.
