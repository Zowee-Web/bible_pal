The people were waiting at the shore when Jesus returned. They pressed close about him — many together, voices rising.

A man came through the press. Jairus, a ruler of the synagogue. His face was drawn and gray. He fell down at the feet of Jesus, his knees upon the sand. He besought him to come to his house. His only daughter, about twelve years of age, lay dying.

Jesus went with him. The multitude followed, thronging him on every side.

A woman came behind him in the crowd. She had an issue of blood twelve years. She had spent all her living upon physicians, and none could heal her. She touched the border of his garment. Immediately her issue of blood stanched.

Jesus said, "Who touched me?"

Peter said, "Master, the multitude throng thee and press thee, and sayest thou, Who touched me?"

Jesus said, "Somebody hath touched me: for I perceive that virtue is gone out of me."

The woman, seeing she was not hid, came trembling. She fell down before him and declared before all the people why she had touched him, and how she was healed immediately.

He said unto her, "Daughter, be of good comfort: thy faith hath made thee whole; go in peace."

While he yet spake, there came one from the ruler's house. "Thy daughter is dead," he said. "Trouble not the Master."

Jesus turned to Jairus. "Fear not," he said. "Believe only, and she shall be made whole."

They came to the house. People wept and bewailed already. Jesus suffered none to go in save Peter, James, John, and the father and mother of the maiden.

"Weep not," he said. "She is not dead, but sleepeth."

They laughed him to scorn, knowing that she was dead.

He took her by the hand and called, saying, "Maid, arise."

Her spirit came again. She arose straightway. Her parents stood without moving, their mouths open. Jesus commanded that something should be given her to eat.
