This is what the Preacher said to the young men of his city, in the days when his own evening had come on.

He had watched many lives finish before him. He had walked the streets when the mourners had walked them, and he had returned to his own house when the lamps were lit. He had thought about what he would say to those who were still young.

This is what he said to them.

"Remember your Creator in the days of your youth, before the evil days come, and the years draw near, when you will say, 'I have no pleasure in them' —

before the sun and the light and the moon and the stars are darkened, and the clouds return after the rain.

In the day when the keepers of the house tremble, and the strong men bow themselves, and the grinders cease because they are few, and those who look out of the windows are darkened. When the doors of the street are shut; when the sound of the grinding is low, and one rises up at the voice of a bird, and all the daughters of music are brought low.

When they are afraid of heights, and terrors are on the way. When the almond tree blossoms, and the grasshopper is a burden, and desire fails — because man goes to his everlasting home, and the mourners go about the streets —

before the silver cord is severed, or the golden bowl broken; or the pitcher broken at the spring, or the wheel broken at the cistern.

Then the dust returns to the earth as it was, and the spirit returns to God who gave it."

The Preacher said it gently. He was not bringing them grief. He was giving them the years of their youth back into their own hands, with a name for what those years held.

The young men in his city listened. They were the ones who would walk the streets a long while yet, and remember the words on a morning that had not yet come for them.
