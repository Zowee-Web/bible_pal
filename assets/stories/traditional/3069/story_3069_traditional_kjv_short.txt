When Paul was come to Jerusalem, the brethren received him gladly. The day following he went with his company unto James, and all the elders were present. Paul saluted them and declared particularly what things God hath wrought among the Gentiles by his ministry. When they heard it, they glorified the Lord.

The elders then described the matter in Jerusalem. They said, “Thou seest, brother, how many thousands of Jews there are which believe,” and they were all zealous of the law. They had been informed that Paul taught the Jews among the Gentiles to forsake Moses, saying that they ought not to circumcise their children nor walk after the customs. The elders expected the multitude to hear that Paul had come.

They set forth a proposal. Four men among them had a vow. Paul should take them, purify himself with them, and pay their charges, that they might shave their heads. The elders said this would show that the reports concerning him were nothing and that he himself walked orderly and kept the law. Touching the Gentiles which believed, they repeated the judgment already written: they should keep themselves from things offered to idols, from blood, from strangled things, and from fornication.

Then Paul took the men. The next day he purified himself with them and entered into the temple, signifying the accomplishment of the days of purification until an offering should be offered for every one of them.

When the seven days were almost ended, Jews from Asia saw Paul in the temple. They stirred up all the people, laid hands on him, and cried for help. They accused him of teaching all men everywhere against the people, the law, and that place. They also charged him with bringing Greeks into the temple and polluting the holy place. They had seen Trophimus the Ephesian with Paul in the city and supposed that Paul had brought him into the temple.

All the city was moved, and the people ran together. They took Paul, drew him out of the temple, and forthwith the doors were shut. As they went about to kill him, tidings came unto the chief captain that all Jerusalem was in an uproar. He immediately took soldiers and centurions. When the people saw them, they left beating Paul.

The chief captain arrested Paul and commanded him to be bound with two chains. He demanded who Paul was and what he had done, but some cried one thing and some another. Unable to know the certainty for the tumult, he commanded Paul to be carried into the castle. At the stairs, the soldiers bare him because of the violence of the people, while the multitude followed after, crying, “Away with him!”
