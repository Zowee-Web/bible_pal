When Paul reached Jerusalem, the brothers received him gladly. The next day he went with his companions to James, where all the elders were present. Paul greeted them and reported in detail what God had done among the Gentiles through his ministry. When they heard it, they glorified God.

The elders then described the situation in Jerusalem. Many thousands of Jews believed, and they were zealous for the law. They had been told that Paul taught Jews living among the Gentiles to forsake Moses, not circumcise their children, and abandon the customs. The elders expected the crowd to hear that Paul had arrived.

They presented a proposal. Four men among them had taken a vow. Paul should join their purification, pay their expenses, and enable them to shave their heads. The elders said this would show that the reports about him were unfounded and that he himself also walked keeping the law. Concerning Gentile believers, they repeated the decision already written: they should keep themselves from food offered to idols, from blood, from strangled things, and from sexual immorality.

Paul took the men. The next day he purified himself with them and entered the temple, declaring the completion of the days of purification, when the offering would be made for each man.

As the seven days were nearly finished, Jews from Asia saw Paul in the temple. They stirred up the crowd, seized him, and cried for help. They accused him of teaching everywhere against the people, the law, and the temple. They also accused him of bringing Greeks into the temple and defiling the holy place. They had previously seen Trophimus the Ephesian with Paul in the city and supposed Paul had brought him inside.

The city was stirred, and the people ran together. They dragged Paul out of the temple, and its doors were immediately shut. While they were trying to kill him, word reached the Roman commander that all Jerusalem was in an uproar. He quickly brought soldiers and centurions. When the crowd saw them, they stopped beating Paul.

The commander arrested Paul and ordered him bound with two chains. He asked who Paul was and what he had done, but the crowd shouted different answers. Unable to learn the truth because of the tumult, he ordered Paul taken into the fortress. At the stairs, the soldiers carried him because of the crowd’s violence, while the multitude followed, crying, “Away with him!”
