Then Yahweh said to Noah, "Come, you and all your household, into the ark. For in this generation, I have seen that you are righteous before me."

The instructions were exact. Seven pairs of every clean animal, the male and his female. A single pair of every animal that was not clean. Seven pairs of the birds of the sky, to keep seed alive on the face of the earth. "In seven days," Yahweh said, "I will cause it to rain on the earth forty days and forty nights, and every living thing I have made, I will destroy from the surface of the ground."

Noah did everything that Yahweh commanded him.

He was six hundred years old when the flood came. He went into the ark with his wife, his sons, and his sons' wives, and the animals came to him two by two, male and female — clean and unclean, birds and everything that creeps on the ground — just as God had commanded.

Then Yahweh shut him in.

After the seven days, on the seventeenth day of the second month, all the fountains of the great deep burst open, and the windows of the sky were opened. Rain fell on the earth forty days and forty nights.

The waters rose and lifted the ark, and it was carried up above the earth. Still the waters rose, and the ark floated on their surface. At last every high mountain under the whole sky was covered, and the water stood fifteen cubits above them.

And everything that moved on the earth died — birds, livestock, wild animals, every creeping thing, and every man. Every living thing was destroyed from the face of the ground. Only Noah was left, and those who were with him in the ark.

For one hundred fifty days, the waters covered the earth.

Then God remembered Noah, and every living thing that was with him in the ark. God made a wind pass over the earth, and the waters began to go down. The fountains of the deep were stopped, the windows of the sky were closed, and the rain was held back. In the seventh month the ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat.

Little by little the waters kept sinking, until the tops of the mountains could be seen.

Noah opened the window he had made and sent out a raven, and it went back and forth until the waters were dried up from the earth. Then he sent out a dove, to see if the waters had gone from the ground. But the dove found no place to rest her foot, and she returned to him, for water still covered the whole earth. He put out his hand, and took her, and brought her in to himself in the ark.

He waited seven days and sent her out again. This time she came back to him at evening, and in her mouth was a freshly plucked olive leaf. Then Noah knew that the waters had gone down from the earth. He waited seven more days and sent her out once more, and she did not return to him again.

So Noah removed the covering of the ark and looked, and the surface of the ground was dry.

God spoke to Noah. "Go out of the ark — you, your wife, your sons, and your sons' wives with you. Bring out every living thing that is with you, of all flesh, that they may breed abundantly in the earth, and be fruitful, and multiply."

Noah went out, and his sons, and his wife, and his sons' wives with him. Every animal, every creeping thing, and every bird went out of the ark by their families.

Then Noah built an altar to Yahweh. He took of every clean animal and every clean bird, and offered burnt offerings on it. Yahweh smelled the pleasing aroma, and said in his heart that he would not curse the ground again for man's sake, though the imagination of man's heart is evil from his youth — that he would never again strike every living thing, as he had done. "While the earth remains — seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night — they will not cease."
