Yahweh said to Noah, "Come with all your household into the ark, for I have seen that you are righteous before me in this generation."

Noah was to take seven pairs of every clean animal, a pair of every animal that was not clean, and birds of the sky by sevens, to keep seed alive on the earth. In seven days, Yahweh said, rain would fall for forty days and forty nights, and every living thing he had made would be destroyed from the ground.

Noah did everything that Yahweh commanded him. He went into the ark with his wife, his sons, and his sons' wives, and the animals came to him two by two, male and female, as God had commanded.

Then Yahweh shut him in.

After the seven days, all the fountains of the great deep burst open, and the windows of the sky were opened. Rain fell forty days and forty nights. The waters rose and lifted the ark, and it floated on the surface of the waters, until every high mountain under the whole sky was covered.

Everything that moved on the earth died. Only Noah was left, and those who were with him in the ark. For one hundred fifty days, the waters covered the earth.

Then God remembered Noah, and every living thing with him. He made a wind pass over the earth, and the waters began to go down, and the ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat.

Noah sent out a raven, and it went back and forth. He sent out a dove, but she found no place to rest her foot and returned to him. He put out his hand, and took her, and brought her in. Seven days later he sent her out again, and at evening she came back to him with a freshly plucked olive leaf in her mouth. Seven days more, and she did not return at all.

Then Noah removed the covering of the ark and looked, and the ground was dry. God said, "Go out of the ark, you and all your household, and bring out every living thing with you, that they may be fruitful and multiply on the earth."

So Noah went out, with his family and every animal by its families. He built an altar to Yahweh and offered burnt offerings on it. Yahweh smelled the pleasing aroma, and said in his heart that he would never again strike every living thing, as he had done. "While the earth remains — seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night — they will not cease."
