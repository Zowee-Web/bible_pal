After the rebellion of Korah had been put down in the wilderness, the children of Israel grumbled against Moses and against Aaron. The Lord said to Moses, "Speak to the children of Israel, and take of them rods, one for each fathers' house, of all their princes according to their fathers' houses, twelve rods. Write each man's name on his rod."

The Lord said, "You shall write Aaron's name on the rod of Levi, for there shall be one rod for each head of their fathers' houses. You shall lay them up in the Tent of Meeting before the testimony, where I meet with you. It shall happen that the rod of the man whom I shall choose shall bud."

Moses spoke to the children of Israel, and all their princes gave him rods, for each prince one, according to their fathers' houses, twelve rods. Aaron's rod was among their rods.

Moses laid up the rods before the Lord in the Tent of the Testimony. The lamps of the tabernacle burned along the walls, and the rods rested upon the ground before the ark of the covenant.

On the next day, Moses went into the Tent of the Testimony. Behold, the rod of Aaron for the house of Levi had budded, and put forth buds, and produced blossoms, and bore ripe almonds.

Moses brought out all the rods from before the Lord to all the children of Israel. They looked, and each man took his rod. The rods of the other princes were dry as they had been, but Aaron's rod was alive, with buds and blossoms and almonds upon it.

The Lord said to Moses, "Put back the rod of Aaron before the testimony, to be kept for a token against the children of rebellion. You shall make an end of their complaining against me, that they may not die."

Moses did so. As the Lord commanded him, so he did. Aaron's rod was set back before the testimony, and the buds remained upon it.

The morning was clear over the camp of Israel. The people walked among their tents, and they did not grumble against Moses and Aaron that day.
