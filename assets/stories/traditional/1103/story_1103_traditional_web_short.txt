King Herod stretched out his hand against the church. He killed James the brother of John with the sword. When he saw it pleased the Jewish leaders, he arrested Peter. This was during the days of Unleavened Bread. He put Peter in prison — four squads of soldiers to guard him — intending to bring him before the people after the Passover.

The church prayed earnestly to God for him.

The night before Herod planned to bring him out, Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains. Sentries stood at the door.

An angel of the Lord appeared. Light shone in the cell. The angel struck Peter on the side and woke him. "Get up quickly," the angel said.

The chains fell off his hands.

"Dress yourself and put on your sandals." Peter did so. "Wrap your cloak around you and follow me."

Peter followed the angel out. He did not know it was real. He thought he was seeing a vision. They passed the first guard post. They passed the second. They came to the iron gate that led into the city. It opened by itself. They walked the length of one street, and the angel departed.

Peter stood alone in the dark. He came to himself. "Now I truly know that the Lord has sent his angel and rescued me from the hand of Herod."

He went to the house of Mary, the mother of John Mark. Many were gathered there, praying. He knocked at the outer gate. A servant girl named Rhoda came to answer. She recognized his voice and was so overjoyed she ran back inside without opening. "Peter is at the gate!" she said.

"You are out of your mind," they told her.

Peter kept knocking. They opened the gate and saw him. They were astonished. He motioned for silence and told them how the Lord had brought him out. "Tell these things to James and the brothers," he said. Then he departed.

When morning came, there was no small disturbance among the soldiers about what had become of Peter.
