King Herod stretched out his hand to harm certain members of the church. He killed James the brother of John with the sword — a swift, public execution. When he saw it pleased the Jewish leaders, he arrested Peter as well. This was during the Feast of Unleavened Bread.

He put Peter in prison. Four squads of soldiers — sixteen men — assigned to guard one prisoner. His plan was to bring Peter out for public trial after the Passover.

Peter was kept in prison. But the church prayed for him earnestly, without ceasing.

The night before Herod intended to bring him out, Peter was sleeping. He lay between two soldiers, a chain binding his left wrist to one and his right wrist to the other. Two sentries stood guard before the door. The cell was dark, the stone floor cold. Peter slept.

An angel of the Lord appeared. Light shone in the cell — sudden and bright, filling the small space. The soldiers did not stir. The angel struck Peter on the side. "Get up quickly," he said.

The chains fell from Peter's wrists. They struck the stone floor. The soldiers on either side did not wake.

Peter sat up, blinking.

"Dress yourself," the angel said. "Put on your sandals."

Peter reached for his sandals and strapped them on.

"Wrap your cloak around you and follow me."

Peter pulled his cloak over his shoulders and followed the angel out of the cell. He moved past the sleeping soldiers, through the open door, into the corridor beyond. He did not understand what was happening. He thought he was seeing a vision — a dream from which he would wake again in chains.

They passed the first guard post. The guards did not see them. They passed the second. The guards stood at their positions, eyes open, and did not turn. They came to the iron gate that led out into the city street. It opened by itself — no hand touching it, no mechanism turning. It swung wide.

They stepped through into the night air. The street was empty and dark. They walked the length of one block together. Then the angel was gone. One moment beside him, the next moment not.

Peter stood alone. The night was quiet. He looked down at his hands — free of chains. He touched the cloak on his shoulders. He felt his sandals on the ground. This was real.

"Now I truly know," he said, "that the Lord has sent his angel and rescued me from the hand of Herod and from all that the Jewish people were expecting."

He went to the house of Mary, the mother of John Mark. Many were gathered there, praying through the night. He reached the outer gate and knocked. The sound was loud in the quiet street.

A servant girl named Rhoda came to answer. She did not open the gate. She listened. She recognized his voice. Joy seized her so suddenly that she turned and ran back inside without opening. "Peter is at the gate!" she said, breathless.

They stared at her. "You are out of your mind," they said.

She kept insisting. They said, "It is his angel."

Outside, Peter kept knocking — steady, patient, his knuckles against the wood.

They opened the gate. Peter stood there in the faint lamplight, alive and free. They were astonished. He raised his hand for silence. He told them — the angel, the light, the chains falling, the guards who did not see, the iron gate opening on its own. When he finished, he said, "Tell these things to James and to the brothers." Then he left.

When morning came, there was no small disturbance among the soldiers. The cell was empty. The chains lay on the floor. Peter was gone.
