Now about that time Herod the king stretched forth his hands to vex certain of the church. He had slain James the brother of John with the sword — a public execution. Because he saw it pleased the Jews, he proceeded to take Peter also. These were the days of unleavened bread.

He apprehended Peter and put him in prison, delivering him to four quaternions of soldiers to keep him. Sixteen men to guard one prisoner. He intended after the Passover to bring him forth unto the people.

Peter therefore was kept in prison. But prayer was made without ceasing of the church unto God for him, day and night.

The night came — the last night before Herod would have brought him forth. Peter was sleeping. He lay upon the cold stone between two soldiers. A chain bound his left hand to one, another bound his right hand to the other. He could not move without pulling against them. The keepers stood before the door, facing outward. The cell was dark save for torchlight from beneath the door.

Peter slept. His breathing was even.

And behold, the angel of the Lord came upon him, and a light shined in the prison — sudden and white, filling the space. The soldiers stirred not. The angel smote Peter on his side and raised him up.

"Arise up quickly," he said.

His chains fell off from his hands. Both at once. The iron struck the stone. The soldiers slept on.

Peter sat up, blinking.

"Gird thyself," the angel said, "and bind on thy sandals."

Peter reached for his sandals and bound them on.

"Cast thy garment about thee, and follow me."

Peter took his cloak and wrapped it about his shoulders. He followed the angel past the sleeping soldiers, through the open door. He wist not that it was true — he thought he saw a vision.

They passed the first ward. The guards perceived nothing. They passed the second ward. The same. They came unto the iron gate that leadeth unto the city. It was heavy and dark. No hand touched it.

The gate opened to them of his own accord. It swung upon its hinges and stood wide. The street lay empty and dark beyond.

They went out and passed through one street together, their sandals soft upon the stones. Buildings rose close on either side — doors shut, windows dark.

And forthwith the angel departed from him. One moment there. The next, gone.

Peter stood alone in the dark. He looked upon his wrists — raw from the iron, but free. He felt the cloak about him. This was real.

He came to himself. "Now I know of a surety," he said, "that the Lord hath sent his angel, and hath delivered me out of the hand of Herod, and from all the expectation of the people of the Jews."

He went to the house of Mary the mother of John Mark, where many were gathered together praying. He knocked at the gate. The sound carried in the quiet.

A damsel came to hearken, named Rhoda. She opened not — she listened through the wood. When she knew Peter's voice, she opened not the gate for gladness. She ran back within. "Peter standeth before the gate!" she cried.

They looked upon her. "Thou art mad," they said.

She constantly affirmed it. "It is his angel," they said.

Without, Peter continued knocking — steady, patient. When they opened the door and saw him, they were astonished. Peter beckoned unto them with his hand to hold their peace. He declared how the Lord had brought him out — the angel, the chains, the gate. "Go show these things unto James, and to the brethren," he said. Then he departed.

As soon as it was day, there was no small stir among the soldiers, what was become of Peter.
