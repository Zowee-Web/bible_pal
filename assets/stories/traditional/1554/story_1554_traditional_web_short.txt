She rises while it is yet night.

The fire in the hearth has burned down to coals. She kneels and feeds it from the wood she set aside the evening before, and the small flame catches, and the room begins to warm. The children are still asleep. Her husband turns under the blanket but does not wake. The lamp she lights from the new fire will not go out again until the work of the day is done.

She gives food to her household. She gives portions to her servant girls. By the time the sun has come up over the wall of the courtyard, the bread is already on the table.

She works wool and flax with her own hands. She seeks out what she needs. She considers a field, and buys it, and with the fruit of her hands she plants a vineyard. She arms her waist with strength. She perceives that her merchandise is good. Her lamp does not go out by night.

She stretches out her hand to the poor. She reaches out her hands to the needy. She is not afraid of the snow for her household, because she has clothed them all in scarlet. She makes coverings of tapestry. She makes linen garments and sells them. She delivers sashes to the merchant.

Strength and dignity are her clothing. She laughs at the time to come.

She opens her mouth with wisdom, and the teaching of kindness is on her tongue. She looks well to the ways of her household. She does not eat the bread of idleness.

Her children rise up and call her blessed. Her husband also praises her. Many women have done worthily, he says, but you excel them all.

Charm is deceitful, and beauty is vain. But a woman who fears the Lord — she shall be praised. Give her of the fruit of her hands, and let her own works praise her in the gates.

And so it was set down in the writing of the wise, in the days when the king's mother spoke to her son, that he might know, when he saw such a woman, what he was looking at.
