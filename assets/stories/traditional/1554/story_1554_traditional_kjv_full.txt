She riseth while it is yet night.

The fire in the hearth hath burned down to coals. She kneeleth upon the packed earth of the kitchen and feedeth the coals from the wood she had laid aside the evening before, and the small flame catcheth, and the room beginneth to warm. The children are yet asleep in the inner room. Her husband turneth under the blanket but waketh not. The candle she lighteth from the new fire shall not go out again until the work of the day is done.

By the time the first gray light cometh over the wall of the courtyard, she hath given meat to her household. She hath given a portion to her maidens. The bread is upon the table. The children come out one by one, rubbing their eyes, and sit down to eat. She seeth to it that each one hath what they need before she sitteth down herself.

The day's labour beginneth.

She seeketh wool, and flax, and worketh willingly with her hands. She is like the merchants' ships — she bringeth her food from afar. She considereth a field, and weigheth the cost, and buyeth it. With the fruit of her hands she planteth a vineyard. She girdeth her loins with strength. She strengtheneth her arms. She perceiveth that her merchandise is good. Her candle goeth not out by night.

She layeth her hands to the spindle. Her hands hold the distaff. The thread groweth long across the morning.

When a beggar cometh to the door, she turneth him not away. She stretcheth out her hand to the poor. She reacheth forth her hands to the needy. She weigheth not whether they have deserved what she giveth. She giveth. She is not afraid of the snow for her household, for she hath clothed them all with scarlet against the cold to come. She maketh herself coverings of tapestry for the beds. Her own clothing is silk and purple.

Her husband is known in the gates, when he sitteth among the elders of the land. They know him because of who cometh home to him at the end of the day, and what waiteth for him when he getteth there. She maketh fine linen and selleth it in the market. She delivereth girdles unto the merchant. The household liveth by her hands.

Strength and honour are her clothing. She shall rejoice in time to come.

She openeth her mouth with wisdom, and in her tongue is the law of kindness. She raiseth not her voice with the children. She rebuketh not the maidens before others. She looketh well to the ways of her household. She eateth not the bread of idleness.

The candle burneth late.

When the sons of the house grow up, they remember her at night more than they remember her in the day. They remember the candle burning when they came in late from the field. They remember the bread waiting upon the table when there was no cause to expect it. They arise up and call her blessed. Her husband, when he speaketh of her in the gates, praiseth her. "Many daughters have done virtuously," he saith, "but thou excellest them all."

Favour is deceitful, the writing saith. Beauty is vain. But a woman that feareth the Lord — she shall be praised.

Give her of the fruit of her hands. Let her own works praise her in the gates.

And so it was set down in the writing of the wise, in the days when the king's mother spake unto her son, that he might know, when he saw such a woman in his life, what he was looking upon.
