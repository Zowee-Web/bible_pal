David the king went in.

He went into the tent where the ark of the covenant of the Lord his God was kept. He put off his sandals at the door. He sat down upon the floor before the Lord.

He had been a shepherd. He had been a fugitive. He had been a king. Now was he a man with a promise which he had not asked for — that the Lord would build him an house that should not fall.

He sat there a long while. Then he began to speak.

He said —

"Who am I, O Lord God? And what is my house, that thou hast brought me hitherto?

And this was yet a small thing in thy sight, O Lord God; but thou hast spoken also of thy servant's house for a great while to come.

And what can David say more unto thee? for thou, Lord God, knowest thy servant.

For thy word's sake, and according to thine own heart, hast thou wrought all this greatness, to make thy servant know it.

Wherefore thou art great, O Lord God: for there is none like thee, neither is there any God beside thee, according to all that we have heard with our ears.

And what one nation in the earth is like thy people, even like Israel, whom God went to redeem for a people unto himself? Thou hast confirmed to thyself thy people Israel to be a people unto thee for ever: and thou, Lord, art become their God.

And now, O Lord God, the word that thou hast spoken concerning thy servant, and concerning his house, establish it for ever, and do as thou hast said. And let thy name be magnified for ever — let it be said, 'The Lord of hosts is the God over Israel.'

For thou, O Lord God, art God, and thy words be true, and thou hast promised this goodness unto thy servant.

Therefore now let it please thee to bless the house of thy servant, that it may continue for ever before thee: for thou, O Lord God, hast spoken it. And with thy blessing let the house of thy servant be blessed for ever."

David sat there in the tent yet a while after he had made an end.

Then he arose and went forth, unto the work of the day.
