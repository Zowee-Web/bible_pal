David sang it in a day when nothing answered him. The trouble was long, the enemy was nigh, and the face of God seemed turned away. He had no song that would form, and that which he had was the old question that had been asked many times before him by men whose songs were kept in the same gathering of psalms. And David lifted up his voice, and said:

How long wilt thou forget me, O Lord?
for ever?
how long wilt thou hide thy face from me?

How long shall I take counsel in my soul,
having sorrow in my heart daily?
how long shall mine enemy be exalted over me?

Consider and hear me, O Lord my God:
lighten mine eyes, lest I sleep the sleep of death;

Lest mine enemy say, "I have prevailed against him";
and those that trouble me rejoice when I am moved.

But I have trusted in thy mercy.
My heart shall rejoice in thy salvation.

I will sing unto the Lord,
because he hath dealt bountifully with me.

And the song was set down. The day was not changed for him at that hour, and the enemy did not yet withdraw, and the answer came not back from the heavens in the words that he had asked for. But the song had turned at the end, and the trust had been spoken, and the singing had been put into the air; and the psalm was kept among the songs of Israel for the days when others should have to ask the same things. And so it was in the days when David sang the thirteenth psalm.
