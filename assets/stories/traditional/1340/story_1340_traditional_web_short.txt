David sang it in a day when nothing answered him. The trouble was long, the enemy was near, and the face of God seemed turned away. He had no song that would form, and what he had was the old question that had been asked many times before him by men whose songs were kept in the same gathering of psalms. And David lifted up his voice and said:

How long, Yahweh?
Will you forget me forever?
How long will you hide your face from me?

How long shall I take counsel in my soul,
having sorrow in my heart every day?
How long shall my enemy triumph over me?

Behold, and answer me, Yahweh, my God.
Give light to my eyes, lest I sleep in death;

Lest my enemy say, "I have prevailed against him";
Lest my adversaries rejoice when I fall.

But I trust in your loving kindness.
My heart rejoices in your salvation.

I will sing to Yahweh,
because he has been good to me.

And the song was set down. The day did not change for him at that hour, and the enemy did not yet withdraw, and the answer did not come back from the heavens in the words that he had asked for. But the song had turned at the end, and the trust had been spoken, and the singing had been put into the air; and the psalm was kept among the songs of Israel for the days when others would have to ask the same things. And so it was in the days when David sang the thirteenth psalm.
