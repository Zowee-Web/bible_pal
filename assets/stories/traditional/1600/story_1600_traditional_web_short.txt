King Solomon loved many foreign women — the daughter of Pharaoh, and women of Moab, Ammon, Edom, Sidon, and the Hittites. These were nations Yahweh had spoken of to Israel: "You shall not intermarry with them, for they will surely turn your heart after their gods." Solomon clung to them in love. He had seven hundred wives, who were princesses, and three hundred concubines; and his wives turned his heart.

When Solomon was old, his wives turned his heart after other gods, and his heart was no longer wholly true to Yahweh his God, as the heart of David his father had been. He went after Ashtoreth the goddess of the Sidonians and after Milcom the abomination of the Ammonites. He did what was evil in the sight of Yahweh and did not follow him fully, as David had. On the mountain east of Jerusalem he built a high place for Chemosh the abomination of Moab, and for Molech the abomination of the Ammonites, and he did the same for all his foreign wives, who burned incense and sacrificed to their gods.

Yahweh was angry with Solomon, because his heart had turned away from the God of Israel, who had appeared to him twice and had commanded him not to go after other gods. Solomon had not kept what Yahweh commanded. So Yahweh said to him, "Because you have done this, and have not kept my covenant and my statutes, I will surely tear the kingdom away from you and give it to your servant. Yet for the sake of David your father I will not do it in your lifetime; I will tear it out of the hand of your son. Even so, I will not tear away the whole kingdom. I will give one tribe to your son, for the sake of David my servant, and for the sake of Jerusalem, which I have chosen."
