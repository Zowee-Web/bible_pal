Solomon was king in Jerusalem, and Solomon loved many strange women. He took the daughter of Pharaoh. He took women of Moab and of Ammon, of Edom and of Zidon, and of the Hittites.

Concerning those nations the Lord had spoken unto the children of Israel: Ye shall not go in to them, neither shall they come in unto you; for surely they will turn away your heart after their gods. Solomon clave unto these women in love.

Seven hundred wives he had, and they were princesses. Three hundred concubines he had beside them. And his wives turned away his heart.

Solomon grew old. His heart went after other gods, and it was no longer perfect with the Lord his God, as the heart of David his father had been. He went after Ashtoreth, the goddess of the Zidonians. He went after Milcom, the abomination of the Ammonites. He did evil in the sight of the Lord, and he followed not fully after the Lord, as David had done.

Then Solomon built. On the hill that is before Jerusalem he raised an high place for Chemosh, the abomination of Moab, and another for Molech, the abomination of the children of Ammon. So did he for every one of his strange wives. They burnt incense upon those hills, and they sacrificed there unto their gods.

And the Lord was angry with Solomon, because his heart was turned from the God of Israel. Twice the Lord had appeared unto him, and had commanded him concerning this very thing, that he should not go after other gods. Solomon kept not that which the Lord commanded.

Wherefore the Lord said unto him: Forasmuch as this is done of thee, and thou hast not kept my covenant and my statutes, I will surely rend the kingdom from thee, and will give it to thy servant.

And the Lord said further: Notwithstanding in thy days I will not do it, for David thy father's sake; but I will rend it out of the hand of thy son. Howbeit I will not rend away all the kingdom. One tribe will I give unto thy son, for David my servant's sake, and for Jerusalem's sake, which I have chosen.
