Night fell upon the Sea of Galilee, and a restless wind swept across the water. The sky was deep and black, the whole lake shimmering with the uncertain glow of distant moonlight. The disciples moved about in a small ship, its wood creaking with every shift of the waves. After a long day, straightway Jesus had constrained them to go before him and pass over unto the other side, while he himself sent the multitudes away. The dim outline of the shore faded behind them as they set forth, and soon the ship was far from the land—compassed about by the wide, shifting dark.

The disciples laboured at the oars, their sinews straining as the wind pressed hard against them. They were no strangers to the water, but this night it seemed restless, the waves smiting against the hull and rocking the ship. Their breath came swift, their hearts tight from the toil. Every few moments, one of them glanced back toward the shore, hoping for a change in wind or a glimpse of morning. None came.

Wave after wave smote the sides as the night wore on. The ship moved slowly, driven by the disciples' weary hands and buffeted by the gusting wind. After hours of striving, it was made plain that they were not making much progress. With every splash, fear pressed deeper into their bones. Their hands were wet and cold. Each man kept his cloak drawn close about him.

In the fourth watch of the night, when sleep is deepest and the world is at its darkest, something troubled the face of the water. Their eyes squinted through the gloom. At the first, it was only a shape—pale and moving across the sea. Then a figure, walking upon the waves.

The disciples were stricken still, their hearts pounding in the stillness betwixt bursts of wind. Every sense was sharpened with alarm. The figure did not sink beneath the surface but moved steadily atop it, nigher and nigher. In the shadows, their faces turned white with fear. Whispered questions passed betwixt them, ragged and desperate.

"It is a spirit!" cried out one. Panic ran through the company. Each disciple gripped the edge of the ship, holding on as if to anchor himself against the impossible sight. Some shielded their eyes, unable to perceive what drew near in the darkness.

The figure drew nigher, his face revealed in the shifting glow of moonlight. Then a voice rose above the wind—a voice they knew, calm and unwavering. "Be of good cheer; it is I; be not afraid." The sound cut through the storm in a way nothing else could.

The disciples gazed, breathless. The outline upon the water was steady now, unmistakable. Relief strove with disbelief; could it indeed be Jesus himself? Fear yet wound tight within them, for men do not walk upon the sea.

Peter, ever quick to speak and to act, called out across the waves, uncertain but desperate for assurance. "Lord, if it be thou, bid me come unto thee on the water." He waited, his eyes fixed upon the figure that stood upright among the white-capped waves.

A single word came back, simple and sure. "Come."

Peter drew in a trembling breath. He lifted one leg over the side, his hand fast upon the edge of the ship. The wind plucked at his garments; cold water sprayed upon his feet. He let go, lowered himself unto the sea, and to the amazement of all, he stood.

Step by step, Peter walked upon the heaving surface. He fixed his gaze upon Jesus, his heartbeat roaring in his ears. Every foot fell upon the water without sinking, and for a moment the water held him as solid ground. The others watched, silent, scarce believing their eyes.

But the storm had not ceased. Waves leaped about him, and the wind roared louder. Peter, feeling the fury of the storm, turned his attention from Jesus unto the chaos swirling round about. The cold spray, the shrieking gale, the darkness pressing at his sides—suddenly the fear before, churning at the edge of his thoughts, rushed upon him. Doubt surged.

In an instant, Peter began to sink. The sea pulled at his legs, the water rising past his knees and up unto his waist. The chill was sharp and sudden, and his hands flailed for something, anything, to grasp. "Lord, save me!" he cried, his voice breaking over the wind.

And immediately Jesus stretched forth his hand. The dark water surged and shook, but his hand was strong and certain, closing upon Peter's. Jesus lifted him up out of the water, steady and unyielding. The wind plucked hard, but Jesus' grip did not falter.

He spake, his tone both gentle and searching: "O thou of little faith, wherefore didst thou doubt?" His words settled in the air, lingering as Peter found footing once again.

Together, with the storm yet twisting about them, Jesus and Peter climbed into the ship. The disciples drew them inside, their hands trembling from the cold and from the miracle they had just beheld.

So soon as Jesus was come into the ship, the wind ceased. The fierce shriek faded into a restless sigh. The water was calmed beneath them, and the ship found steady ground upon the waves. The disciples sat in stunned silence, their hearts pounding with the dawning of awe.

They looked upon Jesus—every eye drawn unto him, every mouth opened in wonder. The edges of their fear melted away in the quiet after the storm. Their cold hands unclenched from the sides of the ship; their shoulders fell in relief.

Then they that were in the ship came and worshipped him, saying, "Of a truth thou art the Son of God."
