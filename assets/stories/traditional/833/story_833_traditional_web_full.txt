Night had fallen over the Sea of Galilee. The disciples sat together in a wooden boat, its sides slick with the spray of restless water. Jesus had urged them to get in and go ahead to the other side, while he stayed behind to send the crowds away. Darkness pressed down on them as they rowed farther from the land. Overhead, the sky held only a sliver of moon between sweeping clouds. Wind whipped across the water, slapping the sails and pushing waves hard against the hull.

The lake stretched in every direction, its surface broken by churning foam and the rhythm of the disciples’ oars. Their arms ached. Water splashed up, biting at skin and eyes. Each time the wind howled stronger, their small boat pitched and rocked. They looked back toward shore, but land had faded into blackness. The world felt narrowed to wood, water, and the relentless wind.

Still, they pressed on. The sky shifted as the night grew deep. Some kept watch, while others strained at the oars. Their voices were low, anxious — sharing worries about the wind, how far they still had to go, and what might come next. No one slept. Exhaustion hovered at the edges of their thoughts.

Sometime between three and six in the morning, with night at its thickest, something changed. At first, only one disciple spotted it: a shape, faint and out of place, moving over the water’s surface. He blinked, wiped spray from his face, then saw it again. In a moment, others looked too. The shape became a man — walking, not swimming, not sinking. Terror swept over them like the wind.

“It’s a ghost!” someone shouted. Voices rose, thin and shaking, carried away by the gusts and the slap of waves. They drew back from the edge of the boat. Some clung to the sides. Fear gripped every heart; this could not be possible.

Then a voice called out to them, clear and strong, cutting through the wind. “Take heart! It is I! Don’t be afraid!”

They knew that voice. Even as fear twisted in their chests, hope sparked. Peter, bold and uncertain, stood and called into the darkness, “Lord, if it is you, command me to come to you on the water.”

From across the heaving waves, Jesus answered him, “Come.”

Peter stepped to the edge. The wind still roared, but he swung his legs over the side, feet dangling above swirling water. One breath, then another. He let go of the boat. Then his foot met water — and held. It did not give way.

Eyes fixed on Jesus, Peter took a step. Then another. Water shifted under him, cool and slick, but he did not sink. The wind battered him, pulling at his tunic and hair, but for a moment, the impossible became real. He walked forward, closing the distance between himself and Jesus, each step more astonishing than the last.

But the wind roared again, fiercer, screaming over the waves and flinging spray into his face. Fear spiked in Peter’s heart. He looked away from Jesus — at the wild whitecaps, at the dark beneath his feet — and there, the miracle faltered. His feet slid. The water gave way. He began to sink.

“Lord, save me!” Peter cried out, water surging up around his legs and chest.

Immediately, Jesus reached out a hand and grasped him. The grip was strong, living, unyielding against the sliding pull of the deep. Jesus held Peter above the water. His gaze was steady and unwavering.

“You of little faith,” Jesus said, “why did you doubt?”

Still holding Peter’s hand, Jesus led him back toward the boat. The wind thrashed and waves pressed in, but then — as soon as they stepped into the boat — the wind stopped. The air calmed. The water stilled beneath them, silent as glass. The storm was over.

The other disciples, stunned and breathless, saw all this. They looked from Peter, dripping and shaken, to Jesus, whose presence filled the boat with quiet power. Awe and wonder overcame their fear. Together, they knelt and worshiped him, voices echoing softly over the now peaceful waters. Those who were in the boat came and worshiped him, saying, “You are truly the Son of God!”
