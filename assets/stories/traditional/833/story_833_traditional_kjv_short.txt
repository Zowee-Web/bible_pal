After the multitudes were dispersed, straightway Jesus constrained his disciples to get into a ship, and to go before him unto the other side, while he stayed behind to send the people away. Evening was come. Darkness settled upon the Sea of Galilee. The disciples' ship drifted far from land, the wind whipping up the water. Waves smote against the sides. The disciples strained at the oars, the air filled with the sound of the wind and the crashing of water against wood.

Night pressed in, heavy and unrelenting. The ship creaked, tossed by the rough waves. Their faces were weary and full of fear, their eyes fixed upon the shifting horizon, searching for land, for help, for Jesus. But he was not with them.

In the fourth watch of the night, when the sky was deepest black, Jesus went unto them, walking on the sea. His footsteps were sure, moving over the swirling waves. The disciples saw a figure coming and were seized with fear. Shadows danced upon the water, and their hearts pounded.

A cry burst forth from the ship. "It is a spirit!" they said, with trembling voices. Panic filled the small vessel. The wind howled, the cold spray stinging their faces. But straightway Jesus spake over the roar of wind and water.

"Be of good cheer; it is I; be not afraid."

His voice carried above the storm. Peter answered him, hope and uncertainty mingling in his words. "Lord, if it be thou, bid me come unto thee on the water."

Jesus answered simply, "Come."

And when Peter was come down out of the ship, he walked on the water, to go to Jesus. The water splashed about his ankles, then his calves. He walked upon the shifting surface, moving toward Jesus. The wind howled, plucking at his garments. Every step drew him nigher, the savour of fear mingling with faith.

But when he saw the wind boisterous, Peter's eyes turned from Jesus unto the storm. He was afraid; and beginning to sink, he cried, saying, "Lord, save me!"

And immediately Jesus stretched forth his hand, and caught him. Wet fingers closed upon Peter's arm, holding him fast. "O thou of little faith," Jesus said unto him, "wherefore didst thou doubt?"

Together, they climbed into the ship, water dripping from their garments. The wind ceased. The sea grew calm, sudden and entire. Silence followed, broken only by the sound of breathing and the gentle lapping of water against the hull.

Then they that were in the ship came and worshipped him, saying, "Of a truth thou art the Son of God."
