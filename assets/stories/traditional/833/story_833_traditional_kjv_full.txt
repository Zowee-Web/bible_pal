Night was fallen upon the Sea of Galilee. The disciples sat together in a wooden ship, its sides slick with the spray of restless water. Straightway Jesus had constrained them to get in and to go before him unto the other side, while he stayed behind to send the multitudes away. Darkness pressed down upon them as they rowed farther from the land. Overhead, the sky held only a sliver of moon betwixt sweeping clouds. The wind whipped across the water, smiting the sails and driving the waves hard against the hull.

The lake stretched in every direction, its surface broken by churning foam and the rhythm of the disciples' oars. Their arms ached. The water splashed up, biting at skin and eyes. Every time the wind howled stronger, their small ship pitched and rocked. They looked back toward the shore, but the land was vanished into blackness. The world seemed narrowed unto wood, water, and the unrelenting wind.

Nevertheless, they pressed onward. The sky shifted as the night grew deep. Some kept watch, while others strained at the oars. Their voices were low and fearful—sharing their worries about the wind, how far they yet had to go, and what might come next. None slept. Weariness hovered at the edges of their thoughts.

In the fourth watch of the night, with the night at its thickest, something was changed. At the first, only one disciple beheld it: a shape, faint and out of place, moving upon the water's surface. He blinked, wiped the spray from his face, then beheld it again. In a moment, the others looked also. The shape became a man—walking, not swimming, not sinking. Terror swept over them like the wind.

"It is a spirit!" cried out one. The voices rose, thin and shaking, borne away by the gusts and the smiting of waves. They drew back from the edge of the ship. Some held fast unto the sides. Fear gripped every heart; this could not be.

Then a voice called unto them, clear and strong, cutting through the wind. "Be of good cheer; it is I; be not afraid!"

They knew that voice. Even as fear twisted in their breasts, hope was kindled. Peter, bold yet uncertain, arose and called into the darkness, "Lord, if it be thou, bid me come unto thee on the water."

From across the heaving waves, Jesus answered him, "Come."

Peter stepped unto the edge. The wind yet roared, but he swung his legs over the side, his feet dangling above the swirling water. One breath, then another. He let go of the ship. Then his foot met the water—and held. It did not give way.

His eyes fixed upon Jesus, Peter took a step. Then another. The water shifted under him, cool and slick, but he did not sink. The wind battered him, plucking at his tunic and his hair, but for a moment, the impossible was made real. He walked forward, closing the distance betwixt himself and Jesus, every step more astonishing than the last.

But the wind roared again, fiercer, screaming over the waves and flinging spray into his face. Fear sprang up in Peter's heart. He looked away from Jesus—unto the wild whitecaps, unto the dark beneath his feet—and there, the miracle faltered. His feet slid. The water gave way. He began to sink.

"Lord, save me!" Peter cried out, the water surging up about his legs and breast.

Immediately, Jesus stretched forth his hand and caught him. The grip was strong, living, unyielding against the sliding pull of the deep. Jesus held Peter above the water. His gaze was steady and unwavering.

"O thou of little faith," Jesus said unto him, "wherefore didst thou doubt?"

Still holding Peter's hand, Jesus led him back toward the ship. The wind thrashed and the waves pressed in, but then—so soon as they were come into the ship—the wind ceased. The air was calmed. The water stilled beneath them, silent as glass. The storm was over.

The other disciples, stunned and breathless, beheld all this. They looked from Peter, dripping and shaken, unto Jesus, whose presence filled the ship with quiet power. Awe and wonder overcame their fear. Together, they knelt and worshipped him, their voices echoing softly across the now peaceful waters. Then they that were in the ship came and worshipped him, saying, "Of a truth thou art the Son of God."
