After the crowds had dispersed, Jesus made his disciples get into the boat and go ahead of him to the other side, while he stayed behind to dismiss the people. Evening came. Darkness settled over the Sea of Galilee. The disciples’ boat drifted far from land, the wind whipping up the water. Waves battered the sides. The disciples strained at the oars, the air filled with the sound of the wind and the crash of water against wood.

Night pressed in, heavy and relentless. The boat creaked, tossed by the rough waves. Faces looked tired and anxious, eyes fixed on the shifting horizon, searching for land, for help, for Jesus. But he was not with them.

In the fourth watch of the night, when the sky was deepest black, Jesus came toward them. He walked across the water, his footsteps sure, moving over the swirling waves. The disciples saw a figure coming and were seized with fear. Shadows danced on the water and hearts pounded.

A cry tore from the boat. “It’s a ghost!” they said, voices trembling. Panic filled the small vessel. The wind howled, cold spray stinging their faces. But immediately Jesus spoke over the roar of wind and water.

“Take courage! It is I; don’t be afraid.”

His voice carried above the storm. Peter answered him, hope and uncertainty mingling in his words. “Lord, if it is you, command me to come to you on the water.”

Jesus replied simply, “Come.”

Peter climbed out of the boat, feet leaving the safety of the wooden boards. Water splashed around his ankles, then his calves. He walked on the shifting surface, moving toward Jesus. The wind howled, tugging at his clothes. Each step drew him closer, the taste of fear mixing with faith.

But as the wind surged, Peter’s eyes shifted from Jesus to the storm. Fear gripped him. He began to sink, water rising around him. “Lord, save me!” he cried.

Immediately, Jesus reached out his hand and caught him. Wet fingers closed around Peter’s arm, holding him fast. “You of little faith,” Jesus said, “why did you doubt?”

Together, they climbed into the boat, water dripping from their garments. The wind ceased. The sea grew calm, sudden and complete. Silence followed, broken only by the sound of breathing and the gentle lap of water against the hull.

Those who were in the boat came and worshiped him, saying, “You are truly the Son of God!”
