Night fell over the Sea of Galilee, and a restless wind swept across the water. The sky was deep and black, the whole lake shimmering with the uncertain glow of distant moonlight. The disciples moved about in a small boat, its wood creaking with each shift of the waves. After a long day, Jesus had urged them to go ahead of him and cross to the other side, while he dismissed the crowds himself. The dim outline of the shore faded behind them as they set out, and soon the boat was far from land—surrounded by the wide, shifting dark.

The disciples worked at the oars, muscles straining as the wind pressed hard against them. They were not strangers to the water, but tonight it seemed restless, the waves slapping against the hull and rocking the boat. Breath came fast, hearts tight from the effort. Every few moments, one of them glanced back toward the shore, hoping for a change in wind or a glimpse of morning. None came.

Wave after wave struck the sides as the night wore on. The boat moved slowly, driven by the disciples’ weary hands and buffeted by the gusting wind. After hours of struggle, it became clear that they were not making much progress. With every splash, anxiety pressed deeper into their bones. Hands were wet and cold. Each man kept his cloak drawn tightly around him.

In the fourth watch of the night, when sleep is deepest and the world is at its darkest, something troubled the surface of the water. Eyes squinted through the gloom. At first, it was only a shape—pale and moving across the sea. Then a figure, walking on the waves.

The disciples froze, hearts pounding in the stillness between bursts of wind. Every sense sharpened with alarm. The figure did not sink beneath the surface but moved steadily atop it, closer and closer. In the shadows, faces turned white with fear. Whispered questions passed between them, ragged and desperate.

"It's a ghost," someone cried out. Panic rippled through the group. Each disciple gripped the edge of the boat, holding on as if to anchor himself against the impossible sight. Some shielded their eyes, unable to understand what drew near in the darkness.

The figure drew closer, face revealed in the shifting glow of moonlight. Then, a voice rose above the wind—a voice they knew, calm and unwavering. "Cheer up! It is I! Don’t be afraid." The sound cut through the storm in a way nothing else could.

The disciples stared, breathless. The outline on the water was steady now, unmistakable. Relief fought with disbelief; could it truly be Jesus himself? Fear still wound tight inside them, for men do not walk on water.

Peter, always quick to speak and act, called out across the waves, uncertain but desperate for assurance. "Lord, if it is you, command me to come to you on the water." He waited, eyes fixed on the figure who stood upright among the white-capped waves.

A single word came back, simple and sure. "Come."

Peter drew in a shaking breath. He lifted one leg over the side, his hand gripping the edge of the boat. The wind pulled at his garments; cold water sprayed on his feet. He let go, lowered himself onto the sea, and to the amazement of all, he stood.

Step by step, Peter walked on the heaving surface. He fixed his gaze on Jesus, heartbeat roaring in his ears. Each foot fell upon the water without sinking, and for a moment the water held him as solid ground. The others watched, silent, scarcely believing their eyes.

But the storm had not ceased. Waves leaped around him, and the wind roared louder. Peter, feeling the fury of the storm, shifted his focus from Jesus to the chaos swirling all around. The cold spray, the shrieking gale, the darkness pressing at his sides—suddenly the fear before, churning at the edge of his mind, rushed upon him. Doubt surged.

In an instant, Peter began to sink. The sea pulled at his legs, water rising past his knees and up to his waist. The chill was sharp and sudden, and his hands flailed for something, anything, to grab. "Lord, save me!" he cried, voice cracking over the wind.

At once, Jesus reached out. The dark water surged and shook, but his hand was strong and certain, closing around Peter’s. Jesus lifted him up from the water, steady and unyielding. The wind tugged hard, but Jesus' grip did not falter.

He spoke, his tone both gentle and searching: "You of little faith, why did you doubt?" His words settled in the air, lingering as Peter found footing once again.

Together, with the storm still twisting about them, Jesus and Peter climbed into the boat. The disciples pulled them inside, hands trembling from the cold and the miracle they had just witnessed.

As soon as Jesus entered the boat, the wind eased. The fierce shriek faded to a restless sigh. The water calmed beneath them, and the boat found steady ground upon the waves. The disciples sat in stunned silence, hearts pounding with realization and awe.

They looked at Jesus—all eyes drawn to him, mouths opened in wonder. The edges of their fear melted away in the quiet after the storm. Cold hands unclenched from the sides of the boat, shoulders fell in relief.

Those who were in the boat came and worshiped him, saying, “You are truly the Son of God!”
