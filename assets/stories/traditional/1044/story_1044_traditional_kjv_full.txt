Ruth and Naomi had walked a long way to come unto Bethlehem. Naomi was old and weary. Her husband was dead. Her two sons were dead. All that remained unto her was Ruth — a young woman of Moab who had refused to depart from her side.

They came in the beginning of barley harvest. The fields round about the little town were golden and full. The labourers moved through the rows with sharp curved blades, cutting the ripe stalks. The air bore the savour of warm grain and of dust.

Ruth and Naomi had no money. No bread. No land. But Ruth was not of such as sit still and wait.

"Let me now go to the field," said Ruth, "and glean ears of corn after him in whose sight I shall find grace."

"Go, my daughter," said Naomi.

Ruth went forth early. She walked along the dusty way until she came unto a field of barley. She knew not whose field it was. She stooped down, gathered a few stalks that the reapers had left. Arose. Moved unto the next place. Stooped down again. Over and over, all the morning long. The sun beat upon her head. Her back wearied. Yet she ceased not.

The field belonged unto a man named Boaz. He was a mighty man of wealth, and held in honour — a kinsman of Naomi's husband. And he was kind.

Boaz came from Bethlehem to see the harvest. "The Lord be with you!" he said unto his reapers.

"The Lord bless thee!" they cried back.

Then Boaz beheld Ruth labouring at the edge of the field. She moved steadily along the rows, and ceased not. "Whose damsel is this?" he asked his servant that was set over the reapers.

"It is the Moabitish damsel that came back with Naomi out of the country of Moab," answered the servant. "She came early this morning and prayed that she might glean and gather after the reapers. She hath continued upon her feet ever since. She hath tarried but a little.

Boaz came over unto Ruth. She arose, marvelling. "My daughter," he said gently, "hearken thou unto me. Go not to glean in another field, neither pass from hence, but abide here fast by my maidens. I have charged the young men that they shall not touch thee. When thou art athirst, go unto the vessels, and drink of that which the young men have drawn."

Ruth fell on her face, and bowed herself to the ground. "Why have I found grace in thine eyes," said she, "that thou shouldest take knowledge of me, seeing I am a stranger?"

Boaz smiled. "It hath fully been shewed me all that thou hast done unto thy mother-in-law since the death of thine husband," said he. "How thou hast left thy father and thy mother, and the land of thy nativity, and art come unto a people which thou knewest not heretofore. The Lord recompense thy work, and a full reward be given thee of the Lord God of Israel, under whose wings thou art come to trust."

At mealtime, Boaz called Ruth unto him. "Come thou hither," said he, "and eat of the bread, and dip thy morsel in the vinegar." Ruth sat down beside the reapers. Boaz reached her parched corn — warm and goodly. She did eat, and was sufficed, and left of it. She kept the residue for Naomi.

When Ruth was risen up to glean, Boaz drew his young men apart. "Let her glean even among the sheaves," said he quietly. "And let fall also some of the handfuls of purpose for her, and leave them, that she may glean them, and rebuke her not."

Ruth wrought until the evening. The sun turned orange and sank toward the hills. Long shadows stretched across the stubble. She gathered all that she had gleaned and beat it out of the ears. She had about an ephah — a great deal for one day. She lifted the heavy bundle upon her shoulder and bare it home.

Naomi waited at the door. Her eyes opened wide when she beheld the grain. "Where hast thou gleaned to day? Blessed be he that did take knowledge of thee!"

Ruth set the bundle down. She brought forth also the parched corn that was left over. "The man's name is Boaz," said she.

Naomi's hand went unto her mouth. Her eyes filled with tears. "Blessed be he of the Lord, who hath not left off his kindness to the living and to the dead. The man is near of kin unto us, one of our next kinsmen."

Ruth said, "He bade me keep fast by his young men, until they have ended all his harvest."

"It is good, my daughter," said Naomi. "Go out with his maidens."

So Ruth kept fast by the maidens of Boaz to glean. She gleaned in his field through the barley harvest and the wheat harvest after. Every evening she came home with her arms full of grain. Every evening Naomi smiled a little wider. They had bread. They had a friend. And they had hope.
