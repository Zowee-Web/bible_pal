Ruth and her mother-in-law Naomi had come to Bethlehem with almost nothing. They were hungry. They needed food.

"Let me go to the fields and pick up leftover grain," Ruth said. "Maybe someone will be kind."

"Go, my daughter," Naomi said.

Ruth walked out to the fields. Workers moved through the rows of barley, cutting tall golden stalks. Behind them, grain fell to the ground. Ruth bent down and began picking it up, handful by handful.

She did not know it, but the field belonged to a man named Boaz. He was a relative of Naomi's family.

Boaz came to check on his workers. "The Lord be with you!" he called. "The Lord bless you!" they called back.

He noticed Ruth. "Who is that young woman?" he asked his servant.

"The Moabite woman who came back with Naomi. She has been working since early morning and has barely stopped."

Boaz walked over to Ruth. "Listen, my daughter. Do not go to any other field. Stay here close to my young women. When you are thirsty, drink from the water jars."

Ruth bowed down. "Why are you being so kind to me? I am a foreigner."

"I have heard everything you have done for your mother-in-law," Boaz said. "May the Lord reward your work, under whose wings you have come to take refuge."

At mealtime Boaz called her. "Come. Eat bread and dip it in the vinegar." She sat with the workers. He handed her roasted grain. She ate until she was full.

When she went back to work, Boaz told his young men quietly. "Let her glean among the sheaves. Pull out stalks on purpose and leave them for her."

Ruth worked until evening. She beat out what she had gathered — about an ephah of barley. She carried it home.

Naomi's eyes went wide. "Where did you work today?"

"The man's name is Boaz," Ruth said.

Naomi's hand went to her mouth. "He is one of our close relatives. May he be blessed by the Lord."

So Ruth stayed close to the young women of Boaz through the barley harvest and the wheat harvest. Every evening she came home with grain.
