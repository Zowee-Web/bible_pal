Ruth and her mother-in-law Naomi had come unto Bethlehem with almost nothing. They were hungry. They had need of bread.

"Let me now go to the field, and glean ears of corn," said Ruth. "Peradventure I shall find favour in some man's sight."

"Go, my daughter," said Naomi.

Ruth went forth unto the fields. The labourers moved through the rows of barley, cutting the tall golden stalks. Behind them, grain fell unto the ground. Ruth stooped down and began to gather it, handful by handful.

She knew not that the field belonged unto a man named Boaz. He was a kinsman of Naomi's house.

Boaz came forth to see his workers. "The Lord be with you!" he said. "The Lord bless thee!" they answered.

He beheld Ruth. "Whose damsel is this?" he asked his servant.

"It is the Moabitish damsel that came back with Naomi. She hath wrought since the morning, and hath scarcely rested."

Boaz came over unto Ruth. "Hearken, my daughter. Go not to glean in another field. Abide here fast by my maidens. When thou art athirst, drink of that which the young men have drawn."

Ruth bowed herself down. "Why have I found grace in thine eyes? I am a stranger."

"It hath fully been shewed me all that thou hast done unto thy mother-in-law," said Boaz. "The Lord recompense thy work, under whose wings thou art come to trust."

At mealtime Boaz called her. "Come thou hither, and eat of the bread, and dip thy morsel in the vinegar." She sat with the labourers. He reached her parched corn. She did eat, and was sufficed.

When she arose to glean again, Boaz spake quietly unto his young men. "Let her glean even among the sheaves. And let fall also some of the handfuls of purpose for her."

Ruth wrought until the evening. She beat out that she had gleaned — about an ephah of barley. She bare it home.

Naomi's eyes opened wide. "Where hast thou gleaned to day?"

"The man's name is Boaz," said Ruth.

Naomi's hand went unto her mouth. "He is one of our near kinsmen. Blessed be he of the Lord."

So Ruth kept fast by the maidens of Boaz through the barley harvest and the wheat harvest. Every evening she came home with grain.
