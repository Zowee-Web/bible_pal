Rehoboam went to Shechem, where all Israel had gathered to make him king. Jeroboam, who had fled to Egypt from Solomon, was called home, and he and the whole assembly came to Rehoboam with a plea. "Your father laid a heavy yoke on us. Lighten the hard service he put on us, and we will serve you."

"Come back to me in three days," Rehoboam said. So the people went away.

He asked the old men who had served his father Solomon what he should answer. "If you will be a servant to these people today, and speak kindly to them, they will be your servants always." But Rehoboam set their counsel aside and turned to the young men he had grown up with. They told him to answer hard. "Tell them your little finger is thicker than your father's waist. He laid a heavy yoke on you; you will make it heavier. He beat you with whips; you will beat them with scorpions."

When the people came back on the third day, the king answered them roughly, just as the young men had said. He would not listen — for this turn of things was brought about by Yahweh, to fulfill the word he had spoken.

And when all Israel saw that the king would not listen, they answered him. "What portion have we in David? What inheritance in the son of Jesse? To your tents, Israel! Look now to your own house, David." So Israel went home.

Rehoboam sent out Adoram, who was over the forced labor, and all Israel stoned him to death. The king barely reached his chariot in time to flee to Jerusalem. So Israel rebelled against the house of David. They made Jeroboam king over all Israel, and only the tribe of Judah still followed David's house.
