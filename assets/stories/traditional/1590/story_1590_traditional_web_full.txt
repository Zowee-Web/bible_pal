Rehoboam went to Shechem, where all Israel had gathered to make him king. Word reached Jeroboam, who had fled from Solomon into Egypt, and they sent and called him home. So Jeroboam and the whole assembly of Israel came to Rehoboam with a plea. "Your father laid a heavy yoke on us. Lighten the hard service he put on us, and we will serve you."

"Come back to me in three days," Rehoboam said. And the people went away.

The king asked the old men who had stood before his father Solomon what he should answer. "If today you will be a servant to these people, and serve them, and speak kindly to them, they will be your servants forever." But Rehoboam set their counsel aside. He turned instead to the young men he had grown up with, who now stood before him. And they told him to answer hard. "Tell the people who asked you to lighten the yoke: My little finger is thicker than my father's waist. He laid a heavy yoke on you; I will add to it. He beat you with whips; I will beat you with scorpions."

When the people came back on the third day, the king answered them roughly. He set aside the counsel of the old men and spoke the words of the young men. He would not listen to the people — for this turn of things was brought about by Yahweh, to fulfill the word he had spoken through Ahijah the prophet to Jeroboam.

When all Israel saw that the king would not listen, they answered him. "What portion have we in David? What inheritance in the son of Jesse? To your tents, Israel! Look now to your own house, David." So Israel went home to their tents, and only in the cities of Judah did Rehoboam still reign. He sent out Adoram, who was over the forced labor, and all Israel stoned him to death, and the king barely reached his chariot in time to flee to Jerusalem. So Israel rebelled against the house of David, and made Jeroboam king over all Israel; only the tribe of Judah followed David's house.

Rehoboam came to Jerusalem and gathered the warriors of Judah and Benjamin to go to war and win back the kingdom. But the word of God came to Shemaiah. "Tell Rehoboam and all Judah and Benjamin: You shall not go up to fight against your brothers, the people of Israel. Let every man go home, for this thing is from me." So they listened, and turned back, and went home.

But Jeroboam said in his heart, If the people go up to sacrifice at the house of Yahweh in Jerusalem, their hearts will turn back to Rehoboam. So he made two calves of gold, and said to the people, "It is too far for you to go up to Jerusalem. Here are your gods, Israel, who brought you up out of Egypt." He set one calf in Bethel and the other in Dan, and the people went even as far as Dan to worship before it. And this thing became a sin.

He built houses on the high places, and made priests from among all the people, though they were not sons of Levi. He ordained a feast in the eighth month, on the fifteenth day, like the feast that was kept in Judah; and he went up to the altar he had made in Bethel, to sacrifice to the calves and to burn incense there.
