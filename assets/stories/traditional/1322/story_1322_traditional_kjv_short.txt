The prophet Habakkuk had walked the streets of Jerusalem in the days when Judah was failing. He had seen the courts of the judges give crooked judgments. He had seen the strong take from the weak. He had seen the priests grow silent and the rulers grow greedy and the law of the Lord dropped in the dust. He had cried out unto the Lord to come and to make it right. And the Lord had not yet come, and the prophet had cried out again. And now, in his book, he set down the cry of those days.

O Lord, how long shall I cry, and thou wilt not hear! Even cry out unto thee of violence, and thou wilt not save!

Why dost thou shew me iniquity, and cause me to behold grievance? For spoiling and violence are before me. There is strife, and contention riseth up.

Therefore the law is slacked, and judgment doth never go forth; for the wicked doth compass about the righteous; therefore wrong judgment proceedeth.

And the prophet waited for the answer of the Lord. The days went on as the days had been going on. The courts kept their crooked judgments. The strong kept taking from the weak. And the cry of Habakkuk rose into the air, day after day, against the day when the answer would come. And so it was in Judah in those days.
