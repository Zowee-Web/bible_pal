There were days in the life of David when his bones felt sick within him, and the night brought no rest.

This is one of the psalms he wrote in those days.

He gave it unto the chief musician, with stringed instruments, upon Sheminith — the eight-stringed harp — to be sung when the people of the Lord were in trouble like this.

He said —

"O Lord, rebuke me not in thine anger, neither chasten me in thy hot displeasure.

Have mercy upon me, O Lord; for I am weak.

O Lord, heal me; for my bones are vexed. My soul is also sore vexed.

But thou, O Lord, how long?

Return, O Lord, deliver my soul: oh save me for thy mercies' sake.

For in death there is no remembrance of thee: in the grave who shall give thee thanks?

I am weary with my groaning; all the night make I my bed to swim; I water my couch with my tears.

Mine eye is consumed because of grief; it waxeth old because of all mine enemies."

David knew not yet the day the Lord would answer him.

The lamp had burned low in his chamber. The morning was not yet come.

But this prayer he had written down, and it had gone forth unto the chief musician and unto the people of the Lord, that when their nights were like his, they might have words to say.
