There were days in the life of David when his bones felt sick within him, and the night brought no rest.

This is one of the psalms he wrote in those days.

He gave it to the music director, with stringed instruments, on the Sheminith — the eight-stringed harp — to be sung when the people of Yahweh were in trouble like this.

He said —

"Yahweh, don't rebuke me in your anger. Don't discipline me in your wrath.

Have mercy on me, Yahweh, for I am faint.

Yahweh, heal me, for my bones are troubled. My soul is also in great anguish.

But you, Yahweh — how long?

Return, Yahweh. Deliver my soul. Save me for your loving kindness' sake.

For in death there is no memory of you. In Sheol, who shall give you thanks?

I am weary with my groaning. Every night I flood my bed. I drench my couch with my tears.

My eye wastes away because of grief. It grows old because of all my adversaries."

David did not yet know the day Yahweh would answer him.

The lamp had burned low in his chamber. The morning had not yet come.

But this prayer he had written down, and it had gone out to the music director and to the people of Yahweh, so that when their nights were like his, they would have words to say.
