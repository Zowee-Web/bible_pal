In those days was Hezekiah sick unto death.

Isaiah the prophet, the son of Amoz, came unto him with a word. "Thus saith the Lord: set thine house in order, for thou shalt die, and not live."

The king turned his face to the wall.

"I beseech thee, O Lord," he prayed, "remember now how I have walked before thee in truth and with a perfect heart, and have done that which is good in thy sight."

And Hezekiah wept sore.

Isaiah departed from him. Afore he was gone out into the middle court, the word of the Lord came unto him, and he turned again.

"Tell Hezekiah the captain of my people: Thus saith the Lord, the God of David thy father, I have heard thy prayer, I have seen thy tears. Behold, I will heal thee. On the third day thou shalt go up unto the house of the Lord. And I will add unto thy days fifteen years."

There was more. "And I will deliver thee and this city out of the hand of the king of Assyria; and I will defend this city for mine own sake, and for my servant David's sake."

So the prophet went back the way that he had come, and carried the word to the sick man's bed.

Then he said, "Take a lump of figs."

They took it and laid it on the boil, and the king recovered.

Hezekiah asked a sign of him. "What shall be the sign that the Lord will heal me, and that I shall go up into the house of the Lord the third day?"

Isaiah gave him the choosing of it. "This sign shalt thou have of the Lord, that the Lord will do the thing that he hath spoken: shall the shadow go forward ten degrees, or go back ten degrees?"

The dial of Ahaz stood in the light, and the shadow upon it had been going down.

"It is a light thing for the shadow to go down ten degrees," Hezekiah answered. "Nay: but let the shadow return backward ten degrees."

And Isaiah the prophet cried unto the Lord.

And the shadow came back up the degrees it had already gone down, ten of them, in the dial of Ahaz.
