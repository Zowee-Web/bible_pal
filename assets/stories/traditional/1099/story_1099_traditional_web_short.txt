Jesus took Peter, James, and John, and led them up a high mountain by themselves, apart from the others. They left the villages below and climbed through rocky terrain where the scrub brush thinned and the wind picked up. The path was steep. The air grew cooler with every step. Below them, the valley spread out wide and green, the lake glinting in the distance, the towns along the shore shrinking to small clusters of stone and thatch.

At the top, the mountain was bare and quiet. The sky felt close. The wind moved across the rock in long, steady gusts.

Then something happened that none of them expected.

Jesus' appearance changed before their eyes. His face began to shine — not like lamplight or firelight, but like the sun itself, blazing and unbearable. His clothes became white, brilliant white, whiter than anything a fuller on earth could bleach. The light came from within him, radiating outward, filling the air with a brightness that was almost impossible to look at.

Then two figures appeared beside him. The disciples knew them — not by sight, for they had never seen them, but by a deeper knowing. They were Moses and Elijah. The two greatest figures in the history of Israel stood with Jesus on the mountaintop, speaking with him, their forms visible in the blazing light.

Peter was terrified. He didn't know what to say, so he said the first thing that came to him.

"Lord, it is good for us to be here," he said. His voice was unsteady. "If you wish, I will make three shelters — one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah."

While he was still speaking, a bright cloud came over the mountain and covered them — not a dark cloud, but a luminous one, thick with light, pressing in on every side. The disciples fell to the ground, their faces in the dirt, their bodies trembling. The air was dense and alive with presence.

Then a voice spoke from the cloud.

"This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. Listen to him."

The disciples pressed their faces harder into the ground. The fear was beyond anything they had felt before — not the fear of danger, but the fear of standing at the edge of something immeasurably vast.

Then a hand touched them. It was Jesus.

"Rise," he said. "Do not be afraid."

They looked up. The cloud was gone. Moses and Elijah were gone. The blazing light had faded. Jesus stood alone on the bare rock, looking the way he always looked — dusty robe, calloused hands, gentle face.

They came down the mountain together, walking in silence. The valley spread out below them, ordinary and still, the lake catching the afternoon light. None of them spoke about what they had seen.
