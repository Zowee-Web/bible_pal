For a few moments upon a mountaintop, three common men beheld a thing they were never meant to forget.

The face of the man they followed — he who ate bread, who grew weary, who walked the same dusty roads — did shine as the sun. The ordinary was drawn back, and what lay beneath was blinding.

They desired to build tabernacles. To contain it. To make it abide. But the moment was not given to be preserved. It was given to be carried.

When they opened their eyes, all appeared as before. Jesus looked as Jesus. The mountain was but a mountain. The path led down to the valley, the multitudes, the work.

But they had seen it. And what thou hast seen, thou canst not unsee. It changeth how thou lookest upon all things — especially the common ones.
