For a few minutes on a mountaintop, three ordinary men saw something they were never meant to forget.

The face of the man they followed — the one who ate bread, who got tired, who walked the same dusty roads — shone like the sun. The ordinary was pulled back, and what was underneath was blinding.

They wanted to build shelters. To contain it. To make it stay. But the moment was not meant to be preserved. It was meant to be carried.

When they opened their eyes, everything looked normal again. Jesus looked like Jesus. The mountain was just a mountain. The path led back down to the valley, to the crowds, to the work.

But they had seen it. And what you have seen, you cannot unsee. It changes how you look at everything — especially the ordinary things.
