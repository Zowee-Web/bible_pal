Jesus taketh with him Peter, James, and John his brother, and bringeth them up into an high mountain apart. He told them not wherefore. He said not what they should see. They simply followed, climbing through rocky ground where the path narrowed and the brush thinned and the air grew cool. The valley fell away below. The towns along the shore became small. The noise of the world was replaced by wind and the sound of their own breathing.

At the top, the mountain was bare and still. The sky pressed close. The rock was pale and smooth.

And he was transfigured before them. His face did shine as the sun — not as firelight nor lamplight, but as the sun itself, blazing and past endurance. The light came from within him, as though the form they had known these many months had been a covering, and now the covering was drawn back. His raiment was white as the light — a searing, brilliant white beyond all earthly comparison.

And, behold, there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him. The giver of the law and the greatest of the prophets stood with Jesus upon the mountain, speaking with him as those that knew him.

Peter was sore afraid. He knew not what to say, and answered, "Lord, it is good for us to be here: if thou wilt, let us make here three tabernacles; one for thee, and one for Moses, and one for Elias." His voice was unsteady, the words tumbling forth.

While he yet spake, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them — not a dark cloud, but one luminous and dense with light, pressing in from every quarter. The disciples fell on their faces to the ground, trembling.

And behold a voice out of the cloud, which said, "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him."

The fear that seized them was not the fear of danger. It was the fear of standing at the edge of something immeasurably vast.

And Jesus came and touched them — a human hand, calloused and warm and real — and said, "Arise, and be not afraid."

When they lifted up their eyes, they saw no man, save Jesus only. The cloud was gone. Moses and Elias were departed. The blazing light had faded. Jesus stood alone upon the bare rock, as he had ever appeared — dusty robe, sun-browned face, gentle eyes.

They came down from the mountain in silence. The valley spread below them, ordinary and green, the lake catching the light of the waning afternoon. None spake of what they had seen.
