Jesus taketh Peter, James, and John his brother, and bringeth them up into an high mountain apart. They left the villages below and climbed through rocky ground where the path narrowed and the wind picked up. The air grew cool. Below them, the valley spread wide, the lake glinting in the distance.

At the top, the mountain was bare and still. The sky pressed close.

And he was transfigured before them. His face did shine as the sun, and his raiment was white as the light — a brightness past all earthly comparison, blazing and pure. The light came from within him, filling the air about him.

And, behold, there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him. The two greatest figures in all the history of Israel stood with Jesus upon the mountain, speaking with him in the midst of the glory.

Peter was sore afraid. He knew not what to say. He answered and said unto Jesus, "Lord, it is good for us to be here: if thou wilt, let us make here three tabernacles; one for thee, and one for Moses, and one for Elias."

While he yet spake, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them — not a dark cloud, but one thick with light, pressing in on every side. The disciples fell on their faces to the ground and were sore afraid.

And behold a voice out of the cloud, which said, "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him."

The disciples pressed their faces into the earth. The fear was beyond aught they had known — not the fear of peril, but of something immeasurably vast.

And Jesus came and touched them, and said, "Arise, and be not afraid."

And when they had lifted up their eyes, they saw no man, save Jesus only. The cloud was gone. Moses and Elias were departed. The blazing light was faded. Jesus stood alone upon the bare rock, as he had ever appeared — dusty robe, gentle face, calloused hands.

They came down from the mountain in silence, the valley spreading below them, the lake catching the afternoon light.
