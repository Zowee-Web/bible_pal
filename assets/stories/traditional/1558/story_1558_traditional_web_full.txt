It was the third hour of the day when they put him on the cross.

The crowd was around him on the hill that men called the Place of the Skull. The soldiers were dividing his garments below him, casting lots for the seamless robe — the one his mother had made for him, the one that should not be torn. The women who had followed him from Galilee stood at a distance and watched. Mary, his mother, was among them. Mary Magdalene was among them. John, the disciple he loved, was somewhere on the edge of the crowd.

The first word he said was for the soldiers.

"Father, forgive them, for they don't know what they are doing."

It was not the kind of thing said in that place. The soldiers down below did not look up. They were good at what they were doing, and what they were doing was the same work they did at every cross. They had not asked who he was, and they did not know. He said the word for them anyway. The text does not say whether any of them heard.

The rulers passed beneath him and scoffed. "He saved others," they said. "Let him save himself, if this is the Christ of God, his chosen one." It was their best joke. They were satisfied with it. The crowd around them laughed.

The soldiers, coming and going, mocked him. They offered him vinegar from the jar. One called up to him about saving the king of the Jews. The inscription over his head, written in Greek and Latin and Hebrew, said it for them: THIS IS THE KING OF THE JEWS.

Two criminals had been crucified with him, one on his right and one on his left.

The one on the left railed against him in the same words the rulers had used. "If you are the Christ, save yourself and us!"

The one on the right answered him back. He had been silent until then. He spoke now without looking at his neighbor. He spoke as if he had been thinking about this for a long time.

"Don't you even fear God, seeing you are under the same condemnation? We are here justly. We receive the due reward for our deeds. But this man has done nothing wrong."

Then he turned his face toward Jesus. He could not move much. But he turned his face.

"Lord, remember me when you come into your kingdom."

Jesus turned his head toward him. His second word was for the man beside him.

"Assuredly I tell you, today you will be with me in paradise."

It was about the sixth hour now. The sun went out. Not slowly, the way the sun goes out at evening. All at once. A heavy darkness came over the whole land, and stayed there, until the ninth hour. The veil of the temple, far off in the city, was torn in two from the top to the bottom. The priests in the inner court did not understand what was happening. The people on the hill did not understand either. They stood beholding. No one knew what to do. The crowd grew quieter than crowds usually grew.

He hung there in the dark. The soldiers stayed at their posts. The women did not leave. The hour passed.

Then Jesus cried with a loud voice. It was his third word.

"Father, into your hands I commit my spirit."

And having said this, he breathed his last.

The centurion who stood facing him saw what happened. He was a Roman soldier. He had been at many crosses. He had seen many men die. He had seen most kinds of dying that there were to see. He had not seen one die like this. He glorified God. He said: certainly, this was a righteous man.

And the people who had come together to behold turned and went home, beating their breasts. The hill emptied. The women who had followed him from Galilee did not leave. They stood at a distance and watched these things. They watched the soldiers take him down. They watched the men carry him to the tomb that had been offered. They watched where he was laid, so that they would know how to come back.

And so it was on the day the King of the Jews was crucified, on the hill outside the city, between the sixth hour and the ninth.
