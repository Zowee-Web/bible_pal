The Lord appeared unto Abraham in the plains of Mamre. It was the heat of the day. The sun beat down, and the oaks spread what shade they might. Abraham sat in the tent door. The air stirred not. Nothing moved.

He lifted up his eyes and looked, and lo, three men stood by him.

He ran to meet them and bowed himself toward the ground.

"My lord," said he, "if now I have found favour in thy sight, pass not away from thy servant. Let a little water be fetched, and wash your feet, and rest yourselves under the tree. I will fetch a morsel of bread, and comfort ye your hearts."

They said, "So do, as thou hast said."

Abraham hastened into the tent. "Make ready quickly three measures of fine meal," he bade Sarah. "Knead it, and make cakes upon the hearth." Three measures was no small portion. This was a feast, not a morsel.

He ran unto the herd and chose a calf, tender and good. He gave it unto a servant, who hasted to dress it. The fire must be kindled. The flesh prepared and cooked.

Whilst the calf roasted, Sarah kneaded the dough with her hands and laid the cakes upon the hearth. The tent smelled of bread baking.

When all was ready, Abraham took butter and milk and the calf. He set it before the three men under the tree. He stood by whilst they did eat. A host stood. A host served.

They did eat. Then they spake.

"Where is Sarah thy wife?"

"Behold," said he, "in the tent."

One of them said, "I will certainly return unto thee according to the time of life, and lo, Sarah thy wife shall have a son."

Sarah heard it in the tent door, which was behind him. Now Abraham and Sarah were old and well stricken in age. It ceased to be with Sarah after the manner of women. She laughed within herself. "After I am waxed old," she thought, "shall I have pleasure, my lord being old also?"

The Lord said unto Abraham, "Wherefore did Sarah laugh? Is any thing too hard for the Lord? At the time appointed I will return unto thee, and Sarah shall have a son."

Sarah was afraid. "I laughed not," said she.

He said, "Nay, but thou didst laugh."

The promise had been spoken. The laughter had been heard. And the question needed no answer.
