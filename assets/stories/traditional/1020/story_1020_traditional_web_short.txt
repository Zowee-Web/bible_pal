The Lord appeared to Abraham by the oaks of Mamre. It was the heat of the day. The sun bore down, and the oaks spread what shade they could. Abraham sat at the entrance of his tent. The air was still. Nothing moved.

He lifted his eyes and looked. Three men stood nearby.

He ran to meet them and bowed himself to the ground.

"My lord," he said, "if I have found favor in your sight, please do not pass by your servant. Let a little water be brought. Wash your feet and rest under the tree. I will bring a morsel of bread, that you may refresh your hearts."

They said, "Do as you have said."

Abraham hurried into the tent. "Quickly," he told Sarah. "Make ready three measures of fine meal. Knead it and make cakes." Three measures was no small amount. This was a feast, not a morsel.

He ran to the herd and chose a calf, tender and good. He gave it to a servant, who hurried to prepare it. The fire had to be built. The meat dressed and cooked.

While the calf roasted, Sarah kneaded the dough with her hands and set the cakes on the hearth. The tent smelled of baking bread.

When everything was ready, Abraham took butter and milk and the calf. He set it before the three men under the tree. He stood nearby while they ate. A host stood. A host served.

They ate. Then they spoke.

"Where is Sarah your wife?"

"There, in the tent," Abraham said.

One of them said, "I will surely return to you when the season comes around, and Sarah your wife will have a son."

Sarah was listening at the tent door behind him. Now Abraham and Sarah were old, well advanced in years. The way of women had ceased to be with Sarah. She laughed within herself. "After I have grown old," she thought, "shall I have pleasure, my lord being old also?"

The Lord said to Abraham, "Why did Sarah laugh? Is anything too hard for the Lord? At the appointed time I will return to you, and Sarah will have a son."

Sarah was afraid. "I did not laugh," she said.

He said, "No, but you did laugh."

The promise had been spoken. The laughter had been heard. And the question needed no reply.
