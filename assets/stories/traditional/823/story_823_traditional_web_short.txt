Long shadows fell across the palace courtyard as news spread of the king’s decree. In distant corners, many among the Jews mourned with fasting, weeping, and lamenting, for their destruction had been ordered. Mordecai, in his grief, tore his clothes and dressed in sackcloth and ashes. He stood at the palace gate, for he could not enter dressed so. Esther’s maidens told her of Mordecai’s distress, and she was deeply troubled. She sent clothes for him, but he would not accept them.

Esther called for Hathach, one of the king’s eunuchs, and sent him to learn what troubled Mordecai. Mordecai told Hathach of Haman’s evil plot and the sum of silver promised to the king’s treasuries in exchange for the lives of the Jews. He also gave Hathach a copy of the written decree so Esther would know the gravity of the threat. He urged Esther to go before King Xerxes and plead for her people.

When Hathach delivered Mordecai’s words, Esther made known the law of the land: any woman or man, who entered the king’s inner court uninvited, faced death unless the king extended his golden scepter. Esther told Mordecai that she had not been summoned for thirty days. Mordecai answered, warning her not to think she would escape because she lived in the palace. “If you remain silent, relief and deliverance will arise from another place, but you and your father’s house will perish. Who knows whether you have come to your royal position for such a time as this?”

Esther resolved with courage and sent word to Mordecai: “Go, gather all the Jews in Susa. Fast for me; do not eat or drink for three days, night or day. I and my maidens will do the same. Then I will go to the king, though it is against the law. If I perish, I perish.” Obediently, Mordecai did as Esther had commanded.

On the third day, Esther dressed in her royal robes and stood in the silent courtyard. The king sat on his golden throne, behind heavy curtains and amidst gleaming riches. Seeing Esther, he extended the golden scepter. Esther approached and touched its tip. The king asked what she desired, promising even half his kingdom. Esther requested that he and Haman attend a banquet she had prepared.

At the banquet, Esther invited the king and Haman to a second feast the following day. At that second banquet, Esther revealed the truth: "We have been sold to destruction—I and my people. The adversary is this wicked Haman." The king, enraged, ordered Haman hanged upon the very gallows he had built for Mordecai. Thus deliverance came through Esther's courage and faith, for such a time as this.
