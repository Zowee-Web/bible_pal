On a Sabbath, Jesus entered the house of a ruler of the Pharisees to eat bread. The people there watched him closely. A man suffering from dropsy stood before him. Jesus asked the lawyers and Pharisees whether it was lawful to heal on the Sabbath, but they remained silent. Jesus took the man, healed him, and let him go.

Then Jesus asked which of them would not immediately pull out a son or an ox that had fallen into a well on the Sabbath. No one could answer him concerning these things.

Jesus also watched the invited guests choosing the best seats. He told them a parable about a wedding feast. A guest who chose the best place might be displaced when someone more honorable arrived. The host could tell that guest to make room, leaving him to take the lowest place with shame. Instead, Jesus said, an invited person should choose the lowest place. Then the host might call him a friend and move him higher, honoring him before everyone at the table. Everyone who exalted himself would be humbled, and whoever humbled himself would be exalted.

Jesus then spoke to the man who had invited him. When making a dinner or supper, the host was not to call friends, brothers, relatives, or rich neighbors who could return the favor. Such guests might invite him back and repay him.

Instead, Jesus told the host to invite the poor, the maimed, the lame, and the blind. These guests did not have the resources to repay him. The host would be blessed, and his repayment would come in the resurrection of the righteous. At a meal where people watched Jesus and competed for honored places, he healed a man, answered silence with mercy, and directed attention toward humble seats and guests unable to repay.
