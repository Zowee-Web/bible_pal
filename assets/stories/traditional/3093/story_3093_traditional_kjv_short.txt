On the sabbath day, Jesus went into the house of one of the chief Pharisees to eat bread. Those present watched him. Behold, a certain man which had the dropsy stood before him. Jesus asked the lawyers and Pharisees, “Is it lawful to heal on the sabbath day?” They held their peace. Jesus took the man, healed him, and let him go.

Then Jesus asked which of them, having an ass or an ox fallen into a pit, would not straightway pull it out on the sabbath day. They could not answer him again to these things.

Jesus marked how the guests chose the chief rooms and put forth a parable. “When thou art bidden of any man to a wedding, sit not down in the highest room.” A more honourable man might also be bidden, and the host could say, “Give this man place.” Then the first guest would begin with shame to take the lowest room. Instead, the guest should sit in the lowest room. The man that bade him might then say unto him, “Friend, go up higher,” and he would have worship before those who sat at meat. Whosoever exalted himself would be abased, and he that humbled himself would be exalted.

Jesus also addressed the man that bade him. When making a dinner or supper, he should not call his friends, brethren, kinsmen, or rich neighbours. They might bid him again, and a recompence would be made unto him.

When making a feast, he was to call the poor, the maimed, the lame, and the blind. They could not recompense him, yet he would be blessed and recompensed at the resurrection of the just. At that meal, Jesus healed the man before him and directed the company away from chief rooms and repayment toward humility and generous welcome.
