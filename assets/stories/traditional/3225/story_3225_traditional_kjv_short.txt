Moses turned and went down from the mount, carrying the two tables of the testimony. The tables were written on both sides. They were the work of God, and the writing graven upon them was God’s writing.

Joshua heard the people shouting and told Moses there was a noise of war in the camp. Moses answered that he heard neither the voice of those shouting for mastery nor of those crying from defeat, but the noise of singing.

Coming nigh the camp, Moses saw the calf and the dancing. His anger waxed hot, and he cast down the tables, breaking them beneath the mount. He burnt the calf, ground it to powder, strawed it upon the water, and made the children of Israel drink of it.

Moses asked Aaron what the people had done unto him, that he had brought so great a sin upon them. Aaron asked his lord not to be angry, saying Moses knew the people were set on mischief. They had demanded gods to go before them, saying they knew not what had become of Moses, who brought them from Egypt. Aaron said, “I said unto them, Whosoever hath any gold, let them break it off. So they gave it me: then I cast it into the fire, and there came out this calf.”

Moses saw that the people were naked, for Aaron had made them naked unto their shame among their enemies. At the camp’s gate he called those on the Lord’s side to come unto him. All the sons of Levi gathered unto him. He declared the command of the Lord God of Israel: every man was to put his sword by his side, go from gate to gate throughout the camp, and slay his brother, companion, and neighbour. The children of Levi obeyed Moses, and about three thousand men fell that day. Moses had told them to consecrate themselves that day unto the Lord, every man upon his son and brother, that he might bestow a blessing upon them.

On the morrow Moses told the people they had sinned greatly. He would go up unto the Lord; peradventure he could make atonement for their sin. Returning unto the Lord, he acknowledged their great sin in making gods of gold. He pleaded for forgiveness; otherwise, he asked to be blotted out of the book the Lord had written.

The Lord answered that whosoever had sinned against him would be blotted out of his book. Moses was to lead the people unto the appointed place. The Lord’s Angel would go before him; nevertheless, in the day of visitation he would visit their sin upon them. The Lord plagued the people because they made the calf, which Aaron made.
