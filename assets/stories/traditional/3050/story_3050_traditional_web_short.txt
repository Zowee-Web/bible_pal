Malachi carried Yahweh’s word to Israel. Yahweh declared that he had loved his people, yet they questioned his love. He pointed to Jacob and Esau and to Edom’s ruined inheritance. Edom might promise to rebuild, but Yahweh of Armies would overthrow what the wicked raised. Israel would see it and confess that Yahweh was great beyond its borders.

Then Yahweh addressed the priests. A son honored his father, and a servant respected his master. Where, then, was the honor due to him? The priests asked how they had despised his name. Their own offerings answered them. They brought blind, lame, sick, and violently taken animals to Yahweh’s altar—gifts they would not dare present to a governor. They treated Yahweh’s table as contemptible and called its service wearisome. Yahweh said he would accept neither them nor their offerings. He was a great King, and his name would be great among the nations.

The warning became a command. Unless the priests listened and took it to heart to glorify Yahweh’s name, their blessings would become a curse. The covenant with Levi had been a covenant of life and peace. A faithful priest had spoken truth, walked with God in uprightness, and turned many from sin. These priests had left that path. By showing partiality in the law, they had made many stumble and corrupted the covenant entrusted to them.

Malachi also confronted treachery among the people. They had one Father and one Creator, yet they dealt faithlessly with one another and profaned the covenant. Men covered Yahweh’s altar with tears because their offerings were no longer received, but they still asked why. Yahweh had witnessed their betrayal of the wives of their youth—their companions and covenant wives. He commanded them to guard their spirits and not be unfaithful.

Even their words wearied Yahweh. They claimed that evildoers pleased him, or they asked where the God of justice was. Their questions could not hide their dishonor. The great King required truth, reverence, and covenant faithfulness.
