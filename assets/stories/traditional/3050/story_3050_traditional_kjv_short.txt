The burden of the word of the LORD came to Israel by Malachi. Thus saith the LORD: he had loved his people, though they asked wherein he had loved them. He set Jacob and Esau before them and spoke of Edom’s desolate inheritance. Edom might say that the ruined places would be rebuilt, but the LORD of hosts would throw them down. Israel would behold it and say that the LORD was magnified beyond her border.

The LORD then called the priests to account. A son honoured his father, and a servant his master; but where was the honour and fear due unto him? The priests asked wherein they had despised his name. Their offerings gave the answer. They brought the blind, the lame, the sick, and that which was torn. Such gifts would not please a governor, yet they laid them upon the LORD’s altar. They called his table contemptible and his service a weariness. The LORD would accept neither their persons nor their offerings. He was a great King, and his name would be dreadful among the heathen.

Therefore a commandment came to the priests. If they would not hear and lay it to heart to give glory unto the LORD’s name, he would curse their blessings. His covenant with Levi had been of life and peace. The faithful priest had carried the law of truth in his mouth, walked with God in peace and equity, and turned many away from iniquity. But these priests had departed from the way. Their partiality in the law caused many to stumble and corrupted the covenant of Levi.

Malachi also named the treachery among the people. Had they not all one father? Hath not one God created them? Yet they dealt faithlessly with their brethren and profaned the covenant. Men covered the altar with weeping because the LORD no longer regarded their offerings, but still asked wherefore. The LORD had witnessed their betrayal of the wives of their youth, their companions and covenant wives. They were commanded to take heed to their spirits and not deal treacherously.

Their words also wearied the LORD. They said that those who did evil were good in his sight, or demanded to know where the God of judgment was. Such words could not conceal their dishonour. The great King required truth, reverence, and faithfulness to his covenant.
