Malachi received a revelation for Israel. Yahweh began with a declaration the people had learned to question: “I have loved you.” When they asked how that love had been shown, he directed them to the brothers Jacob and Esau. Edom, Esau’s inheritance, had become a desolation. Its people could announce that they would rebuild their waste places, but Yahweh of Armies would throw down what they built. They would be known as the land against which his judgment remained. Israel would witness it and acknowledge that Yahweh’s greatness reached beyond Israel’s border.

From that declaration of love, the word turned to the honor Israel withheld. A son honored his father, and a servant respected his master. If Yahweh was Father and Master, where were his honor and reverence? The priests answered with another challenge: how had they despised his name?

Yahweh exposed what their questions concealed. They placed polluted food on his altar and regarded his table as contemptible. They brought blind animals, lame animals, sick animals, and animals taken by violence. He told them to offer such gifts to their governor and see whether the governor would welcome either the offering or the giver. Yet with those same hands they asked God to show favor. Yahweh desired that someone would close the doors rather than let them kindle altar fire in vain. He had no pleasure in such service and would not receive their gifts.

Their contempt did not diminish his majesty. From sunrise to sunset, Yahweh said, his name would be great among the nations, and pure offerings would be presented to his name. The priests, however, profaned that name. They sniffed at the service and called it wearisome. A man who possessed an acceptable male in his flock but vowed and sacrificed a defective animal was under a curse. Yahweh of Armies would not accept a defective sacrifice. He was a great King, and his name was awesome among the nations.

The priests therefore received a direct command. They must listen, take the warning to heart, and give glory to Yahweh’s name. Otherwise their blessings would be cursed. The warning was severe because their office carried a covenant trust. Yahweh’s covenant with Levi had given life and peace and called forth reverence. The faithful pattern was plain: truth in the priest’s mouth, no unrighteousness on his lips, a walk of peace and uprightness with God, and the turning of many away from sin. A priest’s lips were to guard knowledge, and people were to seek instruction from him, for he was Yahweh’s messenger.

But the priests of Malachi’s day had left that path. They had caused many to stumble through their handling of the law and had corrupted Levi’s covenant. They did not keep Yahweh’s ways and showed partiality in instruction. Consequently, the people who should have honored them saw their disgrace.

The covenant charge widened from the priests to Judah. One Father belonged to them all; one God had created them. Nevertheless, they acted treacherously against one another and profaned the covenant of their fathers. Judah joined itself to what belonged to a foreign god, bringing unfaithfulness into Israel and Jerusalem.

Another contradiction appeared at the altar. Men covered it with tears, weeping because Yahweh no longer regarded their offerings. They asked why, but Yahweh had been a witness between each man and the wife of his youth. She was his companion and covenant wife, yet he had dealt treacherously with her. God had made them one and sought godly offspring. Yahweh, the God of Israel, said that one who hated and divorced covered his garment with violence. Twice the command sounded: guard your spirit; do not be unfaithful.

Finally, Yahweh named the weariness caused by their speech. Again they demanded an explanation. They had spoken as if everyone who practiced evil was good in Yahweh’s sight, as if he delighted in wrongdoing. Or they had challenged, “Where is the God of justice?” Their disputes, offerings, instruction, marriages, and words all revealed the same fracture. The God who had loved them was a great King. He called priests and people away from contempt and treachery, back to reverence, truth, and covenant faithfulness.
