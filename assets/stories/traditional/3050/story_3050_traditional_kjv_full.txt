The burden of the word of the LORD came unto Israel by Malachi. Thus saith the LORD: he had loved them. When they asked wherein that love had appeared, he set Jacob and Esau before them. Edom, the inheritance of Esau, had been laid waste. Its people might say that they would return and build the desolate places, but the LORD of hosts would cast down what they raised. They would be called the border of wickedness, the people against whom the LORD had indignation. Israel would see it and confess that the LORD was magnified beyond her border.

From his love the word turned to the honour Israel withheld. A son honoured his father, and a servant his master. If the LORD was Father and Master, where were his honour and fear? The priests answered with a question of their own: wherein had they despised his name?

The LORD uncovered what their question sought to hide. They offered polluted bread upon his altar and counted his table contemptible. They brought the blind, the lame, the sick, and that which was torn. He told them to present such a gift to their governor and learn whether that ruler would be pleased with them or accept their persons. Yet with such offerings in their hands they entreated God for favour. The LORD asked who among them would shut the doors for nought; neither did they kindle fire on his altar for nought. He had no pleasure in them and would not receive an offering from their hand.

Their contempt could not lessen his majesty. From the rising of the sun unto its going down, the LORD said, his name would be great among the Gentiles, and a pure offering would be brought unto his name. The priests nevertheless profaned it. They called the service a weariness and snuffed at it. The deceiver who possessed a sound male in his flock but vowed and sacrificed a corrupt thing was cursed. The LORD of hosts was no lesser ruler to be satisfied with what men despised. He was a great King, and his name was dreadful among the heathen.

A commandment therefore came directly to the priests. They must hear, lay the warning to heart, and give glory unto the LORD’s name. Otherwise he would send a curse upon them and curse their blessings. Their office bore the trust of a covenant. The covenant with Levi had been of life and peace and had called forth fear. Its faithful pattern was clear: the law of truth in the priest’s mouth, no iniquity in his lips, a walk with God in peace and equity, and the turning of many away from iniquity. The priest’s lips should keep knowledge, and the people should seek the law at his mouth, for he was the messenger of the LORD of hosts.

But these priests had departed out of the way. They caused many to stumble at the law and corrupted the covenant of Levi. They did not keep the LORD’s ways and were partial in the law. Therefore those before whom they should have been honoured saw them made contemptible and base.

The charge of broken covenant widened from the priests unto Judah. Had they not all one Father? Hath not one God created them? Yet every man dealt treacherously against his brother and profaned the covenant of their fathers. Judah joined himself unto the daughter of a strange god, bringing abomination into Israel and Jerusalem.

Another contradiction lay at the altar. Men covered it with tears and crying because the LORD no longer regarded their offerings. They asked wherefore, though the LORD had been witness between each man and the wife of his youth. She was his companion and the wife of his covenant, yet he had dealt treacherously with her. God had made them one and sought a godly seed. The LORD, the God of Israel, said that he hated putting away and that one covered violence with his garment. Therefore they were commanded to take heed to their spirit and not deal treacherously.

At last the LORD named the weariness caused by their words. Again they demanded to know wherein. They had spoken as though every evildoer were good in the LORD’s sight and as though he delighted in wickedness. Or they asked, “Where is the God of judgment?” Their questions, offerings, teaching, marriages, and speech disclosed the same breach. The God who had loved them was the great King. He called priest and people from contempt and treachery unto fear, truth, and covenant faithfulness.
