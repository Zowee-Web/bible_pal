Yahweh commanded Moses to observe what he was giving him that day. He would drive out the Amorite, Canaanite, Hittite, Perizzite, Hivite, and Jebusite before Israel. Moses was warned against making a covenant with the inhabitants of the land, lest it become a snare. Their altars were to be broken down, their pillars dashed in pieces, and their Asherah poles cut down. No other god was to be worshiped, for Yahweh, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God.

A covenant with those inhabitants could draw Israel into eating sacrifices offered to their gods. Taking their daughters as wives for Israel’s sons could lead the sons also to prostitute themselves after those gods. They were to make no cast idols.

Yahweh commanded the feast of unleavened bread: seven days of eating unleavened bread at the appointed time in Abib, the month when Israel came out of Egypt. All that opened the womb belonged to him, including the firstborn males of cattle and sheep. A firstborn donkey was to be redeemed with a lamb; otherwise its neck was to be broken. Every firstborn son was to be redeemed, and no one was to appear before Yahweh empty.

Six days were for work, and the seventh for rest, even during plowing and harvest. Israel was to observe the feast of weeks with the first fruits of the wheat harvest, and the feast of harvest at the year’s end. Three times each year all the males were to appear before the Lord Yahweh, the God of Israel. He would drive out nations and enlarge their borders; no man would desire their land when they went up to appear before him.

The blood of his sacrifice was not to be offered with leavened bread, nor was the Passover sacrifice to remain until morning. The first of the ground’s first fruits belonged at the house of Yahweh their God. A young goat was not to be boiled in its mother’s milk.

Yahweh told Moses to write these words, for according to them he had made a covenant with Moses and Israel. Moses remained there with Yahweh forty days and forty nights, eating no bread and drinking no water. He wrote on the tablets the words of the covenant, the ten commandments.
