The dust of the road lay thick upon the ground, pale as ash beneath the sun. It had not rained in many days, and the earth was dry, cracked along the edges of the path where feet had worn it smooth. The crowd had gathered in an open place — some standing, some seated upon the low stones that lined the way, some crouched in the thin shade cast by a wall of rough-cut limestone. They had come from the surrounding villages, from fields where the grain stood tall and uncut, from houses where the work of the day had been set aside.

There were men among them whose shoulders bent forward, whose hands were roughened and dark from labor. Women stood near the edges of the gathering, some carrying young children against their bodies, the fabric of their garments stained with the dust of travel. A few of the older men leaned upon staffs, their sandals worn through at the sole. The sound of their breathing was audible in the stillness — slow, heavy, the breathing of those who had walked a great distance under the open sky.

Jesus stood among them. He was not seated upon a high place, nor lifted above the crowd. He stood upon the same ground, the same dust beneath his feet. His garment was plain, the color of undyed wool. His voice, when he spoke, carried across the gathered company without strain, reaching even those who lingered at the far edges of the assembly.

He called out to them — not to the strong, not to the swift, not to those who stood tall and unburdened. He called to those who labored. He called to those who were heavy laden. He bade them come unto him.

The words moved through the crowd. A man near the front shifted his weight from one foot to the other, his back stooped beneath years of carrying. A woman adjusted the child upon her hip and turned her face toward the speaker. An old man at the edge of the gathering lifted his head.

Jesus spoke again. He told them he would give them rest.

The air was still. No wind stirred the branches of the few trees that stood nearby. The sun pressed down upon the assembly, and the shadows were short and dark upon the ground. A donkey tied to a post near the road stamped once and was quiet.

He told them to take his yoke upon them. He told them to learn of him.

Some among the crowd looked at one another. Others did not move at all but stood fixed, their eyes upon him. A young man near the center of the gathering lowered himself slowly to his knees upon the hard ground, his hands resting open upon his thighs.

Jesus declared himself meek and lowly in heart. He told them they would find rest unto their souls.

His voice did not rise to a shout, nor did it fall to a whisper. It held steady across the open space, clear as water drawn from a deep well. The crowd was silent. Not even the children stirred. The only sound beyond his voice was the distant call of a bird passing overhead, a single sharp note against the wide and cloudless expanse above.

He spoke once more. He said his yoke was easy. He said his burden was light.

Then he was still. The words hung in the air above the gathering like the last tone of a struck bell before it fades. No one moved at first. The dust lay undisturbed upon the ground. The stones along the road held their places as they had held them for generations.

Slowly, movement returned to the crowd. A woman near the back lowered herself to sit upon the earth, her hands folded in her lap, her face turned upward. Two men who had been standing apart from one another stepped closer together, their sandals scraping against the dry ground. The old man with the staff took one step forward, then another, his progress slow and deliberate, moving toward the place where Jesus stood.

Others followed. They came not in a rush, not pressing forward as a crowd surges toward a gate, but one by one, each at their own pace. A man carrying a heavy bundle upon his back set it down beside the road and walked forward with his arms at his sides. A woman with dust upon her brow wiped her face with the edge of her head covering and moved through the gathering toward the front.

The sun continued its passage overhead. The shadows began, almost imperceptibly, to lengthen upon the ground. The crowd drew nearer, closing the distance between themselves and the one who had spoken. Their feet left prints in the dust — prints of the weary, the burdened, the bent, the slow — all pressing forward along the dry and ancient road.

Jesus stood where he had stood, and he did not turn away. The dust settled. The air was warm and close. And the people came.
