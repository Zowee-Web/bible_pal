The road climbed gradually from the lakeside villages toward the hillside where Jesus stood teaching. Dust rose from the sandals of those walking along the path — fishermen with salt still crusting their forearms, women carrying water jars balanced on their shoulders, laborers whose tunics were stained dark with sweat. A few older men moved slowly, leaning on walking sticks, their backs curved forward from years of bending over stone or soil. The crowd gathered in an uneven half-circle on the slope, some standing, some lowering themselves onto the dry grass. A child sat on a man's shoulders near the back, gripping his father's hair.

The afternoon sun pressed down on the hillside. Insects droned in the scrub brush along the edges of the road. A breeze moved through the group, carrying the smell of wild thyme from the rocky ground nearby. Jesus stood slightly above them on the slope, his voice carrying clearly across the open space.

He had been speaking already — about the towns that had seen his works, about the things revealed to little children rather than to the wise. Now he paused. The crowd shifted. A woman near the front adjusted the bundle she carried, setting it down at her feet. A man wiped his face with the back of his hand. Somewhere behind the group, a donkey brayed and was quiet again.

Then Jesus opened his mouth and said, "Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest."

His voice was steady, unhurried. The words moved across the hillside like the breeze itself, reaching the people standing at the edges. A man near the middle of the crowd straightened slightly, his hands hanging at his sides. A woman holding a small child against her hip turned her face fully toward Jesus. The older men leaning on their sticks were still.

No one spoke. The only sound was the wind brushing through the dry grass and the faint creak of a wooden cart somewhere down the road behind them.

Jesus continued. "Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart."

He lifted one hand as he spoke, an open gesture, palm facing the crowd. His sandals were dusty, his robe plain. He stood among them as a man who had walked the same roads they walked, who had eaten bread and olives at the same kind of rough tables, who had slept under the same open sky when no roof was offered. His shoulders were broad from years of work with wood and stone before he began teaching.

A young man near the front shifted his weight from one foot to the other. Beside him, a woman set down a heavy clay jar she had been holding against her chest. The group pressed slightly closer, feet shuffling in the dirt, bodies leaning forward.

"And you will find rest for your souls," Jesus said.

The words hung in the warm air. A bird passed overhead, its shadow flickering across the ground. The child on the man's shoulders stopped fidgeting and was quiet. The woman who had set down her bundle remained still, her hands open at her sides.

Jesus looked across the faces gathered before him — lined faces, young faces, faces darkened by long days under the sun, faces marked by the years of carrying loads along roads like the one that had brought them here. His gaze moved from one person to the next without hurrying.

"For my yoke is easy," he said, "and my burden is light."

Then he was quiet. The silence that followed was not empty. It was filled with the sound of the wind moving through the grass, the distant bleating of a goat on the next hill, the breathing of the crowd standing close together on the slope. A man at the back of the group lowered himself to the ground and sat cross-legged in the dust, resting his elbows on his knees. A woman beside him knelt, her hands folded in her lap. Others remained standing, their faces turned toward Jesus.

The sun had moved lower, casting longer shadows from the scrub brush across the hillside. The light was warmer now, less harsh, falling across the group at an angle. A few people at the edges of the crowd took steps forward, closing the distance between themselves and the place where Jesus stood. Their sandals scraped softly against the stony ground.

No one turned to leave. The fishermen with their rough hands, the mothers with children balanced on their hips, the old men bent over their walking sticks — they stayed where they were. A young girl crouched near her mother, drawing lines in the dust with her finger. Two men who had been standing apart from the group moved closer together, shoulder to shoulder, facing Jesus.

The breeze picked up again, carrying the scent of dry earth and wild herbs. Jesus remained where he stood on the slope, looking out over the gathered people. His hand, which had been raised, came to rest at his side. The crowd was still, and the hillside was quiet except for the wind and the distant sound of water from the lake far below.
