The road climbed slowly through dry hills east of the lake. Dust lay thick on the path, kicked up by sandals and the hooves of a few donkeys carrying bundles of grain. The afternoon sun pressed down, and the travelers moved with heavy steps. Some carried loads strapped across their shoulders — rolled leather, clay jars, baskets of dried fish. A woman shifted a bundle of firewood from one hip to the other. An older man leaned on a walking stick, his back curved forward.

At a wide place where the road leveled off near a cluster of fig trees, a crowd had gathered. People sat on the ground or stood in loose groups, their faces streaked with sweat. Some had come from nearby villages. Others had walked since morning. A few children crouched in the shade of a low stone wall, picking at the dirt.

Jesus stood among them. He had been teaching, and his voice carried across the open ground. The crowd pressed closer, some shielding their eyes from the glare.

He spoke.

"Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest."

A man near the front set down the heavy sack he had been holding against his chest. A woman standing at the edge of the group lowered herself onto a flat rock, her sandals worn nearly through. The crowd was quiet. A breeze stirred the leaves of the fig trees, and somewhere behind the wall a goat bleated once.

Jesus continued, his voice steady and clear enough to reach those standing at the back.

"Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls."

A farmer near the middle of the crowd shifted his weight. His hands, rough and cracked from work, hung at his sides. Two men who had been murmuring to each other stopped and turned toward the speaker. A young woman holding a child adjusted the infant against her shoulder and stood still, listening.

"For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light."

No one moved for a long moment. The words hung in the warm air. Then the older man with the walking stick took a step forward, closer to where Jesus stood. A few others did the same — a boy carrying a water jar, a woman with flour dust on her hands, a laborer whose tunic was dark with sweat across the shoulders.

The fig trees cast their short shadows on the ground. The crowd remained gathered around Jesus in the open space beside the road, the dust settling slowly around their feet. The breeze came again, moving through the dry grass beyond the wall.
