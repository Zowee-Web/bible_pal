As Jesus passed by from Capernaum, he beheld a man named Matthew sitting at the receipt of custom. The booth stood beside the way, and the coins were laid in order upon the wooden boards. Matthew was bent over his accounts, and the noise of the market rose about him.

Jesus said unto him, "Follow me." And Matthew arose and followed him. He left the coins and the boards behind, and he walked away from the booth, and looked not back.

And it came to pass, as he sat at meat in the house, behold, many publicans and sinners came and sat down with Jesus and his disciples. They sat at the table together, and there was bread and fish set before them.

And when the Pharisees beheld it, they said unto his disciples, "Why eateth your Master with publicans and sinners?"

When Jesus heard it, he said unto them, "They that be whole need not a physician, but they that are sick."

He said, "But go ye and learn what that meaneth, 'I will have mercy, and not sacrifice.' For I am not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance."

Matthew sat at the table and hearkened unto the words of Jesus. The lamps were lit within the house, and the room was full of them with whom the Pharisees would not eat.

The afternoon turned unto evening, and they that had been called sat with the teacher who had come to call them. Matthew returned not unto the booth that day, nor any day thereafter.

He gathered up his garment and walked behind Jesus when the meal was ended. The dust of the way was warm beneath his feet, and the streets of Capernaum stretched out before them. The booth he had left was already empty, and another man was bent over the tables in his stead.
