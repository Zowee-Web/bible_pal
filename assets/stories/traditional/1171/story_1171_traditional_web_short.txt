As Jesus passed by from Capernaum, he saw a man called Matthew sitting at the tax collector's place. The booth stood beside the road, and the coins were stacked on the wooden boards. Matthew was bent over his accounts, and the noise of the market rose around him.

Jesus said to him, "Follow me." Matthew got up and followed him. He left the coins and the boards behind, and he walked away from the booth without looking back.

It happened, as he sat in the house, behold, many tax collectors and sinners came and sat down with Jesus and his disciples. They sat at the table together, and there was bread and fish before them.

When the Pharisees saw it, they said to his disciples, "Why does your teacher eat with tax collectors and sinners?"

When Jesus heard it, he said to them, "Those who are healthy have no need for a physician, but those who are sick do."

He said, "But you go and learn what this means: 'I desire mercy, and not sacrifice.' For I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance."

Matthew sat at the table and listened to the words of Jesus. The lamps were lit in the house, and the room was full of those whom the Pharisees would not eat with.

The afternoon turned into evening, and the people who had been called sat with the teacher who had come to call them. Matthew did not return to the booth that day or any day after.

He gathered his garment and walked behind Jesus when the meal was ended. The dust of the road was warm under his feet, and the streets of Capernaum stretched out before them. The booth he had left was already empty, and another man was bent over the tables in his place.
