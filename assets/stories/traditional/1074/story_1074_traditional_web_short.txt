One afternoon, Jesus sat on the hillside with a great crowd of people gathered around him. The grass was green beneath them. A warm breeze moved through the field.

Jesus spoke to them.

"Do not worry about your life," he said. "Do not worry about what you will eat or what you will drink. Do not worry about what you will put on your body. Is not life more than food? Is not the body more than clothing?"

He lifted his hand toward the sky.

"Look at the birds of the air," he said.

Above them, small birds circled and dipped. Their wings caught the light.

"They do not sow," Jesus said. "They do not reap. They do not gather into barns. And your heavenly Father feeds them. Are you not of more value than they?"

The people looked up at the birds. The birds swooped low and climbed again.

"And which of you by worrying can add a single hour to his life?"

No one answered.

Jesus pointed to the hillside around them, where wildflowers grew in bright clusters — purple and white and yellow — swaying gently in the breeze.

"And why do you worry about clothing?" he said. "Consider the lilies of the field. They do not work. They do not spin thread. Yet I tell you that even Solomon in all his glory was not dressed like one of these."

The flowers bent and straightened in the wind.

"If God so clothes the grass of the field, which is here today and tomorrow is thrown into the fire, will he not much more clothe you? O you of little faith."

Jesus looked across their faces.

"So do not worry. Do not say, 'What shall we eat?' Do not say, 'What shall we drink?' Do not say, 'What shall we wear?' The Gentiles seek after all these things. Your heavenly Father knows that you need them all."

He paused. The wind moved through the grass.

"But seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness," Jesus said, "and all these things will be added to you."

The crowd sat still. A bird landed on a stone nearby, turned its small head, and hopped once.

"Therefore do not worry about tomorrow," Jesus said. "For tomorrow will worry about itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own."

The hillside was quiet. The wildflowers swayed. The birds circled overhead, and the people sat in the grass, listening.
