One afternoon in Galilee, Jesus walked up a hillside. The path was dry and dusty under his sandals. The grass on either side of the trail was tall and green, still fresh from the spring rains. Wildflowers grew in patches along the slope — purple and white and yellow — bending gently in the breeze.

A great crowd followed him up the hill.

There were men and women. There were old people walking slowly with sticks. There were young mothers carrying babies against their shoulders. There were children running ahead, ducking through the tall grass and popping up again with bright faces.

The crowd spread out across the hillside as Jesus found a place to sit. He settled onto a flat stretch of ground near the top of the slope. The people arranged themselves below him on the green grass. Some sat cross-legged. Some leaned back on their hands. Some stood at the edges, shading their eyes from the sun.

The sky above was wide and blue. A few small clouds drifted slowly. The breeze came up warm from the valley below and carried the smell of grass and earth and flowers.

Jesus had been teaching them many things. He had spoken about the poor in spirit and the peacemakers. He had spoken about salt and light and about the law of Moses.

Now he spoke to them about worry.

"Do not worry about your life," Jesus said. His voice carried across the hillside. "Do not worry about what you will eat or what you will drink."

The people listened. A baby fussed somewhere in the crowd, and its mother shifted it to the other arm.

"Do not worry about your body," Jesus said, "or what you will put on."

He looked across the faces before him. Some were weathered and brown from working in the sun. Some were soft and young. All of them were still.

"Is not life more than food?" he asked. "Is not the body more than clothing?"

No one answered. The breeze moved through their hair and the edges of their robes.

Jesus lifted his hand and pointed upward.

"Look at the birds of the air," he said.

The people looked up. Above the hillside, small birds circled. There were sparrows turning in quick arcs. There were swallows dipping low and rising again. Their wings flashed dark and then bright as they caught the sunlight.

"They do not sow," Jesus said.

A sparrow dove past, close enough to hear the quick flutter of its wings.

"They do not reap."

Another bird climbed high, riding the warm air that rose from the valley floor.

"They do not gather into barns."

Jesus lowered his hand.

"And your heavenly Father feeds them."

The birds continued their circles. One of them landed on a stone a short distance from the crowd. It hopped twice, pecked at something in the dirt, and cocked its small head to one side.

"Are you not of more value than they?" Jesus said.

The crowd was quiet. The bird on the stone flew off again, its wings beating fast.

Jesus spoke again.

"And which of you by worrying can add a single hour to his life?"

He let the question sit in the air. The wind stirred the tall grass. No one said a word. A child near the front of the crowd picked at the clover growing beside her knee.

Then Jesus gestured to the hillside around them. Wildflowers grew everywhere — thick clusters of them scattered through the green grass like splashes of paint. They grew between the seated people's feet, along the edges of the path, up and over the crest of the hill.

"And why do you worry about clothing?" Jesus said. "Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow."

The flowers swayed. Their thin stems bent and straightened in the breeze. The petals were delicate — white ones with soft yellow centers, purple ones with deep veins of color, small red ones close to the ground.

"They do not labor," Jesus said. "They do not spin."

The people looked at the flowers around them. A man near the middle of the crowd reached out and touched a white petal with one finger. It was thin and smooth.

"Yet I tell you," Jesus said, and he paused.

The hillside was silent except for the wind.

"Even Solomon in all his glory was not dressed like one of these."

The people knew about Solomon. They knew about the great king's palace and his fine robes made of purple and gold thread. They knew about the jewels and the cedar wood and the ivory.

And here were the wildflowers, growing out of plain dirt on a Galilean hillside, with no one to tend them and no one to water them. The sunlight fell across their petals and made them glow.

"If God so clothes the grass of the field," Jesus said, "which is alive today and tomorrow is thrown into the oven —"

He looked across the crowd.

"Will he not much more clothe you?"

His voice was steady and warm.

"O you of little faith."

The words were gentle. Not sharp. The breeze blew, and a handful of white petals lifted from a cluster of flowers and tumbled softly across the ground.

Jesus continued.

"So do not worry," he said. "Do not say, 'What shall we eat?'"

He shook his head slowly.

"Do not say, 'What shall we drink?'"

A cloud passed over the sun, and the light dimmed for a moment. Then the cloud moved on, and the hillside brightened again.

"Do not say, 'What shall we wear?'"

Jesus opened his hands.

"For the Gentiles seek after all these things," he said. "They chase after food and drink and clothing. They spend their days running after what they need."

He brought his hands together.

"But your heavenly Father knows that you need them all."

An old woman near the front nodded slowly. A man beside her rubbed his rough, work-hardened palms together.

"But seek first the kingdom of God," Jesus said, "and his righteousness."

He leaned forward.

"And all these things will be added to you."

The breeze came again, stronger this time. It bent the flowers and flattened the tall grass and lifted the hair from the foreheads of the people sitting on the slope. A line of birds — five or six of them — crossed the sky in a loose formation, heading toward the far side of the valley.

Jesus spoke once more.

"Therefore do not worry about tomorrow," he said.

The crowd was perfectly still. The children had stopped fidgeting. The babies had gone quiet.

"For tomorrow will worry about itself."

A single bird broke away from the group overhead and circled back, tracing a wide loop against the blue sky.

"Each day has enough trouble of its own."

The hillside was quiet. The afternoon sun lay warm across the green grass and the scattered flowers. The birds moved in their slow circles above the valley. Down below, the lake shimmered, flat and silver. A child in the front row leaned against her mother's arm. The wildflowers swayed and straightened, swayed and straightened. Jesus sat on the hillside among the people, and the breeze moved over all of them.
