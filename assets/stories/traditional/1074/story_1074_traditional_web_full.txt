One afternoon, Jesus sat on a hillside in Galilee. A large crowd had gathered around him on the green slope. The sky above was wide and blue. A soft wind came up from the valley below, bending the tall grass.

Men and women and children sat on the ground. Some leaned forward. Some held their knees. All of them were listening.

Jesus spoke to them about worry.

"Do not worry about your life," he said. "Do not worry about what you will eat or what you will drink. Do not worry about your body — what you will put on."

He looked across the crowd. Faces young and old watched him.

"Is not life more than food?" he asked. "Is not the body more than clothing?"

The breeze stirred the edges of their robes.

Jesus lifted his hand and pointed to the sky.

"Look at the birds of the air," he said.

Above the hillside, small birds circled. They dipped and rose, their wings dark against the bright sky. One swooped low over the heads of the people and climbed again.

"They do not sow," Jesus said. "They do not reap. They do not gather into barns."

The birds turned in wide arcs, catching the wind.

"And your heavenly Father feeds them."

Jesus lowered his hand.

"Are you not of more value than they?"

The crowd was still. No one spoke.

"And which of you by worrying," Jesus said, "can add a single hour to his life?"

He let the question rest. The wind blew gently.

Then Jesus pointed to the hillside around them. Wildflowers grew among the grass in thick patches — purple and white and yellow and red. They grew right up to the edge of where the people sat. Some of the children had flowers near their feet.

"And why do you worry about clothing?" Jesus said. "Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow."

The flowers swayed in the breeze, their petals bright and soft.

"They do not labor," he said. "They do not spin thread."

He paused.

"Yet I tell you that even Solomon in all his glory was not dressed like one of these."

The people looked at the flowers. They were small and simple, growing wild on the hillside. The sunlight fell on their petals and made them glow.

"If God so clothes the grass of the field," Jesus said, "which is alive today and tomorrow is thrown into the oven, will he not much more clothe you?"

He looked across the crowd.

"O you of little faith."

The words were gentle. The wind moved through the field.

"So do not worry," Jesus said. "Do not say, 'What shall we eat?' Do not say, 'What shall we drink?' Do not say, 'What shall we wear?'"

He shook his head slowly.

"For the Gentiles seek after all these things."

A bird landed on a flat stone just beyond the edge of the crowd. It tilted its head and pecked at something in the dust.

"Your heavenly Father knows that you need them all," Jesus said.

The bird hopped twice and flew off again.

Jesus leaned forward.

"But seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness," he said, "and all these things will be added to you."

The crowd did not stir. The grass swayed around them. A child near the front pulled a stem of clover from the ground and twisted it between small fingers.

Jesus spoke once more.

"Therefore do not worry about tomorrow," he said. "For tomorrow will worry about itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own."

The hillside was quiet. The sun was warm on the faces of the people. A line of birds crossed the sky, flying steadily toward the far hills. The wildflowers bent and straightened in the breeze, their petals catching the afternoon light. The crowd sat still in the green grass, and Jesus sat among them.
