One afternoon in Galilee, Jesus went up into a hillside. The path was dry and dusty beneath his sandals. The grass on either side of the way was tall and green, yet fresh from the spring rains. Wildflowers grew in patches along the slope — purple and white and yellow — bending gently in the breeze.

A great multitude followed him up the hill.

There were men and women. There were old people walking slowly with staves. There were young mothers bearing babes against their shoulders. There were children running ahead, ducking through the tall grass and popping up again with bright faces.

The multitude spread abroad across the hillside as Jesus found a place to sit. He settled upon a flat stretch of ground near the top of the slope. The people arranged themselves below him upon the green grass. Some sat cross-legged. Some leaned back upon their hands. Some stood at the edges, shading their eyes from the sun.

The sky above was wide and blue. A few small clouds drifted slowly. The breeze came up warm from the valley below and bore the savour of grass and earth and flowers.

Jesus had been teaching them many things. He had spoken concerning the poor in spirit and the peacemakers. He had spoken concerning salt and light, and concerning the law of Moses.

Now he spake unto them concerning thought and care.

"Take no thought for your life," Jesus said. His voice carried across the hillside. "Take no thought what ye shall eat, or what ye shall drink."

The people hearkened. A babe fretted somewhere in the multitude, and its mother shifted it to the other arm.

"Take no thought for your body," Jesus said, "what ye shall put on."

He looked across the faces before him. Some were weathered and brown from labouring in the sun. Some were soft and young. All of them were still.

"Is not the life more than meat?" he asked. "And the body than raiment?"

No man answered. The breeze moved through their hair and the edges of their robes.

Jesus lifted up his hand and pointed upward.

"Behold the fowls of the air," he said.

The people lifted up their eyes. Above the hillside, small birds circled. There were sparrows turning in quick arcs. There were swallows dipping low and rising again. Their wings flashed dark and then bright as they caught the sunlight.

"They sow not," Jesus said.

A sparrow dove past, close enough that one could hear the quick flutter of its wings.

"Neither do they reap."

Another bird climbed high, riding the warm air that rose from the valley floor.

"Nor gather into barns."

Jesus lowered his hand.

"Yet your heavenly Father feedeth them."

The birds continued their circles. One of them alighted upon a stone a short distance from the multitude. It hopped twice, pecked at somewhat in the dirt, and cocked its small head to one side.

"Are ye not much better than they?" Jesus said.

The multitude was quiet. The bird upon the stone flew off again, its wings beating fast.

Jesus spake again.

"And which of you by taking thought can add one cubit unto his stature?"

He let the question sit in the air. The wind stirred the tall grass. No man spake. A child near the front of the multitude picked at the clover growing beside her knee.

Then Jesus gestured unto the hillside round about them. Wildflowers grew everywhere — thick clusters of them scattered through the green grass like splashes of paint. They grew betwixt the seated people's feet, along the edges of the path, up and over the crest of the hill.

"And why take ye thought for raiment?" Jesus said. "Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow."

The flowers swayed. Their thin stems bent and straightened in the breeze. The petals were delicate — white ones with soft yellow centres, purple ones with deep veins of colour, small red ones close to the ground.

"They toil not," Jesus said. "Neither do they spin."

The people beheld the flowers round about them. A man in the midst of the multitude reached out and touched a white petal with one finger. It was thin and smooth.

"And yet I say unto you," Jesus said, and he paused.

The hillside was silent save for the wind.

"Even Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these."

The people knew concerning Solomon. They knew concerning the great king's palace and his fine robes made of purple and gold thread. They knew concerning the jewels and the cedar wood and the ivory.

And here were the wildflowers, growing out of plain dirt upon a Galilean hillside, with no man to tend them and no man to water them. The sunlight fell across their petals and made them glow.

"Wherefore, if God so clothe the grass of the field," Jesus said, "which today is, and tomorrow is cast into the oven —"

He looked across the multitude.

"Shall he not much more clothe you?"

His voice was steady and warm.

"O ye of little faith."

The words were gentle. Not sharp. The breeze blew, and a handful of white petals lifted from a cluster of flowers and tumbled softly across the ground.

Jesus continued.

"Therefore take no thought," he said. "Say not, 'What shall we eat?'"

He shook his head slowly.

"Say not, 'What shall we drink?'"

A cloud passed over the sun, and the light dimmed for a moment. Then the cloud moved on, and the hillside brightened again.

"Say not, 'Wherewithal shall we be clothed?'"

Jesus opened his hands.

"For after all these things do the Gentiles seek," he said. "They run after meat and drink and raiment. They spend their days seeking that whereof they have need."

He brought his hands together.

"But your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things."

An old woman near the front nodded slowly. A man beside her rubbed his rough, work-hardened palms together.

"But seek ye first the kingdom of God," Jesus said, "and his righteousness."

He leaned forward.

"And all these things shall be added unto you."

The breeze came again, stronger this time. It bent the flowers and flattened the tall grass and lifted the hair from the foreheads of the people sitting upon the slope. A line of birds — five or six of them — crossed the sky in a loose formation, heading toward the far side of the valley.

Jesus spake once more.

"Take therefore no thought for the morrow," he said.

The multitude was perfectly still. The children had ceased fidgeting. The babes had gone quiet.

"For the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself."

A single bird brake away from the company overhead and circled back, tracing a wide loop against the blue sky.

"Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof."

The hillside was quiet. The afternoon sun lay warm across the green grass and the scattered flowers. The birds moved in their slow circles above the valley. Down below, the lake shimmered, flat and silver. A child in the front leaned against her mother's arm. The wildflowers swayed and straightened, swayed and straightened. Jesus sat upon the hillside among the people, and the breeze moved over all of them.
