One afternoon, Jesus sat upon a hillside in Galilee. A great multitude had gathered round about him on the green slope. The sky above was wide and blue. A soft wind came up from the valley below, bending the tall grass.

Men and women and children sat upon the ground. Some leaned forward. Some held their knees. All of them were hearkening.

Jesus spake unto them concerning thought and care.

"Take no thought for your life," he said. "Take no thought what ye shall eat, or what ye shall drink. Take no thought for your body — what ye shall put on."

He looked across the multitude. Faces young and old beheld him.

"Is not the life more than meat?" he asked. "And the body than raiment?"

The breeze stirred the edges of their robes.

Jesus lifted up his hand and pointed toward the sky.

"Behold the fowls of the air," he said.

Above the hillside, small birds circled. They dipped and rose, their wings dark against the bright sky. One swooped low over the heads of the people and climbed again.

"They sow not," Jesus said. "Neither do they reap, nor gather into barns."

The birds turned in wide arcs, catching the wind.

"Yet your heavenly Father feedeth them."

Jesus lowered his hand.

"Are ye not much better than they?"

The multitude was still. No man spake.

"And which of you by taking thought," Jesus said, "can add one cubit unto his stature?"

He let the question rest. The wind blew gently.

Then Jesus pointed unto the hillside round about them. Wildflowers grew among the grass in thick patches — purple and white and yellow and red. They grew right up unto the edge of where the people sat. Some of the children had flowers near their feet.

"And why take ye thought for raiment?" Jesus said. "Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow."

The flowers swayed in the breeze, their petals bright and soft.

"They toil not," he said. "Neither do they spin."

He paused.

"And yet I say unto you, that even Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these."

The people beheld the flowers. They were small and simple, growing wild upon the hillside. The sunlight fell upon their petals and made them glow.

"Wherefore, if God so clothe the grass of the field," Jesus said, "which today is, and tomorrow is cast into the oven, shall he not much more clothe you?"

He looked across the multitude.

"O ye of little faith."

The words were gentle. The wind moved through the field.

"Therefore take no thought," Jesus said. "Say not, 'What shall we eat?' Say not, 'What shall we drink?' Say not, 'Wherewithal shall we be clothed?'"

He shook his head slowly.

"For after all these things do the Gentiles seek."

A bird alighted upon a flat stone just beyond the edge of the multitude. It tilted its head and pecked at somewhat in the dust.

"For your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things," Jesus said.

The bird hopped twice and flew off again.

Jesus leaned forward.

"But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness," he said, "and all these things shall be added unto you."

The multitude did not stir. The grass swayed round about them. A child near the front plucked a stem of clover from the ground and twisted it betwixt small fingers.

Jesus spake once more.

"Take therefore no thought for the morrow," he said. "For the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof."

The hillside was quiet. The sun was warm upon the faces of the people. A line of birds crossed the sky, flying steadily toward the far hills. The wildflowers bent and straightened in the breeze, their petals catching the afternoon light. The multitude sat still in the green grass, and Jesus sat among them.
