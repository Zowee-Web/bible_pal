One afternoon, Jesus sat upon the hillside with a great multitude of people gathered round about him. The grass was green beneath them. A warm breeze passed through the field.

Jesus spake unto them.

"Take no thought for your life," he said. "Take no thought what ye shall eat, or what ye shall drink. Take no thought for your body, what ye shall put on. Is not the life more than meat? And the body than raiment?"

He lifted up his hand toward the sky.

"Behold the fowls of the air," he said.

Above them, small birds circled and dipped. Their wings caught the light.

"They sow not," Jesus said. "Neither do they reap, nor gather into barns. Yet your heavenly Father feedeth them. Are ye not much better than they?"

The people lifted up their eyes unto the birds. The birds swooped low and arose again.

"And which of you by taking thought can add one cubit unto his stature?"

No man answered.

Jesus pointed unto the hillside round about them, where wildflowers grew in bright clusters — purple and white and yellow — bending gently in the breeze.

"And why take ye thought for raiment?" he said. "Consider the lilies of the field. They toil not. Neither do they spin. And yet I say unto you, that even Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these."

The flowers bent and straightened in the wind.

"Wherefore, if God so clothe the grass of the field, which today is, and tomorrow is cast into the oven, shall he not much more clothe you, O ye of little faith?"

Jesus looked upon their faces.

"Therefore take no thought, saying, 'What shall we eat?' or 'What shall we drink?' or 'Wherewithal shall we be clothed?' For after all these things do the Gentiles seek. For your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things."

He paused. The wind moved through the grass.

"But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness," Jesus said, "and all these things shall be added unto you."

The multitude sat still. A bird alighted upon a stone nearby, turned its small head, and hopped once.

"Take therefore no thought for the morrow," Jesus said. "For the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof."

The hillside was quiet. The wildflowers swayed. The birds circled overhead, and the people sat in the grass, hearkening.
