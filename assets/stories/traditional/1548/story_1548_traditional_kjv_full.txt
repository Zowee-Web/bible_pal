And on the morrow, about the sixth hour drawing nigh, the men whom Cornelius had sent were yet upon the road. They had departed Caesarea the day before and journeyed northward along the coastal plain, and now they drew near unto the city of Joppa. The sun stood high above them, and the heat of the day was upon the land.

And Peter went up upon the housetop to pray, for it was the sixth hour. The roof was open unto the heavens, and beneath him the narrow ways of Joppa ran down toward the sea. The clay tiles held the warmth of the sun beneath his feet. A thin wind stirred from off the water.

Now he was exceeding hungry. The household below made ready the midday meal, and the savour of it rose up through the house. But while they yet prepared, there fell upon him a trance.

And he beheld the sky rent open above him. A thing descended from on high — a great vessel coming down slowly, as a vast linen held at its four corners by hands unseen. It drew near unto the earth, swaying gently as it came, and Peter perceived what manner of things were gathered within it.

Every kind of living creature was there. Beasts of the field upon four feet. Wild things of the earth. Creatures that crept upon the ground. Birds of the air. All of them stirring, all gathered together in the folds of that cloth. Creatures which the law declared clean and creatures which it forbade — dwelling side by side, mingled together.

Then a voice spake unto him. It was clear and without wavering. "Arise, Peter. Slay, and eat."

And Peter answered straightway. "By no means, Lord. For never have I taken into my mouth any thing that is common or defiled."

The voice came unto him a second time, bearing the same authority, the same sureness as before. "That which God hath made clean, thou shalt not call common."

Peter looked upon the vessel and the creatures that moved within it. The voice had set aside his refusal. He had spoken his objection, and the voice had answered it.

And this thing was done three times over. Three times the vessel was set before him. Three times the words passed between them. Then was the vessel taken up again into the heavens, rising after the manner in which it had come down, and the sky closed above it. And Peter was left alone upon the housetop.

The sun yet stood overhead. The tiles yet held their warmth. The wind yet bore the smell of salt from the harbour below. And Peter sat upon the roof, pressing his hands against his knees, his eyes fixed upon the empty sky where the vessel had been.

And while he yet turned these things over in his mind, a thing was happening below, at the level of the street.

The men from Cornelius had come. They had journeyed from Caesarea, enquiring after the way as they went, and now they stood before the gate of the house of Simon the tanner. They were three in number. And they called out from the entrance, asking whether one Simon, who was called also Peter, lodged there.

Now Peter was yet upon the roof, yet occupied with that which he had seen, when the Spirit spake unto him. The words were plain and unadorned. "Behold, three men seek thee. Rise, and go down unto them. Go with them, doubting nothing, for I have sent them."

And Peter rose up. He crossed the housetop and went down through the house. He came unto the gate and beheld the three men standing there in the street, covered with the dust of the road, waiting. He looked upon them.

"I am he whom ye seek," he said. "For what cause are ye come?"

And they told him. Their master was one Cornelius, a centurion garrisoned at Caesarea. "He giveth alms unto the people and prayeth unto God continually," they said. "He is well regarded among all the nation of the Jews." And a holy messenger had appeared unto him and had charged him to send for Peter — to bring him unto the house of Cornelius, that Cornelius might hear whatsoever Peter would speak.

And Peter hearkened unto them. These were men of the Gentiles, servants of a Roman officer, standing at the gate of a house in Israel. He turned and looked back over his shoulder, up toward the housetop where the open sky yet held the last light of the noon — a vessel filled with every manner of creature, the clean and the unclean together, and a voice declaring unto him that what God hath cleansed, he must not call common.

He called the men within. He gave them lodging for the night. They ate together. They took their rest. And the house of Simon the tanner sheltered them all beneath one roof.

And when the morning was come, Peter made ready and went forth with them. And certain of the brethren from Joppa accompanied him, walking at his side. The company passed together through the gate and out upon the road that led northward along the coast toward Caesarea. The sun was risen. The road stretched before them, and Peter walked with the men whom Cornelius had sent, and the brethren from Joppa kept pace alongside.
