The next day, around noon, the men sent from Cornelius were still traveling. They had been on the road since the day before, moving north along the coastal plain, and now they were drawing near to the city of Joppa. The sun was high and the air was hot.

Peter went up to the flat roof of the house to pray. It was the sixth hour. The rooftop was open to the sky, and below him the narrow streets of Joppa stretched toward the sea. The clay tiles were warm. A faint wind came off the water.

He was very hungry. The household was preparing the midday meal downstairs, and the smell of food drifted up through the house. But while they were still making things ready, a trance fell over him.

He saw the sky open above him. Something came down — a large object descending slowly, like a great sheet held at its four corners by invisible hands. It lowered toward the earth, swaying slightly, and as it came closer Peter could see what was inside.

Every kind of creature. Four-footed beasts of the earth. Wild animals. Things that crept along the ground. Birds of the air. All of them moving, gathered together in the folds of the cloth. Animals that the law permitted and animals that it did not, side by side.

A voice spoke to him. Clear and direct. "Rise, Peter. Kill and eat."

Peter answered immediately. "Not so, Lord. I have never eaten anything common or unclean."

The voice came again, a second time. The same tone, the same certainty. "What God has cleansed, do not call common."

Peter stared at the sheet and the creatures within it. The voice had corrected him. He had refused, and the voice had answered his refusal.

This happened three times. Three times the sheet was presented. Three times the exchange took place. Then the vessel was pulled back up into the sky, rising the way it had come, and the heavens closed above it, and Peter was alone on the rooftop.

The sun was still overhead. The tiles were still warm. The wind still carried the smell of salt from the harbor. Peter sat there, pressing his hands against his knees, his eyes fixed on the empty sky where the sheet had been.

While he was still thinking, something was happening below at the street level.

The men from Cornelius had arrived. They had traveled from Caesarea, asking directions along the way, and now they stood before the gate of Simon the tanner's house. There were three of them. They called out from the entrance, asking whether a man named Simon, who was also called Peter, was staying there.

Peter was still on the roof, still occupied with the vision, when the Spirit spoke to him. The words were plain. "Three men are looking for you. Get up. Go down to them. Go with them without hesitation, for I have sent them."

Peter stood. He crossed the rooftop and went down through the house. He came to the gate and saw the three men standing there in the street, dusty from the road, waiting. He looked at them.

"I am the one you are looking for," he said. "Why have you come?"

They told him. Their master was Cornelius, a centurion stationed in Caesarea. "He gives alms to the people and prays to God always," they said. "He is well spoken of by the whole Jewish nation." A holy angel had appeared to him and had instructed him to send for Peter — to bring him to Cornelius's house, so that Cornelius could hear whatever Peter had to say.

Peter listened. These were Gentile men, servants of a Roman officer, standing at the gate of a Jewish home. He looked back over his shoulder, up toward the rooftop where the open sky still held the last of the noon light — a sheet full of every kind of animal, clean and unclean together, and a voice telling him not to call common what God had cleansed.

He called the men inside. He gave them lodging for the night. They ate. They rested. The house of Simon the tanner held them all under one roof.

The next morning Peter got ready and went out with them. Several of the believers from Joppa came along, walking beside him. The group moved together through the gate and out onto the road that led north along the coast toward Caesarea. The sun was up. The road stretched ahead of them, and Peter walked with the men Cornelius had sent, and the brothers from Joppa kept pace alongside.
