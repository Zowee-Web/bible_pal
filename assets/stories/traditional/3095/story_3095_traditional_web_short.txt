Moses returned to Jethro, his father-in-law, with a request. He asked permission to return to his brothers in Egypt and see whether they were still alive. Jethro answered, “Go in peace.”

While Moses was still in Midian, Yahweh spoke directly: “Go, return into Egypt; for all the men who sought your life are dead.” Moses took his wife and his sons, set them on a donkey, and returned toward the land of Egypt. He carried God’s rod in his hand.

Yahweh told Moses what awaited him. He was to perform before Pharaoh all the wonders placed in his hand, yet Pharaoh would not let the people go. The message to Pharaoh carried both a command and a consequence. Moses was to speak plainly: Israel was Yahweh’s son, his firstborn. Pharaoh had refused the command to release that son to serve Yahweh, and the life of Pharaoh’s firstborn would be required.

On the way, at a lodging place, Yahweh met Moses and wanted to kill him. Zipporah took a flint, circumcised her son, and cast the foreskin at his feet. “Surely you are a bridegroom of blood to me,” she said. Then Yahweh let him alone. Zipporah again spoke of a bridegroom of blood because of the circumcision.

Yahweh also spoke to Aaron: “Go into the wilderness to meet Moses.” Aaron went to God’s mountain, met Moses there, and kissed him. Moses told Aaron every word Yahweh had given him and described all the signs he had been instructed to perform.

Together Moses and Aaron gathered all the elders of the children of Israel. Aaron spoke the words Yahweh had spoken to Moses, and he performed the signs before the people. The people believed. When they heard that Yahweh had visited the children of Israel and had seen their affliction, they bowed their heads and worshiped.
