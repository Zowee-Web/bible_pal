Moses returned unto Jethro, his father in law, with a request. He asked to return unto his brethren in Egypt and see whether they were yet alive. Jethro answered him, “Go in peace.”

While Moses was still in Midian, the Lord commanded him to return into Egypt, for all the men were dead which had sought his life. Moses set his wife and his sons upon an ass and began the return to Egypt with the rod of God in his hand.

The Lord told Moses what lay before him. He was to do before Pharaoh all the wonders which had been put in his hand, yet Pharaoh would not let the people go. Moses was to declare, “Thus saith the Lord, Israel is my son, even my firstborn.” Pharaoh had refused to let that son go and serve the Lord, and the Lord said that Pharaoh’s firstborn would be slain.

By the way, in the inn, the Lord met Moses and sought to kill him. Zipporah took a sharp stone, circumcised her son, and cast the foreskin at his feet. “Surely a bloody husband art thou to me,” she said. Then the Lord let him go. She spoke again of a bloody husband because of the circumcision.

The Lord also said unto Aaron, “Go into the wilderness to meet Moses.” Aaron went and met Moses in the mount of God, and kissed him. Moses told Aaron all the words of the Lord who had sent him and all the signs which he had commanded him.

Moses and Aaron then gathered together all the elders of the children of Israel. Aaron spake all the words which the Lord had spoken unto Moses and did the signs in the sight of the people. The people believed. When they heard that the Lord had visited the children of Israel and had looked upon their affliction, they bowed their heads and worshipped.
