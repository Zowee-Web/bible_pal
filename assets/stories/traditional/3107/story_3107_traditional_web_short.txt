While the number of the disciples was multiplying, a complaint arose from the Hellenists against the Hebrews, because their widows were neglected in the daily service. The twelve summoned the multitude of the disciples. “It is not appropriate for us to forsake the word of God and serve tables,” they said. The brothers were to select from among themselves seven men of good report, full of the Holy Spirit and of wisdom, to be appointed over this business, while the twelve continued steadfastly in prayer and in the ministry of the word.

These words pleased the whole multitude. They chose Stephen, a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit, along with Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolaus, a proselyte of Antioch. They set these seven before the apostles, and when the apostles had prayed, they laid their hands on them.

The word of God increased, and the number of the disciples greatly multiplied in Jerusalem. A great company of the priests were obedient to the faith.

Stephen, full of faith and power, performed great wonders and signs among the people. Then some from the synagogue called “The Libertines,” with Cyrenians, Alexandrians, and men of Cilicia and Asia, arose and disputed with Stephen. They were not able to withstand the wisdom and the Spirit by which he spoke.

So they secretly induced men to say, “We have heard him speak blasphemous words against Moses and God.” They stirred up the people, the elders, and the scribes, came against Stephen, seized him, and brought him into the council. There they set up false witnesses who said, “This man never stops speaking blasphemous words against this holy place and the law. For we have heard him say that this Jesus of Nazareth will destroy this place, and will change the customs which Moses delivered to us.”

All who sat in the council fastened their eyes on Stephen, and they saw his face like it was the face of an angel.
