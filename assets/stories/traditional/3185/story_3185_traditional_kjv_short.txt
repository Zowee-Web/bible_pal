The high priest and the Sadducees with him, filled with indignation, laid hands on the apostles and put them in the common prison. By night the angel of the Lord opened the doors, brought them forth, and said, “Go, stand and speak in the temple to the people all the words of this life.” They entered the temple early in the morning and taught.

The high priest and those with him called the council and all the senate of Israel together, then sent for the prisoners. The officers found the prison shut with all safety and the keepers before the doors, but no man within. The high priest, temple captain, and chief priests doubted whereunto this would grow. One reported that the men were teaching in the temple. The captain and officers brought them without violence, fearing lest the people stone them.

Before the council, the high priest accused them of disobeying the strait command not to teach in this name, filling Jerusalem with their doctrine, and intending to bring this man's blood upon them.

Peter and the other apostles answered, “We ought to obey God rather than men. The God of our fathers raised up Jesus, whom ye slew and hanged on a tree. Him hath God exalted with his right hand to be a Prince and a Saviour, for to give repentance to Israel, and forgiveness of sins. And we are his witnesses of these things; and so is also the Holy Ghost, whom God hath given to them that obey him.”

They were cut to the heart and took counsel to slay them. Gamaliel, a Pharisee and doctor of the law held in reputation among all the people, stood up and commanded the apostles to be put forth a little space. He warned the men of Israel to take heed. Theudas had boasted himself to be somebody, and about four hundred men joined him. He was slain; his followers scattered and came to nought. After him Judas of Galilee rose up in the days of the taxing and drew much people after him. He also perished, and his followers dispersed.

Gamaliel urged them to let these men alone. If their counsel or work were of men, it would come to nought. If it were of God, they could not overthrow it, lest they be found fighting against God.

They agreed, called the apostles, beat them, commanded them not to speak in the name of Jesus, and let them go. The apostles departed rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer shame for his name. Daily in the temple and in every house, they ceased not to teach and preach Jesus Christ.
