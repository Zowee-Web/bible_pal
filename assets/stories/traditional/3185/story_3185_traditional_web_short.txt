The high priest and the Sadducees with him, filled with jealousy, seized the apostles and put them in public custody. By night an angel of the Lord opened the prison doors, brought them out, and said, “Go stand and speak in the temple to the people all the words of this life.” They entered the temple about daybreak and taught.

The high priest and those with him assembled the council and all the senate of Israel, then sent for the prisoners. The officers found the prison locked and the guards at the doors, but no one inside. The high priest, temple captain, and chief priests were perplexed. Someone reported that the men were teaching in the temple. The captain and officers brought them without violence, fearing the people might stone them.

Before the council, the high priest accused them of disobeying the command not to teach in this name, filling Jerusalem with their teaching, and intending to bring this man's blood on them.

Peter and the apostles answered, “We must obey God rather than men. The God of our fathers raised up Jesus, whom you killed, hanging him on a tree. God exalted him with his right hand to be a Prince and a Savior, to give repentance to Israel, and remission of sins. We are his witnesses of these things; and so also is the Holy Spirit, whom God has given to those who obey him.”

The council were cut to the heart and determined to kill them. Gamaliel, a Pharisee and teacher of the law honored by all the people, stood up and ordered the apostles outside briefly. He warned the men of Israel to be careful. Theudas had claimed to be somebody, and about four hundred men joined him. He was slain; his followers were dispersed and came to nothing. Afterward Judas of Galilee rose up during the enrollment and drew people after him. He too perished, and his followers scattered.

Gamaliel urged them to leave these men alone. If their counsel or work was of men, it would be overthrown. If it was of God, they could not overthrow it and would be found fighting against God.

They agreed, called the apostles back, beat them, commanded them not to speak in Jesus' name, and released them. The apostles departed rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer dishonor for Jesus' name. Every day, in the temple and at home, they never stopped teaching and preaching Jesus, the Christ.
