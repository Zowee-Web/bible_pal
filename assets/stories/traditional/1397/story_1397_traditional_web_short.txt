Naomi had heard from Ruth that the barley harvest at Boaz's field had gone well; the gleanings had come in heavy each day, the corner left for the poor had not been gathered in, and Ruth had come home with grain enough for both of them. The wheat harvest was now ending. The threshing was being done at the floor on the high ground outside Bethlehem, where the men slept beside the grain to keep the watch. Naomi sat with Ruth in the evening, and she made a plan, and she said to her this.

Naomi her mother-in-law said to her, "My daughter, shall I not seek rest for you, that it may be well with you?

Now isn't Boaz our kinsman, with whose maidens you were? Behold, he will be winnowing barley tonight on the threshing floor.

Therefore wash yourself, anoint yourself, get dressed, and go down to the threshing floor, but don't make yourself known to the man until he has finished eating and drinking.

It shall be, when he lies down, that you shall note the place where he is lying. Then you shall go in, uncover his feet, and lay down. Then he will tell you what to do."

She said to her, "All that you say, I will do."

She went down to the threshing floor, and did everything that her mother-in-law told her.

When Boaz had eaten and drunk, and his heart was merry, he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain. She came softly, uncovered his feet, and laid down.

At midnight, the man was startled and turned himself; and behold, a woman lay at his feet.

He said, "Who are you?"

She answered, "I am Ruth your servant. Therefore spread the corner of your garment over your servant; for you are a near kinsman."

And the woman lay at the feet of the man at the end of the heap of grain. The moon stood high over the threshing floor at Bethlehem; the men who slept by the piles of barley did not stir; the question had been spoken and the answer had been given; and the night went on quietly with the woman of Moab at the feet of the kinsman of Elimelech the husband of Naomi.
