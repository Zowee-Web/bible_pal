A wind out of the south came gently upon the coast of Crete. The sailors felt it against their faces and counted it a fair token. They drew up anchor at Fair Havens and set their course hard along the shore, making toward Phoenix, where they purposed to abide the winter.

But the gentle wind endured not.

A great and violent wind from the northeast — which men called Euroclydon — fell down upon them from the island. It smote the ship upon her side. The vessel could not bear up against so fierce a tempest, and the crew labored no more to hold her. They gave her over and let her drive before it.

And running beneath the shelter of a small island called Clauda, they drew in the boat which had been dragged behind. It required every man among them to haul it aboard. Then they bound ropes beneath the hull, girding the planks together lest they break apart. And because they feared the great sandbanks of Syrtis along the African coast, they lowered the sea anchor and gave themselves over unto the storm, that it might carry them wheresoever it would.

On the day following, the gale had not abated. They began to cast the cargo into the sea, crate upon crate falling away into gray water. And on the third day the sailors laid hold of the tackle of the ship — the rigging, the spare gear, whatsoever their hands could seize — and with their own strength they cast it overboard.

And for many days thereafter, neither sun nor stars showed themselves. The storm gave no rest. It pressed upon them without ceasing. And all hope that they should be saved was taken from them.

Now when they had gone long without food, Paul stood forth among them upon the heaving deck. He lifted his voice above the roaring of the wind.

He told them that they ought to have hearkened unto him and not departed from Crete. Yet now he bade them be of good courage. Not one soul among them should perish. Only the ship itself would be lost.

For, he said, an angel of the God to whom he belonged and whom he served had come and stood beside him in the night. And the angel had spoken unto him, saying, "Fear not, Paul. Thou shalt be brought before Caesar. And lo, God hath granted unto thee all those who sail with thee."

Then Paul looked upon the men round about him — drenched through, hollow of face, each man gripping fast to whatsoever he could hold. And he said unto them, "Wherefore take courage. For it shall come to pass even as it was spoken unto me."

He was silent a moment. Above them the wind tore at the remnant of the sail.

"But we must be cast upon a certain island."
