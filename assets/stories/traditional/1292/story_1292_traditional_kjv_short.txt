Word of the new decree went forth from the king's palace. Couriers rode upon swift horses unto every province, from India even unto Ethiopia, bearing the letter that gave the Jews the right to stand for their own lives. The first decree of Haman, the one that had drawn the Jews unto mourning and fasting, had not been revoked — but a counter-decree now ran beside it.

The fast was ended. The sackcloth had been laid aside.

And Mordecai went out from the presence of the king in royal apparel of blue and white, and with a great crown of gold, and with a garment of fine linen and purple: and the city of Shushan rejoiced and was glad.

The Jews had light, and gladness, and joy, and honour.

And in every province, and in every city, whithersoever the king's commandment and his decree came, the Jews had joy and gladness, a feast and a good day. Tables that had been empty were filled with bread. Songs that had been silenced were lifted up again. Mourning gave way unto feasting in courtyards from Shushan unto the far ends of the empire.

And many of the people of the land became Jews; for the fear of the Jews fell upon them.

Where there had been weeping, there was wine. The houses that had been quiet sounded with music. The day that had been marked for destruction was kept instead as a day of rejoicing throughout the cities of the king.
