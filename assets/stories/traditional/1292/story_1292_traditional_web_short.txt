Word of the new decree went out from the king's palace. Couriers rode on swift horses to every province, from India to Ethiopia, carrying the letter that gave the Jews the right to stand for their own lives. The first decree of Haman, the one that had drawn the Jews to mourning and fasting, had not been revoked — but a counter-decree now ran beside it.

The fast had ended. The sackcloth had been laid aside.

Mordecai went out of the presence of the king in royal clothing of blue and white, and with a great crown of gold, and with a robe of fine linen and purple; and the city of Shushan shouted and was glad.

The Jews had light, gladness, joy, and honor.

In every province, and in every city, wherever the king's commandment and his decree came, the Jews had gladness, joy, a feast, and a good day. Tables that had been empty filled with bread. Songs that had been silenced lifted again. Mourning gave way to feasting in courtyards from Shushan to the far ends of the empire.

Many from among the peoples of the land became Jews; for the fear of the Jews was fallen on them.

Where there had been weeping, there was wine. The houses that had been quiet sounded with music. The day that had been marked for destruction was held instead as a day of rejoicing throughout the cities of the king.
