For generations the pilgrim road had been worn smooth by the feet of those who came up to Jerusalem. They came at the feast days — Passover, Weeks, Tabernacles. They came in from every direction — from Galilee in the north, from the villages of Judah, from the coast and from the wilderness. They climbed the road that led up to the city. They reached the gates of the temple courts. The trumpets sounded from the walls. The gates were opened. And there, before they crossed the threshold, the song was lifted.

A Psalm of thanksgiving.

Shout for joy to Yahweh, all the earth!

Serve Yahweh with gladness. Come before his presence with singing.

Know that Yahweh, he is God. It is he who has made us, and we are his. We are his people, and the sheep of his pasture.

Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, into his courts with praise. Give thanks to him, and bless his name.

For Yahweh is good. His loving kindness endures forever, his faithfulness to all generations.

And the pilgrims went in through the gates. The sound of the song carried into the courts and rose up over the walls. The lambs were brought. The incense burned. The day went on as the days of feast had always gone on. And so it was at the temple in Jerusalem, year after year, as the people came up to give thanks.
