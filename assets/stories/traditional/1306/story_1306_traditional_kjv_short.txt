For generations the pilgrim road had been worn smooth by the feet of them that came up unto Jerusalem. They came at the feast days — Passover, Weeks, Tabernacles. They came in from every direction — from Galilee in the north, from the villages of Judah, from the coast and from the wilderness. They climbed the road that led up unto the city. They reached the gates of the temple courts. The trumpets sounded from the walls. The gates were opened. And there, before they crossed the threshold, the song was lifted up.

A Psalm of thanksgiving.

Make a joyful noise unto the Lord, all ye lands.

Serve the Lord with gladness: come before his presence with singing.

Know ye that the Lord he is God: it is he that hath made us, and not we ourselves; we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture.

Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name.

For the Lord is good; his mercy is everlasting; and his truth endureth to all generations.

And the pilgrims went in through the gates. The sound of the song was carried into the courts and rose up over the walls. The lambs were brought. The incense burned. The day went on as the days of feast had ever gone on. And so it was at the temple in Jerusalem, year after year, as the people came up to give thanks.
