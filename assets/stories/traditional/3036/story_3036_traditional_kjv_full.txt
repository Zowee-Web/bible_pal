Nahum's burden turneth directly toward Nineveh. He that dasheth in pieces is come up, and the city heareth swift commands: keep the munition, watch the way, make the loins strong, and fortify power mightily. These commands promise no deliverance. The burden groundeth the approaching judgment in the Lord's restoring of the excellency of Jacob and Israel, whose vine branches the emptiers had marred.

Shields are red; valiant men are scarlet; chariots burn like torches; fir trees are terribly shaken. Chariots rage through the streets, justling in the broad ways and running like lightning. Worthies are recounted, yet they stumble while making haste unto the wall. Defence is prepared, but the river gates open and the palace is dissolved.

The decree carrieth the city away. Her maids mourn as doves and beat upon their breasts. Though Nineveh of old was like a pool of water, her people flee. The cry, Stand, stand, bringeth none to look back. The takers are bidden to seize silver and gold from store without end. Three words remain over the city: empty, void, and waste. Hearts melt, knees smite together, pain filleth the loins, and faces gather blackness.

The burden answereth the fallen city with a taunt. Where is the dwelling of lions, wherein lion, lioness, and whelp walked without fear? The lion had torn enough for his whelps and filled holes and dens with ravin. Now the Lord of hosts declareth, Behold, I am against thee. He will burn the chariots, let the sword devour the young lions, cut off the prey, and silence the voice of messengers.

The next woe nameth Nineveh a bloody city, full of lies and robbery, from which prey departeth not. Sound and sight press together: whip, rattling wheels, pransing horses, jumping chariots, bright sword, and glittering spear. A multitude lie slain, with carcases without end. Nineveh had sold nations and families through her corruptions.

Again the Lord of hosts saith, Behold, I am against thee. Nineveh's shame shall be uncovered before nations and kingdoms. She shall become a gazingstock. All that see her shall flee and say, Nineveh is laid waste: who will bemoan her? Then the Lord asketh, Whence shall I seek comforters for thee?

The oracle pointeth unto populous No. Waters compassed that city; the sea served for rampart and wall. Ethiopia and Egypt were her boundless strength, and Put and Lubim helped her. Yet she was carried away. Her children suffered violence, lots were cast for her honourable men, and her great men were bound. Nineveh likewise shall be overwhelmed and shall seek strength because of the enemy.

Her strong holds are likened unto fig trees bearing firstripe figs: when shaken, the fruit falleth into the eater's mouth. Her gates are opened and fire devoureth the bars. She may draw siege water, fortify, tread morter, and strengthen brickwork, yet fire and sword abide. Though she multiply as cankerworm and locust, her many merchants shall spoil and fly away. Crowned men and captains are as grasshoppers settled in cold, which vanish when the sun ariseth.

At the end, the address narroweth unto the king of Assyria. His shepherds slumber, his nobles dwell in dust, and his people scatter upon the mountains with none to gather them. Nineveh's bruise hath no healing; the wound is grievous. They that hear the bruit clap their hands, and the burden closeth with its cause: her wickedness hath passed continually upon them all.
