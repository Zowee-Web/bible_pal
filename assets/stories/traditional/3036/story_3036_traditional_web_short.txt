Nahum's oracle addresses Nineveh as the one who dashes in pieces approaches. The city is commanded to guard its fortress, watch the road, gather strength, and fortify its power. Yet the reason given reaches beyond Nineveh: Yahweh is restoring the excellency of Jacob and Israel after destroyers ruined their vine branches.

The prophecy moves in flashing images. Red shields, scarlet warriors, steel-bright chariots, and brandished spears fill the preparation. Chariots rage through streets like torches and lightning. Troops stumble as they hurry to the wall. River gates open, the palace dissolves, and servants mourn like doves. Nineveh, once like a pool of water, empties as people flee without looking back. Silver and gold are taken; the city becomes empty, void, and waste.

The oracle asks where the lions' den has gone. The lion once filled its caves with prey, but Yahweh of Armies declares, “Behold, I am against you.” Chariots will burn, the sword will consume the young lions, prey will cease, and messengers will be heard no more.

Then comes the woe against the bloody city, full of lies, robbery, and prey. The sounds of whip, wheels, horses, and chariots accompany the prophetic vision of slaughter. Nineveh's exploitation of nations is named, and Yahweh again says that he is against her. Her shame will be exposed. Those who see her ruin will flee and ask who will mourn for Nineveh; Yahweh asks where comforters for her could be found.

No-Amon, though surrounded by waters and supported by Cush, Egypt, Put, and Libya, went into captivity. Nineveh will not escape by multiplying defenses, merchants, guards, or officials. Fortresses will fall like ripe figs; fire and sword will consume. Officials will vanish like locusts when the sun rises. The king of Assyria's shepherds slumber, his nobles lie down, and his people scatter with no one to gather them. The oracle ends without healing for Nineveh's fatal wound. Those who hear clap their hands, because her endless cruelty has reached them all.
