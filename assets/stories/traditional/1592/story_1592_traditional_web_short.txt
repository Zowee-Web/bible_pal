Joseph gave a command to the steward of his house. "Fill the men's sacks with as much food as they can carry," he said, "and put each man's silver back in the mouth of his sack. And put my silver cup in the mouth of the youngest one's sack." The steward did just as Joseph said.

In the morning the brothers were sent on their way with their donkeys. They had not gone far from the city when Joseph told his steward to follow them. "Why have you repaid good with evil?" the steward said when he caught them. "Why have you taken my lord's cup?"

They protested. "Far be it from your servants to do such a thing," they said. "If it is found with any of us, let him die, and the rest will become my lord's slaves."

He searched, from the oldest to the youngest. The cup was found in Benjamin's sack.

They tore their clothes, loaded their donkeys, and went back to the city, and fell to the ground before Joseph. "What is this you have done?" he said. Judah answered, "God has found out the iniquity of your servants. We are my lord's slaves, we and the one who had the cup."

"Far be it from me to do that," Joseph said. "Only the man who had the cup will be my slave. The rest of you, go up in peace to your father."

Then Judah came near to him. "My lord," he said, "let your servant speak one word, and do not let your anger burn against me. You asked us whether we had a father or a brother. We have an aged father, and a child of his old age, the youngest. His brother is dead, and he alone is left of his mother, and his father loves him. You told us to bring him down to you. We said the boy could not leave his father, for his father would die.

"My father said to us, 'You know my wife bore me two sons. One went out from me, and I said he was surely torn to pieces, and I have not seen him since. If you take this one also, and harm comes to him, you will bring my gray head down to the grave in sorrow.'

"His life is bound up in the boy's life. If I go home without him, my father will die. I became surety for the boy to my father. So please, let me stay as your slave in his place, and let the boy go up with his brothers. For how can I go up to my father if the boy is not with me? I could not bear to see the grief that would come upon my father."
