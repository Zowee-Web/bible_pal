Early in the morning, Jesus came to the temple in Jerusalem. The sun was still low, casting long shadows across the stone courtyard. The air was cool. People were beginning to gather — men and women from the city and from the surrounding towns, coming to hear him teach. Jesus sat down on the steps near one of the colonnades, and a crowd formed around him.

Then the scribes and the Pharisees came through the crowd, pushing a woman ahead of them. They brought her to the center of the group and made her stand there, in full view of everyone. Her head was bowed. Her hands were shaking. She did not look up.

"Teacher," they said, "this woman has been caught in adultery, in the very act. Now in the law, Moses commanded us to stone such women. What do you say?"

They were not asking because they cared about the law. They were testing him. They wanted to trap him — to force him into saying something they could use against him. If he said stone her, he contradicted his own teaching about mercy. If he said let her go, he contradicted Moses.

Jesus did not answer them. He bent down and began to write on the ground with his finger. The dust of the courtyard was smooth and warm beneath his hand. He traced words or letters or lines — the scripture does not say what he wrote. He simply wrote, his head bowed, while they stood above him waiting.

They kept pressing him. "What do you say? What is your answer?"

Jesus straightened up. He looked at them — at their faces, their clenched jaws, the certainty in their eyes, the stones they were already thinking about.

"Let him who is without sin among you," he said, "be the first to throw a stone at her."

Then he bent down and wrote on the ground again.

One by one, they left. The older ones first — the ones who had lived long enough to know their own failures — and then the younger ones. They turned and walked away, dropping their accusations in the dust of the courtyard. The crowd thinned. The morning light stretched across the empty stone.

The woman stood alone in the center of the court. Jesus was still kneeling on the ground beside her.

He straightened up and looked at her.

"Woman, where are they?" he said. "Has no one condemned you?"

She looked around the courtyard. It was quiet. The accusers were gone.

"No one, Lord," she said.

Jesus said to her, "Neither do I condemn you. Go, and from now on, sin no more."
