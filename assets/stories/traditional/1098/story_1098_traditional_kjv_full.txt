Jesus went unto the mount of Olives.

And early in the morning he came again into the temple. And all the people came unto him. And he sat down, and taught them. The people gathered around him in the temple and he sat and he taught.

While he taught, the scribes and Pharisees brought unto him a woman. She had been taken in adultery. They had found her in the very act. They brought her to Jesus and they set her in the midst. They set her there in the midst of all the people, in the midst of the temple, before Jesus where he sat teaching.

They say unto him, "Master, this woman was taken in adultery, in the very act. Now Moses in the law commanded us, that such should be stoned: but what sayest thou?"

This they said, tempting him, that they might have to accuse him. The question was not a question. It was a temptation. They desired something to use against him.

But Jesus stooped down. He bent toward the ground. He wrote on the ground with his finger. He said nothing. He looked not at them. He looked not at the woman. He stooped down and with his finger wrote on the ground.

They ceased not. They continued asking him. They pressed the question. They kept asking.

So when they continued asking him, he lifted up himself, and said unto them, "He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her."

He said this. Then he stooped down again. Again he wrote on the ground. He bent down a second time. He wrote on the ground a second time. And he waited.

And they which heard it, being convicted by their own conscience, went out one by one.

The eldest went out first. Then the next. Then the next after that. One by one they departed, beginning at the eldest, even unto the last. Each one turned and walked away. The eldest first. Then each one after him. Until the last one departed.

When the last one had gone, Jesus was left alone. The woman stood yet where they had set her — in the midst. None was there to accuse her. The scribes were gone. The Pharisees were gone. They which had brought her were gone. They which had cited the law of Moses were gone. They which had said "in the very act" were gone. Every one of them had walked away.

And Jesus was left alone, and the woman standing in the midst.

When Jesus had lifted up himself, and saw none but the woman, he said unto her, "Woman, where are those thine accusers? Hath no man condemned thee?"

She said, "No man, Lord."

And Jesus said unto her, "Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more."

The woman had been brought unto the temple. She had been set in the midst. The scribes and Pharisees had spoken. They had cited the law of Moses. They had said she was taken in the very act. They had asked Jesus what he saith.

Jesus had stooped down. He had written on the ground. They had continued asking. He had lifted up himself and spoken one sentence: "He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her."

He had stooped down again. He had written on the ground again.

And they had gone out. One by one. From the eldest unto the last.

When they were all gone, Jesus lifted up himself. He spake to the woman. He did not condemn her. He told her to go. He told her to sin no more.
