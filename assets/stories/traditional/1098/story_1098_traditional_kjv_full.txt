Early in the morning Jesus came unto the temple at Jerusalem. The sun was yet low, and its light fell in long shadows across the stone court. The air was cool. Vendors were setting out their wares along the colonnades. People were gathering to hear him teach. He sat down upon the steps, and a multitude came together.

Then the scribes and Pharisees came through the crowd. They were not alone. Between them they thrust a woman, gripping her arms, steering her roughly through the people until she stood in the midst, exposed before all. Her garments were in disorder. Her head was bowed low. Her hands hung at her sides, trembling.

"Master," said they, "this woman was taken in adultery, in the very act. Now Moses in the law commanded us, that such should be stoned: but what sayest thou?"

They said this tempting him, that they might have to accuse him. If he said stone her, he contradicted his own teaching concerning mercy. If he released her, he contradicted Moses.

Jesus stooped down, and with his finger wrote on the ground, as though he heard them not. The dust of the court was smooth and warm. He traced slowly — letters, words — the scripture saith not what he wrote.

They continued asking him. He lifted up himself, and said unto them, "He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her."

And again he stooped down, and wrote on the ground.

They which heard it, being convicted by their own conscience, went out one by one, beginning at the eldest, even unto the last — each man confronting, in the silence of his heart, the distance between accusation and innocence. The court emptied. The morning light fell warm upon the bare stone.

Jesus was left alone, and the woman standing in the midst.

He lifted up himself, and looked upon her. His countenance was gentle.

"Woman, where are those thine accusers? Hath no man condemned thee?"

She looked about the quiet court. The accusers were gone.

"No man, Lord."

Jesus said unto her, "Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more."
