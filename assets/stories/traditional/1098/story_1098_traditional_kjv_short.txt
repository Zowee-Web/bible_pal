Early in the morning Jesus came unto the temple in Jerusalem. The sun was yet low, casting long shadows across the stone court. The air was cool. People were gathering to hear him teach. He sat down, and a multitude came together unto him.

Then the scribes and Pharisees brought unto him a woman taken in adultery. They set her in the midst before all the people. Her head was bowed. Her hands trembled.

"Master," they said, "this woman was taken in adultery, in the very act. Now Moses in the law commanded us, that such should be stoned: but what sayest thou?"

This they said, tempting him, that they might have to accuse him.

But Jesus stooped down, and with his finger wrote on the ground, as though he heard them not. The dust of the court was smooth and warm beneath his hand.

They continued asking him. He lifted up himself, and said unto them, "He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her."

And again he stooped down, and wrote on the ground.

They which heard it, being convicted by their own conscience, went out one by one, beginning at the eldest, even unto the last. The court emptied. The morning light lay warm across the empty stone.

Jesus was left alone, and the woman standing in the midst.

He lifted up himself, and saw none but the woman. He said unto her, "Woman, where are those thine accusers? Hath no man condemned thee?"

She looked about the quiet court. "No man, Lord," she said.

Jesus said unto her, "Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more."
