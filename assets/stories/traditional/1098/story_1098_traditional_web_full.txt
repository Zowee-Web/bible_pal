Jesus went to the Mount of Olives.

Early in the morning he came again into the temple. All the people came to him. He sat down. He taught them. The people gathered around him in the temple and he sat and he taught.

While he was teaching, the scribes and the Pharisees brought a woman. She had been taken in adultery. They had found her in the very act. They brought her to Jesus and they set her in the middle. They set her there in the midst of all the people, in the middle of the temple, in front of Jesus where he sat teaching.

They said to him, "Teacher, we found this woman in adultery, in the very act. Now in our law, Moses commanded us to stone such women. What then do you say about her?"

They said this testing him, that they might have something to accuse him of. The question was not a question. It was a test. They wanted something to use against him.

But Jesus stooped down. He bent toward the ground. He wrote on the ground with his finger. He said nothing. He did not look at them. He did not look at the woman. He stooped down and he wrote on the ground with his finger.

They did not stop. They continued asking him. They pressed the question. They kept asking.

When they continued asking him, Jesus stood up from the ground. He said to them, "He who is without sin among you, let him throw the first stone at her."

He said this. Then he stooped down again. Again he wrote on the ground with his finger. He bent down a second time. He wrote on the ground a second time. And he waited.

They heard what he said. They were convicted by their own conscience. And they went out. One by one.

The oldest went out first. Then the next. Then the next after that. One by one they left, beginning from the oldest, even to the last. Each one turned and walked away. The oldest first. Then each one after him. Until the last one left.

When the last one had gone, Jesus was left alone. The woman was still standing where they had set her — in the middle. No one was there to accuse her. The scribes were gone. The Pharisees were gone. The ones who had brought her were gone. The ones who had cited the law of Moses were gone. The ones who had said "in the very act" were gone. Every one of them had walked away.

Jesus was left alone with the woman where she was, in the middle.

Jesus stood up from the ground. He saw no one but the woman.

He said to her, "Woman, where are your accusers? Did no one condemn you?"

She said, "No one, Lord."

Jesus said to her, "Neither do I condemn you. Go your way. From now on, sin no more."

The woman had been brought to the temple. She had been set in the middle. The scribes and Pharisees had spoken. They had cited the law of Moses. They had said she was taken in the very act. They had asked Jesus what he said about her.

Jesus had stooped down. He had written on the ground. They had continued asking. He had stood and spoken one sentence: "He who is without sin among you, let him throw the first stone at her."

He had stooped down again. He had written on the ground again.

And they had gone out. One by one. From the oldest to the last.

When they were all gone, Jesus stood. He spoke to the woman. He did not condemn her. He told her to go her way. He told her to sin no more.
