Habakkuk the prophet had heard the answer of Yahweh — that the just shall live by his faith, that the Chaldean would come up and sweep through, that the labor of the olive would fail in the year of the destruction, and the fields would be empty, and the stalls would be empty. The prophet had said at first, "I heard, and my body trembled. My lips quivered at the voice. Rottenness enters into my bones, and I tremble in my place." And then, having trembled, he set down a song at the end of the scroll. The song closed thus.

"For though the fig tree doesn't flourish,
nor fruit be in the vines;
the labor of the olive fails,
the fields yield no food;
the flocks are cut off from the fold,
and there is no herd in the stalls:

yet I will rejoice in Yahweh.
I will be joyful in the God of my salvation!

Yahweh, the Lord, is my strength.
He makes my feet like deer's feet,
and enables me to go in high places.
For the music director, on my stringed instruments."

And the song was set down at the end of the scroll. The fig tree still stood with its leaves over the prophet's garden; the olive trees stood in their orchards; the fields had not yet been emptied; but the song was already written for the day of empty fields, and the line about the deer's feet in the high places was kept at the bottom of the scroll for the music director who would set it for the singing of Israel.
