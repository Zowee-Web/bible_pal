Habakkuk the prophet had heard the answer of the Lord — that the just shall live by his faith, that the Chaldean would come up and sweep through, that the labour of the olive would fail in the year of the destruction, and the fields would be empty, and the stalls would be empty. The prophet had said at first, "I heard, and my belly trembled. My lips quivered at the voice. Rottenness entered into my bones, and I trembled in myself." And then, having trembled, he set down a song at the end of the scroll. The song closed thus.

"Although the fig tree shall not blossom,
neither shall fruit be in the vines;
the labour of the olive shall fail,
and the fields shall yield no meat;
the flock shall be cut off from the fold,
and there shall be no herd in the stalls:

Yet I will rejoice in the Lord,
I will joy in the God of my salvation.

The Lord God is my strength,
and he will make my feet like hinds' feet,
and he will make me to walk upon mine high places.
To the chief singer on my stringed instruments."

And the song was set down at the end of the scroll. The fig tree yet stood with its leaves over the prophet's garden; the olive trees stood in their orchards; the fields had not yet been emptied; but the song was already written for the day of empty fields, and the line about the hinds' feet in the high places was kept at the bottom of the scroll for the chief singer who would set it for the singing of Israel.
