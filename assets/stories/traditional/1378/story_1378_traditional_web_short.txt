The night had passed in the king's palace without sleep. Darius the Mede had refused his meat at evening, and would not have music brought before him, and had stayed awake on his couch turning over the words he had been forced to seal — the words that had thrown Daniel into the den of lions for praying to his God three times a day with his windows opened toward Jerusalem. The sky outside the king's window grew gray at last. The king rose up, and called for his men, and went to the den.

Then the king arose very early in the morning, and went in haste to the den of lions.

When he came near to the den to Daniel, he cried with a troubled voice. The king spoke and said to Daniel, "Daniel, servant of the living God, is your God, whom you serve continually, able to deliver you from the lions?"

Then Daniel said to the king, "O king, live forever!

My God has sent his angel, and has shut the lions' mouths, and they have not hurt me; because as before him innocence was found in me; and also before you, O king, I have done no harm."

Then the king was exceedingly glad, and commanded that they should take Daniel up out of the den. So Daniel was taken up out of the den, and no kind of harm was found on him, because he had trusted in his God.

And the men of the king bent down with their ropes and drew Daniel up into the morning. The king stood and looked at him for some time — at his garments, at his hands, at his face — and saw that there was no scratch upon him, that the lions had laid no tooth upon him; and the answer that had come up from the bottom of the pit was an answer that had passed through a night of lions and come out whole. And the king lifted up his face toward the lightening sky and gave thanks to the God of Daniel.
