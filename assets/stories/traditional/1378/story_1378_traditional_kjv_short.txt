The night had passed in the king's palace without sleep. Darius the Mede had refused his meat at evening, and would not have musick brought before him, and had stayed awake upon his couch turning over the words he had been compelled to seal — the words that had cast Daniel into the den of lions for praying unto his God three times a day with his windows opened toward Jerusalem. The sky without the king's window grew grey at last. The king arose, and called for his men, and went unto the den.

Then the king arose very early in the morning, and went in haste unto the den of lions.

And when he came to the den, he cried with a lamentable voice unto Daniel: and the king spake and said to Daniel, "O Daniel, servant of the living God, is thy God, whom thou servest continually, able to deliver thee from the lions?"

Then said Daniel unto the king, "O king, live for ever.

My God hath sent his angel, and hath shut the lions' mouths, that they have not hurt me: forasmuch as before him innocency was found in me; and also before thee, O king, have I done no hurt."

Then was the king exceeding glad for him, and commanded that they should take Daniel up out of the den. So Daniel was taken up out of the den, and no manner of hurt was found upon him, because he believed in his God.

And the men of the king bent down with their cords and drew Daniel up into the morning. The king stood and beheld him for some time — at his garments, at his hands, at his face — and saw that there was no scratch upon him, that the lions had laid no tooth upon him; and the answer that was come up from the bottom of the pit was an answer that had passed through a night of lions and come forth whole. And the king lifted up his face toward the lightening sky and gave thanks unto the God of Daniel.
