Noah was a good man who walked with God. He had three sons — Shem, Ham, and Japheth. They lived together with their families.

But the rest of the world had become a terrible place. People hurt each other and did cruel things every day. The earth was full of wickedness. God looked down and it made him deeply sad.

So God spoke to Noah. "The end of all flesh has come before me. The earth is filled with violence. I am going to destroy them along with the earth."

Then God gave Noah the biggest job anyone had ever been given.

"Make a ship out of gopher wood," God said. "Make rooms inside it. Cover it with pitch inside and outside so no water can get through."

God gave Noah the exact measurements. The ship would be three hundred cubits long, fifty cubits wide, and thirty cubits high. That was enormous — longer than a row of houses standing end to end. It would have three floors, a roof, and a door set in its side.

Noah listened to every word. He did not argue.

"I am bringing a flood of waters on this earth," God said. "Everything that breathes on the land will die."

Those words were heavy. But God was not finished.

"I will establish my covenant with you," God promised. A covenant was a strong, unbreakable agreement. "You will come into the ship — you, your sons, your wife, and your sons' wives."

God told Noah to bring two of every kind of animal into the ship — birds, creatures that walk, creatures that crawl. Male and female. He would also need to gather every kind of food and store it on board.

It was a staggering amount of work. Build a giant ship on dry land with no rain in sight. Gather animals. Store enough food for all of them.

But Noah trusted God. He picked up his tools. He cut the first beam of gopher wood and laid it on the ground. Day after day he worked — sawing, hammering, fitting boards together, sealing every crack with thick, dark pitch.

The Bible says something simple and powerful about Noah. He did everything just as God commanded him. Every board, every room, every measurement — exactly as God said. Noah did not skip a step. He trusted God, and he obeyed.
