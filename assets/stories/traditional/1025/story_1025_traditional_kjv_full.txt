Noah was a just man. That is to say, he strove to do what was right, even when none round about him did so. He was perfect in his generations. And the chief thing concerning Noah was this — he walked with God. He had three sons named Shem, Ham, and Japheth, and they all dwelt together with their wives.

But the rest of the world was become exceeding evil. Violence was on every hand. Men dealt cruelly one with another. Neighbours stole from neighbours. None shewed mercy. God looked down upon the earth and saw that every imagination of the thoughts of every heart was bent toward wickedness. And it repented God that he had made man upon the earth, and it grieved him at his heart.

So God spake unto Noah. Out of all the people in the world, Noah was the one whom God chose.

"The end of all flesh is come before me," God said. "The earth is filled with violence. I will destroy them with the earth."

Those were fearful words. But then God laid upon Noah a task — the greatest and strangest task that any man had ever been given.

"Make thee an ark of gopher wood," God said. "Rooms shalt thou make in the ark. Pitch it within and without with pitch."

Pitch was a dark and clinging substance which sealed wood against the water. Every crack must needs be covered, that not one drop should come through. God told Noah how great to make the ark. Three hundred cubits long. Fifty cubits broad. Thirty cubits high. That was enormous — longer than a whole row of houses end to end.

"A window shalt thou make to the ark," God went on. "The door of the ark shalt thou set in the side thereof. With lower, second, and third stories shalt thou make it."

Three whole stories within. Noah hearkened carefully unto every word. He interrupted not.

Then God told him wherefore. "And, behold, I, even I, do bring a flood of waters upon the earth, to destroy all flesh, wherein is the breath of life. Every thing that is in the earth shall die."

A flood. Water covering all things. Every hill, every valley, every field and village. Everything Noah could behold would vanish beneath the waves.

But God made Noah a promise — a covenant. A covenant was a special agreement, strong and unbreakable.

"But with thee will I establish my covenant," God said. "And thou shalt come into the ark — thou, and thy sons, and thy wife, and thy sons' wives with thee."

The whole house of Noah should be safe within that ark.

God was not yet finished. "And of every living thing of all flesh, two of every sort shalt thou bring into the ark, to keep them alive with thee. They shall be male and female." Two of every fowl. Two of every beast that walketh upon the ground. Two of every creeping thing that creepeth. All of them should come unto Noah.

"And take thou unto thee of all food that is eaten," God said, "and thou shalt gather it to thee. And it shall be for food for thee, and for them."

Consider what this meant. Noah must needs build an enormous ark upon dry ground. He must seal every inch with pitch. He must divide it into three stories. Then he must gather food enough to feed his house and every kind of beast, no man knew for how long. And he must do all this before any rain had fallen.

None other was building an ark. The people round about him must surely have thought him out of his wits.

But Noah waited not for the world to make sense. He took up his tools and went to his labour.

He chose strong and straight timbers of gopher wood. He cut every beam to the proper length, measuring carefully against the numbers God had given him. He fitted the boards together and drave wooden pegs into the seams. Then he heated the pitch over a fire until it turned liquid and black, and spread it thick over every surface — within and without — filling every gap.

The keel went down first, the long backbone of the ark lying flat upon the ground. Then the ribs rose up on both sides. Crossbeams joined them. Planks were fastened across the frame. The first story took shape, then the second, then the third.

The Scripture saith not how many years Noah wrought. But it saith a simple and mighty thing.

Thus did Noah; according to all that God commanded him, so did he.

Every board cut to the proper length. Every room built to the proper size. Every measure followed exactly as God had spoken. Noah skipped not a step. He fainted not when the labour was hard.

He trusted God. And he obeyed.
