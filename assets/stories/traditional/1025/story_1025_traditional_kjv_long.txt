Noah was a just man. That is to say, he strove every day to do that which was right and good. Among all the people that dwelt upon the earth in that time, Noah stood apart. He was perfect in his generations. Not without fault — but honest and faithful, the kind of man who kept his word and dealt fairly with others. And the chief thing concerning Noah was this. He walked with God.

To walk with God was not to go on long journeys together through the fields. It meant that Noah spake unto God and hearkened unto God. He gave heed unto what God desired. His life moved in step with God, even as two friends walk side by side upon a long road.

Noah had three sons. Their names were Shem, Ham, and Japheth. They dwelt together with their households.

But without the house of Noah, the world was become a very dark place.

The earth was corrupt in the sight of God. That word — corrupt — meaneth broken and rotten, like a piece of fruit that hath gone bad clean through. Violence filled the land. Men hurt one another without thinking twice. They took that which was not their own. They were cruel when they might have been kind. Every thought in every heart seemed bent toward wickedness.

God looked down upon the earth. He saw every thing — every act of cruelty, every broken promise, every soul which had turned away from goodness. And it grieved him at his heart that he had made man upon the earth. His heart was filled with sorrow.

So God made a decree.

He spake unto Noah. Unto Noah only. Out of all the souls upon the whole earth, God chose this one man to hear what was coming.

"The end of all flesh is come before me," God said. His voice was grave and sorrowful. "The earth is filled with violence through them. And, behold, I will destroy them with the earth."

Noah hearkened. The words were heavy, like stones falling into still water.

But God left not Noah without hope. God had a purpose, and Noah was a part of it.

"Make thee an ark of gopher wood," God said.

An ark. Not a small fishing vessel or a raft for crossing a river. A true ark — the greatest thing that any man had ever been bidden to build.

"Rooms shalt thou make in the ark," God went on. "And shalt pitch it within and without with pitch." Pitch was a thick, dark, clinging substance. It filled up the cracks and sealed the seams, that no water might come through. Every surface of the ark — walls, floor, ceiling — must be covered therewith.

Then God gave the measures. "The length of the ark shall be three hundred cubits, the breadth of it fifty cubits, and the height of it thirty cubits."

Three hundred cubits long. That was enormous. Longer than a whole row of houses standing in a line. Fifty cubits broad — wide enough to hold rooms on both sides of a centre walkway. Thirty cubits high — high enough for three full stories one upon another.

"A window shalt thou make to the ark," God said, "and in a cubit shalt thou finish it above." A cubit was about the length of a grown man's forearm. The roof would have a small opening near the top for air and light. "And the door of the ark shalt thou set in the side thereof. With lower, second, and third stories shalt thou make it."

Three decks. Lower, middle, and upper. Each one filled with rooms and spaces. One door in the side. One roof above. Noah hearkened carefully unto every measure, every word.

Then God told Noah wherefore.

"And, behold, I, even I, do bring a flood of waters upon the earth," God said, "to destroy all flesh, wherein is the breath of life, from under heaven. Every thing that is in the earth shall die."

A flood. Not a river overflowing its banks, nor a heavy rain which filleth the ditches. A flood that would cover all things — every valley, every hill, every path and village and field. Water rising higher and higher, till nothing upon the land could live.

Noah must have felt his heart beat hard within him. All things? All flesh?

But God spake again, and this time the words were other. They were a promise.

"But with thee will I establish my covenant."

A covenant. That was the strongest manner of promise there was. Unbreakable. God was making a bond with Noah, and God breaketh not his word.

"And thou shalt come into the ark," God said. "Thou, and thy sons, and thy wife, and thy sons' wives with thee."

Eight souls. Noah and his wife. Shem, Ham, and Japheth, and their three wives. They should all be safe within the ark when the waters came.

But it was not the people only.

"And of every living thing of all flesh," God said, "two of every sort shalt thou bring into the ark, to keep them alive with thee. They shall be male and female."

Two of every fowl — the eagles and the sparrows, the doves and the ravens. Two of every beast that walketh upon the ground — the oxen, the harts, the lions, the conies. Two of every creeping thing — the lizards and the beetles and the serpents. Male and female of every kind. All of them should come unto Noah, and he should bring them into the ark.

"And take thou unto thee of all food that is eaten," God said, "and thou shalt gather it to thee. And it shall be for food for thee, and for them."

Food for eight souls and two of every beast upon the earth. Grain and dried fruit and hay and seeds. Enough to last until the floodwaters were gone. No man knew how long that should be.

Consider the greatness of this labour. Noah must hew down trees and shape them into beams. He must build a frame three hundred cubits long. He must divide the inside into three stories with rooms large enough for beasts of every size. He must seal every surface with pitch. He must lay up mountains of food in their proper places. And he must do all of this upon dry ground, before a single drop of rain had fallen.

The people round about him must have watched and wondered. What was this man doing? Wherefore was he building a great ark where there was no water? Some of them likely laughed. Some likely shook their heads and went their way.

But Noah stopped not. He changed not his mind. He cut not corners, nor skipped any step.

The Scripture saith it simply. Thus did Noah; according to all that God commanded him, so did he.

Every beam cut to the proper length. Every room built to the proper size. Every seam sealed with pitch. Every measure followed exactly as God had spoken. The ark rose slowly upon the dry ground, plank by plank, day by day.

Noah trusted God. He believed that which God had said, though the sky was clear and the ground was dry and all things round about him looked perfectly common. He took up his tools each morning and went again unto his labour. Because God had bidden him. And Noah walked with God.
