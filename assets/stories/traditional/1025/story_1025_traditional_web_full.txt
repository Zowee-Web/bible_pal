Noah was a righteous man. That means he tried to do what was right, even when nobody around him did. He was blameless among the people of his time. And the most important thing about Noah was this — he walked with God. He had three sons named Shem, Ham, and Japheth, and they all lived together with their wives.

But the rest of the world had become very bad. Violence was everywhere. People treated each other cruelly. Neighbors stole from neighbors. No one showed mercy. God looked down at the earth and saw that every person's heart was bent toward wickedness. It made God deeply sorry that he had made human beings on the earth.

So God spoke to Noah. Out of all the people in the world, Noah was the one God chose.

"The end of all flesh has come before me," God said. "The earth is filled with violence. I will destroy them along with the earth."

Those were frightening words. But then God gave Noah a job — the biggest, strangest job anyone had ever been given.

"Make a ship of gopher wood," God said. "Make rooms inside it. Cover it with pitch inside and outside."

Pitch was a dark, sticky material that sealed wood against water. Every crack would need to be covered so not a single drop could get through. God told Noah exactly how big to make the ship. Three hundred cubits long. Fifty cubits wide. Thirty cubits high. That was enormous — longer than a whole row of houses end to end.

"Make a roof for the ship," God continued. "Set the door in its side. Make it with lower, second, and third levels."

Three whole floors inside. Noah listened carefully to every detail. He did not interrupt.

Then God explained why. "I am bringing a flood of waters on this earth to destroy all flesh that has the breath of life. Everything that is on the earth will die."

A flood. Water covering everything. Every hill, every valley, every field and village. Everything Noah could see would disappear under the waves.

But God made Noah a promise — a covenant. A covenant was a special agreement, strong and unbreakable.

"I will establish my covenant with you," God said. "You will come into the ship — you, your sons, your wife, and your sons' wives with you."

Noah's whole family would be safe inside that ship.

God was not finished. "Of every living thing, you shall bring two of every sort into the ship to keep them alive. They shall be male and female." Two of every kind of bird. Two of every animal that walks on the ground. Two of every creature that creeps and crawls. All of them would come to Noah.

"Take every kind of food that is eaten," God said, "and store it up. It will be food for you and for them."

Think about what that meant. Noah had to build an enormous ship on dry land. He had to seal every inch with pitch. He had to divide it into three levels. Then he had to gather enough food to feed his family and every kind of animal for nobody knew how long. And he had to do all of this before any rain had fallen.

Nobody else was building a ship. The people around him must have thought he had lost his mind.

But Noah did not wait for the world to make sense. He picked up his tools and got to work.

He chose strong, straight timbers of gopher wood. He cut each beam to the right length, measuring carefully against the numbers God had given him. He fitted the boards together and hammered wooden pegs into the seams. Then he heated the pitch over a fire until it turned liquid and black, and spread it thick over every surface — inside and outside — filling every gap.

The keel went down first, the long backbone of the ship lying flat on the ground. Then the ribs rose up on both sides. Crossbeams connected them. Planks were nailed across the frame. The first floor took shape, then the second, then the third.

The Bible does not say how many years Noah worked. But it does say something simple and powerful.

Noah did this. He did everything that God commanded him.

Every board cut to the right length. Every room built to the right size. Every measurement followed exactly as God had said. Noah did not skip a step. He did not quit when the work was hard.

He trusted God. And he obeyed.
