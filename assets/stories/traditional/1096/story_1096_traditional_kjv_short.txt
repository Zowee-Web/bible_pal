Now there is at Jerusalem by the sheep market a pool, which is called in the Hebrew tongue Bethesda, having five porches. In these lay a great multitude of impotent folk — of blind, halt, and withered — waiting. The air beneath the porches smelled of sweat and stone and the stale water of the pool.

Among them was a certain man which had an infirmity thirty and eight years. He lay upon his mat, his body wasted, his face lined with the weariness of one who hath waited so long that the waiting itself hath become his life.

Jesus came unto the pool. He saw the man lying there, and knew that he had been now a long time in that case.

He saith unto him, "Wilt thou be made whole?"

The impotent man answered him, "Sir, I have no man, when the water is troubled, to put me into the pool: but while I am coming, another steppeth down before me."

It was not an answer. It was the sound of eight and thirty years of being alone.

Jesus saith unto him, "Rise, take up thy bed, and walk."

And immediately the man was made whole. He took up his bed and walked. His legs held. His back straightened. He stood for the first time in the memory of any who knew him.

And on the same day was the sabbath.

The Jews therefore said unto him that was cured, "It is the sabbath day: it is not lawful for thee to carry thy bed."

He answered them, "He that made me whole, the same said unto me, Take up thy bed, and walk."

"What man is that?" they asked. But he that was healed wist not who it was: for Jesus had conveyed himself away, a multitude being in that place.

Afterward Jesus findeth him in the temple, standing where he had once been carried. Jesus saith unto him, "Behold, thou art made whole: sin no more, lest a worse thing come unto thee."

The man departed, and told the Jews that it was Jesus which had made him whole.
