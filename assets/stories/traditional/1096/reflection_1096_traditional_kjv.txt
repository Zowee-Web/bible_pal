Thirty and eight years is a long season to wait for a thing that cometh not. Long enough to cease expecting it. Long enough to build a life about the absence thereof — to cease asking for help, to cease looking for change, to cease believing the question hath any answer.

When Jesus asked, "Wilt thou be made whole?" — the man said not yea. He explained why it was not possible. That is what long suffering doth: it putteth explanation in the place of hope.

But Jesus asked not for explanation. He addressed not the pool, nor the water, nor the failing of the system. He spake directly to the body that had forgotten how to stand.

Sometimes the thing most needful is not a better way. It is someone who speaketh to us as though change remaineth possible — even when we ourselves have ceased to believe it.
