After this there was a feast of the Jews, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem.

Now there is at Jerusalem by the sheep market a pool, which is called in the Hebrew tongue Bethesda, having five porches. In these lay a great multitude of impotent folk. Some were blind. Some were halt. Some were withered. All of them waited there for the moving of the water.

For an angel went down at a certain season into the pool, and troubled the water. Whosoever then first after the troubling of the water stepped in was made whole of whatsoever disease he had. So the impotent folk lay in those porches, waiting, watching for the water to be troubled.

And a certain man was there, which had an infirmity thirty and eight years. He lay among the multitude of impotent folk. He had been in that case a very long time.

When Jesus saw him lie, and knew that he had been now a long time in that case, he saith unto him, "Wilt thou be made whole?"

The impotent man answered him. He did not say yea. He explained his case. He said, "Sir, I have no man, when the water is troubled, to put me into the pool. But while I am coming, another steppeth down before me."

He had no man to help him. Each time the water was troubled, another stepped in before him. For thirty and eight years, this had been his answer. He had no man. Another always went first.

Jesus saith unto him, "Rise, take up thy bed, and walk."

He did not tell him to go unto the pool. He did not tell him to wait for the water to be troubled. He did not call for a man to carry him. He spake directly unto the man.

And immediately the man was made whole, and took up his bed, and walked.

And on the same day was the sabbath.

The Jews therefore said unto him that was cured, "It is the sabbath day. It is not lawful for thee to carry thy bed."

He answered them, "He that made me whole, the same said unto me, 'Take up thy bed, and walk.'"

Then asked they him, "What man is that which said unto thee, 'Take up thy bed, and walk'?"

And he that was healed wist not who it was, for Jesus had conveyed himself away, a multitude being in that place. The man had been made whole, but he wist not the name of the one who had spoken unto him.

Afterward Jesus findeth him in the temple, and said unto him, "Behold, thou art made whole. Sin no more, lest a worse thing come unto thee."

The man departed and told the Jews that it was Jesus which had made him whole.

For thirty and eight years the man had lain by the pool. He had waited for the water to be troubled. He had watched others step in before him. He had told why he could not be healed. Then Jesus came. He spake. And the man was made whole. He took up his bed. And he walked.
