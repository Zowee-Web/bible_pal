Now there is at Jerusalem by the sheep market a pool, which is called in the Hebrew tongue Bethesda, having five porches. These were long colonnades of stone, and the shadows beneath them fell cool even in the heat of the day. The water in the pool was still and greenish, fed by a spring beneath the ground that sometimes moved without cause.

In these lay a great multitude of impotent folk — of blind, halt, and withered. They were spread along the stone floor upon mats and blankets. Some lay still, staring upward. Some sat propped against the pillars with their eyes upon the water, waiting. Others were tended by their kindred who sat beside them and wiped their brows. The air was heavy — sweat, stale water, the close breath of bodies that had lain in one place overlongAmong them was a certain man which had an infirmity thirty and eight years. None remembered what he had been ere the sickness came. He had lain near this pool so long he had become as the stones themselves. His mat was worn through. His limbs were thin and stiff. His face was lined with the manner of weariness that cometh not from one ill day, but from thousands of them.

When Jesus came unto the pool, he walked through the porches, past the rows of sick folk, and his eyes moved from one to the next. He saw the man lying there, and knew that he had been now a long time in that case.

He saith unto him, "Wilt thou be made whole?"

The impotent man answered, "Sir, I have no man, when the water is troubled, to put me into the pool: but while I am coming, another steppeth down before me."

It was not an answer to the question asked. It was the accumulated weight of eight and thirty years — of watching others receive what he could not reach, of having none to help, of trying and failing until trying itself had become a burden.

Jesus saith unto him, "Rise, take up thy bed, and walk."

And immediately the man was made whole. Strength came into his legs. His knees bent. His feet pressed flat upon the stone. He stood — trembling, uncertain, but standing — for the first time in eight and thirty years. He reached down, took up his mat, and walked forth from the porches into the light.

And on the same day was the sabbath.

The Jews therefore said unto him that was cured, "It is the sabbath day: it is not lawful for thee to carry thy bed."

He answered them, "He that made me whole, the same said unto me, Take up thy bed, and walk."

They asked him, "What man is that which said unto thee, Take up thy bed, and walk?" But he that was healed wist not who it was: for Jesus had conveyed himself away, a multitude being in that place.

Afterward Jesus findeth him in the temple — standing there, upon his own feet, in the courts where he had once been borne by others.

"Behold," Jesus saith unto him, "thou art made whole: sin no more, lest a worse thing come unto thee."

The man departed, and told the Jews that it was Jesus which had made him whole.
