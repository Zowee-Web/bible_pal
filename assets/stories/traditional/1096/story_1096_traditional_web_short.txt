In Jerusalem, near the Sheep Gate, there was a pool called Bethesda. It had five covered porches — long, stone colonnades where the shadows fell cool even in the heat of the day. Under those porches lay a great number of sick people — the blind, the lame, the paralyzed — waiting. They lay on mats and blankets, some alone, some tended by family members who sat beside them and fanned the flies away. The air smelled of sweat and stone and the stale water of the pool.

Among them was a man who had been sick for thirty-eight years. Thirty-eight years of lying near the water, watching others step in, waiting for his turn, knowing no one would carry him. His mat was worn thin. His body had forgotten what it felt like to stand.

Jesus came to the pool. He walked through the porches, past the rows of sick people, and he saw the man lying there. He knew how long the man had been waiting.

He stopped beside him and asked a question.

"Do you want to be made well?"

The man did not say yes. He did not say no. He said, "Sir, I have no one to put me into the pool when the water is stirred up. While I am trying to get in, someone else steps down before me."

It was not an answer. It was a description of thirty-eight years of being alone.

Jesus said to him, "Rise. Take up your mat, and walk."

And immediately the man was made well. He stood up — his legs held, his back straightened, his feet pressed flat against the stone floor for the first time in decades. He picked up his mat, tucked it under his arm, and walked.

It was the Sabbath.

The Jewish leaders saw the man carrying his mat and stopped him. "It is the Sabbath," they said. "It is not lawful for you to carry your mat."

The man answered, "The one who made me well said to me, 'Take up your mat, and walk.'"

"Who told you to do this?" they asked. But the man did not know who it was, because Jesus had slipped away into the crowd.

Later, Jesus found him in the temple. The man was standing there — standing — in the place where he had once been carried. Jesus said to him, "See, you have been made well. Sin no more, so that nothing worse may happen to you."

The man went away and told the Jewish leaders that it was Jesus who had made him well.
