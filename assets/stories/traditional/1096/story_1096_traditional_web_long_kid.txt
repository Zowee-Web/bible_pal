In the city of Jerusalem, there was a special pool called Bethesda. It was surrounded by five covered walkways made of stone. The roof kept the sun off, and the shadows underneath were cool and dark. 

Under those walkways, lots and lots of sick people were lying on thin mats on the stone floor. Some were blind. Some couldn't walk. Some couldn't move at all. They lay there day after day, week after week, waiting. Hoping. Some had family members sitting next to them, fanning away the flies and bringing them water. Others were completely alone.

One man had been sick for THIRTY-EIGHT YEARS.

Think about that. Thirty-eight years is longer than most people go to school, work their first job, and start a family — all combined! This man had been lying by this pool watching the world go by for longer than most people could even imagine.

His mat was so old and thin you could almost see through it. His body was weak and skinny. His face was covered in deep lines — not just from getting old, but from being tired in a way that goes deeper than your body. He was tired of waiting. Tired of watching. Tired of being alone.

Nobody remembered what this man used to be like before he got sick. Nobody asked anymore.

One day, Jesus came to the pool. He walked through the covered walkways, past all the sick people. He saw the blind ones and the paralyzed ones and the ones who just lay there staring at the ceiling. 

Then he stopped. Right next to this man who had been sick for thirty-eight years.

Jesus already knew everything about him. He knew how long the man had been there. He knew that nobody ever helped him. He knew about all those years of waiting.

And Jesus asked him a question that might seem strange:

"Do you want to get well?"

You'd think the answer would obviously be yes. But the man didn't say yes. He didn't say no either. Instead, he said something really sad:

"Sir, I have no one to help me into the pool. Every time the water moves, someone else gets in before me."

That wasn't really an answer to what Jesus asked. It was more like... giving up. After thirty-eight years, the man had stopped believing things could change. He wasn't asking for help anymore. He was just explaining why help was impossible.

But Jesus didn't argue with him. Jesus didn't talk about the pool or the water or whether the man had someone to help him. He just spoke three short sentences:

"Stand up. Pick up your mat. Walk."

And IMMEDIATELY — right at that very second — the man was healed! 

His legs, which hadn't worked in thirty-eight years, suddenly filled with strength. His knees bent. His feet pressed flat on the stone floor. He pushed himself up... and he was STANDING. Actually standing! For the first time in thirty-eight years!

He looked down at the old mat he'd been lying on for so long. He reached down, rolled it up, tucked it under his arm... and walked. Right out of those covered walkways, right out into the bright Jerusalem sunshine.

But there was a problem. It was the Sabbath — the special day of rest — and the religious leaders had strict rules about what you could and couldn't do on the Sabbath. When they saw the man walking through the streets carrying his mat, they stopped him.

"Hey! It's the Sabbath! You're not allowed to carry that mat today!"

The man just shrugged. "The guy who healed me told me to pick it up and walk. So that's what I'm doing."

"Who told you that?" they demanded. But the man didn't know Jesus' name. He'd never even met him before today. And Jesus had already disappeared into the crowd.

Later that day, Jesus found the man again. This time the man was in the temple — STANDING in the temple. Standing in the beautiful courts where he used to have to be carried.

"Look," Jesus said to him. "You're well now. Stop sinning, or something worse might happen."

The man went and told everyone that it was Jesus who had healed him.

After thirty-eight years of lying on a mat, waiting for help that never came... all it took was one person who saw him, stopped, and spoke to him as though things could still change.
