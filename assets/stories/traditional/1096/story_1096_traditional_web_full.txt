After these things, there was a feast of the Jews, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem.

Now in Jerusalem, by the sheep gate, there was a pool. In the Hebrew tongue it was called Bethesda. It had five porches. In those five porches lay a great multitude of people who were sick. Some were blind. Some were lame. Some were paralyzed. All of them waited there for the moving of the water.

For an angel of the Lord went down at certain times into the pool and stirred up the water. And whoever stepped in first after the stirring of the water was healed of whatever disease he had. So the sick lay in the porches, waiting, watching for the water to move.

A certain man was there who had been sick for thirty-eight years. He lay among the multitude of sick people. He had been lying there a very long time.

When Jesus saw him lying there, and knew that he had been sick for a long time, he asked him a question. He said, "Do you want to be made well?"

The sick man answered him. He did not say yes. He explained his situation. He said, "Sir, I have no one to put me into the pool when the water is stirred up. But while I am coming, another steps down before me."

He had no one to help him. Each time the water moved, someone else reached the pool before him. For thirty-eight years, this had been his answer. He had no one. Another always stepped in first.

Jesus said to him, "Arise, take up your mat, and walk."

He did not tell him to go to the pool. He did not tell him to wait for the water to be stirred. He did not arrange for someone to carry him. He spoke directly to the man.

Immediately, the man was made well. He took up his mat. He walked.

Now it was the Sabbath on that day.

So the Jews said to him who was cured, "It is the Sabbath. It is not lawful for you to carry the mat."

He answered them, "He who made me well, the same said to me, 'Take up your mat and walk.'"

Then they asked him, "Who is the man who said to you, 'Take up your mat and walk'?"

But the man who had been healed did not know who it was. For Jesus had withdrawn from that place, a crowd being there. The man had been made well, but he did not know the name of the one who had spoken to him.

Afterward Jesus found him in the temple. He said to him, "Behold, you are made well. Sin no more, so that nothing worse happens to you."

The man went away. He told the Jews that it was Jesus who had made him well.

For thirty-eight years the man had lain by the pool. He had waited for the water to move. He had watched others step in before him. He had explained why he could not be healed. Then Jesus came. He spoke. And the man was made well. He took up his mat. And he walked.
