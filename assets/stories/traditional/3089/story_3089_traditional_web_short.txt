Amaziah king of Judah sent messengers to Jehoash king of Israel and challenged him to meet face to face. Jehoash answered with a story. A thistle in Lebanon had asked a cedar in Lebanon to give its daughter in marriage to the thistle's son. Then a wild animal passed by and trampled the thistle.

Jehoash told Amaziah that his victory over Edom had lifted up his heart. He urged him to take the honor of that victory and remain at home. If Amaziah meddled further, he and Judah would fall together.

Amaziah would not listen. Jehoash therefore went up, and the two kings faced one another at Beth Shemesh, which belonged to Judah. Israel defeated Judah, and every man of Judah fled to his tent. Jehoash captured Amaziah there and came to Jerusalem. He broke down four hundred cubits of the city wall, from the Ephraim Gate to the Corner Gate. He took all the gold and silver, all the vessels found in Yahweh's house and in the treasuries of the royal palace, and hostages also. Then he returned to Samaria.

The other acts of Jehoash, his might, and his war with Amaziah were recorded in the chronicles of Israel's kings. Jehoash died and was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel. His son Jeroboam reigned in his place.

Amaziah lived fifteen years after Jehoash died. His other acts were recorded in the chronicles of Judah's kings. A conspiracy was formed against him in Jerusalem, and he fled to Lachish. Men were sent after him, and they killed him there. His body was brought back on horses and buried at Jerusalem with his fathers in David's city.

All the people of Judah then took Azariah, who was sixteen years old, and made him king in place of his father Amaziah. After Amaziah was laid with his fathers, Azariah rebuilt Elath and restored it to Judah.
