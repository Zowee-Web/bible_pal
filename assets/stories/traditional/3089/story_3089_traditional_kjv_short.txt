Amaziah king of Judah sent messengers unto Jehoash king of Israel, challenging him to come and look one another in the face. Jehoash answered with a tale. A thistle in Lebanon had sent unto a cedar in Lebanon, asking that the cedar give his daughter to the thistle's son to wife. Then a wild beast passed by and trode down the thistle.

Jehoash told Amaziah that he had indeed smitten Edom, and that his heart had lifted him up. He bade him glory in that victory and tarry at home. For if Amaziah meddled to his hurt, both he and Judah would fall.

But Amaziah would not hear. Therefore Jehoash went up, and the two kings looked one another in the face at Bethshemesh, which belonged to Judah. Israel smote Judah, and every man of Judah fled to his tent. Jehoash took Amaziah there and came unto Jerusalem. He brake down four hundred cubits of the wall, from the gate of Ephraim unto the corner gate. He took all the gold and silver, all the vessels found in the house of the Lord and in the treasures of the king's house, and hostages also. Then he returned unto Samaria.

The rest of the acts of Jehoash, his might, and how he fought with Amaziah were written in the chronicles of the kings of Israel. Jehoash slept with his fathers and was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel. Jeroboam his son reigned in his stead.

Amaziah lived fifteen years after the death of Jehoash. His other acts were written in the chronicles of the kings of Judah. Men made a conspiracy against him in Jerusalem, and he fled unto Lachish. They sent after him and slew him there. His body was brought upon horses and buried at Jerusalem with his fathers in the city of David.

Then all the people of Judah took Azariah, which was sixteen years old, and made him king instead of Amaziah his father. After the king slept with his fathers, Azariah built Elath and restored it to Judah.
