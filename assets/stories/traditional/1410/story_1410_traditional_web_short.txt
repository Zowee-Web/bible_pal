Three months had passed on the island of Malta. The shipwrecked centurion and his soldiers had wintered with the company of Paul — Publius the chief man of the island had received them and shown them kindness, the people had brought gifts when the time came to leave — and at the end of the winter they had found another ship of Alexandria that had wintered there too, whose figurehead was the Twin Brothers, Castor and Pollux. Paul went on board with his guards.

After three months, we set sail in a ship of Alexandria which had wintered in the island, whose sign was "The Twin Brothers."

Touching at Syracuse, we stayed there three days.

From there we circled around and arrived at Rhegium. After one day, a south wind sprang up, and on the second day we came to Puteoli,

where we found brothers, and were entreated to stay with them for seven days. So we came to Rome.

From there the brothers, when they heard of us, came to meet us as far as The Market of Appius and The Three Taverns. When Paul saw them, he thanked God, and took courage.

And the apostle walked the Appian Way the last day's miles toward Rome with the brothers from the city beside him. The road that had been long across many seas — through the storm and the shipwreck and the winter on Malta — ran in under his feet beside the company that had come out to find him; and Paul walked into the city among them, with the courage that had been given him at the meeting still on his face.
