Three months had passed upon the island of Melita. The shipwrecked centurion and his soldiers had wintered with the company of Paul — Publius the chief man of the island had received them and shewed them kindness, the people had brought gifts when the time came that they should depart — and at the ending of the winter they had found another ship of Alexandria which had wintered there also, whose sign was the Twin Brethren, Castor and Pollux. Paul went up on board with his keepers.

After three months we departed in a ship of Alexandria, which had wintered in the isle, whose sign was Castor and Pollux.

And landing at Syracuse, we tarried there three days.

And from thence we fetched a compass, and came to Rhegium: and after one day the south wind blew, and we came the next day to Puteoli:

Where we found brethren, and were desired to tarry with them seven days: and so we went toward Rome.

And from thence, when the brethren heard of us, they came to meet us as far as Appii forum, and The three taverns: whom when Paul saw, he thanked God, and took courage.

And the apostle walked the Appian Way the last day's miles toward Rome with the brethren from the city beside him. The road which had been long across many seas — through the tempest and the shipwreck and the winter upon Melita — ran in under his feet beside the company that had come forth to find him; and Paul went into the city among them, with the courage that had been given him at the meeting yet upon his face.
