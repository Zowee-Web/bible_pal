In the town of Capernaum, upon the northern shore of the sea, it was noised about that Jesus had returned and was in the house. Straightway many were gathered together, filling the rooms and pressing close about the door. There was no space remaining, neither within nor without. And he preached the word unto them, and the people hearkened.

Then came four men through the crowd, bearing one sick of the palsy, laid upon a bed. They sought to bring him near unto Jesus, but they could not come nigh unto him for the press of people, which was very great.

The four men looked one upon another. Then they carried the sick man up unto the roof. They uncovered the tiles and the thatch above the place where Jesus taught. When they had broken through, they let down the bed whereon the sick of the palsy lay, into the midst before Jesus.

Dust fell from above into the room. The people looked upward and marveled. And Jesus, seeing their faith, said unto the sick of the palsy:

"Son, thy sins be forgiven thee."

But there were certain of the scribes sitting there, reasoning in their hearts: "Why doth this man thus speak blasphemies? Who can forgive sins but God only?"

And immediately Jesus, perceiving in his spirit that they so reasoned within themselves, saith unto them, "Why reason ye these things in your hearts? Whether is it easier to say to the sick of the palsy, Thy sins be forgiven thee; or to say, Arise, and take up thy bed, and walk?"

He paused. The room was still.

"But that ye may know that the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins —"

He turned unto the sick of the palsy.

"I say unto thee, Arise, and take up thy bed, and go thy way into thine house."

And immediately he arose, took up the bed, and went forth before them all. The people parted to let him pass, and he walked where before he had been carried.

They were all amazed, and glorified God, saying, "We never saw it on this fashion."
