The town of Capernaum sat along the northern shore of the Sea of Galilee. Stone houses with flat roofs lined the narrow streets that ran uphill from the water. Fishing boats bobbed gently in the shallows near the harbor. The smell of drying nets and salted fish hung in the air. Farther from the shore, the streets grew quieter — doors propped open to catch the breeze, women grinding grain on flat stones in their courtyards, children chasing each other between the walls.

Word had spread through the town that Jesus was home. He was staying at a house near the center of Capernaum — a house with thick stone walls and a flat clay roof, the kind that could be reached by an outside stairway. People began gathering almost immediately. They came from the neighboring houses first, then from across the town, then from the roads leading in from surrounding villages. The rooms inside filled. The doorway filled. People crowded along the outer walls and pressed close to the windows. Soon there was no more room — not even space to stand near the door.

Inside, Jesus stood speaking the word to them. His voice was steady and clear, carrying over the heads of those pressed close together. The room was warm from so many bodies. Sunlight came in through the small windows in long, slanting beams, catching the dust that floated in the still air. The crowd listened — some leaning forward, some standing on their toes to see over the shoulders of those in front.

Outside the house, four men made their way through the crowd. Between them they carried a mat, and on the mat lay a man who could not walk. He was paralyzed — his legs had not moved in a long time. His arms lay at his sides, thin beneath the cloth that covered him. His eyes were open, looking up at the pale sky above the rooftops.

His friends had carried him from across the town. They had brought him through the narrow streets, stepping carefully over uneven ground, shifting their grip when the mat tilted or sagged. They had walked slowly, sweating in the midday heat, their arms aching from the weight. They wanted to bring him to Jesus. They believed that if they could just get him close enough, something would change.

When they reached the house and saw the crowd, they stopped. There was no way through. The doorway was blocked solid. Every window was packed with faces. People stood three and four deep along the outer walls.

The four men looked at one another. One of them tilted his head upward, toward the roof.

Without a word, they turned. They carried the mat around the side of the house and up the outside stairway, one step at a time. The stone steps were narrow. They gripped the mat tightly, their knuckles white, their feet scraping against the rough surface. At the top, they laid the mat down gently on the flat clay roof.

Then they knelt and began to break through.

They dug into the dried clay with their hands. They pulled back layers of packed mud and straw. They tore away the branches and brush woven beneath the surface. Their fingers bled. Dust rose around them. Piece by piece, they opened a hole in the roof above the place where Jesus stood teaching.

Inside the house, the crowd looked up. Chunks of clay and bits of dried thatch fell from the ceiling. A shaft of bright sunlight broke through the opening. People pressed back from the falling debris, covering their faces, murmuring to one another. Some were annoyed. Some were astonished.

Through the hole, four pairs of hands appeared. The paralytic was lowered through the opening on his mat — slowly, steadily — the four men gripping each corner, feeding the mat down hand over hand. The crowd inside the room watched in silence as the mat descended. It came to rest on the floor at the feet of Jesus.

Dust hung in the beam of light pouring through the roof. The paralytic lay still on the mat, looking up at the face of Jesus above him.

Jesus looked at the man. Then he looked up through the hole at the four faces peering down — dusty, sweating, breathing hard. He saw their faith. Not just the faith of the one who lay before him, but the faith of the four who had carried him, climbed for him, torn apart a roof for him.

"Son," he said to the paralytic, "your sins are forgiven you."

The words fell into the room like a stone into still water. Near the far wall, several scribes sat together on a low bench. Their expressions changed. They glanced at one another but did not speak. In their hearts, they were deeply troubled. "Why does this man speak this way?" they thought. "He is blaspheming. Who can forgive sins but God alone?"

Jesus perceived in his spirit what they were thinking. He looked at them directly, and they shifted under his gaze.

"Why do you reason these things in your hearts?" he said. His voice was calm but firm. "Which is easier — to say to the paralytic, 'Your sins are forgiven,' or to say, 'Arise, take up your mat, and walk'?"

No one answered. The room was perfectly still.

"But so that you may know," Jesus said, "that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins —"

He turned to the paralytic.

"I tell you," he said. "Arise. Take up your mat, and go to your house."

The man's fingers moved first. A twitch. Then a grip. His hands pressed flat against the mat beneath him. His forearms tightened. His shoulders lifted from the ground. Then, slowly — with the entire room watching — he pushed himself upward. His arms held. His back straightened. His legs, which had been still for so long, bent beneath him, and he rose.

He stood.

The crowd did not breathe.

He bent down, picked up the mat he had been lying on, and rolled it under his arm. Then he turned and walked toward the door. The people pressed against the walls, making a path. Some reached out as if to touch him. Others stood frozen, hands over their mouths, tears starting in their eyes.

He walked out of the house and into the sunlight. The crowd outside saw him coming — this man who had been carried in on a mat — now walking under his own power, the rolled mat tucked under his arm, his steps steady on the stone street.

For a moment, there was silence. Then voices rose — astonished, trembling, full of wonder. People fell to their knees. Others lifted their hands toward the sky. The sound of their praise carried over the rooftops of Capernaum and out across the water.

"We have never seen anything like this," they said.
