The town of Capernaum lay along the northern shore of the Sea of Galilee. Stone houses with flat roofs lined the narrow streets that ran up from the water's edge. Fishing boats rocked gently in the shallows by the harbour. The smell of nets drying in the sun hung upon the air. Farther from the shore, the streets grew quieter — doors standing open to catch the breeze, women grinding grain upon flat stones in their courts, children running between the walls.

Word had gone abroad through the town that Jesus was come home. He was in a certain house near the centre of Capernaum — a house with thick stone walls and a flat clay roof, the manner of house that could be reached by an outer stairway. People began to gather straightway. They came from the houses nearby, then from across the town, then from the roads leading in from the surrounding villages. The rooms within were filled. The doorway was filled. People pressed along the outer walls and crowded about the windows. Soon there was no more room — not so much as about the door.

Within, Jesus stood speaking the word unto them. His voice was steady and carried well over the heads of those pressed close together. The room was warm from so many bodies. The sunlight came in at the small windows in long slanting beams, catching the dust that hung in the still air. The people hearkened — some leaning forward, some standing upon their toes to see past the shoulders of those before them.

Without the house, four men made their way through the crowd. Between them they bore a bed, and upon the bed lay a man sick of the palsy. He was a man whose legs had not moved in many days. His arms lay at his sides, thin beneath the cloth that covered him. His eyes were open, looking upward at the pale sky above the rooftops.

His friends had borne him from the far side of the town. They had carried him through the narrow streets, stepping carefully over uneven ground, shifting their hold when the bed grew heavy. They had walked slowly in the heat of the day, the sweat standing upon their brows, their arms aching from the weight. They desired to bring him unto Jesus. They believed that if they could but bring him nigh enough, all would be changed.

When they came unto the house and saw the press of people, they were stopped. There was no way through. The door was blocked fast. Every window was crowded with faces. People stood three and four deep along the walls without.

The four men looked one upon another. One amongst them lifted his eyes unto the roof.

Without a word they turned. They bore the bed about the side of the house and up the outer stairway, one step at a time. The stone steps were narrow. They gripped the bed firmly, their knuckles white, their feet scraping upon the rough surface. At the top they laid the bed down gently upon the flat clay roof.

Then they knelt and began to break through.

They dug into the dried clay with their hands. They tore away the packed earth and straw. They pulled back the branches woven beneath. Their fingers bled. Dust rose about them. Piece by piece they opened a hole in the roof above the place where Jesus was teaching.

Within the house, the people looked upward. Chunks of clay and dried thatch fell from above. A shaft of bright light broke through the opening. People pressed backward, covering their faces, crying out to one another. Some were wroth. Some marveled.

Through the hole, four pairs of hands appeared. The sick man was let down upon his bed — slowly, steadily — the four men gripping each corner, feeding the bed down hand over hand. The crowd within the room watched in silence as the bed descended. It came to rest upon the floor at the feet of Jesus.

Dust hung in the beam of light that poured through the broken roof. The sick of the palsy lay still upon the bed, looking upward into the face of Jesus above him.

Jesus looked upon the man. Then he looked upward through the hole at the four faces above — dusty, sweating, breathing hard. He saw their faith. Not the faith of the one alone who lay before him, but the faith of the four who had borne him, climbed for him, torn asunder a roof for him.

"Son," he saith unto the sick of the palsy, "thy sins be forgiven thee."

The words fell upon the room as a stone cast into still water. Near the far wall sat certain of the scribes upon a low bench. Their faces changed. They looked one upon another but spake not. In their hearts they were sore troubled. "Why doth this man thus speak blasphemies?" they reasoned. "Who can forgive sins but God only?"

And immediately Jesus, perceiving in his spirit that they so reasoned within themselves, looked upon them directly, and they were uneasy under his gaze.

"Why reason ye these things in your hearts?" saith he. His voice was calm but clear. "Whether is it easier to say to the sick of the palsy, Thy sins be forgiven thee; or to say, Arise, and take up thy bed, and walk?"

No man answered. The room was still as death.

"But that ye may know that the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins —"

He turned unto the sick of the palsy.

"I say unto thee, Arise, and take up thy bed, and go thy way into thine house."

The man's fingers stirred. His hands pressed flat upon the bed. His arms tightened. His shoulders lifted from the ground. Then, slowly, with all the room watching, he rose. His legs, which had been still so long, bent beneath him, and he stood.

He stood.

The crowd breathed not.

He stooped down and took up the bed whereon he had lain, and gathered it beneath his arm. Then he turned and went toward the door. The people pressed against the walls, making a way for him. Some reached forth as though to touch him. Others stood motionless, their hands upon their mouths, tears starting in their eyes.

He went forth from the house and into the light of the day. The crowd without saw him coming — this man who had been borne in upon a bed — now walking of his own strength, the bed rolled beneath his arm, his steps sure upon the stone of the street.

For a moment all was silent. Then voices arose — amazed, trembling, full of wonder. Men fell upon their knees. Others lifted their hands toward heaven. The sound of their praising carried above the rooftops of Capernaum and out over the waters.

"We never saw it on this fashion," they said.
