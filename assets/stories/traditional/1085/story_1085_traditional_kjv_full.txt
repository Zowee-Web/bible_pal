In the town of Capernaum, upon the shore of Galilee, it was noised about that Jesus was come home and was in the house. Straightway many were gathered together, insomuch that there was no room to receive them, no, not so much as about the door. People pressed in from the streets and stood along the walls outside, straining to hear. And he preached the word unto them.

Then came four men through the crowd, bearing between them one sick of the palsy, laid upon a bed. They had carried him through the narrow streets of the town, shifting their grip when the mat grew heavy, seeking to bring him near unto Jesus. But they could not come nigh unto him for the press of people, which was exceeding great.

The four men looked one upon another. Then they looked upward, unto the roof.

They bore the sick man up the outer stairway and upon the housetop. There they began to uncover the roof, breaking away the clay and thatch above the place where Jesus sat teaching. Piece by piece they opened a hole, and dust fell down into the room below. The people within looked upward and marveled.

Through the opening, the four men let down the bed whereon the sick of the palsy lay, lowering him carefully into the midst of the room, until he came to rest before the feet of Jesus.

All was still. Jesus looked upon the four men above, whose faces shone with sweat and dust from the broken roof. He saw their faith. Then he looked upon him that was sick of the palsy.

"Son," he said, "thy sins be forgiven thee."

But there were certain of the scribes sitting there, reasoning in their hearts: "Why doth this man thus speak blasphemies? Who can forgive sins but God only?"

And immediately Jesus, perceiving in his spirit that they so reasoned within themselves, said unto them, "Why reason ye these things in your hearts? Whether is it easier to say to the sick of the palsy, Thy sins be forgiven thee; or to say, Arise, and take up thy bed, and walk?"

No man answered. The room was still as stone.

"But that ye may know that the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins —"

He turned unto the sick of the palsy.

"I say unto thee, Arise, and take up thy bed, and go thy way into thine house."

And immediately the man arose. He took up the bed and went forth before them all. The people stood aside to let him pass, for he walked where before he had been borne upon the hands of others. He went out from the house and into the light of day.

They were all amazed, and glorified God, saying one to another, "We never saw it on this fashion."
