The decree had been signed in the afternoon. Daniel had read the writing in the king's hall while the satraps were still gathered around the throne. He had said nothing in the hall. He walked out of the palace gate. He went home through the streets of the city. He climbed to the upper room where he always prayed.

The windows there stood open toward Jerusalem. They had stood open every day since the year the captivity had brought him into the king's service. The room was small. There was a stone bench along one wall and a low table where he kept the scrolls. There was nothing else. He went into the room. He knelt where he had always knelt. He gave thanks before his God three times in the day, as he had always done.

The men who had been watching for him gathered at the courtyard wall. They saw the bent shape at the window. They went back to the king with the law in their mouths.

The king's face changed when he heard. He labored at the matter until the sun had gone down behind the palace gardens. He was looking for a way to lift the seal he had set with his own hand. The conspirators came back at the going down of the day. They reminded him that a decree of the Medes and Persians did not turn. The king commanded. They brought Daniel. They cast him into the den of lions.

A stone was rolled across the mouth of the den. The king pressed his own signet into the soft clay. The signets of his lords were pressed beside it. The seal hardened in the evening air. He spoke to Daniel through the stone before he turned away. "Your God whom you serve continually," he said. "He will deliver you."

The king went up to his palace and would not eat. He sent the musicians out of the chamber. He sat in his own room with the door closed. The lamps were not lit. He did not call for wine. He sat in the dark and waited for the morning. His sleep fled from him.

Sometime in the night he stood at his window. He looked out across the gardens. The night was very still. The lions were quiet below the palace, and that was not what they did. He listened a long time. He heard nothing. He sat back down on the edge of his bed and waited.

He went down to the den very early, before the city was awake. The torch-bearers walked ahead of him on the stone steps that led to the den. The stone of the king's seal was still in place at the mouth. He stood before it. He cried out with a troubled voice. "Daniel, servant of the living God. Is your God whom you serve continually able to deliver you from the lions?"

The voice that answered was steady through the stone. It came up out of the den the way the answer comes out of any place where a man is still alive. "O king, live forever. My God sent his angel. He shut the lions' mouths. They have not hurt me. Innocence was found in me before him. And before you, O king, I have done no wrong."

The king was exceedingly glad. He gave the command. They broke the seal. They rolled the stone aside. Daniel walked up out of the den. The morning was very fresh on the upper stones. No kind of harm was found upon him. The king embraced him in the doorway.

Daniel went home through the city. The windows in the upper room still stood open. He went up. He knelt where he had knelt the day before. He gave thanks before his God as he had always done. The lions far below the palace were quiet for the rest of that morning.
