The decree was already signed, and Daniel had read it before he went up to his chamber. He passed into his house and crossed to the upper room. His windows being open toward Jerusalem, as they had been open aforetime, he kneeled upon his knees three times a day, and he gave thanks before his God.

The men that had been watching for him gathered without the court. They saw the bowed form at the casement. They went unto the king with the law upon their lips.

The king's countenance was changed when he heard. He laboured at the thing until the going down of the sun, seeking how he might undo the seal he had set with his own hand. The men came again at the evening hour, and reminded him that a decree of the Medes and Persians altereth not. Then the king commanded, and Daniel was brought, and they cast him into the den of lions.

A stone was rolled upon the mouth of the den. The king sealed it with his own signet, and with the signets of his lords beside it, and the seal hardened in the air of evening. He spake unto Daniel through the stone before he turned aside. "Thy God whom thou servest continually," he said, "he will deliver thee."

The king went up unto his palace and would not eat. He commanded the instruments of musick to be put forth from his chamber. His sleep went from him. The window of his own room looked toward the east, and the night was long before the sky began to thin above the gardens.

He arose very early in the morning, and went in haste unto the den. He cried with a lamentable voice at the mouth of the stone. "O Daniel, servant of the living God, is thy God whom thou servest continually able to deliver thee from the lions?"

The voice that answered came steady through the stone. "O king, live for ever. My God hath sent his angel, and hath shut the lions' mouths."

They opened the den. Daniel came forth into the morning of the king, and no manner of hurt was found upon him.
