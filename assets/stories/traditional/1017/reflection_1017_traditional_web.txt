There is a kind of courage that looks nothing like what the world calls brave. It has no sword, no battlefield, no audience cheering from the walls. It looks like a man cutting wood, day after day, building something no one has asked for, in response to a voice no one else has heard.

Noah's obedience was not a single dramatic act. It was sustained, repetitive, daily labor — plank after plank, seam after seam — carried out in a world that saw no reason for any of it. Sometimes faithfulness is not a moment of boldness but a long season of showing up to do the work that has been given.
