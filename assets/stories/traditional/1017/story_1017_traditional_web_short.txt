Noah was a righteous man, blameless among the people of his time. He walked with God. He had three sons: Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

The earth around him had turned. Violence filled it openly. All flesh had corrupted their way on the earth. Men struck each other in the roads and took what they wanted. Blood dried in the dirt, and no one called it strange.

God looked at the earth. He saw what it had become.

God said to Noah, "The end of all flesh has come before me, for the earth is filled with violence through them. I will destroy them with the earth."

The words were final.

Then God gave instructions. Precise. Detailed. Every measurement exact.

"Make yourself an ark of gopher wood. Make rooms in the ark, and cover it inside and outside with pitch."

The length: three hundred cubits. The breadth: fifty cubits. The height: thirty cubits. A window near the top, finished to a cubit from the upper edge. A door set in the side. Three decks — lower, second, and third.

The dimensions described a vessel unlike anything Noah had seen. Three hundred cubits of hull. The timber would take years to cut. The pitch would have to be gathered in vast quantities, heated, and spread by hand across every surface.

God told Noah what was coming. "I will bring a flood of waters on the earth to destroy all flesh in which is the breath of life from under the sky. Everything that is on the earth will die."

But with Noah, God made a covenant. "You will come into the ark — you, your sons, your wife, and your sons' wives with you. Of every living thing, two of every sort you shall bring into the ark to keep them alive. They shall be male and female."

Birds after their kind. Animals after their kind. Every creeping thing after its kind. Two of every sort would come to Noah. He was also to gather food — every kind of food that is eaten — and store it as provision for all.

The scope was enormous. Not just the building, but the provisioning. Food for his family and every creature. Stored in quantities enough to last he knew not how long.

The world around him continued as before. And in the middle of it, on open ground, Noah began to work. He cut the first timber. He measured the first beam.

Noah did this. According to all that God commanded him, so he did.
