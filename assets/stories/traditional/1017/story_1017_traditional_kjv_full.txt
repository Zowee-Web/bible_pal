Noah was a just man, and perfect in his generations. Noah walked with God. And Noah begat three sons: Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

The earth about him had turned to ruin. Violence filled it — not the hidden violence of whispered wrongs, but something open and complete. All flesh had corrupted his way upon the earth. Men struck one another in the roads. They took what was not theirs and called it strength. Blood dried in the dust, and none paused to look. The fields were seized by force. The weak were driven forth. The word "justice" had become a sound without meaning.

God looked upon the earth, and behold, it was corrupt.

God said unto Noah, "The end of all flesh is come before me, for the earth is filled with violence through them. Behold, I will destroy them with the earth."

The words were absolute. No parley followed. No appeal was offered. The sentence had been spoken, and it was total.

Then God gave instruction. It came with the precision of a master builder addressing a craftsman, each particular laid down in order.

"Make thee an ark of gopher wood. Rooms shalt thou make in the ark, and shalt pitch it within and without with pitch."

The length thereof: three hundred cubits. The breadth thereof: fifty cubits. The height thereof: thirty cubits. A window shalt thou make to the ark, and in a cubit shalt thou finish it above. The door of the ark shalt thou set in the side thereof. With lower, second, and third stories shalt thou make it.

Noah hearkened. The dimensions set forth a vessel greater than anything he had seen or heard tell of. Three hundred cubits of hull — a structure so long that a man standing at the one end could scarce discern the shape of the other. Fifty cubits in breadth. Thirty cubits in height. The timber required would strip whole hillsides bare. Every plank must be hewn and shaped and fitted and sealed. The pitch — thick, black, and hot — must be gathered in vast measure and spread by hand upon every surface, within and without. The joints must hold against a pressure none had ever proved.

The labour would take years. Trees felled and dragged to the building site. Logs cleft into planks. Beams shaped with the adze.

God declared what was to come. "Behold, I do bring a flood of waters upon the earth, to destroy all flesh wherein is the breath of life from under heaven. Every thing that is in the earth shall die."

A flood. Water rising till it covered the ground, the trees, the high places. All that breathed would perish. The earth would be given over to the waters.

But with Noah, God established his covenant. "Thou shalt come into the ark — thou, and thy sons, and thy wife, and thy sons' wives with thee."

Eight souls. Of all the earth, eight would be preserved.

"And of every living thing of all flesh, two of every sort shalt thou bring into the ark, to keep them alive with thee. They shall be male and female."

Of fowls after their kind. Of cattle after their kind. Of every creeping thing of the earth after his kind. Two of every sort would come unto Noah. The text saith they would come — not that Noah must trap them or pursue them, but that they would come of themselves.

He was also to take food. All manner of food that is eaten — grain, fruit, dried stores, fodder — whatsoever each creature required. He must gather it and lay it up within the ark as provision for his household and for every living thing that should share the vessel.

The full measure of what God required was immense. The building alone would consume years of toil. But beyond the labour of construction lay the provisioning — reckoning of rations, ordering of stores, gathering food enough for eight souls and every sort of beast for a time none could foretell.

And all this in a world that had no cause to credit it. No flood was seen. The rivers kept their courses. The ground was dry. A man raising a great vessel upon open land, filling it with food and making ready pens for creatures — the sight invited scorn from every neighbour who passed.

The text recordeth no complaint from Noah. No question put. No objection raised. No bargaining, no plea for lighter terms.

Thus did Noah. According to all that God commanded him, so did he.

He hewed the gopher wood and set about to shape it. He measured the beams and fitted the joints. He heated the pitch and laid it thick upon the planks. Plank by plank, deck by deck, room by room, the ark began to rise upon the open ground. The smell of new-cut wood and hot pitch hung in the air about the place of building. Sawdust gathered in drifts at the foot of the growing hull.

Noah builded. According to all that God commanded him, so did he.
