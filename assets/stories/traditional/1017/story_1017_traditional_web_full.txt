Noah was a righteous man, blameless among the people of his time. He walked with God. He had three sons: Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

The earth around him had turned to ruin. Violence filled it — not the hidden violence of whispered wrongs, but something open and total. All flesh had corrupted their way on the earth. Men struck each other in the roads. They took what was not theirs and called it strength. Blood dried in the dirt, and no one stopped to look. The fields were seized by force. The weak were driven out. The word "justice" had become a sound without meaning.

God looked at the earth and saw how corrupt it had become.

God said to Noah, "The end of all flesh has come before me, for the earth is filled with violence through them. I will destroy them with the earth."

The words were absolute. No negotiation followed. No appeal was offered. The sentence had been spoken, and it was total.

Then God gave instructions. They came with the precision of a master builder addressing a craftsman, each specification laid down in turn.

"Make yourself an ark of gopher wood. Make rooms in the ark, and cover it inside and outside with pitch."

The length of the ark: three hundred cubits. The breadth: fifty cubits. The height: thirty cubits. A window was to be set near the top, finished to a cubit from the upper edge. A door was to be placed in the side. Three decks — lower, second, and third.

Noah listened. The dimensions described a vessel larger than anything he had seen or heard of. Three hundred cubits of hull — a structure so long that a man standing at one end could barely make out the shape of the other. Fifty cubits wide. Thirty cubits tall. The timber required would strip whole hillsides. Each plank would have to be cut, shaped, fitted, and sealed. The pitch — thick, black, hot — would have to be gathered in enormous quantities and spread by hand across every surface, inside and out. The joints would need to hold against pressure no one had ever tested.

The work would take years. The materials would have to be sourced and transported. Trees felled and dragged to the building site. Logs split into planks. Beams shaped with adzes. Rooms framed and floored across three separate decks.

God explained what was coming. "I will bring a flood of waters on the earth to destroy all flesh in which is the breath of life from under the sky. Everything that is on the earth will die."

A flood. Water rising until it covered the ground, the trees, the hills. Everything that breathed would perish. The earth would be returned to the waters.

But God established a covenant with Noah. "You will come into the ark — you, your sons, your wife, and your sons' wives with you."

Eight people. Out of all the earth, eight would be preserved.

"Of every living thing of all flesh, two of every sort you shall bring into the ark to keep them alive with you. They shall be male and female."

Birds after their kind. Animals after their kind. Every creeping thing of the ground after its kind. Two of every sort would come to Noah. The text says they would come — not that Noah would trap them or chase them, but that they would come to him.

He was also to take food. Every sort of food that is eaten — grain, fruit, dried stores, fodder — whatever each creature required. He was to gather it and store it aboard the ark as provision for his household and for every living thing that would share the vessel.

The full scope of what God asked was immense. The building alone would consume years of labor. But beyond the construction lay the provisioning — calculating rations, organizing storage, gathering enough food for eight people and every kind of animal for a duration no one could predict.

And all of this in a world that had no reason to believe it. No rain threatened. No flood was visible. The rivers ran in their courses. The ground was dry. A man building a massive vessel on open land, stocking it with food and preparing pens for creatures — the scene invited ridicule from every neighbor who passed.

The text records no complaint from Noah. No question asked. No objection raised. No bargaining, no request for easier terms.

Noah did this. According to all that God commanded him, so he did.

He cut the gopher wood and began to shape it. He measured the beams and fitted the joints. He heated the pitch and spread it thick across the planks. Plank by plank, deck by deck, room by room, the ark began to rise on the open ground. The smell of fresh-cut wood and hot pitch hung in the air around the building site. Sawdust gathered in drifts at the base of the growing hull.

Noah built. According to all that God commanded him, so he did.
