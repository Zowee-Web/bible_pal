In the sixth month, God sent the angel Gabriel unto a city of Galilee, named Nazareth. It sat among terraced slopes where olive trees grew in crooked rows and grapevines climbed the stone walls between the houses. The paths were narrow and dusty. The houses were built of pale stone with flat roofs. Smoke drifted from the fires within. Women bore water jars upon their shoulders. Children moved between the houses, and the sound of their voices mingled with the bleating of goats tethered near the walls.

The day was ordinary. Nothing in it differed from any other day in Nazareth.

Somewhere in the town, a young woman called Mary went about her day. She was espoused to a man named Joseph, of the house of David. She was a virgin. Her life had been small and quiet — the common shape of a young woman in a country village. She drew water. She ground the grain. She mended cloth and swept the earthen floor.

Then the angel came unto her.

He appeared in the place where she was. The room changed — the light, the air, the very stillness. Gabriel stood before her, and he spake.

"Hail, thou that art highly favoured," he said. "The Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women."

Mary was greatly troubled at his saying. She ran not. She cried not out. She stood in the room, her hands still, her breath caught, and cast in her mind what manner of salutation this should be.

The angel saw her trouble, and he spake again. His voice was steady. His words were gentle.

"Fear not, Mary," he said, "for thou hast found favour with God."

He paused, and then continued.

"Behold, thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and bring forth a son, and shalt call his name Jesus. He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Highest. The Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David. And he shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end."

The room was quiet. Without, a bird called from the rooftop. A door closed somewhere down the path.

Mary hearkened. Her hands were still at her sides.

"How shall this be," she said, "seeing I know not a man?"

The angel answered and said unto her gently, "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee. Therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."

He paused once more.

"And, behold, thy cousin Elisabeth — she hath also conceived a son in her old age. And this is the sixth month with her, who was called barren. For with God nothing shall be impossible."

The room grew very still. Without, the wind moved through the village. Somewhere a child called unto another and then was quiet. The afternoon light fell through the doorway and lay in a long warm stripe across the floor.

Mary stood in that light. She drew breath, and let it go. The weight of what she had been told settled upon her — not as a burden, but as a question that required all she had to answer.

Then she spake.

"Behold the handmaid of the Lord," she said. "Be it unto me according to thy word."

And the angel departed from her.
