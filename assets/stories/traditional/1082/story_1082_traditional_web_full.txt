In the sixth month, God sent the angel Gabriel to a small town in the hills of Galilee. The town was called Nazareth. It sat among terraced slopes where olive trees grew in crooked rows and grapevines climbed the stone walls between the houses. The paths were narrow and dusty. The houses were built of pale stone with flat roofs. Smoke drifted from cooking fires. Women carried water jars on their shoulders. Children moved between the houses, and the sound of their voices mixed with the bleating of goats tethered near the walls.

The day was ordinary. Nothing about it was different from any other day in Nazareth.

Somewhere in the town, a young woman named Mary was going about her life. She was pledged to be married to a man named Joseph, from the house of David. She was a virgin. Her life, up until now, had been the small and quiet shape of a young woman in a country village — washing, grinding grain, mending cloth, walking the same narrow paths she had walked since childhood.

Then the angel came to her.

He appeared in the place where she was. The room changed — the light, the air, the stillness. Gabriel stood before her, tall and luminous, and he spoke.

"Rejoice, you highly favored one," he said. "The Lord is with you. Blessed are you among women."

Mary was greatly troubled at his words. She did not run. She did not cry out. She stood there, her hands still, her breath caught, trying to understand what kind of greeting this could be. What was happening. Why her.

The angel saw her trouble, and he spoke again. His voice was steady. His words were kind.

"Do not be afraid, Mary," he said. "For you have found favor with God."

He paused, and then continued.

"Behold, you will conceive in your womb, and bring forth a son. You will call his name Jesus. He will be great. He will be called the Son of the Most High. The Lord God will give him the throne of his father David. He will reign over the house of Jacob forever. There will be no end to his kingdom."

The room was quiet. Outside, a bird called from the rooftop. A door closed somewhere down the path.

Mary listened. Her hands were quiet at her sides.

"How can this be," she said, "seeing I am a virgin?"

The angel answered her gently.

"The Holy Spirit will come on you," he said, "and the power of the Most High will overshadow you. Therefore the holy one who is born from you will be called the Son of God."

He paused again.

"Behold, Elizabeth your relative — she too has conceived a son in her old age. This is the sixth month with her who was called barren. For nothing will be impossible with God."

The room grew very still. Outside, the wind moved through the village. Somewhere a child called to another and then fell quiet. The afternoon light fell through the doorway and lay in a long warm stripe across the floor.

Mary stood in that light. She breathed in, and she breathed out. The weight of what she had been told settled over her — not like a burden, but like a question that required everything she had to answer.

Then she spoke.

"Behold, the servant of the Lord," she said. "Be it to me according to your word."

And the angel departed from her. The room was quiet again. The light on the floor did not move. Outside, the village of Nazareth went on — the goats, the children, the cooking fires, the ordinary afternoon. But inside that room, everything had changed.
