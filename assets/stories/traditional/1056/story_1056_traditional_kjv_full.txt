The people of Israel were hemmed in. Behind them, Pharaoh's host was coming on — hundreds of chariots with horses drawing them swiftly across the level ground. The dust of the wheels rose up in a great brown cloud. The sounds were terrible — the pounding of hooves, the rattling of wheels, the shoutings of the Egyptians.

Before them was the sea. Wide and dark and deep. There was no bridge. There were no ships. There was nowhere to go.

Mothers held their babes close. Fathers caught hold of their children's hands. Old men leaned upon their staves and stared at the water. All were sore afraid — so afraid that some of them were wroth with Moses.

"Wherefore hast thou brought us hither?" they cried. "Were there no graves in Egypt? We shall die in the wilderness!"

Moses stood before them all. He paced not. He shouted not. He looked upon the people and spake in a voice that carried over the multitude.

"Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord, which he will shew to you to day. The Egyptians whom ye have seen to day, ye shall see them again no more for ever. The Lord shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace."

Then the Lord spake unto Moses. "Speak unto the children of Israel, that they go forward. Lift thou up thy rod, and stretch out thine hand over the sea."

Moses lifted up his arm. His rod was stretched forth over the water. And the Lord sent a strong east wind.

The wind blew mightily. It blew all the night without ceasing. It was not a stormy wind that swirled and gusted — it was steady and strong, pressing upon the water like two great hands driving the sea asunder.

And the sea moved.

The waters began to draw back from the midst. Slowly at the first, then more swiftly. They parted and rose up on either hand until they stood as two great walls — one on the left, one on the right — taller than any building the children had ever beheld. The water within those walls churned and swirled, yet it held. It stood in its place.

Between the two walls was a way. The floor of the sea lay open — packed sand and smooth stones and little shells. The wind dried the ground so that it was firm to walk upon.

The people gazed. None moved at the first. Then Moses went forward. And one by one, the families followed.

They went through the midst of the sea upon the dry ground. Mothers bare their smallest children. Fathers led the asses and the sheep. The elder children held hands and lifted up their eyes unto the walls of water with huge eyes. Some of them stretched forth their fingers toward the water, yet touched it not. The ground was firm beneath their sandals. No waves splashed them. No water reached their feet.

It was a long time before all were passed over — thousands of people with all their cattle and all their goods, walking between the walls of the sea in the light of the pillar of fire which the Lord had placed behind them.

When the last one came unto the far shore, Pharaoh's host followed after. The chariots rolled into the sea path. The horses galloped onward. The men of war thought to overtake the children of Israel.

But the Lord looked down out of the pillar of fire and of cloud. He made the chariot wheels to drag heavily. They would not turn. The chariots lurched and stopped. Horses stumbled. The men of war were sore afraid. "Let us flee from the face of Israel!" they cried. "The Lord fighteth for them!"

But it was too late.

The Lord said unto Moses, "Stretch out thine hand over the sea." Moses did so. And the waters came again. The walls fell. The sea rushed in from both sides and covered all — the chariots, the horsemen, the men of war. All the host of Pharaoh that had come in after them into the sea was gone. There remained not so much as one of them.

The people of Israel stood upon the far shore. The morning sun arose over the water. Behind them, the sea was calm again, smooth and still in the early light.
