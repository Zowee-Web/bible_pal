The people of Israel were trapped. In front of them was the sea, wide and deep. Behind them was Pharaoh's army — horses and chariots and soldiers, all coming fast. The ground shook with the sound of their wheels. Dust rose behind them like a brown wall.

Everyone was scared. Mothers pulled their children close. Men looked at the water and then looked back at the army and didn't know what to do. Some of them cried out to Moses. "Why did you bring us here? We're going to die!"

Moses stood in front of them. He was not running. He was not hiding. He said, "Don't be afraid. Stand still. Watch what God is about to do. The Lord will fight for you. You just have to be quiet and trust."

Then God told Moses to hold his staff out over the water.

Moses lifted his arm. A strong wind came from the east. It blew all night long — a steady, powerful wind that pushed the water apart. Slowly, the sea opened up. The water pulled back on both sides and stood up like two tall walls, one on the left and one on the right. Between them was a path of dry ground.

The people walked through. Right through the middle of the sea, on dry land. Children held their parents' hands. Animals walked between the walls of water. Nobody got wet. The ground was firm and solid under their feet. Some of the children looked up at the water towering above them with wide, amazed eyes.

When everyone was safe on the other side, Pharaoh's army tried to follow. Their chariots rolled into the sea path. But God made the chariot wheels get stuck. The soldiers panicked. The horses stumbled and pulled sideways.

Then God told Moses to stretch out his hand again. Moses did. The water came rushing back. It covered the chariots and the horses and all of Pharaoh's soldiers. Not one of them was left.

The people of Israel stood on the far shore and looked back. The sea was calm again, flat and quiet in the morning light.
