The people of Israel were hemmed in. Before them was the sea, wide and deep. Behind them was Pharaoh's host — horses and chariots and men of war, all coming on apace. The ground trembled with the sound of their wheels. Dust rose behind them like a brown wall.

All were sore afraid. Mothers drew their children close. Men beheld the water and beheld again the host, and knew not what to do. Some of them cried out unto Moses. "Wherefore hast thou brought us hither? We shall surely die!"

Moses stood before them. He fled not. He hid not. He said, "Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord. The Lord shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace."

Then the Lord said unto Moses, "Stretch out thine hand over the sea."

Moses lifted up his arm. A strong wind came from the east. It blew all the night — a steady and mighty wind that drave the waters asunder. Slowly, the sea opened. The water drew back on either side and stood up as two great walls, one on the left and one on the right. Between them was a path of dry ground.

The people went through. Even through the midst of the sea, upon dry land. Children held their parents' hands. Beasts walked between the walls of water. None were wet. The ground was firm and sure beneath their feet. Some of the children lifted up their eyes unto the water towering above them with wide, wondering eyes.

When all were safe upon the other side, Pharaoh's host essayed to follow. Their chariots rolled into the sea path. But the Lord made the chariot wheels to drag. The men of war were sore afraid. The horses stumbled and pulled aside.

Then the Lord said unto Moses, "Stretch forth thine hand again." Moses did so. The water came rushing back. It covered the chariots and the horsemen, and all the host of Pharaoh. There remained not so much as one of them.

The people of Israel stood upon the far shore and beheld. The sea was calm again, smooth and still in the morning light.
