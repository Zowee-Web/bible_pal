The people of Israel were trapped. Behind them, Pharaoh's army was coming — hundreds of chariots with horses pulling them fast across the flat ground. The dust from the wheels rose up in a great brown cloud. The sounds were terrifying — the pounding of hooves, the rattling of wheels, the shouts of Egyptian soldiers.

In front of them was the sea. Wide and dark and deep. There was no bridge. There were no boats. There was nowhere to go.

Mothers held their babies tight. Fathers grabbed their children's hands. Old people leaned on their walking sticks and stared at the water. Everyone was scared — so scared that some of them got angry at Moses.

"Why did you bring us out here?" they shouted. "Were there no graves back in Egypt? We're going to die in the wilderness!"

Moses stood before them all. He did not pace. He did not shout. He looked at the people and spoke in a voice that carried over the crowd.

"Do not be afraid. Stand still and watch. The Lord will fight for you today. You will see the Egyptians behind you, but after today you will never see them again. All you have to do is be quiet and trust God."

Then God spoke to Moses. "Tell the people to move forward. Lift your staff and stretch your hand out over the sea."

Moses raised his arm. His staff pointed out over the water. And God sent a strong east wind.

The wind blew hard. It blew all night long without stopping. It was not a storm wind that swirled and gusted — it was steady and strong, pressing against the water like two giant hands pushing the sea apart.

And the sea moved.

The water began to pull back from the center. Slowly at first, then faster. It drew apart and rose up on either side until it stood like two great walls — one on the left, one on the right — taller than any building the children had ever seen. The water inside those walls churned and swirled, but it held. It stayed in place.

Between the two walls was a path. The floor of the sea lay open — packed sand and smooth stones and little shells. The wind dried the ground so it was firm to walk on.

The people stared. Nobody moved at first. Then Moses walked forward. And one by one, the families followed.

They walked through the middle of the sea on dry ground. Mothers carried their smallest children. Fathers led the donkeys and sheep. Older children held hands and looked up at the water walls with huge eyes. Some of them reached out their fingers toward the water but did not quite touch it. The ground was solid beneath their sandals. No waves splashed them. No water reached their feet.

It took a long time for everyone to cross — thousands of people with all their animals and all their belongings, walking between the walls of the sea in the light of the pillar of fire that God had placed behind them.

When the last person reached the far shore, Pharaoh's army followed. The chariots rolled into the sea path. The horses galloped forward. The soldiers thought they would catch the Israelites.

But God looked down from the pillar of fire and cloud. He made the chariot wheels get stuck in the ground. They wouldn't turn. The chariots lurched and stopped. Horses stumbled. The soldiers panicked. "Let's get out of here!" they shouted. "God is fighting for them!"

But it was too late.

God told Moses to stretch out his hand over the sea again. Moses did. And the water came back. The walls fell. The sea rushed in from both sides and covered everything — the chariots, the horses, the soldiers. All of Pharaoh's army that had followed them into the sea was gone.

The people of Israel stood on the far shore. The morning sun rose over the water. Behind them, the sea was calm again, as if nothing had happened. But everything had happened. They were safe. God had made a path through the deepest, scariest place — and brought them through to the other side.

And they believed in the Lord.
