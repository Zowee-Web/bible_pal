In the days of the Sabbath, the Levites gathered in the courts of the temple, and they took up the song of the Sabbath day. The lamps were burning in the house of the Lord, and the morning was clear above the city.

The chief musician lifted up his voice, and he sang the song that was written for the Sabbath day.

He said, "It is a good thing to give thanks to the Lord, and to sing praises to your name, Most High. To proclaim your loving kindness in the morning, and your faithfulness every night, with the ten-stringed lute, with the harp, and with the melody of the lyre."

He sang, "For you, Lord, have made me glad through your work. I will triumph in the works of your hands. How great are your works, Lord. Your thoughts are very deep."

The musician played upon the lyre, and the strings sounded in the courts of the house. The Levites sang together, and the voices rose into the morning.

He said, "A senseless man doesn't know, neither does a fool understand this. Though the wicked spring up as the grass, and all the evildoers flourish, they will be destroyed forever. But you, Lord, are on high forevermore."

He said, "The righteous shall flourish like the palm tree. He will grow like a cedar in Lebanon. They are planted in the Lord's house. They will flourish in our God's courts."

He sang, "They will still bring forth fruit in old age. They will be full of sap and green, to show that the Lord is upright. He is my rock, and there is no unrighteousness in him."

The Levites concluded the song, and the harps grew still in their hands. The lamps burned softly along the walls of the temple, and the people who had gathered for the Sabbath stood in the courts and gave thanks.

The morning was warm above Jerusalem, and the day of rest was kept by the people, and the words of the song were sung again at the evening offering.

The Sabbath came and went over the temple, and the words of the song remained, sung in the days that came after by the children of Israel.
