The word of the Lord came to Jonah, the son of Amittai. "Arise," God said. "Go to Nineveh, that great city, and cry out against it, for their wickedness has come up before me."

Jonah rose. But he did not go to Nineveh. He went the opposite direction.

He went down to Joppa, a port city on the coast. He found a ship bound for Tarshish — as far west as a man could sail, as far from Nineveh as the known world allowed. He paid the fare, climbed aboard, and went down into the hold of the ship. He was running from the presence of the Lord.

The ship set out to sea. The coast fell away behind them. The water stretched wide and blue and open.

Then the Lord sent a great wind upon the sea. A violent storm struck the ship — waves crashing over the bow, timbers groaning, the deck pitching beneath the sailors' feet. The storm was so fierce that the ship threatened to break apart.

The sailors were terrified. Each man cried out to his own god. They threw the cargo overboard to lighten the ship, heaving crates and barrels and bundles into the churning water. The wind howled. The waves battered the hull.

And Jonah was below deck, fast asleep.

The captain found him. "How can you sleep?" he said. "Get up! Call on your God! Perhaps your God will notice us and we will not perish."

The sailors cast lots to find out who was to blame for the disaster. The lot fell on Jonah.

They surrounded him. "Tell us," they said. "Who are you? Where do you come from? What have you done?"

Jonah told them. "I am a Hebrew," he said. "I worship the Lord, the God of heaven, who made the sea and the dry land." And he told them that he was running from God.

The men were terrified. "What should we do to you," they asked, "to make the sea calm down?"

"Pick me up," Jonah said, "and throw me into the sea. Then the sea will become calm. I know that this great storm has come because of me."

The sailors did not want to do it. They rowed hard, trying to reach land. But the storm only grew worse. Finally, they cried out to the Lord: "Please, do not let us perish for this man's life." Then they picked Jonah up and threw him into the sea.

And the sea stopped raging.

The Lord provided a great fish to swallow Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.
