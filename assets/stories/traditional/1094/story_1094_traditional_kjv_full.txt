Now the word of the Lord came unto Jonah the son of Amittai. The command was clear. "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and cry against it; for their wickedness is come up before me."

Nineveh lay to the east — deep in the heart of Assyria, a city of great walls and thronging streets, feared and hated by the nations round about. The journey would have been long, perilous, and deeply unwelcome.

But Jonah rose up to flee unto Tarshish from the presence of the Lord. He went not east. He went west.

He went down to Joppa, a port upon the Mediterranean coast where the waves broke against the stone harbour walls and the air bore the smell of salt and pitch. He found a ship going to Tarshish — as far from Nineveh as the known world reached. He paid the fare thereof, went down into the ship, and lay in the hold. The ship put forth from the harbour, the sails filled, and the coast of Israel fell away behind. He was fleeing from the presence of the Lord.

The sea was open and wide. The wind was fair. The ship moved steadily westward.

Then the Lord sent out a great wind into the sea. It came without warning — a violent, howling tempest that struck the ship broadside. Waves crashed over the deck. Timbers groaned and cracked. The mast swayed. Water poured through the seams. The ship was like to be broken.

The mariners were afraid. Every man cried unto his god, shouting into the wind, gripping the rails. They cast forth the wares into the sea to lighten the ship — crates and barrels and bundles heaved overboard into the churning water.

And Jonah was gone down into the sides of the ship, and was fast asleep.

The shipmaster came unto him. "What meanest thou, O sleeper?" he cried above the wind. "Arise, call upon thy God, if so be that God will think upon us, that we perish not."

The men cast lots. The lot fell upon Jonah.

They gathered about him. "Tell us, we pray thee," they said, "for whose cause is this evil upon us? What is thine occupation? Whence comest thou? What is thy country? Of what people art thou?"

Jonah looked upon them. "I am an Hebrew," he said, "and I fear the Lord, the God of heaven, which hath made the sea and the dry land." And he told them that he fled from the presence of the Lord.

The men were exceedingly afraid. "What shall we do unto thee," they asked, "that the sea may be calm unto us?"

"Take me up," said Jonah, "and cast me forth into the sea; so shall the sea be calm unto you: for I know that for my sake this great tempest is upon you."

The men would not. They rowed hard to bring the ship to land. But they could not, for the sea wrought and was tempestuous against them. At last they cried unto the Lord: "We beseech thee, O Lord, we beseech thee, let us not perish for this man's life, and lay not upon us innocent blood." And they took up Jonah, and cast him forth into the sea.

And the sea ceased from her raging. The wind was still. The waves fell flat. The mariners stood upon the deck in the sudden calm, and they feared the Lord exceedingly.

Now the Lord had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.
