God had a job for Jonah. And it was a big one.

"Jonah," God said, "I need you to go to the city of Nineveh. It's a huge, powerful city, and the people there are doing terrible things. Go there and tell them to stop."

Now, Nineveh was a scary place. It was the capital of Assyria — a kingdom that had been cruel to a lot of people. Going there to tell them they were doing wrong was about the LAST thing anyone would want to do.

And Jonah? He did NOT want to go. Not even a little bit.

So instead of going EAST to Nineveh, Jonah went WEST. As far west as he could possibly go. He went to a port city called Joppa, right on the coast. He found a ship that was sailing to a place called Tarshish — basically the opposite end of the world from Nineveh. He bought a ticket, climbed aboard, went below deck into the bottom of the ship, and fell asleep.

He was running away from God. Which, if you think about it, is a pretty silly thing to try.

The ship set sail. The coast disappeared behind them. The open sea stretched out wide and blue in every direction. Everything was quiet. Everything was calm.

And then God sent a storm.

Not just any storm. A HUGE storm. The kind where the wind screams and the waves tower over the ship and water comes crashing in from every side. The boat rocked back and forth so hard the sailors thought it would crack in half!

The sailors were terrified. These were tough guys who lived on the sea, and THEY were scared. Each one started praying to whatever god he believed in. They threw everything they could overboard — all the boxes and barrels and supplies — trying to make the ship lighter so it wouldn't sink.

And where was Jonah during all this? Fast asleep below deck! Can you believe it?

The captain found him. "WHAT ARE YOU DOING SLEEPING?!" he yelled over the roar of the wind. "Get up and pray! Maybe your God can help us!"

The sailors decided to figure out whose fault this terrible storm was. They drew straws. And guess whose name came up?

Jonah.

They all gathered around him, holding onto the ship's rails to keep from falling. "Who ARE you?" they asked. "Where are you from? What did you DO?"

Jonah told them the truth. "I'm a Hebrew. I worship the God who made the sea and the land. And... I'm running away from him. That's why this storm is happening. It's because of me."

The sailors' faces went pale. The God who MADE the sea?!

"What should we do?" they asked desperately.

Jonah looked out at the raging waves. He took a deep breath. "Throw me into the sea," he said. "Then the storm will stop. I know this is all my fault."

The sailors didn't want to throw him overboard. They weren't mean people. They tried rowing to shore instead, pulling the oars as hard as they could. But the waves just got bigger and bigger and BIGGER.

Finally, when they had no other choice, they prayed to God: "Please don't blame us for this!" And they picked Jonah up and threw him into the water.

IMMEDIATELY, the storm stopped.

The wind just... stopped. The waves went flat. The water became as smooth as a mirror. The whole sea was completely, perfectly calm.

The sailors stood on the deck, dripping wet, staring at each other with wide eyes. They knew they had just seen the power of the real God.

But what about Jonah?

He was sinking into the dark water. Down, down, down.

And then — a MASSIVE fish appeared. The biggest fish you could ever imagine. It swam up from the deep water and swallowed Jonah whole.

Jonah was inside that fish for three days and three nights. It was dark. It was cramped. It probably smelled really, REALLY bad.

But Jonah was alive. And finally — inside the belly of a fish at the bottom of the sea — he started to pray.

Turns out, you can't actually run from God. But you CAN always turn back.
