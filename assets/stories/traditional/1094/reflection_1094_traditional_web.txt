Running from something you've been asked to do is not always dramatic. Sometimes it looks like buying a ticket, boarding a ship, and going to sleep.

Jonah did not argue with God. He did not negotiate. He simply left — quietly, efficiently, in the opposite direction. And the sea, which had been perfectly calm, became the instrument that brought everything to a stop.

What is striking is not that the storm came. It is that Jonah already knew. "Throw me in," he said. "This is my fault." He was not surprised. He had known the whole time.

Sometimes the hardest thing is not hearing the call. It is admitting that you heard it clearly — and chose to walk the other way.
