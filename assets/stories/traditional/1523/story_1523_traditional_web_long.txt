The work of the LORD's house had begun. The foundation had been laid in the second year of the people's return from captivity. The priests had been set in their places with their trumpets in their hands. The Levites had taken up the cymbals. They had sung together after the ordinance of David king of Israel.

The shout had gone up over Jerusalem.

The weeping had gone up with it. The old men who had seen the first house with their own eyes wept with a loud voice when the foundation was laid before them. The young men shouted aloud for joy. The two sounds rose together and mingled in the air over the broken walls, so that no man could tell the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping of the people. The sound was heard far off.

The work was going on.

The stones were being shaped. The cedars from Lebanon were being carried up. The masons were at the line. The carpenters were at the wood. The hire was being paid out of the king's house.

Then there came the adversaries.

The adversaries were the people the king of Assyria had brought into the land long ago. They had been put in the cities of Samaria after the children of Israel were carried out of them. They had taken wives of the land. They had built their own altars. They had stayed in the cities of Samaria for many generations. They were not of the children of the captivity that had come up from Babylon.

They heard that the children of the captivity were building a temple unto the LORD God of Israel. They saw the foundation that had been laid. They came down the road to Jerusalem.

They came to Zerubbabel and to the chief of the fathers. They said, Let us build with you. For we seek your God, as you do. We do sacrifice unto him since the days of Esar-haddon king of Assur, who brought us up here.

Zerubbabel and Jeshua and the rest of the chief of the fathers of Israel answered them. They said, You have nothing to do with us to build a house unto our God. We ourselves together will build unto the LORD God of Israel, as king Cyrus the king of Persia has commanded us.

The adversaries went away from the work. They went back the road they had come.

They did not stay away.

They weakened the hands of the people of Judah. They troubled them in the building. They came among them in the street. They stood near the work and spoke against it. They hired counsellors against them to frustrate their purpose. They did this all the days of Cyrus king of Persia. They did this even unto the reign of Darius king of Persia.

In the days of Ahasuerus, in the beginning of his reign, they wrote an accusation against the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem. The accusation was sealed. It was put into the hand of a messenger. It went up the long road from Samaria, through the country and the cities of the king, to Susa where the king sat.

In the days of Artaxerxes, they wrote a letter against Jerusalem. The names of the men who wrote it were set at the top. They called the city a rebellious city, and a bad city. They said the walls of it had been built up in old time, and that the kings of it had risen up against kings. They said that if this city should be built and the walls set up again, the people would not pay toll, tribute, or custom. They said the king would have no portion on this side of the river.

The letter was sent. It went up the same long road to Susa. It was put into the hand of the king's servant. It was read before the king.

The king answered. He commanded that search be made. The search was made in the house of the rolls, where the records were kept. The records were found. The king sent his answer back down the road. He commanded that the work cease. He said that the city be not built, until another commandment should be given from him.

The answer came to Jerusalem. The letter was read before the people. The men went up to them in haste, and made them to cease by force and power.

The work ceased.

The men went home from it. The stones they had been setting that day were left as they had set them. The lines were left where they had been drawn. The tools were taken up out of the dust. The trumpets were put back into their cases. The cymbals were put away. The walls of the house were as they had been.

So the work of the house of God which was at Jerusalem ceased. It ceased unto the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia.

That was many years.

The stones lay where they had been laid. The wood lay where it had been carried in. The cedars darkened in the weather. The grass came up among the stones. The wind brought the dust down upon the wood, and the rain washed it, and the sun dried it again. The lizards ran in and out among the stones. The birds nested where the workmen had stood.

The people went on with their own houses. They cieled their own roofs. They set their own doors in their own doorways. They planted their vineyards. They went up and down the streets of Jerusalem on their own business. The house of the LORD was the only house in the city that was not finished. The foundation was there. The walls were not raised upon it.

The trumpets stayed in their cases. The cymbals stayed where they had been put. The Levites went about their other work. The priests went about theirs.

The old men who had remembered the first house grew older. Some of them sat in the gate. Some of them sat in their doorways and looked out toward the place where the house was to stand. Some of them died, and were buried with their fathers, and saw no more building.

The house was left waiting.
