The work of the LORD's house had begun. The foundation had been laid in the second year of the people's return. The priests had been set with their trumpets. The Levites had sounded the cymbals. They had sung after the ordinance of David. The shout had gone up. The weeping of the old men who remembered the first house had gone up too. The two had mingled so that no man could tell the noise of the joy from the noise of the weeping.

The work was going on.

Then there came the adversaries.

The adversaries were the people the king of Assyria had brought into the land long ago. They had been put in the cities of Samaria after Israel was taken out. They had married into the land. They were not of the children of the captivity. They had heard of the work. They saw the foundation. They came down to Jerusalem.

They said unto Zerubbabel and the chief of the fathers: let us build with you. For we seek your God, as ye do. We do sacrifice unto him since the days of Esar-haddon king of Assur, which brought us up hither.

Zerubbabel and Jeshua and the rest of the chief of the fathers answered them. Ye have nothing to do with us to build an house unto our God. We ourselves together will build unto the LORD God of Israel, as king Cyrus the king of Persia hath commanded us.

The adversaries went away from the work. They did not stay away.

They weakened the hands of the people of Judah. They troubled them in building. They hired counsellors against them to frustrate their purpose. They did so all the days of Cyrus king of Persia. They did so unto the reign of Darius king of Persia.

In the days of Ahasuerus, in the beginning of his reign, they wrote an accusation against the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem. The accusation was sent. It went up the long road from Samaria to Susa.

In the days of Artaxerxes, they wrote a letter against Jerusalem. They named the city a rebellious and bad city. They said the king would have no portion on this side of the river if it were rebuilt. The letter was sent. It was read before the king.

The king answered. He commanded that the work cease. The work ceased. The men went home from it. The stones they had been setting that day were left as they had set them. The walls were as they had been.

So the work ceased unto the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia.

That was many years. The stones lay where they had been laid. The wood lay where it had been carried in. The grass came up among the stones. The dust came down upon the wood. The people went on with their own houses. The house of the LORD was the only one that was not finished. The trumpets were put away. The cymbals were put away. The old men who had remembered the first house grew older. Some of them died. The house was left waiting.
