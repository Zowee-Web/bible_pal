The work of the house of the LORD was begun. The foundation was laid in the second year of the return of the people out of captivity. The priests stood in their places, and the trumpets were in their hands. The Levites took up the cymbals. Together they sang after the ordinance of David, king of Israel.

And the shout went up over Jerusalem.

The weeping went up with it. The old men, who had beheld the first house with their own eyes, wept with a loud voice when the foundation was laid before them. The young men shouted aloud for gladness. The two sounds rose together and were mingled in the air above the broken walls, so that no man could discern the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping of the people. And the sound was heard afar off.

The work went forward.

The stones were hewn. The cedars of Lebanon were brought up. The masons were at the line, and the carpenters at the wood. The wages thereof were paid out of the house of the king.

Then came the adversaries.

These were the people whom the king of Assyria had brought into the land in days long past. They had been set in the cities of Samaria after the children of Israel were carried out of them. They had taken wives of the land. They had builded their own altars. Many generations had they dwelt in the cities of Samaria. They were not of the children of the captivity which came up out of Babylon.

They heard that the children of the captivity did build a temple unto the LORD God of Israel. They beheld the foundation that was laid. And they came down the road unto Jerusalem.

They came unto Zerubbabel, and unto the chief of the fathers, and they spake, saying, Let us build with you, for we also seek your God, and do sacrifice unto him from the days of Esar-haddon, king of Assur, who brought us up hither.

Then answered Zerubbabel, and Jeshua, and the rest of the chief of the fathers of Israel. Ye have nothing to do with us, said they, to build a house unto our God. We ourselves together will build unto the LORD God of Israel, as Cyrus, king of Persia, hath commanded us.

The adversaries went from the work. They returned by the way they came.

But they tarried not away.

They weakened the hands of the people of Judah. They troubled them in the building. They came among them in the street. They stood near unto the work and spake against it. They hired counsellors against them, to frustrate their purpose. This they did all the days of Cyrus, king of Persia. And this they did even unto the reign of Darius, king of Persia.

In the days of Ahasuerus, in the beginning of his reign, they wrote an accusation against the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem. The accusation was sealed. It was put into the hand of a messenger. And it went up the long road from Samaria, through the country and through the cities of the king, even unto Shushan, where the king sat.

In the days of Artaxerxes, they wrote yet another letter against Jerusalem. The names of the men who wrote it were set at the head thereof. They called the city a rebellious city, and an evil city. They said that the walls thereof had been builded up in old time, and that the kings thereof had risen up against kings. And they said, If this city be builded, and the walls thereof set up again, the people will not pay toll, tribute, nor custom. So shall the king have no portion on this side of the river.

The letter was sent. It went up the same long road unto Shushan. It was put into the hand of the king's servant. And it was read before the king.

The king made answer. He commanded that search should be made. And search was made in the house of the rolls, where the records were kept. The records were found. And the king sent his answer back down the road. He commanded that the work should cease. The city, said he, be not builded, until another commandment go forth from me.

The answer came unto Jerusalem. The letter was read before the people. The men went up unto them in haste, and made them to cease by force and by power.

And the work ceased.

The men went home from it. The stones they had been setting that day were left as they had set them. The lines were left where they had been drawn. The tools were taken up out of the dust. The trumpets were put again into their cases. The cymbals were laid away. And the walls of the house were as they had been.

So the work of the house of God which was at Jerusalem ceased. It ceased unto the second year of the reign of Darius, king of Persia.

And these were many years.

The stones lay where they had been laid. The wood lay where it had been brought in. The cedars darkened in the weather. The grass came up among the stones. The wind brought down the dust upon the wood, and the rain washed it, and the sun dried it again. The lizards ran in and out among the stones. The birds nested where the workmen had stood.

And the people went on with their own houses. They cieled their own roofs. They set their own doors in their own doorways. They planted their vineyards. They went up and down the streets of Jerusalem about their own business. The house of the LORD was the only house in the city that was not finished. The foundation was there. The walls were not raised upon it.

The trumpets abode in their cases. The cymbals abode where they had been laid. The Levites went about other work. The priests went about theirs.

The old men who had remembered the first house waxed older. Some sat in the gate. Some sat in their doorways and looked out toward the place where the house was to stand. Some died, and were buried with their fathers, and saw no more building.

And the house was left waiting.
