Daniel was on his knees in the chamber where he had been all afternoon. The sky behind him had begun to deepen at the high window. He had been praying for the holy mountain of his God and for the long sin of his people, the words moving steadily through him, the sound of them going out into the empty room with no one to hear.

He was still speaking when something stood beside him.

It was the same one he had seen at the beginning, the one called Gabriel, and the air around him moved as it had moved that other time, as though he had come a great distance very quickly. He had come down through the dim light of the chamber at the hour of the evening offering, and the wind of his coming had stilled when he stopped.

Gabriel touched him. The hand on his shoulder was not heavy. The voice that came was quiet and near.

"Daniel," he said. "I have come to give you wisdom and understanding. At the beginning of your petitions the word went out, and I have come to tell you, for you are greatly beloved."

Daniel did not lift his face. His mouth was still shaped around the prayer he had not yet finished. The words he had been saying about his people had stopped, and the words Gabriel had brought were settling into the place where his own had been.

"Therefore consider the matter," Gabriel said, "and understand the vision."

The light had thinned at the window while he spoke. The evening offering would be rising in Jerusalem now, far from where Daniel knelt, the smoke going up in the country he could not see. The chamber where he was kneeling had grown very still. The voice was gone, but the hand had been on his shoulder, and the words about being beloved were on him too, the way light remains on the skin when one steps in out of the sun.

He stayed where he was a long time, and the night came on around him.
