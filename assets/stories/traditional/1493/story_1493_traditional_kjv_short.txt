Daniel was upon his knees in the chamber where he had been all the afternoon. The sky behind him had begun to darken at the high window. He had been praying for the holy mountain of his God and for the long iniquity of his people, the words moving steadily through him, the sound of them going forth into the empty room with no ear to receive them.

He was yet speaking when one stood beside him.

It was the same that he had seen at the beginning, even the man Gabriel, and the air round about him moved as it had moved that other time, as one who had come a great way swiftly. He was come down through the dim light of the chamber at the time of the evening oblation, and the wind of his coming had stilled when he stayed.

Gabriel touched him. The hand upon his shoulder was not heavy. The voice that came was quiet and near.

"O Daniel," he said. "I am come forth to give thee skill and understanding. At the beginning of thy supplications the commandment came forth, and I am come to shew thee; for thou art greatly beloved."

Daniel lifted not his face. His mouth was yet shaped about the prayer he had not finished. The words he had been saying for his people were stayed, and the words Gabriel had brought were settling into the place where his own had been.

"Therefore understand the matter," said Gabriel, "and consider the vision."

The light had thinned at the window while he spake. The evening oblation would be rising in Jerusalem now, far from where Daniel was bowed, the smoke going up in the land he could not see. The chamber wherein he kneeled was grown very still. The voice was gone, but the hand had been upon his shoulder, and the words concerning being beloved were upon him also, after the manner of the light which remaineth on the skin when one cometh in out of the sun.

He abode where he was a great while, and the night came on round about him.
