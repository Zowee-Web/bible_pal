The woman had been the wife of one of the sons of the prophets — the company of young prophets that gathered around Elisha at Gilgal and at Bethel and at Jericho. Her husband had died and left her with two sons and with a debt that she could not pay; and the creditor had come to her, saying that he would take her two boys to be his slaves until the debt was worked off. She had run out of the house and gone to find Elisha at the door of his lodging.

Now a certain woman of the wives of the sons of the prophets cried out to Elisha, saying, "Your servant my husband is dead. You know that your servant feared Yahweh. Now the creditor has come to take for himself my two children to be slaves."

Elisha said to her, "What should I do for you? Tell me: what do you have in the house?"

She said, "Your servant has nothing in the house, except a pot of oil."

Then he said, "Go, borrow empty containers from of all your neighbors. Don't borrow just a few containers.

Go in and shut the door on you and on your sons, and pour oil into all those containers; and set aside those which are full."

So she went from him, and shut the door on herself and on her sons. They brought the containers to her, and she poured oil.

When the containers were full, she said to her son, "Bring me another container."

He said to her, "There isn't another container." Then the oil stopped flowing.

Then she came and told the man of God. He said, "Go, sell the oil, and pay your debt; and you and your sons live on the rest."

And the boys carried the last full container down the steps of the house and set it with the others against the wall of the inner room. The neighbors who had lent the jars stood out in the courtyard waiting for the return of their pots; the widow stepped out to count what she had been given; and the oil from the one small pot stayed in all the containers of the neighborhood, ready for the market in the morning, in the small courtyard of the widow of the prophet's son.
