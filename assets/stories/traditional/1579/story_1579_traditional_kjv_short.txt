Joseph was brought down to Egypt, where Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh and captain of the guard, bought him of the Ishmaelites. And the Lord was with Joseph. He prospered in his master's house, and Potiphar saw that the Lord was with him, and made all that he did to prosper in his hand. So Joseph found grace in his sight, and was set over the house; all that Potiphar had, he put into Joseph's hand. And for Joseph's sake the Lord blessed the Egyptian's house, in the field and in the house alike, so that his master left all to Joseph, and knew not aught he had, save the bread he did eat. Now Joseph was a goodly person, and well favoured.

After a time his master's wife cast her eyes upon Joseph, and said, Lie with me. But he refused. My master hath kept back nothing from me but thee, because thou art his wife, he said. There is none greater in this house than I. How then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God? Day by day she spake to him, and he hearkened not, to lie by her, or to be with her.

One day he went in to do his business, and none of the men of the house were within. She caught him by his garment — Lie with me — but he left the garment in her hand, and fled, and got him out. Then she called her household, and accused him: the Hebrew had been brought in to mock them; he had come in unto her, and had fled and left his garment when she cried out. She laid up the garment by her until his master came home, and told him the same. His master's wrath was kindled, and he put Joseph into the prison where the king's prisoners were bound.

But the Lord was with Joseph in the prison also, and gave him favour with the keeper, who committed all the prisoners into his hand. For the Lord was with him, and made whatsoever he did to prosper.
