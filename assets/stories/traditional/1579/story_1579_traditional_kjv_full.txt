When Joseph was brought down to Egypt and sold to Potiphar — an officer of Pharaoh, and captain of the guard — the Lord was with him. In that foreign house he prospered at all he set his hand to, and his master saw the reason: the Lord was with this bondman, and made whatsoever he did to prosper. So Potiphar raised him up from among the servants to minister unto him, and set him over the whole house, and put all that he owned into Joseph's hand. From that day the blessing of the Lord was upon the Egyptian's house for Joseph's sake, upon all he had in the house and in the field, until his master left everything to Joseph, and knew not aught he had, save the bread he did eat. Now Joseph was a goodly person, and well favoured.

That was where the trouble found him. His master's wife cast her eyes upon him, and said, Lie with me. He refused her. His master, he said, knew not aught that was with him in the house, having put it all into Joseph's hand; he had kept nothing back from him — nothing at all but her, because she was his wife; none under that roof was greater than Joseph. How then could he do so great a wickedness, and sin against God? But she did not ask once only. Day after day the words came, and day after day he turned them aside, and would not lie by her, nor so much as be with her.

Then came a day when he went in to do his business, and none of the men of the house were within. This time she caught him by his garment — Lie with me — and he left it in her hand and got him out of doors. But the cloth she was left holding became her charge against him. She called the men of her house in, and told them their master had brought a Hebrew among them to mock them: he had come in unto her to lie with her, and when she lifted up her voice and cried, he had left his garment by her and fled. She laid it up beside her until her husband came home, and gave him the same account — that the Hebrew servant he had brought in had come in to mock her, and had fled and left his garment when she cried out.

Potiphar's wrath was kindled at his wife's words, and he took Joseph and put him into the prison, the place where the king's prisoners were bound. But even there the Lord was with Joseph, and shewed him mercy. He gave him favour in the sight of the keeper of the prison, who committed every prisoner into Joseph's hand; whatsoever was done there, Joseph was the doer of it, and the keeper looked to none of it himself — because the Lord was with Joseph, and made whatsoever he did to prosper.
