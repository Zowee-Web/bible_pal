When Joseph was brought down to Egypt and sold to Potiphar — an officer of Pharaoh, and captain of the guard — Yahweh was with him. In that foreign house he prospered at everything he set his hand to, and his master could see the reason: Yahweh was with this slave and made all that he did come out well. So Potiphar drew him up out of the ranks of the servants to wait on him himself, and set him over the whole household, and put everything he owned into Joseph's hands. From that day the blessing of Yahweh lay on the Egyptian's house for Joseph's sake, on all he had in the house and in the field, until his master left it all to Joseph and troubled himself over nothing but the food he ate. Joseph was well-built and handsome.

That was where the trouble found him. His master's wife set her eyes on him and said, "Lie with me." He refused her. His master, he said, kept no account of anything in the house, having trusted it all to Joseph's hand; he had held nothing back from him — nothing at all but her, because she was his wife; no one under that roof stood higher than Joseph. How then could he do so great a wickedness, and sin against God? But she did not ask only once. Day after day the words came, and day after day he turned them aside, and would not lie beside her or so much as be with her.

Then came a day when he went into the house to do his work, and none of the other men were inside. This time she caught him by his garment — "Lie with me!" — and he left the garment in her hand and fled out of doors. But the cloth she was left holding became her accusation. She called in the men of the household and told them their master had brought a Hebrew among them to mock them: he had come in to her to lie with her, and when she lifted her voice and cried out, he had left his garment beside her and run. She kept it laid up beside her until her husband came home, and gave him the same account — that the Hebrew servant he had brought in had come in to mock her, and had fled and left his garment when she cried out.

Potiphar's anger caught fire at his wife's words, and he took Joseph and put him in the prison, the place where the king's prisoners were bound. But even there Yahweh was with Joseph, and showed him kindness. He gave him favor in the eyes of the keeper of the prison, who put every prisoner into Joseph's hand; whatever was done in that place, Joseph was the one who saw it done and answered for it, and the keeper looked to none of it himself — because Yahweh was with Joseph, and made whatever he did succeed.
