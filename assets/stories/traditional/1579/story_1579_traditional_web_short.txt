Joseph was brought down to Egypt, where Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh and captain of the guard, bought him from the Ishmaelites. And Yahweh was with Joseph. He prospered in his master's house, and Potiphar saw that Yahweh was with him, and made all that he did succeed in his hand. So Joseph found favor in his sight, and was set over the house; all that Potiphar had, he put into Joseph's hand. And for Joseph's sake Yahweh blessed the Egyptian's house, in the field and in the house alike, so that his master left everything to Joseph and gave no thought to anything but the food he ate. Now Joseph was well-built and handsome.

After a time his master's wife set her eyes on Joseph, and said, "Lie with me." But he refused. "My master has kept back nothing from me but you, because you are his wife," he said. "No one in this house is greater than I am. How then could I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?" Day by day she spoke to him, and he would not listen, or lie beside her, or be with her.

One day he went in to do his work, and no one else was in the house. She caught him by his garment — "Lie with me!" — but he left the garment in her hand and ran outside. Then she called her household and accused him: the Hebrew had been brought in to mock them; he had come in to her, and had fled and left his garment when she cried out. She kept the garment beside her until his master came home, and told him the same. His master's wrath was kindled, and he put Joseph in the prison where the king's prisoners were bound.

But Yahweh was with Joseph in the prison too, and gave him favor with the keeper, who put all the prisoners into his hand. For Yahweh was with him, and made whatever he did prosper.
