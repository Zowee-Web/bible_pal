When the days of Jacob drew near to die, he called for his sons.

The room was small. A single lamp burned on a low stand, its flame steady, the oil pooling clear at the base of the wick. The walls were close. The bed was a low pallet near the floor, layered with woven cloths and a heavier covering pulled up to Jacob's chest. The old man sat propped against a folded blanket, his white hair loose against his shoulders, his hands resting flat upon his thighs.

The sons came in. Twelve men, grown and weathered, the eldest already gray at the temples, the youngest still carrying the leanness of a younger frame. They gathered around the bed, some standing, some kneeling close. The lamplight fell on their faces. They were silent.

Jacob lifted his eyes.

"Gather yourselves together," he said, "that I may tell you that which will happen to you in the days to come. Assemble yourselves, and hear, you sons of Jacob. Listen to Israel your father."

Then he began to speak. He named them one by one, in the order of their birth, and the room held still around each name.

Reuben.

The eldest. Jacob's voice steadied on the word, gentle and grieved at once. "Reuben, you are my firstborn, my might, and the beginning of my strength, excelling in dignity, and excelling in power." Reuben's face was lifted, hopeful. Then the blessing turned. "Boiling over like water, you shall not excel." Reuben lowered his eyes. The lamp burned on.

Simeon and Levi. Jacob's hands tightened on his thighs as he named them together. He spoke of swords and of anger fierce and cruel. "I will divide them in Jacob, and scatter them in Israel." The two brothers stood close to each other and said nothing.

Then Jacob said the name of Judah.

Judah was the fourth son. The blessing landed on him like a hand on the head. Jacob's voice rose, steadier now, with the cadence of prophecy. "Judah, your brothers will praise you. Your hand will be on the neck of your enemies. Your father's sons will bow down before you." Judah stood at the foot of the bed. His shoulders straightened.

"Judah is a lion's cub. From the prey, my son, you have gone up. He stooped down, he crouched as a lion, as a lioness. Who will rouse him up?" The image filled the small room. The lion at rest, the prey already taken, the gathered strength holding still. "The scepter will not depart from Judah, nor the ruler's staff from between his feet, until he comes to whom it belongs. The obedience of the peoples will be to him." Jacob's hand lifted slightly from his thigh. "His eyes will be red with wine, his teeth white with milk."

The lamp guttered once and steadied. Judah did not move.

Jacob spoke on. Zebulun by the sea. Issachar lying down between the saddlebags. Each name a brief shape, each blessing carried on the same patient rhythm.

Then Dan.

"Dan will be a serpent on the trail, an adder in the path, that bites the horse's heels, so that his rider falls backward." Jacob paused. He drew a slow breath. Then his voice went quieter, almost to himself. "I have waited for your salvation, Yahweh."

Gad. Asher. Naphtali. The doe set free. Each name spoken, each son standing where he stood.

Then Joseph.

Jacob's eyes found him at the side of the bed. Joseph knelt close. Jacob's voice softened and warmed. "Joseph is a fruitful vine, a fruitful vine by a spring. His branches run over the wall." The picture lifted in the room — green leaves spilling over stone, fruit hanging heavy. "The archers have severely grieved him, shot at him, and persecuted him. But his bow remained strong. The arms of his hands were made strong, by the hands of the Mighty One of Jacob." Joseph's head lowered.

"The blessings of your father have prevailed above the blessings of my ancestors, above the boundaries of the ancient hills. They will be on the head of Joseph, on the crown of the head of him who is separated from his brothers."

Last came Benjamin. The youngest. Jacob's voice gathered for one final image. "Benjamin is a ravenous wolf. In the morning he will devour the prey. At evening he will divide the plunder."

The blessings ended. The lamp burned. The twelve sons stood around the bed in the small lamplit room. Jacob looked at each of them in turn.

Then he gave them one more word. He told them where he was to be buried — in the cave of Machpelah, in the field Abraham had bought from Ephron the Hittite. Where Abraham and Sarah lay. Where Isaac and Rebekah lay. Where he had buried Leah.

When he had finished, he gathered up his feet into the bed. He breathed his last. He was gathered to his people.

The lamp burned on in the still room. The twelve sons stood around their father, and the words he had spoken stayed with them — son by son, blessing by blessing — long after the breath had gone from his lips and the small flame burned alone.
