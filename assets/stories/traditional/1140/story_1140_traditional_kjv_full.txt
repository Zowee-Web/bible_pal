The palm tree stood between Ramah and Beth-el, in mount Ephraim. Its trunk leaned slightly to the north, marked by the years. Beneath it sat Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth, judging Israel. The children of Israel came up to her there. They came with their disputes and their grievances, and they came with the weight of twenty years.

For twenty years Jabin king of Canaan had mightily oppressed them. His captain Sisera kept nine hundred chariots of iron at Harosheth, and the fields of Israel had grown quiet under their shadow. The people cried unto the LORD.

Deborah sent and called Barak the son of Abinoam from Kedesh-naphtali. He came up through the hills and stood before her under the palm. The shade fell across his shoulders.

She spake. "Hath not the LORD God of Israel commanded, saying, Go and draw toward mount Tabor, and take with thee ten thousand men of the children of Naphtali and of the children of Zebulun? And I will draw unto thee, to the river Kishon, Sisera, the captain of Jabin's army, with his chariots and his multitude; and I will deliver him into thine hand?"

Barak answered not at once. He looked at the ground between them. Then he lifted his eyes. "If thou wilt go with me, then I will go. But if thou wilt not go with me, I will not go."

Deborah rose from her seat under the palm. "I will surely go with thee. Notwithstanding the journey that thou takest shall not be for thine honour. For the LORD shall sell Sisera into the hand of a woman."

So Deborah arose and went with Barak to Kedesh. Barak called Zebulun and Naphtali together. Ten thousand men gathered unto him. They went up to mount Tabor, and Deborah went up with them.

Word was shewed unto Sisera. He gathered all his chariots, even nine hundred chariots of iron, and all his host, from Harosheth unto the river Kishon. The plain below Tabor filled with horses and wheels and the sound of bronze.

On the mountain, Deborah turned unto Barak. "Up; for this is the day in which the LORD hath delivered Sisera into thine hand. Is not the LORD gone out before thee?"

Barak went down from mount Tabor, and ten thousand men after him. The LORD discomfited Sisera and all his chariots and all his host with the edge of the sword before Barak. The chariots that had ruled the plain for twenty years would not run. Sisera lighted down off his chariot and fled away on his feet. Barak pursued after the host unto Harosheth, and all the host of Sisera fell upon the edge of the sword. There was not a man left.

But Sisera fled away northward, alone, on foot. He came at last to the tent of Jael the wife of Heber the Kenite, for there was peace between Jabin and the house of Heber.

Jael went out to meet him. Her voice was calm. "Turn in, my lord, turn in to me. Fear not."

He turned in unto her into the tent. She covered him with a mantle.

He spake from beneath the cloth. "Give me, I pray thee, a little water to drink, for I am thirsty."

She opened a bottle of milk, and gave him drink. She covered him again. He gave her one last charge at the door of the tent. Then a heavy sleep came upon him. The man who had commanded nine hundred chariots that morning fell into the sleep of the weary.

Jael stood at the door of the tent. She hearkened to his breathing. She looked out across the slope toward the road from which Barak should come.

Then she went into the tent. She took a nail of the tent, and took an hammer in her hand. She went softly unto him. She smote once. The nail pierced through into the ground. He awaked not. So he fainted and died.

She went out from the tent.

Soon Barak came up the slope in pursuit. Jael came out to meet him. "Come, and I will shew thee the man whom thou seekest."

He came in unto her. Sisera lay dead, and the nail was in his temples.

So God subdued Jabin the king of Canaan before the children of Israel on that day. The hand of the children of Israel prevailed more and more against Jabin, until they had destroyed him. And in mount Ephraim, Deborah went back unto the palm tree between Ramah and Beth-el, and the children of Israel came up to her again, and the land began to grow still.
