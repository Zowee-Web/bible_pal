The palm tree stood between Ramah and Bethel, in the hill country of Ephraim. Its trunk leaned slightly to the north, marked by the years. Beneath it sat Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth, judging Israel. The children of Israel came up to her there. They came with their disputes and their grievances, and they came with the weight of twenty years.

For twenty years Jabin king of Canaan had oppressed them. His captain Sisera kept nine hundred chariots of iron at Harosheth, and the fields of Israel had grown quiet under their shadow. The people cried out to the Lord.

Deborah sent and called Barak the son of Abinoam from Kedesh-naphtali. He came up through the hills and stood before her under the palm. The shade fell across his shoulders.

She spoke. "Has not the Lord, the God of Israel, commanded, 'Go and lead the way to Mount Tabor, and take with you ten thousand men of Naphtali and of Zebulun? I will draw to you, to the river Kishon, Sisera, the captain of Jabin's army, with his chariots and his multitude; and I will deliver him into your hand.'"

Barak did not answer at once. He looked at the ground between them. Then he lifted his eyes. "If you will go with me, then I will go. But if you will not go with me, I will not go."

Deborah rose from her seat under the palm. "I will surely go with you. Nevertheless, the journey that you take will not be for your honor. For the Lord will sell Sisera into a woman's hand."

So Deborah arose and went with Barak to Kedesh. Barak called Zebulun and Naphtali together. Ten thousand men gathered to him. They climbed Mount Tabor, and Deborah went up with them.

Word reached Sisera. He gathered all his chariots, nine hundred chariots of iron, and all his army from Harosheth to the river Kishon. The plain below Tabor filled with horses and wheels and the sound of bronze.

On the mountain, Deborah turned to Barak. "Go. For this is the day in which the Lord has delivered Sisera into your hand. Has not the Lord gone out before you?"

Barak went down from Mount Tabor, and ten thousand men after him. The Lord confused Sisera and all his chariots and all his army with the edge of the sword before Barak. The chariots that had ruled the plain for twenty years would not run. Sisera abandoned his chariot and fled away on his feet. Barak pursued the army to Harosheth, and all the army of Sisera fell. There was not a man left.

But Sisera fled north, alone, on foot. He came at last to the tent of Jael the wife of Heber the Kenite, for there was peace between Jabin and the house of Heber.

Jael went out to meet him. Her voice was calm. "Turn in, my lord, turn in to me. Do not be afraid."

He came in. She covered him with a rug.

He spoke from beneath the cloth. "Please give me a little water to drink, for I am thirsty."

She opened a container of milk and gave him a drink. She covered him again. He gave her one last instruction at the door of the tent. Then weariness took him. The man who had commanded nine hundred chariots that morning fell into a deep sleep.

Jael stood at the tent door. She listened to his breathing. She looked out across the slope toward the road from which Barak would come.

Then she went into the tent. She took a tent peg, and she took a hammer in her hand. She went softly to him. She struck once. The pin pierced through into the ground. He did not wake. So he fainted and died.

She went out from the tent.

Soon Barak came up the slope in pursuit. Jael came out to meet him. "Come, and I will show you the man whom you seek."

He came to her. Sisera lay dead, the tent peg in his temples.

So God subdued Jabin the king of Canaan before the children of Israel on that day. The hand of the children of Israel prevailed more and more against Jabin, until they had destroyed him. And in the hill country of Ephraim, Deborah went back to the palm tree between Ramah and Bethel, and the children of Israel came up to her again, and the land began to grow still.
