In the days when Israel had again done evil in the sight of the Lord, the Lord sold them into the hand of Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor. The captain of his army was Sisera. He lived in Harosheth of the Gentiles, and he had nine hundred chariots of iron. For twenty years he mightily oppressed the children of Israel. The grain ripened, and the chariots came through it. The roads were not safe. The wells were not safe. The children of Israel cried out to the Lord.

In the hill country of Ephraim, between Ramah and Bethel, there stood a palm tree. It was tall, and its leaves clattered in the dry wind. Beneath its shade sat a woman named Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth. She judged Israel at that time. The children of Israel came up to her there for judgment. They came one by one and in small groups, up the long path from the valleys. They sat in the dust before her, and she listened. She spoke. They went away again with the matter settled.

She sent and called Barak the son of Abinoam out of Kedesh-naphtali. The runner went north, and the runner returned with him.

Barak came up through the hills and stood before her under the palm. The shade fell across his shoulders. Deborah did not rise. She looked at him.

"Has not the Lord, the God of Israel, commanded, 'Go and lead the way to Mount Tabor, and take with you ten thousand men of the children of Naphtali and of the children of Zebulun? I will draw to you, to the river Kishon, Sisera, the captain of Jabin's army, with his chariots and his multitude; and I will deliver him into your hand.'"

Barak did not answer at once. He looked at the ground between them. He had heard of the chariots. Every man in Israel had heard of the chariots. Then he lifted his eyes to her.

"If you will go with me, then I will go. But if you will not go with me, I will not go."

Deborah was silent for a long moment. Then she rose from her seat under the palm.

"I will surely go with you. Nevertheless, the journey that you take will not be for your honor. For the Lord will sell Sisera into a woman's hand."

So Deborah arose, and she went with Barak to Kedesh.

Barak called Zebulun and Naphtali together at Kedesh. They came in from the hill villages with their cloaks and their staffs and what blades they had. Ten thousand men followed him. They climbed Mount Tabor, and Deborah went up with them.

Word reached Sisera in the lowlands. They told him that Barak the son of Abinoam had gone up to Mount Tabor. Sisera gathered together all his chariots, even nine hundred chariots of iron, and all the people who were with him, from Harosheth of the Gentiles to the river Kishon. The plain below the mountain filled with horses and with wheels and with the sound of bronze on bronze. The dust of them rose in a long brown wall against the sky.

On the mountain, Deborah turned to Barak. Her face was lit by the morning. She did not lift her voice, but it carried.

"Go. For this is the day in which the Lord has delivered Sisera into your hand. Has not the Lord gone out before you?"

So Barak went down from Mount Tabor, and ten thousand men after him. They came down off the slope at a run.

And the Lord confused Sisera, all his chariots, and all his army, with the edge of the sword before Barak. The chariots that had ruled the plain for twenty years would not run. The wheels caught. The horses pulled against each other. The drivers shouted and could not be heard. Sisera abandoned his chariot. He stepped down into the dirt where he had not stood in years. He ran.

Barak pursued the chariots and the army to Harosheth of the Gentiles. All the army of Sisera fell by the edge of the sword. There was not a man left.

But Sisera fled away on his feet, alone, northward through the brush. The captain of nine hundred chariots ran on bruised feet. He did not look behind him. He came at last to the tent of Jael the wife of Heber the Kenite, for there was peace between Jabin the king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite.

Jael saw him coming from far off. She went out from the tent to meet him. Her voice was even.

"Turn in, my lord, turn in to me. Do not be afraid."

He turned aside. He came in to her into the tent. She covered him with a rug.

He spoke from beneath the cloth. His mouth was dry. "Please give me a little water to drink, for I am thirsty."

She did not bring water. She opened a container of milk and gave him a drink. He drank, and the milk steadied him. She covered him again with the rug.

He spoke once more, his words slower now. "Stand in the door of the tent, and if any man comes and inquires of you, and says, 'Is there any man here?' you shall say, 'No.'"

She did not answer. She stood at the door of the tent. She listened to his breathing change. The man who had commanded the chariots that morning fell into a deep sleep. The cloth rose and fell over him slowly.

Jael looked out across the slope toward the south. The sun was low on the hills. The dust of Barak's pursuit was still rising in a thin line along the horizon.

Then she went back into the tent. She took a tent peg. She took a hammer in her hand. She went softly to him. She did not hurry. She did not hesitate. She struck once. The pin pierced through into the ground. He did not wake. So he fainted and died.

She set the hammer down. She went out from the tent and stood in the door again.

Behold, as Barak pursued Sisera, he came up the slope toward the tents of the Kenite. Jael came out to meet him.

"Come, and I will show you the man whom you seek."

He came to her. He stepped into the tent behind her, and behold, Sisera lay dead, and the tent peg was in his temples.

Barak stood and looked. He did not speak. The word that Deborah had spoken under the palm came back to him then. The Lord had sold Sisera into a woman's hand. The honor of the day was not his.

So God subdued Jabin the king of Canaan before the children of Israel on that day. The hand of the children of Israel prevailed more and more against Jabin the king of Canaan, until they had destroyed him.

And in the hill country of Ephraim, between Ramah and Bethel, Deborah went back to the palm tree. She sat again in its shade. The children of Israel came up to her once more for judgment, as they had always come, and the dry leaves moved above her in the wind, and across the valleys the fields began to grow still.
