In the days when the children of Israel again did evil in the sight of the LORD, the LORD sold them into the hand of Jabin king of Canaan, that reigned in Hazor. The captain of his host was Sisera. He dwelt in Harosheth of the Gentiles, and he had nine hundred chariots of iron. Twenty years he mightily oppressed the children of Israel. The grain ripened, and the chariots came through it. The ways were not safe. The wells were not safe. The children of Israel cried unto the LORD.

In mount Ephraim, between Ramah and Beth-el, there stood a palm tree. It was tall, and its leaves clattered in the dry wind. Beneath its shade sat a woman named Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth. She judged Israel at that time. The children of Israel came up to her there for judgment. They came one by one and in small companies, up the long path from the valleys. They sat in the dust before her, and she hearkened. She spake. They went their way again with the matter settled.

She sent and called Barak the son of Abinoam out of Kedesh-naphtali. The runner went north, and the runner returned with him.

Barak came up through the hills and stood before her under the palm. The shade fell across his shoulders. Deborah rose not. She looked upon him.

"Hath not the LORD God of Israel commanded, saying, Go and draw toward mount Tabor, and take with thee ten thousand men of the children of Naphtali and of the children of Zebulun? And I will draw unto thee, to the river Kishon, Sisera, the captain of Jabin's army, with his chariots and his multitude; and I will deliver him into thine hand?"

Barak answered not at once. He looked at the ground between them. He had heard of the chariots. Every man in Israel had heard of the chariots. Then he lifted his eyes unto her.

"If thou wilt go with me, then I will go. But if thou wilt not go with me, I will not go."

Deborah held her peace a long moment. Then she rose from her seat under the palm.

"I will surely go with thee. Notwithstanding the journey that thou takest shall not be for thine honour. For the LORD shall sell Sisera into the hand of a woman."

So Deborah arose, and she went with Barak to Kedesh.

Barak called Zebulun and Naphtali together at Kedesh. They came in from the hill villages with their cloaks and their staves and what blades they had. Ten thousand men followed him. They went up to mount Tabor, and Deborah went up with them.

Word was shewed unto Sisera in the lowlands. They told him that Barak the son of Abinoam was gone up to mount Tabor. Sisera gathered together all his chariots, even nine hundred chariots of iron, and all the people that were with him, from Harosheth of the Gentiles unto the river Kishon. The plain below the mountain filled with horses and with wheels and with the sound of bronze upon bronze. The dust of them rose in a long brown wall against the sky.

On the mountain, Deborah turned unto Barak. Her face was lit by the morning. She lifted not her voice, but it carried.

"Up; for this is the day in which the LORD hath delivered Sisera into thine hand. Is not the LORD gone out before thee?"

So Barak went down from mount Tabor, and ten thousand men after him. They came down off the slope at a run.

And the LORD discomfited Sisera, and all his chariots, and all his host, with the edge of the sword before Barak. The chariots that had ruled the plain for twenty years would not run. The wheels caught. The horses pulled one against another. The drivers cried out and could not be heard. Sisera lighted down off his chariot. He stepped down into the dirt where he had not stood in years. He fled.

Barak pursued after the chariots and after the host unto Harosheth of the Gentiles. All the host of Sisera fell upon the edge of the sword. There was not a man left.

Howbeit Sisera fled away on his feet, alone, northward through the brush. The captain of nine hundred chariots ran on bruised feet. He looked not behind him. He came at last to the tent of Jael the wife of Heber the Kenite, for there was peace between Jabin the king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite.

Jael saw him coming from afar off. She went out from the tent to meet him. Her voice was even.

"Turn in, my lord, turn in to me. Fear not."

He turned aside. He came in unto her into the tent. She covered him with a mantle.

He spake from beneath the cloth. His mouth was dry. "Give me, I pray thee, a little water to drink, for I am thirsty."

She brought not water. She opened a bottle of milk, and gave him drink. He drank, and the milk steadied him. She covered him again with the mantle.

He spake once more, his words slower now. "Stand in the door of the tent, and it shall be, when any man doth come and inquire of thee, and say, Is there any man here? that thou shalt say, Nay."

She answered him not. She stood in the door of the tent. She hearkened to his breathing change. The man who had commanded the chariots that morning fell into a heavy sleep. The cloth rose and fell over him slowly.

Jael looked out across the slope toward the south. The sun was low upon the hills. The dust of Barak's pursuit was yet rising in a thin line along the horizon.

Then she went again into the tent. She took a nail of the tent. She took an hammer in her hand. She went softly unto him. She hasted not. She faltered not. She smote once. The nail pierced through into the ground. He awaked not. So he fainted and died.

She set the hammer down. She went out from the tent and stood in the door again.

Behold, as Barak pursued Sisera, he came up the slope toward the tents of the Kenite. Jael came out to meet him.

"Come, and I will shew thee the man whom thou seekest."

He came unto her. He stepped into the tent behind her, and behold, Sisera lay dead, and the nail was in his temples.

Barak stood and looked. He spake not. The word that Deborah had spoken under the palm came back unto him then. The LORD had sold Sisera into the hand of a woman. The honour of the day was not his.

So God subdued Jabin the king of Canaan before the children of Israel on that day. The hand of the children of Israel prevailed more and more against Jabin the king of Canaan, until they had destroyed him.

And in mount Ephraim, between Ramah and Beth-el, Deborah went back unto the palm tree. She sat again in its shade. The children of Israel came up to her once more for judgment, as they had ever come, and the dry leaves moved above her in the wind, and across the valleys the fields began to grow still.
