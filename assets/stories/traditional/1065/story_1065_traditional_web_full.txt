King Ahab sent word throughout all Israel and gathered the people and the prophets of Baal together at Mount Carmel. The mountain rose above the coastal plain, its ridgeline open to the wind. Hundreds climbed the slopes — the four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal, and behind them the people of Israel, and Ahab himself.

Elijah stood before them all. The wind pressed against his cloak. "How long will you go limping between two opinions?" he said. "If the Lord is God, follow him. But if Baal, then follow him."

The people did not answer him a word. The silence held across the mountainside.

Elijah spoke again. "I, even I only, am left a prophet of the Lord. But Baal's prophets are four hundred and fifty men." He let that number settle over the crowd. "Let two bulls be given to us. Let them choose one bull for themselves, cut it in pieces, and lay it on the wood, but put no fire to it. I will prepare the other bull and lay it on the wood and put no fire to it."

He turned so his voice carried across the ridge. "Then you call upon the name of your god, and I will call upon the name of the Lord. And the god who answers by fire — he is God."

All the people answered together. "It is well spoken."

Elijah said to the prophets of Baal, "Choose for yourselves one bull and prepare it first, for you are many. Call upon the name of your god, but put no fire to it."

They took the bull that was given them. They prepared it. They built their altar and arranged the wood and laid the pieces of meat upon it. From morning they began to call. "O Baal, answer us!" Their voices carried across the open summit. They limped and danced around the altar they had made.

No voice answered. No one responded.

The sun climbed. The hours stretched. The people watched from the slopes.

At noon, Elijah mocked them. "Cry aloud, for he is a god. Either he is musing, or he is relieving himself, or he is on a journey, or perhaps he is asleep and must be awakened."

They cried aloud. They cut themselves with swords and lances, as was their custom, until blood gushed over their skin and ran down their arms. Midday passed and they raved on, shouting and swaying, until the time of the evening offering. The afternoon shadows lengthened across the stones. Still no voice. No answer. No one paid attention.

Elijah said to all the people, "Come near to me."

They came near. The crowd pressed forward, feet scraping over rock and dry grass. Before them lay the ruins of an altar of the Lord that had been thrown down. Elijah knelt beside the scattered stones. He took twelve stones, one for each of the tribes of the sons of Jacob, to whom the word of the Lord had come saying, "Israel shall be your name." With the twelve stones he built an altar in the name of the Lord.

He dug a trench around the altar, wide and deep — large enough to hold two measures of seed. He arranged the wood on the stones. He cut the bull in pieces and laid them on the wood. Every movement was deliberate, unhurried. The people watched in silence.

"Fill four jars with water," Elijah said, "and pour it on the burnt offering and on the wood."

They filled the jars and poured. Water splashed over the meat and soaked into the wood and ran down the sides of the stones.

"Do it a second time," he said.

They poured again. The wood darkened. Water pooled at the base of the altar.

"Do it a third time."

They poured a third time. Water ran off the altar in streams and flowed into the trench until the trench was full. The stones glistened. The wood was soaked through. The pieces of the bull sat in water.

At the time of the evening offering, Elijah stepped forward. He stood alone before the drenched altar, the crowd behind him, the four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal bloodied and silent on the ridge.

He opened his mouth and prayed. "O Lord, God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, let it be known this day that you are God in Israel, and that I am your servant, and that I have done all these things at your word. Answer me, O Lord, answer me, that this people may know that you, O Lord, are God."

Fire fell from the Lord.

It struck the altar. It consumed the burnt offering. It consumed the wood. It consumed the stones. It consumed the dust. It licked up the water that stood in the trench. Heat rolled across the mountainside.

All the people saw it. Every one of them went down — knees to rock, faces to the ground. Their voices rose together across the summit of Carmel.

"The Lord, he is God! The Lord, he is God!"
