King Ahab sent word throughout all Israel, and gathered the people and the prophets of Baal together unto Mount Carmel. The mountain rose above the coastal plain, its ridgeline open unto the wind. Hundreds climbed the slopes — the four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal, and behind them the people of Israel, and Ahab himself.

Elijah stood before them all. The wind pressed against his mantle. "How long halt ye between two opinions?" he saith. "If the Lord be God, follow him. But if Baal, then follow him."

The people answered him not a word. The silence held across the mountainside.

Elijah spake again. "I, even I only, am left a prophet of the Lord. But Baal's prophets are four hundred and fifty men." He let that number settle over the multitude. "Let two bullocks be given unto us. Let them choose one bullock for themselves, and cut it in pieces, and lay it upon the wood, but put no fire under it. I will dress the other bullock and lay it upon the wood and put no fire under it."

He turned so that his voice carried across the ridge. "Then call ye upon the name of your god, and I will call upon the name of the Lord. And the god that answereth by fire — he is God."

All the people answered together. "It is well spoken."

Elijah saith unto the prophets of Baal, "Choose you one bullock for yourselves and dress it first, for ye are many. Call upon the name of your god, but put no fire under it."

They took the bullock which was given them. They dressed it. They built their altar and laid the wood in order and set the pieces of flesh upon it. From morning they began to call. "O Baal, hear us!" Their voices carried across the open summit. They leaped and limped about the altar which they had made.

No voice answered. Neither was there any that regarded.

The sun climbed. The hours stretched. The people watched from the slopes.

At noon, Elijah mocked them. "Cry aloud, for he is a god. Either he is talking, or he is pursuing, or he is in a journey, or peradventure he sleepeth and must be awaked."

They cried aloud. They cut themselves after their manner with swords and lancets, till the blood gushed out upon them and ran down their arms. Midday passed and they prophesied, crying out and swaying, until the time of the offering of the evening sacrifice. The afternoon shadows lengthened across the stones. Still there was neither voice, nor any to answer, nor any that regarded.

Elijah saith unto all the people, "Come near unto me."

They came near. The multitude pressed forward, feet scraping over rock and dry grass. Before them lay the ruins of an altar of the Lord that was broken down. Elijah knelt beside the scattered stones. He took twelve stones, according to the number of the tribes of the sons of Jacob, unto whom the word of the Lord came, saying, "Israel shall be thy name." With the twelve stones he built an altar in the name of the Lord.

He made a trench about the altar, great and deep — as great as would contain two measures of seed. He put the wood in order upon the stones. He cut the bullock in pieces and laid the pieces upon the wood. Every movement was deliberate, unhurried. The people watched in silence.

"Fill four barrels with water," Elijah saith, "and pour it upon the burnt sacrifice and upon the wood."

They filled the barrels and poured. Water splashed over the flesh and soaked into the wood and ran down the sides of the stones.

"Do it the second time," he saith.

They poured again. The wood darkened. Water pooled at the base of the altar.

"Do it the third time."

They poured the third time. Water ran off the altar in streams and flowed into the trench until the trench was full. The stones glistened. The wood was soaked through. The pieces of the bullock sat in water.

At the time of the offering of the evening sacrifice, Elijah drew near. He stood alone before the drenched altar, the multitude behind him, the four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal bloodied and silent upon the ridge.

He opened his mouth and prayed. "Lord God of Abraham, Isaac, and of Israel, let it be known this day that thou art God in Israel, and that I am thy servant, and that I have done all these things at thy word. Hear me, O Lord, hear me, that this people may know that thou art the Lord God."

Then the fire of the Lord fell.

It struck the altar. It consumed the burnt sacrifice. It consumed the wood. It consumed the stones. It consumed the dust. It licked up the water that was in the trench. The heat rolled across the mountainside.

And when all the people saw it, every one of them fell — knees unto the rock, faces unto the ground. Their voices rose together across the summit of Carmel.

"The Lord, he is the God! The Lord, he is the God!"
