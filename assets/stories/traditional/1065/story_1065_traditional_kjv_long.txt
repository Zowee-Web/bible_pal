And King Ahab sent messengers throughout all Israel, and gathered the people together at Mount Carmel. He gathered the four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal as well, summoning them from their high places and their groves. They came up the mountain in a long line — robed figures moving through the scrub and dry grass along the ridge. Behind them came the people of Israel, hundreds of them, climbing the rocky slopes as the morning light spread across the coastal plain far below.

Mount Carmel rose above the land as a broad shoulder of limestone. The wind moved across the summit thereof without obstruction. The sky was wide and pale. The people spread themselves out along the ridge and among the rocks, finding places to stand and to watch.

Elijah stood before them all.

One man. Over against him, four hundred and fifty.

He looked out over the multitude. "How long halt ye between two opinions?" he saith. His voice was hard and clear in the open air. "If the Lord be God, follow him. But if Baal, then follow him."

And the people answered him not a word. The wind moved through the silence.

Elijah spake again. "I, even I only, remain a prophet of the Lord." He gestured toward the great company of prophets over against him. "But Baal's prophets are four hundred and fifty men."

He let the number hang in the air. Then he laid out the terms thereof.

"Let two bullocks be given us. And let them choose one bullock for themselves, and cut it in pieces, and lay it on the wood, and put no fire under it. And I will dress the other bullock, and lay it on the wood, and put no fire under it. Then call ye upon the name of your god, and I will call upon the name of the Lord. And the god that answereth by fire — he is God."

And all the people answered and said together, "It is well spoken."

Two bullocks were brought forward, led up from the lower slopes upon ropes. Their hooves scraped upon the rocks. And Elijah turned unto the prophets of Baal. "Choose you one bullock for yourselves, and dress it first, for ye are many. And call upon the name of your god, but put no fire under it."

And the prophets of Baal chose their bullock. They slew it there upon the ridge. They built an altar of stones and laid the wood in order upon it and laid the pieces of the bullock upon the wood. The blood ran down the stones and into the dust. And they set themselves round about their altar — a great ring of men, shoulder to shoulder.

From morning they began to call.

"O Baal, hear us!"

Their voices rose together across the summit. Some lifted up their hands toward the heavens. Some pressed their faces toward the altar. They called again. "O Baal, hear us!" They moved round about the altar, limping and swaying in a measured circle, their robes dragging over the rocky ground.

The morning passed.

There was neither voice, nor any that answered. The flesh sat upon the wood. The wood sat upon the stones. Nothing burned.

The sun climbed unto his highest point. The heat pressed down upon the mountain. The prophets of Baal kept calling, their voices growing hoarse. And the people of Israel stood upon the slopes and watched.

At noon, Elijah mocked them.

"Cry aloud," he saith, "for he is a god." His voice carried across the ridge, sharp with scorn. "Either he is musing, or he hath gone aside, or he is on a journey, or peradventure he sleepeth and must be awaked."

And the prophets of Baal cried with a louder voice. They drew swords and lancets. They cut themselves — arms, breasts, faces — gashing their own flesh after their manner, until the blood gushed out upon them. It ran down their forearms and dripped from their elbows. It streaked their garments. They raved and cried aloud and staggered round about their altar, their voices cracking, their limbs smeared red.

Midday passed. The afternoon wore on. The shadows of the rocks began to lengthen across the dry grass. The prophets of Baal continued — crying out, bleeding, staggering — until the time of the offering of the evening sacrifice. Their voices had gone thin. Some of them kneeled in the dust, scarce able to speak.

No voice. No answer. Neither was there any that regarded.

The altar of Baal sat cold and dark in the late afternoon light. The flies had begun to gather upon the raw flesh.

And Elijah turned unto the people.

"Come near unto me," he saith.

And the multitude drew near. Feet scraped over rock and gravel. The people pressed closer, gathering themselves round about Elijah upon the open ridge. Before them, upon the ground, lay the ruins of an altar of the Lord that had been thrown down. The stones were scattered and half buried in the earth. The grass grew between them.

Elijah kneeled beside the wreckage thereof. He began pulling the stones from the ground, brushing off the earth, setting them upright. He took twelve stones — one for each of the tribes of the sons of Jacob, unto whom the word of the Lord came, saying, "Israel shall be thy name." One by one he set them in their place, fitting them together with his hands, building the altar up again from its ruin.

With the twelve stones he built an altar in the name of the Lord.

Then he made a trench round about the altar. He wrought with his hands and peradventure a blade, scooping out the rocky soil, widening and deepening the channel until it was as great as would contain two measures of seed. The earth was heaped up on either side. The people watched his every movement.

He put the wood in order upon the altar that was rebuilt. He cut the bullock in pieces. He laid the pieces upon the wood. The flesh glistened. The wood was dry and pale in the evening light.

Then gave he a commandment which no man looked for.

"Fill four barrels with water," Elijah saith, "and pour it upon the burnt sacrifice, and upon the wood."

Men brought the barrels. They filled them and bare them unto the altar and poured them out upon the flesh and upon the wood. The water splashed down over the offering, soaked into the grain of the wood, and ran down the faces of the twelve stones.

"Do it the second time," Elijah saith.

They filled the barrels again and poured. The wood darkened utterly. Water gathered between the pieces of the flesh and dripped from the edges of the altar into the dust beneath.

"Do it the third time."

And they poured the third time. The water streamed off the altar now, running in rivulets down the stones, flowing into the trench. And the trench was filled. The face of the water reflected the sky. The whole altar stood soaked — the wood heavy with water, the flesh drenched, the stones slick and gleaming. Twelve barrels of water poured out upon a sacrifice that was meant to burn.

And the multitude stood in silence. The prophets of Baal stood bleeding and spent hard by, their altar yet cold.

At the time of the offering of the evening sacrifice, Elijah drew near.

He stood alone before the drenched altar. The trench of water lay at his feet. The multitude pressed behind him. The wind had fallen. The ridge was still.

He prayed once. One prayer.

"Lord God of Abraham, Isaac, and of Israel," he saith, "let it be known this day that thou art God in Israel, and that I am thy servant, and that I have done all these things at thy word. Hear me, O Lord, hear me, that this people may know that thou art the Lord God, and that thou hast turned their heart back again."

Then the fire of the Lord fell.

It came down from above and struck the altar. It consumed the burnt sacrifice. It consumed the wood beneath it. It consumed the twelve stones — the very rocks split and brake asunder in the heat. It consumed the dust round about the altar. It licked up all the water that was in the trench. The heat rolled outward as a wave and pressed against the faces of the multitude.

Where the altar had stood, the ground was scorched bare. Stone, wood, flesh, water — all of it was consumed.

And when all the people saw it, every man and every woman upon that ridge fell upon their faces. Knees smote against the rock. Faces were pressed into the dust. And their voices rose together across the summit of Mount Carmel, hundreds of them crying out the selfsame words at once.

"The Lord, he is the God! The Lord, he is the God!"
