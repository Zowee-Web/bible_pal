King Ahab sent word throughout Israel and gathered the people and the prophets of Baal to Mount Carmel.

Elijah stood before them all. "How long will you go limping between two opinions?" he said. "If the Lord is God, follow him. But if Baal, then follow him."

The people did not answer him a word.

Elijah spoke again. "I, even I only, am left a prophet of the Lord. But Baal's prophets are four hundred and fifty men. Let two bulls be given to us. Let them choose one bull for themselves, cut it in pieces, and lay it on wood, but put no fire to it. I will prepare the other bull and lay it on wood and put no fire to it. Then you call upon the name of your god, and I will call upon the name of the Lord. The god who answers by fire, he is God."

All the people answered, "It is well spoken."

The prophets of Baal chose their bull and prepared it. From morning until noon they called upon the name of Baal. "O Baal, answer us!" they cried. No voice answered. No one responded. They limped around the altar they had made.

At noon Elijah mocked them. "Cry aloud, for he is a god. Either he is musing, or he is relieving himself, or he is on a journey, or perhaps he is asleep and must be awakened."

They cried louder. They cut themselves with swords and lances until blood gushed over them. Midday passed. They raved on until the time of the evening offering. Still no voice. No answer. No one paid attention.

Elijah said to the people, "Come near to me." They came near. He repaired the altar of the Lord that had been thrown down. He took twelve stones, one for each tribe of the sons of Jacob. With the stones he built an altar in the name of the Lord. He dug a trench around the altar, large enough to hold two measures of seed. He arranged the wood. He cut the bull in pieces and laid it on the wood.

"Fill four jars with water and pour it on the burnt offering and on the wood," he said. They did so. "Do it a second time," he said. They did it a second time. "Do it a third time," he said. They did it a third time. Water ran around the altar and filled the trench.

At the time of the evening offering, Elijah stepped forward. "O Lord, God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel," he said, "let it be known this day that you are God in Israel, and that I am your servant, and that I have done all these things at your word. Answer me, O Lord, answer me."

Fire fell from the Lord. It consumed the burnt offering and the wood and the stones and the dust and licked up the water in the trench.

All the people saw it. They fell on their faces. "The Lord, he is God," they said. "The Lord, he is God."
