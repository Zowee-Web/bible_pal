Naaman was commander of the army of the king of Syria. He was a great man with his master and held in high favor, because by him the Lord had given victory to Syria. He was a mighty man of valor. But he was a leper.

The Syrians had gone out on raids and had carried away a little girl from the land of Israel. She served Naaman's wife. One day the girl said to her mistress, "If only my lord were with the prophet who is in Samaria. He would cure him of his leprosy."

Word reached Naaman. The king of Syria sent him with a letter and gifts — ten talents of silver, six thousand shekels of gold, and ten changes of clothing. Naaman came with his horses and chariots and stood at the door of Elisha's house. The horses stamped in the dust. The servants stood behind him.

Elisha did not come out. He sent a messenger. "Go and wash in the Jordan seven times, and your flesh shall be restored and you shall be clean."

Naaman was furious. He turned and went away in a rage. "I thought he would surely come out to me and stand and call upon the name of the Lord his God, and wave his hand over the place and cure the leprosy. Are not the rivers of Damascus better than all the waters of Israel? Could I not wash in them and be clean?"

His servants came near. "My father, if the prophet had commanded you to do some great thing, would you not have done it? How much rather then when he says, wash and be clean?"

Naaman went down to the Jordan. The banks were muddy, the reeds thick at the water's edge. He stepped into the river and dipped himself once. Twice. Three times. Four. Five. Six. Each time he came up unchanged.

Seven times. He came up and the water ran from his skin. Where the leprosy had been, the flesh was smooth. His flesh was restored like the flesh of a little child. He was clean.
