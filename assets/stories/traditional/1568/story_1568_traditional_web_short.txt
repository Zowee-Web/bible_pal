The people of Israel set out from Mount Hor, going by the way of the Red Sea to go around the land of Edom. It was a long road, and it wore them down, until they grew deeply discouraged on the journey.

And they spoke against God, and against Moses. "Why have you brought us up out of Egypt to die in the wilderness? There is no bread here. There is no water. And our souls loathe this worthless food."

So Yahweh sent venomous snakes among the people, and the snakes bit them, and many of Israel died.

Then the people came to Moses, and this time their words had changed. "We have sinned," they said, "for we have spoken against Yahweh, and against you. Pray to Yahweh to take the snakes away from us." So Moses prayed for the people.

And Yahweh answered him. "Make a venomous snake," he said, "and set it on a pole. And everyone who has been bitten, when he looks at it, will live."

He did not take the snakes away, as they had asked. Instead he gave them something to look to, so that the bitten might live. So Moses made a serpent out of bronze and set it high on a pole where all could see. And so it was: whenever a snake bit someone, that person had only to look up at the bronze serpent — and he lived.
