Isaac was forty years old when his father Abraham sent the oldest servant of the house back to the country of Nahor to find a wife for him. The servant had been gone many weeks. Isaac dwelt now in the south country, at Beer Lahai Roi — the well that Hagar had named when she had fled into the wilderness — and the days had passed without word. One evening Isaac walked out into the field at sunset to meditate. He looked up and saw the camels coming.

Isaac came from the way of Beer Lahai Roi, for he lived in the land of the South.

Isaac went out to meditate in the field at the evening. He lifted up his eyes and looked. Behold, there were camels coming.

Rebekah lifted up her eyes, and when she saw Isaac, she got off the camel.

She said to the servant, "Who is the man who is walking in the field to meet us?"

The servant said, "It is my master."

She took her veil, and covered herself.

The servant told Isaac all the things that he had done.

Isaac brought her into his mother Sarah's tent, and took Rebekah, and she became his wife. He loved her. Isaac was comforted after his mother's death.

And the camels were loosed and watered at the well of Beer Lahai Roi in the evening of that day. The servant gave to Isaac all the gifts that had been given by the household of Bethuel; the lamp was lit in the tent of his mother Sarah that had been kept folded these three years; and the son of Abraham was comforted after the death of his mother, with the veil of the woman of Aram drawn over the face of the wife who had come to him in the field at evening.
