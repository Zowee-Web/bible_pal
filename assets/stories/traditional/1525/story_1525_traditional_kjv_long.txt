And it came to pass in the third year that Jehoshaphat king of Judah went down unto the king of Israel. Together had they eaten at the table in Samaria. Three years were past, and no war between Syria and Israel. The wine stood between them. The servants moved along the walls. The lamps burned low.

Then said Ahab king of Israel unto his servants: Know ye that Ramoth in Gilead is ours, and we sit still, and take it not out of the hand of the king of Syria? And he turned unto Jehoshaphat, and entreated him to go with him to the battle.

And Jehoshaphat said unto the king of Israel: I am as thou art, my people as thy people, my horses as thy horses. But inquire, I pray thee, at the word of the LORD this day.

Then gathered Ahab the prophets together, four hundred men. They came into the open place by the gate of Samaria. The kings sat upon their thrones, robed, at the threshing floor at the entering in of the gate. The prophets stood before them. And Ahab asked of them: Shall I go up against Ramoth-Gilead to battle, or shall I forbear?

And they said: Go up. The LORD shall deliver it into the hand of the king.

The voices rose as one. The kings sat watching. Jehoshaphat looked upon the company of four hundred, and said: Is there not here a prophet of the LORD besides, that we might inquire of him?

Ahab paused. And he said: There is yet one man, Micaiah the son of Imlah, by whom we may inquire of the LORD. But I hate him. For he prophesieth not good concerning me, but evil.

And Jehoshaphat said: Let not the king say so.

Then sent the king for Micaiah. The messenger went down through the streets. The prophets remained at the gate. Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah had made him horns of iron. And he said: With these shalt thou push the Syrians, till thou hast consumed them. And all the prophets prophesied the like. They said: Go up to Ramoth-Gilead, and prosper. The LORD shall deliver it into the hand of the king.

The messenger came unto Micaiah, and said: Behold, the words of the prophets speak good unto the king with one mouth. Let thy word, I pray thee, be as the word of one of them.

And Micaiah said: As the LORD liveth, what the LORD saith unto me, that will I speak.

Together they returned through the streets. The two kings yet sat upon their thrones at the gate. The four hundred yet stood in their places. Micaiah came before the king. And the king said: Micaiah, shall we go against Ramoth-Gilead to battle, or shall we forbear?

And Micaiah said: Go, and prosper. For the LORD shall deliver it into the hand of the king.

His voice was not as theirs. The king leaned forward. And the king said: How many times shall I adjure thee that thou tell me nothing but truth in the name of the LORD?

Then said Micaiah: I beheld all Israel scattered upon the hills, as sheep that have no shepherd. And the LORD said: These have no master. Let them return every man to his house in peace.

Ahab turned unto Jehoshaphat, and said: Did I not tell thee that he would prophesy no good concerning me, but evil?

But Micaiah ceased not. He stood in the open place before the thrones, and said: Hear therefore the word of the LORD. I saw the LORD sitting upon his throne. And all the host of heaven stood by him, on his right hand and on his left.

The four hundred were silent now. Zedekiah held the iron horns at his side.

And Micaiah said: The LORD said, Who shall persuade Ahab, that he may go up and fall at Ramoth-Gilead?

One spake on this manner, and another on that manner. Then came forth a spirit, and stood before the LORD, and said: I will persuade him. And the LORD said: By what means? And he said: I will go forth, and be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets. And the LORD said: Thou shalt persuade him, and prevail also. Go forth, and do so.

And Micaiah said: Now therefore, behold, the LORD hath put a lying spirit in the mouth of all these thy prophets, and the LORD hath spoken evil concerning thee.

The gate was still. The horses shifted somewhere behind the wall. Then drew near Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah. He came across the open ground. And he smote Micaiah upon the cheek. The sound carried across the gate. And he said: Which way went the Spirit of the LORD from me to speak unto thee?

Micaiah lifted not his hand. The mark of the blow was upon his face. And he said: Behold, thou shalt see in that day, when thou shalt go into an inner chamber to hide thyself.

Zedekiah stood near. His hand was yet uplifted.

Then said the king of Israel: Take Micaiah, and carry him back unto Amon the governor of the city, and to Joash the king's son. Say: Put this fellow in the prison, and feed him with bread of affliction and with water of affliction, until I come in peace.

The guards stepped forth. They laid hold of Micaiah on either side.

And Micaiah said: If thou return at all in peace, the LORD hath not spoken by me.

He turned his head toward the gathering at the gate, and said: Hearken, O people, every one of you.

The guards led him forward. They took him out through the gate, down through the streets of Samaria. The sound of his sandals went with the sound of theirs. The crowd at the gate parted before them. And they passed out of sight.

The four hundred prophets stood where they had stood. The iron horns lay near unto the feet of Zedekiah. His hand was yet half-raised from the smiting of the cheek. He let it not down straightway. Then spake Ahab to his captains. The word went forth for the chariots and the horsemen. Ahab made ready to go up to Ramoth-Gilead. And Jehoshaphat king of Judah stood with him.

The messengers ran. The harnesses were brought. The horses were led out into the open ground beyond the gate. And the dust began to rise from the road that went eastward toward Gilead.

Micaiah went unto the prison alone, between the two guards. The door of the inner room was opened. They set the bread before him, and the water beside it. They shut the door. The bolt slid into his place.

Without, the chariots were moving. And the word he had spoken remained in the room behind him.
