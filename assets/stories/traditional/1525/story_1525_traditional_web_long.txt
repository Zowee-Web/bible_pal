It came to pass in the third year that Jehoshaphat king of Judah came down to the king of Israel. They had eaten together at the table in Samaria. Three years had passed without war between Syria and Israel. The wine sat between them. The servants moved along the walls. The lamps burned low.

Ahab king of Israel said to his servants: do you know that Ramoth in Gilead belongs to us, and we sit still, and do not take it out of the hand of the king of Syria? He turned to Jehoshaphat. He asked him to go with him to the battle.

Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel: I am as you are, my people as your people, my horses as your horses. But inquire, I pray you, at the word of the LORD today.

Ahab gathered the prophets together. About four hundred men. They came into the open space by the gate of Samaria. The kings sat on their thrones, robed, at the threshing floor at the entrance of the gate. The prophets stood before them. Ahab asked them: shall I go against Ramoth-Gilead to battle, or shall I hold back?

They said: go up. The LORD shall deliver it into the hand of the king.

The voices rose together. The kings sat watching. Jehoshaphat looked over the assembly of four hundred. He said: is there not here a prophet of the LORD besides, that we might inquire of him?

Ahab paused. He said: there is yet one man, Micaiah the son of Imlah, by whom we may inquire of the LORD. But I hate him. For he does not prophesy good concerning me, but evil.

Jehoshaphat said: let not the king say so.

The king sent for Micaiah. The messenger went down through the streets. The prophets remained at the gate. Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah had made horns of iron. He said: with these you shall push the Syrians, until you have consumed them. All the prophets prophesied the same. They said: go up to Ramoth-Gilead, and prosper. The LORD shall deliver it into the hand of the king.

The messenger reached Micaiah. He said: behold, the words of the prophets declare good to the king with one mouth. Let your word, I pray you, be like the word of one of them.

Micaiah said: as the LORD lives, what the LORD says to me, that will I speak.

They went back together through the streets. The two kings still sat on their thrones at the gate. The four hundred still stood in their places. Micaiah came before the king. The king said: Micaiah, shall we go against Ramoth-Gilead to battle, or shall we hold back?

Micaiah said: go, and prosper. For the LORD shall deliver it into the hand of the king.

His voice did not match the others. The king leaned forward. The king said: how many times shall I make you swear that you tell me nothing but the truth in the name of the LORD?

Micaiah said: I saw all Israel scattered upon the hills, as sheep that have no shepherd. The LORD said: these have no master. Let them return every man to his house in peace.

Ahab turned to Jehoshaphat. He said: did I not tell you that he would prophesy no good concerning me, but evil?

Micaiah did not stop. He stood in the open space before the thrones. He said: hear therefore the word of the LORD. I saw the LORD sitting on his throne. All the host of heaven was standing by him on his right hand and on his left.

The four hundred prophets were quiet now. Zedekiah held the iron horns at his side.

Micaiah said: the LORD said: who shall persuade Ahab, that he may go up and fall at Ramoth-Gilead?

One said on this manner, another said on that manner. There came forth a spirit and stood before the LORD. He said: I will persuade him. The LORD said: by what means? He said: I will go forth, and I will be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets. The LORD said: you shall persuade him and prevail also. Go forth and do so.

Micaiah said: now therefore, behold, the LORD has put a lying spirit in the mouth of all these your prophets, and the LORD has spoken evil concerning you.

The gate was silent. The horses shifted somewhere behind the wall. Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah went near. He came across the open ground. He smote Micaiah on the cheek. The sound carried across the gate. He said: which way went the Spirit of the LORD from me to speak to you?

Micaiah did not lift his hand. The mark of the blow showed on his face. He said: you shall see in that day when you shall go into an inner chamber to hide yourself.

Zedekiah stood close. His hand was still raised.

The king of Israel said: take Micaiah, and carry him back to Amon the governor of the city, and to Joash the king's son. Say: put this fellow in the prison, and feed him with bread of affliction and with water of affliction, until I come in peace.

The guards stepped forward. They took hold of Micaiah on either side.

Micaiah said: if you return at all in peace, the LORD has not spoken by me.

He turned his head toward the gathering at the gate. He said: hear, O people, every one of you.

The guards moved him forward. They took him away through the gate, down through the streets of Samaria. The sound of his sandals went with the sound of theirs. The crowd at the gate parted for them. They passed out of sight.

The four hundred prophets stood where they had been. The iron horns lay near Zedekiah's feet. Zedekiah's hand was still half-raised from the blow to the cheek. He did not lower it at once. Ahab spoke to his captains. The order went out for the chariots and the horsemen. Ahab made ready to go up to Ramoth-Gilead. Jehoshaphat king of Judah stood with him.

The messengers ran. The harnesses were brought. The horses were led out into the open ground beyond the gate. The dust began to rise from the road that went east toward Gilead.

Micaiah went to the prison alone, between the two guards. The door of the inner room was opened. They set the bread before him and the water beside it. They closed the door. The bolt slid into place.

Outside, the chariots were moving. The word he had spoken stayed in the room behind him.
