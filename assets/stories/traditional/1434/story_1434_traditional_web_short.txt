David wrote it as a song of ascents — one of the fifteen short songs that pilgrims sang on the road up to Jerusalem for the great festivals. Three short verses. He set it down in the latter years of his life, having known both the king's house and the wilderness caves, having stilled his own soul through many hours when he might have grasped at high matters. And he set the song among the songs of the ascents for those who would walk up to the temple in the years that followed.

Yahweh, my heart isn't haughty, nor my eyes lofty;
nor do I concern myself with great matters,
or things too wonderful for me.

Surely I have stilled and quieted my soul,
like a weaned child with his mother,
like a weaned child is my soul within me.

Israel, hope in Yahweh,
from this time forward and forever more.

And the song was sung by the pilgrims on the road up to Jerusalem in the years that followed. Three short verses of stilled soul and weaned child set among the longer songs of the ascents, kept for any pilgrim who would hear them on the road up to the temple.
