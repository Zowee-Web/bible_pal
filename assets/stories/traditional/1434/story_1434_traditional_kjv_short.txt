David wrote it as a song of degrees — one of the fifteen short songs which the pilgrims sang upon the road up to Jerusalem for the great feasts. Three short verses. He set it down in the latter years of his life, having known both the king's house and the caves of the wilderness, having stilled his own soul through many hours when he might have grasped at high matters. And he set the song among the songs of the degrees for them that should walk up unto the temple in the years that followed.

Lord, my heart is not haughty, nor mine eyes lofty:
neither do I exercise myself in great matters,
or in things too high for me.

Surely I have behaved and quieted myself,
as a child that is weaned of his mother:
my soul is even as a weaned child.

Let Israel hope in the Lord
from henceforth and for ever.

And the song was sung of the pilgrims upon the road up to Jerusalem in the years that followed. Three short verses of stilled soul and weaned child set among the longer songs of the degrees, kept for any pilgrim that should hear them upon the road up unto the temple.
