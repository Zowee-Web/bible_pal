The lookout saw it first. A line of dust on the horizon behind them, rising and widening. He turned and ran into the camp, calling out, and at his voice the children of Israel lifted up their eyes. They saw what he had seen. Chariots. Horsemen. The army of Egypt, marching after them.

The camp went still. Then it broke. Children were lifted onto shoulders. Women dropped their water jars and ran to their tents. Men reached for staffs they had no skill to use as weapons. The sea was at their backs. The wilderness was on their sides. The army of Pharaoh was filling the plain.

They were very afraid. They cried out to the Lord.

And then they turned on Moses.

The voices rose around him. "Is it because there were no graves in Egypt that you have taken us away to die in the wilderness? Why have you treated us this way, to bring us out of Egypt? Did we not say to you in Egypt, 'Leave us alone, that we may serve the Egyptians'? It would have been better for us to serve the Egyptians than to die in the wilderness!"

Old men gripped his sleeves. Mothers held out their children before him. The cry to the Lord and the cry against Moses rose at once. The dust kept coming on.

Moses stood before the people. He raised his hands. The nearest voices quieted. Then the next ring of voices. The cry traveled outward into silence.

"Do not be afraid," he said. "Stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord, which he will work for you today. For the Egyptians whom you see today, you will never see them again. The Lord will fight for you, and you shall be still."

Then, in the silence that followed, the word of the Lord came to Moses.

"Why do you cry to me? Speak to the children of Israel, that they go forward. Lift up your rod and stretch out your hand over the sea and divide it. The children of Israel shall go into the middle of the sea on dry ground. I myself will harden the hearts of the Egyptians, and they will go in after them. I will get myself honor over Pharaoh, and over all his armies."

The angel of God had been going before the camp of Israel through all the days of the wilderness. The pillar of cloud had stood ahead of them by day, leading them on. Now the angel moved. The pillar lifted. It rose into the air above the heads of the people, slow and immense, and it passed over them, and it settled behind them. It came between the camp of Egypt and the camp of Israel. From the side of Egypt it was cloud and darkness. From the side of Israel it gave light by night. One camp did not come near the other all night.

Moses stretched out his hand over the sea. A wind began to rise. It came from the east, hard and steady, and it tore at the cloaks of the people. They turned their faces away from it. The wind blew through the night. The water at the shore drew back. It drew back further. The bed of the sea began to show. Stones appeared. Then the slick floor beneath. Then more stones, further out. The wind did not stop. The wind blew all that night. By the dark hours before morning, the sea was divided. Two great walls of water stood on the right hand and on the left, and the dry ground ran between them out to the far shore.

Moses turned to the people. "Go."

They went. They went into the middle of the sea on the dry ground, and the waters were a wall to them on their right hand and on their left. Children walked beside their fathers. Old men leaned on their staffs. The women carried what they could. The corridor was long. The walls of water rose high above their heads on either side. Now and then a fish flickered inside the green, suspended, holding still. The ground beneath their feet was firm but cold. They walked all night.

On the other side of the pillar, the Egyptians waited. When light broke they saw that the sea was open. The commander gave the order. The chariots rolled forward. The horsemen followed. All of Pharaoh's horses, his chariots, and his horsemen pursued and went in after Israel into the middle of the sea.

In the morning watch, the Lord looked on the army of the Egyptians through the pillar of fire and of cloud, and he confused them. The chariot wheels caught in the floor of the sea. They came loose. The chariots dragged. The horses stumbled. Drivers reined them up short. The corridor narrowed in the chariot drivers' minds even though the walls had not moved. Their eyes lifted upward at the water held above them and could not look away. The Egyptians said to one another, "Let us flee from the face of Israel, for the Lord fights for them against us."

But it was too late. Israel had reached the far shore.

The Lord said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the sea, that the waters may come back on the Egyptians, on their chariots, and on their horsemen."

Moses stretched out his hand over the sea. The sea returned to its strength when the morning appeared. The walls trembled. The trembling ran along their length. Then the walls fell. The waters came down upon the chariots and upon the horsemen, upon all the army of Pharaoh that had gone in after Israel. The corridor closed behind them. There remained not so much as one of them.

The children of Israel walked on dry land in the middle of the sea, and they came up out of it on the other side, and they stood on the shore at first light. Behind them the sea lay smooth where the corridor had been. Bodies of Egyptians washed up along the seashore as the sun rose. The army that had been so loud at evening was silent in the morning.

Israel stood and looked. No one spoke. The wind had died with the morning. The pillar that had stood between them was gone. An old man near the front of the crowd lifted his face into the new light and wept.

Israel saw the great work which the Lord did to the Egyptians, and the people feared the Lord. They believed in the Lord and in his servant Moses. And on that shore, in the morning, the children of Israel began their journey on.
