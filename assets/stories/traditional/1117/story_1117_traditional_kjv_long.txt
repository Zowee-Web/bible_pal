The watchman saw it first. A line of dust upon the horizon behind them, rising and spreading wide. He turned and ran into the camp, crying out, and at his voice the children of Israel lifted up their eyes. They beheld what he had seen. Chariots. Horsemen. The army of Egypt, marching after them.

The camp stood still. Then it brake. Children were lifted upon shoulders. Women let fall their water jars and ran unto their tents. Men reached for staves they had no skill to wield as weapons. The sea was at their backs. The wilderness was on their sides. The host of Pharaoh was filling the plain.

They were sore afraid. They cried out unto the Lord.

And then they turned upon Moses.

The voices rose round about him. "Because there were no graves in Egypt, hast thou taken us away to die in the wilderness? Wherefore hast thou dealt thus with us, to bring us forth out of Egypt? Did we not say unto thee in Egypt, 'Let us alone, that we may serve the Egyptians'? For it had been better for us to serve the Egyptians, than that we should die in the wilderness!"

Old men gripped his sleeves. Mothers held forth their children before him. The cry unto the Lord and the cry against Moses rose at once. The dust came on still.

Moses stood before the people. He lifted up his hands. The nearest voices were stilled. Then the next ring of voices. The cry travelled outward unto silence.

"Fear ye not," he said. "Stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord, which he will shew to you this day. For the Egyptians whom ye have seen this day, ye shall see them again no more for ever. The Lord shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace."

Then, in the silence that followed, the word of the Lord came unto Moses.

"Wherefore criest thou unto me? Speak unto the children of Israel, that they go forward. But lift thou up thy rod, and stretch out thine hand over the sea, and divide it. And the children of Israel shall go into the midst of the sea upon the dry ground. And I, behold, I will harden the hearts of the Egyptians, and they shall follow them: and I will get me honour upon Pharaoh, and upon all his host."

The angel of God had gone before the camp of Israel through all the days of the wilderness. The pillar of the cloud had stood before them by day, leading them on. Now the angel removed. The pillar lifted. It rose into the air above the heads of the people, slow and exceeding great, and it passed over them, and it came down behind them. It came between the camp of the Egyptians and the camp of Israel. From the side of Egypt it was a cloud and darkness. From the side of Israel it gave light by night. The one came not near the other all the night.

And Moses stretched out his hand over the sea. A wind began to rise. It came from the east, mighty and steady, and it tore at the cloaks of the people. They turned their faces away from it. The wind blew through the night. The water at the shore drew back. It drew back yet further. The bed of the sea began to appear. Stones came forth. Then the slick floor beneath. Then more stones, further out. The wind ceased not. The wind blew all that night. By the dark hours before morning, the sea was divided. Two great walls of water stood on the right hand and on the left, and the dry ground ran between them out unto the far shore.

Moses turned unto the people. "Go forward."

And they went. They went into the midst of the sea upon the dry ground, and the waters were a wall unto them on their right hand and on their left. Children walked beside their fathers. Old men leaned upon their staves. The women bare what they could. The way was long. The walls of water rose high above their heads on either side. Now and again a fish flickered within the green, suspended, holding still. The ground beneath their feet was firm but cold. They walked all the night.

On the other side of the pillar, the Egyptians waited. When the light brake they saw that the sea was opened. The captain gave the word. The chariots rolled forward. The horsemen followed after. All Pharaoh's horses, his chariots, and his horsemen pursued, and went in after Israel unto the midst of the sea.

And it came to pass, that in the morning watch the Lord looked unto the host of the Egyptians through the pillar of fire and of the cloud, and troubled them. The chariot wheels caught in the floor of the sea. They came loose. The chariots dragged. The horses stumbled. The drivers reined them up short. The way narrowed in the minds of the chariot drivers though the walls had not moved. Their eyes were lifted up unto the water held above them and could not look away. And the Egyptians said one unto another, "Let us flee from the face of Israel, for the Lord fighteth for them against us."

But it was too late. Israel had come unto the far shore.

And the Lord said unto Moses, "Stretch out thine hand over the sea, that the waters may come again upon the Egyptians, upon their chariots, and upon their horsemen."

And Moses stretched out his hand over the sea. The sea returned to his strength when the morning appeared. The walls trembled. The trembling ran along their length. Then the walls fell. The waters came down upon the chariots, and upon the horsemen, even upon all the host of Pharaoh that came into the sea after Israel. The way closed behind them. There remained not so much as one of them.

But the children of Israel walked upon dry land in the midst of the sea, and they came up out of it on the other side, and they stood upon the shore at the first light. Behind them the sea lay smooth where the way had been. The bodies of the Egyptians washed up along the seashore as the sun rose. The host that had been so loud at evening was silent in the morning.

Israel stood and looked. No man spake. The wind was fallen with the morning. The pillar that had stood between them was gone. An old man near the front of the company lifted up his face into the new light and wept.

And Israel saw that great work which the Lord did upon the Egyptians, and the people feared the Lord. They believed in the Lord, and in his servant Moses. And on that shore, in the morning, the children of Israel began their journey on.
