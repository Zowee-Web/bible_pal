When Pharaoh drew near, the children of Israel lifted up their eyes. On the horizon behind them, a long line of dust was rising. Chariots. Horsemen. The army of Egypt was marching after them. The dust came on quickly.

They were very afraid. They cried out to the Lord. And then they turned on Moses.

"Is it because there were no graves in Egypt that you have taken us away to die in the wilderness? Why have you treated us this way, to bring us out of Egypt? Did we not say to you in Egypt, 'Leave us alone, that we may serve the Egyptians'? It would have been better for us to serve the Egyptians than to die in the wilderness."

Their voices rose against him. Children were lifted onto shoulders. Women clutched bundles. The sea was at their backs and the army was in front of them, and there was nowhere to go.

Moses stood before the people. "Do not be afraid. Stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord, which he will work for you today. For the Egyptians whom you see today, you will never see them again. The Lord will fight for you, and you shall be still."

The Lord said to Moses, "Why do you cry to me? Speak to the children of Israel, that they go forward. Lift up your rod and stretch out your hand over the sea and divide it. The children of Israel shall go into the middle of the sea on dry ground."

The angel of God, who had been going before the camp of Israel, moved. The pillar of cloud, which had stood ahead of them through the day, lifted and turned. It passed over the heads of the people and settled behind them. It came between the camp of Egypt and the camp of Israel. There was the cloud, and there was darkness, and yet to Israel it gave light by night. One camp did not come near the other all night.

Moses stretched out his hand over the sea. The Lord caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all that night. The wind tore at the cloaks of the people. The waters were divided. They lifted in two great walls on the right hand and on the left. The bed of the sea was made dry land between them.

The children of Israel went into the middle of the sea on the dry ground. They walked between walls of water. They walked all night.

The Egyptians pursued and went in after them — all of Pharaoh's horses, his chariots, and his horsemen. The chariot wheels cut into the floor of the sea. The horses' hooves beat down between the walls of water.

In the morning watch, the Lord looked on the army of the Egyptians through the pillar of fire and of cloud, and he confused them. He took off their chariot wheels so that they drove them heavily. The chariots faltered. The horses staggered. The Egyptians said to one another, "Let us flee from the face of Israel, for the Lord fights for them against us."

But it was too late. Israel had already reached the far shore.

The Lord said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the sea, that the waters may come back on the Egyptians, on their chariots, and on their horsemen."

Moses stretched out his hand over the sea. The sea returned to its strength when the morning appeared. The Egyptians fled against it, and the walls fell. The waters came down upon the chariots and upon the horsemen, upon all the army of Pharaoh that had gone in after Israel. There remained not so much as one of them.

The children of Israel walked on dry land in the middle of the sea, and they came up out of it on the other side, and they stood on the shore at first light.

They looked back. The water lay still where the sea had been opened. Bodies of Egyptians washed up along the seashore in the morning sun. The army that had pursued them was gone.

Israel saw the great work which the Lord did to the Egyptians. The people feared the Lord. They believed in the Lord and in his servant Moses, and they stood on the shore in the new light of the morning.
