After Moses died, the weight of what came next must have settled heavily on Joshua's shoulders. A whole nation looking to him, a river yet uncrossed, a land full of unknowns. And into that silence, a voice spoke — not with a list of strategies or battle plans, but with a repeated phrase: "Be strong and courageous."

Three times those words came, as if the repetition itself carried meaning. Not because Joshua was naturally fearless, but perhaps because courage is something that needs reminding. It grows not from the absence of fear but from the presence of something greater than fear.

There is a quiet pattern woven through this moment — the way strength is paired not with self-reliance but with nearness. "I will be with you." The promise was not that the path would be easy or the obstacles small. It was that the walking would not be done alone.

Seasons of transition arrive in every life — new responsibilities, unfamiliar terrain, the echo of what came before meeting the uncertainty of what lies ahead. And sometimes, in the stillness between what was and what will be, there is a voice that simply says, "I am with you wherever you go."
