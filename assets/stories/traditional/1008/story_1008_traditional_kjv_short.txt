Moses the servant of the LORD was dead, and the camp lay still beneath the weight of that silence. The tents of Israel stretched wide across the plains of Moab, and beyond the river, the land of Canaan waited under a pale sky. Smoke rose thin from scattered fires. No trumpet sounded. No elder called assembly. The people moved slowly among their dwellings, carrying water, mending rope, speaking little.

Then the LORD spoke unto Joshua the son of Nun, who had served Moses and stood now at the edge of the camp, near the banks of the Jordan. The voice came, and Joshua stood where he was, his sandals pressed into the dry earth, the wind pulling at his garment.

The LORD declared that Moses His servant was dead, and that Joshua must arise and go over this Jordan — he, and all this people — into the land which was given unto the children of Israel. Every place whereon the sole of his foot should tread, that land was given unto him, even as the LORD had spoken unto Moses.

The borders were named. From the wilderness southward unto Lebanon, from the great river Euphrates through all the land of the Hittites and unto the great sea toward the going down of the sun — this was the territory appointed.

No man would stand before Joshua all the days of his life. As the LORD had been with Moses, so would He be with Joshua. He would not fail him, nor forsake him. The LORD commanded him to be strong and of good courage, for unto this people he would divide the inheritance which the LORD had sworn unto their fathers.

Then the command came again. He was to be strong and very courageous, observing to do according to all the law which Moses had commanded him. He was not to turn from it to the right hand or to the left, that he might prosper whithersoever he went. The book of the law was not to depart out of his mouth. He was to meditate therein day and night, and to observe all that was written within it, and then his way would be made prosperous, and he would have good success.

A third time the LORD spoke it plainly: be strong and of good courage. He was not to be afraid, neither was he to be dismayed, for the LORD his God was with him whithersoever he went.

The wind moved across the Jordan, bending the reeds along its banks. The water ran swift and full, swollen with the season. Joshua stood alone on the high ground, the camp of Israel spread behind him, the river before him, and beyond it, the land. His hand rested upon the hilt of his sword. The fires of the camp sent their smoke upward into the morning air, and the sound of the river filled the silence where the voice had been.
