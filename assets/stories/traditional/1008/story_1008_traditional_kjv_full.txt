Moses the servant of the LORD was dead. His body lay upon the mountain, and the camp of Israel spread below in the plains of Moab, vast and still. The tents stretched in ordered rows, thousands upon thousands, their cloth stirring in the dry wind that came off the wilderness. Smoke rose from scattered fires. Children moved between the tent lines. Men sat with their weapons across their knees. Women carried water from the river's edge. The Jordan lay before them, its current moving steadily, the land beyond it green and distant under the open sky.

Joshua the son of Nun stood among them. He had served Moses, had walked beside him through the years of wandering, had carried his words to the elders and his commands to the captains. Now Moses was gone from the earth, and the weight of the nation rested where it had been laid.

And the LORD spake unto Joshua.

The voice came, and Joshua stood where he was. The LORD declared that Moses His servant was dead, and that now Joshua must arise and go over this Jordan — he, and all this people — unto the land which the LORD did give to the children of Israel. Every place that the sole of Joshua's foot should tread upon, the LORD said He had given it unto him, even as He had spoken unto Moses.

The boundaries were named aloud. From the wilderness southward, and from Lebanon northward, unto the great river Euphrates — all the land of the Hittites — and unto the great sea toward the going down of the sun: this would be their coast. The words fell across the stillness of the camp, marking territories that stretched beyond what any eye among them could see from where they stood.

The LORD spoke again. No man would be able to stand before Joshua all the days of his life. As the LORD had been with Moses, so He would be with Joshua. He would not fail him, nor forsake him. The words were direct and without ornament, spoken as a commander speaks to the one who must lead the march at dawn.

Then the charge was given. Joshua was told to be strong and of a good courage, for unto this people he would divide for an inheritance the land which the LORD had sworn unto their fathers to give them. The command carried the full authority of the oath made to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob — the promise repeated across generations, now arriving at its appointed hour beside the Jordan's banks.

A second time the LORD commanded strength and courage, and added a further charge. Joshua was to observe and to do according to all the law which Moses had commanded him. He was not to turn from it to the right hand or to the left, that he might prosper whithersoever he went. The law was not to depart out of his mouth. He was to meditate therein day and night, that he might observe to do according to all that was written therein — for then his way would be made prosperous, and then he would have good success.

The words bore the weight of exactness. Not some of the law, but all of it. Not occasionally, but day and night. Not loosely followed, but observed and done. The charge was precise, and it was given to a man who stood at the edge of a river with a nation behind him and fortified cities ahead.

A third time the LORD spoke the command. He told Joshua plainly: be strong and of a good courage. He was not to be afraid, neither was he to be dismayed, for the LORD his God was with him whithersoever he went.

Three times the command to strength. Three times the call to courage. Each time delivered with a different weight — the first joined to the inheritance of the land, the second joined to the keeping of the law, the third joined to the presence of the LORD Himself wherever Joshua's feet would carry him.

The voice ceased. The camp remained. The Jordan still moved in its channel, brown and full. Across it lay Jericho, its walls visible from the higher ground, pale stone set against the green of its surrounding palms. The armies of Canaan held their cities. The roads were unknown. The battles had not yet been fought.

Joshua stood in the plains of Moab, the dust of the wilderness on his garments, the nation of Israel spread behind him in their tents. The fires still burned low among the encampments. The animals shifted in their pens. Somewhere a child called out, and a mother answered. The ordinary sounds of the camp continued as they had continued through forty years of wandering — the clink of metal, the murmur of voices, the bleating of goats along the outer edges where the flocks were kept.

The river lay ahead. The land lay beyond. The words of the LORD had been spoken, and Joshua the son of Nun had heard them, standing in the place where Moses had stood before him. The camp waited. The Jordan ran on toward the Salt Sea, and the sun moved westward over the land of promise, casting long shadows across the water.
