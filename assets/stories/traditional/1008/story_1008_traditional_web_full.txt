The camp of Israel stretched wide across the plains east of the Jordan. Tents of goat hair and woven cloth stood in rows, their edges lifting and falling in the dry wind that came off the open ground. Smoke rose from cooking fires scattered among the families. Children carried water jars along the worn paths between shelters. Livestock shifted and lowed in their pens. The sound of hammers on tent pegs rang out here and there as men secured their dwellings against the gusts.

Moses, the servant of Yahweh, was dead.

The people had buried him, and the days of mourning had passed. His voice no longer carried across the assembly. His staff no longer struck the ground before the tabernacle. The place where he had stood to speak to the congregation was empty now, and the dust settled undisturbed over the flattened earth there.

Joshua the son of Nun stood among the tents. He was Moses' servant, the man who had walked beside the elder leader through the wilderness years, who had commanded the armies of Israel in battle. His hands were roughened from labor and war. His cloak hung over broad shoulders, and his sandals were cracked from the long road behind him.

Then Yahweh spoke to Joshua the son of Nun, Moses' servant. The words came to him clearly, as a voice spoken aloud in the open air.

"Moses my servant is dead. Now therefore arise, go across this Jordan, you and all this people, to the land which I am giving to them, even to the children of Israel."

Joshua stood still. The wind pressed against the fabric of the nearest tent, and a rope creaked on its stake.

"I have given you every place that the sole of your foot will tread on, as I told Moses. From the wilderness and this Lebanon, even to the great river, the river Euphrates, all the land of the Hittites, and to the great sea toward the going down of the sun, shall be your border."

The Jordan was visible from the higher ground at the edge of camp — a line of green growth cutting through the brown land, the water moving southward. Beyond it, the hills of Canaan rose in uneven ridges, their slopes covered with scrub and stone. The land was vast. It reached in every direction the voice described — north toward the mountains of Lebanon, east to the distant Euphrates, west to the coast where the sun dropped below the horizon each evening.

"No man will be able to stand before you all the days of your life. As I was with Moses, so I will be with you. I will not fail you nor forsake you."

A group of elders sat nearby under a stretched awning, their faces turned toward the place where Joshua stood. Young soldiers passed on the path beyond, carrying spears with bronze points. A woman paused at her grinding stone, her hands resting on the grain.

"Be strong and courageous, for you shall cause this people to inherit the land which I swore to their fathers to give them."

The voice continued, and the camp sounds — the bleating of goats, the clatter of wooden bowls, the voices of children — went on around the edges of that speaking.

"Only be strong and very courageous. Be careful to do according to all the law which Moses my servant commanded you. Don't turn from it to the right hand or to the left, that you may have good success wherever you go."

A gust lifted sand from the open ground and carried it past the nearest row of tents. The awning above the elders snapped taut and then sagged again.

"This book of the law shall not depart from your mouth, but you shall meditate on it day and night, that you may observe to do according to all that is written in it; for then you shall make your way prosperous, and then you shall have good success."

Joshua's feet were planted on the packed earth. His hands hung at his sides. The Jordan lay ahead, and beyond it the land — the hills, the walled cities, the valleys thick with trees and cut through by streams. The people of Israel filled the camp behind him, thousands upon thousands, their tents spreading back across the plain like a great flock settled on open pasture.

"Haven't I commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Don't be afraid. Don't be dismayed, for Yahweh your God is with you wherever you go."

The voice finished. The wind moved through the camp. Tent ropes hummed against their stakes. Somewhere a man called out to his neighbor, and the neighbor answered. A child ran between two shelters, bare feet slapping the dry ground. The fires sent their thin columns of smoke upward into the broad sky.

Joshua stood at the edge of the camp, facing the Jordan. The river moved in its channel. The far bank was green and tangled with growth. Beyond it, the land of Canaan waited under the afternoon light — stone and soil and open ridge, stretching west and north, silent and wide.
