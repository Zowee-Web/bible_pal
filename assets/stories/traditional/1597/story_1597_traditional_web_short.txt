As Balaam rode toward Moab, an angel of Yahweh stood in the road with a drawn sword — and only the donkey could see him. Balaam had saddled her that morning and set out with the princes of Moab, and God's anger burned because he went.

When the donkey saw the angel standing in the way, she turned aside into a field, and Balaam struck her to turn her back. Farther on, the angel stood in a narrow path between two vineyard walls. The donkey pressed against the wall and crushed Balaam's foot, and he struck her again. Then the angel stood in a place so narrow there was no room to turn to the right or the left. The donkey lay down under Balaam, and his anger burned, and he beat her with his staff.

Then Yahweh opened the donkey's mouth. "What have I done to you," she said, "that you have struck me these three times?"

"Because you have made a mockery of me," Balaam said. "If I had a sword in my hand, I would kill you now."

"Am I not your own donkey, the one you have ridden all your life?" she said. "Have I ever been in the habit of doing this to you?" "No," he said.

Then Yahweh opened Balaam's eyes, and he saw the angel standing in the road with his drawn sword, and he bowed his head and fell on his face. "Why have you struck your donkey these three times?" the angel said. "I have come out to stand against you, because your way is perverse before me. The donkey saw me and turned aside three times. If she had not turned away, I would surely have killed you by now, and let her live."

"I have sinned," Balaam said. "I did not know you were standing in the road against me. If this displeases you, I will go back." But the angel said, "Go with the men. Only speak the word that I tell you." So Balaam went on with the princes of Balak.
