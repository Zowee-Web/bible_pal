Peter called his readers to come to Christ, the living stone rejected by men but chosen and precious to God. They too were living stones, built into a spiritual house and a holy priesthood to offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ. Scripture named him the chosen cornerstone in Zion. To believers belonged honor, while the disobedient stumbled at the word. Those who had once not been a people were now God’s people; those who had not obtained mercy had now received mercy. They were a chosen race called from darkness into God’s marvelous light to proclaim his excellence.

As foreigners and pilgrims, they were to abstain from desires that war against the soul and maintain good behavior among the nations. Those who spoke against them as evildoers might see their good works and glorify God. Peter told them to subject themselves to every ordinance of man for the Lord’s sake and to silence ignorant accusations by doing good. Their freedom was not to cover wickedness; they were to live as God’s servants. “Honor all men. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honor the king.”

Servants were to be in subjection to their masters with all respect, not only to the good and gentle, but also to the wicked. It was commendable if someone endured pain, suffering unjustly, because of conscience toward God. There was no glory in patiently enduring a beating for sin, but patiently enduring suffering while doing good was commendable with God. Peter said they were called to this, because Christ also suffered for them, leaving an example that they should follow his steps.

Christ was the one who didn’t sin, and no deceit was found in his mouth. When he was cursed, he didn’t curse back. When he suffered, he didn’t threaten, but committed himself to him who judges righteously. He bore their sins in his body on the tree so that, having died to sins, they might live to righteousness; by his wounds they were healed. They had been going astray like sheep, but now had returned to the Shepherd and Overseer of their souls.
