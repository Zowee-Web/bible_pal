Jesus came with his disciples into the coasts of Cesarea Philippi, in the far north of the land, where the way climbed toward the high country. They had walked the long road there together, and as they went, he turned and asked them, “Whom do men say that I the Son of man am?”

One after another they told him what they had heard men saying along the way. “Some say that thou art John the Baptist; some, Elias; and others, Jeremias, or one of the prophets.” These were the opinions that had passed from one man to another, carried along the ways and into the villages.

He let their answers rest. Then he asked them again, and this time the question was turned toward them. “But whom say ye that I am?”

It was no longer a matter of what other men were saying. The question had been put to them directly, and for a moment no man spoke. Then Simon Peter answered and said, “Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God.”

And Jesus looked upon him. “Blessed art thou, Simon Bar-jona, for flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee, but my Father which is in heaven.”

Then he gave him a name. “Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it. And I will give unto thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven; and whatsoever thou shalt bind on earth shall be bound in heaven, and whatsoever thou shalt loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven.”

The words settled over the small company on the road. Then he charged them that they should tell no man that he was Jesus the Christ. And they went on together, the way climbing before them into the hills, and what he had said stayed among them.
