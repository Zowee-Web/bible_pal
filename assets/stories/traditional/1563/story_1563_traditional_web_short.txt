Jesus came with his disciples into the region of Caesarea Philippi, in the far north of the land, where the road climbed toward the high country. They had walked the long road there together, and as they went, he turned to them with a question. “Who do men say that I, the Son of Man, am?”

One after another they told him what they had heard people saying along the way. “Some say John the Baptizer. Some say Elijah. Others say Jeremiah, or one of the prophets.” These were the opinions that had passed from one person to another, carried along the roads and into the villages.

He let their answers rest. Then he asked them again, and this time the question was turned toward them. “But who do you say that I am?”

It was no longer a matter of what other men were saying. The question had been put to them directly, and for a moment none of them spoke. Then Simon Peter answered him. “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.”

Jesus looked at him. “Blessed are you, Simon son of Jonah, for flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but my Father who is in heaven.”

Then he gave him a name. “You are Peter, and on this rock I will build my assembly, and the gates of Hades will not prevail against it. I will give you the keys of the Kingdom of Heaven. Whatever you bind on earth will have been bound in heaven, and whatever you release on earth will have been released in heaven.”

The words settled over the small company on the road. Then he charged them to tell no one that he was the Christ. And they went on together, the road climbing before them into the hills, and what he had said stayed among them.
