Saul came back from chasing the Philistines, and word was brought to him: David is in the wilderness of En-gedi. So Saul took three thousand chosen men out of all Israel and went to seek him among the rocks of the wild goats.

He came to the sheep pens beside the road, where there was a cave, and Saul went in to relieve himself. Far back in the sides of that same cave, David and his men were sitting in the shadows.

The men whispered to David. “This is the day Yahweh spoke of, when he said he would deliver your enemy into your hand. Do to him as it seems good to you.” David rose without a sound. He came close behind the king. He cut off the skirt of Saul’s robe and slipped back to his men.

But afterward David’s heart struck him, because he had cut off the skirt. “Yahweh forbid that I should do this thing to my lord,” he said, “to stretch out my hand against Yahweh’s anointed.” With these words he held his men back, and did not let them rise against Saul. And Saul got up, left the cave, and went on his way.

Then David went out after him and cried, “My lord the king!” When Saul looked behind him, David bowed with his face to the earth. He held up the piece of cloth in his hand. “See the skirt of your robe. I cut it off, and I did not kill you. There is no evil or rebellion in my hand, though you hunt my life to take it. May Yahweh judge between me and you. But my hand will not be against you.”

Saul heard the voice, and it broke him. “Is that your voice, my son David?” He lifted up his voice and wept. “You are more righteous than I,” he said, “for you have done good to me, and I have done evil to you. Today you have shown how you dealt well with me, when Yahweh delivered me into your hand and you did not kill me. Now I know that you will surely be king.”

So David swore to him that he would not cut off his name after him. Then Saul went home. And David and his men went up to the stronghold.
