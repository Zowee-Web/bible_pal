When Saul came back from fighting the Philistines, word reached him that David was in the wilderness of En-gedi. So he took three thousand of the best men in all Israel and set out to find him among the rocks of the wild goats.

On the way he came to the sheep pens beside the road, where there was a cave, and he went inside to relieve himself. David and his men were in the inner parts of that same cave.

David's men told him that this was the day the LORD had promised him, when his enemy would be handed over to do with as he wished. David got up quietly and cut a corner from Saul's robe. But even that troubled his conscience, that he had cut the robe of the king at all. "The LORD forbid that I should raise my hand against the LORD's anointed," he said. With those words he held his men back and would not let them attack Saul. So Saul got up, left the cave, and went on his way.

Then David went out after him and called, "My lord the king!" When Saul looked back, David bowed low with his face to the ground.

"Why do you listen to those who tell you I mean you harm?" he said. "Today the LORD gave you into my hand in the cave. My men urged me to kill you, but I spared you, for I will not raise my hand against the LORD's anointed. See the piece of your robe here in my hand," he said. "I took only the edge, and I did you no harm. There is no evil or rebellion in me; I have not wronged you, though you hunt my life to take it. Let the LORD judge between us and give me justice. The old proverb says, 'Out of the wicked comes wickedness,' but my hand will not be against you. Whom has the king of Israel come out to chase? A dead dog? A single flea? May the LORD be our judge, take up my cause, and deliver me from your hand."

When David had finished, Saul answered him. "Is that your voice, my son David?" And he wept aloud. "You are more righteous than I," he said. "I repaid you with evil, but you have repaid me with good. Today you showed how well you dealt with me: the LORD gave me into your hand, and you did not kill me. When a man finds his enemy in his power, does he let him go away unharmed? Yet you did. Now I know it for certain — you will be king, and the kingdom of Israel will be secure in your hands." Then he asked one thing of David — that he swear by the LORD not to cut off his descendants after him, or blot out his name from his father's house.

So David gave him his oath. Then Saul went home, and David and his men went up to the stronghold.
