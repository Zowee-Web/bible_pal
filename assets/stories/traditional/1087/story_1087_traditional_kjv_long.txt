The river Jordan moved slowly through the low country, brown and steady, winding between banks of tamarisk and willow. The hills of Judaea rose to the west, dry and stony. To the east the land lay flat — a wide, dusty plain stretching toward the wilderness. The river was not deep at that place — waist-high for the most part, chest-deep in a few — and its current was gentle enough that a man might stand in it and not be moved.

John had been baptizing there many days. He was a rough figure — broad-shouldered, darkened by the sun, his hair wild, his raiment woven of camel's hair, girded at the waist with a leathern belt. His voice was loud and direct, carrying well across the water and the bank. He flattered not. He softened not his words. He called the people to repent, to confess their sins, to make ready for what was to come. "The kingdom of heaven is at hand," he said, and the people hearkened.

They came from all quarters. From Jerusalem, from the villages along the river, from towns in the hill country and the plains beyond. Publicans came. Soldiers came. Husbandmen and merchants and mothers with children came. They gathered upon the bank in great companies, sitting upon the rocks or standing in the shallows, waiting. The sound of their voices mingled with the sound of the water.

One at a time they waded in. John set his hands upon their shoulders, spake a word, and lowered them beneath the surface. The water closed over them and they came up — gasping, dripping, some weeping, some silent, some standing a long while in the current with their eyes shut and their arms at their sides, suffering the water to move past. It was not a spectacle. It was quiet, solemn work — one soul at a time, in the brown water of the Jordan.

Then cometh Jesus from Galilee to Jordan unto John, to be baptized of him.

He came from the north, having walked the roads south through the valley. He came to the river's edge without heralding, without a company of his own. He was one figure among many, standing upon the bank in the afternoon light. He stepped into the water.

John saw him. He stayed his hand. Something crossed his countenance — recognition, and then something nigh unto fear.

"I have need to be baptized of thee," said John. His voice, usually so bold, was strained. "And comest thou to me?"

Jesus looked upon him with calm, steady eyes. "Suffer it to be so now," he said. "For thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness."

John held his gaze. The water moved about their legs. The crowd upon the shore watched, understanding not. Then John inclined his head. He placed his hands upon the shoulders of Jesus — the same hands that had lowered hundreds beneath the surface — and he lowered him down.

The water closed over Jesus. Brown, cool, quiet. The current tugged at his garments and passed. Above, the light was filtered through the surface in shifting patterns of gold and green. For a moment all was still — the river, the sky, the watching multitude, the man beneath the water.

Then John brought him up.

Jesus rose from the surface, water pouring from his hair and his face and his garments. He drew breath. And as he stood there in the river, the heavens were opened unto him.

It was not slight. The very sky seemed to part — not with violence, but wholly, as a curtain drawn back from a great window. The ordinary blue gave way to something deeper, brighter — something the eye could scarce contain. Light came through — not the light of the sun, but something older and purer. And the Spirit of God descended. It came down like a dove — gently, without haste, drifting upon the air — and it lighted upon him.

Then a voice spake from heaven.

It was not thunder. It was not wind nor water. It was a voice — clear, deep, past all mistaking — and it said:

"This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased."

The words filled the air above the river. They reached the people standing upon the bank. They reached John, standing waist-deep in the current, his arms hanging at his sides, his face turned upward. They reached Jesus, standing in the shallows, his garments clinging to his body, his face lifted toward the opened sky.

No man stirred. The river flowed on. The wind moved the willow branches upon the bank. A bird cried once from somewhere upriver and was silent.

The heavens closed. The dove-like presence tarried, then was lifted. The ordinary light returned — the sun, the hills, the brown water moving south as it had ever done.

John stood in the current. He spake not. Jesus walked out of the river and up the bank, his wet garments heavy, his sandals dark with water. The crowd upon the shore parted before him, their eyes wide, their voices low.

The river flowed on. The day continued. But something had been spoken in that place — declared aloud, from the very heavens — and every soul that stood upon that shore carried it with them when they departed thence.
