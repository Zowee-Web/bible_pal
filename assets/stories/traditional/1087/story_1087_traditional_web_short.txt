In those days, John was baptizing in the Jordan River. The water was brown and slow-moving, flowing south through the low country between the hills of Judea and the plains beyond. People came from all around — from Jerusalem, from the towns along the river, from the countryside — to confess their sins and be baptized. They waded into the water one at a time, and John lowered them beneath the surface and brought them up again, the water streaming from their hair and their clothes.

John was a rough man. He wore clothing made of camel's hair, cinched at the waist with a leather belt. His voice carried across the water. He spoke plainly, without softness, calling people to repent and prepare the way of the Lord.

Then Jesus came.

He walked down from Galilee to the Jordan, traveling the dusty roads south until he reached the place where John was baptizing. He came to the river's edge and stepped into the water.

John looked at him. He knew who he was. He drew back. "I need to be baptized by you," John said, "and you come to me?"

Jesus answered him gently. "Allow it now," he said, "for it is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness."

John looked at him a moment longer, then he agreed. He placed his hands on Jesus and lowered him into the water. The river closed over him — brown and cool and quiet. For a moment, Jesus was beneath the surface, the current moving past, the light above him filtered and broken.

Then John brought him up.

Jesus rose from the water, and as he came up, the heavens opened. It happened above them like a curtain drawn back — the sky splitting apart, the light changing, something vast and bright breaking through the ordinary blue. The Spirit of God descended like a dove. It came down gently, without hurry, and rested on him.

And a voice came from heaven.

"This is my beloved Son," the voice said, "in whom I am well pleased."

The words moved across the water and the riverbank and the people standing there. John stood still in the current, his hands at his sides, the water dripping from his arms. Jesus stood in the shallow water, his clothes clinging to his body, his face lifted toward the sky.

The dove-like presence rested on him, and the voice faded, and the river kept moving south through the low hills, the same as it always had — but nothing, from that moment, was the same.
