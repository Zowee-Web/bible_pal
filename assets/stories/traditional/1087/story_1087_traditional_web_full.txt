In those days, John was baptizing people in the Jordan River. The river moved slowly through the low country between the hills of Judea and the dry plains to the east, its banks lined with tamarisk and willow. People came from all around — from Jerusalem, from the villages along the river, from the surrounding countryside — to confess their sins and be baptized. They gathered on the shore in loose crowds, some standing in the shallows with their sandals in their hands, others sitting on the bank watching and waiting their turn.

John stood in the water, waist-deep, the current pulling at the hem of his rough garment. He wore clothing made of camel's hair, cinched at the waist with a leather belt. His voice was strong and carried across the water. He spoke plainly, without softness — calling the people to repent, to prepare the way of the Lord, to turn from what they had been doing and begin again.

One by one, the people came forward. They waded into the brown water, and John placed his hands on them and lowered them beneath the surface and brought them up again. Water streamed from their hair and their faces. Some came up weeping. Some came up quiet. Some came up changed.

Then Jesus came.

He walked down from Galilee, traveling the roads south through the Jordan Valley until he reached the place where John was baptizing. He came to the water's edge and stepped in. The crowd did not know him. He was one more man among many. But John looked at him and stopped.

"I need to be baptized by you," John said, "and you come to me?"

Jesus answered him quietly, gently. "Allow it now," he said, "for it is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness."

John held his gaze for a long moment. Then he agreed. He placed his hands on Jesus' shoulders and lowered him into the river. The water closed over him — brown and cool and quiet. The current moved past. The light above was broken and golden through the surface. For a moment, Jesus was beneath the water, and everything was still.

Then John brought him up.

Jesus rose from the river, water streaming from his hair, his face, his arms. And as he came up, the heavens opened. The sky broke apart — not violently, but completely, as a curtain drawn back to reveal something vast behind it. The light changed. The air changed. And the Spirit of God descended like a dove, gentle and unhurried, and came to rest on him.

Then a voice came from heaven. It moved across the water and the riverbank and the people standing there — not like thunder, but like something deeper, something that vibrated in the chest.

"This is my beloved Son," the voice said, "in whom I am well pleased."

John stood still in the current, his hands at his sides, the water dripping from his arms. The dove-like presence rested on Jesus. The crowd on the shore was silent. The wind moved through the willows along the bank, and the river kept flowing south through the low hills, the same as it always had — but everything, from that moment, was different.
