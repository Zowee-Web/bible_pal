In those days, John was baptizing the people in the river Jordan. The water moved slowly through the low country between the hills of Judaea and the dry plains to the east, its banks lined with tamarisk and willow. People came from all about — from Jerusalem, from the villages along the river, from the surrounding country — to confess their sins and be baptized. They gathered upon the bank, some standing in the shallows, others sitting upon the rocks watching and waiting their turn.

John stood in the water, waist-deep, the current pulling at the hem of his rough garment. He wore raiment of camel's hair, girded about with a leathern belt. His voice was strong and carried across the water. He spake plainly, without softness — calling the people to repent, to prepare the way of the Lord, to turn from their former ways.

One by one the people came forward. They waded into the brown water, and John laid his hands upon them and lowered them beneath the surface and brought them up again. Water streamed from their hair and faces. Some came up weeping. Some came up quiet. Some came up changed.

Then cometh Jesus from Galilee to Jordan unto John. He came unto the water's edge and stepped in. The crowd knew him not. He was one man among many. But John looked upon him and stayed his hand.

"I have need to be baptized of thee," said John, "and comest thou to me?"

Jesus answered him quietly. "Suffer it to be so now: for thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness."

John looked upon him long. Then he consented. He laid his hands upon the shoulders of Jesus and lowered him into the river. The water closed over him — brown, cool, quiet. The current moved past. The light above was broken and golden through the surface. For a moment Jesus was beneath the water, and all was still.

Then John brought him up.

Jesus rose from the river, water pouring from his hair, his face, his arms. And as he came up, the heavens were opened unto him. The sky parted — not violently, but wholly, as a curtain drawn aside to shew what lay beyond. The light changed. The air changed. And the Spirit of God descended like a dove — gently, without haste — and lighted upon him.

Then a voice came from heaven. It moved across the water and the bank and the people standing there — not as thunder, but as something deeper, something that shook the very bones.

"This is my beloved Son," saith the voice, "in whom I am well pleased."

John stood still in the current, his arms at his sides, the water dripping from his hands. The dove-like presence rested upon Jesus. The crowd upon the shore was silent. The wind stirred the willows along the bank, and the river flowed on through the low hills, as it ever had — but all things, from that hour, were different.
