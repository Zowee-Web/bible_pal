In those days, John was baptizing in the river Jordan. The water moved slowly through the low country, brown and still, between the hills of Judaea and the plains beyond. People came from all about — from Jerusalem, from the towns along the river, from the countryside — to confess their sins and be baptized. They waded into the water one at a time, and John lowered them beneath the surface and brought them up again, the water running from their hair and their garments.

John was a rough man. He wore raiment of camel's hair, with a leathern girdle about his loins. His voice carried across the water. He spake plainly, calling the people to repent and prepare the way of the Lord.

Then cometh Jesus from Galilee to Jordan unto John, to be baptized of him. He came to the river's edge and stepped into the water.

John looked upon him and drew back. "I have need to be baptized of thee," he said, "and comest thou to me?"

Jesus answered him, "Suffer it to be so now: for thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness."

Then John suffered him. He set his hands upon Jesus and lowered him into the water. The river closed over him — brown and cool and quiet. For a moment, Jesus was beneath the surface, and the light above was broken and golden.

Then John brought him up.

And Jesus, when he was baptized, went up straightway out of the water: and, lo, the heavens were opened unto him. The sky parted as a curtain drawn back, the light changing, something vast and bright breaking through. The Spirit of God descended like a dove and lighted upon him.

And lo a voice from heaven, saying, "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased."

The words moved across the water and the bank. John stood still in the current, the water dripping from his arms. Jesus stood in the shallows, his face lifted heavenward.

The dove-like presence rested upon him, and the voice ceased, and the river flowed on through the low hills — but from that hour, nothing was the same.
