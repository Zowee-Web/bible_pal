The word of the Lord came unto Jeremiah. "Arise, and go down to the potter's house, and there I will cause thee to hear my words."

Then Jeremiah went down to the potter's house. He found the door standing open. The workshop was low of ceiling and dim, the dust of clay hanging in the air, the smell of wet earth thick about him. The floor was of packed dirt, darkened with water. Shelves along the walls held vessels finished and dry — jars with narrow mouths, wide bowls for kneading, pitchers with looped handles — each smooth and waiting. A cat lay sleeping in a patch of sunlight near the threshold.

The potter sat at his wheels, a man advanced in years, with thick arms and hands darkened by long seasons of clay. A lump of wet earth sat upon the stone before him. A bowl of water rested nigh his knee.

He set the wheel turning with his foot. The stone hummed. The clay began to spin.

The potter wet his hands and pressed them about the clay. His fingers guided and pressed. The clay rose between his palms, taking shape — narrowing at the neck, widening at the brim. Water dripped from his hands upon the spinning surface, running in thin lines down the sides.

And the vessel that he made of clay was marred in the hand of the potter. Something shifted within the clay — a pocket of air, a grain of sand, a weakness unseen by any save the potter himself. The wall fell inward. The brim folded and sagged.

The potter cast not the clay aside. He cursed not, nor reached for new earth. His hands, yet wet, pressed the clay back down into itself, folding the marred vessel inward, working the mass smooth again with the heel of his hand. The wheel turned still. The clay flattened and rounded and became a lump once more — wet, formless, ready.

So he made it again another vessel, as seemed good to the potter to make it. His hands closed about the clay and it rose. A different shape this time. Wider at the base, shorter in the neck. His thumbs opened the hollow slowly, the walls building thicker than before. Water ran in thin lines down the turning surface. The clay yielded where he pressed and held where he gave support. The wheel hummed.

Then came the word of the Lord unto Jeremiah. "O house of Israel, cannot I do with you as this potter? saith the Lord. Behold, as the clay is in the potter's hand, so are ye in mine hand, O house of Israel."

The wheel turned. The vessel sat whole upon the stone, its brim smooth and even beneath the potter's wet fingers. He reached for the wire to cut it free.
