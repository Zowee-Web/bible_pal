The word of the Lord came to Jeremiah. "Arise and go down to the potter's house, and there I will let you hear my words."

Jeremiah went down to the potter's house. He found the door standing open. The workshop was low-ceilinged and dim, clay dust hanging in the air, the smell of wet earth thick and close. The floor was packed dirt, darkened with water stains. Shelves along the walls held finished vessels — jars with narrow mouths, wide bowls for kneading, pitchers with looped handles — each smooth and dry and waiting. A cat slept in a patch of sunlight near the threshold.

The potter sat at his wheel, an older man with thick forearms and hands darkened by years of working the clay. A lump of wet earth sat on the stone before him. A bowl of water rested near his knee.

He set the wheel turning with his foot. The stone hummed. The clay began to spin.

The potter wet his hands and pressed them around the clay. His fingers guided and pressed. The clay rose between his palms, taking shape — narrowing at the neck, widening at the rim. Water dripped from his hands onto the spinning surface, running in thin lines down the sides.

Then the vessel was spoiled. Something shifted in the clay — a pocket of air, a grain of sand, a weakness invisible to anyone but the potter. The wall collapsed inward. The rim folded and sagged.

The potter did not throw the clay aside. He did not curse or reach for new material. His hands, still wet, pressed the clay back down into itself, folding the failed vessel inward, working the mass smooth again with the heel of his hand. The wheel kept turning. The clay flattened and rounded and became a lump once more — wet, shapeless, ready.

Then the potter began again. His hands closed around the clay and it rose. A different shape this time. Wider at the base, shorter in the neck. His thumbs opened the hollow slowly, the walls building thicker than before. Water ran in thin lines down the turning surface. The clay yielded where he pressed and held where he supported. The wheel hummed.

The Lord spoke. "O house of Israel, can I not do with you as this potter has done? Behold, like the clay in the potter's hand, so are you in my hand."

The wheel turned. The vessel sat whole on the stone, its rim smooth and even under the potter's wet fingers. He reached for a wire to cut it free.
