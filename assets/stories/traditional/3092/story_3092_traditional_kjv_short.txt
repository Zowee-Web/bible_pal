A messenger came unto David with urgent tidings: the hearts of the men of Israel were after Absalom. David at once told all the servants with him in Jerusalem, “Arise, and let us flee.” Unless they made speed to depart, Absalom might overtake them suddenly, bring evil upon them, and smite the city with the edge of the sword.

The king’s servants answered, “Behold, thy servants are ready to do whatsoever my lord the king shall appoint.” David went forth with all his household after him, though he left ten concubines to keep the house. The king and the people tarried in a place that was far off while his company passed on.

All David’s servants moved beside him. The Cherethites, the Pelethites, and six hundred Gittites who had come after him from Gath passed before the king. Ittai the Gittite was among them. David asked, “Wherefore goest thou also with us?” Ittai was a stranger and an exile who had come but yesterday. David told him to return unto his place, take back his brethren, and abide with the king. He sent him with mercy and truth.

Ittai would not leave David. He answered, “As the Lord liveth, and as my lord the king liveth,” wherever David should be, in death or life, there also would his servant be. David accepted his pledge and said, “Go and pass over.” Ittai crossed with all his men and all the little ones that were with him.

All the country wept with a loud voice as the people passed over. David crossed the brook Kidron, and the company went toward the way of the wilderness. The flight carried danger and sorrow, yet Ittai and his people remained with the king.

Servants, soldiers, little ones, and the king all went forth together as danger drew near.
