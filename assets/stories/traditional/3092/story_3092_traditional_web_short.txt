A messenger came to David with urgent news: the hearts of the men of Israel were following Absalom. David immediately told every servant with him in Jerusalem to arise and flee. If they delayed, he said, none of them would escape Absalom. He feared that Absalom would overtake them quickly, bring evil upon them, and strike the city with the edge of the sword.

The king’s servants answered that they were ready to do whatever their lord the king chose. David went out with his household following him, although he left ten concubines behind to keep the house. The king and the people stopped at Beth Merhak while his company moved past him.

David’s servants passed beside him. The Cherethites, Pelethites, and six hundred Gittites who had followed him from Gath also passed before the king. Among them was Ittai the Gittite. David asked why Ittai was going with them. Ittai was a foreigner and an exile who had arrived only recently. David urged him to return and stay with the king, taking his brothers back with him. David sent him away with words of mercy and truth.

Ittai refused to abandon David. He swore by the life of Yahweh and by the life of the king that wherever David might be, whether for death or for life, his servant would be there also. David accepted his answer and told him to pass over. Ittai crossed with all his men and all the little ones who were with him.

The whole country wept loudly as the people passed over. David crossed the brook Kidron, and the company moved beyond it toward the way of the wilderness. The departure carried fear and grief, yet Ittai and his household stayed beside the fleeing king.

The servants, soldiers, families, and king all moved out together as the danger drew near.
