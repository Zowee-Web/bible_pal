God spoke to the fathers through the prophets at many times and in many ways. In these last days, he has spoken by his Son. The Son is appointed heir of all things; through him God made the worlds. He is the radiance of God's glory and the very image of his substance, upholding all things by his powerful word. After purifying sins, he sat at the right hand of the Majesty on high.

God calls him Son and promises to be his Father. God commands the angels to worship the Son and speaks of the Son's eternal throne. The Son loves righteousness and hates iniquity. He laid earth's foundation; heavens are his handiwork. Creation grows old and changes like clothing, but he remains and his years do not fail. Angels are serving spirits sent for those who will inherit salvation; the Son sits at God's right hand until his enemies become his footstool.

Therefore the hearers must pay closer attention, lest they drift away. If the word spoken through angels brought just penalty for disobedience, there is no escape in neglecting so great a salvation. The Lord first spoke it, hearers confirmed it, and God bore witness by signs, wonders, works of power, and gifts of the Holy Spirit.

The world to come is not subjected to angels. Scripture asks why God remembers humanity, made a little lower than angels yet crowned with glory and honor. All things are subjected, though that completion is not yet seen. What is seen is Jesus: made lower because of suffering death, now crowned with glory and honor, tasting death for everyone by God's grace.

It was fitting that the one for whom and through whom all things exist should bring many children to glory and perfect the author of their salvation through sufferings. The one who sanctifies and those sanctified are from one, so Jesus is not ashamed to call them brothers.

Because the children share flesh and blood, he likewise partook of them. Through death he brought to nothing the one holding the power of death and delivered those enslaved by its fear. He helps Abraham's offspring, not angels. Made like his brothers, he became a merciful and faithful high priest, making atonement for the people's sins. Because he suffered when tempted, he can help those who are tempted.
