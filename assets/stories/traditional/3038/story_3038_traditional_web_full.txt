In the past, God spoke to the fathers through the prophets at many times and in various ways. At the end of these days, he has spoken by his Son. The Son is appointed heir of all things, and through him God made the worlds.

The Son is the radiance of God's glory and the very image of his substance. He upholds all things by the word of his power. After making purification for sins, he sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high. His inherited name is more excellent than that of the angels.

To no angel did God say, “You are my Son,” or promise to be his Father. When the firstborn is brought into the world, the angels are commanded to worship him. Angels are made winds and servants like flame, but the Son is addressed concerning an eternal throne and an upright scepter. He loves righteousness and hates iniquity and is anointed with gladness above his companions.

The Son is also addressed as the Lord who laid earth's foundation and formed the heavens. They will perish and grow old like a garment. They will be rolled up and changed, but he remains the same and his years do not fail. No angel is invited to sit at God's right hand until enemies become a footstool. Angels are serving spirits sent out for those who will inherit salvation.

Therefore the hearers must give greater attention to what they heard so that they do not drift away. If the word spoken through angels proved steadfast and every transgression received a just penalty, neglect of so great a salvation cannot offer escape. The Lord first spoke this salvation; those who heard confirmed it; God added witness through signs, wonders, various powers, and gifts of the Holy Spirit according to his will.

The world to come is not subjected to angels. A testimony asks, “What is man, that you think of him?” Humanity was made a little lower than angels, crowned with glory and honor, with all things placed in subjection. If all things are subjected, nothing is left outside; yet all things are not now visibly subjected.

What can be seen is Jesus. He was made a little lower than angels because of the suffering of death and is now crowned with glory and honor. By God's grace he tasted death for everyone. It was fitting for the one for whom and through whom all things exist, while bringing many children to glory, to perfect the author of their salvation through sufferings.

The one who sanctifies and those sanctified are all from one. For this reason, Jesus is not ashamed to call them brothers. Jesus speaks of declaring God’s name to his brothers, of praising him among the congregation, and of standing with the children God has given him.

Those children share flesh and blood. The Son likewise partook of the same, so that through death he might bring to nothing the one who held the power of death, the devil, and deliver those kept in lifelong bondage by fear of death. His help is directed not to angels but to Abraham's offspring.

Therefore he was obligated to be made like his brothers in all things. In this likeness and suffering he became a merciful and faithful high priest in matters pertaining to God, making atonement for the sins of the people. Because he himself suffered when tempted, he is able to help those who are tempted.
