That same day Jesus went out of the house and sat down beside the sea. Such large crowds gathered around him that he got into a boat and sat in it, while all the people stood on the shore. And he taught them many things in parables.

"Listen," he said. "A farmer went out to sow his seed. As he scattered it, some of the seed fell along the path, and the birds came and ate it up. Some fell on rocky ground, where there was little soil; it sprang up quickly, but when the sun rose it was scorched, and because it had no root, it withered away. Some fell among thorns, and the thorns grew up and choked it. But other seed fell on good soil, and it came up and produced a crop — a hundred times, or sixty, or thirty times what was sown." Then he said, "Whoever has ears to hear, let them hear."

Later his disciples came to him and asked, "Why do you speak to the crowds in parables?" He answered that the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven were being given to them, but not to everyone. To the one who has, more would be given, until he had abundance; but from the one who has not, even the little he had would be taken away. That was why he spoke in parables — because the crowds looked without ever seeing, and listened without ever understanding. Their hearts had grown dull; their ears had grown dull of hearing, and they had closed their own eyes, so that they would not see and turn and be healed. "But blessed are your eyes," he said, "for they see; and your ears, for they hear. Many prophets and righteous people longed to see what you see, and did not see it, and to hear what you hear, and did not hear it."
