That same day Jesus went out of the house and sat down beside the sea. Such large crowds gathered around him that he got into a boat and sat in it, while all the people stood together on the shore. And he taught them many things in parables.

"Listen," he said. "A farmer went out to sow his seed. As he scattered it across the field, some of the seed fell along the path, and the birds came and ate it up. Some fell on rocky ground, where there was little soil; it sprang up quickly, but when the sun rose it was scorched, and because it had no root, it withered away. Some fell among thorns, and the thorns grew up and choked it. But other seed fell on good soil, and it came up and produced a crop — a hundred times, or sixty, or thirty times what was sown." Then he said, "Whoever has ears to hear, let them hear."

Afterward his disciples came to him and asked, "Why do you speak to the crowds in parables?" He answered that the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven were being given to them, but not to everyone. To the one who has, more would be given, until he had abundance; but from the one who has not, even the little he had would be taken away. That was why he spoke to them in parables — because they looked without seeing, and listened without hearing or understanding. In them, he said, the words of the prophet Isaiah were coming true: "You will listen and listen, but never understand; you will look and look, but never perceive." For this people's heart had grown callous; they could barely hear, and they had closed their own eyes — for if they saw with their eyes and heard with their ears and understood with their hearts, they might turn, and be healed. "But blessed are your eyes," he said, "for they see, and your ears, for they hear. Many prophets and righteous people longed to see what you see, and did not see it, and to hear what you hear, and did not hear it."

"Now hear what the parable means," he told them. "The seed is the word of the kingdom. When someone hears it but does not understand it, the evil one comes and snatches away what was sown in his heart. That is the seed on the path.

"The seed on the rocky ground is the one who hears the word and receives it at once with joy — but it takes no root in him, and lasts only a while. When trouble or persecution comes because of the word, he quickly falls away.

"The seed among the thorns is the one who hears the word, but the worries of this life and the deceitfulness of riches crowd in and choke it, and it never bears fruit.

"But the seed on the good soil is the one who hears the word and understands it. This is the one who bears fruit and yields a harvest — a hundred times, or sixty, or thirty times what was sown."
