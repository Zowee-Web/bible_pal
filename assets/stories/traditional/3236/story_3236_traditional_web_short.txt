Abner son of Ner brought the servants of Ishbosheth, Saul's son, from Mahanaim to Gibeon. Joab son of Zeruiah came with David's servants. The two companies sat on opposite sides of Gibeon's pool.

Abner proposed that the young men rise and compete before them. Joab agreed. Twelve men for Benjamin and Ishbosheth faced twelve of David's servants. Each seized his opponent's head and drove a sword into his side. They fell together, and the place was called Helkath Hazzurim. A severe battle followed that day. David's servants defeated Abner and Israel's men.

Zeruiah's three sons were there: Joab, Abishai, and Asahel. Swift as a wild gazelle, Asahel pursued Abner without turning aside. Abner looked back and called his name. Asahel answered.

Abner told him to turn right or left, seize one of the young men, and take his armor. Asahel kept following. Again Abner urged him to turn away, asking why he should strike him down and how he could then face Asahel's brother Joab.

Asahel refused. Abner struck him in the body with the back end of his spear. It passed through and came out behind him. Asahel fell and died there. Everyone who reached the place where he lay stood still.

Joab and Abishai continued after Abner. At sunset they reached the hill of Ammah, before Giah on the wilderness road of Gibeon. Benjamin's men gathered behind Abner in one band on a hilltop.

Abner called to Joab, “Shall the sword devour forever?” He warned of bitterness at the end and asked how long Joab would wait before calling the men back from pursuing their brothers.

Joab answered, “As God lives, if you had not spoken, surely then in the morning the people would have gone away, and not each followed his brother.”

Then Joab blew the trumpet. The people halted. They stopped pursuing Israel, and the fighting ceased.

Abner and his men traveled all night through the Arabah, crossed the Jordan, passed through Bithron, and reached Mahanaim. Joab gathered his people after the pursuit. Nineteen of David's men were missing, besides Asahel. David's servants had killed three hundred sixty of Benjamin and Abner's men.

They carried Asahel to Bethlehem and buried him in his father's tomb. Joab and his men traveled through the night, and daybreak found them at Hebron.
