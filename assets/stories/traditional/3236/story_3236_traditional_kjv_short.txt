From Mahanaim, Abner the son of Ner led the servants of Ish–bosheth, Saul's son, toward Gibeon. There Joab the son of Zeruiah met them with David's servants. They took their places on either side of the pool.

At Abner's proposal, Joab consented that the young men should arise and play before them. Twelve of Benjamin, belonging to Ish–bosheth, went over against twelve of David's servants. Every man caught his fellow by the head and thrust his sword into his fellow's side. Together they fell. That place in Gibeon was named Helkath–hazzurim.

The battle that day was sore, and David's servants overcame Abner and the men of Israel. Joab, Abishai, and Asahel, the three sons of Zeruiah, were present. Asahel, light of foot as a wild roe, followed hard after Abner, turning neither right nor left.

Looking behind him, Abner asked, “Art thou Asahel?”

“I am,” he answered.

Abner bade him turn aside, lay hold upon one of the young men, and take his armour. Asahel would not turn. Abner warned him again: why should he smite Asahel to the ground, and how could he afterward lift his face to Joab his brother?

Still Asahel refused. With the hinder end of his spear, Abner struck him under the fifth rib. The spear came out behind him, and he fell dead upon that spot. Those who came to the place of his death stood still.

Joab and Abishai pursued Abner as far as the hill of Ammah, before Giah by the wilderness way of Gibeon. The sun was setting. The children of Benjamin gathered after Abner, forming one troop upon a hill.

“Shall the sword devour for ever?” Abner called. He spoke of the bitterness that would follow and asked when Joab would bid the people cease pursuing their brethren.

Joab answered, “As God liveth, unless thou hadst spoken, surely then in the morning the people had gone up every one from following his brother.”

Joab sounded a trumpet. All the people halted; the pursuit of Israel and the fighting ended.

Abner's men walked all night through the plain, crossed Jordan, and passed through Bithron to Mahanaim. Joab assembled his company. Nineteen of David's servants and Asahel were lacking. Of Benjamin and Abner's men, three hundred and threescore had been slain by David's servants.

Asahel was borne to Beth–lehem and laid in his father's sepulchre. Joab and his men journeyed all night and reached Hebron at the breaking of day.
