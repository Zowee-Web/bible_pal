David sat in his house. The roof of the palace was the same roof upon which he had walked the evening the trouble began. The window looked out over the same Jerusalem. He was a king in the city he had taken with his own hand, and he was no longer the man who had walked the hillsides with the harp.

Nathan came up from the gate. He had been sent. He stood before the king and said: there were two men in one city, the one rich, and the other poor. The rich man had exceeding many flocks and herds. But the poor man had nothing, save one little ewe lamb, which he had bought and nourished up. It grew up together with him and with his children. It did eat of his own meat, and drank of his own cup, and lay in his bosom, and was unto him as a daughter.

There came a traveller unto the rich man, the prophet said. And the rich man spared to take of his own flock and of his own herd to dress for the wayfaring man that was come unto him, but took the poor man's lamb, and dressed it for the man that was come to him.

David's anger was greatly kindled. As the Lord liveth, he said, the man that hath done this thing shall surely die. He shall restore the lamb fourfold, because he did this thing, and because he had no pity.

Nathan looked at him for a long moment.

Thou art the man, he said.

There was no sound in the room. David did not stand up. He did not call for the guard. He did not order the prophet's death. He sat very still while Nathan spake the word of the Lord — the gifts the Lord had given him, the wives and the kingdom and the house of Israel, and the sword that should not depart from his own house from this day on.

When the prophet finished, the king's mouth opened, and the words that came out were the only words he had left to say.

I have sinned against the Lord.

Nathan said: the Lord also hath put away thy sin. Thou shalt not die.

And the prophet turned, and went down the stairs the way he had come up. The king sat alone in the room, on the same roof, in the same city. The long judgment had begun, and the long mercy had begun. There was nothing he could do about either one of them now but receive them.
