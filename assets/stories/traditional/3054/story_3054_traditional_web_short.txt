Paul placed prayer first among the practices of God’s household. Petitions, intercessions, and thanksgiving were to be made for all people, including kings and others in authority, so believers might live quietly in godliness and reverence. Such prayer is good before God our Savior, who desires all people to be saved and to know the truth. There is one God and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, who gave himself as a ransom for all.

Prayer was to be joined with holy conduct. Men in every place were to pray without anger and doubting. Women were to adorn themselves modestly, giving greater attention to the good works fitting for those who profess godliness than to costly display. Paul directed a woman to learn quietly and with submission, and he did not permit her to teach or exercise authority over a man. He grounded those instructions in Adam and Eve, said that she would be saved through childbearing if they continued, and named faith, love, holiness, and sobriety.

Those who desired oversight desired a good work, so their character had to withstand examination. An overseer must be without reproach, the husband of one wife, temperate, sensible, modest, hospitable, and good at teaching. He must not be given to wine, violent, or greedy for money, but gentle and not quarrelsome. He had to manage his own house well, for one unable to manage it could not take care of God’s assembly. He must not be a new convert, lest pride lead him into the same condemnation as the devil. He also needed a good testimony from those outside, lest he fall into reproach and the devil’s snare.

Servants in the assembly likewise had to be reverent, not double-tongued, not addicted to much wine, and not greedy for money. They were to hold the mystery of faith with a pure conscience and be tested before serving. Their wives also were to be reverent, temperate, faithful, and not slanderers. The husband of one wife and sound household leadership were required of servants as well. Those who served well gained good standing and boldness in faith in Christ Jesus.

These instructions showed how people ought to conduct themselves in God’s house: the assembly of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth. At its center stood the great mystery of godliness—God was revealed in the flesh, justified in the spirit, seen by angels, preached among the nations, believed on in the world, and received up in glory.
