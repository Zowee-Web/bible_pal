Evening was come upon the Sea of Galilee. The water lay calm — a flat, silver surface stretching between the brown hills on either side. The last light of the day fell warm across the water. Fishing vessels rocked near the shore. Birds moved in low arcs above the surface, dipping and climbing.

Jesus and his disciples entered into a ship and put forth from the shore. They were crossing to the other side. The disciples took the oars and began to row, pulling the vessel out into the open water. The town behind them grew small. The shore fell away.

Jesus went into the hinder part of the ship. A pillow lay against the wooden frame. He laid down his head upon it. He was weary — weary from teaching, weary from the multitudes, weary from the long day. In a moment he was asleep. His breathing slowed. The ship rocked gently beneath him. The water lapped softly against the hull.

The disciples rowed in the failing light. The sky turned from blue to amber to a deep, heavy grey. The wind was light at first. Then it changed.

It came from the hills — sudden, violent, dropping off the high ground and smiting the surface of the lake. The water churned. Waves rose and beat against the ship. Spray flew across the bow. The vessel pitched, tilting sharply as each wave struck. Water poured over the sides. The oars availed little against the force of the wind.

The disciples laid hold upon the ropes and the sides of the ship. They bailed water with their hands and whatever they could find. The wind howled about them. The waves grew exceeding great. Water sloshed across the floor. The sky was near black.

And Jesus was yet asleep in the stern.

The disciples came to him and awoke him, saying, "Lord, save us: we perish!"

Jesus arose. He stood up in the ship, steadying himself. Water dripped about him. The wind tore at his garments. The waves crashed. He looked out upon the storm.

He rebuked the winds and the sea.

"Peace," he said. "Be still."

And there was a great calm. The wind ceased. The waves were laid flat. The surface of the lake became as glass. The ship steadied. The ropes went slack. The only sound was the dripping of water from the oars and the ragged breathing of the men aboard.

The disciples sat in the sudden stillness, soaked through, their hands yet gripping the sides. Their faces were pale.

Jesus turned unto them. "Why are ye fearful," he said, "O ye of little faith?"

They marvelled exceedingly, saying one to another, "What manner of man is this, that even the winds and the sea obey him?"
