Evening was come upon the Sea of Galilee. The water lay calm — flat and silver beneath the fading sky. Jesus and his disciples entered into a ship and put forth from the shore, making for the far side.

Jesus went unto the hinder part of the ship, where a pillow lay against the wooden frame. He laid down his head and fell asleep. He was weary. The ship rocked beneath him, and the water lapped softly against the hull.

The disciples rowed in the failing light. The sky turned from blue to amber to a deep and heavy grey. Then the wind rose.

It came sudden and violent, dropping down from the hills and smiting the surface of the lake. The waves rose and beat into the ship. Spray flew across the bow. The vessel pitched and rolled. Water poured over the sides. The wind howled about them, and the waves grew exceeding great. The sky was dark as night.

And Jesus was asleep.

The disciples came to him and awoke him, crying, "Lord, save us: we perish!"

Jesus arose. He stood in the rocking ship, the wind tearing at his garments, the waves crashing about him. He looked out upon the storm.

He rebuked the winds and the sea.

"Peace," he said. "Be still."

And there was a great calm. The wind ceased. The waves fell flat. The water grew smooth as glass. The ship steadied. The only sound was the dripping of water from the oars and the breathing of the men aboard.

The disciples sat in the sudden silence, soaking, their hands gripping the sides of the vessel. Their faces were pale.

Jesus turned unto them. "Why are ye fearful," he said, "O ye of little faith?"

They marvelled greatly, saying one to another, "What manner of man is this, that even the winds and the sea obey him?"
