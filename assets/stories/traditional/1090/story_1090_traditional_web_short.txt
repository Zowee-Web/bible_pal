Evening was falling over the Sea of Galilee. The water had been calm all day — a flat, silver-blue surface stretching between the hills on either side. Jesus and his disciples climbed into a boat and pushed off from the shore. They were crossing to the other side.

Jesus settled into the stern of the boat, where a cushion lay against the wooden frame. He was tired. He laid his head down and closed his eyes. Within moments, he was asleep. The boat rocked gently beneath him. The oars dipped and the shoreline fell away behind them.

The disciples rowed in the fading light. The sky turned from blue to amber to a deep, bruised gray. The air cooled. And then, without warning, the wind changed.

It came from the hills — sudden, violent, dropping down the slopes and slamming across the surface of the lake. The water churned. Waves rose and crashed against the side of the boat. Spray flew across the bow. The boat pitched and rolled, tipping sharply as each wave struck. Water began to pour over the sides.

The disciples grabbed the oars and the ropes and the edges of the boat. They bailed water with their hands. The wind howled around them, and the waves grew higher, and the boat groaned under the force of the storm. Water sloshed across the floor. The sky had gone nearly black.

And Jesus was still asleep.

The disciples stumbled to the stern. They shook him awake. "Lord, save us!" they cried. "We are perishing!"

Jesus opened his eyes. He stood up in the rocking boat, steadying himself against the rail. He looked out at the storm — the wind, the waves, the dark sky, the spray flying through the air.

He spoke to the wind and the sea.

"Peace," he said. "Be still."

And the wind stopped. The waves flattened. The surface of the lake went smooth, as though a hand had been laid across it. The boat steadied. The ropes went slack. The only sound was the water dripping from the oars and the quiet breathing of the men on board.

The disciples sat in the sudden silence, soaking wet, their hands still gripping the sides of the boat. They looked at one another. Their faces were pale.

Jesus turned to them. "Why are you afraid?" he said. "Do you still have no faith?"

They did not answer. They stared at him, and then at the calm water, and then at each other. A great fear came over them — not the fear of the storm, but something deeper and older.

"What kind of man is this," they whispered, "that even the wind and the sea obey him?"
