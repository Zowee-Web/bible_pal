The storm came from nowhere. One moment the water lay still; the next the vessel was filling. Thus doth fear sometimes work — not building by degrees, but arriving all at once, without warning, turning a quiet evening into a thing desperate.

What is striking is that Jesus was asleep. Not in some distant place. Not absent. Asleep — there in the ship, in the midst of the selfsame tempest. His peace depended not upon the weather.

The question the disciples asked afterward was the right one. Not "How did he do this?" but "What manner of man is this?" For what they had witnessed was not a feat or a device. It was something concerning who he was.

Sometimes the most fearful part of a storm is not the wind nor the waves. It is the silence ere someone speaketh — and the way all things change when at last they do.
