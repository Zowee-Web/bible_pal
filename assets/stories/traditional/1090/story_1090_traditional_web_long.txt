The Sea of Galilee lay calm in the late afternoon light. The water was flat and silver-blue, reflecting the brown hills that rose on either side. Near the shore, fishing boats rocked gently at their moorings, their hulls dark with tar, their nets draped over the gunwales to dry. The air smelled of fish and wet rope and the warm stone of the harbor wall. Children played along the water's edge, throwing pebbles and watching them skip. A few women sat on the rocks nearby, mending nets with quick, practiced hands.

Jesus and his disciples came down to the shore. He had been teaching all day — crowds pressing close, voices calling out, hands reaching. He was tired. His face showed it. His steps were slower than they had been in the morning.

They climbed into a boat — a broad, flat-bottomed fishing vessel, the kind common on the lake. The wood was smooth and dark from years of use. The disciples took the oars and pushed off from the shore. The town fell away behind them. The water opened up.

Jesus moved to the stern of the boat. A cushion lay against the wooden frame — a simple thing, rough cloth stuffed with straw. He laid his head on it, pulled his cloak around his shoulders, and closed his eyes. Within moments, he was asleep. His breathing slowed. His body settled into the rocking of the boat. The water lapped against the hull in a gentle, steady rhythm.

The disciples rowed. The sun moved lower. The sky turned from pale blue to gold to a deep, darkening gray. The hills on either side of the lake cast long shadows across the water. The air cooled. A few of the disciples talked quietly among themselves. Others watched the far shore grow slowly closer.

Then the wind changed.

It came from the east, off the hills — sudden and violent, dropping down the slopes and slamming across the surface of the lake like a fist. The calm water shattered. Waves rose out of nothing — short, steep, crashing against the side of the boat in rapid succession. Spray flew across the bow. The boat lurched sideways, then pitched forward, the prow digging into a wave and coming up streaming water.

The disciples scrambled. They grabbed the oars, the ropes, the edges of the boat. The wind howled. The waves grew higher. Water poured over the sides — not spray but solid sheets of lake water, flooding the floor of the boat. The hull groaned under the force. The mast, if there was one, bent and shuddered. The sky had gone nearly black. Lightning flickered somewhere in the distance, and the hills were gone — lost behind walls of rain and churning water.

The disciples bailed with their hands, with bowls, with whatever they could grab. Their clothes were soaked through. Their sandals slipped on the wet wood. The boat rolled so far to one side that the water rushed in from the gunwale. They were fishermen — men who knew the lake, who had grown up on its shores, who had weathered storms before. And they were terrified.

And Jesus was asleep in the stern.

He lay on the cushion, his cloak wrapped around him, his face still and peaceful. The boat pitched and rolled beneath him. Water splashed across his body. The wind screamed. And he slept on.

The disciples stumbled toward the stern, gripping the sides to keep from being thrown overboard. They reached him. They shook his shoulder. "Lord!" they shouted over the wind. "Save us! We are perishing!"

Jesus opened his eyes. He blinked once. Water dripped from the sides of the boat above him. The wind tore at his hair. He sat up slowly, then rose to his feet. The boat rolled, and he braced himself against the rail, his hand steady on the wood.

He looked out at the storm. He saw the waves — black and white-capped, crashing against the hull. He saw the sky — dark, swirling, heavy with rain. He saw the spray flying through the air, and the water sloshing across the floor, and the faces of his disciples — pale, wet, wide-eyed, trembling.

He spoke to the wind and the sea.

"Peace," he said. "Be still."

And everything stopped.

The wind died. The waves flattened. The surface of the lake smoothed itself out, as though a vast hand had been laid across it, pressing the water down until it was glass. The boat steadied. The ropes went slack. The groaning of the hull went quiet. The rain stopped. The sky began to lighten at the edges.

The silence was absolute.

The disciples sat where they were — soaking wet, breathing hard, their hands still gripping the sides of the boat. Water dripped from the oars. A fish flopped once on the wet floor of the hull and was still. No one spoke.

Jesus turned to them. His voice was not angry. It was not loud. But it carried across the silent boat with the same authority that had stilled the storm.

"Why are you afraid?" he said. "Do you still have no faith?"

No one answered. They looked at him. They looked at the water — flat, calm, impossibly still. They looked at one another.

A great fear came over them. Not the fear they had felt in the storm — not the fear of drowning, not the fear of the wind and the waves. This was something else. Something older. Something that went deeper than the body.

"What kind of man is this," they whispered to one another, "that even the wind and the sea obey him?"

The boat drifted on the quiet water. The far shore was visible now, dark against the clearing sky. The disciples picked up the oars and began to row — slowly, silently — toward land. Jesus settled back into the stern. The cushion was wet, and his clothes were soaked, but his face was calm. The lake was still. The wind was gone. And the only sound was the dip of the oars and the soft drip of water from the hull.
