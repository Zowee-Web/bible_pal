Evening was falling over the Sea of Galilee. The water was calm — a flat, silver-blue surface stretching between the brown hills on either side. The last light of the day lay warm across the water. Fishing boats bobbed near the shore. Birds moved in low arcs above the surface, dipping and climbing.

Jesus and his disciples climbed into a boat and pushed off from the shore. They were heading to the other side. The disciples took the oars and began to row, pulling the boat out into open water. The town behind them grew smaller. The shore fell away.

Jesus settled into the stern of the boat. A cushion lay against the wooden frame, and he laid his head on it. He was tired — tired from teaching, tired from the crowds, tired from the long days. Within moments, he was asleep. The boat rocked gently beneath him. The water lapped softly against the hull.

The disciples rowed in the fading light. The sky turned from blue to amber to a deep, bruised gray. The wind was light at first, just enough to fill the edges of the sail. Then it changed.

It came from the hills — sudden, violent, dropping off the high ground to the east and slamming across the surface of the lake. The water churned. The calm surface shattered into whitecaps. Waves rose and crashed against the side of the boat, throwing spray across the bow. The boat pitched sharply, tilting as each wave struck. Water poured over the sides. The oars were nearly useless against the force of the wind.

The disciples grabbed the ropes and the edges of the boat. They bailed water with their hands and with whatever they could find. The wind howled around them. The waves grew higher. The boat groaned and creaked under the force. Water sloshed across the floor, ankle-deep. The sky was nearly black. The far shore had vanished.

And Jesus was still asleep in the stern.

The disciples stumbled toward him, gripping the sides of the boat to keep from falling. They shook him. "Lord, save us!" they cried. "We are perishing!"

Jesus opened his eyes. He sat up, then stood, steadying himself against the rail. Water dripped from the boat around him. The wind tore at his clothing. The waves crashed and the spray flew. He looked out at the storm — the dark water, the white foam, the sky churning overhead.

Then he spoke.

"Peace," he said. "Be still."

And the wind stopped. The waves flattened. The surface of the lake went smooth, as though a great hand had been laid across it. The boat steadied. The ropes went slack. The creaking stopped. The only sound was water dripping from the oars and the quiet, ragged breathing of the men on board.

The disciples sat in the sudden stillness, soaking wet, their hands still white-knuckled on the sides of the boat. They looked at one another. Their faces were pale. Their hearts were pounding.

Jesus turned to them. His voice was gentle, but the question carried weight. "Why are you afraid?" he said. "Do you still have no faith?"

No one answered. They stared at him — at this man who had been asleep a moment ago, who had stood up in the middle of a storm and spoken to the wind as though it were a child — and a deep, quiet fear came over them. Not the fear of the storm. Something older. Something larger.

"What kind of man is this," they whispered to one another, "that even the wind and the sea obey him?"
