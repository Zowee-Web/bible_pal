Job was on the ash heap without his city.

He had been upon the heap for some time. The sores covered his body. He had been scraping at them with a piece of broken pottery, the same way he had been doing every morning since the day he had sat down there.

His three friends were with him. They had ceased their speaking, after their long words, because Job had answered them and they had no answer for that which he had said.

He lifted up his face out of the dust.

He spake unto them — and unto the air round about them, and unto the wall of the city behind them — words that he would have written down. Not for the dust of his own day. For another day, after he was gone.

"Oh that my words were now written! Oh that they were printed in a book!

That they were graven with an iron pen and lead in the rock for ever!

For I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth:

And though after my skin worms destroy this body, yet in my flesh shall I see God:

Whom I shall see for myself, and mine eyes shall behold, and not another;

though my reins be consumed within me."

Job said no more for a while.

His friends did not answer him.

The dust of the heap was upon his arms and upon his face. The piece of broken pottery was yet in his hand.

But he had said it. He had said it from where he sat. And the words went out from the ash heap toward a day that he could not yet see — and toward a Redeemer he could not yet name.
