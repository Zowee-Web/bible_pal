Job was on the ash heap outside his city.

He had been on the heap for some time. The sores covered his body. He had been scraping at them with a piece of broken pottery, the same way he had been doing every morning since the day he had sat down there.

His three friends were with him. They had stopped speaking, after their long words, because Job had answered them and they had no answer for what he had said.

He lifted his face out of the dust.

He said to them — and to the air around them, and to the wall of the city behind them — words that he meant to be written down. Not for the dust of his own day. For another day, after he was gone.

"Oh that my words were now written! Oh that they were inscribed in a book!

That with an iron pen and lead, they were engraved in the rock forever!

But as for me — I know that my Redeemer lives.

In the end, he will stand upon the earth.

After my skin is destroyed, then in my flesh shall I see God.

Whom I, even I, shall see on my side. My eyes shall see, and not as a stranger.

My heart is consumed within me."

Job said no more for a while.

His friends did not answer him.

The dust of the heap was on his arms and on his face. The piece of broken pottery was still in his hand.

But he had said it. He had said it from where he was sitting. And the words went out from the ash heap toward a day that he could not yet see — and toward a Redeemer he could not yet name.
