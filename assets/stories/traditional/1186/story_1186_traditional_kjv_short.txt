In the days when the prophet Habakkuk waited upon the Lord, he stood at his watch, and he set himself upon the rampart. He looked out and waited to see what the Lord would say unto him.

When the word of the Lord had come unto him, and he had heard the message of the coming day, he lifted up his voice in prayer. The morning was clear over the watchtower, and Habakkuk beheld the land.

He said, "O Lord, I have heard thy speech, and was afraid. I stand in awe of thy works."

He prayed, "O Lord, revive thy work in the midst of the years. In the midst of the years make known. In wrath remember mercy."

Then Habakkuk looked out upon the fields and the orchards, and he beheld the lean year that was coming. The vines were thin, and the figs had not yet come, and the flocks were few in the folds.

But he lifted up his voice and sang, "Although the fig tree shall not blossom, neither shall fruit be in the vines; the labour of the olive shall fail, and the fields shall yield no meat; the flock shall be cut off from the fold, and there shall be no herd in the stalls: Yet I will rejoice in the Lord, I will joy in the God of my salvation."

He said, "The Lord God is my strength, and he will make my feet like hinds' feet, and he will make me to walk upon mine high places."

Habakkuk stood upon the rampart, and the morning grew warm above the land. The wind moved across the fields where the harvest would be small that year. He folded his hands and waited upon the Lord, and he stirred not from his watch.

The words of his prayer were written down, that they might be sung of them that came after, and that the people might learn to wait upon the Lord in the lean years.

The watchtower stood quiet under the sky. Habakkuk abode in his place, and his eyes were toward the morning.
