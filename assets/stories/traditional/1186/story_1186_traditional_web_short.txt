In the days when the prophet Habakkuk waited upon the Lord, he stood at his watch, and he set himself upon the rampart. He looked out and waited to see what the Lord would say to him.

When the word of the Lord had come to him, and he had heard the message of the coming day, he lifted up his voice in prayer. The morning was clear over the watchtower, and Habakkuk looked out across the land.

He said, "I have heard your report, Lord. I stand in awe of your works."

He prayed, "Lord, revive your work in the middle of the years. In the middle of the years, make it known. In wrath, you remember mercy."

Then Habakkuk looked out at the fields and the orchards, and he saw the lean year that was coming. The vines were thin, and the figs had not yet come, and the flocks were few in the folds.

But he lifted up his voice and sang, "Though the fig tree doesn't flourish, and there is no fruit on the vines, though the labor of the olive fails, the fields yield no food, the flocks are cut off from the fold, and there is no herd in the stalls, yet I will rejoice in the Lord. I will be joyful in the God of my salvation."

He said, "The Lord, the Lord, is my strength. He makes my feet like deer's feet, and enables me to go in high places."

Habakkuk stood upon the rampart, and the morning grew warm above the land. The wind moved across the fields where the harvest would be small that year. He folded his hands and waited for the Lord, and he did not move from his watch.

The words of his prayer were written down, that they might be sung by those who came after, and that the people might learn to wait for the Lord in the lean years.

The watchtower stood quiet under the sky. Habakkuk remained at his place, and his eyes were toward the morning.
