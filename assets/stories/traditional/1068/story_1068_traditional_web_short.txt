Then Job answered the Lord.

"I know that you can do all things," he said. "No purpose of yours can be stopped."

He knelt in the dust, his robe torn, ashes still clinging to his arms. "You asked, 'Who is this that hides counsel without knowledge?' I spoke of things I did not understand. Things too wonderful for me, which I did not know."

His voice was low and rough. "I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear. But now my eye sees you."

He pressed his face toward the ground. "I despise myself," he said, "and repent in dust and ashes."

The Lord spoke then to Eliphaz the Temanite. "My anger burns against you and against your two friends. You have not spoken of me what is right, as my servant Job has."

The three men stood still, the wind pulling at their cloaks.

"Take seven bulls and seven rams," the Lord said, "and go to my servant Job. Offer up a burnt offering for yourselves. My servant Job will pray for you. I will accept his prayer and not deal with you according to your folly."

Eliphaz and Bildad the Shuhite and Zophar the Naamathite went and did what the Lord commanded. They brought the animals. They built the fire. The smoke rose thick and white against the sky. Job stood near the altar and prayed for them. And the Lord accepted Job's prayer.

After Job prayed for his friends, the Lord restored his fortunes. The Lord gave Job twice as much as he had before.

His brothers came. His sisters came. Everyone who had known him before came to his house. They ate bread with him. They showed him sympathy. They comforted him for all the trouble the Lord had brought upon him. Each one gave him a piece of silver and a gold ring.

He had fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, a thousand yoke of oxen, and a thousand female donkeys.

He had seven sons and three daughters. He named the first daughter Jemimah, the second Keziah, and the third Keren-happuch. Their father gave them an inheritance among their brothers.

Job lived a hundred and forty years after this. He saw his children and his children's children, four generations. Then Job died, old and full of days.
