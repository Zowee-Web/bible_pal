When the children of Israel had returned to Jerusalem from the exile, they gathered to begin the work of the house of the Lord. The walls of the city were broken down, and the courts of the old temple lay in ruins. But the builders began to lay the foundation, stone upon stone, on the place where the house of God had stood.

When the foundation of the temple of the Lord was laid, the priests put on their vestments with trumpets. The Levites, the sons of Asaph, took up their cymbals to praise the Lord, after the order of David king of Israel.

They sang one to another in praising and giving thanks to the Lord. They said, "For he is good. For his loving kindness endures forever toward Israel."

All the people shouted with a great shout when they praised the Lord, because the foundation of the house of the Lord was laid. The trumpets sounded, and the cymbals rang together, and the voices rose into the morning sky.

But many of the priests, and the Levites, and the heads of the fathers' houses, who were old men, had seen the first house in their youth. When the foundation of this house was laid before their eyes, they wept with a loud voice.

Many also shouted aloud for joy, so that the people could not discern the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping of the people. The young men shouted, and the old men wept, and the sounds rose together over the broken walls.

The noise was heard far away. It went out across the hill country, and the villages around Jerusalem heard the great cry, and they did not know if it was a cry of joy or a cry of sorrow.

But the foundation had been laid. The stones were set upon the ground where the first house had stood, and the people of Israel stood upon the place where the Lord had once dwelled.

The morning was bright over Jerusalem, and the trumpets sounded again before the sun rose to its height.
