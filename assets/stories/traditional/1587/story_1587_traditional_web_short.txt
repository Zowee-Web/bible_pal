Every year at the Passover feast, the governor would release to the crowd one prisoner, whomever the people asked for, and set him free before them all, whatever he had done. That year there was a notorious prisoner in the jail named Barabbas. So when the crowd had gathered before the judgment seat, Pilate put a choice to them. "Which do you want me to release to you — Barabbas, or Jesus who is called Christ?" For he knew well that it was out of envy they had handed Jesus over to him.

And while he was sitting there on the seat, his wife sent word to him. "Have nothing to do with that righteous man," she said. "I have suffered much in a dream today because of him."

But the chief priests and the elders moved through the crowd, from one group to the next, and persuaded them to ask for Barabbas, and to have Jesus destroyed. So when the governor asked them again, "Which of the two do you want me to release to you?" they answered, "Barabbas."

"Then what shall I do with Jesus who is called Christ?" Pilate said. And they all answered, "Let him be crucified."

"Why?" said the governor. "What evil has he done?" But they only cried out the louder, again and again, "Let him be crucified!"

When Pilate saw that he was getting nowhere, and that a riot was breaking out instead, he called for water and washed his hands before them all, where the whole crowd could see. "I am innocent of this man's blood," he said. "See to it yourselves." And all the people answered, "His blood be on us and on our children."

So he released Barabbas to them. But Jesus he had flogged, and handed him over to be crucified.
