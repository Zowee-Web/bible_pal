The famine had brought Jacob and his sons down into Egypt. The bread of his land had failed. The flocks had grown thin. Word had come from Egypt that there was corn to be bought there, and the brethren had gone, and they had returned, and they had told their father that Joseph was yet alive, and the spirit of the old man had revived within him. He had said, "Joseph my son is yet alive. I will go and see him before I die." And so the household had come down — Jacob and his sons and the wives and the little ones and the flocks and the herds — and Joseph had met them in Goshen, and the brethren had been brought before Pharaoh, and now Joseph brought in his old father.

And Joseph brought in Jacob his father, and set him before Pharaoh: and Jacob blessed Pharaoh.

And Pharaoh said unto Jacob, "How old art thou?"

And Jacob said unto Pharaoh, "The days of the years of my pilgrimage are an hundred and thirty years: few and evil have the days of the years of my life been, and have not attained unto the days of the years of the life of my fathers in the days of their pilgrimage."

And Jacob blessed Pharaoh, and went out from before Pharaoh.

And Jacob went forth from the court of Pharaoh, and Joseph led him back unto the land of Goshen. The old man sat down at the door of his tent, and the day grew long, and the evening came on, and the lamps were lighted. And so it was in the days when Jacob came down into Egypt, in the last years of his pilgrimage.
