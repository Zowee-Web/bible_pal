Moses had been upon the mountain forty days.

The cloud sat upon the summit. The fire moved in the cloud. The people had watched the mountain from below as long as they could endure to watch it, and then they had ceased to watch. They had eaten the manna. They had drawn the water. The days had grown long.

And they gathered themselves together unto Aaron at last, and said unto him: up, make us gods, which shall go before us; for as for this Moses, the man that brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him.

Aaron said unto them: break off the golden earrings, which are in the ears of your wives, of your sons, and of your daughters, and bring them unto me. They brake them off and brought them. He received them at their hand, and fashioned it with a graving tool, after he had made it a molten calf.

And they said: these be thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.

Aaron built an altar before it. He made proclamation, and said: tomorrow is a feast to the Lord. They rose up early on the morrow, and offered burnt offerings, and brought peace offerings. The people sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to play.

And the Lord said unto Moses upon the mountain: go, get thee down; for thy people, which thou broughtest out of the land of Egypt, have corrupted themselves. They have turned aside quickly out of the way which I commanded them: they have made them a molten calf, and have worshipped it.

I have seen this people, and, behold, it is a stiffnecked people. Now therefore let me alone, that my wrath may wax hot against them, and that I may consume them: and I will make of thee a great nation.

Moses let him not alone.

He stood upon the mountain, with the fire in the cloud, and he besought the Lord his God. Lord, why doth thy wrath wax hot against thy people, which thou hast brought forth out of the land of Egypt with great power, and with a mighty hand? Wherefore should the Egyptians speak, and say, for mischief did he bring them out, to slay them in the mountains, and to consume them from the face of the earth?

Turn from thy fierce wrath. Repent of this evil against thy people.

Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, thy servants, to whom thou swarest by thine own self, and saidst unto them, I will multiply your seed as the stars of heaven.

Moses stood between God and the people he was set over, and offered himself in their place. He knew not yet what should be done with that offer. He knew not yet what the people had done in detail. He stood there and besought.

And the Lord repented of the evil which he thought to do unto his people.

The mountain held its quiet. The cloud sat upon it. The fire moved in the cloud. And Moses began to come down. And so it was on the day Moses stood between God and Israel, on Mount Sinai, in the last hour of the forty days.
