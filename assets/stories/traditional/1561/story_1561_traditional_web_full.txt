Moses had been on the mountain forty days.

The cloud sat on the summit. The fire moved in the cloud. The people had watched the mountain from below for as long as they could stand to watch it, and then they had stopped watching. They had eaten the manna. They had drawn the water. The days had grown long. They had forgotten how long forty days felt, even though they had walked enough roads to know.

Aaron had been left in charge. He had not been given clear instructions. He had simply been told that Moses would be on the mountain a while. He had grown more uncertain as the days passed. He was not Moses. He had not been the one who stood at the bush. He had been the one who carried the words. He was uncertain.

The people came together to Aaron at last, and the request was put to him by enough of them at once that he could not easily refuse it.

Come, they said, make us gods, which shall go before us. As for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we don't know what has become of him.

It was not a small thing they asked. Aaron knew it was not a small thing. But the camp was large, and the pressure was real, and he was alone.

He said: take off the golden rings which are in the ears of your wives, of your sons, and of your daughters, and bring them to me. Perhaps he hoped, when he asked it, that they would refuse. They did not refuse. They brought the rings. He received what they handed him, and fashioned it with an engraving tool, and made it a molten calf.

They said: these are your gods, Israel, which brought you up out of the land of Egypt.

Aaron built an altar before it. He made proclamation, and said: tomorrow shall be a feast to Yahweh. He tried to keep the name. He tried to fold what they had done into something familiar. The people rose up early the next day, and offered burnt offerings, and brought peace offerings. They sat down to eat and to drink, and they rose up to play.

The noise of it carried.

Yahweh spoke to Moses on the mountain.

Go down, he said, for your people, whom you brought up out of the land of Egypt, have corrupted themselves. They have turned aside quickly out of the way which I commanded them. They have made themselves a molten calf and have worshiped it. They have sacrificed to it. They have said: these are your gods, Israel, which brought you up out of the land of Egypt.

I have seen these people. They are a stiff-necked people. Now therefore leave me alone, that my wrath may burn hot against them, and that I may consume them. And I will make of you a great nation.

It was an offer. It was the same offer that had been made to Noah, and to Abraham. The promise had been carried in a single family before. It could be carried in a single family again. Yahweh was telling Moses he could begin over with him.

Moses did not leave him alone.

He stood on the mountain, with the fire in the cloud, and he begged Yahweh his God.

Why does your wrath burn hot against your people, that you have brought out of the land of Egypt with great power and with a mighty hand? Why should the Egyptians speak, saying, he brought them out for evil, to kill them in the mountains, and to consume them from the surface of the earth?

Turn from your fierce wrath. Turn away from this evil against your people.

Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, your servants, to whom you swore by your own self, and said, I will multiply your offspring as the stars of the sky, and all this land that I have spoken of I will give to your offspring, and they shall inherit it forever.

He did not ask to be made a great nation. He did not accept the offer. He stood between God and the people he was responsible for, and offered himself in their place — he would say, a few verses on, that he was willing to be blotted out of the book that had been written if Yahweh would not forgive their sin. He had not yet said that, but he was on his way to it.

He did not yet know what would be done with the offer. He did not yet know what the people had done in detail. He stood there and pleaded.

And Yahweh repented of the evil which he had said he would do to his people.

The mountain held its quiet. The cloud sat on it. The fire moved in the cloud. The pleading had been heard. The judgment had been turned. The long mercy had been turned along with it. And Moses began to come down — the tablets in his hands, the people in his throat, the long descent ahead of him before he would see what waited at the bottom.

And so it was on the day Moses stood between God and Israel, on Mount Sinai, in the last hour of the forty days.
