Moses had been upon the mountain forty days.

The cloud sat upon the summit. The fire moved in the cloud. The people had watched the mountain from below as long as they could endure to watch it, and then they had ceased to watch. They had eaten the manna. They had drawn the water. The days had grown long. They had forgotten how long forty days felt, though they had walked enough roads to know.

Aaron had been left in charge. He had not been given clear instructions. He had simply been told that Moses should be on the mountain a while. He had grown more uncertain as the days passed. He was not Moses. He had not been the one who stood at the bush. He had been the one who bore the words. He was uncertain.

The people gathered themselves together unto Aaron at last, and the request was put unto him by enough of them at once that he could not easily refuse it.

Up, they said, make us gods, which shall go before us; for as for this Moses, the man that brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him.

It was no small thing they asked. Aaron knew it was no small thing. But the camp was large, and the pressure was real, and he was alone.

He said: break off the golden earrings, which are in the ears of your wives, of your sons, and of your daughters, and bring them unto me. Perhaps he hoped, when he asked it, that they should refuse. They refused not. They brake them off and brought them. He received them at their hand, and fashioned it with a graving tool, after he had made it a molten calf.

And they said: these be thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.

Aaron built an altar before it. He made proclamation, and said: tomorrow is a feast to the Lord. He sought to keep the name. He sought to fold what they had done into something familiar. The people rose up early on the morrow, and offered burnt offerings, and brought peace offerings. They sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to play.

The noise of it carried.

And the Lord said unto Moses upon the mountain:

Go, get thee down; for thy people, which thou broughtest out of the land of Egypt, have corrupted themselves. They have turned aside quickly out of the way which I commanded them: they have made them a molten calf, and have worshipped it, and have sacrificed thereunto, and said, these be thy gods, O Israel, which have brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.

I have seen this people, and, behold, it is a stiffnecked people. Now therefore let me alone, that my wrath may wax hot against them, and that I may consume them: and I will make of thee a great nation.

It was an offer. It was the same offer that had been made unto Noah, and unto Abraham. The promise had been borne in a single family before. It could be borne in a single family again. The Lord was telling Moses he could begin over with him.

Moses let him not alone.

He stood upon the mountain, with the fire in the cloud, and he besought the Lord his God.

Lord, why doth thy wrath wax hot against thy people, which thou hast brought forth out of the land of Egypt with great power, and with a mighty hand? Wherefore should the Egyptians speak, and say, for mischief did he bring them out, to slay them in the mountains, and to consume them from the face of the earth?

Turn from thy fierce wrath. Repent of this evil against thy people.

Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, thy servants, to whom thou swarest by thine own self, and saidst unto them, I will multiply your seed as the stars of heaven, and all this land that I have spoken of will I give unto your seed, and they shall inherit it for ever.

He asked not to be made a great nation. He accepted not the offer. He stood between God and the people he was set over, and offered himself in their place — he should say, a few verses on, that he was willing to be blotted out of the book that had been written if the Lord should not forgive their sin. He had not yet said it. He was on his way to it.

He knew not yet what should be done with the offer. He knew not yet what the people had done in detail. He stood there and besought.

And the Lord repented of the evil which he thought to do unto his people.

The mountain held its quiet. The cloud sat upon it. The fire moved in the cloud. The pleading had been heard. The judgment had been turned. The long mercy had been turned along with it. And Moses began to come down — the tables in his hands, the people in his throat, the long descent ahead of him before he should see what waited at the bottom.

And so it was on the day Moses stood between God and Israel, on Mount Sinai, in the last hour of the forty days.
