Moses had been on the mountain forty days.

The cloud sat on the summit. The fire moved in the cloud. The people had watched the mountain from below for as long as they could stand to watch it, and then they had stopped watching. They had eaten the manna. They had drawn the water. The days had grown long.

They came together to Aaron at last, and said: come, make us gods, which shall go before us. As for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we don't know what has become of him.

Aaron said to them: take off the golden rings which are in the ears of your wives, of your sons, and of your daughters, and bring them to me. They brought them. He received what they handed him, and fashioned it with an engraving tool, and made it a molten calf.

They said: these are your gods, Israel, which brought you up out of the land of Egypt.

Aaron built an altar before it. He made proclamation, and said: tomorrow shall be a feast to Yahweh. They rose up early the next day, and offered burnt offerings, and brought peace offerings. The people sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to play.

Yahweh spoke to Moses on the mountain. Go down, he said, for your people, whom you brought up out of Egypt, have corrupted themselves. They have turned aside quickly out of the way which I commanded them. They have made themselves a molten calf and have worshiped it.

I have seen these people. They are a stiff-necked people. Now therefore leave me alone, that my wrath may burn hot against them, and that I may consume them. And I will make of you a great nation.

Moses did not leave him alone.

He stood on the mountain, with the fire in the cloud, and he begged Yahweh his God. Why does your wrath burn hot against your people, that you have brought out of the land of Egypt with great power and with a mighty hand? Why should the Egyptians speak, saying, he brought them out for evil, to kill them in the mountains, and to consume them from the surface of the earth?

Turn from your fierce wrath. Turn away from this evil against your people.

Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, your servants, to whom you swore by your own self, and said, I will multiply your offspring as the stars of the sky.

Moses stood between God and the people he was responsible for, and offered himself in their place. He did not yet know what was going to be done with that offer. He did not yet know what the people had done in detail. He stood there and pleaded.

And Yahweh repented of the evil which he had said he would do to his people.

The mountain held its quiet. The cloud sat on it. The fire moved in the cloud. And Moses began to come down. And so it was on the day Moses stood between God and Israel, on Mount Sinai, in the last hour of the forty days.
