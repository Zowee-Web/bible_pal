When Samuel was old, he made his sons judges over Israel. But they did not walk in his ways. They turned aside after dishonest gain, took bribes, and perverted justice.

So all the elders of Israel gathered and came to Samuel at Ramah. "You are old," they said, "and your sons do not walk in your ways. Now appoint a king to judge us, like all the other nations."

It displeased Samuel, and he prayed to Yahweh. Yahweh answered him, "Listen to the people in all they say to you. They have not rejected you; they have rejected me from being king over them. As they have done from the day I brought them out of Egypt — forsaking me and serving other gods — so they are doing to you. Listen to them, but warn them solemnly, and tell them how the king who reigns over them will act."

So Samuel told the people who were asking for a king. "This is how your king will act," he said. "He will take your sons for his chariots and his horsemen. He will take your daughters to be perfumers and cooks and bakers. He will take the best of your fields and vineyards and olive groves and give them to his servants. He will take a tenth of your grain and your flocks, and you yourselves will become his servants. In that day you will cry out because of the king you have chosen, and Yahweh will not answer you."

But the people refused to listen. "No," they said. "We will have a king over us, so that we may be like all the nations, and our king may judge us and go out before us and fight our battles."

Samuel listened to all they said and repeated it to Yahweh. And Yahweh said, "Listen to them, and give them a king." Then Samuel said to the people of Israel, "Every one of you, go back to your own city."
