Solomon was the new king of Israel. He was young — very young to be a king. There were so many people to take care of. Every day, people came unto him and asked him to make hard decisions. Who was right? Who was wrong? What was just? Solomon did not always know the answer.

Solomon loved God. He worshipped and offered sacrifices at Gibeon. The smoke rose up thick and white against the sky.

One night, God appeared unto Solomon in a dream. The room about him glowed with warm, golden light. It was peaceful — more peaceful than aught Solomon had ever felt. God said, "Ask what I shall give thee."

Solomon could have asked for any thing. Heaps of gold. Long life. Victory over his enemies. Any thing at all.

But Solomon thought upon all the people he was to lead. He thought upon how hard it was to know the right thing to do. He held his peace a long moment. Then he spake.

"I am but a little child," said Solomon. "I know not how to go out or come in. Thy servant is in the midst of thy people which thou hast chosen — a great people that cannot be numbered. Give thy servant an understanding heart to judge thy people, that I may discern between good and bad."

Solomon had asked for wisdom. Not riches. Not fame. Not power. Only the ability to understand what was right and what was wrong, that he might take good care of God's people.

It pleased the Lord. The warm light in the room seemed to glow brighter. "Because thou hast asked this thing, and hast not asked for thyself long life, neither hast asked riches for thyself, nor hast asked the life of thine enemies, but hast asked for thyself understanding to discern judgment — I have done according to thy words."

God gave Solomon a wise and an understanding heart. None before him had been so wise, neither after him should any arise like unto him.

But God was not yet done. "And I have also given thee that which thou hast not asked," said God, "both riches and honour, so that there shall not be any among the kings like unto thee all thy days. And if thou wilt walk in my ways, I will lengthen thy days."

Solomon awoke. The dream was ended. The night was quiet and still about him. But all was changed. He had asked God for one thing — the right thing — and God had given him that and much more.
