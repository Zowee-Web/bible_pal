Solomon was the new king of Israel. He was young — very young to be a king. There were so many people to take care of. Every day, people came to him and asked him to make hard decisions. Who was right? Who was wrong? What was fair? Solomon did not always know the answer.

Solomon loved God. He worshipped and offered sacrifices at Gibeon. The smoke rose up thick and white against the sky.

One night, God appeared to Solomon in a dream. The room around him glowed with warm, golden light. It was peaceful — more peaceful than anything Solomon had ever felt. God said, "Ask what I should give you."

Solomon could have asked for anything. Piles of gold. A long life. Victory over his enemies. Anything at all.

But Solomon thought about all the people he was supposed to lead. He thought about how hard it was to know the right thing to do. He was quiet for a long moment. Then he spoke.

"I am just a little child," Solomon said. "I do not know how to go out or come in. Your servant is among your people which you have chosen — a great people that cannot be numbered. Give your servant an understanding heart to judge your people, that I may discern between good and evil."

Solomon had asked for wisdom. Not riches. Not fame. Not power. Just the ability to understand what was right and what was wrong, so he could take good care of God's people.

God was pleased. The warm light in the room seemed to glow brighter. "Because you have asked this thing, and have not asked for yourself long life, and have not asked for riches, and have not asked for the life of your enemies, but have asked for understanding to discern justice — I have done according to your words."

God gave Solomon a wise and understanding heart. No one before him had been so wise, and no one after him would be either.

But God was not finished. "I have also given you what you have not asked for," God said, "both riches and honor, so that there will not be any among the kings like you all your days. And if you walk in my ways, I will lengthen your days."

Solomon woke up. The dream was over. The night was quiet and still around him. But everything had changed. He had asked God for one thing — the right thing — and God had given him that and so much more.
