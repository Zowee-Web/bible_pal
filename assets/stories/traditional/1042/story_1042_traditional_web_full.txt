Solomon was the new king of Israel. His father David had been king before him — a great king, a brave warrior, a man after God's own heart. Now Solomon sat on the throne. He wore the crown. The people looked to him for answers.

But Solomon was young. Very young to be a king. The nation of Israel was enormous — more people than anyone could count. They had problems, arguments, needs. Every day, people came to Solomon and asked him to make decisions. Who was right? Who was wrong? What was fair? Solomon tried his best, but it was hard.

Solomon loved God. He worshipped at Gibeon, where the great altar stood. He offered a thousand burnt offerings on that altar — the smoke rose up thick and white against the sky. The sweet smell of the sacrifice filled the air.

That night, Solomon lay down to sleep. The palace was quiet. Oil lamps flickered low, casting soft shadows on the walls. A cool breeze came through the window and rustled the curtains.

And God appeared to him in a dream.

The presence of God filled the room with warm golden light. It was peaceful — more peaceful than anything Solomon had ever felt. There was no fear, no worry. Just God, close and real and gentle.

God spoke. "Ask what I should give you."

Think about that for a moment. The God who made the stars and the mountains and the seas was standing right there, saying: tell me what you want, and I will give it to you.

Solomon could have asked for gold — piles of it, enough to fill every room in the palace. He could have asked to live for a hundred years. He could have asked for power over every other nation. He could have asked for his enemies to be destroyed.

But Solomon did not ask for any of those things. He was quiet for a long moment.

Then he spoke. His voice was humble and small. "You have shown great loving kindness to your servant David my father," Solomon said, "because he walked before you in truth and in righteousness and in uprightness of heart. And you have kept for him this great loving kindness, that you have given him a son to sit on his throne."

Solomon took a breath. "Now, O Lord my God, you have made your servant king instead of David my father. But I am just a little child. I do not know how to go out or come in. Your servant is among your people which you have chosen — a great people that cannot be numbered or counted."

Then came his request. "Give your servant an understanding heart to judge your people, that I may discern between good and evil. For who is able to judge this great people of yours?"

Wisdom. That was what Solomon asked for. Not treasure. Not a long life. Not revenge on anyone. Just wisdom — the ability to tell right from wrong, to be fair, to take good care of all the people God had put in his hands.

God was pleased. The warm light in the room seemed to glow even brighter.

"Because you have asked this thing," God said, "and have not asked for yourself long life, and have not asked riches for yourself, and have not asked for the life of your enemies, but have asked for understanding to discern justice — I have done according to your words. I have given you a wise and understanding heart, so that there has been no one like you before, and after you none shall arise like you."

No king before Solomon had been this wise. No king after him would be either.

But God was not finished. "I have also given you that which you have not asked," God said, "both riches and honor, so that there will not be any among the kings like you all your days. And if you will walk in my ways, to keep my statutes and my commandments, as your father David walked, then I will lengthen your days."

Solomon opened his eyes. The dream was over. The room was dark and still. Outside, stars filled the sky like tiny candles. A night bird called from the garden.

He got up and went back to Jerusalem. He stood before the ark of the covenant of the Lord and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings. Then he made a feast for all his servants. The tables were full of bread and meat and fruit. Music played. People laughed and celebrated together.

The young king looked around and smiled. He did not need to be afraid of the big job ahead. God had given him everything he needed — and more than he ever thought to ask for.
