Solomon was the new king of Israel. His father David had been king before him — a great king, a brave warrior, a man after God's own heart. Now Solomon sat upon the throne. He wore the crown. The people looked unto him for answers.

But Solomon was young. Very young to be a king. The nation of Israel was great — more people than any could number. They had troubles, contentions, needs. Every day, people came unto Solomon and asked him to make decisions. Who was right? Who was wrong? What was just? Solomon did his best, but it was hard.

Solomon loved God. He worshipped at Gibeon, where the great altar stood. He offered a thousand burnt offerings upon that altar — the smoke rose up thick and white against the sky. The sweet savour of the sacrifice filled the air.

That night, Solomon laid him down to sleep. The palace was quiet. Oil lamps flickered low, casting soft shadows upon the walls. A cool breeze came through the window and stirred the curtains.

And the Lord appeared unto him in a dream.

The presence of God filled the room with warm golden light. It was peaceful — more peaceful than aught Solomon had ever felt. There was no fear, no care. Only God, near and real and gentle.

God spake. "Ask what I shall give thee."

Think upon that for a moment. The God which made the stars and the mountains and the seas stood right there, saying: tell me what thou wouldst have, and I will give it unto thee.

Solomon could have asked for gold — heaps of it, enough to fill every chamber in the palace. He could have asked to live a hundred years. He could have asked for power over every other nation. He could have asked for his enemies to be cut off.

But Solomon asked for none of these things. He held his peace a long moment.

Then he spake. His voice was humble and small. "Thou hast shewed unto thy servant David my father great mercy," said Solomon, "because he walked before thee in truth, and in righteousness, and in uprightness of heart with thee. And thou hast kept for him this great kindness, that thou hast given him a son to sit on his throne."

Solomon drew a breath. "And now, O Lord my God, thou hast made thy servant king instead of David my father. But I am but a little child. I know not how to go out or come in. And thy servant is in the midst of thy people which thou hast chosen — a great people, that cannot be numbered nor counted."

Then came his asking. "Give therefore thy servant an understanding heart to judge thy people, that I may discern between good and bad. For who is able to judge this thy so great a people?"

Wisdom. That was what Solomon asked for. Not treasure. Not long life. Not vengeance upon any. Only wisdom — the ability to tell right from wrong, to be just, to take good care of all the people God had put into his hands.

It pleased the Lord. The warm light in the room seemed to glow yet brighter.

"Because thou hast asked this thing," said God, "and hast not asked for thyself long life, neither hast asked riches for thyself, nor hast asked the life of thine enemies, but hast asked for thyself understanding to discern judgment — I have done according to thy words. I have given thee a wise and an understanding heart, so that there hath been none like thee before thee, neither after thee shall any arise like unto thee."

No king before Solomon had been so wise. No king after him should be either.

But God was not yet done. "And I have also given thee that which thou hast not asked," said God, "both riches and honour, so that there shall not be any among the kings like unto thee all thy days. And if thou wilt walk in my ways, to keep my statutes and my commandments, as thy father David did walk, then I will lengthen thy days."

Solomon opened his eyes. The dream was ended. The chamber was dark and still. Without, the stars filled the sky like small candles. A night bird called from the garden.

He arose and went unto Jerusalem. He stood before the ark of the covenant of the Lord and offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings. Then he made a feast for all his servants. The tables were laden with bread and meat and fruit. Music played. The people laughed and made merry together.

The young king looked round about and smiled. He need not fear the great task ahead. God had given him all he had need of — and more than ever he thought to ask.
