In the days when King Darius reigned over the kingdom, he set governors and satraps over his realm, appointing one hundred and twenty to rule and three high officials above them. Among these officials, Daniel distinguished himself by his excellent spirit. The king saw his wisdom and skill and considered setting him over the whole kingdom. This stirred jealousy among the other officials. They searched for any fault in Daniel, any error or corruption in the way he handled matters of the kingdom, but they found none. He was faithful, and no negligence or corruption was found in him.

Knowing they could find no fault against Daniel concerning the affairs of the kingdom, the officials plotted together and said, “We shall not find anything against this Daniel unless we find it concerning the law of his God.” They assembled and came before King Darius with their plan. “O King Darius, live forever,” they said. “All the officials of the kingdom, the governors, counselors, and satraps have consulted together to establish a royal statute and make a firm decree. That whoever petitions any god or man for thirty days, except you, O king, shall be cast into the den of lions. Now, O king, establish the decree and sign the writing, so that it cannot be changed, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot be altered.” The king, not suspecting the scheme, signed the written decree.

Daniel, when he knew the writing was signed, went into his house. His windows in his upper chamber were open toward Jerusalem. He knelt upon his knees three times a day and prayed and gave thanks before his God, just as he always had done. The officials watched carefully and found Daniel making prayer and supplications before his God. Quickly, they went before the king and reminded him, “O king, have you not signed a decree that every man who makes petition to any god or man within thirty days, except to you, O king, shall be cast into the den of lions?” The king answered, “This thing is true, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot be revoked.” Then they said, “That Daniel, who is one of the captives from Judah, pays no attention to you, O king, or the decree. He makes his prayer three times a day.”

When the king heard these words, he was deeply distressed and set his heart to deliver Daniel. He labored until the going down of the sun to rescue him. But the officials gathered together and said to the king, “Know, O king, that it is a law of the Medes and Persians, that no decree or statute which the king establishes may be changed.” Then the king commanded, and Daniel was brought and cast into the den of lions. The king spoke with a troubled heart, “Your God whom you serve continually, he will deliver you.” A stone was brought and laid upon the mouth of the den. The king sealed it with his own signet and those of his nobles so that the purpose toward Daniel could not be changed.

The night in the palace was filled with unease. The king passed the cold hours in fasting, refusing any entertainment, and sleep fled from him. The den below was dark and deep, stone walls surrounding Daniel, and fierce lions prowled restlessly. Yet God sent his angel to shut the mouths of the lions, and Daniel was unharmed through the long, anxious night.

At the break of day, as the first rays of light slipped into the city, King Darius rose in haste and hurried to the den of lions. Approaching, his voice trembled as he called out, “Daniel, servant of the living God, has your God, whom you serve continually, been able to deliver you from the lions?” Then from within the den came Daniel’s voice, strong and unharmed, “O king, live forever. My God sent his angel, and has shut the lions’ mouths so that they have not hurt me, because I was found innocent before him. Also before you, O king, I have done no harm.” Greatly relieved and filled with joy, the king commanded that Daniel be taken up out of the den. When Daniel was brought out, no injury was found upon him, for he had trusted in his God.

The king then commanded, and those men who had maliciously accused Daniel were brought, along with their families, and were cast into the den. Before they reached the bottom, the lions overpowered them and broke all their bones in pieces. Afterward, King Darius wrote to all peoples and nations in every language who dwell on the earth: “Peace be multiplied to you. I make a decree, that in all the dominion of my kingdom men are to fear and tremble before the God of Daniel. For he is the living God, enduring forever. His kingdom will not be destroyed; his dominion shall be to the end. He delivers and rescues; he works signs and wonders in heaven and on earth, who has delivered Daniel from the power of the lions.” So Daniel prospered during the reign of Darius and the reign of Cyrus the Persian.
