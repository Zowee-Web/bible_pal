In the grand city of Babylon, beneath vaulted ceilings and behind stone walls, the palace of King Darius bustled with life and purpose. King Darius, ruler over an immense realm, appointed one hundred twenty satraps to oversee his kingdom. Over these, he set three high officials to ensure the king’s interests were protected. Among the three, Daniel stood out, an exile from Judah, respected and wise. His exceptional spirit and faithfulness drew the king’s favor, and soon Darius planned to appoint Daniel over the whole realm. Word of the king’s intention spread quickly, and whispers passed among the officials and governors. Some looked upon Daniel’s advancement with envy, troubled that an outsider would rise above the native Babylonians.

Those men, eager to diminish Daniel, searched for fault in his service to the king. Day after day, they examined his records, sought error in his judgments, listened for careless words, yet found no fault. Daniel was neither corrupt nor negligent; his work was above reproach. Frustrated, the officials gathered in private and spoke in hushed tones. “We shall never find any charge against Daniel unless we find it against him concerning the law of his God.” The men knew that Daniel’s steadfast devotion to the Lord of Israel guided every action and prayer of his day.

Soon a plot took form. The officials and satraps approached King Darius with words of flattery and unity. “O King Darius, live forever,” they said as they bowed low before him. “All the high officials of the kingdom, the prefects, and the satraps, the counselors and the governors, have consulted together that the king should establish a statute and enforce an interdict, that whoever makes petition to any god or man for thirty days, except to you, O king, shall be cast into the den of lions. Now, O king, establish the interdict and sign the document, so that it cannot be changed, according to the law of the Medes and the Persians, which does not pass away.” The king, flattered and trusting his advisors, signed the decree. The edict became law, unchangeable and sealed with his authority.

Daniel, when he learned that the document had been signed, made no change to his routine. Each day, as was his custom from his youth, he went to his upper chamber. The windows were opened toward Jerusalem, and Daniel knelt upon the cool stones three times a day. He prayed and gave thanks before his God, unwavering despite the risk. Through the lattice, the sun cast its glow upon him as he spoke quietly to the Lord, his prayers carrying like gentle breath upon the drifting air.

But the officials, waiting for this act, gathered as one and rushed to Daniel’s quarters. From the hallway of the palace, through parted curtains, they saw Daniel on his knees in supplication. Their eyes hardened with satisfaction, and they entered at once, gathering the evidence they needed. Without delay, they approached the king with their charge. “O king, have you not signed an interdict, that anyone who prays to any god or man except to you, O king, shall be cast into the den of lions?” Darius responded, “The thing stands fast, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot be revoked.”

The officers pressed on. “Daniel, who is one of the exiles from Judah, pays no attention to you, O king, or to the interdict that you have signed, but makes his prayer three times a day.” At these words, King Darius was greatly distressed. He valued Daniel above others and worked all day to deliver him. The heavy weight of the law pressed hard. Sunset drew near, and with it, the men returned, reminding the king that no interdict or statute the king establishes could be altered. Bound by the decree, King Darius was powerless before his own law.

The king gave the command, and Daniel was brought and cast into the den of lions. The ancient, iron-studded door to the pit creaked open, and the chill of evening swept over Daniel as he was lowered down. The cries and growls of hungry lions echoed below, their shadows flickering in the torchlight. Before the stone was rolled across the entrance, King Darius called down to Daniel, his voice heavy with sorrow and reluctant hope. “Your God, whom you serve continually, he will deliver you.” The officials pushed the stone over the mouth of the den and sealed it with the king’s signet and with the signets of his lords, ensuring that no hand might interfere.

Night settled upon the palace like a shroud. In the king’s chambers, sleep fled from Darius. He refused food and the sound of music, his heart weighted by concern for Daniel. In the stillness, he paced, waiting for dawn. Down in the den, among the scattered bones and cold stones, Daniel waited in peace. Though the breath of the lions was hot, and their bodies pressed around him, they did no harm. For an angel of God descended, silent and unseen, standing between Daniel and the jaws of danger. Through the long hours, the mouths of the lions remained shut, and Daniel, faithful and brave, trusted his God as the night wore on.

With the first light of morning, King Darius rose, hope and apprehension warring within him. Hastily, he hurried to the den and called out in a sorrowful voice, “Daniel, servant of the living God, has your God, whom you serve continually, been able to deliver you from the lions?” From within the pit came Daniel’s calm reply, “O king, live forever. My God sent his angel, and shut the lions’ mouths, and they have not hurt me, because innocence was found in me before him. Also before you, O king, I have done no harm.”

Relief and wonder flooded Darius. He ordered Daniel taken up out of the den. When Daniel emerged, no wound was found upon him, for he had trusted in his God. The officials who had accused Daniel stood nearby. In the presence of all, the king gave orders, and those men, along with their families, were cast into the den of lions. Before they reached the bottom of the pit, the lions overpowered them and broke all their bones.

After these events, King Darius wrote to all the peoples, nations, and languages that dwelled in all the earth, proclaiming the greatness of the God of Daniel. “Peace be multiplied to you. I make a decree, that in all the dominion of my kingdom men tremble and fear before the God of Daniel, for he is the living God, and enduring forever, and his kingdom will not be destroyed. His dominion shall last to the end. He delivers and rescues, he works signs and wonders in heaven and in earth, who has delivered Daniel from the power of the lions.”

So Daniel prospered during the reign of Darius, and in the reign of Cyrus the Persian.

And though the memories of the lions’ den lingered, Daniel met each new day with quiet gratitude. Hope stirred among the people, who saw that steadfast faith could outlast any night, and trust might grow strong, even within exile’s shadow.
