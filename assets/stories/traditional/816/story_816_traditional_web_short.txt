In the days when Darius was king, Daniel served with wisdom and integrity in the courts of Babylon. The king set over his kingdom a hundred and twenty leaders, and above them he appointed three high officials, one of whom was Daniel. Because of Daniel’s excellent spirit, the king considered setting him over the whole realm. This stirred up envy among the other officials. They looked for a fault in Daniel’s service, but he was faithful. No error or corruption could be found in him. So these men devised a plan, saying, “We shall not find any occasion against this Daniel, unless it is concerning the law of his God.”

They approached King Darius with flattery. “O King Darius, live forever!” they said. “All the officials, prefects, and governors have agreed that you should establish an ordinance. For thirty days, whoever asks a petition of any god or man besides you, O king, shall be cast into the den of lions.” Pleased by these words, King Darius signed the decree, making it law according to the Medes and Persians, which cannot be changed.

When Daniel learned that the writing was signed, he went to his house. In his upper room, with its windows open toward Jerusalem, he knelt down three times a day, prayed and gave thanks before his God, as he had always done. The officials watched and soon found Daniel praying. They hurried to the king and accused him, “Daniel, who is one of the captives from Judah, pays no attention to you, O king, or the decree you have signed.”

When King Darius heard these words, he was deeply distressed. He valued Daniel and tried until sundown to deliver him. Yet the law stood firm. The officials pressed him: “Know, O king, that the law cannot be changed.” So the king gave the command, and Daniel was brought and thrown into the den of lions. The king spoke, “Your God whom you serve continually, He will deliver you.” A stone was laid over the den, and the king sealed it with his own signet and that of his lords.

That night, King Darius could not eat nor sleep. At dawn, he hurried to the den. Calling out in anguish, he said, “Daniel, servant of the living God, has your God delivered you from the lions?” Daniel answered, “O king, live forever! My God sent His angel and shut the lions’ mouths. They have not hurt me, for I was found blameless before Him, and also before you, O king.”

With joy, the king ordered Daniel lifted from the den. No harm was found on him, because he believed in his God. Then, by the law, those men who had accused Daniel were cast into the lions’ den and were destroyed. King Darius wrote to all peoples, declaring that Daniel’s God endures forever. Daniel prospered in the reign of Darius and Cyrus the Persian, shining with courage and steadfast faith in the courts of kings.
