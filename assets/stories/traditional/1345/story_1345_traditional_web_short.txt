Jacob had crossed the Jordan with his wives and his children and his flocks. He had sent messengers ahead to his brother Esau in the land of Seir, and the messengers had returned with word that Esau was coming to meet him, and with him four hundred men. And Jacob was greatly afraid, and was distressed; and he divided the people that were with him into two companies; and then he stood alone in the camp, and prayed.

Jacob said, "God of my father Abraham, and God of my father Isaac, Yahweh, who said to me, 'Return to your country, and to your relatives, and I will do you good,'

I am not worthy of the least of all the loving kindnesses, and of all the truth, which you have shown to your servant; for with just my staff I crossed over this Jordan; and now I have become two companies.

Please deliver me from the hand of my brother, from the hand of Esau: for I fear him, lest he come and strike me, and the mothers with the children.

You said, 'I will surely do you good, and make your offspring as the sand of the sea, which can't be counted because there are so many.'"

And Jacob remained where he had been standing. The night that was coming had not yet come; the brother that was coming had not yet come; the wrestling that would happen had not yet happened. But the prayer had been spoken, and the promise had been laid before the One who had given it; and Jacob waited where he had been standing, on the near side of the Jabbok, with the two companies of his household behind him. And so it was in the days when Jacob crossed over the Jordan toward the land of his brother Esau.
