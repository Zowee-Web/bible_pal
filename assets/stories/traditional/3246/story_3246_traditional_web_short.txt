Moses told Israel how he had sent messengers from the wilderness of Kedemoth to Sihon, king of Heshbon. They carried words of peace. Moses asked permission to cross Sihon’s land on the highway, turning neither right nor left.

He offered money for food to eat and water to drink, asking only to pass through on foot. He named the children of Esau dwelling in Seir and the Moabites dwelling in Ar as those who had done this for him. His request was to continue until he crossed the Jordan into the land Yahweh their God was giving them.

But Sihon refused to let Israel pass. Moses said that Yahweh their God had hardened Sihon’s spirit and made his heart obstinate, so that he might deliver Sihon into their hand, as had now happened.

Then Moses recounted Yahweh’s words to him: “Behold, I have begun to deliver up Sihon and his land before you. Begin to possess, that you may inherit his land.”

Sihon came out with all his people to fight Israel at Jahaz. Yahweh their God delivered him before them, Moses said, and Israel struck Sihon, his sons, and all his people.

They took all his cities at that time. Moses described how they utterly destroyed every inhabited city, with the women and the little ones, leaving no one remaining. They took only the livestock for themselves as plunder, together with the plunder from the cities they had captured.

Moses traced their advance from Aroer on the edge of the Arnon valley, and from the city within the valley, as far as Gilead. No city was too high for them; Yahweh their God delivered all before them.

Yet there were places Israel did not approach. Moses named the land of the children of Ammon, all the banks of the river Jabbok, and the cities of the hill country. They did not come near wherever Yahweh their God had forbidden them.
