Moses rehearsed before Israel how he had sent messengers from the wilderness of Kedemoth unto Sihon, king of Heshbon. His message was peaceable: let him pass through the land by the high way, turning neither to the right hand nor to the left.

He would pay money for meat to eat and for water to drink, asking only passage on his feet. The children of Esau dwelling in Seir and the Moabites dwelling in Ar had done thus unto him. He sought passage until he should cross Jordan into the land which the Lord their God was giving them.

Sihon, however, would not let Israel pass by him. Moses declared that the Lord their God had hardened Sihon’s spirit and made his heart obstinate, to deliver him into their hand, as appeared that day.

Moses then repeated what the Lord had said unto him: “Behold, I have begun to give Sihon and his land before thee: begin to possess, that thou mayest inherit his land.”

With all his people, Sihon came forth to fight at Jahaz. The Lord their God delivered him before Israel, Moses said. They smote Sihon, his sons, and all his people.

At that time they took all his cities. Moses told how they utterly destroyed the men, women, and little ones of every city, leaving none to remain. Only the cattle were taken for a prey unto themselves, together with the spoil of the cities they had captured.

Their conquest reached from Aroer by the brink of the river Arnon, and from the city by the river, even unto Gilead. Moses declared that not one city had been too strong for them. The Lord their God had delivered all unto them.

But Israel had not entered the land of the children of Ammon. Neither had they approached any place of the river Jabbok, nor the cities in the mountains, nor any place which the Lord their God had forbidden them.
