Jehoash charged the priests concerning all the money of the dedicated things brought into the house of the Lord. It included the money of every one that passeth the account, the money that every man was set at, and whatsoever came into any man’s heart to bring. Every priest was to receive it of his acquaintance, and the priests were to repair the breaches of the house wheresoever a breach was found.

Yet in the three and twentieth year of king Jehoash, the priests had not repaired the breaches. The king called Jehoiada and the other priests and asked why the work remained undone. He ordered them to receive no more money of their acquaintance, but to deliver it for the breaches of the house. The priests consented both to cease receiving the people’s money and to cease directing the repairs.

Jehoiada took a chest and bored a hole in its lid. He set it beside the altar, on the right side as one came into the house of the Lord. The priests who kept the door put therein all the money brought into the house.

When much money was seen in the chest, the king’s scribe and the high priest came up. They put the money into bags and told it. The money, being told, was delivered into the hands of those who had oversight of the work. These men laid it out to the carpenters and builders who wrought upon the house, to masons and hewers of stone, and to buy timber and hewed stone. Thus the money met all the charges of repairing the breaches.

None of this money was used to make bowls of silver, snuffers, basons, trumpets, or vessels of gold or silver. It was given unto the workmen, and the house of the Lord was repaired therewith. The supervisors were not required to reckon for the money delivered into their hands to bestow on the workers, for they dealt faithfully.

The trespass money and the sin money were kept apart from this repair system. They were not brought into the house of the Lord; they belonged unto the priests.
