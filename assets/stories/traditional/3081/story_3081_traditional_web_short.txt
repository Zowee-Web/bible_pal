Jehoash spoke to the priests about the money brought into Yahweh’s house. This included the money of the holy things, current money, the money assessed for each person, and whatever anyone chose to bring. The priests were to receive these gifts from their donors and use them to repair every damaged place in the house.

By the twenty-third year of King Jehoash, however, the priests had not repaired the damage. The king summoned Jehoiada the priest and the other priests. He asked why the damaged places remained unrepaired. He then changed the arrangement: the priests were to take no more money from their treasurers, but were to deliver the money for the repair of the house. The priests agreed that they would no longer receive money from the people or manage the repairs themselves.

Jehoiada took a chest, bored a hole in its lid, and placed it beside the altar, on the right side of the entrance into Yahweh’s house. The priests who guarded the threshold put into the chest all the money brought to the house.

Whenever they saw that the chest held much money, the king’s scribe and the high priest came. They put the money in bags and counted it. The weighed money was handed to those who oversaw the work on Yahweh’s house. These supervisors paid the carpenters and builders, the masons and stone cutters. They also bought timber and cut stone for the damaged places and paid every expense required for the repairs.

The money brought into the house was not used to make silver cups, snuffers, basins, trumpets, or vessels of gold or silver. It went to the workers, and with it they repaired Yahweh’s house. No accounting was demanded from the men entrusted to pay the workers, because they dealt faithfully.

The money for trespass offerings and sin offerings followed a different course. It was not brought into Yahweh’s house for the repairs; it belonged to the priests.
