In the spring, when kings go out to war, David sent Joab and all Israel against the Ammonites. But David stayed in Jerusalem.

One evening he rose and walked on the roof of his house, and from there he saw a woman bathing. She was very beautiful. He asked who she was, and was told: Bathsheba, the wife of Uriah the Hittite, one of his own soldiers away at the war. David sent messengers and took her. He lay with her, and she returned to her house. Afterward she sent him word: I am with child.

So David sent to the front for Uriah, and asked him how the war went, and told him to go down and rest at his own house. But Uriah would not go home. He slept at the palace gate among the servants. "The ark is in a tent," he told the king, "and Israel and Joab are camped in the open fields. Shall I go to my house to eat and drink and lie with my wife? As you live, I will not do this thing." David kept him another day and made him drunk, and still Uriah slept at the gate.

In the morning David wrote a letter to Joab, and sent it in Uriah's own hand. Put him where the fighting is fiercest, it said, and draw back, so that he is struck down and dies. Joab set Uriah at the strongest point of the wall. The defenders came out, and Uriah fell with the rest.

When the messenger brought the news, David sent his word back to Joab. "Don't let this trouble you. The sword devours one as well as another. Press the attack."

Bathsheba mourned for her husband. When the mourning was over, David brought her to his house, and she became his wife and bore him a son.

But the thing that David had done displeased Yahweh.
