In the spring, when kings march out to war, David sent Joab and his servants and all Israel against the Ammonites. They struck them down and laid siege to Rabbah. But David stayed in Jerusalem.

One evening he rose from his bed and walked on the roof of his house. From there he saw a woman bathing, and she was very beautiful. He sent to ask about her, and the answer came back: she was Bathsheba, the daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite — one of the king's own soldiers, away at the siege. David sent messengers and took her. She came to him, and he lay with her, and she returned to her house.

Then she sent him word. "I am with child."

So David sent to Joab at the front: send me Uriah the Hittite. When Uriah came, the king asked how Joab was, and how the troops fared, and how the war went. Then he told him to go down to his house and wash his feet. A gift from the king followed him out the door.

But Uriah did not go home. He slept at the palace gate among the king's servants. They told David, and David sent for him. "Haven't you come from a journey? Why didn't you go down to your house?"

Uriah answered plainly. "The ark is in a tent. Israel and Judah are camped in the open fields. My lord Joab and his servants are sleeping on the bare ground. Shall I go to my house, to eat and drink and lie with my wife? As surely as you live, I will not do this thing."

David kept him one more day. He called him to eat and drink at his own table, and made him drunk — and still Uriah would not go home. He lay down at the gate with the servants of his lord.

In the morning David wrote a letter to Joab, and sent it in Uriah's own hand. Put Uriah where the fighting is fiercest, the letter said; then draw back from him, so that he is struck down and dies.

Joab was watching the city. He set Uriah at the place where the strongest defenders held the wall. The men of the city came out and fought, and some of David's soldiers fell. Uriah the Hittite was among the dead.

Joab sent a messenger to the king with word of the battle, and told him how to answer if David's anger rose that they had gone so near the wall: at the end, only add that Uriah the Hittite was dead also. The messenger came and delivered it all. And David gave him his reply for Joab. "Don't let this thing trouble you. The sword devours one as well as another. Press the attack against the city, and overthrow it." Encourage him, he said.

When Uriah's wife heard that her husband was dead, she mourned for him. And when the mourning was past, David sent and brought her to his house. She became his wife, and bore him a son.

But the thing that David had done displeased Yahweh.
