At the time when kings go forth to battle, David sent Joab and all Israel against the children of Ammon. But David tarried still at Jerusalem.

In an eveningtide he arose and walked upon the roof of his house, and from thence he saw a woman washing herself. She was very beautiful to look upon. He enquired after her, and one said, Is not this Bathsheba, the wife of Uriah the Hittite, one of the king's own men, away at the war? And David sent messengers, and took her; and he lay with her, and she returned unto her house. And afterward she sent and told him, saying, I am with child.

So David sent for Uriah from the battle, and demanded how the war prospered, and said, Go down to thy house, and wash thy feet. But Uriah went not down. He slept at the door of the king's house among the servants. The ark, and Israel, and Joab, said he, abide in the open fields; shall I go into mine house, to eat and to drink, and to lie with my wife? As thou livest, I will not do this thing. David kept him yet a day, and made him drunk; and still he went not down to his house.

In the morning David wrote a letter to Joab, and sent it by the hand of Uriah. Set him in the forefront of the hottest battle, it said, and retire ye from him, that he may be smitten, and die. Joab assigned Uriah unto a place where the valiant men were. The men of the city came out, and Uriah fell with the rest.

When the messenger brought the tidings, David sent word again unto Joab. Let not this thing displease thee, for the sword devoureth one as well as another. Make thy battle more strong.

The wife of Uriah mourned for her husband. And when the mourning was past, David fetched her to his house, and she became his wife, and bare him a son.

But the thing that David had done displeased the Lord.
