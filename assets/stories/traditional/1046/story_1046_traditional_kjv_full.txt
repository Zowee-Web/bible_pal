In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.

Before any thing was — before the sun, before the mountains, before a single flower or fowl — there was God. And God purposed to make a wondrous thing.

The earth was without form, and void. Darkness was upon the face of the deep. Then the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters, hovering gently as a bird over her nest.

And God spake.

"Let there be light."

Light burst forth into the darkness. It was warm and bright and beautiful. God beheld the light, and saw that it was good. He divided the light from the darkness. He called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. And the evening and the morning were the first day.

On the second day, God said, "Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters." And God made the firmament — a great wide dome of blue, stretched from one end of the world to the other. He divided the waters which were under the firmament from the waters which were above the firmament. And the evening and the morning were the second day.

On the third day, God said, "Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear." The waters drew back, swirling and rushing, and up arose the dry ground — hills and valleys and great mountains with rocky peaks. God called the dry land Earth, and the gathering together of the waters called he Seas.

But God had not yet finished the third day. "Let the earth bring forth grass, herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit," said he. And the bare brown ground brake forth into life. Green grass pushed up out of the soil. Wildflowers opened their petals — yellow and red and purple and white. Tall trees stretched toward the heavens, heavy with figs and olives and pomegranates. The whole earth bore the savour of fresh and green and newborn things. And God saw that it was good.

On the fourth day, God made the sun — a great burning lamp of light which made the daytime bright and warm. He made the moon — a circle of silver that shone gently by night. And he made the stars. Thousands of thousands of them, sparkling across the darkness as jewels strewn upon dark cloth. God set them all in their places. And the evening and the morning were the fourth day.

On the fifth day, God turned unto the water and the firmament. "Let the waters bring forth abundantly the moving creature that hath life, and let fowl fly above the earth." The seas came alive with movement. Fishes darted through the waters — silver ones and striped ones and great ones with wide mouths. Great whales rose unto the surface and blew mist into the air. Dolphins leaped among the waves.

Above, the heavens filled with fowl. Eagles soared on wide wings. Sparrows flicked between the branches. Pelicans plunged into the sea and came up with fish in their beaks. The air was full of songs and cries and the rushing sound of wings. "Be fruitful, and multiply," said God. And the evening and the morning were the fifth day.

On the sixth day, God said, "Let the earth bring forth the living creature after his kind." And so it came to pass. Lions padded through the tall grass, their golden manes catching the sunlight. Bears lumbered through cool forests. Harts leaped across green meadows. Conies hopped into burrows. Frogs croaked beside the rivers. Lizards sunned themselves on warm flat stones. Every kind of creature that walketh or creepeth — God made them all.

Then God spake a new thing. A thing different from all that had been before. "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness."

So God created man in his own image. Male and female created he them. He blessed them. "Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth," said he. "Behold, I have given you every herb and every tree for meat."

God stepped back and beheld all that he had made. The light and the dark. The firmament and the sea. The flowers and the fruit trees. The sun blazing and the stars twinkling. The fishes leaping and the fowl singing. The beasts running and playing. And the people — made in his own image, standing in the midst thereof.

And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good.

And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.
