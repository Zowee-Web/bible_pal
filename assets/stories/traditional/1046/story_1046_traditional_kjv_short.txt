In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth. The earth was without form, and void. Darkness was upon the face of the deep. The Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.

And God said, "Let there be light." And there was light. It burst forth bright and warm — the very first light that ever was. And God saw the light, that it was good. He divided the light from the darkness. He called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. That was the first day.

On the second day, God made the firmament — a wide blue dome stretched from one end of the world to the other. Water beneath, sky above.

On the third day, God gathered the waters together unto one place that the dry land might appear. Hills and mountains arose up out of the deep. He called the dry land Earth, and the gathering together of the waters called he Seas. Then God said, "Let the earth bring forth grass, herb yielding seed, and the tree yielding fruit." And up from the ground came green grass, wildflowers of every colour, and fruit trees heavy with figs and pomegranates and apples. The whole earth bore the savour of fresh and newborn things. And God saw that it was good.

On the fourth day, God made two great lights — the sun to rule the day, and the moon to rule the night. He made the stars also. Thousands upon thousands of them, scattered across the darkness like shining sand. God set them all in their places.

On the fifth day, God filled the seas with every living creature — whales and dolphins and fish of every kind. He filled the heavens with fowl. Eagles soaring on high, sparrows darting between the branches, herons standing still beside the water. "Be fruitful, and multiply!" said God.

On the sixth day, God made the beasts. Lions with manes of gold. Bears padding through the wood. Conies leaping in the grass. Cattle grazing in the meadows. Lizards in the warm rocks. Every kind of creature that walketh or creepeth upon the earth.

Then God spake a thing that was special. "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness." So God created man in his own image. Male and female created he them. He blessed them. "Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth," said he. "Behold, I have given you every herb and every tree for meat."

God stepped back and beheld all that he had made. All of it — the light, the firmament, the seas, the herbs, the sun and moon and stars, the fishes and the fowl and the beasts, and the people standing in the midst thereof.

And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good.
