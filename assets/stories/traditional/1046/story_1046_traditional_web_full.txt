In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.

Before anything existed — before the sun, before the mountains, before a single flower or bird — there was God. And God decided to make something wonderful.

The earth was formless and empty. Darkness covered the face of the deep. Then the Spirit of God moved over the surface of the waters, hovering gently like a bird over a nest.

And God spoke.

"Let there be light."

Light exploded into the darkness. It was warm and bright and beautiful. God looked at the light and saw that it was good. He separated the light from the darkness. He called the light Day and the darkness Night. There was evening and there was morning — the first day.

On the second day, God said, "Let there be an expanse in the middle of the waters." God made the sky — a great wide dome of blue, stretching from one edge of the world to the other. He separated the water below from the sky above. There was evening and morning — the second day.

On the third day, God said, "Let the waters be gathered together and let dry land appear." The waters pulled back, swirling and rushing, and up rose the dry ground — hills and valleys and tall mountains with rocky peaks. God called the dry land Earth and the gathered waters Seas.

But God was not finished with the third day. "Let the earth produce grass, herbs, and fruit trees," he said. And the bare brown ground burst into life. Green grass pushed up through the soil. Wildflowers opened their petals — yellow and red and purple and white. Tall trees stretched toward the sky, heavy with figs and olives and pomegranates. The whole earth smelled fresh and green and new. God saw that it was good.

On the fourth day, God made the sun — a great burning ball of light that turned the daytime bright and warm. He made the moon — a silver circle that glowed gently at night. And he made the stars. Millions of them, sparkling across the darkness like diamonds scattered on dark cloth. God set them all in their places. There was evening and morning — the fourth day.

On the fifth day, God turned to the water and the sky. "Let the waters swarm with living creatures, and let birds fly above the earth." The oceans came alive with movement. Fish darted through the water — silver ones and striped ones and fat ones with wide mouths. Great whales rose to the surface and blew mist into the air. Dolphins leaped through the waves.

Above, the sky filled with birds. Eagles soared on wide wings. Sparrows flicked between branches. Pelicans dove into the sea and came up with fish in their beaks. The air was full of songs and calls and the rushing sound of wings. "Be fruitful and multiply," God said. There was evening and morning — the fifth day.

On the sixth day, God said, "Let the earth produce living creatures." And so it happened. Lions padded through tall grass, golden manes catching the sunlight. Bears lumbered through cool forests. Deer leaped across green meadows. Rabbits hopped into burrows. Frogs croaked by the rivers. Lizards sunned themselves on warm flat stones. Every kind of creature that walks or crawls — God made them all.

Then God said something new. Something different from everything before. "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness."

God created man in his own image. Male and female he created them. He blessed them. "Be fruitful, multiply, fill the earth," he said. "I have given you every herb and every tree for food."

God stepped back and looked at everything he had made. The light and the dark. The sky and the sea. The flowers and the fruit trees. The sun blazing and the stars twinkling. The fish leaping and the birds singing. The animals running and playing. And the people — made in his own image, standing in the middle of it all.

God saw everything that he had made, and it was very good.

There was evening and there was morning — the sixth day.
