Ben Hadad king of Syria gathered his army. Thirty-two kings joined him with horses and chariots. He went up against Samaria, besieged it, and fought against it. His messengers entered the city and told Ahab king of Israel that Ben Hadad claimed Ahab’s silver, gold, wives, and best children. Ahab answered that he and all he possessed belonged to Ben Hadad.

The messengers returned with a greater demand. Ben Hadad would send servants the next day to search Ahab’s house and the houses of his servants. They would take away whatever was pleasant in Ahab's eyes.

Ahab summoned the elders of the land and described both demands. The elders and all the people told him not to listen or consent. Ahab sent word that he would fulfill the first demand but could not accept the second.

Ben Hadad replied with an oath, boasting that Samaria would not provide enough dust for handfuls for all his followers. Ahab answered that one putting on armor should not boast like one taking it off. Ben Hadad heard the answer while drinking with the allied kings in the pavilions. He ordered his servants to prepare to attack the city.

A prophet approached Ahab and declared Yahweh’s word: the great multitude would be delivered into Ahab’s hand that day, so Ahab would know that he was Yahweh. Ahab asked by whom. The prophet named the young men of the provincial princes. When Ahab asked who would begin the battle, the prophet answered that Ahab would.

Ahab mustered two hundred thirty-two young men of the princes and then seven thousand Israelites. They went out at noon while Ben Hadad and the thirty-two kings were drinking themselves drunk. The young men went first. When Ben Hadad heard that men were leaving Samaria, he commanded that they be taken alive whether they came for peace or war.

The young men and the army following them came out of the city. Each struck his opponent. The Syrians fled, and Israel pursued. Ben Hadad escaped on a horse with horsemen. The king of Israel went out, struck the horses and chariots, and inflicted a great slaughter on the Syrians.
