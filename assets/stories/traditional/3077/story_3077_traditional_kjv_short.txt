Ben–hadad king of Syria gathered all his host together. Thirty and two kings were with him, with horses and chariots. He went up against Samaria, besieged it, and warred against it. His messengers came unto Ahab king of Israel and declared that Ben–hadad claimed Ahab’s silver, gold, wives, and goodliest children. Ahab answered that he and all he had were Ben–hadad’s.

The messengers came again with a greater demand. Ben–hadad would send his servants on the morrow to search Ahab’s house and the houses of his servants. Whatsoever was pleasant in Ahab's eyes, they would put in their hand and take away.

Ahab called all the elders of the land and told them both demands. The elders and all the people bade him neither hearken nor consent. Ahab sent word that he would perform the first demand, but this later thing he could not do.

Ben–hadad answered with an oath, boasting that the dust of Samaria would not suffice for handfuls for all who followed him. Ahab replied that he who girdeth on his harness should not boast as he that putteth it off. Ben–hadad heard the message while drinking with the kings in the pavilions, and he commanded his servants to set themselves in array against the city.

A prophet came unto Ahab and delivered the word of the Lord: the great multitude would be given into Ahab’s hand that day, and he would know that he was the Lord. Ahab asked by whom. The prophet named the young men of the princes of the provinces. When Ahab asked who would order the battle, the answer was, “Thou.”

Ahab numbered two hundred and thirty-two young men of the princes, and after them seven thousand of the children of Israel. They went out at noon while Ben–hadad and the thirty and two kings were drinking themselves drunk. The young men went out first. When Ben–hadad heard that men had come out of Samaria, he commanded that they be taken alive, whether they came for peace or for war.

The young men and the army that followed them came forth from the city. They slew every one his man. The Syrians fled, and Israel pursued them. Ben–hadad escaped on a horse with horsemen. The king of Israel went out, smote the horses and chariots, and slew the Syrians with a great slaughter.
