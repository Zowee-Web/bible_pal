When Rachel had borne Joseph, Jacob sought leave of Laban to depart unto his own place and country. He asked for the wives and children for whom he had served, calling Laban to witness the service he had rendered.

Laban entreated him to tarry. He said that he had learned by experience how the Lord had blessed him for Jacob's sake. Jacob was to name his wages, and Laban would give them.

Jacob spoke of the cattle entrusted to him. Before he came, Laban had little; now it was increased unto a multitude. The Lord had blessed Laban since Jacob's coming. Jacob asked when he should provide for his own house also.

“What shall I give thee?” Laban asked.

Jacob would take no gift. He proposed to feed and keep the flock again if Laban accepted his terms. That day Jacob would pass through the flock, removing the speckled and spotted cattle, the brown among the sheep, and the spotted and speckled goats. Of such would his hire be. In time to come, those markings would answer for his righteousness when his wages came before Laban. Goats without the appointed markings and sheep that were not brown, found with Jacob, would be reckoned stolen.

Laban assented. He himself removed the ringstraked and spotted he goats, the speckled and spotted she goats, all that bore white, and the brown sheep. These he delivered to his sons. Laban set three days' journey between himself and Jacob, who fed the remaining flocks.

Taking rods of green poplar, hazel, and chesnut, Jacob pilled white strakes into them. He set the rods in the gutters of the watering troughs before the cattle as they came to drink, that they might conceive there. They conceived before the rods and brought forth young that were ringstraked, speckled, and spotted.

Jacob set the lambs apart and turned the faces of the flocks toward the ringstraked and the brown in Laban's flock. His own flocks he kept separate. When the stronger cattle conceived, he laid the rods before their eyes in the gutters, that they might conceive among them. For the feeble cattle he put no rods there.

Thus the feebler were Laban's and the stronger Jacob's. Jacob increased exceedingly, possessing much cattle, maidservants and menservants, camels and asses.
