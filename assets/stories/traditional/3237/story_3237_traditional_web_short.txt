After Rachel gave birth to Joseph, Jacob asked Laban to let him return to his own country. He wanted his wives and children, for whom he had served. Laban knew the service he had given.

Laban asked him to stay. He said he had divined that Yahweh had blessed him for Jacob's sake, and he offered to pay whatever wages Jacob named.

Jacob reminded Laban how little livestock he had possessed before Jacob came, and how it had grown into a multitude. He said Yahweh had blessed Laban wherever Jacob turned. When, he asked, would he provide for his own household?

Laban asked what he should give him. Jacob proposed no gift. Instead, he would continue tending the flock under an agreement: the speckled and spotted animals, the black sheep, and the spotted and speckled goats would be his wages. He proposed passing through the flock that day to remove them. Their markings would answer for his honesty whenever Laban inspected his hire. Any goat without those markings, or any sheep that was not black, found with Jacob would count as stolen.

Laban agreed. That day Laban removed the streaked and spotted male goats, the speckled and spotted females, every goat with white in it, and the black sheep. He placed them with his sons and set three days' journey between himself and Jacob. Jacob tended the rest of Laban's flocks.

Jacob took rods of fresh poplar, almond, and plane tree. He peeled them in streaks, exposing the white beneath, and placed them opposite the flocks in the watering troughs. The animals conceived when they came to drink. Before the rods they conceived, and their young were streaked, speckled, and spotted.

Jacob separated the lambs. He turned the flocks toward the streaked animals and all the black ones in Laban's flock, keeping his own droves apart from Laban's. Whenever the stronger animals conceived, he laid the rods before their eyes at the troughs so they would conceive among them. When the animals were feeble, he left the rods out.

The feebler animals became Laban's, and the stronger became Jacob's. Jacob increased greatly, with large flocks, female and male servants, camels, and donkeys.
