The Lord said unto Samuel, "How long wilt thou mourn for Saul, seeing I have rejected him from reigning over Israel? Fill thine horn with oil, and go. I will send thee to Jesse the Bethlehemite, for I have provided me a king among his sons."

Samuel stirred not at once. Fear held him.

"How can I go?" said he. "If Saul hear it, he will kill me."

The concern was no idle thing. Saul was king. Saul was dangerous. The anointing of a new king whilst the present one yet reigned could be read in but one way.

The Lord said, "Take an heifer with thee, and say, I am come to sacrifice to the Lord. Call Jesse to the sacrifice, and I will shew thee what thou shalt do. Thou shalt anoint unto me him whom I name unto thee."

Samuel obeyed. He filled his horn with oil and journeyed unto Bethlehem. The town was small. Word moved swiftly.

The elders came forth to meet him, and they did tremble. A prophet came not without cause. When God was at work, things were altered.

"Comest thou peaceably?" they asked.

"Peaceably," said Samuel. "I am come to sacrifice unto the Lord. Sanctify yourselves, and come with me to the sacrifice."

He sanctified Jesse and his sons and bade them come to the offering. The fire was built. The beast was brought. And then the true purpose of Samuel's coming began.

When they were come, Samuel looked upon Eliab, the firstborn. He was tall. He stood upright, with broad shoulders and the bearing of a man accustomed to command. Samuel looked on him and felt sureness settle in his breast. Surely the Lord's anointed stood before him.

But the Lord said unto Samuel, "Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature, because I have refused him. For the Lord seeth not as man seeth. Man looketh on the outward appearance, but the Lord looketh on the heart."

The words cut through Samuel's reckoning. He set Eliab aside.

Jesse called his second son, Abinadab. The young man came forth and stood before the prophet. Samuel studied him, hearkened, waited. The word came quietly: "Neither hath the Lord chosen this."

Jesse called Shammah. He passed before Samuel. Not this one.

Then the fourth son came forward. Not this one. The fifth. Not this one. The sixth. Not this one. The seventh son of Jesse drew near and stood before the aged prophet, and Samuel hearkened, and the word was the same. Not this one.

Seven sons. Seven times the Lord said nay. The temper of the gathering had shifted from expectation to bewilderment. The sacrifice waited. The fire smoked. Jesse stood with his seven sons, every one weighed, every one passed over.

Samuel turned unto Jesse. "Are here all thy children?"

Jesse considered. He seemed to weigh whether the question demanded account of a son he had not thought worthy to bring.

"There remaineth yet the youngest," said he. "And behold, he keepeth the sheep."

The youngest. Left behind. Tending the flock upon the hillside whilst his elder brethren stood before the prophet of God at the sacrifice. Not called. Not considered.

Samuel said, "Send and fetch him. For we will not sit down till he come hither."

The words bore weight. The sacrifice would tarry. The prophet would tarry. All things ceased until the absent son was brought.

Jesse sent a servant. The minutes passed. The brethren stood near the altar, looking one upon another. Samuel stood apart, the horn of oil at his side, saying nothing. The fire crackled. A thin line of smoke bent before the wind.

Then the lad came in from the field.

He was ruddy — browned by the sun, with the flush of open life upon his countenance. His eyes were bright and clear. He was withal of a beautiful countenance, and goodly to look to. He bore the smell of the pasture — wool and grass and sweat and open air. His hands were rough with labour. His garments were plain, the garments of a shepherd, not of one arrayed for sacrifice. He stood before Samuel, the youngest son, called from the margins of his own house.

The Lord said, "Arise, anoint him, for this is he."

Samuel tarried not. He took the horn of oil and stepped forth. He poured it upon the lad's head in the midst of his brethren — the same brethren who had stood before the prophet and been passed over one after another. The golden oil ran down from the crown of the youngest son's head. It ran upon his brow and caught in his hair. The anointing was done before them all, beyond question.

And the Spirit of the Lord came upon David from that day forward.
