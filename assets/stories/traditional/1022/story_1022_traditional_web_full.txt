The Lord said to Samuel, "How long will you mourn for Saul, since I have rejected him as king over Israel? Fill your horn with oil and go. I am sending you to Jesse the Bethlehemite, for I have provided for myself a king among his sons."

Samuel did not move at once. Fear held him.

"How can I go?" he said. "If Saul hears about it, he will kill me."

The concern was real. Saul was king. Saul was dangerous. The anointing of a new king while the old one still reigned could be read only one way.

The Lord said, "Take a heifer with you and say, 'I have come to sacrifice to the Lord.' Invite Jesse to the sacrifice, and I will show you what you are to do. You will anoint for me the one I name to you."

Samuel obeyed. He filled his horn with oil and traveled to Bethlehem. The town was small. Word moved quickly.

The elders of the town came to meet him, and they trembled. A prophet did not arrive without cause. When God acted, things changed.

"Do you come peaceably?" they asked.

"Peaceably," Samuel said. "I have come to sacrifice to the Lord. Consecrate yourselves and come with me to the sacrifice."

He consecrated Jesse and his sons and invited them to the offering. The fire was built. The animal was brought. And then the true purpose of Samuel's visit began.

When they arrived, Samuel saw Eliab, Jesse's firstborn. He was tall. He stood straight, with broad shoulders and the bearing of a man accustomed to authority. Samuel looked at him and felt certainty settle in his chest. Surely the Lord's anointed stood before him.

But the Lord said to Samuel, "Do not look at his appearance or at the height of his stature, because I have rejected him. For the Lord does not see as man sees. Man looks at the outward appearance, but the Lord looks at the heart."

The words cut through Samuel's assumption. He set Eliab aside.

Jesse called his second son, Abinadab. The young man came forward and stood before the prophet. Samuel studied him, listened, waited. The answer came quietly: "The Lord has not chosen this one either."

Jesse called Shammah. He passed before Samuel. Not this one.

Then the fourth son came forward. Not this one. The fifth. Not this one. The sixth. Not this one. The seventh son of Jesse approached and stood before the old prophet, and Samuel listened, and the answer was the same. Not this one.

Seven sons. Seven times the Lord said no. The tension in the gathering had shifted from anticipation to confusion. The sacrifice waited. The fire smoked. Jesse stood with his seven sons, all examined, all passed over.

Samuel turned to Jesse. "Are these all the sons you have?"

Jesse paused. He seemed to weigh whether the question required honesty about a son he had not thought worth bringing.

"There remains yet the youngest," he said. "He is out keeping the sheep."

The youngest. The one left behind. The one tending the flock on the hillside while his older brothers stood before the prophet of God at the sacrifice. He had not been called. He had not been considered.

Samuel said, "Send and bring him. We will not sit down until he comes here."

The words carried weight. The sacrifice would wait. The prophet would wait. Everything stopped until the missing son arrived.

Jesse sent a messenger. The minutes passed. The brothers stood near the altar, glancing at one another. Samuel stood apart, the horn of oil at his side, saying nothing. The fire crackled. A thin line of smoke bent in the wind.

Then the boy came in from the field.

He was ruddy — sun-darkened, with the flush of outdoor life on his skin. His eyes were bright and clear. He was good-looking. He carried the smell of the pasture — wool and grass and sweat and open air. His hands were rough from work. His clothes were plain, the clothes of a shepherd, not of a man dressed for a sacrifice. He stood before Samuel, the youngest son, summoned from the margins of his own family.

The Lord said, "Arise, anoint him, for this is the one."

Samuel did not hesitate. He took the horn of oil and stepped forward. He poured the oil on David's head in the midst of his brothers — the same brothers who had stood before the prophet and been passed over one by one. The golden oil ran down from the crown of the youngest son's head. It ran over his forehead and caught in his hair. The anointing was public, unmistakable.

And the Spirit of the Lord rushed upon David from that day forward.
