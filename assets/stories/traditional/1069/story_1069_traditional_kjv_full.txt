David sang unto the Lord.

The words rose from him as breath drawn from deep within his breast, steady and deliberate. He sang that God had searched him. Searched him and known him. The searching was thorough, complete, leaving nothing overlooked.

God knew when David sat down. God knew when he rose up. Every small motion of his day — the lowering of himself upon a stone bench in the evening, the rising at first light when the cold air pressed against his skin — God beheld it all.

David sang that the Lord discerned his thoughts from afar. Not close, not standing beside him, but from a distance that should have made knowing impossible. Yet God knew. He was acquainted with all David's ways. The roads David walked, the places he tarried to rest, the ground whereon he lay at night with dust beneath his back and stars above his face.

Before a word was on David's tongue, the Lord knew it altogether. Every syllable. Every sound that had not yet formed behind his teeth.

The song moved forward. David sang that God hemmed him in. Behind and before. That God laid his hand upon him. Such knowledge was too wonderful. Too high. David could not attain unto it, could not grasp it with his mind.

Then came the question. Whither could he go from God's Spirit? Whither could he flee from God's presence?

David answered his own asking. If he ascended up into heaven, God was there. If he made his bed in Sheol, the deep place of the dead, the place beneath every other place — God was there also.

If he took the wings of the morning and dwelt in the uttermost parts of the sea, where the water met the edge of the world and the light stretched thin — even there, God's hand should lead him. God's right hand should hold him fast.

David sang further. If he said, Surely the darkness shall cover me. If he called out for the night to close about him as a curtain, to swallow the light and hide him — even the darkness was not dark unto God. The night shineth as the day in God's sight. Darkness and light were both alike unto him. There was no difference. No shadow deep enough to sunder David from the one who watched.

The song shifted then. David sang concerning his own body. God had formed his inward parts. Knit him together in his mother's womb. The stitching of sinew to bone, the threading of veins through flesh, the delicate work wrought in hidden places — God had done it.

David praised God, for he was fearfully and wonderfully made. His voice bare the words out over the still air. Wonderful were God's works. David's soul knew it right well. Not learned from a teacher nor read from a scroll. Known in the marrow of himself.

His frame had not been hid from God when David was being made in secret. When he was curiously wrought in the lowest parts of the earth, fashioned in the darkness before he drew his first breath — God saw him. God's eyes beheld his substance, yet being unformed. The raw substance of him, the unshaped clay of his limbs and members, ere they were anything that could be known.

In God's book, every one of David's days had been written. Fashioned for him. Set down before one of them came to pass. The mornings and the evenings, the long afternoons, the watches of the night — all of them recorded ere they existed.

David's voice pressed into the final words. He sang that if he should count the thoughts of God, they would be more in number than the sand. More than the grains along the riverbeds, more than the fine particles that filled the shores of the western sea, more than the dust that blew across the wilderness floor and settled in the creases of a man's hands.

If he should count through the night and into the morning, through one day and into the next, through years of counting — when he awaked, when he reached at last the end of his labour, he should still be with God.

David's voice held the last note. Then it faded. The air about him was still. Motes of dust drifted in the slant of light. His hands rested open at his sides, palms turned upward, empty and quiet.
