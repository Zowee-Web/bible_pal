David sang to the Lord.

He sang that God had searched him. Searched him and known him. Known when he sat down and when he rose up. Known every movement before it happened, every path before it was walked.

The words came steady and sure. God discerned his thoughts from far off. Before a word formed on David's tongue, the Lord knew it completely. Every road David traveled, every place he rested his body at night — God was familiar with all of it.

David sang that the Lord hemmed him in. Behind and before. That God's hand was laid upon him. The knowledge was too wonderful, too high. He could not attain it.

Then he asked the question aloud. Where could he go from God's Spirit? Where could he flee from God's presence? If he ascended to heaven, God was there. If he made his bed in Sheol, the place of the dead, God was there. If he took the wings of the morning and dwelled in the uttermost parts of the sea, even there God's hand would lead him. God's right hand would hold him.

If he said the darkness would cover him, that the light around him would become night — even the darkness was not dark to God. The night shone like the day. Darkness and light were the same to him.

David sang on. God had formed his inward parts. God had knit him together in his mother's womb. He praised God because he was fearfully and wonderfully made. Wonderful were God's works, and David's soul knew it well.

His frame had not been hidden from God when he was being made in secret, woven together in the depths of the earth. God's eyes had seen his unformed substance. In God's book were written every one of the days that were formed for him, before any one of them existed.

David's voice pressed forward through the final words. He lifted his hands and sang of the thoughts of God toward him — more than could be numbered. If he tried to count them, they would outnumber the sand. The grains on the shore, the dust along the riverbeds, the fine particles that shifted between his fingers in the wind.

He sang that if he should wake, if he came to the end of counting, he would still be with God.

The song held there. David's voice carried over the stone and the dust and the open air, and the last note faded into silence.
