David sang unto the Lord.

He was alone when the words began. The chamber was close and warm, the stones of the floor worn smooth beneath his feet. Light entered through a narrow window set high in the wall. Dust hung suspended in the shaft thereof, turning slowly, going nowhere.

David opened his mouth and sang.

He sang that God had searched him. The word he used was thorough — the kind of searching a man doth when he emptieth a bag upon the ground and turneth every object over in his hands, examining each one. God had done this unto David. Searched him completely. And known him.

The knowing was not partial. God knew when David sat down. When he lowered himself upon a bench at the end of the day, when his legs folded beneath him upon a patch of dry grass in the field, when he settled upon a stone ledge and let his weight come to rest — God saw it. God knew when he rose. The first movement of morning, the shift of limbs under a wool covering, the press of feet against cold ground.

David sang that God discerned his thoughts from afar. Not standing beside him. Not leaning close to listen. From a great distance, a height beyond measuring, God perceived what moved within David's mind. The half-formed imaginings, the cares that surfaced in the night, the sudden flashes of remembrance that arrived without bidding — God saw them all, clear and distinct, ere David himself understood them.

God was acquainted with all David's ways. The paths he travelled, the roads he chose, the places where he tarried to rest. When David walked through the hill country with dust rising about his ankles, God was familiar with every step. When David lay down at night and drew his cloak over his breast and looked up into the darkness, God knew the exact position of his body against the earth.

Before a word was upon David's tongue, the Lord knew it altogether. The sentence that had not yet been spoken. The prayer that had not yet been formed. The cry that was still gathering in the throat — God knew it ere the air moved past David's lips.

The song continued. David sang that God hemmed him in. Behind and before. As a wall on every side, as the stones of a narrow pass that rise on the left hand and on the right with no way through save forward. God laid his hand upon David. The weight thereof. The presence thereof.

David sang that such knowledge was too wonderful for him. Too high. He could not attain unto it. He could not climb unto the place where such understanding rested. It was above him, beyond his reach, as the top of a cedar that vanisheth into cloud.

Then came the question.

Whither could he go from the Spirit of God? Whither could he flee from the presence of God?

David waited not for an answer from without. He answered with his own voice, steady and unhurried.

If he ascended up into heaven — climbed past the clouds, past the highest point a bird could fly, past the place where the air grew thin and the blue deepened unto black — God was there.

If he made his bed in Sheol — descended into the abode of the dead, the silent country beneath the earth where no light reached and no voice called — God was there. Even there. In the lowest place, the furthest distance a man could fall from the land of the living, God waited.

If he took the wings of the morning — the first spreading of light that raced across the heavens before the sun, swifter than any runner, swifter than any chariot — and if those wings bare him unto the uttermost parts of the sea, unto the place where the waters met the rim of the world and the horizon bent down into nothing — even there the hand of God would lead him. The right hand of God would hold him.

There was no distance far enough.

David sang on. If he said, Surely the darkness shall cover me. If he called upon the night to wrap itself about him as a thick garment. If he desired the light to become night, to be swallowed up, to vanish, so that he stood in a blackness so complete he could not see his own hand before his face — even that darkness was not dark unto God.

The night shineth as the day. Unto God, there was no difference between them. Darkness and light were both alike unto him. No shadow could hide what God had already seen. No night could conceal what God had already known.

The tone of the song changed. David's voice dropped lower, quieter, closer unto the ground.

He sang concerning his own making. God had formed his inward parts. The organs that moved within him, the heart that beat against his ribs, the lungs that filled and emptied — God had fashioned every one. God had knit him together in his mother's womb. The word was precise. Knit. The careful interlocking of thread upon thread, the patient work of making something whole from separate strands.

David praised God. He was fearfully and wonderfully made. The words came forth from him plainly, without ornament. Wonderful were the works of God. David's soul knew it right well. Knew it as a man knoweth the weight of his own hands, the rhythm of his own breathing. Not argued into. Not persuaded of. Known.

His frame had not been hid from God when he was made in secret. When his bones were being wrought in darkness, when his body was being formed where no human eye could behold — God saw him. In the depths of the earth, in the hidden places, in the silence before birth, God watched.

David was curiously wrought there. The sinew and the skin. The fine network of veins that carried blood unto his fingers and his toes. The tendons that joined muscle unto bone. All of it fashioned in a place none other could enter or behold.

The eyes of God had seen his substance, yet being unformed. Before David had arms or legs. Before his face bore features. Before his fingers could grip or his mouth could cry out. God looked upon the raw matter of him and saw what he should become.

In the book of God, all of David's days were written. Every one of them. Fashioned and set down ere yet a single day thereof existed. Before the first morning brake over the hills where David would in time tend sheep. Before the first evening settled upon the fields where he would one day flee from a king who sought his life. Before the first night he would pass upon a palace roof with the sounds of the city beneath him. All of it written. All of it known.

David's voice grew thin but ceased not. He sang the final words with the selfsame steadiness he had carried from the beginning.

How great was the sum of them. David sang that if he should count those thoughts, they would be more in number than the sand. More than the grains upon the shore of the Great Sea. More than the fine particles that shifted along the banks of Jordan when the waters were low. More than the dust that blew across the Negev and settled in the cracks of dry ground. More than a man could hold in both his hands. More than a man could hold in a lifetime of gathering.

David's mouth closed. The last sound faded into the walls of the chamber. The shaft of light from the window had moved, falling now across the floor in a long bright stripe. Dust still turned therein, slow and weightless. David stood with his hands at his sides, his breast rising and falling, the silence settling about him like sand.
