David sang unto the Lord.

He sang that God had searched him. Searched him and known him. Known when he sat down and when he rose up. Known every movement ere it came to pass, every path ere it was trodden.

The words came steady and sure. God discerned his thoughts from afar off. Before a word was formed upon David's tongue, the Lord knew it altogether. Every road David had travelled, every place he laid his body down by night — God was acquainted with all of it.

David sang that the Lord had hemmed him in. Behind and before. That God had laid his hand upon him. Such knowledge was too wonderful, too high. He could not attain unto it.

Then he asked the question aloud. Whither could he go from God's Spirit? Whither could he flee from God's presence? If he ascended up into heaven, God was there. If he made his bed in Sheol, the place of the dead, God was there. If he took the wings of the morning and dwelt in the uttermost parts of the sea, even there God's hand would lead him. God's right hand would hold him.

If he said, Surely the darkness shall cover me, that the light round about me should become night — even the darkness was not dark unto God. The night shone as the day. The darkness and the light were both alike unto him.

David sang on. God had formed his inward parts. God had knit him together in his mother's womb. He praised God, for he was fearfully and wonderfully made. Marvellous were God's works, and David's soul knew it right well.

His frame had not been hid from God when he was made in secret, and curiously wrought in the lowest parts of the earth. God's eyes had seen his substance, yet being unperfect. And in God's book were written every one of the days that were fashioned for him, when as yet there was none of them.

David's voice pressed forward through the last words. He lifted up his hands and sang of the thoughts of God toward him — more than could be numbered. If he should count them, they would be more in number than the sand. The grains upon the shore, the dust along the riverbeds, the fine particles that shifted between his fingers in the wind.

He sang that if he should awake, if he came to the end of counting, he would still be with God.

The song held there. David's voice carried over the stone and the dust and the open air, and the last note faded into silence.
