David sang to the Lord.

The words rose from him like breath drawn from deep in the chest, steady and deliberate. He sang that God had searched him. Searched him and known him. The searching was thorough, complete, leaving nothing overlooked.

God knew when David sat down. God knew when he rose. Every small motion of his day — lowering himself to a stone bench in the evening, standing at first light when the cold air pressed against his skin — God saw it all.

David sang that the Lord discerned his thoughts from afar. Not close, not standing beside him, but from a distance that should have made knowing impossible. Yet God knew. He was acquainted with all David's ways. The roads David walked, the places he stopped to rest, the ground where he lay at night with dust beneath his back and stars above his face.

Before a word was on David's tongue, the Lord knew it completely. Every syllable. Every sound that had not yet formed behind his teeth.

The song moved forward. David sang that God hemmed him in. Behind and before. That God laid his hand upon him. The knowledge was too wonderful. Too high. David could not reach it, could not grasp it with his mind.

Then came the question. Where could he go from God's Spirit? Where could he flee from God's presence?

David answered his own asking. If he ascended to heaven, God was there. If he made his bed in Sheol, the deep place of the dead, the place below every other place — God was there too.

If he took the wings of the morning and flew to the uttermost parts of the sea, where the water met the edge of the world and the light stretched thin — even there, God's hand would lead him. God's right hand would hold him fast.

David sang further. If he said, Surely the darkness shall cover me. If he called out for the night to close around him like a curtain, to swallow the light and hide him — even the darkness was not dark to God. Night shone like the day in God's sight. Darkness and light were alike to him. There was no difference. No shadow deep enough to separate David from the one who watched.

The song shifted then. David sang about his own body. God had formed his inward parts. Knit him together in his mother's womb. The stitching of muscle to bone, the threading of veins through flesh, the delicate work done in hidden places — God had done it.

David praised God, because he was fearfully and wonderfully made. His voice carried the words out over the still air. Wonderful were God's works. David's soul knew it well. Not learned from a teacher or read from a scroll. Known in the marrow of himself.

His frame had not been hidden from God when David was being made in secret. When he was woven together in the depths of the earth, shaped in the darkness before he drew his first breath — God saw him. God's eyes saw his unformed substance. The raw material of him, the unshaped clay of his limbs and organs, before they were anything recognizable.

In God's book, every one of David's days had been written. Formed for him. Set down before a single one of them came to pass. The mornings and the evenings, the long afternoons, the watches of the night — all of them recorded before they existed.

David's voice pressed into the final words. He sang that if he tried to count God's thoughts, they would be more than the sand. More than the grains along the riverbeds, more than the fine particles that filled the shores of the western sea, more than the dust that blew across the wilderness floor and settled in the creases of a man's hands.

If he should count through the night and into the morning, through one day and into the next, through years of counting — when he awoke, when he finally reached the end of his effort, he would still be with God.

David's voice held the last note. Then it faded. The air around him was still. Dust motes drifted in the slant of light. His hands rested open at his sides, palms turned upward, empty and quiet.
