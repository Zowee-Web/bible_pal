And David said, "Is there yet any that is left of the house of Saul, that I may shew him kindness for Jonathan's sake?"

And there was of the house of Saul a servant whose name was Ziba. When they had called him unto David, the king sat upon the carved throne, the light from the high windows falling across the stone floor. He said unto him, "Art thou Ziba?" And he said, "Thy servant is he."

And the king said, "Is there not yet any of the house of Saul, that I may shew the kindness of God unto him?"

And Ziba said unto the king, "Jonathan hath yet a son, which is lame on his feet." The child had been five years of age when the tidings came of Saul and Jonathan from Jezreel. His nurse took him up and fled. In her haste, running down the stone passage with the child against her breast, he fell and became lame. His name was Mephibosheth.

David said, "Where is he?" "He is in the house of Machir, the son of Ammiel, in Lodebar."

Then king David sent and fetched him out of Lodebar. The journey was long — days upon the road, across the Jordan, through dry hills, the dust settling upon the litter that bare him. Mephibosheth came borne or upheld, a man who could not walk of his own strength, summoned by a king whom he had every cause to fear. The house of Saul had been the house of David's enemy.

Now when Mephibosheth was come unto David, he fell on his face and did reverence, his body flat against the cold stone of the palace floor, his broken feet behind him. And David said, "Mephibosheth." And he answered, "Behold thy servant."

And David said, "Fear not: for I will surely shew thee kindness for Jonathan thy father's sake, and will restore thee all the land of Saul thy father; and thou shalt eat bread at my table continually."

And he bowed himself and said, "What is thy servant, that thou shouldest look upon such a dead dog as I am?"

And David called Ziba and told him all the land was given unto Mephibosheth. Ziba and his sons and servants would till the land and bring in the fruits.

So Mephibosheth dwelt in Jerusalem, and did eat at the king's table continually. A man lame on both his feet, his sandals worn unevenly, seated among the king's own sons. His place was set. His bread was served.
