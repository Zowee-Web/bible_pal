David said, "Is there still anyone left of the house of Saul, that I may show him kindness for Jonathan's sake?"

There was a servant of Saul's house whose name was Ziba. They called him before David. The king sat on the carved throne, the light from the high windows falling across the stone floor. He said, "Are you Ziba?" He said, "I am your servant."

The king said, "Is there not still someone of the house of Saul, that I may show the kindness of God to him?"

Ziba said, "There is still a son of Jonathan. He is lame in both feet." The boy had been five years old when news came of the deaths of Saul and Jonathan at Jezreel. His nurse had picked him up to flee. In her haste, running down the stone corridor with the child against her chest, he fell and became lame. His name was Mephibosheth.

David said, "Where is he?"

"He is in the house of Machir, the son of Ammiel, in Lo-debar."

King David sent and brought him from Lo-debar. The journey was long — days on the road, across the Jordan, through dry hills, the dust settling on the litter that carried him. Mephibosheth came carried or supported, a man who could not walk on his own.

Mephibosheth came into the king's presence and fell on his face and paid homage, his body flat against the cold stone of the palace floor, his damaged feet behind him. David said, "Mephibosheth." He said, "Behold, I am your servant."

David said, "Do not fear. I will show you kindness for the sake of your father Jonathan. I will restore to you all the land of Saul your father, and you shall eat at my table always."

Mephibosheth bowed. "What is your servant, that you should show regard for a dead dog such as I?"

David called Ziba and told him to farm the land for Mephibosheth. Ziba and his sons and servants would work the fields. The produce would feed the household.

But Mephibosheth himself would eat at the king's table. So he lived in Jerusalem, and ate at the king's table continually. A man lame in both feet, his sandals worn unevenly, seated among the king's own sons. His place was set. His bread was served.
