It was night. The sky was black and thick with stars. Jacob stood alone by the Jabbok River, listening to water rush over smooth stones.

He had sent everyone else across. His wives, his servants, and his eleven children had waded through the shallow crossing in the dark. His flocks and herds had gone too — hundreds of animals splashing through the water.

Now the last torch was gone. Jacob stood by himself on the near side of the river. A cool breeze moved through the tall grass along the bank.

Then someone was there. A man. Jacob did not see where he came from. Suddenly strong hands gripped him, and the man began to wrestle.

Jacob fought back hard. He planted his feet in the soft ground and pushed. The man pushed back. They locked arms, pulling and twisting, feet sliding in the river mud. Neither one would give in.

Hour after hour they struggled. The stars moved slowly across the sky. Jacob's muscles burned. Sweat ran down his face. His breath came in rough gasps. Still he held on.

When the man saw that he could not win against Jacob, he reached out and touched Jacob's hip. Just a touch. The hip popped out of its socket. Pain shot through Jacob's whole body like fire.

But he did not let go.

The sky began to lighten in the east. Dawn was coming.

"Let me go," the man said, "for the day is breaking."

Jacob tightened his grip. His hip screamed with pain, but his hands held firm. "I will not let you go unless you bless me."

The man asked, "What is your name?"

"Jacob."

"Your name will no longer be called Jacob," the man said. "It will be Israel. For you have wrestled with God and with men, and you have prevailed."

Jacob asked, "Please, tell me your name."

But the man only said, "Why do you ask my name?" And right there, by the river in the gray light of morning, he blessed Jacob.

Jacob called that place Peniel. The name means "face of God." He said, "I have seen God face to face, and my life is preserved."

The sun rose over the river, turning the water gold and white. Jacob crossed the Jabbok, limping with every step. But he had wrestled through the darkest hours and held on. He had a blessing, and he had a new name.
