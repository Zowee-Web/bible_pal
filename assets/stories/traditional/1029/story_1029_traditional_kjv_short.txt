It was night. The sky was black and thick with stars. Jacob stood alone by the river Jabbok, hearkening to the water that rushed over smooth stones.

He had sent every one else over. His wives, his servants, and his eleven children had passed through the shallow ford in the dark. His flocks and his herds had gone also — hundreds of beasts splashing through the water.

Now the last torch was gone. Jacob stood by himself on the near side of the river. A cool wind moved through the tall grass along the bank.

And behold, there was a man. Jacob beheld not whence he came. On a sudden strong hands gripped him, and the man began to wrestle.

Jacob strove against him with all his might. He planted his feet in the soft ground and pushed. The man pushed back. They locked their arms together, pulling and twisting, feet sliding in the river mud. Neither one would give way.

Hour after hour they wrestled. The stars moved slowly across the sky. Jacob's sinews burned. The sweat ran down his face. His breath came in rough gasps. Yet still he held on.

When the man saw that he prevailed not against Jacob, he put forth his hand and touched Jacob's hip. Only a touch. The hip was put out of joint. Pain shot through Jacob's whole body as fire.

But he would not let him go.

The sky began to lighten in the east. The dawn was at hand.

"Let me go," the man said, "for the day breaketh."

Jacob held him the tighter. His hip cried out with pain, yet his hands held firm. "I will not let thee go, except thou bless me."

The man asked, "What is thy name?"

"Jacob."

"Thy name shall be called no more Jacob," the man said. "It shall be Israel. For thou hast wrestled with God and with men, and hast prevailed."

Jacob asked, "Tell me, I pray thee, thy name."

But the man only said, "Wherefore is it that thou dost ask after my name?" And there, by the river in the grey light of morning, he blessed him.

Jacob called the name of the place Peniel. The name meaneth "the face of God." He said, "I have seen God face to face, and my life is preserved."

The sun arose over the river, turning the water gold and white. Jacob passed over the Jabbok, halting upon his thigh with every step. But he had wrestled through the darkest hours and held on. He had a blessing, and he had a new name.
