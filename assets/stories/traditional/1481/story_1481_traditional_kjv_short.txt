This is what Jeremiah said in his lamentation, in the days after the city had been broken.

He had been kept in the dungeon for a time. The pit had been dark. The water had risen high enough at the bottom of it to wet his garments. There had been no light by day and no light by night, and no man came unto him in the place where they had put him.

He had called out from there.

He spake of it afterwards, when he set down the words he had said.

"I called upon thy name, O Lord, out of the low dungeon.

Thou hast heard my voice — 'Hide not thine ear at my breathing, at my cry.'

Thou drewest near in the day that I called upon thee.

Thou saidst, 'Fear not.'

O Lord, thou hast pleaded the causes of my soul; thou hast redeemed my life."

These were the words he had brought up out of the dungeon with him.

He set them down upon the same page as the lament he had written for the broken city, so that they who would read it afterward would know — in the same book where the streets had been described empty, and the gates broken down, and the children fainted for hunger — that a voice had still called, and had still been heard, from the lowest place.
