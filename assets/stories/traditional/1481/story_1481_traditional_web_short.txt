This is what Jeremiah said in his lamentation, in the days after the city had been broken.

He had been kept in the dungeon for a time. The pit had been dark. The water had risen high enough at the bottom of it to wet his clothes. There had been no light by day and no light by night, and no one came to him in the place where they had put him.

He had called out from there.

He spoke of it later, when he set down the words he had said.

"I called on your name, Yahweh, out of the lowest dungeon.

You heard my voice — 'Don't hide your ear from my sighing, and my cry.'

You came near in the day that I called on you.

You said, 'Don't be afraid.'

Lord, you have pleaded the causes of my soul. You have redeemed my life."

These were the words he had carried up out of the dungeon with him.

He set them down on the same page as the lament he had written for the broken city, so that those who would read it later would know — in the same book where the streets had been described empty, and the gates broken down, and the children fainted for hunger — that a voice had still called, and had still been heard, from the lowest place.
