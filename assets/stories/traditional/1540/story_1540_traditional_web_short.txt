The room was small. Oil lamps sat on a stone ledge near the window, and their light reached the walls but not the corners. Seven or eight people sat on mats and low wooden stools. A man named Lucius stood near the doorway, holding a scroll open between both hands.

He had been reading aloud for some time. The letter was from Paul, written from far away, carried by hand across the roads and the sea. The ink was dark and the script was careful, the work of a trained scribe. Lucius had read through arguments and explanations, through talk of suffering and hope and the Spirit. Now he came to the end of the letter's eighth section, and his voice changed.

"What shall we then say to these things?" he read. He looked up from the scroll. The room was quiet. A woman near the back shifted on her mat. "If God is for us, who can be against us?"

No one answered. The question hung in the lamplight.

Lucius read on. "He who did not spare his own Son, but delivered him up for us all — how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?"

A man sitting near the wall leaned forward and put his hands on his knees. His mouth was slightly open.

"Who shall lay anything to the charge of God's elect?" Lucius read. "It is God who justifies. Who is he that condemns? It is Christ who died — yes, rather, who is risen again, who is even at the right hand of God, who also makes intercession for us."

Lucius paused and drew breath. His hands were steady on the edges of the scroll.

"Who shall separate us from the love of Christ?" he read. "Shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword? As it is written: For your sake we are killed all the day long. We are accounted as sheep for the slaughter."

The woman near the back pressed her knuckles against her lips. The man by the wall had not moved.

"No," Lucius read. His voice rose. "In all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us."

He tilted the scroll closer to the lamplight. His fingers tightened on the edges.

"For I am persuaded," he read, "that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord."

Lucius lowered the scroll. The oil lamps flickered against the walls. The room was full of breathing, and no one spoke.
