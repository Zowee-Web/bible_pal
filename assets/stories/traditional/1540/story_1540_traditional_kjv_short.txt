The room was small. Lamps of oil rested upon a ledge of stone beside the window, and their light fell upon the walls but reached not unto the corners. Seven or eight souls sat upon mats and upon low stools of wood. A man called Lucius stood nigh unto the doorway, and he held a scroll open between his two hands.

He had been reading aloud for some good while. The letter was of Paul, set down in a far country and borne by hand across the roads and over the sea. The ink was dark and the script was careful, the labour of a trained scribe. Lucius had read through reasonings and explanations, through words of suffering and of hope and of the Spirit. Now he came unto the close of the letter's eighth portion, and his voice changed.

"What then shall we say unto these things?" he read. He lifted his eyes from the scroll. The room was still. A woman near the back shifted upon her mat. "If God be for us, what man shall stand against us?"

None answered. The question abode in the lamplight.

Lucius read on. "He that withheld not his own Son, but gave him over for us all — shall he not also, together with him, freely bestow upon us all things?"

A man sitting near the wall leaned forward and set his hands upon his knees. His mouth was opened slightly.

"Who shall bring a charge against the chosen of God?" Lucius read. "It is God himself that declareth them righteous. Who then is he that shall condemn? It is Christ that died — yea, and more than this, that was raised up again, that sitteth now at the right hand of God, that maketh entreaty for us even now."

Lucius paused and drew breath. His hands were steady upon the edges of the scroll.

"Who shall sever us from the love of Christ?" he read. "Shall trouble, or anguish, or the hand of them that persecute, or hunger, or want of garment, or danger, or the edge of the sword? Even as it is written: For thy sake we are given over to death throughout the day. We are reckoned as sheep appointed for the killing."

The woman near the back pressed her knuckles against her lips. The man by the wall had not moved.

"Nay," Lucius read. His voice rose. "In all these things we prevail far beyond conquering, through him that loved us."

He turned the scroll nearer unto the lamplight. His fingers tightened upon the edges.

"For I am assured of this," he read, "that neither death, nor life, nor the angels, nor rulers, nor powers, nor what is present, nor what is yet to come, nor the heights, nor the depths, nor any created thing whatsoever shall have the power to sever us from the love of God, which abideth in Christ Jesus our Lord."

Lucius lowered the scroll. The oil lamps flickered against the walls. The room was full of breathing, and no one spake.
