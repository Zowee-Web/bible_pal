In the third year of king Belshazzar, a vision appeared unto Daniel after the vision he had seen at the first. He saw himself at Shushan in the palace, in the province of Elam, beside the river Ulai.

Lifting his eyes, Daniel beheld a ram before the river. It had two high horns, but one stood higher, and that higher horn had risen last. The ram pushed westward, northward, and southward. No beast could withstand him; none could deliver out of his hand. He did according to his will and became great.

As Daniel considered, an he goat came from the west over the face of the whole earth, touching not the ground. A notable horn stood between his eyes. With the fury of his power, he ran upon the ram Daniel had seen by the river.

The goat came close, moved with choler against the ram. He smote him and brake both horns. The ram could not withstand him. Cast to the ground, he was stamped upon, and none could deliver him from the goat’s hand.

The he goat waxed very great. When he was strong, his great horn was broken. In its place rose four notable horns toward the four winds of heaven.

Out of one came a little horn, waxing exceeding great toward the south, the east, and the pleasant land. It grew even to the host of heaven, casting some of that host and the stars to the ground and stamping upon them.

He magnified himself even to the prince of the host. By him the daily sacrifice was taken away, and the sanctuary’s place was cast down. An host was given him against the daily sacrifice by reason of transgression. Truth was cast to the ground; the horn practised and prospered.

Then Daniel heard one saint speaking. Another saint asked that speaker how long the vision would be: the daily sacrifice, the transgression of desolation, and both the sanctuary and the host given to be trodden under foot.

Unto Daniel came the answer: “Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed.”
