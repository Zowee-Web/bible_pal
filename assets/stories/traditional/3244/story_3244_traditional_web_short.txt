In King Belshazzar’s third year, Daniel saw another vision after the one he had seen before. In this vision he was in the citadel of Susa, in Elam, beside the river Ulai.

He looked up. Before the river stood a ram with two high horns. One horn was higher than the other, and the higher one had come up last. The ram pushed west, north, and south. No animal could stand against him, and none could rescue anything from his power. He did as he pleased and magnified himself.

While Daniel considered this, a male goat came from the west across the whole earth without touching the ground. Between his eyes was a notable horn. He rushed in fury at the ram beside the river.

Daniel saw the goat come close, enraged against the ram, and strike him. Both horns broke. The ram had no strength to stand against the goat, who threw him down and trampled him. No one could deliver the ram.

The goat magnified himself exceedingly. Yet at the height of his strength, the great horn broke. Four notable horns came up in its place, pointing toward the four winds of the sky.

From one of them came a little horn. It grew exceedingly great toward the south, the east, and the glorious land. It reached even to the army of the sky, casting some of the army and stars to the ground and trampling them.

The horn magnified itself even to the prince of the army. It took away his continual burnt offering, and his sanctuary was cast down. Through disobedience, the army and continual burnt offering were given over to the horn. It cast truth to the ground, doing its pleasure and prospering.

Then Daniel heard a holy one speaking. Another holy one asked the speaker how long this vision would last: the continual burnt offering, the disobedience that made desolate, and both sanctuary and army given over to be trampled.

The answer was spoken to Daniel: “To two thousand and three hundred evenings and mornings. Then the sanctuary will be cleansed.”
