Jeremiah had walked the streets of Jerusalem and seen the falsehood in the mouths of the people, and the dealing of the rich with the poor, and the prophets who prophesied lies, and the priests who spoke peace where there was no peace. The burden of the seeing had grown in him until at last he lifted up a cry — and the cry was for his own people. He set the words down as they had come, and the words began thus.

"Oh that my head were waters,
and my eyes a spring of tears,
that I might weep day and night
for the slain of the daughter of my people!

Oh that I had in the wilderness
a lodging place of wayfaring men;
that I might leave my people,
and go from them!
For they are all adulterers,
an assembly of treacherous men.

"They bend their tongue,
as their bow, for falsehood.
They have grown strong in the land,
but not for truth;
for they proceed from evil to evil,
and they don't know me," says Yahweh.

And the cry of the prophet was written down. Jeremiah did not leave the people, though he had wished to; he stayed in the city through the long days that would follow, and the words "Oh that my head were waters" stood at the head of the lamentation, kept in the scroll for any who would read them. And so it was in the days of the prophet who wept for his people.
