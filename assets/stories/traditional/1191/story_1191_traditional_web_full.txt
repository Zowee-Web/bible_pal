The wall stood. Around the city of Jerusalem it ran, joined and whole, and no breach was left in it. The doors of the gates had not yet been hung, but the long line of stone closed where it had not closed for many years. Nehemiah walked the top of it in the late light. Below him the workers had set down their tools and gone to their houses, and the smoke of evening fires rose between the streets.

Word of the finished wall went out across the province. It came to Sanballat in the north. It came to Tobiah the Ammonite. It came to Geshem the Arabian. And what they heard grieved them.

A messenger came to Nehemiah at the gate. He carried a letter from Sanballat and Geshem. Nehemiah opened it and read.

"Come, let us meet together in some one of the villages in the plain of Ono."

Nehemiah folded the letter. The plain of Ono lay a day's ride from Jerusalem. He understood what the letter meant. He had not built a wall to walk away from it.

He sent his answer back by the same messenger.

"I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down. Why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you?"

A second letter came, asking the same thing. He answered the same way. A third came. He answered again. A fourth. His words did not change. "I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down."

Then Sanballat sent a fifth time, by his own servant, with an open letter in the man's hand. The letter was open so that any who passed in the street might read it.

"It is reported among the heathen, and Gashmu saith it, that thou and the Jews think to rebel: for which cause thou buildest the wall, that thou mayest be their king. Come now therefore, and let us take counsel together."

Nehemiah read the letter standing. He did not lower his head. He gave his answer.

"There are no such things done as thou sayest, but thou feignest them out of thine own heart."

The servant carried the answer away. Nehemiah watched him go. The wind moved through the half-finished streets. He knew what they wanted. They wanted the workers to be afraid. They wanted hands to fall from the stone. He spoke under his breath, as a man speaks who has only one place to bring such a prayer.

"Now therefore, O God, strengthen my hands."

Afterward he went to the house of Shemaiah the son of Delaiah, who was shut up within his own walls. Shemaiah spoke to him quickly, with the manner of a man delivering a true word.

"Let us meet together in the house of God, within the temple, and let us shut the doors of the temple: for they will come to slay thee; yea, in the night will they come to slay thee."

Nehemiah stood in the small room. He looked at the man who would not come out into the light to speak. He weighed the words against the day's work, and against the kind of man he had been called to be.

"Should such a man as I flee? And who is there, that, being as I am, would go into the temple to save his life? I will not go in."

He turned and went out. As he walked back through the streets he understood. God had not sent Shemaiah. Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him, that Nehemiah might be afraid, and run, and shut himself in, and give them a report of his fear to carry to the king.

He brought the matter once more before God. "My God, think thou upon Tobiah and Sanballat according to these their works, and on the prophetess Noadiah, and the rest of the prophets, that would have put me in fear."

The hammers began again in the morning. The doors of the gates were lifted into their places. The workers carried timber and iron, and the watchmen took their stations at the towers. So the wall was finished in the twenty and fifth day of the month of Elul, in fifty and two days.

When the enemies of the Jews heard of it, and all the heathen round about saw the wall standing, they were much cast down in their own eyes. For they perceived that this work was wrought of our God.
