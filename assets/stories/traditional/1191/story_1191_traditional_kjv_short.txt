When Sanballat, Tobiah, and Geshem the Arabian, and the rest of the enemies of the Jews heard that Nehemiah had builded the wall, and that there was no breach left therein, they sent messengers four times unto Nehemiah, saying, "Come, let us meet together in some one of the villages in the plain of Ono." But they thought to do him mischief.

Nehemiah sent messengers back unto them, saying, "I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down: why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you?"

Thus they sent unto him four times after this sort, and Nehemiah answered them after the same manner. Then Sanballat sent his servant the fifth time with an open letter in his hand, accusing Nehemiah of seeking to be king. But Nehemiah sent unto him, saying, "There are no such things done as thou sayest, but thou feignest them out of thine own heart."

For they all would have made the workers afraid, saying, "Their hands shall be weakened from the work, that it be not done." But now, Nehemiah said, "Now therefore, O God, strengthen my hands."

So the wall was finished in the twenty and fifth day of the month Elul, in fifty and two days. When all the enemies of the Jews heard thereof, all the heathen that were about them feared. They were much cast down in their own eyes, for they perceived that this work was wrought of our God.

The morning was clear over the city, and the wall stood whole round about Jerusalem. The gates were set in their places, and the watchmen stood at the towers, and the people of the city walked along the streets that had been cleared of the rubbish.

The sound of the masons was ended, and the sound of singing arose from the courts of the city. The work that had been begun in fasting and prayer was now standing in stone, and the city of David was guarded again.

Nehemiah stood at the gate and beheld the length of the wall. The workers had laid down their tools, and the people gathered round about him. The morning sun was warm above Jerusalem, and the wall held its ground.
