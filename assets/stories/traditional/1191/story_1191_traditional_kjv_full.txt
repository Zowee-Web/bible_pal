The wall stood. Round about the city of Jerusalem it ran, joined and whole, and there was no breach left therein. The doors of the gates had not yet been hung, but the long line of stone closed where it had not closed for many years. Nehemiah walked the top of it in the late light. Below him the workers had laid down their tools and gone unto their houses, and the smoke of evening fires rose betwixt the streets.

Word of the finished wall went out across the province. It came unto Sanballat in the north. It came unto Tobiah the Ammonite. It came unto Geshem the Arabian. And what they heard grieved them.

A messenger came unto Nehemiah at the gate. He bare a letter from Sanballat and Geshem. Nehemiah opened it and read.

"Come, let us meet together in some one of the villages in the plain of Ono."

Nehemiah folded the letter. The plain of Ono lay a day's ride from Jerusalem. He understood what the letter meant. He had not builded a wall to walk away from it.

He sent his answer back by the same messenger.

"I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down. Why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you?"

A second letter came, asking the same thing. He answered after the same manner. A third came. He answered again. A fourth. His words did not change. "I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down."

Then Sanballat sent unto him the fifth time, by his own servant, with an open letter in the man's hand. The letter was open that any who passed in the street might read it.

"It is reported among the heathen, and Gashmu saith it, that thou and the Jews think to rebel: for which cause thou buildest the wall, that thou mayest be their king. Come now therefore, and let us take counsel together."

Nehemiah read the letter standing. He did not lower his head. He gave his answer.

"There are no such things done as thou sayest, but thou feignest them out of thine own heart."

The servant carried the answer away. Nehemiah watched him go. The wind moved through the half-finished streets. He knew what they sought. They sought that the workers should be afraid. They sought that hands should fall from the stone. He spake under his breath, as a man speaketh who hath but one place to bring such a prayer.

"Now therefore, O God, strengthen my hands."

Afterward he went unto the house of Shemaiah the son of Delaiah, who was shut up within his own walls. Shemaiah spake unto him quickly, with the manner of a man delivering a true word.

"Let us meet together in the house of God, within the temple, and let us shut the doors of the temple: for they will come to slay thee; yea, in the night will they come to slay thee."

Nehemiah stood in the small room. He looked upon the man who would not come out into the light to speak. He weighed the words against the day's work, and against the kind of man he had been called to be.

"Should such a man as I flee? And who is there, that, being as I am, would go into the temple to save his life? I will not go in."

He turned and went out. As he walked back through the streets he perceived. God had not sent Shemaiah. Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him, that Nehemiah should be afraid, and do so, and shut himself in, and that they might have matter for an evil report to carry unto the king.

He brought the matter once more before God. "My God, think thou upon Tobiah and Sanballat according to these their works, and on the prophetess Noadiah, and the rest of the prophets, that would have put me in fear."

The hammers began again in the morning. The doors of the gates were lifted into their places. The workers bare timber and iron, and the watchmen took their stations at the towers. So the wall was finished in the twenty and fifth day of the month Elul, in fifty and two days.

When the enemies of the Jews heard thereof, and all the heathen round about saw the wall standing, they were much cast down in their own eyes. For they perceived that this work was wrought of our God.
