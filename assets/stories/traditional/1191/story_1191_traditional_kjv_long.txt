The wall stood whole round about the city.

For weeks the workers had borne stone and timber up the slopes, and the masons had set block to block, and the watchmen had stood at half-built towers with their weapons within reach. Now the wall ran the length of Jerusalem. It met itself at every corner. There was no breach left therein. The doors of the gates had not yet been hung in their frames, but the long line of stone was joined and unbroken, and a man could walk it from the Fish Gate round unto the Valley Gate and find no opening on his way.

Nehemiah walked the top of it in the late light. The sun was low over the western country. Below him the streets were quieting. Workers had laid down their tools and gone unto their houses. The smoke of evening fires rose betwixt the roofs. He laid his hand upon the stone at the parapet and felt it warm from the day.

Word of the finished wall went out across the province. It came unto Sanballat in Samaria. It came unto Tobiah the Ammonite. It came unto Geshem the Arabian. And what they heard grieved them. They had spoken against the work from its first day. They had laughed at the rubble. They had sent threats by night. Now the rubble was gone, and the wall was up.

A messenger came unto Nehemiah at the gate the next morning. He bare a letter from Sanballat and Geshem. Nehemiah brake the seal and read.

"Come, let us meet together in some one of the villages in the plain of Ono."

He folded the letter slowly. The plain of Ono lay a day's hard ride to the north and west. He understood what the letter meant. A man that travelled unto such a meeting might not travel back. He had not laboured fifty days to walk away from the work for the sake of a quiet conference in a far village. He gave his answer unto the messenger.

"I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down. Why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you?"

The messenger went his way. Nehemiah watched the dust of the road close behind him and turned back unto the gate.

A second letter came. It said the same thing. He answered it after the same manner. A third letter came. He answered again. The fourth time, when the messenger spread the parchment open, the words within were almost word for word what had come before, and the answer Nehemiah gave was almost word for word what he had given. He did not raise his voice. He did not vary the form. "I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down."

Then Sanballat sent unto him the fifth time, by his own servant, with an open letter in the man's hand. The letter was open that any who passed in the street might read it as the servant bare it through the city. Nehemiah received it in the open ground near the gate. Workers paused upon the wall above to watch. The servant unrolled the letter before him and held it up.

"It is reported among the heathen, and Gashmu saith it, that thou and the Jews think to rebel: for which cause thou buildest the wall, that thou mayest be their king, according to these words. And thou hast also appointed prophets to preach of thee at Jerusalem, saying, There is a king in Judah: and now shall it be reported to the king according to these words. Come now therefore, and let us take counsel together."

Nehemiah read the letter standing. He did not lower his head. He did not draw the servant aside. If the letter were open, his answer would be open also.

"There are no such things done as thou sayest, but thou feignest them out of thine own heart."

The servant rolled the letter and went. The workers above shifted upon the wall and went back unto their tools. Nehemiah stood a moment longer in the open ground. He knew what they sought. They sought not a meeting. They sought that hands should fall from the stone. They had said it among themselves, and the saying of it had reached his ear: their hands shall be weakened from the work, that it be not done. He stood in the open ground and spake under his breath, as a man speaketh who hath but one place to bring such a prayer.

"Now therefore, O God, strengthen my hands."

That afternoon he went unto the house of Shemaiah the son of Delaiah the son of Mehetabeel, who was shut up within his own walls. The door was barred. A servant opened it after Nehemiah knocked. Shemaiah was in the inner room. He rose not when Nehemiah came in. He spake quickly, with the manner of a man delivering an urgent and holy word.

"Let us meet together in the house of God, within the temple, and let us shut the doors of the temple: for they will come to slay thee; yea, in the night will they come to slay thee."

Nehemiah stood in the small room. He looked upon the man who would not come out into the light to speak. The lamp upon the table cast Shemaiah's face in unsteady shadow. He weighed the words against the day's work, and against the kind of man he had been called to be. A governor of Judah hiding within the temple. The doors of the house of God shut against the night. A tale to be borne out into every street by morning.

He gave his answer.

"Should such a man as I flee? And who is there, that, being as I am, would go into the temple to save his life? I will not go in."

He turned and went out. The door closed behind him. The street without was bright after the room. He walked back through the city with the sun in his eyes.

As he walked he perceived. God had not sent Shemaiah. The man had pronounced his prophecy against him, not for him. Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him. The errand was the same errand that had come in the letters, only changed in shape. They had sought to draw him away unto the plain of Ono. They had sought to draw him into the temple in the dark. They sought that he should be afraid, and do somewhat out of fear, and sin in the doing, that they might have matter for an evil report and bear his name down unto the king.

He brought the matter once more before God, in the same quiet voice he had used in the open ground.

"My God, think thou upon Tobiah and Sanballat according to these their works, and on the prophetess Noadiah, and the rest of the prophets, that would have put me in fear."

The next morning the hammers began again. The doors of the gates were lifted into their frames and the hinges were set. The watchmen took their stations at the towers. The masons finished the last courses where they were still to be finished. The carriers bare timber from the king's forest. The wall held its line.

So the wall was finished in the twenty and fifth day of the month Elul, in fifty and two days.

When the enemies of the Jews heard thereof, and all the heathen round about saw the wall standing whole about Jerusalem, they were much cast down in their own eyes. The laughter that had carried across the province went silent. The letters ceased. The plain of Ono went on without its meeting.

For they perceived that this work was wrought of our God.

Nehemiah stood at the gate in the morning of the fifty and second day and looked along the length of the wall. The watchmen were at the towers. The doors hung in their places. The streets were waking within the line of stone. The sun climbed over Jerusalem, and the wall stood, and the work that had been begun in fasting and prayer was standing now in stone, and the city of David was guarded again.
