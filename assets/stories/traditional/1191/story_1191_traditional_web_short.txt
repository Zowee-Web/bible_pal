When Sanballat, Tobiah, and Geshem the Arabian, and the rest of the enemies of the Jews heard that Nehemiah had built the wall, and that there was no breach left in it, they sent messengers four times to Nehemiah, asking him to come down to them to a plain in the country of Ono. They thought to do him harm.

Nehemiah sent messengers back to them, saying, "I am doing a great work, so that I can't come down. Why should the work cease while I leave it and come down to you?"

They sent to him in this way four times, and Nehemiah answered them in the same way each time. Then Sanballat sent his servant a fifth time with an open letter in his hand, accusing Nehemiah of seeking to be king. But Nehemiah sent to him, saying, "There are no such things done as you say, but you imagine them out of your own heart."

For they all would have made the workers afraid, saying, "Their hands will be weakened from the work, that it not be done." But now, Nehemiah said, "Strengthen my hands."

So the wall was finished in the twenty-fifth day of the month of Elul, in fifty-two days. When all the enemies of the Jews heard of it, all the nations that were around them feared. They were greatly cast down in their own eyes, for they perceived that this work was done by our God.

The morning was clear over the city, and the wall stood whole around Jerusalem. The gates were set in their places, and the watchmen stood at the towers, and the people of the city walked along the streets that had been cleared of the rubble.

The sound of the masons had ended, and the sound of singing rose from the courts of the city. The work that had been begun in fasting and prayer was now standing in stone, and the city of David was guarded again.

Nehemiah stood at the gate and looked along the length of the wall. The workers had laid down their tools, and the people gathered around him. The morning sun was warm above Jerusalem, and the wall held its ground.
