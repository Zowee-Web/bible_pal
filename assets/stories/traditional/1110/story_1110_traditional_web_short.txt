Jesus led them out as far as Bethany. They walked together from the city, through the gate, down the road that descended into the Kidron Valley. The path crossed the dry streambed and climbed the western slope of the Mount of Olives. The ground was rocky. Olive trees lined the way, their silver-green leaves catching the light. The air was warm. The smell of dust and wild thyme rose from the earth.

They climbed. The road was steep in places, worn smooth by years of travelers. Jerusalem fell behind them — the rooftops, the temple walls, the narrow streets growing smaller as they gained height. The disciples walked in a loose group. Their sandals scuffed the packed dirt. No one spoke of where they were going.

They crested the ridge. The eastern slope of the Mount of Olives opened before them. Below lay the village of Bethany — small stone houses, garden walls, a few trees clustered near the road. The landscape stretched beyond in brown and green folds toward the wilderness.

Jesus stopped. They gathered around him on the high ground. The wind moved across the hilltop. Jerusalem was visible behind them to the west. Bethany lay below to the east.

He lifted up his hands. He blessed them.

While he blessed them, he was parted from them. He was carried up into heaven.

The disciples watched. Their faces were turned upward. The sky was open and blue above the ridge. The wind blew across their robes. They stood on the hilltop with their eyes fixed on the place where he had been.

They worshiped him. They bowed on the rocky ground where they stood.

Then they returned to Jerusalem with great joy. They walked back over the ridge, down the slope, across the valley, through the gate. They went into the temple. They were continually in the temple, praising and blessing God.

The road between the city and Bethany lay quiet behind them. The olive trees moved in the wind. The hilltop stood empty under the open sky.
