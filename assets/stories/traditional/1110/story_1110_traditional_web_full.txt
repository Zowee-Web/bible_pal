Jesus led them out as far as Bethany. They left the city together — through the narrow streets, past the market stalls and the houses with their flat roofs, out through the eastern gate. The road descended into the Kidron Valley. The streambed was dry. Stones lay scattered along the bottom, bleached white by the sun. They crossed it and began to climb.

The Mount of Olives rose ahead of them. The path was steep and uneven, cut by rain and worn by the feet of pilgrims and merchants and farmers for generations. Olive trees stood on both sides — ancient trunks twisted and thick, their leaves silver in the sunlight. The shade moved in patches on the ground as the wind stirred the branches.

The disciples walked in a loose group behind Jesus. Their robes swayed with their steps. Their sandals scraped the rock. The air was warm and dry. The smell of wild thyme and sun-heated stone rose from the earth around them. Insects hummed in the low scrub along the roadside. A bird called from somewhere among the olive trees and was answered by another farther up the slope.

They climbed higher. Jerusalem fell away behind them. The temple mount was visible first, then the walls, then the full spread of the city — rooftops packed tight, smoke rising from cooking fires, the distant sound of voices and animals fading as the distance grew. The road curved along the flank of the hill. The ground leveled briefly, then rose again.

They passed the place called Gethsemane. The garden sat in its grove of old olive trees, the trunks dark even in daylight. They walked past it without stopping.

The path continued upward. The vegetation thinned as they gained altitude. The rock showed through the soil in pale slabs. The wind picked up — steady and clean, coming from the east across the wilderness. It pressed against their robes and lifted their hair from their foreheads.

They crested the ridge. The land opened on both sides. To the west, Jerusalem spread below them — familiar, golden in the afternoon light. To the east, the slope descended toward Bethany. The village was small — a cluster of stone houses, low garden walls, a few fig trees and palms near the road. Beyond Bethany, the land rolled away in brown and green folds toward the Jordan Valley and the wilderness beyond. The Dead Sea glinted in the far distance, a strip of silver between the hills.

Jesus stopped. They gathered around him on the high ground. The wind blew across the hilltop. The sky was wide and clear above them. The light was strong.

He lifted up his hands.

The disciples stood still. The wind moved. The olive branches swayed below them on the slopes. Jerusalem sat behind them. Bethany lay ahead.

He blessed them.

While he blessed them — his hands still raised, his voice still carrying on the wind — he was parted from them. He was carried up into heaven.

The disciples watched. Their faces were turned upward. Their eyes followed. The sky was open and depthless above the ridge — blue fading into blue. The wind blew across their robes and their hair. They stood on the hilltop, their feet on the rocky ground, their eyes fixed on the place where he had been.

The wind continued. The olive trees moved on the slopes below. The sound of the city was far away.

They worshiped him. They dropped to their knees on the stone and the dry grass. Their foreheads touched the earth.

Then they rose. They turned back toward Jerusalem. They walked with great joy — back over the ridge, down the familiar path, past the garden, across the valley, through the gate. The road they had walked with him they now walked without him. But they were not grieving. Their steps were quick. Their faces were lifted.

They went into the temple. They were continually there, praising and blessing God.

The road between the city and Bethany lay quiet behind them. The hilltop stood empty. The wind moved across the ridge where he had lifted his hands. The olive trees swayed. The sky held nothing but light.
