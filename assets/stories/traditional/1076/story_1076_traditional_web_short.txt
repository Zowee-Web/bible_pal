Job had lost everything. His animals, his servants, his sons and daughters — all gone. His body had been covered in painful sores from head to foot. And through all of it, Job had spoken to God.

Now God had answered him. God had spoken out of a whirlwind.

Job opened his mouth. "I know that you can do all things," he said. "No purpose of yours can be stopped."

He took a slow breath. "I talked about things I did not understand," he said. "Things too wonderful for me."

Job lowered his face toward the dust and ashes where he sat. "I had heard about you with my ears," he said. "But now my eyes have seen you."

He pressed his hands against the ground. "I take back what I said. I sit here in dust and ashes."

Then God spoke to Eliphaz, one of Job's three friends. "I am angry with you and your two friends," God said. "You did not speak what was right about me, the way my servant Job did."

God told them what to do. "Take seven bulls and seven rams. Go to my servant Job and offer a burnt offering for yourselves. Job will pray for you, and I will accept his prayer."

So Eliphaz and Bildad and Zophar did exactly what God told them. They brought the animals. They made the offering. And Job prayed for his three friends. God accepted Job's prayer.

After Job prayed for his friends, God gave Job back more than he had before. Everything was doubled.

Where Job once had seven thousand sheep, now he had fourteen thousand. Where he once had three thousand camels, now he had six thousand. He had a thousand yoke of oxen and a thousand female donkeys.

His brothers and sisters came to see him. Everyone who had known him before came too. They sat down and ate a meal with him in his house. Each one gave Job a piece of silver and a gold ring.

God gave Job seven sons and three daughters. The oldest daughter was named Jemimah. The second was named Keziah. The third was named Keren-happuch. Job gave them an inheritance along with their brothers.

Job lived one hundred and forty years after this. He saw his children and their children, down to the fourth generation.

Four generations gathered around the old man, silver rings on their fingers, bread on the table, children on the floor at his feet.
