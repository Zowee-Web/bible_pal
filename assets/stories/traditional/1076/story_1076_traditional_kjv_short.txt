Job had lost all. His cattle, his servants, his sons and daughters — all gone. His body had been smitten with sore boils from head to foot. And through all of it, Job had spoken unto God.

Now God had answered him. God had spoken out of the whirlwind.

Job opened his mouth. "I know that thou canst do every thing," he said. "And that no thought of thine can be withholden from thee."

He drew a slow breath. "I have uttered that I understood not," he said. "Things too wonderful for me."

Job bowed his face toward the dust and ashes where he sat. "I have heard of thee by the hearing of the ear," he said. "But now mine eye seeth thee."

He pressed his hands against the ground. "Wherefore I abhor myself, and repent in dust and ashes."

Then God spake unto Eliphaz, one of Job's three friends. "My wrath is kindled against thee and against thy two friends," God said. "For ye have not spoken of me the thing that is right, as my servant Job hath."

God told them what to do. "Take unto you now seven bullocks and seven rams, and go to my servant Job, and offer up for yourselves a burnt offering. And my servant Job shall pray for you. And I will accept his prayer."

So Eliphaz and Bildad and Zophar did even as God had commanded them. They brought the beasts. They made the offering. And Job prayed for his three friends. The Lord accepted the prayer of Job.

And the Lord turned the captivity of Job, when he prayed for his friends. The Lord also gave Job twice as much as he had before.

Where Job once had seven thousand sheep, now he had fourteen thousand. Where he once had three thousand camels, now he had six thousand. He had a thousand yoke of oxen, and a thousand she asses.

His brethren and his sisters came to see him. All they that had been of his acquaintance before came also. They sat down and did eat bread with him in his house. Every man gave him a piece of money, and every one an earring of gold.

The Lord gave Job seven sons and three daughters. The name of the first was Jemima. The name of the second was Kezia. The name of the third was Kerenhappuch. And Job gave them inheritance among their brethren.

Job lived an hundred and forty years after this. He saw his sons and his sons' sons, even four generations.

Four generations gathered round the old man, gold rings upon their fingers, bread upon the table, children upon the floor at his feet.
