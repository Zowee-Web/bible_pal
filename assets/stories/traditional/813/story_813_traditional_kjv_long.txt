It was evening by the sea of Galilee. The sun had dipped low behind the hills, painting the sky with soft purple and deep blue. Jesus was with his disciples after a busy day by the water's edge, teaching the multitudes and telling parables. The people had gathered from many places to hearken unto him. As the sky grew darker, Jesus said unto his disciples, "Let us pass over unto the other side." He desired to leave the multitudes behind and find a quiet place beyond the sea.

So the disciples helped Jesus into a small wooden ship. The water was gentle at the first. Other ships floated nigh, following along. The air was turning cool as night began to fall. As they moved away from the shore, the lights and sounds of the multitude grew still. Jesus found a place in the hinder part of the ship and lay down, his head upon a pillow. He was very weary, and anon he was fast asleep.

Suddenly, without warning, the wind was changed. Black clouds rushed across the sky, and a strong wind began to blow. The quiet lake turned wild. The waves began to rise and beat against the ship, sending cold water over the sides. The ship rocked sore as more wind howled round about them. The little ships that were nigh tossed in the same fierce wind. The disciples laboured to row, but the waves were too great. The water was dark and deep round about them. Every breaking wave shook the ship and made it tilt and creak. Water splashed into the ship, soaking their feet. It sloshed about their toes and splattered upon their garments. The air was filled with noise—wind howling, water breaking, timbers groaning.

The storm waxed stronger. The wind battered their faces, wild and cold. The ship rocked and tipped in the choppy water. The disciples could see the shore no more. The sky was dark and heavy with clouds, and even the stars were hidden. They could scarcely hear themselves above the storm. Every time a wave beat into the ship, more water poured in. The hearts of the disciples began to beat fast. They feared the ship might sink. They felt weary, cold, and afraid. They had sailed this sea aforetime, but they had never seen it so wild.

While the wind roared and the disciples laboured to keep the ship straight, Jesus was still asleep. He slept calmly in the hinder part, even as the water spattered close by. His breathing was peaceful, and his face was quiet. The disciples looked back upon him, marvelling that he was yet asleep. Their voices rose above the wind as they cried one to another. They thought upon how much water was in the ship, and how strong the wind was become.

At last, one of the disciples ran unto Jesus. He kneeled beside him and cried out, "Master, carest thou not that we perish?" The others crowded close, their eyes wide and afraid. The sound of the storm was loud. It was hard to speak, but they had need that Jesus should awake. They all felt sore afraid. They waited as Jesus slowly opened his eyes. He sat up, and looked round about. He beheld the wind, the high waves, and his disciples drenched with water. He arose in the rocking ship. The wind whipped his hair and pulled at his garments, but he was not afraid.

Jesus beheld the swirling water. He lifted up his hand and spake unto the wind. His voice was calm and sure. "Peace, be still," he said. Straightway the wind hearkened. The wild air grew quiet. The waves ceased to rise and fall. The water became flat, smooth, and gentle again. The ship rested softly upon the calm lake. The dark clouds were gone, and only the gentle sound of quiet water and a soft breeze could be heard. All was still.

The disciples gazed upon Jesus. Their hearts yet beat fast, but now for a new reason. They marvelled and wondered how one could speak to the wind and the waves what they should do. Jesus turned unto them and said, "Why are ye so fearful? how is it that ye have no faith?" His eyes were gentle as he looked upon them. The disciples answered him not. They looked one upon another in awe. They said one to another, "What manner of man is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?"

Round about the ship, the night was quiet again. The lake looked as smooth as a glass. The little ships that were nigh now floated gently, no longer tossed by wild waves. The air felt safe and cool. The garments of the disciples were yet wet, but they could breathe easy again. The fear of the storm was gone from them, and in its stead came wonder and peace.

Jesus tarried with his friends in the ship as they floated upon the sea of Galilee. The storm was come and gone. The lake was calm and safe. Jesus had shown forth his power, and the disciples remembered how he had brought peace by his word alone.

For a long moment, no man spake. The men beheld Jesus as he gazed out over the peaceful water, his face soft in the moonlight. Their hearts were still pounding, but slowly the fear faded, and in its place came something lighter—as a gentle glow growing within them. A small splash echoed from somewhere in the darkness, and one of them reached out to steady the side of the ship, feeling the boards no longer tremble beneath his hand. They saw their own faces in the reflection upon the lake—weary, wet, and wide-eyed. But now there was relief and something like hope reflected there also.

Peter let out a shaky breath, scarce believing the storm was truly past. He looked at the others, and beheld the same silent question in their eyes, but also a hint of a smile twisting upon a few of their lips. The wind was gone, but in its place was a sense of something more—the feeling that they were held safe, even in the wildest moment, even when it seemed the world might sink beneath them. James gazed upon Jesus, still finding no words, but his shoulders slowly relaxed. Somewhere behind them, another small ship called out, asking if all were safe. The disciples answered, their voices steadier now, and the simple sound warmed the darkness.

Jesus, beholding their wonder and relief, sat down again. He looked upon each one of them, kind and understanding, as though he already knew all their thoughts and questions. "We shall go unto the other side," he said quietly, his tone gentle and steady. The disciples nodded, trusting him in a new way. They took up the oars again, labouring together this time, not so much with fear but with a quiet sense that all should be well. The night air bare the savour of lake water and cool stone. The splash of oars was steady and soothing.

Slowly, across the quiet sea, the lights of distant houses came into view. The storm was but a memory now, edged with fear yet edged also with faith. As they drew nearer unto the shore, each man knew deep within that, whatsoever storms might come, he that calmed the sea was with them. A hush settled upon their hearts—a hush filled with quiet hope for whatsoever lay ahead.
