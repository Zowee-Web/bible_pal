As the sun began to go down, Jesus and his disciples entered into a small wooden ship by the edge of the sea of Galilee. The sky was turning orange and pink, and the air was gentle and cool. Jesus said unto them, "Let us pass over unto the other side." The disciples helped untie the ship and pushed it away from the shore. Other little ships floated nigh, following along as they set sail. The water was dark, and the gentle waves rocked the ship as they went out upon the sea.

Not long after they had set out, the wind began to blow much harder. The quiet evening was changed swiftly. Dark clouds moved over the water, and the gentle waves became great and strong. Anon, a fierce storm came upon the lake. The wind howled aloud and shook the ship. Tall waves beat against the sides, sending cold, salty spray over the disciples. The water within the ship rose higher as every wave splashed in, making the wooden planks slippery and wet. Round about them, the wind roared, and the disciples laboured to keep the ship from being overthrown. The sky grew dark, and the stars and moon were hidden behind angry clouds.

While the storm shook the ship, Jesus was in the hinder part, asleep on a pillow. He lay still, calm and quiet, even as the rain poured down and the waves smote round about him. The disciples looked one upon another with wide eyes. The water kept rising within the ship, and the wind seemed to wax even louder. Their hands shook as they sought to keep the ship steady, but the power of the storm was too much for them. Though many of the disciples were fishermen, and knew the sea well, the strength of the storm made them sore afraid and small.

At length, one of the disciples ran unto Jesus and stirred him gently. "Master, carest thou not that we perish?" he said, his voice trembling with fear. The storm did not cease, and the water still poured into the ship. Jesus awoke and looked round about at his friends and at the high, rolling waves. He looked not afraid. He arose, water dripping from his garments, and went unto the fore part of the ship. The wind whipped his hair and the waves washed about his feet.

Jesus looked upon the wild wind and the high waves. With a strong, calm voice, he spake unto the wind and the sea. "Peace, be still," he said. Straightway the wind ceased. The waves were stilled. The noise that had filled the air was gone in a moment. The ship, that had rocked and bounced upon the water but a moment before, was now still. The water became smooth and quiet, and the clouds began to drift away. Round about them was a gentle silence, as though the whole world were hearkening.

After the storm was over, Jesus turned unto his disciples and said, "Why are ye so fearful? how is it that ye have no faith?" The disciples looked one upon another with wide eyes and whispered together. They marvelled and were sore amazed. "What manner of man is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?" they said. The water was calm, and their ship floated quietly upon the sea of Galilee. Jesus was with them, and the storm was past.

Slowly, the disciples let out the breaths they had been holding. The fear in their eyes began to fade, and was succeeded by wonder. Some of them wiped the rain from their faces, seeking to understand what was come to pass. The little ships that had followed were now quiet also, their sailors gazing in awe upon Jesus. The clouds brake apart above them, suffering the last colours of the sunset to shine through. The cool evening breeze felt no more frightful—it was soft and peaceful again. One of the disciples reached over and pressed his friend's shoulder, thankful to be safe and together. Gently, they steered the ship toward the far shore, their hearts yet full of questions but also of hope. Jesus looked upon them with warmth, and they knew they were not alone. Even after the fiercest storms, they could trust that he should see them through.
