The morning air was cool and still. Dew rested on every blade of grass, each tiny drop catching the first light. A wide green meadow stretched out in every direction. The grass was soft and thick, bending gently where a light breeze moved across it. Small white flowers dotted the field, their petals open and fresh.

A shepherd stood at the edge of this meadow. His robe was simple and brown, worn soft from many seasons. A sturdy wooden staff rested in his hand, smooth where his fingers had held it for years. Over his shoulder hung a leather bag, and inside it were bread and dried fruit. His sandals were dusty from the path behind him, and his face was tanned from long days under open skies.

Around him, sheep moved slowly through the grass. Some nibbled at the green blades. Others stood close together, their wool thick and white. A few young lambs stayed near their mothers, their small legs stepping carefully through the meadow. The flock was quiet. Birds sang from a row of low trees nearby. A cricket chirped somewhere in the tall grass. Everything here was calm and unhurried.

This was the song of David, sung long ago in the land of Israel. David had once been a shepherd himself. He walked with sheep across hills and through valleys. And so he sang about what he knew — a shepherd who cared for every living thing in his flock.

"The Lord is my shepherd," David sang. "I shall not be in want."

The shepherd in the meadow lifted his hand and whistled softly. The sheep raised their heads. Then they began to walk, following him without rushing. He led them further into the green pasture, where the grass grew even thicker. Here the ground was cool and damp beneath their hooves. Clover grew in patches, purple and sweet-smelling. Butterflies drifted above the flowers, their wings bright orange and yellow.

The shepherd walked slowly so the smallest lambs could keep up. He looked back often, counting each one. When a lamb stumbled on a root, the shepherd waited. The lamb found its footing and trotted forward again. The shepherd nodded and kept walking.

"He makes me lie down in green pastures," the song continued.

One by one, the sheep lowered themselves into the soft grass. Their legs folded beneath them. They rested their chins on the ground. The wool on their backs rose and fell with each slow breath. A warm breeze carried the smell of wild mint across the field. The shepherd sat down among them, setting his staff across his knees. He pulled bread from his bag and broke it into pieces. He chewed quietly, watching the clouds drift overhead. They were high and white and shaped like hills.

No one hurried. No one called out. The meadow held only the sound of wind in the grass and the gentle breathing of resting sheep.

"He leads me beside still waters," David sang.

After a long rest, the shepherd stood and brushed crumbs from his robe. He picked up his staff and began walking again. The sheep rose and followed. The path curved downhill through a grove of olive trees. Their silver-green leaves rustled overhead. Sunlight came through in patches, making bright shapes on the ground. The bark of the trees was rough and twisted, and some of the trunks were very old and wide.

At the bottom of the hill, a stream appeared. It was not rushing or loud. The water moved slowly over smooth brown stones, barely making a sound. The surface was so clear that pebbles on the bottom were easy to see. Green moss grew along the edges, soft as velvet. Tiny fish held still in the current, their silver sides flashing when they turned.

The sheep gathered at the water's edge. They dipped their mouths and drank. The water was cool and clean. The lambs stood beside their mothers, drinking in small sips. The shepherd knelt upstream and cupped water in his hands. He drank, then splashed a little on his face. Drops ran down his beard and fell back into the stream.

"He restores my soul," the song said.

The flock rested near the water for a while. Some sheep stood in the shallow part of the stream, the water barely covering their hooves. Others lay in the shade of the olive trees. A dragonfly landed on a reed at the water's edge, its wings thin and shining. The shepherd leaned against a tree trunk and closed his eyes for a moment. The sound of the stream was steady and soft, like a whisper that never stopped.

When he opened his eyes, the shepherd stood and tapped his staff twice on the ground. The sheep gathered around him. He began walking along a narrow path that followed the stream.

"He guides me in paths of righteousness for his name's sake," David sang.

The path was well-worn and easy to follow. Flat stones were set into the ground in places where the dirt was muddy. The shepherd had walked this way before. He chose each turn carefully, leading the flock around thorny bushes and over gentle rises. Wildflowers grew along both sides — yellow ones and blue ones, nodding on their stems. A rabbit sat near a rock, watching the sheep pass, its nose twitching.

The path led through a low valley. The hills rose on both sides, green and round. Trees lined the ridges above. Here in the valley, the light was softer. Long grass swayed in a quiet breeze. The air smelled of earth and warm stone.

"Even though I walk through the valley," the song continued, "I will not be nervous."

The shepherd walked steadily through the valley. His staff tapped the ground with each step. The sheep followed close behind him, their hooves pressing softly into the dirt path. A hawk circled high above, riding the warm air without flapping its wings. The valley was peaceful and wide. Sunlight reached the valley floor in golden patches where the hills parted.

The shepherd began to hum. The sound was low and steady. The sheep walked in a loose group, some side by side, others in a line. The lambs kept close to the middle of the flock. No one wandered. No one stumbled.

"For you are with me," David sang. "Your rod and your staff, they comfort me."

The shepherd held up his staff so the sheep could see it. He used it to guide a lamb back toward the group when it drifted too far to the side. He tapped the ground ahead to check for loose stones. The staff was long and curved at the top, worn smooth from years of use. It was a tool for helping, for guiding, for keeping everyone together.

The valley opened into a wide, flat area where the grass was green and short. A large flat rock sat in the middle, like a table set out in the open. The shepherd walked to the rock and set down his leather bag. He spread out a cloth and placed food on it — bread, olives, a small jar of oil, a bunch of grapes, and a round of cheese.

"You prepare a table before me," the song said.

The shepherd sat beside the rock and ate. He poured oil from the jar into his hand and rubbed it into the wool of a lamb that came near. The oil was golden and smelled of herbs. It smoothed the lamb's wool and kept its skin from drying in the sun. The lamb stood still, letting the shepherd work.

"You anoint my head with oil," David sang.

Then the shepherd filled a wooden cup from a small spring that bubbled up near the rock. The water rose to the very top of the cup and spilled over the sides, running down his fingers.

"My cup overflows," the song said.

The shepherd drank and set the cup down on the stone. Around him, the sheep grazed in the short grass. The grapes on the cloth were dark purple, dusty with bloom. A ladybug crawled across one of the olives. The breeze was warm and carried the smell of wild thyme from the hillside.

The afternoon light grew golden and long. The shepherd gathered his cloth and food back into his bag. He stood, stretched, and picked up his staff. The sheep lifted their heads and began to follow once more.

"Surely goodness and loving kindness will follow me all the days of my life," David sang.

The path led gently uphill now. The grass was soft underfoot. Small stones clicked together as the sheep walked over them. The sky turned from blue to pale gold, then to a soft orange near the horizon. A few early stars appeared, faint and small.

Ahead, a stone enclosure came into view. Its walls were low and sturdy, built from stacked gray stones. Inside, clean straw was spread thick on the ground. A wooden gate stood open, waiting.

The shepherd led the flock through the gate. Each sheep passed by him, and he touched their backs as they entered. He counted every one. The lambs went in last, stepping over the threshold on wobbly legs. The straw was dry and warm beneath them.

"And I will dwell in the house of the Lord forever," the song ended.

The shepherd closed the gate and latched it with a wooden peg. He sat down just outside, leaning his back against the stone wall. His staff rested beside him. The sheep settled into the straw inside the enclosure. Their breathing slowed. The lambs curled up beside their mothers.

Above, the sky was wide and full of stars now. The air was cool and carried the smell of straw and olive wood. Somewhere in the distance, another shepherd played a flute, the notes drifting across the hills. The music was simple and slow.

The shepherd by the gate pulled his cloak around his shoulders. He watched the stars for a while. Inside the enclosure, the flock was still and safe and warm. The night was quiet. The meadow, the stream, the valley, the table of stone — all rested under the same wide sky. And the shepherd stayed near, keeping watch through the gentle night.
