The morning air was cool and still. Dew rested upon every blade of grass, each tiny drop catching the first light. A wide green meadow stretched out on every side. The grass was soft and thick, bending gently where a light breeze moved athwart it. Small white flowers dotted the field, their petals open and fresh.

A shepherd stood at the edge of this meadow. His robe was simple and brown, worn soft from many seasons. A sturdy wooden staff rested in his hand, smooth where his fingers had held it for years. Over his shoulder hung a leather bag, and within it were bread and dried fruit. His sandals were dusty from the path behind him, and his face was browned from long days under open skies.

Round about him, sheep moved slowly through the grass. Some nibbled at the green blades. Others stood close together, their wool thick and white. A few young lambs stayed near their mothers, their small legs stepping carefully through the meadow. The flock was quiet. Birds sang from a row of low trees nearby. A cricket chirped somewhere in the tall grass. Everything here was calm and unhurried.

This was the song of David, sung long ago in the land of Israel. David himself had once been a shepherd. He walked with sheep across hills and through valleys. And so he sang of that which he knew — a shepherd that cared for every living thing in his flock.

"The Lord is my shepherd," sang David. "I shall not want."

The shepherd in the meadow lifted his hand and whistled softly. The sheep raised their heads. Then they began to walk, following him without haste. He led them further into the green pasture, where the grass grew yet thicker. Here the ground was cool and damp beneath their hooves. Clover grew in patches, purple and sweet of savour. Butterflies drifted above the flowers, their wings bright orange and yellow.

The shepherd walked slowly that the smallest lambs might keep up. He looked back oft, counting each one. When a lamb stumbled upon a root, the shepherd waited. The lamb found its footing and trotted forward again. The shepherd nodded and kept walking.

"He maketh me to lie down in green pastures," the song continued.

One by one, the sheep lowered themselves into the soft grass. Their legs folded beneath them. They rested their chins upon the ground. The wool upon their backs rose and fell with each slow breath. A warm breeze bore the savour of wild mint athwart the field. The shepherd sat down among them, setting his staff athwart his knees. He took bread from his bag and brake it into pieces. He chewed quietly, beholding the clouds drift overhead. They were high and white and shaped like hills.

None hasted. None called out. The meadow held only the sound of wind in the grass and the gentle breathing of resting sheep.

"He leadeth me beside the still waters," sang David.

After a long rest, the shepherd arose and brushed crumbs from his robe. He took up his staff and began walking again. The sheep rose and followed. The path curved downhill through a grove of olive trees. The silver-green leaves thereof rustled overhead. Sunlight came through in patches, making bright shapes upon the ground. The bark of the trees was rough and twisted, and some of the trunks were very old and wide.

At the foot of the hill, a stream appeared. It was not rushing nor loud. The water moved slowly over smooth brown stones, scarce making a sound. The surface was so clear that pebbles upon the bottom were easy to be seen. Green moss grew along the edges, soft as velvet. Tiny fish held still in the current, their silver sides flashing when they turned.

The sheep gathered at the water's edge. They dipped their mouths and drank. The water was cool and clean. The lambs stood beside their mothers, drinking in small sips. The shepherd kneeled upstream and cupped water in his hands. He drank, then sprinkled a little upon his face. Drops ran down his beard and fell back into the stream.

"He restoreth my soul," said the song.

The flock rested near the water for a season. Some sheep stood in the shallow part of the stream, the water scarce covering their hooves. Others lay in the shadow of the olive trees. A dragonfly lighted upon a reed at the water's edge, its wings thin and shining. The shepherd leaned against a tree trunk and closed his eyes for a moment. The sound of the stream was steady and soft, like a whisper that never ceased.

When he opened his eyes, the shepherd arose and tapped his staff twice upon the ground. The sheep gathered round about him. He began walking along a narrow path that followed the stream.

"He leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name's sake," sang David.

The path was well-worn and easy to follow. Flat stones were set into the ground in places where the dirt was miry. The shepherd had walked this way before. He chose each turn carefully, leading the flock about thorny bushes and over gentle rises. Wildflowers grew along both sides — yellow ones and blue ones, nodding upon their stems. A hare sat near a rock, watching the sheep pass, his nose twitching.

The path led through a low valley. The hills rose up on both sides, green and round. Trees lined the ridges above. Here in the valley, the light was softer. Long grass swayed in a quiet breeze. The air bore the savour of earth and warm stone.

"Yea, though I walk through the valley," the song continued, "I will fear no evil."

The shepherd walked steadily through the valley. His staff tapped the ground with each step. The sheep followed close behind him, their hooves pressing softly into the dirt path. A hawk circled high above, riding the warm air without flapping its wings. The valley was peaceful and wide. Sunlight reached the valley floor in golden patches where the hills parted.

The shepherd began to hum. The sound was low and steady. The sheep walked in a loose company, some side by side, others in a line. The lambs kept close to the midst of the flock. None wandered. None stumbled.

"For thou art with me," sang David. "Thy rod and thy staff they comfort me."

The shepherd held up his staff that the sheep might see it. He used it to guide a lamb back toward the company when it drifted too far to the side. He tapped the ground ahead to prove for loose stones. The staff was long and curved at the top, worn smooth from years of use. It was a tool for helping, for guiding, for keeping every one together.

The valley opened into a wide, flat place where the grass was green and short. A large flat rock sat in the midst, like a table set forth in the open. The shepherd walked unto the rock and set down his leather bag. He spread out a cloth and placed food thereon — bread, olives, a small cruse of oil, a cluster of grapes, and a round of cheese.

"Thou preparest a table before me," said the song.

The shepherd sat beside the rock and ate. He poured oil from the cruse into his hand and rubbed it into the wool of a lamb that came near. The oil was golden and bore the savour of herbs. It smoothed the lamb's wool and kept its skin from drying in the sun. The lamb stood still, suffering the shepherd to work.

"Thou anointest my head with oil," sang David.

Then the shepherd filled a wooden cup from a small spring that bubbled up near the rock. The water rose unto the very brim of the cup and ran over the sides, running down his fingers.

"My cup runneth over," said the song.

The shepherd drank and set the cup down upon the stone. Round about him, the sheep grazed in the short grass. The grapes upon the cloth were dark purple, dusty with bloom. A ladybird crawled athwart one of the olives. The breeze was warm and bore the savour of wild thyme from the hillside.

The afternoon light grew golden and long. The shepherd gathered his cloth and food back into his bag. He arose, stretched, and took up his staff. The sheep lifted their heads and began to follow once more.

"Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life," sang David.

The path led gently uphill now. The grass was soft underfoot. Small stones clicked together as the sheep walked over them. The sky turned from blue to pale gold, then to a soft orange near the horizon. A few early stars appeared, faint and small.

Ahead, a stone fold came into view. The walls thereof were low and sturdy, builded of stacked grey stones. Within, clean straw was spread thick upon the ground. A wooden gate stood open, waiting.

The shepherd led the flock through the gate. Each sheep passed by him, and he touched their backs as they entered. He counted every one. The lambs went in last, stepping over the threshold on wobbly legs. The straw was dry and warm beneath them.

"And I will dwell in the house of the Lord for ever," the song ended.

The shepherd shut the gate and fastened it with a wooden peg. He sat down just without, leaning his back against the stone wall. His staff rested beside him. The sheep settled into the straw within the fold. Their breathing slowed. The lambs curled up beside their mothers.

Above, the sky was wide and full of stars now. The air was cool and bore the savour of straw and olive wood. Somewhere afar off, another shepherd played upon a pipe, the notes drifting athwart the hills. The music was simple and slow.

The shepherd by the gate drew his cloak about his shoulders. He beheld the stars for a season. Within the fold, the flock was still and safe and warm. The night was quiet. The meadow, the stream, the valley, the table of stone — all rested under the same wide sky. And the shepherd stayed near, keeping watch through the gentle night.
