They came to a place which was named Gethsemane. The garden sat upon the slope of the Mount of Olives, east of the city. Olive trees stood in thick rows, their gnarled trunks dark in the moonlight. The air was cool. The ground was soft beneath their feet — packed earth and fallen leaves. Below them, across the Kidron Valley, the walls of Jerusalem were lit by lamps and torches.

Jesus saith unto his disciples, "Sit ye here, while I shall pray."

Most of them tarried. They found places among the trees — backs against trunks, cloaks drawn tight. The night was quiet. The meal was heavy in them.

Jesus took with him Peter and James and John, and they went further into the garden. The trees grew close together. The moonlight came through in patches upon the ground. They walked in silence.

Jesus stopped. Something changed in him. His steps grew slow. His shoulders fell. He began to be sore amazed and to be very heavy. He turned unto the three.

"My soul is exceeding sorrowful unto death: tarry ye here, and watch."

He went forward — a stone's cast beyond them. He fell on the ground. His knees struck the earth. His hands pressed into the dirt. His forehead touched the ground.

"Abba, Father, all things are possible unto thee; take away this cup from me: nevertheless not what I will, but what thou wilt."

The olive branches moved overhead. The wind passed through the garden. The three men sat against the trees behind him. The sounds of the city were far off.

He rose. He came unto them and found them sleeping. Peter's head was tilted against a tree trunk. James and John lay upon the ground, their cloaks gathered beneath them.

Jesus stood over Peter.

"Simon, sleepest thou? Couldest not thou watch one hour? Watch ye and pray, lest ye enter into temptation. The spirit truly is ready, but the flesh is weak."

Peter opened his eyes. He stirred. He sat upright. Jesus turned and walked back into the darkness.

He fell to the ground again. He prayed, speaking the selfsame words. The same request. The same yielding. His breath was heavy. Sweat ran down his face in the cool air.

He rose and returned. They were asleep again. Their eyes were heavy, neither wist they what to answer him. Their faces were blank with weariness.

He left them the third time. He went back to the same place. The ground was marked where his knees had pressed. He fell again. He prayed again. The garden held no sound but the wind in the branches and his voice against the earth.

The minutes passed. The moon moved across the sky. The olive leaves shifted and settled.

He came the third time. Peter and James and John were sleeping still. Their breathing was slow and even.

"Sleep on now, and take your rest: it is enough, the hour is come; behold, the Son of man is betrayed into the hands of sinners. Rise up, let us go; lo, he that betrayeth me is at hand."

Through the olive trees, light appeared — torches moving between the trunks. The sound of many footsteps carried through the garden. Voices murmured. Metal clinked against metal. The light grew brighter, drawing nigh through the darkness.

The three awoke. They stood. They looked toward the approaching torches.

Jesus stood facing the light. He moved not.
