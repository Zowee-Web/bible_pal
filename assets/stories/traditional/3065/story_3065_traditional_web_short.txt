The people of Reuben and Gad owned very many livestock. When they saw the lands of Jazer and Gilead, they recognized country suited for their herds. They came to Moses, Eleazar the priest, and the leaders of the congregation. After naming the towns in the region Yahweh had struck before Israel, they asked that this land be given to them. They did not want to be brought across the Jordan for an inheritance.

Moses answered with a warning. Their brothers were going to war; would Reuben and Gad remain behind? Such a choice could discourage the Israelites from entering the land Yahweh had given them. At Kadesh Barnea, the fathers of Israel had gone up to see the land. Except for Caleb and Joshua, they had turned the people’s hearts away from following Yahweh. His anger had burned, and that generation had wandered forty years until it was consumed. Moses warned the two tribes not to rise in their fathers’ place and increase that anger. If they turned away, Israel could again be left in the wilderness.

The men revised their pledge. They would build sheepfolds for their livestock and fortified towns for their little ones. Then they would arm themselves and go ahead of the Israelites until every tribe had been brought to its inheritance. Their families would remain in the fortified towns because of the land’s inhabitants. The warriors would not return home until the other Israelites had received their portions. They would claim no inheritance west of the Jordan, because their portion had come to them on its eastern side.

Moses made the agreement conditional. If their armed men crossed the Jordan before Yahweh, remained for the battle, and stayed until Yahweh’s enemies were driven out and the land subdued, they could return guiltless. Gilead would then be their possession. Moses told them that failure would be sin against Yahweh, and their sin would find them out. They were to build the towns and folds they had promised and perform their word.

Reuben and Gad accepted the terms. Their children, wives, flocks, and livestock would remain in Gilead’s towns, but every servant equipped for war would cross before Yahweh into battle, just as Moses had said.
