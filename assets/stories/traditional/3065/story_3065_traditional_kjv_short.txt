The children of Reuben and the children of Gad had a very great multitude of cattle. When they beheld the land of Jazer and the land of Gilead, they saw that the place was fit for cattle. They came unto Moses, Eleazar the priest, and the princes of the congregation. Having named the cities in the country which the LORD had smitten before Israel, they asked that this land be given unto them. They desired not to be brought over Jordan for an inheritance.

Moses answered them with a warning. “Shall your brethren go to war, and shall ye sit here?” Their choice could discourage the heart of the children of Israel from going into the land which the LORD had given them. The fathers of Israel had gone up from Kadeshbarnea to see the land. Save Caleb and Joshua, they had turned Israel’s heart from following the LORD. His anger was kindled, and that generation wandered forty years until it was consumed. Moses warned these men not to rise in their fathers’ stead as an increase of sinful men. If they turned away, the LORD would yet again leave Israel in the wilderness.

The men then changed their pledge. They would build sheepfolds for their cattle and cities for their little ones. Afterward they would go ready armed before the children of Israel until every tribe was brought unto its place. Their little ones would dwell in fenced cities because of the inhabitants of the land. The warriors would not return unto their houses until the other Israelites had inherited, every man his inheritance. They would not inherit on the farther side of Jordan, for their inheritance had fallen on the eastern side.

Moses made the agreement conditional. If their armed men went before the LORD to war, passed over Jordan, and remained until the LORD had driven out his enemies and the land was subdued, they might return guiltless. Gilead should then be their possession. Moses said that failure would be sin against the LORD: “Be sure your sin will find you out.” They were to build cities for their little ones and folds for their sheep, and then do that which had proceeded out of their mouth.

The children of Gad and Reuben accepted the terms. Their children, wives, flocks, and cattle would remain in the cities of Gilead, but every servant armed for war would pass over before the LORD to battle, even as Moses spake.
