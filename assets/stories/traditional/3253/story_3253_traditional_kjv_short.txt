King Solomon called Shimei before him and commanded him to build a house in Jerusalem. There he was to dwell, without going elsewhere. Solomon warned him that on the day he went out and crossed the brook Kidron, he would surely die. His blood would be upon his own head.

Shimei answered that the saying was good. He promised that the king's servant would do as his lord the king had said.

For many days Shimei dwelt in Jerusalem. At the end of three years, two of his servants ran away to Achish, son of Maachah, king of Gath. Shimei was told that his servants were in Gath.

He saddled his ass and went to Achish at Gath to seek them. From Gath he brought his servants home.

Solomon heard that Shimei had gone from Jerusalem to Gath and had returned. The king summoned Shimei once more.

Had he not made Shimei swear by the Lord, Solomon asked, and warned him that he would surely die on the day he went abroad anywhere? The king recalled Shimei's agreement: he had said that the word he heard was good. Why then, Solomon asked, had Shimei broken the oath of the Lord and the command with which the king had charged him?

Solomon spoke further of David, his father. Solomon told Shimei, “Thou knowest all the wickedness which thine heart is privy to, that thou didst to David my father.” Therefore, the king declared, the Lord would return Shimei's wickedness upon his own head.

Solomon went on to declare that King Solomon would be blessed, and that David's throne would be established before the Lord for ever.

The king then gave his command to Benaiah, son of Jehoiada. Benaiah went out, fell upon Shimei, and slew him.

The kingdom was established in Solomon's hand.
