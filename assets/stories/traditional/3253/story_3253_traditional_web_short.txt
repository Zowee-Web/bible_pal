King Solomon sent for Shimei and told him, “Build yourself a house in Jerusalem, and live there, and don’t go anywhere else.” Solomon warned Shimei, “For on the day you go out and pass over the brook Kidron, know for certain that you will surely die. Your blood will be on your own head.”

Shimei answered the king, “What you say is good. As my lord the king has said, so will your servant do.”

Shimei stayed in Jerusalem many days. At the end of three years, two of his slaves ran away to Achish, son of Maacah, the king of Gath. Word came to Shimei that his slaves were there.

Shimei got up and saddled his donkey. He went to Achish at Gath to seek his slaves, and brought them back from Gath.

Solomon received a report of Shimei’s journey: he had gone from Jerusalem to Gath and had come back again. The king sent for Shimei and called him before him.

The king asked whether he had not put Shimei under oath by Yahweh and warned him that he would surely die on the day he went anywhere else. Solomon reminded Shimei of his answer: he had called the saying good. Why, the king asked, had Shimei failed to keep Yahweh's oath and the command Solomon had given him?

Solomon then spoke of David, his father. Solomon told Shimei, “You know in your heart all the wickedness that you did to David my father.” Therefore, Solomon declared, Yahweh would return Shimei's wickedness upon his own head.

The king continued: King Solomon would be blessed, and David's throne would be established before Yahweh forever.

Then Solomon gave the command to Benaiah, the son of Jehoiada. Benaiah went out, struck Shimei, and killed him.

The kingdom was established in Solomon's hand.
