It was the day after John the Baptist had pointed unto Jesus passing by and said, "Behold the Lamb of God." Two of John's disciples had followed Jesus — one of them Andrew of Bethsaida, who had been a fisherman with his brother on the sea of Galilee — and they had abode with Jesus in the lodging where he dwelt, and it was about the tenth hour when they came unto him. In the morning, Andrew went forth to find his brother Simon.

One of the two which heard John speak, and followed him, was Andrew, Simon Peter's brother.

He first findeth his own brother Simon, and saith unto him, "We have found the Messias!" (which is, being interpreted, the Christ).

And he brought him to Jesus. And when Jesus beheld him, he said,

"Thou art Simon the son of Jona: thou shalt be called Cephas" (which is by interpretation, A stone).

And the three of them stood together upon the way by the village of Bethsaida — the elder fisherman who had been brought to see, the younger brother who had brought him, and the Master who had given the elder brother a new name — with the new sun of the day arising over the fishing boats moored along the shore of Galilee.
