It was the day after John the Baptist had pointed to Jesus passing by and said, "Behold, the Lamb of God." Two of John's disciples had followed Jesus — one of them Andrew of Bethsaida, who had been a fisherman with his brother on the lake of Galilee — and they had spent the day with Jesus in the lodging where he was staying, and it had been about the tenth hour when they came to him. In the morning, Andrew went out to find his brother Simon.

One of the two who heard John, and followed him, was Andrew, Simon Peter's brother.

He first found his own brother, Simon, and said to him, "We have found the Messiah!" (which is, being interpreted, Christ).

He brought him to Jesus. Jesus looked at him, and said,

"You are Simon the son of Jonah. You shall be called Cephas" (which is by interpretation, Peter).

And the three of them stood together on the road by the village of Bethsaida — the older fisherman who had been brought to see, the younger brother who had brought him, and the teacher who had given the older brother a new name — with the new sun of the day rising over the fishing boats moored along the shore of Galilee.
