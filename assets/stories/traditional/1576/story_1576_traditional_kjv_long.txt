The Lord sent Moses unto Pharaoh, with Aaron his brother for his prophet, and told him from the first how it would be. Pharaoh would not hearken; and the Lord would harden Pharaoh's heart, and multiply his signs in Egypt, until by great judgments he brought forth his people, and the Egyptians knew that he was the Lord.

So the two went in. When Pharaoh asked a wonder of them, Aaron cast down his rod, and it became a serpent; and though the magicians of Egypt cast down their rods and made serpents also, Aaron's rod swallowed up all of theirs. Yet Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he hearkened not, as the Lord had said.

Then the signs began. Aaron smote the river, and the waters were turned to blood before Pharaoh and his servants; the fish died, the river stank, and Egypt could not drink thereof. But the magicians did so with their enchantments, and Pharaoh's heart was hardened; he turned into his house, neither did he set his heart to this. Seven days were fulfilled, and the frogs came up, and covered the land, and came into the houses and the bedchambers and the ovens of Egypt. This time Pharaoh sent for Moses. Entreat the Lord to take away the frogs, he said, and I will let the people go. Moses prayed, and the frogs died out of the houses, and were gathered in heaps, till the land stank. But when Pharaoh saw there was respite, he hardened his heart, and hearkened not.

Aaron smote the dust of the earth, and it became lice upon man and beast throughout all the land. The magicians assayed with their enchantments to do the like, and could not, and they said unto Pharaoh, This is the finger of God. But Pharaoh's heart was hardened still. Then the Lord sent grievous swarms of flies into the house of Pharaoh and over all Egypt; yet in Goshen, where his people dwelt, there was none, that Pharaoh might know the Lord was in the land. Pharaoh bargained, that they should sacrifice in the land, or not go far, and desired their prayer; Moses prayed, and there remained not one fly. But Pharaoh hardened his heart at this time also, and would not let them go.

Then the hand of the Lord fell upon the cattle of Egypt, and all of them died in the field, while of the cattle of Israel not one died. Yet the heart of Pharaoh was hardened. Moses sprinkled ashes of the furnace toward heaven, and it became a boil breaking forth with blains upon man and beast, so that the magicians could not stand before him. And now it was the Lord who hardened the heart of Pharaoh, and he hearkened not, as the Lord had spoken.

Moses stretched forth his rod, and the Lord rained down hail such as had not been in Egypt since it was a nation, with thunder, and fire running along upon the ground, smiting man and beast and every tree of the field; only in Goshen was there no hail. Pharaoh sent for Moses. I have sinned this time, he said. The Lord is righteous, and I and my people are wicked. Entreat the Lord, and I will let you go. But when the thunder and the hail were ceased, he sinned yet more, and hardened his heart, he and his servants, and would not let Israel go.

The locusts came up on an east wind and covered the land, till it was darkened, and ate all that the hail had left, till nothing green remained. Pharaoh called in haste and confessed again — I have sinned — and begged that this death be taken away; a west wind carried the locusts into the sea, and not one was left. But the Lord hardened Pharaoh's heart. Then Moses stretched forth his hand toward heaven, and a thick darkness that might be felt was over Egypt three days, while the children of Israel had light in their dwellings. Pharaoh would let the people go, but keep back their flocks; Moses would not leave a hoof behind. And the Lord hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he drove Moses from him. See my face no more, he said; for in the day thou seest my face thou shalt die. Thou hast spoken well, Moses answered; I will see thy face again no more.

One plague remained. About midnight, Moses warned, the Lord would go out into the midst of Egypt, and all the firstborn should die — from the firstborn of Pharaoh upon his throne to the firstborn of the maidservant behind the mill, and all the firstborn of the cattle — and there should be a great cry throughout all the land, such as had never been, nor should be again. But against the children of Israel not a dog should move his tongue, that they might know the Lord put a difference between Egypt and Israel. Then Moses went out from Pharaoh in a great anger.

And the Lord told his people how they should be kept through that night. Every household should take a lamb without blemish, a male of the first year, and keep it until the fourteenth day, and kill it at evening. They should take of the blood and strike the two side posts and the upper door post of the house, and roast the lamb, and eat it that night with unleavened bread and with bitter herbs, their loins girded, their shoes on their feet, and their staff in their hand, and eat it in haste; for it is the Lord's passover. When he passed through Egypt to smite the firstborn, the blood should be a token upon their houses; when he saw it he would pass over, and no plague should be upon them. This night they should keep as a feast for ever, Moses told the elders, and when their children asked its meaning, they should say it was the sacrifice of the Lord's passover, who passed over the houses of Israel when he smote Egypt. And the people bowed their heads and worshipped, and did as they were commanded.

At midnight the Lord smote all the firstborn of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh upon his throne to the firstborn of the captive in the dungeon, and all the firstborn of the cattle. Pharaoh rose up in the night, he and all his servants and all Egypt, and there was a great cry, for there was not a house where there was not one dead. He called for Moses and Aaron in the night. Rise up, and get you forth from among my people, he said, both ye and the children of Israel; go, serve the Lord, as ye have said. Take your flocks and your herds, and be gone — and bless me also. The Egyptians were urgent upon them to send them out in haste, saying, We be all dead men, and gave them silver and gold and raiment for the asking. So that night the children of Israel journeyed from Rameses, about six hundred thousand men on foot beside their families, and a mixed multitude with them, carrying the dough before it was leavened. It was the night to be much observed unto the Lord throughout their generations — the night he brought them out from the land of Egypt.
