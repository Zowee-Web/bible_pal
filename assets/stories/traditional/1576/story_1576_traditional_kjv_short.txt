The Lord sent Moses and Aaron unto Pharaoh with a demand — let my people go — and a warning that Pharaoh would not hearken, for the Lord would harden his heart and multiply his signs until he brought Israel out. When they came, Aaron's rod became a serpent and swallowed up the serpents of the magicians; yet Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he hearkened not.

So the plagues came, one upon another. The river was turned to blood. Frogs came up over the land, and lice, and swarms of flies; and each time, when Pharaoh was given respite, he hardened his heart, though his own magicians said, This is the finger of God. The cattle of Egypt died while Israel's were spared, and still his heart was hardened. Then boils breaking forth with blains came upon man and beast, and this time it was the Lord who hardened Pharaoh's heart. Hail and thunder and fire smote the field, and when they ceased Pharaoh hardened his heart again, he and his servants. Then locusts ate what was left, and a thick darkness that might be felt was over Egypt three days while Israel had light — and now the Lord hardened Pharaoh's heart, and hardened it again, until Pharaoh drove Moses from him: See my face no more.

One plague remained. About midnight, Moses warned, all the firstborn of Egypt should die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh to the firstborn of the maidservant; but against Israel not a dog should move his tongue.

And the Lord told his people how they should be kept through that night. Every household took a lamb without blemish, killed it at evening, and struck the blood on the two side posts and the upper door post. They ate it roasted, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs, their loins girded and in haste; for it is the Lord's passover: when he saw the blood, he would pass over, and no plague should fall there. And the people bowed their heads and worshipped.

At midnight the Lord smote all the firstborn of Egypt, and there was a great cry, for there was not a house where there was not one dead. Pharaoh rose up in the night and called for Moses. Get you forth from among my people, he said, both ye and the children of Israel; go, serve the Lord — and bless me also. The Egyptians were urgent to send them out in haste, and the children of Israel journeyed from Rameses, six hundred thousand men beside their families, carrying dough before it was leavened — the night the Lord brought them out of Egypt.
