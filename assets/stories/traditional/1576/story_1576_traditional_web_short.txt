Yahweh sent Moses and Aaron to Pharaoh with a demand — let my people go — and a warning that Pharaoh would not listen, for Yahweh would harden his heart and multiply his signs until he brought Israel out. When they came, Aaron's rod became a serpent and swallowed the serpents of the magicians; yet Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he would not listen.

So the plagues came, one upon another. The river turned to blood. Frogs swarmed the land, and gnats, and flies; and each time, when Pharaoh was given respite, he hardened his own heart, though his own magicians said, "This is the finger of God." The livestock of Egypt died while Israel's were spared, and still his heart was stubborn. Then boils broke out on man and beast — and from that plague on, it was Yahweh who hardened Pharaoh's heart. Hail and thunder and fire struck the field; locusts ate what was left; a darkness that could be felt lay three days over Egypt, while Israel had light. Pharaoh confessed his sin, and bargained, and each time Yahweh hardened his heart, until he drove Moses away: "See my face no more."

One plague remained. At midnight, Moses warned, every firstborn in Egypt would die, from Pharaoh's son to the servant's; but against Israel not a dog would move its tongue.

And Yahweh told his people how to be kept through that night. Each household took a lamb without blemish, killed it at evening, and struck its blood on the two doorposts and the lintel. They ate it roasted, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs, dressed for the road and in haste, for it was Yahweh's Passover: where he saw the blood, he would pass over, and no plague would fall there. And the people bowed their heads and worshiped.

At midnight Yahweh struck all the firstborn of Egypt, and there was a great cry, for there was not a house without one dead. Pharaoh rose in the night and called for Moses. "Get out from among my people," he said, "you and the children of Israel; go, serve Yahweh — and bless me also." The Egyptians pressed them out in haste, and the children of Israel went out from Rameses, six hundred thousand men besides their families, carrying dough that had no time to rise — the night Yahweh brought them out of Egypt.
