Yahweh sent Moses to Pharaoh with his brother Aaron for his spokesman, and told him from the first how it would go. Pharaoh would not listen; and Yahweh would harden Pharaoh's heart, and multiply his signs in Egypt, until by great judgments he brought his people out and the Egyptians knew that he was Yahweh.

So the two went in. When Pharaoh demanded a wonder, Aaron cast down his rod, and it became a serpent; and though the magicians of Egypt threw down their rods and made serpents of their own, Aaron's rod swallowed up all of theirs. Yet Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he would not listen, as Yahweh had said.

Then the signs began. Aaron struck the river, and its waters turned to blood before Pharaoh and his servants; the fish died, the river stank, and Egypt could not drink of it. But the magicians did the same by their secret arts, and Pharaoh's heart was hardened; he turned into his house and would not take even this to heart. Seven days passed, and the frogs came, swarming out of the river into the houses and the bedchambers and the ovens of Egypt. This time Pharaoh sent for Moses. Entreat Yahweh to take the frogs away, he said, and I will let the people go. Moses prayed, and the frogs died in heaps until the land stank of them. But when Pharaoh saw there was a respite, he hardened his own heart, and did not listen.

Aaron struck the dust, and it became gnats upon man and beast through all the land. The magicians tried by their arts to do the same and could not, and they said to Pharaoh, "This is the finger of God." But Pharaoh's heart was hardened still. Then Yahweh sent thick swarms of flies into Pharaoh's house and over all Egypt — yet in Goshen, where his people lived, there were none, that Pharaoh might know Yahweh was in the land. Pharaoh bargained, that they should sacrifice in the land, or not go far, and he asked for prayer; Moses prayed, and not one fly remained. But Pharaoh hardened his heart this time also, and did not let them go.

Yahweh's hand fell next on the livestock of Egypt, and all of it died in the field, while of Israel's not one beast died. Still the heart of Pharaoh was stubborn. Then Moses scattered ashes of the furnace toward the sky, and boils broke out on man and beast, so that the magicians could not so much as stand before him. And now it was Yahweh who hardened the heart of Pharaoh, and he would not listen, as Yahweh had spoken.

Moses stretched out his rod, and Yahweh rained down hail such as Egypt had never known, with thunder and with lightning that flashed down to the earth, striking man and beast and every tree of the field; only in Goshen there was no hail. Pharaoh sent for Moses. "I have sinned this time," he said. "Yahweh is righteous, and I and my people are wicked. Pray, and I will let you go." But when the thunder and the hail had ceased, he sinned yet more, and hardened his heart, he and his servants, and would not let Israel go.

The locusts came on an east wind and covered the land until it was darkened, and ate all that the hail had left, till nothing green remained on tree or field. Pharaoh called in haste and confessed again — "I have sinned" — and begged that this death be taken from him; a west wind drove the locusts into the sea, and not one was left. But Yahweh hardened Pharaoh's heart. Then Moses stretched out his hand toward the sky, and a darkness that could be felt lay three days over Egypt, while the children of Israel had light in their dwellings. Pharaoh would let the people go, but keep back their flocks; Moses would not leave a hoof behind. And Yahweh hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he drove Moses away. "See my face no more," he said, "for in the day you see it you shall die." "You have spoken well," Moses answered. "I will see your face no more."

One plague remained. About midnight, Moses warned, Yahweh would go out into the midst of Egypt, and every firstborn would die — from the firstborn of Pharaoh on his throne to the firstborn of the servant behind the mill, and all the firstborn of livestock — and a great cry would go up such as Egypt had never heard, nor would hear again. But against Israel not a dog would so much as move its tongue, that they might know Yahweh had made a distinction between Egypt and Israel. Then Moses went out from Pharaoh in hot anger.

And Yahweh told his people how they would be kept through that night. Each household was to take a lamb without blemish, a male of the first year, and keep it until the fourteenth day, and kill it at evening. They were to take its blood and strike the two doorposts and the lintel of the house, and to roast the lamb and eat it that night with unleavened bread and bitter herbs, dressed for the road — belt fastened, sandals on their feet, staff in hand — and eat it in haste, for it was Yahweh's Passover. When he passed through Egypt to strike the firstborn, the blood would be a sign upon their houses; where he saw it he would pass over, and no plague would fall there. This night was to be kept as a feast to Yahweh forever, Moses told the elders, so that when their children asked what it meant, they would answer that it was the sacrifice of Yahweh's Passover, who passed over the houses of Israel when he struck Egypt. And the people bowed their heads and worshiped, and did as they were commanded.

At midnight Yahweh struck all the firstborn of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh on his throne to the firstborn of the captive in the dungeon, and all the firstborn of livestock. Pharaoh rose in the night, and all his servants, and all Egypt, and there was a great cry, for there was not a house without one dead. He called for Moses and Aaron in the night. "Rise up, and get out from among my people," he said, "both you and the children of Israel; go, serve Yahweh, as you have said. Take your flocks and your herds, and be gone — and bless me also." The Egyptians pressed them to leave in haste, saying, "We are all dead men," and gave them silver and gold and clothing for the asking. So that night the children of Israel set out from Rameses, about six hundred thousand men on foot besides their families, and a mixed multitude with them, carrying the dough that had no time to rise. It was the night to be kept to Yahweh through all their generations — the night he brought them out of the land of Egypt.
