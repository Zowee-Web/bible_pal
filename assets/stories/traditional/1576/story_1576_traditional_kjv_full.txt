The Lord sent Moses unto Pharaoh, with Aaron his brother to speak for him, and told him beforehand that Pharaoh would not hearken — that the Lord would harden Pharaoh's heart and multiply his signs, until by great judgments he brought Israel out of Egypt. So they went in; and when Pharaoh asked a wonder, Aaron's rod became a serpent, and swallowed up the serpents of the magicians. Yet Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he hearkened not.

Then the plagues began. Aaron smote the river, and it was turned to blood; the fish died, and Egypt could not drink. The magicians did so with their enchantments, and Pharaoh's heart was hardened. Frogs came up out of the river into every house, and Pharaoh promised to let the people go if Moses would entreat them away; but when the frogs died and there was respite, he hardened his heart. The dust became lice, and the magicians who could not do the like said, This is the finger of God — but Pharaoh's heart was hardened still. Swarms of flies filled Egypt while Goshen was spared; Pharaoh bargained, and desired their prayer, then hardened his heart at this time also, and would not let them go.

The cattle of Egypt died in the field while Israel's died not, and the heart of Pharaoh was hardened. Moses sprinkled ashes toward heaven, and boils breaking forth with blains came upon man and beast, till the magicians could not stand before him. And now it was the Lord who hardened the heart of Pharaoh. Hail fell such as had not been in all the land, with thunder and with fire running along the ground, smiting all that was in the field, though Goshen was spared; Pharaoh confessed, I have sinned; the Lord is righteous; yet when it ceased he hardened his heart once more. Locusts covered the land and ate all that the hail had left; Pharaoh confessed again and begged their prayer, but the Lord hardened his heart. Then a thick darkness that might be felt was over Egypt three days, while Israel had light; Pharaoh would let the people go but keep back their flocks, and Moses would not leave a hoof behind; and the Lord hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he drove Moses out — See my face no more.

One plague remained. About midnight, Moses warned, all the firstborn of Egypt should die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh upon his throne to the firstborn of the maidservant, and all the firstborn of the cattle; but against Israel not a dog should move his tongue. Then he went out from Pharaoh in a great anger.

And the Lord told his people how they should be kept. Every household should take a lamb without blemish, kill it at evening on the fourteenth day, and strike the blood on the two side posts and the upper door post. They should eat it that night, roasted, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs, their loins girded and their staff in hand, and in haste; for it is the Lord's passover. When he passed through to smite Egypt, the blood should be a token upon their houses; when he saw it, he would pass over, and no plague should fall there. This night they should keep for ever, and tell their children it was the sacrifice of the Lord's passover, who spared the houses of Israel when he smote Egypt. And the people bowed their heads and worshipped.

At midnight the Lord smote all the firstborn of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh upon his throne to the firstborn of the captive in the dungeon. Pharaoh rose up in the night, and all Egypt, and there was a great cry, for there was not a house where there was not one dead. He called for Moses and Aaron. Get you forth from among my people, he said, both ye and the children of Israel; go, serve the Lord. Take your flocks and your herds, and bless me also. The Egyptians were urgent to send them out in haste, saying, We be all dead men. So the children of Israel journeyed from Rameses, about six hundred thousand men on foot beside their families, carrying the dough before it was leavened — the night to be much observed unto the Lord, who brought them out from the land of Egypt.
