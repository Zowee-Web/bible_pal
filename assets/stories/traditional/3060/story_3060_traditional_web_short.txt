After Abijah died, Asa reigned in his place, and the land was quiet for ten years. Asa did what was good and right in Yahweh’s eyes. He removed foreign altars and high places, broke the pillars, cut down the Asherah poles, and commanded Judah to seek Yahweh and obey his law. During the years of rest, he built fortified cities with walls, towers, gates, and bars.

Then Zerah the Ethiopian came to Mareshah with a million troops and three hundred chariots. Asa led out Judah and Benjamin and set the battle in array in the valley of Zephathah. Before the armies met, Asa cried to Yahweh: “There is no one besides you to help between the mighty and the one who has no strength. Help us, Yahweh our God, for we rely on you.”

Yahweh struck the Ethiopians before Asa and Judah. They fled, and Asa’s people pursued them to Gerar. The Ethiopian force could not recover. Judah carried away much plunder, struck the cities around Gerar, took sheep and camels from the livestock tents, and returned to Jerusalem.

The Spirit of God came on Azariah son of Oded. He went to meet Asa and told Judah and Benjamin that Yahweh would be with them while they were with him. If they sought him, he would be found; if they forsook him, he would forsake them. “Be strong,” Azariah said. “Do not let your hands be slack, for your work will be rewarded.”

Asa took courage. He removed abominations from Judah, Benjamin, and the captured cities of Ephraim, and renewed Yahweh’s altar. People from Israel joined the gathering in Jerusalem. They sacrificed from the plunder and entered a covenant to seek Yahweh with all their heart and soul. Whoever would not seek him was to be put to death, whether small or great, man or woman. They swore with shouting, trumpets, and cornets, and all Judah rejoiced at the oath. Yahweh was found by them and gave them rest all around.

Asa also removed Maacah from being queen mother because she had made an abominable Asherah image. He cut it down, ground it to dust, and burned it at the brook Kidron. Though the high places remained in Israel, Asa brought dedicated silver, gold, and vessels into God’s house. There was no more war until the thirty-fifth year of his reign.
