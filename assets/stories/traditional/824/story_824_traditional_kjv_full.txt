The sun hung gently over the hills of Galilee. A warm, golden light touched the grass and shone upon the people gathered together in the open place. Mothers held little children close, while fathers stood nigh with weary faces. The ground was dusty, and every one wore sandals that left small marks in the earth. Some wiped the sweat from their brows, having walked far to be there. Round about, there was a quiet sense of waiting, as though every one felt somewhat of great moment was about to come to pass.

A multitude filled the place. Many in the multitude were weary. Their shoulders drooped, and their steps were heavy. They had come from far-off fields and busy markets, from small villages and quiet homes. Each one bare cares and troubles—heavy loads that they carried every day. Some were anxious for bread sufficient for their households. Others felt heavy laden by rules they strove to keep, or by hard labour that seemed never to end. Their hard days made them weary, deep within.

The afternoon pressed on. The heat made the air shimmer. Some sat under the shade of trees, rubbing their sore feet. Others leaned upon their staves, closing their eyes for but a moment of rest. There were sad faces and quiet sighs. The people hearkened as the elders spake of old laws—rules that seemed too hard to keep. The speech of all the things they must do became another burden. The weight of their troubles felt as a heavy sack laid upon every back.

Into this weary multitude came Jesus. His eyes were kind and gentle. He beheld the people with deep care. His voice was calm but strong as he spake. The people hearkened, some turning from their thoughts, some straightening somewhat at the sound of his words. Jesus saw their heavy loads. He understood their weary bodies and weary hearts.

Then Jesus spake words full of comfort. His voice was soft, as a cool breeze upon a warm day. He called out, "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest." The words were as water unto thirsty mouths. Some blinked with wonder. Others felt a small hope begin to grow within. Jesus' eyes were warm, and his smile held peace.

He continued, "Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls." The people sought to understand this new yoke. A yoke was that which oxen bare upon their shoulders while labouring in the field. Their own troubles felt as rough yokes, pressing down and making every step hard. But Jesus' yoke was different. He promised it would neither hurt nor weary them. His yoke was light and easy to bear.

At the first, some in the multitude marvelled whether this could be true. But as they hearkened, they beheld kindness in his countenance. Jesus was not giving more rules to keep, nor more labour to be done. Rather, he offered rest. He cared in a gentle manner, welcoming each one even as he was. He turned no man away. His words made heads lift and shoulders relax. Some smiled upon their children. A hush fell over the gathering as Jesus' promise sank in.

He ended with words that fell as a soft mantle wrapping round about the whole multitude. "For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light." The people let out the breaths they had been holding. Their hearts felt lighter, as though a great weight had been taken away. Some closed their eyes and whispered a quiet thanks. The warmth of the afternoon now brought comfort in the stead of weariness. With Jesus among them, the world seemed full of hope and rest.

And so, in the quiet gathering place in Galilee, the people found what they had been longing for. They came weary, but through Jesus' gentle bidding, they found rest. His kind voice and open arms welcomed each weary heart. The people went their way with lighter steps, knowing that they might always come unto Jesus and find the rest their souls needed.

As the people stood, the last words of Jesus echoed in their minds. Some lingered, not willing for the peace to end nor the moment to pass too quickly. Children plucked at their mothers' sleeves, their countenances brighter than before. A father knelt to gather his youngest into his arms, tears mingling with a smile. In small companies, friends spake in gentle voices, repeating the words they had heard and letting the hope settle deep within.

As the sun dipped lower, the hills glowed with soft colour. The people walked toward their homes bearing somewhat new—a rest that was not earned but freely given. And so in Galilee, under the gentle watch of the evening sky, peace grew quietly, and hope lingered through the night.
