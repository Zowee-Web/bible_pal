The sun stood high over the land of Samaria, its light beating down upon the rocky way that led unto the small city called Sychar. The air was dry, and every footfall stirred a puff of fine dust that floated across the land. Just without the city, at the edge of the fields, a well digged long ago by Jacob shone in the sunlight. Its ancient stones promised refreshment unto the weary traveller, and beside it, the earth was cool underfoot.

Jesus, having left Judaea and journeyed toward Galilee, came unto this place. He was wearied with his journey. He sat alone upon the stone ledge of Jacob's well, his garments brushed with the dust of the way. The disciples were gone away unto Sychar to buy meat, leaving him to rest by the quiet road, compassed about with the stillness of noon. Even the birds were silent beneath the heavy sun.

Not far down the path came a woman of Samaria. She walked with a steady but wary pace, bearing her waterpot. It was the sixth hour, midday, when most would seek the shade. Yet she came at this hour, choosing the hottest part of the day, when the well would be deserted. Alone she drew near, her eyes quick and watchful, bearing the unseen burdens that set her apart from others in the city.

As she came nigh, Jesus spake. "Give me to drink," he said unto her simply. His voice was gentle, but the words startled her. Marvelling, she beheld him. He was a Jew, and the Jews have no dealings with the Samaritans, much less did they speak so openly. The woman hesitated, her wonder mingled with suspicion, and she saith unto him, "How is it that thou, being a Jew, askest drink of me, which am a woman of Samaria?" The gulf between them was plain—race, tradition, ages of division.

Jesus answered and said unto her, "If thou knewest the gift of God, and who it is that saith to thee, Give me to drink; thou wouldest have asked of him, and he would have given thee living water." His words hung in the warm air, strange and full of mystery. She glanced at the deep well and the empty hands of the stranger. "Sir," saith she unto him, "thou hast nothing to draw with, and the well is deep: from whence then hast thou that living water? Art thou greater than our father Jacob, which gave us the well, and drank thereof himself?" She named the history that bound her people unto the land and unto the well's unchanging waters.

Jesus answered and said unto her, "Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again: but whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life." Something in his tone stirred the longings she scarce named, her own thirst deeper than she could utter. She saith unto him, "Sir, give me this water, that I thirst not, neither come hither to draw."

There was a pause, the quiet broken only by the faint echo of her request. Jesus then saith unto her, "Go, call thy husband, and come hither." The question pressed gently into a wound she had laboured hard to cover. She answered and said, "I have no husband." Jesus looked upon her with knowing kindness and said unto her, "Thou hast well said, I have no husband: for thou hast had five husbands; and he whom thou now hast is not thy husband: in that saidst thou truly."

Her mask slipped as astonishment swept over her. The stranger saw her as she truly was, past the veils of her weariness and hidden griefs. She saith unto him, "Sir, I perceive that thou art a prophet. Our fathers worshipped in this mountain; and ye say, that in Jerusalem is the place where men ought to worship." Pain and confusion coloured her words—the old contention between Jew and Samaritan a shield for her still-bruised spirit.

Jesus answered gently, "Woman, believe me, the hour cometh, when ye shall neither in this mountain, nor yet at Jerusalem, worship the Father. Ye worship ye know not what: we know what we worship: for salvation is of the Jews. But the hour cometh, and now is, when the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth: for the Father seeketh such to worship him. God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth."

The woman hearkened, her heart stirred with a new manner of hope and wonder. She saith unto him, "I know that Messias cometh, which is called Christ: when he is come, he will tell us all things." Jesus looked upon her with compassion and deep certainty. He saith unto her, "I that speak unto thee am he."

In that quiet place, beside the cool stones of Jacob's well, the woman was seen and known, her past uncovered not for shame, but for healing. As the disciples returned along the dusty road, she stood by the well, forever changed by her meeting with the Messiah who offered her living water that would never run dry. (John 4:4-26, KJV)
