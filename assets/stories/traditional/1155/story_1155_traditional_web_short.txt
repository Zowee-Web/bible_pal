After the donkeys of Saul's father were lost, Saul had gone out with his servant to seek them. They came to the land of Zuph, and they did not find them. So they came to the city of Samuel the seer, and Samuel met them at the gate.

The Lord had told Samuel the day before, "Tomorrow about this time I will send you a man out of the land of Benjamin. You shall anoint him to be prince over my people Israel, that he may save my people out of the hand of the Philistines."

When Samuel saw Saul, the Lord said to him, "Behold, the man of whom I told you. This one shall have authority over my people."

Samuel took Saul into the high place. He set him at the head of those who were guests, and he said to him, "Set this portion before you and eat. It is reserved for you against the appointed time."

In the morning, Samuel went out with Saul to the edge of the city. He said to Saul, "Tell the servant to pass on before us, but you stand still a while, that I may show you the word of God."

Then Samuel took the vial of oil and poured it on Saul's head, and he kissed him. He said, "Has not the Lord anointed you to be prince over his inheritance?"

Samuel said, "When you have departed from me today, you shall meet two men by Rachel's tomb. They will tell you the donkeys are found. Then you shall go on, and you shall meet a band of prophets coming down from the high place. The Spirit of the Lord will come mightily on you, and you shall prophesy with them, and shall be turned into another man."

It was so, that when Saul had turned his back to go from Samuel, God gave him another heart. All those signs came to pass that day. When they came to the hill, behold, a band of prophets met him, and the Spirit of God came mightily on him, and he prophesied among them.

The people who had known him before saw him prophesying with the prophets, and they said one to another, "What has come over the son of Kish? Is Saul also among the prophets?"
