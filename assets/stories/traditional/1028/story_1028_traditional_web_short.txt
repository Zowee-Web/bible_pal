The Lord appeared to Abraham near the great oaks of Mamre. It was the hottest part of the day. The sun stood high in a pale sky, and the air shimmered above the dry ground. Abraham sat at the opening of his tent, resting in the shade.

Then he looked up. Three men stood nearby. He had not heard them coming.

As soon as Abraham saw them, he jumped to his feet. He did not walk — he ran. An old man running across the hot ground to meet three strangers. He bowed low, his face near the dust.

"My lord," Abraham said, "please do not pass by. Let me bring water so you can wash your feet. Rest under the tree. Let me bring bread so you can refresh yourselves."

The visitors agreed. "Do as you have said."

Abraham hurried into the tent. "Quick!" he told Sarah. "Take fine flour and make bread cakes." Sarah's hands went to work right away, pressing and shaping the dough.

Abraham did not stop. He ran to the herd and picked out a tender young calf. He gave it to a servant, who began preparing it. While the bread baked and the meat cooked, Abraham gathered butter and fresh milk.

When everything was ready, he set it all before the visitors under the tree. Bread, butter, milk, and tender meat — a feast prepared as fast as he could manage. Abraham stood nearby while they ate, ready to bring anything more they needed.

Then one of the visitors spoke. "Where is Sarah your wife?"

"There, in the tent," Abraham answered.

The visitor said, "I will certainly return to you when the season comes round, and Sarah your wife will have a son."

Sarah was listening just inside the tent door. She and Abraham were both very old — far past the age when people have babies. Sarah laughed quietly to herself. A baby? At her age?

But the Lord said to Abraham, "Why did Sarah laugh? Is anything too hard for the Lord? At the appointed time I will return, and Sarah will have a son."

Sarah was afraid. "I did not laugh," she said.

But God said, "No. You did laugh."

The words hung in the warm air under the oak tree. Nothing too hard for the Lord. The promise settled over them like shade on a hot day.
