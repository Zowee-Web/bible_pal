The Lord appeared unto Abraham in the plains of Mamre. It was the heat of the day. The sun stood high in a pale sky, and the air shimmered above the dry ground. Abraham sat in the tent door, resting in the shade.

Then he lifted up his eyes. And lo, three men stood by him. He had not heard them coming.

When Abraham saw them, he ran to meet them from the tent door. An old man running across the hot ground to meet three strangers. He bowed himself toward the ground, his face nigh unto the dust.

"My lord," Abraham said, "I pray thee, pass not away. Let a little water, I pray you, be fetched, that ye may wash your feet. Rest yourselves under the tree. And I will fetch a morsel of bread, and comfort ye your hearts."

The visitors agreed. "So do, as thou hast said."

Abraham made haste into the tent. "Make ready quickly!" he said unto Sarah. "Take fine meal and make cakes upon the hearth." Sarah's hands went to work straightway, pressing and shaping the dough.

Abraham did not tarry. He ran unto the herd and fetched a tender calf. He gave it unto a servant, who hasted to dress it. While the bread baked and the meat was made ready, Abraham gathered butter and fresh milk.

When all was ready, he set it before the visitors under the tree. Bread, butter, milk, and tender meat — a feast prepared with all the haste he could muster. Abraham stood by them under the tree while they did eat, ready to fetch any thing else they had need of.

Then one of the visitors spake. "Where is Sarah thy wife?"

"Behold, in the tent," Abraham answered.

The visitor said, "I will certainly return unto thee according to the time of life, and lo, Sarah thy wife shall have a son."

Sarah was hearkening just within the tent door. She and Abraham were both well stricken in years — far past the age when folk have children. Sarah laughed within herself. A child? At her age?

But the Lord said unto Abraham, "Wherefore did Sarah laugh? Is any thing too hard for the Lord? At the time appointed I will return, and Sarah shall have a son."

Sarah was afraid. "I laughed not," she said.

But God said, "Nay; but thou didst laugh."

The words hung in the warm air beneath the oak. Nothing too hard for the Lord. The promise settled over them like shade in the heat of the day.
