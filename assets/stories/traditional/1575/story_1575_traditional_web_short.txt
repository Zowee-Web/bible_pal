Jesus said, "The Kingdom of Heaven will be like ten virgins, who took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom."

Five of them were foolish, and five were wise. When the foolish took their lamps, they took no oil with them. But the wise took oil in their vessels, along with their lamps — enough to fill them again when they burned low. So ten lamps went out to meet the bridegroom, and only five carried oil to keep them burning.

The bridegroom did not come at once. The evening stretched on, and while he delayed, they all slumbered, and they slept — the wise and the foolish alike.

Then, at midnight, a cry rang out in the dark: "Look! The bridegroom is coming! Come out to meet him!"

All ten virgins rose, and each one trimmed her lamp to make it burn. Five lamps steadied. Five were going out.

"Give us some of your oil," the foolish said to the wise, "for our lamps are going out."

But the wise answered, "What if there is not enough for us and for you together? Go instead to those who sell, and buy oil for yourselves."

So the foolish went away to buy. And while they were gone, the bridegroom came. Those who were ready — the five whose lamps still burned — went in with him to the wedding feast.

And the door was shut behind them.

Afterward, the other virgins came back. They stood at the shut door and called, "Lord, Lord, open to us!"

But the answer came from within: "Most certainly I tell you — I do not know you." And the door did not open.

Then Jesus said, "Watch therefore. For you do not know the day, nor the hour, in which the Son of Man is coming."
