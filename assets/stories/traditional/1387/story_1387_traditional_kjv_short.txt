David and his six hundred men had come down from Aphek on the third day. They had marched a long road back from the host of the Philistines that had not desired them in the battle, and they had come unto the rise above Ziklag in the late afternoon, looking to behold the roofs of the town and the smoke of the cooking fires. They beheld smoke of another kind. And they came down.

When David and his men were come to Ziklag on the third day, the Amalekites had invaded the south, and Ziklag, and smitten Ziklag, and burned it with fire;

And had taken the women captives, that were therein: they slew not any, either great or small, but carried them away, and went on their way.

So David and his men came to the city, and, behold, it was burned with fire; and their wives, and their sons, and their daughters, were taken captives.

Then David and the people that were with him lifted up their voice and wept, until they had no more power to weep.

And David's two wives were taken captives, Ahinoam the Jezreelitess, and Abigail the wife of Nabal the Carmelite.

And David was greatly distressed; for the people spake of stoning him, because the soul of all the people was grieved, every man for his sons and for his daughters: but David encouraged himself in the Lord his God.

And the men sat down in the ashes of that which had been their houses. Some put dust upon their heads. Some stood and would not sit. The wives were gone; the sons and the daughters were gone; the stones of the well were blackened in the heat. And David, after he had wept with the men, found his strength again in his God, and the camp at Ziklag began to gather itself for the road that should follow the Amalekites south into the wilderness.
