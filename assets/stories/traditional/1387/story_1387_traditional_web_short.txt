David and his six hundred men had come down from Aphek on the third day. They had marched a long road back from the Philistine army that had not wanted them in the battle, and they had come to the rise above Ziklag in the late afternoon expecting to see the roofs of the town and the smoke of the cooking fires. They saw smoke of another kind. And they came down.

When David and his men had come to Ziklag on the third day, the Amalekites had made a raid on the South, and on Ziklag, and had struck Ziklag, and burned it with fire,

and had taken captive the women and all who were in it, both small and great. They didn't kill any, but carried them off, and went their way.

When David and his men came to the city, behold, it was burned with fire; and their wives, their sons, and their daughters were taken captive.

Then David and the people who were with him lifted up their voice and wept until they had no more power to weep.

David's two wives were taken captive, Ahinoam the Jezreelitess, and Abigail the wife of Nabal the Carmelite.

David was greatly distressed; for the people spoke of stoning him, because the souls of all the people were grieved, every man for his sons and for his daughters; but David strengthened himself in Yahweh his God.

And the men sat down in the ashes of what had been their houses. Some put dust on their heads. Some stayed standing and would not sit. The wives were gone; the sons and the daughters were gone; the stones of the well were blackened in the heat. And David, after weeping with the men, found his strength again in his God, and the camp at Ziklag began to gather itself for the road that would follow the Amalekites south into the wilderness.
