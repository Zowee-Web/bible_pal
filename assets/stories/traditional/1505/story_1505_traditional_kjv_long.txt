The king of Syria warred against Israel. In his tent at the end of the day he took counsel with his servants, and he leaned over a low table where lamps burned in clay dishes, and he set his finger on a place. "In such and such a place shall be my camp," he said. The captains nodded. They went out into the dusk and gave the orders. By morning the chosen ground was held.

But the king of Israel passed not that way. He sent men ahead, and they found the Syrian camp where the king had set his finger. He saved himself there.

It came to pass again. The king of Syria moved his tent and chose another place, and again the king of Israel passed not. It came to pass more than twice. Three times. Four. The Syrian captains began to count the failures upon their fingers and to look one upon another.

The heart of the king of Syria was sore troubled. He called his servants together within the tent. The lamps had burned low. His voice was low with them. "Will ye not shew me which of us is for the king of Israel?"

The servants were silent. They knew the question was an accusation. Each man's eye searched the eye of the man next unto him. Then one of them spake. "None, my lord, O king. But Elisha, the prophet that is in Israel, telleth the king of Israel the words that thou speakest in thy bedchamber."

The king sat back. The accusation in the room lifted, and another thing settled in its place — a quieter, stranger fear. He had thought he was alone when he made his plans. He had thought the walls of his tent were walls. He took a breath.

"Go and spy where he is," he said, "that I may send and fetch him."

Word came back. "Behold, he is in Dothan."

The king sent horses and chariots and a great host. They came by night. They moved without torches over the last hills. They compassed the city of Dothan about in the dark, and they waited for the dawn to take the prophet.

Within the city, the servant of the man of God was risen early in the morning. The light was yet thin. He climbed the stairs unto the wall to look out over the country, as servants will do at first light, half awake, expecting nothing. He reached the top. He set his hands upon the stones.

He saw them.

The hills round about Dothan were no longer hills. They were horses. They were chariots. They were a great host, ringed wholly round about the city, banner and shield and spear. The first sun caught their helmets. The servant breathed not. He turned and went back down the stairs. He ran not; his legs would not let him run; he walked the way a man walketh in a dream wherein the air hath gone thick.

He found Elisha. "Alas, my master!" he said. "How shall we do?"

Elisha was already awake. He stood near the door of the house, looking out at the morning the way a man looketh at a morning he hath been given many times. He went not unto the wall. He asked not the servant to describe what he had seen. He answered without turning.

"Fear not: for they that be with us are more than they that be with them."

The servant could not answer. The number he had counted was very great.

Then Elisha prayed. He raised not his voice. The words were spoken into the open air of the doorway. "LORD, I pray thee, open his eyes, that he may see."

The servant felt nothing change in his body. But the morning changed.

He looked again at the hills.

The mountain was full of fire.

Horses of fire. Chariots of fire. They stood upon the ridges and upon the slopes, ringed round about Elisha, ringed round about the city — outside the Syrian ring, around it, above it. They burned without smoke. They moved not. Their light was not the light of the rising sun; the rising sun was beside it, smaller. The servant stood in the doorway and his mouth was open, and Elisha stood beside him with the same settled face he had worn when he had not gone forth to look.

The servant began, very slowly, to understand what Elisha had been seeing all the morning.

The Syrian army came down the slope toward the city. Elisha went forth to meet them. He prayed again, with the same quiet voice. "Smite this people, I pray thee, with blindness." The word went forth. The men of Syria stopped where they were. Their eyes were open. Their eyes saw nothing they could name.

Elisha came down unto where they stood. He spake unto them as a man speaketh unto lost travellers. "This is not the way," he said, "neither is this the city. Follow me, and I will bring you to the man whom ye seek."

He turned. He began to walk. The whole army of Syria, the horses and chariots and the great host, fell in behind him. The walk was a long one. The road climbed. The road fell. The road passed villages whose names the Syrian captains had carried in their heads for years and could not now place. Children stood by the way and watched the strange procession pass — a single man on foot, leading an army that looked not at where it was going. Elisha walked at the pace of an old prophet on a road he knew. The men of Syria followed him, step by step, all the long miles unto Samaria.

When they were come into the city, Elisha prayed once more. "LORD, open the eyes of these men, that they may see."

Their eyes were opened.

The Syrian captains looked up. The gates they had passed under were the gates of Samaria. The streets round about them were the streets of the king of Israel's own city. Israelite soldiers stood at every corner of the square, in arms, watching them.

The king of Israel came down. When he saw the host standing in his own square, his hand went to his sword and his voice rose in eagerness. "My father, shall I smite them? Shall I smite them?"

Elisha looked upon him. He answered slowly.

"Thou shalt not smite them. Wouldest thou smite those whom thou hast taken captive with thy sword and with thy bow? Set bread and water before them, that they may eat and drink, and go to their master."

The king stood with his hand upon the hilt. Then he let his hand fall.

A great feast was prepared in the city. Long tables were set up in the open. Loaves were carried out and set down, and jars of water, and dishes of meat. The men of Syria sat. They sat the way men sit who know not if they shall rise. They looked upon the food before them. They looked upon the Israelite soldiers round about them, who stood with their arms lowered. They looked upon Elisha, who walked among the tables as an host walketh among guests.

They did eat. They drank. The captains who had counted failures upon their fingers in the king's tent ate the bread of Israel out of Israel's own hand. The strangeness of it was a quietness over the whole square.

When they had eaten and drunken, Elisha sent them away. They went back unto their king.

And the bands of Syria came no more into the land of Israel.
