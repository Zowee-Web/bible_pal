In the seventh month, Ishmael came to Mizpah with ten men, princes of the king. He was Nethaniah's son, Elishama's grandson, of the seed royal. They ate bread there with Gedaliah, son of Ahikam.

Then Ishmael and his ten men rose and slew Gedaliah with the sword. Gedaliah, Ahikam's son and Shaphan's grandson, had been made governor by Babylon's king. Ishmael also slew all the Jews with Gedaliah at Mizpah, the Chaldeans found there, and the men of war.

On the second day after Gedaliah's death, when no man knew of it, fourscore men came from Shechem, Shiloh, and Samaria. Their beards were shaven, their clothes rent, and they had cut themselves. They bore offerings and incense for the house of the Lord.

Ishmael went out from Mizpah to meet them, weeping along the way. He bade them come to Gedaliah, son of Ahikam.

When they reached the midst of the city, Ishmael and his men slew them and cast them into a pit. But ten men pleaded that he spare them, saying they had treasures of wheat, barley, oil, and honey in the field. Ishmael forbore and did not slay those ten among their brethren.

The pit into which he cast the bodies of those slain because of Gedaliah was one King Asa had made for fear of Baasha, king of Israel. Ishmael filled it with the dead.

He carried away captive everyone remaining in Mizpah, including the king's daughters and the people Nebuzar–adan, captain of the guard, had entrusted to Gedaliah. With these captives he departed toward the Ammonites.

Johanan, son of Kareah, and all the captains with him heard of Ishmael's evil deeds. Taking all their men, they went to fight him and found him beside the great waters at Gibeon.

The captives were glad when they saw Johanan and his captains. All those taken from Mizpah turned about and went unto Johanan.

Ishmael escaped from Johanan with eight men and went to the Ammonites.

Johanan and his captains gathered the remnant recovered from Ishmael after Gedaliah's death: mighty men of war, women, children, and eunuchs brought back from Gibeon. They departed and dwelt at the habitation of Chimham, beside Beth–lehem, intending to enter Egypt.

They feared the Chaldeans because Ishmael had slain Gedaliah, whom Babylon's king had made governor in the land.
