In the seventh month, Ishmael came to Mizpah with ten men. He was Nethaniah's son, Elishama's grandson, of royal descent and one of the king's chief officers. They ate bread there with Gedaliah, son of Ahikam.

Then Ishmael and his ten men rose and killed Gedaliah with the sword. Gedaliah, Ahikam's son and Shaphan's grandson, was the governor appointed by Babylon's king. Ishmael also killed all the Jews with Gedaliah at Mizpah and the Chaldean soldiers found there.

On the second day after Gedaliah's death, when no one knew of it, eighty men arrived from Shechem, Shiloh, and Samaria. Their beards were shaved, their clothes torn, and they had cut themselves. They carried meal offerings and frankincense for Yahweh's house.

Ishmael went from Mizpah to meet them, weeping as he went. He invited them to come to Gedaliah, Ahikam's son.

Once they reached the city's middle, Ishmael and his men killed them and cast them into a pit. But ten men pleaded for their lives, saying they had stores of wheat, barley, oil, and honey hidden in the field. Ishmael stopped and spared those ten from their brothers' fate.

The pit where he threw the dead beside Gedaliah was one King Asa had made for fear of Baasha, Israel's king. Ishmael filled it with the slain.

He took captive everyone left in Mizpah, including the king's daughters and the people Nebuzaradan, captain of the guard, had entrusted to Gedaliah. With these captives he departed toward the children of Ammon.

Johanan, Kareah's son, and all the captains with him heard of Ishmael's evil deeds. They took all their men to fight him and found him beside Gibeon's great waters.

When the captives saw Johanan and his captains, they were glad. All those carried away from Mizpah turned back and went to Johanan.

Ishmael escaped from Johanan with eight men and went to the children of Ammon.

Johanan and his captains gathered the remnant recovered from Ishmael after Gedaliah's killing: men of war, women, children, and eunuchs brought back from Gibeon. They departed and stayed at Geruth Chimham, near Bethlehem, intending to enter Egypt.

They feared the Chaldeans because Ishmael had killed Gedaliah, the governor whom Babylon's king had appointed over the land.
