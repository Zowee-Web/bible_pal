Paul and Silas passed through Amphipolis and Apollonia and came unto Thessalonica, where was a synagogue of the Jews. Paul went in unto them, as his manner was. For three sabbath days he reasoned with them out of the scriptures, opening and alleging that Christ must needs have suffered and risen again from the dead. He preached that Jesus was Christ.

Some believed and consorted with Paul and Silas. A great multitude of devout Greeks joined them, and of the chief women not a few. But certain Jews which believed not, being moved with envy, took lewd fellows of the baser sort and gathered a company. They set the city on an uproar, assaulted Jason’s house, and sought to bring Paul and Silas out unto the people.

Finding them not, the company drew Jason and certain brethren unto the rulers of the city. They cried that men who had turned the world upside down had come thither also and that Jason had received them. Their accusation charged the men with acting contrary to Caesar’s decrees and saying that there was another king, one Jesus. The people and the rulers were troubled upon hearing these things. Having taken security of Jason and of the other, they let them go.

The brethren immediately sent Paul and Silas away by night unto Berea. When they came thither, they entered the synagogue of the Jews. The Bereans received the word with all readiness of mind and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so. Therefore many believed, including honourable Greek women and not a few men.

When the Jews of Thessalonica learned that Paul preached the word of God at Berea, they came thither also and stirred up the people. Then the brethren immediately sent Paul away toward the sea, while Silas and Timotheus abode there still. Those who conducted Paul brought him unto Athens. They received his commandment that Silas and Timotheus should come unto him with all speed, and then departed.
