Paul and Silas passed through Amphipolis and Apollonia and came to Thessalonica, where there was a Jewish synagogue. Paul entered according to his custom. For three Sabbath days he reasoned from the Scriptures, explaining and demonstrating that the Christ had to suffer and rise again from the dead. He proclaimed that Jesus was the Christ.

Some were persuaded and joined Paul and Silas. A great multitude of devout Greeks joined them, along with many chief women. Other hearers remained unpersuaded. Some unpersuaded Jews gathered wicked men from the marketplace, formed a crowd, and set the city in an uproar. They assaulted Jason’s house and sought Paul and Silas there, intending to bring them out before the people.

When they did not find Paul and Silas, they dragged Jason and certain brothers before the city rulers. They accused the visitors of turning the world upside down and charged Jason with receiving them. They further alleged that the men acted against Caesar’s decrees by saying there was another king, Jesus. These charges troubled the multitude and the rulers. After taking security from Jason and the others, the rulers released them.

The brothers immediately sent Paul and Silas away by night to Beroea. Upon arriving, the two men entered the Jewish synagogue there. The people in Beroea received the word with readiness of mind and examined the Scriptures daily to determine whether these things were so. Many believed, including prominent Greek women and not a few men.

When Jews from Thessalonica learned that Paul was also proclaiming God’s word in Beroea, they came there and agitated the multitudes. The brothers immediately sent Paul toward the sea, while Silas and Timothy remained in Beroea. Paul’s escorts brought him as far as Athens. Before they departed, they received his command that Silas and Timothy should come to him very quickly.
