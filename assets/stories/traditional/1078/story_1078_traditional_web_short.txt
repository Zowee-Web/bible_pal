Far to the east, in a land of wide deserts and ancient cities, wise men studied the night sky. They watched the stars the way farmers watch the rain. They knew the patterns. They knew when something was new.

One night, a star appeared that they had never seen before. It was bright. It was different. The wise men rose from their charts and spoke to one another: "A king has been born — a king of the Jews."

So they packed their camels. They gathered treasures. They set out on a long, long journey, following that star toward the land of Israel.

When they arrived in Jerusalem, they began asking everyone they met. "Where is the one who has been born king of the Jews?" they said. "We saw his star when it rose, and we have come to worship him."

Word spread quickly through the narrow streets. Soon King Herod himself heard about these visitors and their strange question.

Herod was troubled. The whole city of Jerusalem was troubled with him.

He called together the chief priests and the teachers of the law. "Where is this Christ supposed to be born?" he asked them.

They answered right away. "In Bethlehem, in the land of Judah. For this is what the prophet has written: 'And you, Bethlehem, in the land of Judah, are by no means least among the rulers of Judah. For out of you will come a ruler who will shepherd my people Israel.'"

Then Herod called the wise men to meet with him secretly. He asked them exactly when the star had appeared. He listened carefully to every detail.

"Go to Bethlehem," Herod told them. "Search carefully for the child. When you find him, report back to me, so that I too may go and worship him."

The wise men left Jerusalem. And there was the star — the same star they had seen in the east — going ahead of them. When they saw it, joy filled them completely. Such joy.

The star moved across the sky and stopped. It stopped right over the place where the child was.

The wise men stepped inside. They saw the child with Mary, his mother. They knelt down. Their foreheads touched the ground.

Then they opened their treasure boxes. They set out gold, gleaming and heavy. They set out frankincense, rich and sweet-smelling. They set out myrrh, dark and fragrant.

That night, God warned them in a dream not to go back to Herod. So the wise men packed their camels again and took a different road, winding east through the hills, back toward home.
