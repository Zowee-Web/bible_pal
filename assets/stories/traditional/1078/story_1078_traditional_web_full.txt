Far to the east, beyond the deserts and the rivers, wise men studied the night sky. They lived in a land of wide plains and ancient cities where scholars kept careful records of every star and every pattern above them. They knew the heavens the way a shepherd knows his flock.

One night, something changed.

A star appeared that none of them had ever seen before. It was bright and steady, and it hung in the western sky like a lamp set on a high shelf. These wise men believed it was a sign. A king had been born — a king of the Jews.

So they prepared for a journey. They loaded their camels with supplies and with treasures wrapped carefully in cloth. They filled waterskins and packed dried fruit and bread for the long miles ahead. Then they set out, following the star westward across the desert.

The journey was not short. Day after day, their camels swayed beneath them across sand and stone. Night after night, the star burned in the sky ahead of them.

At last they reached the land of Israel. They came to Jerusalem, the great city with its tall walls and crowded markets and the gleaming temple on the hill. Surely this was where a king would be born.

They began asking people in the streets. "Where is the one who has been born king of the Jews?" they said. "We saw his star when it rose in the east, and we have come to worship him."

The people stared. They whispered to one another. Word traveled fast through the winding streets and open courtyards.

When King Herod heard what these foreign visitors were asking, he was troubled. A new king? He had not heard of any new king. The whole city of Jerusalem buzzed with unease alongside him.

Herod summoned the chief priests and the teachers of the law. These were the men who knew the scriptures best, who had spent their lives reading the ancient scrolls.

"Where is the Christ supposed to be born?" Herod asked them.

They did not hesitate. "In Bethlehem, in the land of Judah," they said. "For this is what the prophet has written: 'And you, Bethlehem, in the land of Judah, are by no means least among the rulers of Judah. For out of you will come a ruler who will shepherd my people Israel.'"

Herod nodded slowly. Then he sent for the wise men. He called them to a private meeting, away from the crowds and the priests.

"Tell me," he said, leaning forward. "When exactly did this star appear?"

The wise men told him. They described the night, the brightness, the position in the sky. Herod listened to every word.

Then he said, "Go to Bethlehem. Search carefully for the child. And when you find him, come back and tell me, so that I too may go and worship him."

The wise men left Jerusalem. They passed through the city gate and out onto the road. The evening air was cool, and dust rose beneath their camels' feet.

And there it was.

The star. The same star they had followed from the east. It moved ahead of them in the darkening sky, steady and bright. When they saw it, their hearts leapt. Joy — deep, rushing, overwhelming joy — filled every one of them. They had been right. They had not traveled all those miles for nothing.

The star led them south, past olive groves and low stone walls, past fields where shepherds tended their flocks. The road was quiet. The air smelled of earth and dry grass.

Then the star stopped. It hung perfectly still over one particular place in the town of Bethlehem.

The wise men climbed down from their camels. Their robes were dusty from the journey, their sandals worn. They stepped toward the door.

Inside, they found the child with Mary, his mother. The room was small and plain. Light fell across the floor. The child was there — quiet, small, breathing softly.

The wise men knelt. One by one, they lowered themselves to the ground and bowed their faces to the floor.

Then they brought out their treasures. They opened boxes and unwrapped bundles of fine cloth. Gold, heavy and gleaming, was set before the child. Frankincense, pale and sweet, filled the room with its rich smell. Myrrh, dark and fragrant, was placed beside the rest.

The gifts lay on the floor in the lamplight — gold, frankincense, and myrrh — the finest things these men had carried across the desert.

That night, God warned the wise men in a dream. He told them not to return to Herod. So when morning came, they loaded their camels, wrapped their robes against the cold, and took a different road. They turned east and wound through the low brown hills, following a new path back toward home.
