Six days before the passover, Jesus came unto Bethany, where Lazarus was, whom he had raised from the dead. They made him a supper there at the house, and Martha served. Lazarus was one of them that sat at the table with him.

The lamps were lit along the walls of the room, and the evening was quiet over the village. The disciples gathered round about the table, and the air bore the savour of bread and of roasted meat.

Then Mary took a pound of ointment of spikenard, very costly, and anointed the feet of Jesus, and wiped his feet with her hair: and the house was filled with the odour of the ointment.

But Judas Iscariot, one of his disciples, which should betray him, said, "Why was not this ointment sold for three hundred pence, and given to the poor?" This he said, not that he cared for the poor; but because he was a thief, and had the bag, and was wont to take what was put therein.

Then said Jesus, "Let her alone: against the day of my burying hath she kept this. For the poor always ye have with you; but me ye have not always."

Mary spake nothing. She knelt at his feet, and her hair lay across his ankles, and the fragrance of the ointment filled the chamber.

Much people of the Jews therefore knew that Jesus was there: and they came, not for Jesus' sake only, but that they might see Lazarus also, whom he had raised from the dead. The chief priests had said among themselves that Lazarus also they would put to death; because that by reason of him many of the Jews went away, and believed on Jesus.

But on that night the room was still, and Mary tarried at his feet, and the odour of the ointment faded not until the lamps burned low.
