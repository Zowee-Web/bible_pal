Tax collectors and sinners were drawing near to hear Jesus. The Pharisees and the scribes had complained, saying, "This man receives sinners and eats with them." And Jesus, hearing the complaint, had begun to speak to them in three parables — and he had finished the first about the lost sheep, when he turned and gave them a second one. He set it among them simply, as a question first.

"Or what woman, if she had ten drachma coins, if she lost one drachma coin, wouldn't light a lamp, sweep the house, and seek diligently until she found it?

When she has found it, she calls together her friends and neighbors, saying, 'Rejoice with me, for I have found the drachma which I had lost.'

Even so, I tell you, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner repenting."

And the second parable was finished. The tax collectors and the sinners who had been crowding close stood listening for the third when it began; and the scribes and Pharisees stood at the edge of the crowd with their complaint quieter than it had been. The lamp had been lit. The corners had been swept. The drachma had been found. And the picture of the angels of God rejoicing over a single sinner returning was set down among the parables that the Lord told in the days when he ate with the tax collectors of Galilee.
