Publicans and sinners drew near unto Jesus to hear him. The Pharisees and the scribes had murmured, saying, "This man receiveth sinners, and eateth with them." And Jesus, hearing the murmuring, had begun to speak unto them in three parables — and he had finished the first concerning the lost sheep, when he turned and gave unto them a second. He set it among them simply, as a question first.

"Either what woman having ten pieces of silver, if she lose one piece, doth not light a candle, and sweep the house, and seek diligently till she find it?

And when she hath found it, she calleth her friends and her neighbours together, saying, 'Rejoice with me; for I have found the piece which I had lost.'

Likewise, I say unto you, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner that repenteth."

And the second parable was finished. The publicans and the sinners which had been pressing close stood listening for the third when it began; and the scribes and Pharisees stood at the edge of the multitude with their murmuring quieter than it had been. The candle had been lit. The corners had been swept. The piece of silver had been found. And the picture of the angels of God rejoicing over a single sinner returning was set down among the parables that the Lord told in the days when he ate with the publicans of Galilee.
