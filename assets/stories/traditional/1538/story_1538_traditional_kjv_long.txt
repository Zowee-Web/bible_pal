The apostles returned unto Jesus. They came in from the towns and villages whither he had sent them, and they gathered close about him upon the ground where he sat. They told him all. What they had done. What they had taught. The words they had spoken in the houses and in the streets. The healings, the meetings, the long roads, the doors that opened and the doors that opened not. At times they spake one over another, then grew still, then spake again. Jesus hearkened. He suffered them to finish.

And when they had told it all, he said unto them, Come ye apart into a quiet place, and rest a little while.

The need was true. The people came and went about them in a ceaseless stream — men with their sick kin, women bearing children in their arms, neighbours with questions, strangers pressing through to look the closer. The apostles had not so much as sat down to eat. Voices pressed in on every side. The air about them was thick with bodies and with dust.

So they entered into a boat and put off from the shore. The hull rocked beneath them as they climbed aboard. The oars dipped, and the water parted before the bow in long pale folds. The crowd upon the bank slid backward and grew small. For a little season there was only the slap of water against the wood, and the sound of the wind.

But the people saw them departing. They knew Jesus afar off, and word ran swiftly through every city near at hand. Men and women left their houses and ran on foot along the shore. They followed the line of the coast, calling unto others as they passed, gathering more people as they went. They went quickly, climbing over stones, cutting through the fields, and by the time the boat drew in toward the farther side, a great multitude was already gathered upon the bank. They had outrun the boat.

Jesus stepped forth upon the shore. The keel scraped against the gravel, and he came down into the shallows and up upon the grass. And, lo, he beheld them — a great host of people standing in the open. Thousands of faces turned toward him, waiting, uncertain, pressing forward. Children held the hands of their mothers. Old men leaned upon the younger. They were as sheep having no shepherd. Compassion rose within him, and he began to teach them. He spake long. The multitude sat down upon the ground and gave ear, row behind row, the nearest close enough to behold his face, and they that were farthest straining to hear.

The hours wore on. The sun moved across the sky and bent toward the west. The air grew cool. A breeze came in off the water and stirred the grass. Shadows stretched long across the hillside, and the light turned golden and thin. Birds crossed overhead toward the trees.

His disciples came unto him. They stood near him and waited till he paused, then spake softly.

This is a desert place, said one, and the hour is already far spent. Send the people away, that they may go into the country round about and into the villages, and buy themselves bread, for they have nothing to eat.

Jesus looked upon them. He said, Give ye them to eat.

The disciples looked one upon another. And one of them said, Shall we go and spend two hundred pence on bread, and give them to eat?

Jesus said, How many loaves have ye? Go and see.

They turned and went out into the multitude. It took time. The people sat in clusters across the hillside, and the disciples walked among them, stooping down, asking, searching what food might be found in the bags and bundles the people had borne with them. They came again together unto Jesus and stood before him.

Five loaves, they said. And two fishes.

Five round loaves of bread, such as a household might bake for a single day. Two small dried fishes. That was all. They held them forth before him.

Jesus gave commandment. He bade the disciples make the people sit down by companies upon the green grass. The word passed outward through the multitude from one disciple unto the next, and then from man to man — sit down, gather together, abide as one. The people shifted and settled. They drew near to their kin, to their neighbours, to the strangers nearest them. They set themselves in orderly ranks across the wide green slope, some companies of an hundred, some of fifty. From above, the hillside was as a patterned field — dark clusters of seated figures against the bright grass, stretching every way toward the edges of the open land.

The wind moved through the open place. The multitude grew still. Even the children ceased to stir.

Jesus took the five loaves and the two fishes in his hands. He lifted them up. He looked up toward heaven and blessed the food. Then he brake the loaves. He brake them with his hands and gave the pieces unto his disciples, and they took the bread and bare it out into the ranks. And he divided the two fishes among them all, and the disciples carried these portions out into the companies, their hands full, going from cluster to cluster.

They went to and fro, basket after basket, through companies of fifty and companies of an hundred. Bread passed from the disciples into the hands of the people, and from those hands into the hands beside them. Fish was divided and shared. Men brake pieces for their children. Women passed portions unto the aged that sat near them. The sound of eating rose softly across the hillside — quiet speech, the rustle of garments, the small steady noises of a meal.

Every man did eat. Every man had enough. Every man was filled.

The meal was ended. The hillside was still. Five thousand men sat upon the grass with their households, and not one was an hungered.

Jesus bade the disciples gather up that which remained. They went again through the ranks, stooping down, taking up the fragments of bread and the pieces of fish that lay upon the ground beside the people. They wrought their way through one company, and then the next. They filled one basket. Then another. And still they gathered. The light had grown softer now. The grass beneath was yet warm with the heat of the day.

Twelve baskets. Full. Heavy with broken bread and with the remnants of the fish. The disciples bare them back unto the place where Jesus stood near the water and set them down in a row upon the ground. Twelve woven baskets packed close, the fragments heaped above the rims. The multitude sat still upon the green hillside in their ordered ranks — hundreds, and fifties — and the last light of the day rested upon the grass round about them.
