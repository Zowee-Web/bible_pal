And the apostles came again unto Jesus, and gathered themselves together about him. They told him all things — what they had done in the places whither he had sent them, what they had taught, and the words they had spoken unto the people in every town.

Jesus hearkened unto them. And when they had ended, he said unto them, "Come ye apart into a quiet place, and rest yourselves a little while."

For the need was great upon them. So many were coming and going about them that the apostles had not so much as leisure to sit and take bread. The press of bodies and the noise of voices ceased not.

So they entered into a boat and departed privately toward a desolate stretch of shore. The water opened before the bow, and the multitude fell away behind them.

But the people beheld them departing. They knew Jesus even from afar off, and tidings passed swiftly through every city round about. Men and women came forth from their dwellings and ran on foot along the shore. They made haste, following the line of the coast, and by the time the boat drew nigh the far side, a great throng had already assembled upon the bank. They had outrun the vessel.

And Jesus came forth upon the shore and saw them. A great multitude stood in the open place — thousands of faces turned toward him, waiting, uncertain, pressing near. They were as sheep having no shepherd over them. And his heart was moved with compassion, and he began to teach them. He spake unto them a long while, and the multitude sat upon the ground and gave ear.

And the hours passed away. The sun moved across the heavens and drew low toward the edge of the land. The air grew cool. Shadows stretched long across the grass, and the light became golden and thin.

Then came his disciples unto him, and they said, "This place is desolate, and the hour is already far spent. Send the people hence, that they may go into the country round about and into the villages, and buy for themselves bread. For they have nothing to eat."

And Jesus looked upon them and said, "Give ye them to eat."

The disciples looked one upon another. And one among them answered, "Shall we then go and lay out two hundred denarii upon bread, and set it before them?"

Jesus said unto them, "How many loaves have ye? Go and look."

And they went forth among the multitude, asking and searching. It required time. The people sat in companies upon the hillside, and the disciples passed among them, inquiring what food might be found. Then they returned unto Jesus.

"Five loaves," they said. "And two fish."

Five round loaves of bread. Two small dried fish. That was all.

Then Jesus gave commandment. He bade the disciples cause all the people to sit down by companies upon the green grass. And the word passed outward through the multitude — sit down, form yourselves together, abide in your place. The people moved and settled. They arranged themselves in orderly ranks across the wide green slope, some companies of an hundred, some of fifty. Seen from above, the hillside appeared as a patterned field — dark gatherings of seated figures set against the bright grass, stretching forth in every direction.

And the wind moved through the open place. The multitude grew still.

Then Jesus took the five loaves and the two fish into his hands. He lifted his eyes toward heaven and blessed the food. Then he brake the loaves. He brake them and gave the pieces unto his disciples, and they carried the bread out unto the ranks. He divided the two fish likewise among all the people, and the disciples bore those portions forth also.

The disciples went to and fro, basket upon basket, through companies of fifty and companies of an hundred. Bread passed from hand to hand. Fish was divided and shared among them. And the multitude did eat.

Every soul ate. Every soul had enough. Every soul was filled.

And the meal was ended. The hillside lay quiet. Five thousand men sat upon the grass with their households, and not one among them hungered.

Then Jesus bade the disciples gather up that which remained. They went again through the ranks, stooping down, taking up the fragments of bread and the pieces of fish that lay upon the ground. They filled one basket. Then another. And still they gathered.

Twelve baskets. Full. Heavy with broken bread and with fish remaining. The disciples set them down in a line near the shore, twelve woven baskets pressed full, the fragments heaped above the rims. And the multitude sat yet upon the green hillside in their ordered ranks — hundreds, fifties — and the last light of the day rested upon the grass round about them.
