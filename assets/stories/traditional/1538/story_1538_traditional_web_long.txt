The apostles returned to Jesus. They came in from the towns and villages where he had sent them out, and they gathered close around him on the ground where he was sitting. They told him everything. What they had done. What they had taught. The words they had spoken in the homes and streets. The healings, the meetings, the long roads, the doors that opened and the doors that did not. They talked over one another at times, then settled, then spoke again. Jesus listened. He let them finish.

When they had told it all, he said, "Come away by yourselves to a quiet place and rest for a while."

The need was real. People were coming and going around them in a constant stream — men with sick relatives, women carrying children, neighbors with questions, strangers pushing through to get a closer look. The apostles had not even had time to sit down and eat a meal. Voices pressed in from every side. The air around them was thick with bodies and dust.

So they got into a boat and pushed off from the shore. The hull rocked under them as they climbed aboard. The oars dipped, and the water opened ahead of the bow in long pale folds. The crowd on the bank slid backward and grew smaller. For a little while there was only the slap of water against the wood and the sound of the wind.

But the people saw them leaving. They recognized Jesus even from a distance, and word moved fast through every nearby city. Men and women left their homes and ran on foot along the shore. They followed the line of the coast, calling to others as they passed, gathering more people as they went. They moved quickly, climbing over stones, cutting through fields, and by the time the boat angled in toward the far side, a massive crowd had already gathered on the bank. They had outrun the boat.

Jesus stepped out onto the shore. The keel scraped against the gravel, and he climbed down into the shallows and up onto the grass. He saw them — a great mass of people standing in the open. Thousands of faces turned toward him, waiting, uncertain, pressing forward. Children held the hands of their mothers. Old men leaned on younger ones. They looked like sheep without a shepherd. Compassion rose in him, and he began to teach them. He spoke for a long time. The crowd sat down on the ground and listened, row behind row, the nearest ones close enough to see his face, the farthest ones straining to hear.

The hours passed. The sun moved across the sky and sank toward the horizon. The air cooled. A breeze came in off the water and stirred the grass. Shadows stretched long across the hillside, and the light turned golden and thin. Birds crossed overhead toward the trees.

His disciples came to him. They stood near him and waited until he paused, then spoke quietly.

"This is a remote place," one said. "The hour is already late. Send the people away so they can go to the surrounding countryside and villages and buy themselves bread. They have nothing to eat."

Jesus looked at them. He said, "You give them something to eat."

The disciples exchanged glances. One of them said, "Should we go and spend two hundred denarii on bread and give it to them to eat?"

Jesus said, "How many loaves do you have? Go and see."

They turned and moved out into the crowd. It took time. The people sat in clusters across the hillside, and the disciples walked among them, bending down, asking, checking what food could be found in the bags and bundles people had carried with them. They came back together near Jesus and stood in front of him.

"Five loaves," they said. "And two fish."

Five round loaves of bread, the kind a household might bake for a single day. Two small dried fish. That was all. They held them out in front of him.

Jesus gave a command. He told the disciples to have everyone sit down in groups on the green grass. The instruction passed outward through the crowd from one disciple to the next, then from person to person — sit down, form groups, stay together. People shifted and settled. They moved closer to family members, to neighbors, to the strangers nearest them. They arranged themselves in orderly rows across the wide green slope, some groups of a hundred, some groups of fifty. From above, the hillside looked like a patterned field — dark clusters of seated figures against the bright grass, stretching in every direction toward the edges of the open land.

The wind moved through the open space. The crowd grew quiet. Even the children stopped moving.

Jesus took the five loaves and the two fish in his hands. He held them up. He looked up toward heaven and blessed the food. Then he broke the loaves. He broke them with his hands and handed the pieces to his disciples, and they took the bread and carried it out into the rows. He divided the two fish among all the people as well, and the disciples carried those portions out into the groups, hands full, moving from cluster to cluster.

They went back and forth, basket after basket, through groups of fifty, through groups of a hundred. Bread passed from the disciples into the hands of the people, and from those hands into the hands beside them. Fish was divided and shared. Men tore pieces for their children. Women passed portions to the elderly sitting next to them. The sound of eating rose softly across the hillside — quiet talk, the rustle of clothing, the small steady noises of a meal.

Every person ate. Every person had enough. Every person was filled.

The meal ended. The hillside was quiet. Five thousand men sat on the grass with their families, and no one was hungry.

Jesus told the disciples to collect what was left. They moved again through the rows, bending down, gathering the fragments of bread and the pieces of fish that remained on the ground beside the people. They worked their way through one group, then the next. They filled one basket. Then another. They kept gathering. The light had grown softer now. The grass underneath was still warm from the day.

Twelve baskets. Full. Heavy with broken bread and leftover fish. The disciples carried them back toward the place where Jesus stood near the water and set them down in a line on the ground. Twelve woven baskets packed tight, the fragments piled above the rims. The crowd sat still on the green hillside in their ordered rows — hundreds, fifties — and the last light of the day rested on the grass around them.
