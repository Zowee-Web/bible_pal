The apostles returned to Jesus. They gathered close around him and told him everything — what they had done in the places he had sent them, what they had taught, the words they had spoken to the people in every town.

Jesus listened. When they had finished, he said, "Come away by yourselves to a quiet place and rest for a while."

The need was real. So many people were coming and going around them that the apostles had not even had time to sit down and eat a meal. The press of bodies and voices was constant.

So they got into a boat and set out privately toward a remote stretch of shore. The water opened ahead of the bow, and the crowd fell behind.

But the people saw them leaving. They recognized Jesus even from a distance, and word moved fast through every nearby city. Men and women left their homes and ran on foot along the shore. They moved quickly, following the line of the coast, and by the time the boat reached the far side, a massive crowd had already gathered on the bank. They had outrun the boat.

Jesus stepped out onto the shore and saw them. A great mass of people standing in the open — thousands of faces turned toward him, waiting, uncertain, pressing forward. They looked like sheep without a shepherd. Compassion rose in him, and he began to teach them. He spoke for a long time, and the crowd sat on the ground and listened.

The hours passed. The sun moved across the sky and sank toward the horizon. The air cooled. Shadows stretched long across the grass, and the light turned golden and thin.

His disciples came to him. They said, "This is a remote place. The hour is already late. Send the people away so they can go to the surrounding countryside and villages and buy themselves bread. They have nothing to eat."

Jesus looked at them and said, "You give them something to eat."

The disciples exchanged glances. One of them said, "Should we go and spend two hundred denarii on bread and give it to them to eat?"

Jesus said, "How many loaves do you have? Go and see."

They moved through the crowd asking, searching. It took time. The people sat in clusters across the hillside, and the disciples walked among them checking what food could be found. They came back to Jesus.

"Five loaves," they said. "And two fish."

Five round loaves of bread. Two small dried fish. That was all.

Jesus gave a command. He told the disciples to have everyone sit down in groups on the green grass. The instruction passed outward through the crowd — sit down, form groups, stay together. People shifted and settled. They arranged themselves in orderly rows across the wide green slope, some groups of a hundred, some groups of fifty. From above, the hillside looked like a patterned field — dark clusters of seated figures against the bright grass, stretching in every direction.

The wind moved through the open space. The crowd grew quiet.

Jesus took the five loaves and the two fish in his hands. He looked up toward heaven and blessed the food. Then he broke the loaves. He broke them and handed the pieces to his disciples, and they carried the bread out into the rows. He divided the two fish among all the people, and the disciples carried those portions out as well.

The disciples moved back and forth, basket after basket, through groups of fifty, groups of a hundred. Bread passed from hand to hand. Fish was divided and shared. The crowd ate.

Every person ate. Every person had enough. Every person was filled.

The meal ended. The hillside was quiet. Five thousand men sat on the grass with their families, and no one was hungry.

Jesus told the disciples to collect what was left. They moved again through the rows, bending down, gathering the fragments of bread and the pieces of fish that remained on the ground. They filled one basket. Then another. They kept gathering.

Twelve baskets. Full. Heavy with broken bread and leftover fish. The disciples set them down in a line near the shore, twelve woven baskets packed tight, the fragments piled above the rims. The crowd sat still on the green hillside in their ordered rows — hundreds, fifties — and the last light of the day rested on the grass around them.
