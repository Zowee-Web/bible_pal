The apostles came back to Jesus and told him everything. What they had done. What they had taught. The words they had spoken in the towns and the responses they had received.

Jesus said to them, "Come away by yourselves to a quiet place and rest for a while."

There were so many people coming and going that they could not even sit down to eat. So they got into a boat and crossed toward a remote place, alone.

But the crowds saw them leaving. People recognized Jesus from the shore, and they ran on foot from every nearby city. They moved fast along the coast and arrived at the far side before the boat did. By the time the hull scraped the shallows, a great gathering had already formed on the bank.

Jesus stepped out and saw them. Hundreds of faces. Thousands. They stood without direction, without a leader, like sheep scattered across open country with no shepherd. Compassion moved through him, and he began to teach them. He taught for a long time.

The sun dropped. The shadows stretched long over the grass. His disciples came to him and said, "This is a remote place, and the hour is already late. Send the people away so they can go to the surrounding farms and villages and buy themselves something to eat. They have nothing."

Jesus said, "You give them something to eat."

They stared at him. "Should we go and spend two hundred denarii on bread and give it to them?"

He said, "How many loaves do you have? Go and see."

They went through the crowd and came back. "Five loaves. And two fish."

Jesus told them to have everyone sit down in groups on the green grass. The people sat in orderly rows — some groups of a hundred, some of fifty. The hillside filled with color, row after row on the bright green slope.

Jesus took the five loaves and the two fish. He looked up toward heaven and blessed the food. Then he broke the loaves and handed the pieces to his disciples, and they carried them out to the people. He divided the two fish among them all.

Every person ate. Every person was filled.

Afterward the disciples moved through the groups collecting what remained. They gathered twelve baskets, heavy and full, packed with broken bread and pieces of fish. The men who had eaten numbered about five thousand, seated in their rows across the grass, the empty baskets of the meal still warm beside them.
