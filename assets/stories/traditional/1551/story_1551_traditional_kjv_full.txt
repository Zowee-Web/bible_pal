The tribes of Israel gathered themselves together at Shechem. They came out of every territory — Judah and Ephraim, Manasseh and Dan, Simeon and Zebulun, Asher and Issachar, Benjamin and Naphtali, Reuben and Gad. They came with their households, their beasts, their tents and their provision. And the roads into Shechem were thick with dust, and the sound of many feet filled the way. The valley that lieth between Mount Ebal and Mount Gerizim received them all, and upon the air was the smell of dry grass and of cattle and of fires kindled for the evening meal.

The elders came. The judges came. The officers came. Every leader of every tribe and every clan presented himself before the assembly, and the whole nation stood together in that place before God.

And Joshua stood before them.

He was old. His years were exceedingly many, and every man and woman among them knew it well. He had crossed over Jordan at the head of the people when the waters rose up in a heap and stood fast. He had compassed the walls of Jericho. He had made war at Gibeon, where the sun tarried in the heavens and went not down. He had taken the hill country and the lowlands and the wilderness of the Negev. He had divided the land among the tribes, apportioning it by lot, territory unto territory. And now he stood at Shechem with the sun upon his face, and the whole nation spread out before him upon the open ground.

And he spake.

"Fear the Lord," said Joshua. His voice carried through the still air. "Serve him in sincerity and in truth."

The multitude was silent. A thousand faces and more turned toward the old commander where he stood.

"Put away the gods which your fathers served on the far side of the River," said he. "Put away the gods they served in the land of Egypt. And serve ye the Lord."

Then he was still. And the wind came through the valley and bowed the dry grass upon the hillsides. A child cried out somewhere amid the throng and was quieted. Joshua waited. He let the silence grow until it lay upon the assembly as a weight upon the breast.

Then he spake again, and his voice did not waver.

"If it seemeth evil unto you to serve the Lord," said Joshua, "then choose this day whom ye will serve."

He swept forth his hand, a slow and deliberate motion that encompassed the whole of the land about them — the hills, the valleys, the far ridgelines where the sky met the earth.

"Whether the gods which your fathers served beyond the River. Or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land ye now dwell."

He lowered his hand. His shoulders were set square. His jaw was firm. His eyes moved across the faces of the people — the old men who yet remembered Egypt, the young men who had been born in the wilderness, the women, the children standing close beside their mothers.

"But as for me and my house," said Joshua, "we will serve the Lord."

The words fell across the assembly like a stone cast into deep water. No man moved. No man spake. The only sound was the wind and the breathing of beasts tethered at the edges of the congregation. And Joshua stood with both feet planted upon the ground, his arms at his sides, and he turned not his gaze from the people.

Then the people answered.

It began as a murmuring, voices near unto the front, and it spread and grew until the whole assembly gave voice together.

"Far be it from us," they said, "that we should forsake the Lord and go after other gods."

The words came from every quarter of the throng — from the elders upon the left hand and the families upon the right and the officers standing in their ranks.

"The Lord our God," the people declared, "he it is that brought us and our fathers up out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage."

Men lifted their hands as they spake. Women drew their children nearer. And the words rolled forth across the floor of the valley.

"He wrought great wonders before our eyes," they said. "He preserved us upon every road wherein we walked. He kept us safe among all the nations through whose midst we passed."

And the assembly pressed forward, even as a field of grain leaneth when the wind cometh from one quarter. Their faces were turned toward Joshua, and their voices were strong.

"The Lord drove out from before us all the peoples," said they. "Even the Amorites who dwelt in this land."

The hills received the sound and held it. The sun stood high and white above Shechem. Dust hung upon the air where thousands of feet had stirred the dry ground.

"We also will serve the Lord," the people said, "for he is our God."

And Joshua stood before them. The whole nation of Israel stood before him. The valley was full of their voices, and the wind bore the sound upward into the hills and carried it away.
