The tribes of Israel gathered at Shechem. They came from every territory — Judah and Ephraim, Manasseh and Dan, Benjamin and Naphtali, all of them. The elders came. The judges came. The officers came. They stood before God in that place, and Joshua stood before them.

He was old. His years were many, and every man there knew it. He had led them across the Jordan. He had stood at the walls of Jericho. He had fought at Gibeon, at Hazor, in the hill country and the lowlands and the Negev. Now he stood at Shechem with the sun on his face and the whole nation spread before him.

He spoke.

"Fear the Lord," Joshua said. "Serve him with sincerity and truth. Put away the gods your fathers served beyond the River and in Egypt. Serve the Lord."

The crowd was still. The wind moved through the trees on the surrounding hills. Joshua let the silence hold.

Then he spoke again, and his voice carried across that assembly like a blade drawn clean from its sheath.

"If it seems evil to you to serve the Lord, then choose today whom you will serve. The gods your fathers served beyond the River. Or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you now live." He paused. His shoulders were square. His eyes moved across the faces of the people. "But as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord."

No one spoke. The words hung over the gathering like the last sound of a struck bell. Thousands of men and women stood in the open air, and the only sound was the wind.

Then the people answered.

"God forbid that we should leave the Lord to serve other gods," they said. "The Lord our God — he is the one who brought us and our fathers out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery. He did those great signs before our eyes. He kept us safe on every road we walked. He kept us safe among every people we passed through. The Lord drove out before us all the peoples, even the Amorites who lived in this land."

Their voices rose together, filling the space between the hills.

"We also will serve the Lord," the people said, "for he is our God."
