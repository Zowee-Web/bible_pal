The tribes of Israel were gathered together at Shechem. They came forth out of every territory — Judah and Ephraim, Manasseh and Dan, Benjamin and Naphtali, all of them. The elders came. The judges came. The officers came. And they presented themselves before God in that place, and Joshua stood before them.

He was old. His years were exceedingly many, and every man there knew it. He had led them over the Jordan. He had stood before the walls of Jericho. He had gone up to battle at Gibeon, at Hazor, in the hill country and the lowlands and the desert to the south. Now he stood at Shechem with the sun upon his face, and the whole nation spread out before him.

He spake.

"Fear the Lord," said Joshua unto them. "Serve him in sincerity and in truth. Put away from you the gods which your fathers served on the far side of the River, and in Egypt. Serve ye the Lord."

And the people were still. The wind moved through the trees upon the hills round about. Joshua suffered the silence to hold.

Then he spake again, and his voice went forth over that assembly as a blade drawn clean from its sheath.

"If it seemeth evil unto you to serve the Lord, then choose ye this day whom ye will serve — whether the gods which your fathers served beyond the River, or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land ye now dwell." He paused. His shoulders were set square. His eyes passed over the faces of the people. "But as for me and my house, we shall serve the Lord."

No man spake. The words hung over the gathering as the last sound of a bell that hath been struck. Thousands of men and women stood in the open air, and there was no sound save the wind.

Then the people answered.

"Far be it from us that we should forsake the Lord to serve other gods," they said. "For the Lord our God, he it is that brought us and our fathers up out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage. He wrought those great signs before our eyes. He kept us in safety upon every road we walked. He kept us in safety among every people through whom we passed. And the Lord drave out before us all the peoples, even the Amorites that dwelt in this land."

And their voices rose together, and filled the space between the hills.

"We also will serve the Lord," the people said, "for he is our God."
