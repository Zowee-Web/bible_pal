In the valley of Sorek Samson came to love a woman named Delilah, and the Philistine lords found in her their way to him. Each of them would weigh out eleven hundred pieces of silver, they told her, if only she could entice him to show where his great strength lay, and learn by what means he might be bound and brought low. So she asked him outright to tell her the secret of his strength, and what could hold him — and three times he sent her after a lie. He named seven green withs never dried, and let her bind him with them while her hired men waited in the chamber; at her shout that the Philistines were upon him, he tore the withs like a thread of tow caught in the fire. He named new ropes never yet used, and let her bind him with those while the men lay in wait again; the ropes came off his arms like thread. He named the seven locks of his head, woven into the web on the beam and made fast with the pin; and when she had done it and raised the same cry, he woke and rose and carried the pin and the web away with him. Each time the secret stayed hidden, and each time she had been mocked.

At last she turned from the trap to the man. How could he say he loved her, she pressed him, when his heart was not with her? Three times now he had made a fool of her and told her lies, and still she knew nothing of where his great strength lay. Day after day she pressed him with her words, and urged him, until his very soul was vexed unto death — and then he told her all his heart. There had never come a razor upon his head, he said, for he had been a Nazarite unto God from his mother's womb; were he shaven, his strength would go from him, and he would become as weak as any other man.

Delilah heard the whole of it in that, and knew it. She sent word to the Philistine lords to come up once more, for he had shown her all his heart, and they came to her with the silver in their hands. She lulled him to sleep upon her knees, called for a man to shave away the seven locks of his head, and as the hair fell his strength went from him.

Then she cried out as she had before, that the Philistines were upon him. He woke, meaning to shake himself free as he had done at every other time — and he did not know that the Lord was departed from him. So the Philistines took him and put out his eyes. They brought him down to Gaza in fetters of brass, and set him to grinding at the mill in the prison house. And the hair of his head, once shaven, began to grow again.
