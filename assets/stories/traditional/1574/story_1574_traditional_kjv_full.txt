In the valley of Sorek, Samson gave his heart to a woman named Delilah; and the lords of the Philistines saw their chance in her. Each of them would pay her eleven hundred pieces of silver, they promised, if only she could coax from him the secret of his great strength, and learn by what means he might be bound and brought low.

So she set herself to ask him, and three times he turned her aside with a lie. The first time he named seven green withs, still moist and never dried, that would leave him weak as any other man. She bound him fast in them, while men lay hidden and waiting in the chamber, and cried out that the Philistines were upon him — and he burst the withs like a strand of tow brushed by fire. The second time he named new ropes, never yet used; and though she bound him and the ambush waited again, he snapped them from his arms as though they were thread. The third time he spoke of the seven locks of his head, woven into the loom; and while he slept she wove and pinned them, and raised the same cry — but he woke and rose and carried off pin and loom together. Each time the secret slipped through her fingers, and his strength was never known.

Still she did not leave off. How canst thou say thou lovest me, she pressed him, when thy heart is not with me? Three times now he had mocked her and hidden the truth. Day upon day she wearied him with the same complaint and the same asking, until his very soul was vexed unto death.

And at the last, he told her all his heart. No razor, he said, had ever touched his head; for from his mother's womb he had been a Nazarite unto God, and were he ever shaven, his strength would go from him, and he would be weak as any other man.

Delilah knew this time she held the whole of it. She sent word to the lords of the Philistines — come but once more, for he hath told me all his heart — and they came up to her with the silver in their hands. She lulled him to sleep upon her knees, and called for a man to shave away the seven locks of his head; and as the hair fell his strength went with it, and she began to afflict him.

Then she woke him as she had before: the Philistines be upon thee, Samson! And he stirred, meaning to go out and shake himself free as at every other time. But this time it was not so. He knew not that the Lord had departed from him.

The Philistines laid hold of him and put out his eyes. They led him down to Gaza, bound him with fetters of brass, and set him to grind at the mill in the prison house. Yet there in the dark, unregarded, the hair of his head began to grow again.
