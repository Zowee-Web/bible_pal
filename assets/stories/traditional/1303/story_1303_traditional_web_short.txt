For four hundred years the children of Israel had been in the land of Egypt. They had come down in the days of Joseph, to escape the famine, in the favor of Pharaoh. The bones of Jacob and Joseph lay in their coffins. The promise to Abraham — the promise of a land and a people of their own — had grown dim in the long years.

A king had risen long before, who did not know Joseph. He had been afraid of the Hebrews, for they had multiplied in the land. He had set taskmasters over them. They built the store cities of Pithom and Raamses with bricks they made themselves, and with straw they were forced to gather. Their sons had been thrown into the river. Their daughters had been kept for slaves.

Their voices had grown hoarse with weeping. Their backs had grown bent under the loads they carried. The bondage had been bitter. The bondage had been long.

In the course of those many days, the king of Egypt died, and the children of Israel sighed because of the bondage, and they cried, and their cry came up to God because of the bondage.

God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob.

God saw the children of Israel, and God was concerned about them.

And the sun rose on Egypt as it had risen all those years. The brick kilns burned. The taskmasters went out into the fields. The mothers nursed their children in the dark of the morning. The cry of Israel did not stop. And God did not forget. And so it was, in Egypt, in those days.
