For four hundred years the children of Israel had been in the land of Egypt. They had come down in the days of Joseph, to escape the famine, in the favour of Pharaoh. The bones of Jacob and Joseph lay in their coffins. The promise unto Abraham — the promise of a land and a people of their own — had grown dim in the long years.

A king had risen up long before, which knew not Joseph. He had feared the Hebrews, for they had multiplied in the land. He had set taskmasters over them. They builded the store cities of Pithom and Raamses with bricks which they made themselves, and with straw which they were constrained to gather. Their sons had been cast into the river. Their daughters had been kept for handmaids.

Their voices had waxed hoarse with weeping. Their backs had bowed down under the burdens which they bare. The bondage had been bitter. The bondage had been long.

And it came to pass in process of time, that the king of Egypt died: and the children of Israel sighed by reason of the bondage, and they cried, and their cry came up unto God by reason of the bondage.

And God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob.

And God looked upon the children of Israel, and God had respect unto them.

And the sun rose upon Egypt as it had risen all those years. The brick kilns burned. The taskmasters went forth into the fields. The mothers gave suck unto their children in the darkness of the morning. The cry of Israel ceased not. And God did not forget. And so it was, in Egypt, in those days.
