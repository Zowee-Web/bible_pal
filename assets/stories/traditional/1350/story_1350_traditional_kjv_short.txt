Paul and Barnabas were come into Lystra. They had healed the lame man at the gate, and the people had cried out that the gods had come down unto them in the likeness of men, and the priest of Zeus had brought oxen and garlands unto the gates to do sacrifice. Paul and Barnabas had rent their clothes and ran in among the multitude, declaring that they were men of like passions and preaching the living God. The multitudes had been turned back from the sacrifice. And then there came thither certain Jews from Antioch and Iconium.

And there came thither certain Jews from Antioch and Iconium, who persuaded the people, and, having stoned Paul, drew him out of the city, supposing he had been dead.

Howbeit, as the disciples stood round about him, he rose up, and came into the city: and the next day he departed with Barnabas to Derbe.

And when they had preached the gospel to that city, and had taught many, they returned again to Lystra, and to Iconium, and Antioch,

Confirming the souls of the disciples, and exhorting them to continue in the faith, and that we must through much tribulation enter into the kingdom of God.

And the apostles went on their way. Paul bare the bruises of the stones beneath his cloak; and in the cities wherein he had been dragged out for dead he sat down again with the disciples, and confirmed their souls, and told them that through much tribulation they must enter into the kingdom. And so it was in the days when Paul rose up at Lystra, and went on with Barnabas unto Derbe.
