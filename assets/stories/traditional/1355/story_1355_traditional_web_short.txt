Naomi and her two daughters-in-law had set out from the country of Moab back toward the land of Judah, for word had come to Naomi in Moab that Yahweh had visited his people and given them bread. The three women walked together on the road that led toward Bethlehem. And Naomi turned and said to them, "Go, return each of you to her mother's house"; and they had wept; and Orpah had kissed her mother-in-law and turned back. But Ruth would not turn back.

They lifted up their voices, and wept again; then Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth joined with her.

She said, "Behold, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people, and to her god. Follow your sister-in-law."

Ruth said, "Don't urge me to leave you, and to return from following you, for where you go, I will go; and where you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my people, and your God my God.

Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May Yahweh do so to me, and more also, if anything but death parts you and me."

When she saw that she was determined to go with her, she stopped urging her.

And the two of them went on together toward Bethlehem. Naomi who had gone out full was coming back empty, as she would later say; but she was not coming back alone, for the Moabite widow had bound herself to her with words that could not be unsaid. The road went on, and the two of them went on with it. And so it was in the days when Ruth the Moabitess clave to Naomi her mother-in-law on the road from Moab to Judah.
