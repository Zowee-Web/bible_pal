Night had come over Jerusalem. The oil lamps in David's house burned low, their small flames steady in the still air. David was inside, seated. Nathan the prophet came to him.

Nathan had received the word of the LORD, and he spoke it now directly, his voice clear in the quiet room.

"This is what the LORD of hosts says: I took you from the sheepcote, from following the sheep, to be ruler over my people, over Israel."

The words hung in the lamplight. David sat and listened.

"I was with you wherever you went. I cut off all your enemies out of your sight. I have made you a great name, like the name of the great men that are in the earth."

Nathan stood before the king and did not pause. His hands were still at his sides.

"Moreover, I will appoint a place for my people Israel and will plant them, that they may dwell in a place of their own and move no more. Neither shall the children of wickedness afflict them any more, as before. And as since the time that I commanded judges to be over my people Israel, I have caused you to rest from all your enemies."

Nathan drew a breath. The next words came with weight.

"Also the LORD tells you that he will make you a house. When your days are fulfilled and you sleep with your fathers, I will set up your seed after you, which shall come from your own body, and I will establish his kingdom. He shall build a house for my name, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever."

David's hands rested on his knees. The flames flickered once and settled.

"I will be his father, and he shall be my son. If he commits iniquity, I will chasten him with the rod of men and with the stripes of the children of men. But my mercy shall not depart from him, as I took it from Saul, whom I put away before you."

"And your house and your kingdom shall be established forever before you. Your throne shall be established forever."

The lamps burned. Nathan stood before David in the dim light.
