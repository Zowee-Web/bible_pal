Night was fallen upon Jerusalem. The stone walls of David's house yet held the last warmth of the day within them, and oil lamps burned in the chambers, their small flames casting a soft light upon the floors and upon the walls. Without, the city lay quiet. Dogs gave voice somewhere in the lower streets, far off. And the stars shone overhead.

David was within. He had reigned as king over Israel these many years, and his wars were behind him for a season, and his enemies were driven back. The palace bore the smell of cedar wood, for the panels thereof lined every wall. It was a goodly house, well wrought by the craftsmen whom Hiram of Tyre had sent.

And Nathan the prophet came unto him that night.

He came with purpose, passing through the doorway into the lamplight, his robe brushing against the stone threshold. Nathan had stood before the king many times, but this night he bore a word that had come unto him from the LORD himself. A vision had been given him, and he was come to deliver it.

David was seated. Nathan stood before him and opened his mouth to speak.

"Thus saith the LORD of hosts."

He paused but briefly. Then came the words.

"I took thee from the sheepcote, from following after the flock, that thou shouldest be ruler over my people, over Israel."

The room was still. David's hands rested in his lap. The lamps burned and did not flicker. And Nathan continued.

"I was with thee whithersoever thou wentest. I struck down thine enemies from before thee. And I have made thy name great, even as the names of the great ones who are upon the earth."

Nathan's voice was steady, measured — the voice of a man delivering that which had been entrusted unto him with care. He did not hasten. He did not lift his voice. He spake as one who had been told precisely what words to say.

"Moreover, I will appoint a place for my people Israel and will plant them there, that they may dwell in a place of their own and be moved no more. Neither shall the sons of wickedness trouble them again, as in former days."

The shadows in the room shifted, for one of the lamp flames bent upon itself. The air was warm and close.

"And even as from the time when I set judges over my people Israel, so have I given thee rest from all thine enemies round about."

Nathan drew breath. He looked upon David. And the words that followed bore a different weight, for they concerned not what had been, but what was yet to come.

"The LORD declareth unto thee that he himself shall build thee a house."

David did not move. His eyes rested upon the face of the prophet.

"When thy days be fulfilled and thou art laid to rest with thy fathers, I shall raise up thy seed after thee, one who shall come forth from thine own body. And I will establish his kingdom."

The lamplight fell upon the lines of Nathan's face. His hands hung still at his sides, the palms turned slightly open, as a man standeth when he carrieth nothing save words alone.

"He shall build a house unto my name. And the throne of his kingdom I will establish for ever."

The word hung in the warm air of the room. For ever.

Nathan went on. His voice did not waver.

"I will be unto him a father, and he shall be unto me a son. And if he turn to iniquity, I will chasten him with the rod of men and with such stripes as the children of men do give."

He paused for the space of a breath.

"But my mercy shall not depart from him, as I took it from Saul, whom I did put away from before thee."

David sat still. The cedar walls stood close about them. The oil in the nearest lamp gave a faint sound, a small thing in the silence between the words of the prophet.

Nathan spake the final portion. His voice was even and clear, bearing the shape of the promise in the very manner it had been given unto him.

"Thy house and thy kingdom shall be made sure before thee for ever. Thy throne shall be established for ever."

The prophet ceased. The words were finished. He had spoken according to all these words and according to all this vision, even as the LORD had given it unto him. The lamps burned in their places. The warm air stirred faintly through the room. And Nathan stood before David in the stillness, his hands at his sides, the last words yet abiding in the air between them.
