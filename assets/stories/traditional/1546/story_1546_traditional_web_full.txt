Night had settled over Jerusalem. The stone walls of David's house held the last warmth of the day. Oil lamps burned in the rooms, their small flames throwing soft light across the floors and walls. Outside, the city was quiet. Dogs barked somewhere in the lower streets, far off. The stars were out.

David was inside. He had been king over Israel for years now, his wars behind him for a season, his enemies driven back. The palace smelled of cedar wood from the panels that lined its walls. It was a good house, well built by the craftsmen Hiram of Tyre had sent.

Nathan the prophet came to him that night.

He came with purpose, walking through the doorway into the lamplight, his robe brushing the stone threshold. Nathan had been before the king many times, but tonight he carried a word that had come to him from the LORD himself. He had received a vision, and he was here to deliver it.

David was seated. Nathan stood before him and began to speak.

"This is what the LORD of hosts says."

He paused only briefly. Then the words came.

"I took you from the sheepcote, from following the sheep, to be ruler over my people, over Israel."

The room was still. David's hands rested in his lap. The lamps burned without flickering. Nathan continued.

"I was with you wherever you went. I cut off all your enemies out of your sight. I have made you a great name, like the name of the great men that are in the earth."

Nathan's voice was steady, measured, the voice of a man delivering something entrusted to him with care. He did not rush. He did not raise his voice. He spoke as one who had been told exactly what to say.

"Moreover, I will appoint a place for my people Israel and will plant them, that they may dwell in a place of their own and move no more. Neither shall the children of wickedness afflict them any more, as before."

The shadows in the room shifted slightly as one of the lamp flames bent. The air was warm and close.

"And as since the time that I commanded judges to be over my people Israel, I have caused you to rest from all your enemies."

Nathan drew a breath. He looked at David. The next words carried a different weight. They were not about the past. They were about what was to come.

"Also the LORD tells you that he will make you a house."

David did not move. His eyes were on Nathan's face.

"When your days are fulfilled and you sleep with your fathers, I will set up your seed after you, which shall come from your own body. And I will establish his kingdom."

The lamplight caught the lines of Nathan's face. His hands were still at his sides, palms open slightly, the way a man stands when he is carrying nothing but words.

"He shall build a house for my name. And I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever."

The word hung in the warm air of the room. Forever.

Nathan went on. His voice did not waver.

"I will be his father, and he shall be my son. If he commits iniquity, I will chasten him with the rod of men and with the stripes of the children of men."

He paused for the space of a breath.

"But my mercy shall not depart from him, as I took it from Saul, whom I put away before you."

David sat still. The cedar walls of the room were close around them. The oil in the nearest lamp popped faintly, a small sound in the silence between the prophet's words.

Nathan spoke the final portion. His voice was even, clear, carrying the shape of the promise exactly as it had been given.

"And your house and your kingdom shall be established forever before you. Your throne shall be established forever."

The prophet stopped. The words were finished. He had spoken according to all these words and according to all this vision, just as the LORD had given it to him. The lamps burned in their places. The warm air moved faintly through the room. Nathan stood before David in the quiet, his hands at his sides, the last words still in the air between them.
