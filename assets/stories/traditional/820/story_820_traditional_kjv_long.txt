Upon the rolling hills, where the grass ran wild and the sky opened wide, a shepherd kept watch over his flock. The sun hung warm above the land, and the wind swept gently across the slope, fluttering the wildflowers and stirring the quiet murmur of sheep. An hundred sheep grazed upon the hillside. Some nibbled in the shelter of scattered stones, while others wandered through tangled thickets of grass and bramble. Amidst the soft bleating and peaceful rustle, the shepherd kept watch. His eyes moved over the sheep, attentive and steady.

He knew each sheep by sight. Their markings, the shape of each ear, the way some favoured a leg or carried a mark upon their wool — nothing escaped his careful gaze. He cherished them all, from the strongest ram to the youngest lamb. Their lives rested in his hands. To him, the whole flock belonged together. As he moved among them, his staff in hand, the sheep felt his presence. They trusted the hand that guided and guarded.

But beyond the cluster of ninety and nine moved one sheep, slower and farther from the rest. Drawn by a patch of green beyond a rocky edge, it lingered. The sheep wandered, step by step, straying beyond the gentle safety of the flock. The noise of the others faded. There was only the wind, and the uncertain call of distant birds.

The shepherd's eyes swept the flock, counting softly. Ninety and seven, ninety and eight, ninety and nine. He paused, his brow furrowed. He looked again, seeking that familiar tuft of white wool. One was missing. He turned in every direction. The familiar bleating came from the flock, but the sound he strained for was absent. He tarried not.

He drew closer, his heart steady, but now more urgent. The hillside ran wild with dark rocks and sudden dips. He moved among the sheep, searching for a glimpse of movement in the grass beyond. The sheep yet present crowded together, uneasy at his quickened pace. But he could not rest. One was gone. Not just any, but one whose life and well-being mattered deeply.

From the crest of the hill, he gazed outward. The sun flickered across the rough terrain. Rocky slopes tumbled into narrow ravines. Shrubs grew thick along the ledges, and sharp thorns lurked in the shadows. Somewhere out there, a sheep wandered — alone and vulnerable.

The shepherd called out, his voice strong. The sound travelled over the hills. "Come!" he called, hope threading through the air. No answer came. He steadied himself, setting his staff to the earth with quiet resolve. The grass brushed against his sandals as he pressed onward, further from the safety of the flock.

Behind him, the remaining sheep milled together. He left them in the open pasture, trusting they would abide there. His heart beat with purpose. He moved steadily, his eyes sweeping over every uneven stone, every cluster of thorn that might catch at wool. The distance waxed between the shepherd and the flock. Still, he pressed onward, following the faint hope that hung upon the wind.

He lifted up his voice again, calling into the wild. Silence answered. The only reply was the whisper of grass and the far-off cry of a solitary bird. Yet the shepherd was not deterred. Every step he took was for that one — to find it and restore it unto safety.

He passed under the shadow of crooked trees. Branches reached forth like arms, brushing his shoulders. The rocky terrain made each step uncertain, but he pressed onward, not sparing himself pain or hesitation. The sun drifted lower, sending golden light along the ridges. Still, the shepherd did not give over.

His voice rang out once more: "Where art thou?" His call echoed against stone and hillside. Then, through the hush, he heard it. Faint, thin, plaintive — a lone bleat drifted upon the wind. He stilled at once, his breath held, his heart hopeful.

Again, the sheep called. The sound was weak, but it reached him. The shepherd scanned the wild, his eyes narrowing as he moved quickly now, drawn unto the sound. He clambered across sharp stones, parted the thick brush, and peered into every hollow.

The cry waxed stronger. His pace quickened. Rocks shifted underfoot, but he fell not. He pressed through tangled thorns, unfazed by the scratch upon his skin. There, caught in a tangle near a steep drop, the lost sheep stood shivering, its fleece stained by dust and bramble. Fear and longing gleamed in its wide, uncertain eyes.

The shepherd rushed forward, his arms open. Relief and joy flooded him. He knelt by the trembling sheep, brushing tenderly at its coat and speaking gentle words. "I am here." His hands were careful. He looked for wounds, touched the matted wool, and freed it from the grasp of the thorns.

With strength born of love, he lifted the sheep and laid it upon his shoulders. Its weight felt right — a familiar burden, welcome and beloved. The sheep nuzzled close, warmth seeping between shepherd and lost sheep. Their heartbeats mingled, steady and comforted.

The journey back was filled with joy. His footsteps now sounded with purpose, and the hillside that once held only tension now echoed with hope. The sun dipped behind the far ridge, painting the sky with gentle gold and rose. The shepherd's voice arose in glad proclamation: "Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost!" His words rang through the air, a bright call that stirred the flocks of birds and filled the silent spaces between the rocks.

He came forth at the crest, bearing the lost sheep, and the flock turned toward him. The bleating of ninety and nine arose in a soft chorus, welcoming the return. The grass seemed greener, and the wind sang in friendly tones. He bade all that heard, "Rejoice with me!" There was laughter and gladness — not only in the heart of the shepherd, but among all those that loved the flock.

As the sheep settled once more into safety, the wonder of recovery filled the hillside. The savour of warm wool drifted across the air, mingling with the smell of grass and earth. Everywhere, a sense of belonging returned. Though the day wore on and shadows lengthened, the joy did not fade. All were together now. None were forgotten.

In this wise, the shepherd revealed a deeper joy — the kind found not merely in the keeping, but in the saving and the restoring. He rejoiced more over the one which had been lost and was found than over the ninety and nine that needed no rescue. And so, as the stars pressed softly into the gathering dusk, gladness abode over the hills.

"I say unto you," came the final words, echoing over green pastures and deep into the hearts of all that heard, "that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance."
