Across the open hillside, the shepherd led his flock under the wide sky. The sun cast long shadows over the uneven grass, and the air brimmed with the sound of sheep calling softly to one another. Ninety-nine sheep grazed together, scattered over the soft rise and fall of the land. Wind moved steadily past, carrying the scent of wildflowers and the low rumble of distant rocks tumbling down the slopes.

The shepherd knew each animal. Early in the day, he had counted and watched them. He guided the flock away from the stony patches, directing them toward fresh grass and quiet water. His staff rested in his hand, familiar and strong. Sometimes he paused, tilting his head to listen to the breeze or the uneven bleating of the flock. He knew the difference between contentment and distress in their voices.

Among the flock was one younger sheep, soft and white, whose gaze often wandered. While the others fed in a loose circle, this sheep edged closer to the fringe, drawn toward curious shapes on the far side of the hillside. Small stones scattered beneath its hooves as it wandered further, the sound muffled by grass.

The sun moved higher, washing the land in gold. The main flock pressed together, shifting from one patch of grass to another. But the one sheep strayed, slipping past the familiar shelter of the others. Soon, it wandered over a low ridge, hidden from the sight of the flock and the shepherd.

The hillside fell away into rougher ground. Dry wind rustled thorny bushes. Though the flock’s voices drifted faintly over the rise, the lost sheep no longer heard them. Its bleat was thin, uncertain, and swallowed quickly by the open air.

The shepherd finished counting his flock and paused. He counted again, realizing with a jolt that the number was one fewer than before. Ninety-nine. He looked over the field, eyes sharp and searching. Another count. Still, one missing.

Anxiety pressed on him, sharpened by love and responsibility. He could not let a single sheep vanish into the wild hills, defenseless and alone. Without hesitation, the shepherd turned from the flock. He left the ninety-nine in the open pasture, sure that together they were safer, and set off into the wilderness.

The wind whipped his robe and carried dust against his legs. Grass gave way to stony ground as he traversed the ridge, moving quickly now. Every sound reached his ear—each faint rustle or cry. His eyes searched the jagged horizon.

At first, no sign met him. He walked on, calling out. His voice, steady and kind, echoed along the hillsides, “Sheep, where are you?” Rocks tumbled under his feet as he crossed into rougher terrain. He listened. Silence returned, save for the whisper of wind and the distant chorus of the flock behind him.

Again he called, scanning every hollow and crag. At last, through the hush, there came a soft, weak bleating. The shepherd stopped. He listened. The sound was faint, carried on the wind, but it was there—somewhere among the stones.

He hurried toward it, heart lifted with hope. Closer now, he called again, and the lost sheep answered. Its cry, small and fearful, led him through patches of brambles and loose rock. The shepherd moved carefully, his staff ready to guide and protect.

Then, wedged behind a tangle of thornbush and a pile of fallen stones, he found the sheep. Dirt marked its coat, and its sides heaved with each uncertain breath. He reached out, his hands gentle and sure, and lifted the animal to his shoulders.

Relief flooded the hillside. The shepherd’s heart was light as he carried the sheep. Dust and scratches mattered little—what counted was the sheep, now safe, pressed near the warmth of its rescuer.

As he journeyed back, his voice rose with joy. “Rejoice with me,” he called out, laughter threaded in his words, “for I have found my sheep which was lost!” His face shone with gladness as he returned to the pasture.

He gathered friends and neighbors from nearby fields, his joy overflowing. The air crackled with happiness, and all who heard laughed and shared in the celebration. The flock was whole once more.

And so, as the shepherd rejoiced over finding the one sheep that was lost, there was even greater joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous people who need no repentance.
