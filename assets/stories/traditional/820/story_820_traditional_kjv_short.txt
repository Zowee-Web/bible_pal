Upon a quiet hillside beneath a wide, open sky, a shepherd kept watch over his flock of an hundred sheep. The grass swayed in the gentle wind, and the sun warmed the land. The sheep grazed, scattered upon the rocky slopes, their white wool bright against the green and grey of the plain.

The shepherd knew each sheep. He counted them as they wandered, ever watchful. But as he searched the hillside, he beheld that somewhat was amiss. A single sheep, one of his own, was missing from the flock of an hundred. Only ninety and nine remained.

He tarried not. Leaving the ninety and nine safe upon the open land, he went forth into the rough wilderness. The shepherd moved with purpose, crying out, "Come, little one! Where art thou?" His voice echoed off the rocky stones. Here the grass grew sparse, and sharp thorns clave unto every step. The shepherd's heart beat with hope.

He hearkened. In the hush, a faint bleat drifted upon the wind. The shepherd paused, listening closely. Again, the lost sheep cried out. It was nigh, but hidden among the rocks and brush.

The shepherd pressed forward, his hands parting brambles and brush. The cry waxed louder. There among the jagged stones, tangled in thorny branches, lay his lost sheep. Its wool was soiled, its eyes wide and afraid.

Without delay, the shepherd bowed down and gently lifted the sheep up. He smiled with relief and joy. With strong but gentle hands, he laid the sheep upon his shoulders, holding it steady.

Returning from the wild places, the shepherd drew nigh unto his home. Warm sunlight bathed the hillside. As he came near his village, he called out unto his friends and neighbours.

"Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost!"

Joy filled his voice, and the hillside rang with rejoicing. The lost sheep, once frighted and alone, was now safe, enfolded in the shepherd's care.

I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.
