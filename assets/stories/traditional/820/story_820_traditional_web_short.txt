On a quiet hillside beneath a wide, open sky, a shepherd watched over his flock of one hundred sheep. The grass swayed in the gentle wind, and the sun warmed the land. The sheep grazed, scattered across the rocky slopes, their white wool bright against the green and gray of the plain.

The shepherd knew each sheep. He counted them as they wandered, always alert. But as he scanned the hillside, he noticed something was wrong. A single sheep, one of his own, was missing from the flock of one hundred. Only ninety-nine remained.

He did not hesitate. Leaving the ninety-nine safe upon the open land, he set out into the rough wilderness. The shepherd moved with purpose, calling out, "Come, little one! Where are you?" His voice echoed off the rocky stones. Here, the grass grew sparse, and sharp thorns clung to every step. The shepherd’s heart beat with hope.

He listened. In the hush, a faint bleat drifted through the wind. The shepherd paused, listening closely. Again, the lost sheep called out. It was close, but hidden among the rocks and brush.

The shepherd pressed forward, his hands parting brambles and brush. The cry grew louder. There among the jagged stones, tangled in thorny branches, lay his lost sheep. Its wool was dirty, its eyes wide and afraid.

Without delay, the shepherd bent down and gently lifted the sheep up. He smiled with relief and joy. With strong but gentle hands, he placed the sheep on his shoulders, holding it steady.

Returning from the wild places, the shepherd approached his home. Warm sunlight bathed the hillside. Drawing near his village, he called out to his friends and neighbors.

"Rejoice with me! I have found my sheep which was lost!"

Joy filled his voice and the hillside rang with celebration. The lost sheep, once frightened and alone, was now safe, wrapped in the shepherd’s care.

In the same way, there will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous people who need no repentance.
