Across the open hillside, the shepherd led his flock under the wide sky. The sun cast long shadows over the uneven grass, and the air was filled with the sound of sheep calling softly one to another. Ninety and nine sheep grazed together, scattered over the soft rise and fall of the land. The wind moved steadily past, bearing the savour of wildflowers and the low rumble of distant rocks tumbling down the slopes.

The shepherd knew each beast. Early in the day, he had counted and watched them. He guided the flock away from the stony patches, leading them toward fresh grass and quiet water. His staff rested in his hand, familiar and strong. Sometimes he paused, tilting his head to hearken unto the breeze or the uneven bleating of the flock. He knew the difference between contentment and distress in their voices.

Among the flock was one younger sheep, soft and white, whose gaze oft wandered. While the others fed in a loose circle, this sheep edged closer unto the fringe, drawn toward curious shapes upon the far side of the hillside. Small stones scattered beneath its hooves as it wandered further, the sound muffled by grass.

The sun moved higher, washing the land in gold. The main flock pressed together, shifting from one patch of grass unto another. But the one sheep strayed, slipping past the familiar shelter of the others. Soon, it wandered over a low ridge, hidden from the sight of the flock and of the shepherd.

The hillside fell away into rougher ground. A dry wind rustled the thorny bushes. Though the voices of the flock drifted faintly over the rise, the lost sheep heard them no more. Its bleat was thin, uncertain, and swallowed quickly by the open air.

The shepherd finished counting his flock and paused. He counted again, perceiving with a sudden start that the number was one fewer than before. Ninety and nine. He looked over the field, his eyes sharp and searching. Another count. Still, one was missing.

Anxiety pressed upon him, sharpened by love and responsibility. He could not suffer a single sheep to vanish into the wild hills, defenceless and alone. Without hesitation, the shepherd turned from the flock. He left the ninety and nine in the open pasture, sure that together they were safer, and went forth into the wilderness.

The wind whipped his robe and bare the dust against his legs. Grass gave way to stony ground as he crossed the ridge, moving quickly now. Every sound reached his ear — each faint rustle or cry. His eyes searched the jagged horizon.

At first, no sign met him. He walked on, calling out. His voice, steady and kind, echoed along the hillsides, "Sheep, where art thou?" Rocks tumbled under his feet as he passed into rougher terrain. He hearkened. Silence returned, save for the whisper of wind and the distant chorus of the flock behind him.

Again he called, scanning every hollow and crag. At length, through the hush, there came a soft, weak bleating. The shepherd stayed. He listened. The sound was faint, borne upon the wind, but it was there — somewhere among the stones.

He hastened toward it, his heart lifted with hope. Closer now, he called again, and the lost sheep answered. Its cry, small and fearful, led him through patches of brambles and loose rock. The shepherd moved carefully, his staff ready to guide and protect.

Then, wedged behind a tangle of thornbush and a pile of fallen stones, he found the sheep. Dirt marked its coat, and its sides heaved with each uncertain breath. He reached out, his hands gentle and sure, and lifted the beast unto his shoulders.

Relief flooded the hillside. The shepherd's heart was light as he bare the sheep. Dust and scratches mattered little — what counted was the sheep, now safe, pressed near the warmth of its deliverer.

As he journeyed back, his voice arose with joy. "Rejoice with me," he called out, laughter threaded in his words, "for I have found my sheep which was lost!" His face shone with gladness as he returned unto the pasture.

He gathered together his friends and neighbours from the nearby fields, his joy overflowing. The air crackled with happiness, and all that heard laughed and shared in the rejoicing. The flock was whole once more.

And so, as the shepherd rejoiced over finding the one sheep which was lost, likewise was there even greater joy in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.
