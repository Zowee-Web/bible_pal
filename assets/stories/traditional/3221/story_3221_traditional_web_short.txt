Wherever Saul sent David, David went and behaved wisely. Saul placed him over the men of war, and the appointment pleased the people and Saul’s servants alike.

When David returned from the slaughter of the Philistine, women came from the cities of Israel to meet King Saul. They sang and danced with tambourines, joy, and musical instruments. As they sang to one another, they credited Saul with his thousands and David with his ten thousands. Saul became very angry at their words. He said, “They have credited David with ten thousands, and they have only credited me with thousands. What can he have more but the kingdom?” From that day Saul watched him.

The next day an evil spirit from God came mightily on Saul, and he prophesied in the house. David played with his hand as he did day by day, while Saul held a spear. Saul threw it, saying he would pin David to the wall. Twice David escaped from his presence.

Saul feared David because Yahweh was with David and had departed from Saul. He removed David from his presence and made him captain over a thousand. David went out and came in before the people, behaving wisely in all his ways, and Yahweh was with him. Seeing how wisely David behaved, Saul stood in awe of him. All Israel and Judah loved David, for he went out and came in before them.

Saul offered David his elder daughter Merab as a wife. He called on David to be valiant for him and fight Yahweh’s battles. For Saul said, “Don’t let my hand be on him, but let the hand of the Philistines be on him.”

David answered Saul by asking who he was, and what his life or his father’s family in Israel was, that he should become the king’s son-in-law. But when the time came for Merab, Saul’s daughter, to be given to David, she was given as wife to Adriel the Meholathite.
