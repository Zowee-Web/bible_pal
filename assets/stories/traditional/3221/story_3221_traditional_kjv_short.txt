David went whithersoever Saul sent him and behaved himself wisely. Saul set him over the men of war, and he was accepted by all the people and by Saul’s servants.

As David returned from the slaughter of the Philistine, the women came out of the cities of Israel to meet king Saul. With singing and dancing, tabrets, joy, and instruments of musick, they answered one another: “Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands.” Saul was very wroth, and their saying displeased him. And he said, “They have ascribed unto David ten thousands, and to me they have ascribed but thousands: and what can he have more but the kingdom?” From that day forward Saul eyed David.

On the morrow the evil spirit from God came upon Saul, and he prophesied in the midst of the house. David played with his hand as at other times, while Saul held a javelin. Saul cast it, saying that he would smite David even to the wall. David avoided out of his presence twice.

Saul was afraid because the Lord was with David and had departed from him. He removed David from his presence and made him captain over a thousand. David went out and came in before the people. He behaved wisely in all his ways, and the Lord was with him. When Saul saw how wisely David behaved, he was afraid of him; but all Israel and Judah loved David, because he went out and came in before them.

Saul spoke unto David of Merab, his elder daughter, promising her to him as a wife if he would be valiant for Saul and fight the Lord’s battles. For Saul said, “Let not mine hand be upon him, but let the hand of the Philistines be upon him.”

David asked Saul who he was, and what his life or his father’s family in Israel was, that he should be son in law to the king. Nevertheless, when Merab should have been given unto David, Saul’s daughter was given unto Adriel the Meholathite to wife.
