The words came through the prophet Isaiah, spoken unto a weary people.

Hast thou not known? Hast thou not heard?

The Lord is the everlasting God. He made the ends of the earth, every ridge and valley, every river that cutteth through stone. He shaped the mountains and spread the seas wide. He set the stars in their places, every one of them.

And he fainteth not. Neither is he weary.

The strongest young men in all the land — they that could bear heavy burdens up steep roads, they that could run from one town unto the next without stopping — even they stumbled at times. Even they fell down upon the dusty ground, breathing hard, knees shaking, unable to take another step.

Young men, full of life and sinew, tripped over their own weary feet.

But the prophet spake of somewhat else. He spake of them that waited upon the Lord. They that trusted and hoped and held fast, even when the days were long and the nights were longer.

They that wait upon the Lord, said Isaiah, shall renew their strength.

Not strength from bread or water. Not strength from sleep. A new strength, given unto them.

They shall mount up with wings as eagles.

High above the dusty roads and the heavy burdens, above the hills and the weary towns, eagles spread their wide wings and caught the wind. They rose without flapping, without straining. The air itself bare them higher and higher until the fields below seemed small and green.

That was the picture Isaiah painted with his words.

They shall run, and not be weary.

Feet upon the ground, moving swiftly, breath steady, legs strong — running the way a child runneth down a hill, arms loose, chest open, never slackening.

And they shall walk, and not faint.

Step after step after step, over rocky paths and through long valleys, walking when walking was all that was left to do. Not stumbling. Not falling. Not giving up.

The prophet's voice carried across the people gathered there — men and women with dust upon their robes and weariness in their bones. The wind stirred the hems of their garments. Above them, against the pale sky, an eagle turned in a slow wide circle, his wings stretched open and still.
