The words came through the prophet Isaiah, spoken to a weary people.

Have you not known? Have you not heard?

The Lord is the everlasting God. He made the ends of the earth, every ridge and valley, every river that cuts through stone. He shaped the mountains and spread the seas wide. He set the stars in their places, every one of them.

And he does not faint. He does not grow weary.

The strongest young men in all the land — the ones who could carry heavy loads up steep roads, the ones who could run from one town to the next without stopping — even they stumbled sometimes. Even they fell down on the dusty ground, breathing hard, legs shaking, unable to take another step.

Young men, full of life and muscle, tripped over their own tired feet.

But the prophet spoke of something different. He spoke of people who waited for the Lord. People who trusted and hoped and held on, even when the days were long and the nights were longer.

Those who wait for the Lord, Isaiah said, shall renew their strength.

Not strength from bread or water. Not strength from sleep. A new kind of strength, given to them.

They shall mount up with wings like eagles.

High above the dusty roads and the heavy loads, above the hills and the tired towns, eagles spread their wide wings and caught the wind. They rose without flapping, without straining. The air itself lifted them higher and higher until the fields below looked small and green.

That was the picture Isaiah painted with his words.

They shall run and not be weary.

Feet on the ground, moving fast, breath steady, legs strong — running the way a child runs downhill, arms loose, chest open, never slowing.

They shall walk and not faint.

Step after step after step, over rocky paths and through long valleys, walking when walking was all that was left to do. Not stumbling. Not falling. Not giving up.

The prophet's voice carried across the people gathered there — men and women with dust on their robes and tiredness in their bones. The wind stirred the edges of their garments. Above them, against the pale sky, an eagle turned in a slow wide circle, its wings stretched open and still.
