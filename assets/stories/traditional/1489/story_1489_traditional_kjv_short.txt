This is one of the songs David wrote for the chief Musician on Neginoth, to be sung at the time of the evening sacrifice.

He had been spoken against by men of his own people. They had turned his glory into shame. They had loved vanity, and sought after leasing.

He answered them not with anger.

He answered with this prayer, which he set down to be sung in the assembly, and carried home by every one of them unto the bed where he or she should lie down that night.

He said —

"Hear me when I call, O God of my righteousness: thou hast enlarged me when I was in distress. Have mercy upon me, and hear my prayer.

O ye sons of men, how long will ye turn my glory into shame? How long will ye love vanity, and seek after leasing?

But know that the Lord hath set apart him that is godly for himself: the Lord will hear when I call unto him.

Stand in awe, and sin not: commune with your own heart upon your bed, and be still.

Offer the sacrifices of righteousness, and put your trust in the Lord.

There be many that say, Who will shew us any good? Lord, lift thou up the light of thy countenance upon us.

Thou hast put gladness in my heart, more than in the time that their corn and their wine increased.

I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, Lord, only makest me dwell in safety."

The song was carried out into the city after the evening sacrifice. The lamps were burning low in the houses by the time the singers had come down from the temple.

David lay down in his own house. The men who had spoken against him kept speaking elsewhere. But the lamp in his chamber had gone low, and David slept in peace.
