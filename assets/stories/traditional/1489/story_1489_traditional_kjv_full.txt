This is one of the songs David wrote for the chief Musician on Neginoth, to be sung at the time of the evening sacrifice.

He had been spoken against by men of his own people. They had been saying things in the streets and in the courts of the city. They had turned his glory into shame. They had loved vanity, and sought after leasing. The words had reached him in his own house.

He answered them not with anger.

He sat down and wrote this prayer. He gave it unto the chief Musician, to be sung in the assembly when the evening sacrifice was offered. He set it down so that the people which had heard the same accusations against him — and the people which had brought such accusations against others — could carry it home unto the bed where they should lie down that night.

He said —

"Hear me when I call, O God of my righteousness: thou hast enlarged me when I was in distress. Have mercy upon me, and hear my prayer.

O ye sons of men, how long will ye turn my glory into shame? How long will ye love vanity, and seek after leasing?

But know that the Lord hath set apart him that is godly for himself: the Lord will hear when I call unto him.

Stand in awe, and sin not: commune with your own heart upon your bed, and be still.

Offer the sacrifices of righteousness, and put your trust in the Lord.

There be many that say, Who will shew us any good? Lord, lift thou up the light of thy countenance upon us.

Thou hast put gladness in my heart, more than in the time that their corn and their wine increased.

I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, Lord, only makest me dwell in safety."

The song was sung at the temple that evening, with the smoke of the sacrifice rising above the city.

When it was finished, the singers went down from the temple by the long stairs. The people went home unto their houses. The lamps were lit in the windows. The streets quieted. The accusations that had filled the day grew smaller as the night closed in.

David lay down in his own house. The men who had spoken against him kept speaking elsewhere in the city. They would speak again in the morning.

But the lamp in his chamber had gone low. The night had come. David slept in peace.
