A traveler stood on a dusty road at the bottom of a wide valley. Dry grass bent in a soft breeze on both sides of the path. Small white wildflowers dotted the ground ahead, their petals open and still. The road was packed brown dirt, worn smooth by many feet over many years. It curved gently upward toward a long row of green hills in the distance.

Birds called to each other from the branches of an old fig tree nearby. The fig tree had wide, dark leaves that spread out like open hands. Two sparrows hopped along one thick branch, tilting their small heads. A third sparrow sat lower, near the trunk, ruffling its feathers. The morning air smelled clean and fresh, like wild mint growing somewhere close.

The traveler wore simple clothes, a long tunic tied with a cloth belt. A leather bag hung from one shoulder. Sandals, dusty and well-worn, pressed into the warm dirt. A walking staff rested against the other shoulder, made of pale wood, smooth from long use. The top of the staff was rounded where a hand had gripped it many times.

The traveler lifted a hand to shade tired eyes and looked up at those hills. They rose high and round against the sky, covered in low green bushes and smooth gray stones. Some of the stones caught the morning light and glinted. The hills looked far away, but the road led straight toward them, steady and clear. A few small trees grew along the lower slopes, their branches moving in the breeze.

The traveler spoke out loud, voice calm and clear. "Where does my help come from?" The words floated up toward the wide blue sky. A grasshopper leaped from one walk quietly of dry grass to another near the traveler's feet. Then the traveler answered, still looking at the hills. "My help comes from the Lord, who made heaven and earth."

The traveler shifted the staff from one shoulder and planted it on the ground. One foot stepped forward, then another. The road began to climb. Loose pebbles rolled underfoot and tumbled back down the slope. The traveler's staff tapped the ground with each steady step, leaving small round marks in the dirt. Dust rose in tiny puffs with every footfall.

A warm wind carried the smell of wild herbs growing between the rocks. Low bushes of rosemary and thyme spread across the hillside. Their tiny purple and white flowers swayed as the breeze passed through. A brown beetle crawled slowly across a flat stone beside the path. The traveler stepped around it and kept climbing.

The road grew steeper. The traveler's sandals gripped the packed earth. Small roots crossed the path in places, and the traveler stepped over each one carefully. The staff found solid ground every time, pressing firm against rock or hard dirt. The traveler spoke again while walking. "He will not let my foot slip." Each step landed sure and steady on the rising path. The traveler's voice stayed even and calm, like someone talking to a friend walking beside them.

"The one who watches over me will not fall asleep." The traveler said these words while climbing past a cluster of yellow wildflowers growing from a crack in a large rock. A butterfly with orange and brown wings rested on one of the flowers. Its wings opened and closed slowly. The traveler paused for just a moment to look, then kept walking upward.

Higher and higher the road went. The valley below grew wider and smaller at the same time. The old fig tree near the bottom of the road looked tiny now. The traveler could see more of the land from up here. Fields of golden grain stretched out to the west. A few farmhouses with flat roofs sat among groves of trees far below.

The sun moved across the sky and climbed higher. Heat pressed down on the dusty road. The traveler wiped sweat from both hands on the cloth belt and kept walking. Then a cloud, white and wide, drifted across the sun. Cool shade spread over the hillside. The air turned pleasant. The traveler lifted both eyes toward the sky and said, "The sun will not harm me by day."

A lizard sat on a flat rock beside the path, blinking in the soft light. It was small and green, with tiny toes gripping the warm stone. It watched the traveler pass without moving. The traveler smiled and kept walking. "The moon will not harm me by night." The words came out like a song, easy and unhurried. The traveler's voice carried across the open hillside and faded into the quiet air.

The cloud moved on, and warm light returned. But the air was cooler now at this height. A second breeze came from the north, gentle and steady. It rustled the dry grass on both sides of the path. The traveler's tunic rippled. The walking staff swung forward and tapped the ground again. Step after step, the road continued upward.

A small pile of stones marked a turn in the path. Someone had stacked them carefully, five smooth rocks balanced on top of each other. The traveler followed the turn and found the road leveling out. The steep climbing was finished. The ground became flatter, and the dirt under the traveler's sandals felt softer, mixed with bits of grass.

Olive trees grew in rows along the ridge of the hills. Their trunks were thick and twisted, with rough gray bark. Their leaves were narrow and silver-green, and they rustled and flickered whenever the breeze moved through them. The sound was soft, like many small whispers all at once. Patches of sunlight and shade moved across the ground beneath the branches.

A shepherd sat under one of the olive trees, leaning back against the wide trunk. The shepherd wore a brown cloak and held a long stick across both knees. A small flock of sheep nibbled grass nearby. Some of the sheep were white, and a few had light brown wool. One small lamb stood close to its mother, pressing against her side. Their wool looked soft and clean in the afternoon light.

The shepherd lifted a hand in greeting as the traveler came near. The traveler waved back with an open palm. The shepherd's dog lay in the grass a few steps away, its chin resting on its front paws. Its ears perked up, and its tail swished once against the ground. A water jug sat beside the shepherd, and a folded cloth held bread and dried figs.

From up here on the ridge, the whole valley spread out below. Golden fields and green orchards stretched in every direction. A stream sparkled far down in the distance, winding through the low ground like a silver ribbon laid across the earth. The road the traveler had walked looked thin and pale, curving down into the valley floor. Everything was quiet and still, except for the sound of sheep pulling at the grass and the rustling of olive leaves.

The traveler stopped under a large olive tree and set the walking staff against the trunk. The bark was rough and cool against the smooth wood of the staff. The leather bag slid off the traveler's shoulder and settled on the ground. Cool shade covered the soft grass beneath the tree. Small purple flowers grew in a patch nearby, close to one of the tree's thick roots.

A gentle breeze moved through the branches overhead. Leaves turned and caught the light, flashing silver, then green, then silver again. The traveler sat down on the soft grass and stretched both legs out. Sandals, still dusty from the long walk, rested in the warm grass. The traveler leaned back against the tree trunk and let out a long, easy breath.

"The Lord watches over my coming and going," the traveler said quietly. The words were steady and sure. A sheep looked up from its grazing for a moment, then bent its head back to the grass. "From this time on and forever." The traveler said this last part softly, looking out over the wide valley below.

Sheep bells clinked nearby, small and bright. The shepherd broke off a piece of bread and ate it slowly. The dog rolled onto its side in the warm grass. A bird landed on a low branch of the olive tree above the traveler and sang three bright notes, clear and sweet. Then it sang three more.

The afternoon light turned everything warm and golden. The olive leaves glowed. The sheep's wool looked soft as clouds. The stream in the valley below caught the light and shone. The traveler rested there on the hilltop, safe and warm, with the whole beautiful land stretching out in every direction. The breeze kept moving through the trees, gentle and steady, carrying the smell of wild herbs and warm grass across the ridge.
