A traveller stood upon a dusty road at the foot of a wide valley. Dry grass bent in a soft breeze. Small white wildflowers dotted the path ahead. The road curved upward toward a long row of green hills. Birds called one to another from the branches of an old fig tree nearby.

The traveller lifted a hand to shade weary eyes and beheld those hills. The hills were tall and round, covered in low bushes and smooth stones. A walking staff rested upon one shoulder. Sandals pressed into the warm dirt. The traveller spake aloud, voice calm and clear. "Whence cometh mine help?" The words floated up toward the wide blue sky. Then the traveller answered, still beholding the hills. "My help cometh from the Lord, which made heaven and earth."

One foot stepped forward, then another. The road began to climb. Loose pebbles rolled underfoot. The traveller's staff tapped the ground with each steady step. A warm wind bore the savour of wild herbs growing betwixt the rocks. The traveller spake again while walking. "He will not suffer my foot to be moved." Each step landed firm and sure upon the rising path. "He that keepeth me will not slumber."

Higher and higher the road went. The sun moved athwart the sky. Heat pressed down, but a cloud drifted over and gave cool shadow for a while. The traveller wiped dust from both hands and kept walking. "The sun shall not smite me by day," said the traveller. A lizard sat upon a flat rock, blinking in the light. "Nor the moon by night." The words came forth like a song, easy and unhurried.

The path leveled out near the top of the hills. Olive trees grew in rows along the ridge. Their silver-green leaves rustled and flickered. A shepherd sat under one of the trees, watching a small flock of sheep nibble grass. The shepherd lifted a hand in greeting. The traveller waved back. From up here, the whole valley spread out below, golden and green. A stream sparkled afar off in the distance.

The traveller stopped under an olive tree and set the walking staff against the trunk. Cool shadow covered the ground. A gentle breeze moved through the branches overhead. The traveller sat down upon the soft grass and stretched both legs out. "The Lord shall preserve my going out and my coming in," the traveller said quietly, "from this time forth, and even for evermore."

Sheep bells clinked nearby. A bird lighted upon a low branch and sang three bright notes. The afternoon light turned everything warm and golden. The traveller rested there upon the hilltop, safe and warm, with the whole beautiful land stretching out below.
