A man stood on a dirt road in the hill country. Dust covered his sandals. Small stones crunched under his feet. The morning air carried the smell of wild herbs growing between the rocks. A few goats wandered nearby, pulling at dry grass. The road stretched ahead, winding up through brown and green hillsides.

Other travelers walked the same road. Some carried bundles on their shoulders. Some led donkeys loaded with cloth and grain. Children walked beside their parents, holding on to the hems of long robes. Voices carried in the open air. Someone called out a greeting from farther up the path. Birds circled high above the hills, riding the warm wind.

The man lifted his eyes. He looked up at the hills rising ahead of him. They were tall and rocky, with patches of scrubby trees clinging to their sides. The road curved between them, narrow in some places, wide in others. He spoke out loud, and the people near him could hear his words.

"Where does my help come from?" he said.

His voice was steady and clear. He kept walking as he spoke. A woman carrying a water jar on her head glanced over. A boy leading a donkey slowed his steps to listen.

Then the man answered his own question. He lifted one hand toward the wide sky above the hills.

"My help comes from the Lord, who made heaven and earth," he said.

The words rang out in the open air. A few travelers nodded as they walked. The hills stood still and solid around them. Above, the sky stretched out blue and enormous, without a single cloud.

The man kept walking. His staff tapped the ground with each step. He spoke again, and his voice carried easily along the road.

"He will not let your foot slip," he said.

The path was uneven. Loose pebbles rolled under sandals. But the man placed each foot carefully. He watched the ground and kept his balance. Other travelers did the same, stepping around rough spots and finding solid places to stand.

"He who watches over you will not slumber," the man continued.

A young girl walking with her family looked up at her father. Her father walked steadily beside her, his hand resting on her shoulder. The man on the road kept speaking.

"Indeed, he who watches over Israel will neither slumber nor sleep," he said.

The sun had risen higher now. Warm light spread across the hillsides. Lizards sat on flat rocks, soaking in the heat. The smell of dry earth and sage drifted through the air. A shepherd on a distant hill called to his flock, and the sound echoed softly across the valley.

The man's voice came again, strong and even.

"The Lord watches over you," he said. "The Lord is your shade at your right hand."

The sun was bright overhead. Some travelers pulled their head coverings forward to shade their faces. A few walked close to the rocky walls where the hillside blocked the sun. Cool air gathered there, and the walking was easier in those spots. Children pressed close to their parents on the sunny side.

"The sun will not harm you by day," the man said, "nor the moon by night."

The travelers moved together along the road. Their sandals made a soft, steady sound on the packed dirt. Someone ahead began to hum a tune. The melody floated back through the group like a ribbon of sound. A donkey flicked its ears and kept walking.

The road began to level out. The steep parts were behind them now. Ahead, the land opened up, and they could see green fields in the distance. A well stood near a cluster of olive trees, and a few women were drawing water there. The smell of bread baking drifted from a small house nearby.

The man spoke once more. His voice was calm and full.

"The Lord will keep you from all harm," he said. "He will watch over your life."

A breeze moved through the olive trees. Their silver-green leaves turned and flickered in the light. The women at the well looked up and smiled at the travelers. One of them offered a cup of water. The boy with the donkey took it and drank. Water dripped from his chin, and he wiped it with the back of his hand.

The man stood near the olive trees now. He planted his staff in the ground and rested both hands on top of it. He looked back at the road they had traveled. The hills rose behind them, layered one behind the other, brown and green and hazy blue in the distance.

"The Lord will watch over your coming and going," he said, "from this time on and forevermore."

The travelers gathered near the well. Some sat on low stone walls. Some spread cloths on the ground and unpacked bread and dried fruit. Children ran between the olive trees, laughing and calling to each other. A baby slept in its mother's arms, wrapped in soft cloth.

The man sat down on a flat stone. He set his staff across his knees. The breeze touched his face and moved through his hair. Around him, the sounds of the group filled the air. Cups clinked against the stone edge of the well. Someone tore bread and passed the pieces around. A goat wandered close and nudged a child's hand, looking for crumbs.

The olive trees rustled overhead. The wide sky held steady above the hills and the fields and the road. The man rested there, quiet and still, his journey continuing and the words he had spoken still hanging in the warm afternoon air.

Nearby, the children played among the tree roots. The women refilled their jars at the well. The travelers ate together and passed food to one another. Everything was calm and unhurried. The bread was warm. The water was cool. The afternoon stretched out, gentle and bright, over all of them.
