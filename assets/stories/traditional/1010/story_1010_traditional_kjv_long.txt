A traveller stood upon a dusty road at the foot of a wide valley. Dry grass bent in a soft breeze on both sides of the path. Small white wildflowers dotted the ground ahead, their petals open and still. The road was packed brown dirt, worn smooth by many feet over many years. It curved gently upward toward a long row of green hills afar off.

Birds called one to another from the branches of an old fig tree nearby. The fig tree had wide, dark leaves that spread out like open hands. Two sparrows hopped along one thick branch, tilting their small heads. A third sparrow sat lower, near the trunk, ruffling its feathers. The morning air bore a savour clean and fresh, like wild mint growing somewhere close.

The traveller wore simple clothes, a long tunic girt with a cloth belt. A leather bag hung from one shoulder. Sandals, dusty and well-worn, pressed into the warm dirt. A walking staff rested against the other shoulder, made of pale wood, smooth from long use. The top of the staff was rounded where a hand had gripped it many times.

The traveller lifted a hand to shade weary eyes and beheld those hills. They rose high and round against the sky, covered in low green bushes and smooth grey stones. Some of the stones caught the morning light and glinted. The hills looked far away, but the road led straight toward them, steady and clear. A few small trees grew along the lower slopes, their branches moving in the breeze.

The traveller spake aloud, voice calm and clear. "Whence cometh mine help?" The words floated up toward the wide blue sky. A grasshopper leaped from one tuft of dry grass to another near the traveller's feet. Then the traveller answered, still beholding the hills. "My help cometh from the Lord, which made heaven and earth."

The traveller shifted the staff from one shoulder and planted it upon the ground. One foot stepped forward, then another. The road began to climb. Loose pebbles rolled underfoot and tumbled back down the slope. The traveller's staff tapped the ground with each steady step, leaving small round marks in the dirt. Dust rose in tiny puffs with every footfall.

A warm wind bore the savour of wild herbs growing betwixt the rocks. Low bushes of rosemary and thyme spread athwart the hillside. Their tiny purple and white flowers swayed as the breeze passed through. A brown beetle crawled slowly athwart a flat stone beside the path. The traveller stepped about it and kept climbing.

The road grew steeper. The traveller's sandals gripped the packed earth. Small roots crossed the path in places, and the traveller stepped over each one carefully. The staff found solid ground every time, pressing firm against rock or hard dirt. The traveller spake again while walking. "He will not suffer my foot to be moved." Each step landed sure and steady upon the rising path. The traveller's voice stayed even and calm, as one talking to a friend walking beside them.

"He that keepeth me will not slumber." The traveller said these words while climbing past a cluster of yellow wildflowers growing from a crack in a great rock. A butterfly with orange and brown wings rested upon one of the flowers. Its wings opened and closed slowly. The traveller paused for just a moment to behold, then kept walking upward.

Higher and higher the road went. The valley below grew wider and smaller at the same time. The old fig tree near the foot of the road looked tiny now. The traveller could see more of the land from up here. Fields of golden grain stretched out to the west. A few farmhouses with flat roofs sat among groves of trees far below.

The sun moved athwart the sky and climbed higher. Heat pressed down upon the dusty road. The traveller wiped sweat from both hands upon the cloth belt and kept walking. Then a cloud, white and wide, drifted athwart the sun. Cool shadow spread over the hillside. The air turned pleasant. The traveller lifted both eyes toward the sky and said, "The sun shall not smite me by day."

A lizard sat upon a flat rock beside the path, blinking in the soft light. It was small and green, with tiny toes gripping the warm stone. It watched the traveller pass without moving. The traveller smiled and kept walking. "Nor the moon by night." The words came forth like a song, easy and unhurried. The traveller's voice carried athwart the open hillside and faded into the quiet air.

The cloud moved on, and warm light returned. But the air was cooler now at this height. A second breeze came from the north, gentle and steady. It rustled the dry grass on both sides of the path. The traveller's tunic rippled. The walking staff swung forward and tapped the ground again. Step after step, the road continued upward.

A small heap of stones marked a turning in the path. One had stacked them carefully, five smooth rocks balanced one upon another. The traveller followed the turning and found the road leveling out. The steep climbing was finished. The ground became flatter, and the dirt beneath the traveller's sandals felt softer, mingled with bits of grass.

Olive trees grew in rows along the ridge of the hills. Their trunks were thick and twisted, with rough grey bark. Their leaves were narrow and silver-green, and they rustled and flickered whensoever the breeze moved through them. The sound was soft, like many small whispers all at once. Patches of sunlight and shadow moved athwart the ground beneath the branches.

A shepherd sat under one of the olive trees, leaning back against the wide trunk. The shepherd wore a brown cloak and held a long staff athwart both knees. A small flock of sheep nibbled grass nearby. Some of the sheep were white, and a few had light brown wool. One small lamb stood close to its mother, pressing against her side. Their wool looked soft and clean in the afternoon light.

The shepherd lifted a hand in greeting as the traveller came near. The traveller waved back with an open palm. The shepherd's dog lay in the grass a few steps away, its chin resting upon its front paws. Its ears perked up, and its tail swished once against the ground. A water jar sat beside the shepherd, and a folded cloth held bread and dried figs.

From up here on the ridge, the whole valley spread out below. Golden fields and green orchards stretched on every side. A stream sparkled afar off in the distance, winding through the low ground like a silver ribbon laid athwart the earth. The road the traveller had walked looked thin and pale, curving down into the valley floor. Everything was quiet and still, save for the sound of sheep pulling at the grass and the rustling of olive leaves.

The traveller stopped under a great olive tree and set the walking staff against the trunk. The bark was rough and cool against the smooth wood of the staff. The leather bag slid off the traveller's shoulder and settled upon the ground. Cool shadow covered the soft grass beneath the tree. Small purple flowers grew in a patch nearby, close to one of the tree's thick roots.

A gentle breeze moved through the branches overhead. Leaves turned and caught the light, flashing silver, then green, then silver again. The traveller sat down upon the soft grass and stretched both legs out. Sandals, still dusty from the long walk, rested in the warm grass. The traveller leaned back against the tree trunk and let out a long, easy breath.

"The Lord shall preserve my going out and my coming in," said the traveller quietly. The words were steady and sure. A sheep looked up from its grazing for a moment, then bent its head back to the grass. "From this time forth, and even for evermore." The traveller said this last part softly, beholding the wide valley below.

Sheep bells clinked nearby, small and bright. The shepherd brake off a piece of bread and ate it slowly. The dog rolled onto its side in the warm grass. A bird lighted upon a low branch of the olive tree above the traveller and sang three bright notes, clear and sweet. Then it sang three more.

The afternoon light turned everything warm and golden. The olive leaves glowed. The sheep's wool looked soft as clouds. The stream in the valley below caught the light and shone. The traveller rested there upon the hilltop, safe and warm, with the whole beautiful land stretching out on every side. The breeze kept moving through the trees, gentle and steady, bearing the savour of wild herbs and warm grass athwart the ridge.
