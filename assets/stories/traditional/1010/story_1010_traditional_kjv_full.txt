A man stood upon a dirt road in the hill country. Dust covered his sandals. Small stones crunched beneath his feet. The morning air bore the savour of wild herbs growing betwixt the rocks. A few goats wandered nearby, pulling at the dry grass. The road stretched ahead, winding up through brown and green hillsides.

Other travellers walked the same road. Some bare bundles upon their shoulders. Some led asses laden with cloth and grain. Children walked beside their parents, holding to the hems of long robes. Voices carried in the open air. One called out a greeting from farther up the path. Birds circled high above the hills, riding the warm wind.

The man lifted up his eyes. He beheld the hills rising ahead of him. They were tall and rocky, with patches of scrubby trees clinging to their sides. The road curved betwixt them, narrow in some places, wide in others. He spake aloud, and the people near him could hear his words.

"Whence cometh mine help?" said he.

His voice was steady and clear. He kept walking as he spake. A woman bearing a water pitcher upon her head glanced over. A boy leading an ass slowed his steps to hearken.

Then the man answered his own question. He lifted one hand toward the wide sky above the hills.

"My help cometh from the Lord, which made heaven and earth," said he.

The words rang forth in the open air. A few travellers nodded as they walked. The hills stood still and solid round about them. Above, the sky stretched out blue and exceeding great, without a single cloud.

The man kept walking. His staff tapped the ground with each step. He spake again, and his voice carried easily along the road.

"He will not suffer thy foot to be moved," said he.

The path was uneven. Loose pebbles rolled beneath sandals. But the man placed each foot carefully. He watched the ground and kept his balance. Other travellers did likewise, stepping about rough spots and finding solid places to stand.

"He that keepeth thee will not slumber," the man continued.

A young maid walking with her family looked up at her father. Her father walked steadily beside her, his hand resting upon her shoulder. The man upon the road kept speaking.

"Behold, he that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep," said he.

The sun had risen higher now. Warm light spread athwart the hillsides. Lizards sat upon flat rocks, soaking in the heat. The savour of dry earth and sage drifted through the air. A shepherd upon a distant hill called unto his flock, and the sound echoed softly athwart the valley.

The man's voice came again, strong and even.

"The Lord is thy keeper," said he. "The Lord is thy shade upon thy right hand."

The sun was bright overhead. Some travellers pulled their head coverings forward to shadow their faces. A few walked close to the rocky walls where the hillside blocked the sun. Cool air gathered there, and the walking was easier in those spots. Children pressed close to their parents upon the sunny side.

"The sun shall not smite thee by day," said the man, "nor the moon by night."

The travellers moved together along the road. Their sandals made a soft, steady sound upon the packed dirt. One ahead began to hum a tune. The melody floated back through the company like a ribbon of sound. An ass flicked its ears and kept walking.

The road began to level out. The steep parts were behind them now. Ahead, the land opened up, and they could see green fields afar off. A well stood near a cluster of olive trees, and a few women were drawing water there. The savour of bread baking drifted from a small house nearby.

The man spake once more. His voice was calm and full.

"The Lord shall preserve thee from all evil," said he. "He shall preserve thy soul."

A breeze moved through the olive trees. Their silver-green leaves turned and flickered in the light. The women at the well looked up and smiled at the travellers. One of them offered a cup of water. The boy with the ass took it and drank. Water dropped from his chin, and he wiped it with the back of his hand.

The man stood near the olive trees now. He planted his staff in the ground and rested both hands upon it. He looked back at the road they had travelled. The hills rose behind them, layered one behind the other, brown and green and hazy blue afar off.

"The Lord shall preserve thy going out and thy coming in," said he, "from this time forth, and even for evermore."

The travellers gathered near the well. Some sat upon low stone walls. Some spread cloths upon the ground and unpacked bread and dried fruit. Children ran betwixt the olive trees, laughing and calling one to another. A babe slept in his mother's arms, wrapped in soft cloth.

The man sat down upon a flat stone. He laid his staff athwart his knees. The breeze touched his face and moved through his hair. Round about him, the sounds of the company filled the air. Cups clinked against the stone edge of the well. One brake bread and passed the pieces around. A goat wandered close and nudged a child's hand, seeking crumbs.

The olive trees rustled overhead. The wide sky held steady above the hills and the fields and the road. The man rested there, quiet and still, his journey continuing and the words he had spoken yet hanging in the warm afternoon air.

Nearby, the children played among the tree roots. The women filled their pitchers afresh at the well. The travellers ate together and passed food one to another. Everything was calm and unhurried. The bread was warm. The water was cool. The afternoon stretched out, gentle and bright, over all of them.
