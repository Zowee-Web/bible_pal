On that day, when the evening had come, Jesus said to his disciples, "Let us go over to the other side."

They left the crowd on the shore. They took him into the boat with them, just as he was. Other small boats were on the water too, near them.

They put out from the shore.

The wind came up before they were halfway across.

It came up suddenly. The waves beat into the boat. The sail strained. The wood of the hull groaned in the swell. Water began to come up over the side.

The boat was already filling.

The disciples worked at the ropes, and at the bailing, and at the rudder. They could not keep ahead of the water.

Jesus was in the stern.

He was asleep on the cushion. The wind was loud above his head. The water was loud against the planks beneath him. He did not move.

They went to him in the stern. They shook him awake. They had to shout above the wind.

"Teacher," they said. "Don't you care that we are dying?"

He stood up in the boat.

He rebuked the wind. He said to the sea, "Peace. Be still."

The wind ceased. The sea went flat.

There was a great calm on the water, and the boats on either side of them were no longer pitching.

Jesus turned to his disciples.

"Why are you so afraid?" he said. "How is it that you have no faith?"

They did not answer him.

They looked at each other in the boat, and they said one to another in the silence after the wind, "Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?"
