On that day, when the even was come, Jesus saith unto his disciples, "Let us pass over unto the other side."

They left the multitude upon the shore. They took him into the ship with them, even as he was. There were also other little ships with them upon the water.

They put forth from the shore.

The wind arose before they were halfway across.

It arose suddenly. The waves beat into the ship. The sail strained. The wood of the hull groaned in the swell. Water began to come up over the side.

The ship was now full.

The disciples laboured at the ropes, and at the bailing, and at the rudder. They could not keep ahead of the water.

Jesus was in the hinder part of the ship.

He was asleep upon a pillow. The wind was loud above his head. The water was loud against the planks beneath him. He moved not.

They went unto him in the hinder part. They awoke him. They had to shout above the wind.

"Master," said they. "Carest thou not that we perish?"

He arose in the ship.

He rebuked the wind. He said unto the sea, "Peace, be still."

The wind ceased. The sea went flat.

There was a great calm upon the water, and the ships on either side of them were no longer pitching.

Jesus turned unto his disciples.

"Why are ye so fearful?" said he. "How is it that ye have no faith?"

They answered him not.

They beheld one another in the ship, and they said one to another in the silence after the wind, "What manner of man is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?"
