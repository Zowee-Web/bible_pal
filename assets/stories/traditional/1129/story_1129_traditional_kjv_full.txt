In Caesarea by the sea, about the ninth hour of the day, Cornelius was at prayer in his house. He was a centurion of the band called the Italian band, a devout man that feared God with all his household. He gave much alms to the people, and he prayed alway.

The light at the ninth hour was already softening upon the stone walls of the courtyard. Cornelius had set himself apart at the hour of prayer as the Jews kept it, though he was a Roman. He bowed his head.

Then he saw evidently, in a vision, an angel of God coming in unto him.

"Cornelius," the angel said.

Cornelius fastened his eyes upon him. He was afraid. The strength went out of his shoulders, and he answered as a man answereth his commander. "What is it, Lord?"

"Thy prayers and thine alms are come up for a memorial before God. Now send men to Joppa, and call for one Simon, whose surname is Peter. He lodgeth with one Simon a tanner, whose house is by the sea side."

When the angel was departed, Cornelius did not tarry. He called two of his household servants, and he called a devout soldier of them that waited on him continually. He told them all the things that had been done. He sent them forth upon the road that ran south along the coast unto Joppa. The morning was clear when they went.

On the morrow, as they went on their journey and drew nigh unto the city, Peter went up upon the housetop to pray about the sixth hour. The sea was bright below him. He had become very hungry, and the smell of the meal which was making ready in the courtyard rose up unto the place where he stood. While they made ready, he fell into a trance.

He saw heaven opened. A certain vessel was descending unto him, as it had been a great sheet knit at the four corners, let down to the earth. Wherein were all manner of fourfooted beasts of the earth, and wild beasts, and creeping things, and fowls of the air. The sheet settled before him.

There came a voice unto him. "Rise, Peter; kill, and eat."

Peter drew back. "Not so, Lord; for I have never eaten any thing that is common or unclean."

The voice spake unto him again the second time. "What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common."

The sheet remained. The creatures within it stirred. And a third time the voice spake the same words. Then straightway the vessel was received up again into heaven, and the brightness closed above it.

Peter stood upon the housetop, perplexed in himself, turning the vision in his mind. He wist not what it meant.

Below him at the gate, three men were come. They had inquired for the house of Simon the tanner, and they called at the entrance.

The Spirit said unto Peter, "Behold, three men seek thee. Arise therefore, get thee down, and go with them, doubting nothing: for I have sent them."

Peter came down. He went forth unto the men at the gate. "Behold, I am he whom ye seek," he said. "Wherefore are ye come?"

They told him of Cornelius, the centurion, a just man, one that feared God, who was warned of a holy angel to send for him. Peter called them in and lodged them. On the morrow he arose and went away with them, and certain brethren from Joppa accompanied him.

On the day after, they entered into Caesarea. Cornelius waited for them. He had called together his kinsmen and near friends, and the house was full.

When Peter was come in, Cornelius met him at the door. He fell down at Peter's feet, and worshipped him. But Peter took him up, and raised him up.

"Stand up," he said. "I myself also am a man."

He went in with him, and found many that were come together. He looked upon the faces of the Gentiles which were gathered to hear, and the meaning of the sheet upon the housetop came clear unto him.

He opened his mouth and spake unto them. He told them of Jesus of Nazareth, whom God anointed with the Holy Ghost and with power, who went about doing good. He told them of the cross and of the third day. He told them that whosoever believeth in his name shall receive remission of sins.

While Peter yet spake these words, the Holy Ghost fell on all them which heard the word. They of the circumcision which came with Peter were astonished, because that on the Gentiles also was poured out the gift of the Holy Ghost. For they heard them speak with tongues, and magnify God.

Peter looked upon them. "Can any man forbid water, that these should not be baptized, which have received the Holy Ghost as well as we?"

And he commanded them to be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ. Then, while the sun moved over the sea and the household of Cornelius stood washed and shining in the open doorway, they prayed him to tarry certain days, and he abode there on.
