In Caesarea by the sea, in the house of a Roman centurion, the ninth hour came. The household paused. Cornelius was a centurion of what was called the Italian Regiment, but inside his own walls he kept the hour of prayer as the Jews kept it. He was a devout man. He feared God with all his house. He gave generously to those in need, and he prayed always.

He had been fasting that day until the ninth hour. His servants moved quietly through the courtyard. He went into the inner room. He bowed his head.

Then he saw clearly, in a vision, an angel of God coming in to him. The angel stood in bright clothing before him.

"Cornelius," the angel said.

Cornelius fastened his eyes on him. He was frightened. The breath caught in his chest, and the strength of a soldier went out of his shoulders. He answered as a man answers his commander.

"What is it, Lord?"

"Your prayer is heard," the angel said. "Your prayers and your gifts to the needy have gone up for a memorial before God. Now send men to Joppa, and call for one Simon, who is also called Peter. He is staying with a tanner named Simon, whose house is by the seaside. When he comes, he will speak to you."

The angel departed. The room was as it had been before — the lamp on its stand, the matting on the floor — and Cornelius stood in it alone.

He did not delay. He called two of his household servants. He called a devout soldier from among those who waited on him continually. He told them everything that had happened, every word the angel had spoken. He set the road before them. They were to go south along the coast, by the way that ran from Caesarea to Joppa, and they were to ask in that town for the house of Simon the tanner, by the seaside.

They went out while the light was still good. The road ran along the shore. The sea lay open on their right hand as they walked.

The next day, as they were on their journey and were drawing near to the city, Peter went up on the housetop of the tanner's house to pray. It was about the sixth hour. The sun stood high. Below him the sea of Joppa shone, and gulls turned over the water, and the smell of the meal being prepared in the courtyard rose up to him on the warm air. He had grown hungry. He waited for the food.

While they were preparing it below, he fell into a trance.

He saw the sky opened above him. A great sheet, like a wide linen cloth, was descending toward him. It came down by its four corners, as a sheet is let down by four hands, and it settled toward the earth before him. In it were all kinds of four-footed animals of the earth, and wild beasts, and creeping things, and birds of the sky. The creatures stirred within it. The corners of the cloth lay open on the ground.

A voice came to him. "Rise, Peter. Kill and eat."

Peter drew back. The whole life of his people stood up in him at the words. He had kept the law from his youth. He had eaten only what the law of his fathers allowed.

"Not so, Lord," he said. "For I have never eaten anything that is common or unclean."

The sheet did not lift away. The creatures within it did not depart. And the voice came to him a second time.

"What God has cleansed, you must not call common."

Peter looked again at the sheet. The same animals. The same word. And a third time, the voice spoke. The same words. The same call. Three times in all the call came down, and three times the same answer rose to meet it.

Then immediately the sheet was received back up into the sky. The brightness closed above it. The housetop was as it had been — the matting, the low wall, the sun on the stone — and Peter stood alone with the smell of the meal below.

He was very perplexed in himself. He turned the vision in his mind. He did not know what it meant.

Below him at the gate, three men had arrived. They had asked along the way for the house of Simon the tanner. They had found it. They stood at the entrance now and called out, asking whether Simon, who was also called Peter, was lodging in that house.

Peter did not yet hear them.

While he was pondering the vision, the Spirit spoke to him.

"Behold, three men seek you. Arise, get down, and go with them, doubting nothing. For I have sent them."

Peter came down from the housetop. He went to the gate. He saw the three men there — two of them in the dress of household servants, one in the dress of a soldier — and he opened the gate to them.

"Behold, I am he whom you seek. Why have you come?"

They answered him. They told him of Cornelius, a centurion, a righteous man and one who feared God, well spoken of by all the nation of the Jews, who had been directed by a holy angel of God to invite him to his house, and to listen to what he would say.

Peter called them in. He gave them a place to stay that night. The next morning he arose. He went out with them, and some of the brothers from Joppa went also with him on the road.

On the day after that, they entered Caesarea.

Cornelius had been waiting for them. He had called together his relatives and his close friends, and the house was full. The courtyard was full. The household stood ready in clean garments. When they heard that Peter was at the gate, every face turned toward the door.

Peter entered.

Cornelius met him at the threshold. He fell down at Peter's feet to worship him. But Peter took hold of him by the arms and raised him up.

"Stand up," he said. "I myself am also a man."

He talked with him as they went in, and he found many gathered together inside.

He looked at them — the centurion's relatives, his close friends, the household, the Gentiles all — and the meaning of the sheet on the housetop came clear to him. He understood now what God had cleansed. He understood now why the call had come three times. He understood now why the Spirit had said to doubt nothing.

He opened his mouth and spoke.

"Truly I perceive that God shows no favoritism," he said. "In every nation, he who fears him and works righteousness is acceptable to him."

He spoke to them then of Jesus of Nazareth, whom God had anointed with the Holy Spirit and with power, who went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil. He spoke of the cross. He spoke of the third day. He spoke of the witnesses chosen before by God, who had eaten and drunk with him after he rose from the dead. He told them that all the prophets bore witness about him, and that everyone who believed in his name would receive the forgiveness of sins.

While Peter was still speaking these words, the Holy Spirit fell on all those who heard the word.

The believers of the circumcision who had come with Peter were amazed. The gift of the Holy Spirit had been poured out on the Gentiles also. They heard them speaking in other languages and magnifying God. They stood there in the courtyard of a Roman house and could not deny what had been given.

Peter looked at the brothers from Joppa. He looked at the Gentile household around him. The room was full of the same Spirit on both sides of him.

"Can anyone forbid these people from being baptized with water?" he said. "They have received the Holy Spirit just as we have."

He commanded that they be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ. They brought water. One by one they came forward. The centurion came. His household came. His relatives and his close friends came. Each received baptism into the name. The roof above Joppa and the courtyard in Caesarea were two open places now, and they were one open room above them all.

When it was finished, the household asked Peter to remain with them some days. He stayed on with the Gentiles in the house of the centurion, and the door stood open to the sea, and the next morning the sun rose on a house in which the boundary had fallen.
