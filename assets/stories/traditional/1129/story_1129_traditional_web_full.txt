In Caesarea by the sea, at about the ninth hour of the day, Cornelius was at prayer in his house. He was a centurion of the Italian Regiment, a devout man who feared God with all his household. He gave generously to those in need, and he prayed always.

The light at the ninth hour was already softening on the stone walls of the courtyard. Cornelius had set himself apart at the hour of prayer as the Jews kept it, though he was a Roman. He bowed his head.

Then he saw clearly, in a vision, an angel of God coming in to him.

"Cornelius," the angel said.

Cornelius fastened his eyes on him. He was afraid. The strength went out of his shoulders, and he answered as a man answers his commander. "What is it, Lord?"

"Your prayers and your gifts to the needy have gone up for a memorial before God. Now send men to Joppa, and call for one Simon, who is also called Peter. He is staying with a tanner named Simon, whose house is by the seaside."

When the angel had departed, Cornelius did not delay. He called two of his household servants, and he called a devout soldier from among those who waited on him continually. He told them everything that had happened. He sent them out on the road that ran south along the coast toward Joppa. The morning was clear when they went.

The next day, as they were on their journey and were drawing near to the city, Peter went up on the housetop to pray at about the sixth hour. The sea was bright below him. He had grown hungry, and the smell of the meal being prepared in the courtyard rose up to where he stood. While they were preparing it, he fell into a trance.

He saw the sky opened. A great sheet, like a wide linen cloth, was descending toward him, let down by its four corners onto the earth. In it were all kinds of four-footed animals, and wild beasts, and creeping things, and birds of the sky. The sheet settled before him.

A voice came to him. "Rise, Peter. Kill and eat."

Peter drew back. "Not so, Lord, for I have never eaten anything that is common or unclean."

A second time the voice came. "What God has cleansed, you must not call common."

The sheet remained. The creatures within it stirred. And a third time the voice spoke the same words. Then immediately the sheet was received back up into the sky, and the brightness closed above it.

Peter stood on the housetop, perplexed in himself, turning the vision in his mind. He did not know what it meant.

Below him at the gate, three men had arrived. They had asked for the house of Simon the tanner, and they were calling at the entrance.

The Spirit said to Peter, "Behold, three men seek you. Arise, get down, and go with them, doubting nothing. For I have sent them."

Peter came down. He went out to the men at the gate. "I am he whom you seek," he said. "Why have you come?"

They told him about Cornelius, the centurion, a righteous man who feared God, who was directed by a holy angel to send for him. Peter called them in and gave them a place to stay. The next day he arose and went with them, and some of the brothers from Joppa went also.

On the day after that, they entered Caesarea. Cornelius was waiting for them. He had called together his relatives and his close friends, and the house was full.

When Peter entered, Cornelius met him at the door. He fell down at Peter's feet to worship him. But Peter took hold of him and raised him up.

"Stand up," he said. "I myself am also a man."

He went in with him, and he found many gathered together. He looked at the faces of the Gentiles who had come to listen, and the meaning of the sheet on the housetop came clear to him.

He opened his mouth and spoke to them. He told them of Jesus of Nazareth, whom God had anointed with the Holy Spirit and with power, who had gone about doing good. He told them of the cross and of the third day. He told them that everyone who believed in his name would receive the forgiveness of sins.

While Peter was still speaking these words, the Holy Spirit fell on all those who heard the word. The believers of the circumcision who had come with Peter were amazed, for the gift of the Holy Spirit had been poured out on the Gentiles also. They heard them speaking in other languages and magnifying God.

Peter looked at them. "Can anyone forbid these people from being baptized with water? They have received the Holy Spirit just as we have."

He commanded that they be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ. Then, while the sun moved over the sea and the household of Cornelius stood washed and shining in the open doorway, they asked Peter to stay with them some days, and he stayed on.
