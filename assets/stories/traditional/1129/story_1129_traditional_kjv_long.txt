In Caesarea by the sea, in the house of a Roman centurion, the ninth hour was come. The household paused. Cornelius was a centurion of the band called the Italian band, but within his own walls he kept the hour of prayer as the Jews kept it. He was a devout man. He feared God with all his house. He gave much alms to the people, and he prayed alway.

He had fasted that day until the ninth hour. His servants moved quietly through the courtyard. He went into the inner chamber. He bowed his head.

Then he saw evidently, in a vision, an angel of God coming in unto him. The angel stood before him in bright clothing.

"Cornelius," the angel said.

Cornelius fastened his eyes upon him. He was afraid. The breath caught in his chest, and the strength of a soldier went out of his shoulders. He answered as a man answereth his commander.

"What is it, Lord?"

"Thy prayer is heard," the angel said. "Thy prayers and thine alms are come up for a memorial before God. Now send men to Joppa, and call for one Simon, whose surname is Peter. He lodgeth with one Simon a tanner, whose house is by the sea side. When he is come, he shall speak unto thee."

The angel departed. The chamber was as it had been before — the lamp upon its stand, the matting upon the floor — and Cornelius stood in it alone.

He did not tarry. He called two of his household servants. He called a devout soldier of them that waited on him continually. He told them all the things that had been done, every word the angel had spoken. He set the road before them. They were to go south along the coast, by the way that ran from Caesarea unto Joppa, and they were to inquire in that town for the house of Simon the tanner, by the sea side.

They went forth while the light was yet good. The road ran along the shore. The sea lay open upon their right hand as they walked.

On the morrow, as they went on their journey and drew nigh unto the city, Peter went up upon the housetop of the tanner's house to pray. It was about the sixth hour. The sun stood high. Below him the sea of Joppa shone, and gulls turned over the water, and the smell of the meal which was making ready in the courtyard rose up unto him upon the warm air. He had become very hungry. He waited for the meat.

While they made ready below, he fell into a trance.

He saw heaven opened above him. A certain vessel was descending unto him, as it had been a great sheet knit at the four corners. It came down by its four corners, as a sheet is let down by four hands, and settled toward the earth before him. Wherein were all manner of fourfooted beasts of the earth, and wild beasts, and creeping things, and fowls of the air. The creatures stirred within it. The corners of the cloth lay open upon the ground.

There came a voice unto him. "Rise, Peter; kill, and eat."

Peter drew back. The whole life of his people rose up in him at the words. He had kept the law from his youth. He had eaten only what the law of his fathers suffered him to eat.

"Not so, Lord," he said. "For I have never eaten any thing that is common or unclean."

The sheet did not lift away. The creatures within it did not depart. And the voice came unto him the second time.

"What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common."

Peter looked again upon the sheet. The same beasts. The same word. And a third time, the voice spake. The same words. The same call. This was done thrice, and three times the same answer rose to meet it.

Then straightway the vessel was received up again into heaven. The brightness closed above it. The housetop was as it had been — the matting, the low wall, the sun upon the stone — and Peter stood alone with the smell of the meal below.

He was sore perplexed in himself. He turned the vision in his mind. He wist not what it meant.

Below him at the gate, three men were come. They had inquired along the way for the house of Simon the tanner. They had found it. They stood at the entrance now and called out, asking whether Simon, which was surnamed Peter, were lodged therein.

Peter heard them not as yet.

While he thought on the vision, the Spirit spake unto him.

"Behold, three men seek thee. Arise therefore, get thee down, and go with them, doubting nothing: for I have sent them."

Peter came down from the housetop. He went unto the gate. He saw the three men there — two of them in the apparel of household servants, one in the apparel of a soldier — and he opened the gate unto them.

"Behold, I am he whom ye seek. Wherefore are ye come?"

They answered him. They told him of Cornelius, a centurion, a just man, one that feared God, of good report among all the nation of the Jews, who was warned by a holy angel of God to send for him into his house, and to hear words of him.

Peter called them in. He lodged them that night. On the morrow he arose. He went away with them, and certain brethren from Joppa went with him on the way.

On the day after, they entered into Caesarea.

Cornelius had waited for them. He had called together his kinsmen and near friends, and the house was full. The courtyard was full. The household stood ready in clean garments. When they heard that Peter was at the gate, every face turned toward the door.

Peter entered in.

Cornelius met him at the threshold. He fell down at Peter's feet, and worshipped him. But Peter took him up by the arms, and raised him up.

"Stand up," he said. "I myself also am a man."

He talked with him as they went in, and found many that were come together within.

He looked upon them — the centurion's kinsmen, his near friends, the household, the Gentiles all — and the meaning of the sheet upon the housetop came clear unto him. He understood now what God had cleansed. He understood now wherefore the call had come three times. He understood now wherefore the Spirit had said to doubt nothing.

He opened his mouth and spake.

"Of a truth I perceive that God is no respecter of persons," he said. "But in every nation he that feareth him, and worketh righteousness, is accepted with him."

He spake unto them then of Jesus of Nazareth, whom God anointed with the Holy Ghost and with power, who went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil. He spake of the cross. He spake of the third day. He spake of the witnesses chosen before of God, which did eat and drink with him after he rose from the dead. He told them that to him give all the prophets witness, that whosoever believeth in his name shall receive remission of sins.

While Peter yet spake these words, the Holy Ghost fell on all them which heard the word.

They of the circumcision which came with Peter were astonished. The gift of the Holy Ghost was poured out on the Gentiles also. They heard them speak with tongues, and magnify God. They stood there in the courtyard of a Roman house and could not deny that which had been given.

Peter looked upon the brethren from Joppa. He looked upon the Gentile household round about him. The room was full of the same Spirit on both sides of him.

"Can any man forbid water," he said, "that these should not be baptized, which have received the Holy Ghost as well as we?"

And he commanded them to be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ. They brought water. One by one they came forward. The centurion came. His household came. His kinsmen and his near friends came. Each received baptism into the name. The roof above Joppa and the courtyard in Caesarea were two open places now, and they were one open chamber above them all.

When it was finished, the household prayed Peter to tarry with them certain days. He abode there with the Gentiles in the house of the centurion, and the door stood open unto the sea, and on the morrow the sun rose upon a house wherein the boundary was fallen.
