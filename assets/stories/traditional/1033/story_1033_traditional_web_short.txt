A man of the house of Levi went and married a daughter of Levi. The woman conceived and bore a son. When she saw that he was a fine child, she hid him three months.

The house was small. The walls were mud brick. Every sound carried — a cough, a cry, a neighbor's footstep outside. Pharaoh had commanded that every Hebrew son born should be cast into the Nile. Soldiers moved through the streets. They listened at doors.

For three months the woman hid the baby. She nursed him in the dark. She held her hand near his mouth when he stirred. When he cried, she pressed him close and swayed until the sound faded. Her older daughter helped. The girl learned to move without noise and to watch the road.

When she could hide him no longer, she took bulrushes from the riverbank. She wove them into a basket. She daubed it with tar and with pitch, pressing the mixture into every seam. She lined it with cloth. She laid the child in it. He fit exactly, his arms drawn up beside his face.

She carried the basket to the river and set it among the reeds at the water's edge. The current moved around it, slow and brown. The reeds held it steady. She turned and walked away. She did not look back.

His sister stood at a distance, behind the tall grass, her feet in the mud. She watched. The basket rocked gently. The baby was quiet.

Pharaoh's daughter came down to the river to bathe. Her attendants walked along the bank. She saw the basket among the reeds and sent her servant to fetch it.

She opened it. The baby was crying. She had compassion on him. "This is one of the Hebrews' children," she said.

Then his sister stepped forward. "Shall I go and find you a nurse from the Hebrew women to nurse the child for you?"

Pharaoh's daughter said, "Go."

The girl ran. She found her mother and brought her back. Pharaoh's daughter said to the woman, "Take this child and nurse him for me, and I will give you your wages." So the woman took her own child and nursed him.

The child grew. When he was old enough, she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son. She named him Moses, saying, "Because I drew him out of the water."
