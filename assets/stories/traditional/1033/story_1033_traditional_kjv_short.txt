And there went a man of the house of Levi, and took to wife a daughter of Levi. And the woman conceived and bare a son. When she saw him that he was a goodly child, she hid him three months.

The house was small. The walls were of mud brick. Every sound passed through — a cough, a cry, a neighbour's step without. Pharaoh had commanded that every son born to the Hebrews should be cast into the river. Soldiers moved through the streets. They hearkened at doors.

Three months the woman kept the babe hidden. She nursed him in the dark. She held her hand near his mouth when he stirred. When he cried, she pressed him close and swayed until the sound was stilled. Her elder daughter helped. The girl learned to tread softly and to watch the road.

When she could not longer hide him, she took bulrushes from the river's brink. She wove them into an ark. She daubed it with slime and with pitch, pressing the matter into every seam. She lined it with cloth. She laid the child therein. He fit within, his arms drawn up beside his face.

She carried the ark to the river and set it among the flags at the water's edge. The current moved about it, slow and brown. The flags held it steady. She turned and went away. She looked not back.

His sister stood afar off, behind the tall grass, her feet in the mire. She watched. The ark rocked gently. The babe was quiet.

And the daughter of Pharaoh came down to wash herself at the river. Her maidens walked along the bank. She saw the ark among the flags and sent her maid to fetch it.

She opened it. The babe was weeping. She had compassion on him. "This is one of the Hebrews' children," she said.

Then his sister came forth. "Shall I go and call to thee a nurse of the Hebrew women, that she may nurse the child for thee?"

And Pharaoh's daughter said, "Go."

The girl ran. She found her mother and brought her back. Pharaoh's daughter said unto the woman, "Take this child away and nurse it for me, and I will give thee thy wages." And the woman took her own child and nursed it.

And the child grew. When he was grown, she brought him unto Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son. She called his name Moses, saying, "Because I drew him out of the water."
