Amos came up from Tekoa. He was a herdsman and a dresser of sycamore trees, and he had not been trained in the schools of the prophets, but the word of Yahweh had come to him in the field, and he had carried it north.

He stood now at Bethel, in the days of Jeroboam the son of Joash. The kingdom was rich. The altars were many. The festivals filled the calendar. There were silver in the houses and ivory in the beds and feasts that went on for days. And in the alleys of the same city the poor were sold for a pair of sandals, and the gleanings were not left at the corners of the field, and the judges at the gate took the bribe.

Amos lifted up his voice.

"Woe to you who desire the day of Yahweh," he said. "Why do you long for the day of Yahweh? It is darkness, and not light. It is as when a man flees from a lion and a bear meets him, or as when he goes into the house and leans his hand on the wall, and a snake bites him.

"Shall not the day of Yahweh be darkness, and not light? Even very dark, with no brightness in it?

"I hate, I despise your feasts, and I cannot stand your solemn assemblies. Though you offer me your burnt offerings and your meal offerings, I will not accept them. Neither will I regard the peace offerings of your fat animals. Take away from me the noise of your songs. I will not listen to the music of your harps.

"But let justice roll on like rivers, and righteousness like a mighty stream."

The words struck the steps of the altar at Bethel and ran back out into the streets of the city. The priests there did not love them. The king's man at Bethel sent word south to the palace that the prophet from Tekoa was speaking against the king's house. Amos was told to go home.

He did not go home yet. He had not finished saying what he had been sent to say.

And so it was in those days, in the city of Bethel, that the herdsman from Tekoa stood in the place of the king's altar and called for the river of justice, and the noise of the festival went on around him, and the river had not yet begun to run.
