King David sat on his throne in Jerusalem. The stone room was quiet. The guards stood along the walls. The light from the high windows fell across the floor.

David spoke.

"Is there anyone left from Saul's family," David asked, "that I can show kindness to — for Jonathan's sake?"

A servant named Ziba was brought in. He had worked for Saul's family.

"Is there anyone left?" David asked again.

Ziba nodded. "There is one. Jonathan's son. His name is Mephibosheth. He is lame in both feet."

When Mephibosheth was just five years old, terrible news had come — Jonathan and Saul were dead. The Philistines were winning. Everyone panicked. Mephibosheth's nurse had grabbed him and started running. But in her hurry, she tripped or he slipped, and the little boy fell hard. Both his feet were crushed. He never walked right again.

"Where is he?" David asked.

"He lives in Lo-debar." A small, dusty place out beyond the Jordan River.

"Bring him to me," David said.

Servants rode out to Lo-debar and brought Mephibosheth back to Jerusalem. The journey was long. Mephibosheth could not walk on his own — someone carried him or helped him onto a donkey. The road was dusty and the sun was hot.

The doors of the palace opened. Mephibosheth was brought inside. The hallway was long and cool, the floor made of smooth stone. He saw the throne at the far end. He saw the guards standing at attention on either side. He saw David sitting there, watching him come closer.

He fell flat on his face on the stone floor. His damaged feet were behind him. His whole body trembled.

"Mephibosheth," David said.

A tiny voice answered from the floor. "Here I am. Your servant."

David said, "Don't be afraid. I am going to show you kindness for the sake of your father Jonathan. I'm going to give back all the land that belonged to your grandfather Saul. And you — you will eat at my table. Every day. For the rest of your life. Like one of my own sons."

He bowed his head low, his forehead against the cool stone. "What am I," he whispered, "that you would even notice me? I am nothing — like a dead dog."

So Mephibosheth moved to Jerusalem. Servants were assigned to farm his land and bring in food for his household. But Mephibosheth himself ate at the king's table — the same table where David's own sons sat, where the most important people in the kingdom gathered.

Every evening, the servants set out the food — bread and roasted meat, bowls of lentils, cups of wine, dishes of figs and honey. The wooden platters steamed. The oil lamps flickered along the walls. The princes took their seats. The officials sat down. And Mephibosheth was carried in and placed at his chair.

A man who couldn't walk. A man from a family that had lost everything.

Sitting at the king's table. Eating the king's bread. His damaged feet hidden beneath the tablecloth, and his place set right alongside the princes.
