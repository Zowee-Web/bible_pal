King David sat upon his throne in Jerusalem.

David asked, "Is there yet any that is left of the house of Jonathan, that I may shew him kindness for Jonathan's sake?"

A servant told him, "Jonathan hath yet a son. His name is Mephibosheth. He is lame on his feet — both his feet were hurt when he was little."

When Mephibosheth was but five years old, his nurse had taken him up and fled with him. She made so much haste that he fell, and both his feet were sore hurt. He had not been able to walk on his own ever since.

"Where dwelleth he?" David asked.

"In a place called Lo-debar. Afar off."

David sent for him straightway.

When Mephibosheth came unto the palace, he fell on his face to the ground before David. His whole body trembled.

"Mephibosheth," David said.

"Behold thy servant," Mephibosheth whispered.

"Fear not. I will surely shew thee kindness for Jonathan thy father's sake. I will restore thee all the land of Saul thy grandfather. And thou shalt eat bread at my table continually — even as one of mine own sons."

"What is thy servant," he said, "that thou shouldest look upon such a one as I am? I am nothing."

But David meant every word. From that day forth, Mephibosheth dwelt in Jerusalem. He did eat at the king's table continually, sitting hard by the king's own sons. His place was set. The bread was warm. And his lame feet rested beneath the tablecloth.
