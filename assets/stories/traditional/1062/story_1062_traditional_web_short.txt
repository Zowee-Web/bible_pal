King David was thinking about his old friend Jonathan. Jonathan had died in a battle a long time ago, but David had never forgotten him. They had been best friends — the kind of friends who promised to take care of each other's families no matter what happened.

David asked, "Is there anyone left from Jonathan's family? I want to be kind to them — for Jonathan's sake."

A servant told him, "Jonathan has a son. His name is Mephibosheth. He can't walk — both his feet were hurt when he was little."

When Mephibosheth was only five years old, his nurse had been carrying him away from danger. She was running so fast that he fell, and both his feet were badly injured. He had been unable to walk on his own ever since.

"Where does he live?" David asked.

"In a place called Lo-debar. Far away." A small, dusty, forgotten town in the middle of nowhere.

David sent for him right away.

Mephibosheth must have been very scared on the journey to the palace. When kings sent for people from an enemy's family, it usually meant trouble. Mephibosheth's grandfather had been King Saul, and Saul had tried to hurt David many times. The whole ride to Jerusalem, Mephibosheth must have been wondering what the king wanted with him.

When Mephibosheth arrived at the palace, he fell flat on the floor in front of David. He was shaking.

"Mephibosheth," David said gently.

"I'm here," Mephibosheth whispered. "I'm your servant."

David said the most wonderful words. "Don't be afraid. I'm going to be kind to you because of your father Jonathan. I'm giving you back all the land that belonged to your grandfather. And from now on, you will eat at my table — every single day — like one of my own children."

"Why would you pay attention to someone like me?" he asked. "I'm nothing."

But David meant every word. From that day on, Mephibosheth lived in Jerusalem. He ate at the king's table every day, sitting right alongside the king's own sons. His place was set. The bread was warm. And his damaged feet rested beneath the tablecloth.
