Paul wrote the second letter to Timothy from the prison in Rome in the last days of his life. He had been left now by almost all his companions: Demas had departed, loving the present world; Crescens had gone to Galatia; Titus to Dalmatia; only Luke was with him in the cell. He sat at the small table where the chain allowed his hands to write. He remembered Timothy's tears at the parting many months before in Ephesus, and he set down the opening of the letter while the lamp burned low beside him.

I thank God, whom I serve as my forefathers did, with a pure conscience. How unceasing is my memory of you in my petitions, night and day

longing to see you, remembering your tears, that I may be filled with joy;

having been reminded of the sincere faith that is in you; which lived first in your grandmother Lois, and your mother Eunice, and, I am persuaded, in you also.

For this cause, I remind you that you should stir up the gift of God which is in you through the laying on of my hands.

For God didn't give us a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and self-control.

And the letter went out toward Ephesus in the hand of the messenger Tychicus a few days later. The lamp in the small cell burned down to the last of the oil; Luke wrote on at the table as Paul dictated more of the letter; and the memory of the faith of grandmother Lois and mother Eunice stayed warm in the apostle's mind, in the quiet of his last days at Rome.
