Paul wrote the second epistle unto Timothy out of the prison at Rome in the last days of his life. He had been forsaken now of almost all his companions: Demas had departed, loving this present world; Crescens was gone unto Galatia; Titus unto Dalmatia; only Luke was with him in the cell. He sat at the small table where the chain suffered his hands to write. He called to remembrance the tears of Timothy at the parting many months before in Ephesus, and he set down the opening of the epistle while the lamp burned low beside him.

I thank God, whom I serve from my forefathers with pure conscience, that without ceasing I have remembrance of thee in my prayers night and day;

Greatly desiring to see thee, being mindful of thy tears, that I may be filled with joy;

When I call to remembrance the unfeigned faith that is in thee, which dwelt first in thy grandmother Lois, and thy mother Eunice; and I am persuaded that in thee also.

Wherefore I put thee in remembrance that thou stir up the gift of God, which is in thee by the putting on of my hands.

For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind.

And the epistle went forth toward Ephesus in the hand of the messenger Tychicus a few days after. The lamp in the little cell burned down unto the last of the oil; Luke wrote on at the table as Paul dictated more of the epistle; and the remembrance of the faith of grandmother Lois and mother Eunice abode warm in the mind of the apostle, in the quiet of his last days at Rome.
