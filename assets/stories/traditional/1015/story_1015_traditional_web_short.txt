Warm afternoon sunlight spread across the wide stone steps of the temple in Jerusalem. People walked up and down, talking quietly. Some carried small bundles. Others led their children by the hand. Birds landed near the edges of the steps and pecked at crumbs. The stones were smooth and pale, worn by many feet over many years.

A man sat near the gate called Beautiful. He had been unable to walk since the day he was born. Every day, friends carried him here and set him down gently on a soft mat. He would ask people passing by if they could share a few coins with him. His voice was calm and familiar to many who came this way. The gate behind him was tall, with bronze edges that caught the light.

Peter and John walked together toward the temple. It was about three o'clock in the afternoon, the regular hour for prayer. Their sandals tapped softly on the stone. As they came close to the gate, the man on the mat looked up and asked them for something.

Peter stopped walking. John stopped too. Peter looked straight at the man and said, "Look at us." The man turned his face toward them, watching carefully.

Peter spoke again. "I don't have silver or gold," he said. "But what I do have, I give to you." He paused. "In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, get up and walk."

Peter reached down and took the man by his right hand. He lifted him up. Right away, the man's feet and ankles became strong. He stood. For the first time in his whole life, his legs held him steady.

He took one step. Then another. His feet pressed against the warm stone. He walked forward through the gate called Beautiful. Then he began to leap. He jumped once, twice, three times. His arms lifted high. He began praising God out loud, his voice ringing across the open courtyard.

People nearby turned to look. They saw the man walking and jumping. Many of them recognized him. He was the same man who used to sit by the gate every single day. They watched him move freely across the wide stone floor of the temple.

He stayed close to Peter and John, walking beside them. His steps were quick and light. He kept speaking words of thanks to God. His voice carried through the open air, past the columns, past the other people gathered for prayer.

All around the temple courtyard, people stopped what they were doing. They stared. They could see clearly — this was the man from the Beautiful Gate. And now he was walking and leaping and lifting his voice in praise.

The afternoon sun rested warmly on the courtyard stones. The man's sandals touched the ground again and again, steady and sure. He walked with Peter and John through the open temple, still praising God with every step.
