Afternoon sunlight warmed the wide stone steps of the temple in Jerusalem. Dust from the busy road settled upon sandals and bare feet. Merchants cried out nearby, selling bread and figs. Birds alighted upon the edges of the stone walls, pecking at crumbs. The people walked up and down the steps in small companies. Some bore baskets. Others held the hands of their children. The temple gate called Beautiful stood tall and wide. Its bronze surface caught the light and glowed a deep golden brown. Vines crept along the lower edges of the surrounding walls. A warm wind bore the savour of olive oil and baked grain from the courts within.

Near the bottom of the steps, a man sat upon a folded cloth beside the gate. He had been borne thither that morning, as he was every day, by friends which brought him from his house. His legs had never served him, no, not from the day he was born. He could neither stand nor walk. So each day, his friends laid him down gently by the gate, and he sat and asked an alms of them which passed by. His hands rested open upon his lap. His voice was calm and steady as he spake unto them that walked near. Some dropped small coins into his hands. Others nodded and went on. He thanked each one which stopped.

The hour of afternoon prayer was come. Many of the people went up the steps toward the entering in of the temple. Among them walked two men named Peter and John. They moved together through the multitude, stepping around children and merchants. Their robes shifted with every step. Peter had broad hands and sun-browned skin. John walked close beside him. They were going up to the temple to pray, even as many others did at that hour.

As Peter and John drew near unto the gate called Beautiful, the man sitting upon the cloth looked up at them. He stretched forth his hands and asked an alms of them. He spake the same words he spake unto many a man each day, asking if they might give him a coin.

Peter stayed his steps. He turned and fastened his eyes upon the man. John stayed also. Peter spake in a clear, steady voice. "Look on us," he said.

The man turned his face toward them. He beheld them carefully, holding forth his hands.

Peter spake again. "Silver and gold have I none," he said. "But such as I have give I thee." He paused. "In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk."

Peter reached down. He took him by the right hand. Peter's grip was firm and steady. He helped the man rise up from the ground. Immediately his feet and ankle bones received strength. The bones and sinews which had never borne him up before now upheld his whole body. His legs straightened beneath him. His ankles steadied.

The man stood. For the first time in all his life, he stood upon his own two feet. The stone beneath his soles was warm from the sun. He pressed down and his legs held. He shifted his weight from one foot to the other. Then he lifted one foot and set it forward. He took a step. Then another. His arms moved at his sides for balance. His steps grew quicker and more sure.

He began to walk. He walked beside Peter and John. Then he began to leap. His feet left the ground and came down again, and his legs caught him each time. He leaped once more, higher, and lighted upon both feet. A wide smile spread across his face. His voice rang out across the courtyard. He praised God with loud and joyful words. His voice echoed off the stone walls and the bronze gate.

Peter and John passed through the gate called Beautiful, and the man went in with them. He walked and leaped and praised God with every step. His bare feet smote against the smooth temple stones. The people nearby turned to behold him. They watched him leap and heard his voice lifted up in thanksgiving unto God.

Within the temple courts, more of the people gathered. They knew him. They had seen him sitting by the gate day after day, year after year. They had passed him many times. And lo, here he was, walking upon his own feet, leaping like one which had walked his whole life. His arms stretched wide as he praised God again and again. His voice carried across the open space.

The people stayed their work. The women set down their baskets. The men paused mid-step. The children turned to look. All gazed upon him. The man kept walking, kept leaping, kept lifting up thanks unto God. His cloth yet hung from his shoulder, dusty from the road. His hands, which had been held open for coins but a moment before, now clapped together and reached upward.

The temple courts were filled with the sound of his voice. It mingled with the cooing of doves upon the rooftops and the soft rustle of robes as the people pressed closer to see. The man moved freely among them. His steps were strong and steady. He walked from one side of the courtyard to the other, then turned and walked back again.

Peter and John stood nearby. The man held fast unto them, praising God yet. The afternoon light stretched long across the temple stones. The warm air bore the sound of his thankful words over the walls and forth into the streets without.

And all that saw him walking and heard him giving thanks unto God were filled with wonder. They gathered round about, beholding the man which once sat beside the gate now moving through the temple upon his own strong feet. His voice grew not quiet. He praised God with every new step he took across those wide and sunlit stones.
