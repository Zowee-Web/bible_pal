Warm afternoon sunlight spread across the wide stone steps of the temple in Jerusalem. The air bore the savour of dust and dried herbs. The people walked up and down the steps in small companies, speaking quietly one to another. Some bore bundles wrapped in cloth. Others held baskets close at their sides. A few led their children by the hand, moving slowly that small legs might keep pace.

Birds alighted near the bottom of the steps and pecked at crumbs left behind from the morning. Their soft cooing mingled with the sound of sandals upon stone. The steps themselves were smooth and pale, worn down by many feet over many years. Wide cracks ran between some of the stones, and tiny green plants sprang up in the gaps. The temple walls rose high above, golden and white in the bright sun.

Near the top of the steps, a tall gate stood open. The people called it the gate Beautiful. Its edges were of polished bronze, which caught the afternoon light and glowed. The gate was wide enough that several might walk through at once. Its frame was carven with patterns that curved and looped across the surface. Many of the people passed through this gate every day on their way to pray within the temple courts.

A man sat upon a soft mat near the foot of this gate. He had been lame from his mother's womb. His legs had never carried him anywhere. Every morning, his friends came unto his house and lifted him up carefully. They bore him through the streets of Jerusalem, past market stalls and doorways and low stone walls. They brought him hither and laid him down gently upon his mat beside the gate.

He sat with his back resting against the warm stone wall. His mat was made of woven cloth, soft and faded from many days of use. A small clay cup sat upon the ground beside him. He would call out in a calm and steady voice unto them that walked past. He asked if they might share with him a few coins. Many that came this way knew his face and his voice. He had sat in this same place for a long time.

The sun moved slowly across the sky. More of the people began to come up unto the temple. It drew nigh unto the ninth hour, even the hour of prayer. Families walked together. Older men leaned upon wooden staves. Women bore small jars of oil. The steps grew busier, and the sound of many voices filled the air like a low hum.

Peter and John walked side by side along the stone road that led up unto the temple. Their robes swayed gently as they moved. Their sandals tapped softly against the paving stones. Peter walked with long, steady steps. John kept pace beside him. They made no haste. They moved with the multitude, going up toward the gate Beautiful for the afternoon prayer.

As they drew nearer, the tall bronze gate grew greater before them. Sunlight reflected off its polished face. The people moved through the gate in a steady stream. Peter and John stepped around a woman bearing a basket of bread. They passed a company of children sitting upon the edge of a low wall, swinging their feet.

The man upon the mat near the gate looked up. He saw Peter and John drawing near. He lifted up his hand and spake unto them. He asked if they had aught to share with him. His voice was clear and gentle, even the same words he spake unto many a man each day.

Peter stayed his steps. His sandals pressed flat against the stone. John stayed also, standing just beside him. Peter turned his whole body toward the man upon the mat. He fastened his eyes upon him.

"Look on us," Peter said.

The man turned his face toward Peter and John. He beheld them carefully. His hands rested still upon his lap. His eyes moved from one countenance to the other.

Peter spake again. His voice was steady and clear. "Silver and gold have I none," he said. "But such as I have give I thee." He paused for but a moment. The people walking nearby kept moving past. A bird alighted upon the step behind Peter and tilted its head.

Then Peter said, "In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk."

Peter reached down with his right hand. He took hold of the man's right hand. His fingers wrapped firmly about the man's palm. Then Peter lifted him up. The man's weight shifted. His body rose from the mat.

Immediately his feet and ankle bones received strength. His legs straightened beneath him. He stood upright upon the smooth stone for the first time in all his life. His sandals pressed flat against the ground. His knees held steady. His back was straight.

He stood still for a moment. The warm stone was solid under his feet. Then he took one step. His right foot moved forward and lighted upon the ground. Then his left foot followed. One step. Then another. His arms moved at his sides for balance. His mat lay empty behind him upon the ground, the small clay cup yet sitting beside it.

He walked forth. Each step came a little quicker than the one before. His feet pressed firmly against the warm stone. He passed through the gate Beautiful, his shoulders moving between the tall bronze edges. Sunlight poured through the opening and fell across his face and arms.

Within the gate, the temple courtyard opened wide. Pale stone tiles stretched out in every direction. Tall pillars stood in rows along the edges. The people gathered in companies, some standing, some sitting upon low benches. The sound of quiet speech filled the open space.

The man kept walking. Then his steps were changed. He began to leap. One leap, then another. His feet left the ground and came down with a soft tap. He leaped again, higher this time. His arms lifted into the air above his head. His fingers spread wide.

He began praising God aloud. His voice rang across the open courtyard. The words came quickly, one after another. He thanked God again and again. His voice was strong and full. It carried past the pillars, past the companies of the people, past the far edges of the courtyard.

The people nearby turned to behold him. A woman holding a water jar stayed her steps and gazed. Two men standing near a pillar turned their heads. A boy plucked his mother's sleeve and pointed. More and more of the people looked toward the sound of the man's voice.

They saw him walking and leaping across the wide stone floor. His arms swung freely at his sides. His feet moved in quick, light steps. He turned in a wide circle, praising God yet. His robe swayed with every motion.

Many of the people knew him. They had seen his face before, day after day, sitting by the gate Beautiful. They had walked past him that very morning. They had heard his calm voice asking for alms. And now they beheld him moving across the courtyard upon his own two feet.

He held fast unto Peter and John. He walked beside them as they moved through the courtyard. His steps were quick and light. Sometimes he walked. Sometimes he leaped. He spake words of thanks unto God without ceasing. His voice grew not quiet. It carried through the open air, past the tall stone pillars, past the others which had come up to pray.

Peter and John walked at their wonted pace. The man moved alongside them, sometimes stepping ahead, sometimes turning back. His sandals tapped against the pale courtyard stones. Every step was steady and sure.

All round about the temple courtyard, the people stayed their work. A man setting down a bundle looked up. A company near the entering in turned together. They gazed upon him. They saw plainly. This was the man from the gate Beautiful. The same man which had sat upon his mat every single day. And now he was walking and leaping and lifting up his voice unto God.

More of the people gathered. They stood in loose companies, beholding him. Some spake quietly one to another. Others simply looked, their hands still at their sides. The courtyard was filled with the sound of the man's praise and the soft murmur of the growing multitude.

The afternoon sun rested warmly upon the courtyard stones. Light touched the tops of the pillars and spread across the wide floor. A gentle wind moved through the open space, bearing the faint savour of olive wood and incense from deeper within the temple.

The man's sandals touched the ground again and again. Every step was steady and sure. He walked with Peter and John through the open temple, praising God yet with every step. His voice echoed softly off the tall stone walls, rising into the warm, bright afternoon air.
