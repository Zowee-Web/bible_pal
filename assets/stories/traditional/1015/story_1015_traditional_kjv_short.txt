Warm afternoon sunlight spread across the wide stone steps of the temple in Jerusalem. People walked up and down the steps, speaking quietly. Some bore small bundles. Others led their children by the hand. Birds alighted near the edges of the steps and pecked at crumbs. The stones were smooth and pale, worn by many feet over many years.

A man sat near the gate called Beautiful. He had been lame from his mother's womb. Every day, his friends bore him hither and laid him down gently upon a soft mat. He would ask of them that passed by if they might share with him a few coins. His voice was calm and familiar unto many that came this way. The gate behind him was tall, with bronze edges that caught the light.

Peter and John walked together toward the temple. It was about the ninth hour, even the hour of prayer. Their sandals tapped softly upon the stone. As they drew near unto the gate, the man upon the mat looked up and asked an alms of them.

Peter stayed his steps. John stayed also. Peter fastened his eyes upon him and said, "Look on us." The man turned his face toward them, and beheld them carefully.

Peter spake again. "Silver and gold have I none," he said. "But such as I have give I thee." He paused. "In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk."

Peter took him by the right hand and lifted him up. Immediately his feet and ankle bones received strength. He stood. For the first time in all his life, his legs held him steady.

He took one step. Then another. His feet pressed against the warm stone. He walked forth through the gate called Beautiful. Then he began to leap. He leaped once, twice, thrice. His arms lifted high. He began praising God aloud, his voice ringing across the open courtyard.

The people nearby turned to behold him. They saw the man walking and leaping. Many of them knew him. He was the same man which used to sit by the gate every single day. They watched him move freely across the wide stone floor of the temple.

He held fast unto Peter and John, walking beside them. His steps were quick and light. He spake words of thanks unto God without ceasing. His voice carried through the open air, past the pillars, past the others gathered for prayer.

All round about the temple courtyard, the people stayed their work. They beheld him. They saw plainly — this was the man from the Beautiful Gate. And now he walked and leaped and lifted up his voice in praise.

The afternoon sun rested warmly upon the courtyard stones. The man's sandals touched the ground again and again, steady and sure. He walked with Peter and John through the open temple, praising God yet with every step.
