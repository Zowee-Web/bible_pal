Warm afternoon sunlight spread across the wide stone steps of the temple in Jerusalem. The air smelled like dust and dried herbs. People walked up and down the steps in small groups, talking quietly to one another. Some carried bundles wrapped in cloth. Others held baskets close to their sides. A few led their children by the hand, moving slowly so small legs could keep up.

Birds landed near the bottom of the steps and pecked at crumbs left behind from the morning. Their soft cooing mixed with the sound of sandals on stone. The steps themselves were smooth and pale, worn down by many feet over many years. Wide cracks ran between some of the stones, and tiny green plants grew in the gaps. The temple walls rose high above, golden and white in the bright sun.

Near the top of the steps, a tall gate stood open. People called it the Beautiful Gate. Its edges were made of polished bronze that caught the afternoon light and glowed. The gate was wide enough for several people to walk through at once. Its frame was carved with patterns that curved and looped across the surface. Many people passed through this gate every day on their way to pray inside the temple courts.

A man sat on a soft mat near the base of this gate. He had been unable to walk since the day he was born. His legs had never carried him anywhere. Every morning, friends came to his home and lifted him carefully. They carried him through the streets of Jerusalem, past market stalls and doorways and low stone walls. They brought him here and set him down gently on his mat beside the gate.

He sat with his back resting against the warm stone wall. His mat was made of woven cloth, soft and faded from many days of use. A small clay cup sat on the ground beside him. He would call out in a calm, steady voice to the people walking past. He asked if they could share a few coins with him. Many who came this way recognized his face and his voice. He had been sitting in this same spot for a long time.

The sun moved slowly across the sky. More people began arriving at the temple. It was close to three o'clock in the afternoon, the regular hour of prayer. Families walked together. Older men leaned on wooden staffs. Women carried small jars of oil. The steps grew busier, and the sound of many voices filled the air like a low hum.

Peter and John walked side by side along the stone road that led to the temple. Their robes swayed gently as they moved. Their sandals tapped softly against the paving stones. Peter walked with long, steady steps. John kept pace beside him. They did not hurry. They moved with the crowd, heading toward the Beautiful Gate for the afternoon prayer.

As they came closer, the tall bronze gate grew larger in front of them. Sunlight reflected off its polished surface. People moved through the gate in a steady stream. Peter and John stepped around a woman carrying a basket of bread. They passed a group of children sitting on the edge of a low wall, swinging their feet.

The man on the mat near the gate looked up. He saw Peter and John approaching. He lifted his hand and spoke to them. He asked if they had something to share with him. His voice was clear and polite, the same words he said to many people each day.

Peter stopped walking. His sandals pressed flat against the stone. John stopped too, standing just beside him. Peter turned his whole body toward the man on the mat. He looked straight at him.

"Look at us," Peter said.

The man turned his face toward Peter and John. He watched them carefully. His hands rested still on his lap. His eyes moved from one face to the other.

Peter spoke again. His voice was steady and clear. "I don't have silver or gold," he said. "But what I do have, I give to you." He paused for just a moment. The people walking nearby kept moving past. A bird landed on the step behind Peter and tilted its head.

Then Peter said, "In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, get up and walk."

Peter reached down with his right hand. He took hold of the man's right hand. His fingers wrapped firmly around the man's palm. Then Peter lifted him up. The man's weight shifted. His body rose from the mat.

Right away, the man's feet and ankles became strong. His legs straightened beneath him. He stood upright on the smooth stone for the first time in his whole life. His sandals pressed flat against the ground. His knees held steady. His back was straight.

He stood still for a moment. The warm stone was solid under his feet. Then he took one step. His right foot moved forward and landed on the ground. Then his left foot followed. One step. Then another. His arms moved at his sides for balance. His mat lay empty behind him on the ground, the small clay cup still sitting beside it.

He walked forward. Each step came a little faster than the one before. His feet pressed firmly against the warm stone. He passed through the Beautiful Gate, his shoulders moving between the tall bronze edges. Sunlight poured through the opening and fell across his face and arms.

Inside the gate, the temple courtyard opened wide. Pale stone tiles stretched out in every direction. Tall columns stood in rows along the edges. People gathered in groups, some standing, some sitting on low benches. The sound of quiet conversation filled the open space.

The man kept walking. Then his steps changed. He began to leap. One jump, then another. His feet left the ground and came back down with a soft tap. He jumped again, higher this time. His arms lifted into the air above his head. His fingers spread wide.

He began praising God out loud. His voice rang across the open courtyard. The words came quickly, one after another. He thanked God again and again. His voice was strong and full. It carried past the columns, past the groups of people, past the far edges of the courtyard.

People nearby turned to look. A woman holding a water jar stopped and stared. Two men standing near a column turned their heads. A boy tugged on his mother's sleeve and pointed. More and more people looked toward the sound of the man's voice.

They saw him walking and jumping across the wide stone floor. His arms swung freely at his sides. His feet moved in quick, light steps. He turned in a wide circle, still praising God. His robe swayed with every movement.

Many of the people recognized him. They had seen his face before, day after day, sitting by the Beautiful Gate. They had walked past him that very morning. They had heard his calm voice asking for coins. And now they watched him move across the courtyard on his own two feet.

He stayed close to Peter and John. He walked beside them as they moved through the courtyard. His steps were quick and light. Sometimes he walked. Sometimes he leaped. He kept speaking words of thanks to God. His voice did not grow quiet. It carried through the open air, past the tall stone columns, past the other people who had come to pray.

Peter and John walked at their usual pace. The man moved alongside them, sometimes stepping ahead, sometimes turning back. His sandals tapped against the pale courtyard stones. Each step was steady and sure.

All around the temple courtyard, people stopped what they were doing. A man setting down a bundle looked up. A group near the entrance turned together. They stared. They could see clearly. This was the man from the Beautiful Gate. The same man who had sat on his mat every single day. And now he was walking and leaping and lifting his voice to God.

More people gathered. They stood in loose groups, watching. Some spoke quietly to one another. Others simply looked, their hands still at their sides. The courtyard filled with the sound of the man's praise and the soft murmur of the growing crowd.

The afternoon sun rested warmly on the courtyard stones. Light touched the tops of the columns and spread across the wide floor. A gentle breeze moved through the open space, carrying the faint smell of olive wood and incense from deeper inside the temple.

The man's sandals touched the ground again and again. Each step was steady and sure. He walked with Peter and John through the open temple, still praising God with every step. His voice echoed softly off the tall stone walls, rising into the warm, bright afternoon air.
