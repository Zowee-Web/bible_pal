Afternoon sunlight warmed the wide stone steps of the temple in Jerusalem. Dust from the busy road settled on sandals and bare feet. Merchants called out nearby, selling bread and figs. Birds landed on the edges of the stone walls, pecking at crumbs. People walked up and down the steps in small groups. Some carried baskets. Others held the hands of their children. The temple gate called Beautiful stood tall and wide. Its bronze surface caught the light and glowed a deep golden brown. Vines crept along the lower edges of the surrounding walls. A warm breeze carried the smell of olive oil and baked grain from the courtyards inside.

Near the bottom of the steps, a man sat on a folded cloth beside the gate. He had been carried there that morning, as he was every day, by friends who brought him from his home. His legs had never worked, not from the day he was born. He could not stand or walk. So each day, his friends set him down gently by the gate, and he sat and asked people passing by for coins. His hands rested open on his lap. His voice was calm and steady as he spoke to those who walked near. Some people dropped small coins into his hands. Others nodded and moved on. He thanked each person who stopped.

The afternoon prayer time was beginning. Many people headed up the steps toward the temple entrance. Among them walked two men named Peter and John. They moved together through the crowd, stepping around children and merchants. Their robes shifted with each step. Peter had broad hands and sun-browned skin. John walked close beside him. They were heading to the temple to pray, just as many others were doing at that hour.

As Peter and John came near the gate called Beautiful, the man sitting on the cloth looked up at them. He held out his hands and asked them for something. He spoke the same words he spoke to many people each day, asking if they might give him a coin.

Peter stopped walking. He turned and looked straight at the man. John stopped too. Peter spoke in a clear, steady voice. "Look at us," he said.

The man turned his face toward them. He watched them closely, holding out his hands.

Peter spoke again. "I don't have silver or gold," he said. "But what I do have, I give you." He paused. "In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, get up and walk."

Peter reached down. He took the man by his right hand. Peter's grip was firm and steady. He helped the man rise up from the ground. Right away, the man's feet and ankles became strong. The bones and muscles that had never held him up before now supported his whole body. His legs straightened beneath him. His ankles steadied.

The man stood. For the first time in his life, he stood on his own two feet. The stone beneath his soles was warm from the sun. He pressed down and his legs held. He shifted his weight from one foot to the other. Then he lifted one foot and set it forward. He took a step. Then another. His arms moved at his sides for balance. His steps grew quicker and more sure.

He began to walk. He walked beside Peter and John. Then he began to leap. His feet left the ground and came back down again, and his legs caught him each time. He jumped once more, higher, and landed on both feet. A wide smile spread across his face. His voice rang out across the courtyard. He praised God with loud, joyful words. His voice echoed off the stone walls and the bronze gate.

Peter and John walked through the gate called Beautiful, and the man went in with them. He walked and leaped and spoke praises with every step. His bare feet slapped against the smooth temple stones. People nearby turned to look. They watched him jump and heard his voice lifting up thanks to God.

Inside the temple courts, more people gathered. They recognized the man. They had seen him sitting by the gate day after day, year after year. They had passed him many times. Now here he was, walking on his own feet, leaping like someone who had walked his whole life. His arms stretched wide as he praised God again and again. His voice carried across the open space.

People stopped what they were doing. Women set down their baskets. Men paused mid-step. Children turned to watch. Everyone stared. The man kept walking, kept leaping, kept calling out thanks to God. His cloth still hung from his shoulder, dusty from the road. His hands, which had been held open for coins just minutes before, now clapped together and reached upward.

The temple courts filled with the sound of his voice. It mixed with the cooing of doves on the rooftops and the soft rustle of robes as people pressed closer to see. The man moved freely among them. His steps were strong and steady. He walked from one side of the courtyard to the other, then turned and walked back again.

Peter and John stood nearby. The man stayed close to them, still praising God. The afternoon light stretched long across the temple stones. Warm air carried the sound of his thankful words over the walls and out into the streets beyond.

Everyone who saw him walking and heard him giving thanks to God was amazed. They gathered around, watching the man who once sat beside the gate now moving through the temple on his own strong feet. His voice did not grow quiet. He praised God with each new step he took across those wide, sunlit stones.
