After his baptism, the Spirit led Jesus into the wilderness. He walked east, away from the river, away from the green banks and the crowds and the sound of water, into the dry hills where nothing grew. The ground was rocky and pale. The sun beat down without shade. The wind moved through the stones carrying dust and heat and silence.

He was alone. For forty days and forty nights he ate nothing. His body grew thin. His skin darkened under the relentless sun. His lips cracked. His hands rested at his sides as he sat on the bare ground or walked the empty ridges, and hunger pressed against him like a living thing.

Then the tempter came to him.

"If you are the Son of God," the devil said, "command these stones to become bread."

Jesus looked at the stones around him — flat and round, baked smooth by the sun, the color of bread crusts. His stomach ached. His body begged for food.

But he answered, "It is written: Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from the mouth of God."

Then the devil took him to the holy city and set him on the highest point of the temple. The city spread out below them — rooftops, courtyards, the smoke of cooking fires, the streets full of people going about their ordinary lives.

"If you are the Son of God," the devil said, "throw yourself down. For it is written that the angels will bear you up, so that you will not strike your foot against a stone."

Jesus said to him, "Again it is written: You shall not put the Lord your God to the test."

Then the devil took him to a very high mountain and showed him all the kingdoms of the world — spread out below like a carpet of cities and rivers and green valleys, shining in the light. All of it. Every nation. Every throne. Every glittering thing.

"All of this I will give you," the devil said, "if you will fall down and worship me."

Jesus turned to him. His voice was steady and clear.

"Away from me, Satan," he said. "For it is written: You shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve."

The devil left him. The wilderness was quiet. The wind moved through the stones. The sun was hot and the ground was dry and bare.

Then angels came and attended to him.
