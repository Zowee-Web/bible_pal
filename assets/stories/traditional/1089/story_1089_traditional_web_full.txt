After his baptism in the Jordan, the Spirit of God led Jesus into the wilderness. He walked east, away from the green banks of the river, away from the crowds and the sound of water, into the dry hills where the land turned to stone and dust and silence. The terrain was barren — rocky ridges and shallow wadis baked white by the sun. No trees grew here. No grass. Only scrub brush and thorns clinging to the cracks between the stones.

He was alone. The sky above was enormous and pale, the sun relentless. For forty days and forty nights he fasted, eating nothing. His body thinned. His skin darkened. His lips cracked in the dry heat. His hands rested at his sides as he sat on the bare ground or walked the empty ridges. Hunger pressed against him constantly — a deep, hollow ache that sharpened with each passing day.

When the forty days were finished, the tempter came to him.

"If you are the Son of God," the devil said, "command these stones to become bread."

Jesus looked at the stones around him — flat and round, baked smooth by the sun, the color and shape of bread loaves. His stomach ached. Every part of his body wanted food.

But he answered, "It is written: Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from the mouth of God."

Then the devil took him to the holy city and set him on the highest point of the temple. Below them, Jerusalem spread out in every direction — rooftops and courtyards, the smoke of cooking fires, the streets full of people going about their ordinary lives. The drop from the pinnacle was sheer and dizzying.

"If you are the Son of God," the devil said, "throw yourself down. For it is written: He will command his angels concerning you, and they will bear you up in their hands, so that you will not strike your foot against a stone."

Jesus stood on the edge of the temple, the wind pulling at his clothing. He answered, "Again it is written: You shall not put the Lord your God to the test."

Then the devil took him to a very high mountain. From its peak, the kingdoms of the world spread out below them — cities and rivers and green valleys, distant mountains touched with snow, harbors where ships rocked at anchor, fields golden with grain. All of it shimmering in the light. Every nation. Every throne. Every beautiful, glittering thing the world had to offer.

"All of this I will give you," the devil said, "if you will fall down and worship me."

Jesus turned to him. His body was weak from forty days without food, but his voice was steady and clear.

"Away from me, Satan," he said. "For it is written: You shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve."

The devil left him. The wilderness was quiet again — only the wind moving through the stones, the sun hot on the dry ground, the vast empty sky overhead.

Then angels came and attended to him. They came in the silence of that barren place, and they ministered to him there, and he was not alone.
