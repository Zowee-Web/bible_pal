After his baptism in Jordan, the Spirit of God led Jesus into the wilderness. He went eastward, away from the green banks of the river, away from the multitudes and the sound of water, into the dry hills where the land was turned to stone and dust and silence. The country was barren — rocky ridges and shallow wadis baked white by the sun. No trees grew there. No grass. Only scrub brush and thorns clinging to the cracks between the stones.

He was alone. The sky above was vast and pale, the sun unrelenting. For forty days and forty nights he fasted, eating nothing. His body grew thin. His skin was darkened. His lips were parched and cracked. His hands rested at his sides as he sat upon the bare ground or walked the empty ridges. Hunger was upon him continually — a deep, hollow ache that sharpened with each passing day.

When the forty days were accomplished, the tempter came unto him.

"If thou be the Son of God," said the devil, "command that these stones be made bread."

Jesus looked upon the stones round about him — flat and round, baked smooth by the sun, in the colour and shape of loaves. His stomach ached. Every part of his body craved food.

But he answered, "It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God."

Then the devil taketh him up into the holy city, and setteth him on a pinnacle of the temple. Below them, Jerusalem lay spread out in every direction — rooftops and courts, the smoke of fires, the streets full of people about their business. The fall from the pinnacle was sheer.

"If thou be the Son of God," the devil said, "cast thyself down: for it is written, He shall give his angels charge concerning thee: and in their hands they shall bear thee up, lest at any time thou dash thy foot against a stone."

Jesus stood upon the edge of the temple, the wind pulling at his garments. He answered, "It is written again, Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God."

Again, the devil taketh him up into an exceeding high mountain, and sheweth him all the kingdoms of the world, and the glory of them — cities and rivers and green valleys, distant mountains, harbours where ships lay at anchor, fields golden with harvest. All of it shining in the light. Every nation. Every throne. Every comely and glittering thing the world possessed.

"All these things will I give thee," the devil said, "if thou wilt fall down and worship me."

Jesus turned unto him. His body was weakened by forty days without food, but his voice was steady and clear.

"Get thee hence, Satan," he said. "For it is written, Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve."

Then the devil leaveth him. The wilderness was quiet — only the wind among the stones, the sun hot upon the dry ground, the vast empty sky above.

Then, behold, angels came and ministered unto him. They came in the silence of that barren place, and they attended him there, and he was not alone.
