And Solomon loved the Lord, walking in the statutes of David his father. He sacrificed and burnt incense in high places, for there was no house yet built unto the name of the Lord.

And the king went to Gibeon to sacrifice there, for that was the great high place. A thousand burnt offerings did Solomon offer upon that altar. The smoke rose thick and steady in the still air, spreading over the hilltop and into the valley beneath. The priests tended the fires from morning through evening. The sky darkened over Gibeon, and the stars came forth one by one until the whole firmament was filled with them.

In that night did God appear unto Solomon in a dream. And God said, "Ask what I shall give thee."

The words hung in the dream as a thing one might reach forth and touch. Ask what I shall give thee. No condition set. No bound named. The God who made the heavens and the earth stood before a young king in the quiet of his sleep and said: ask.

And Solomon answered. He spake in the dream as a man speaketh who hath weighed his words and knoweth their measure.

"Thou hast shewed unto thy servant David my father great mercy, according as he walked before thee in truth, and in righteousness, and in uprightness of heart with thee. And thou hast kept for him this great kindness, that thou hast given him a son to sit on his throne, as it is this day."

He paused. The dream held still about him, still as the air over Gibeon before the dawn. No sound moved through it. Only his voice, and the presence that hearkened.

"And now, O Lord my God, thou hast made thy servant king instead of David my father. And I am but a little child. I know not how to go out or come in. And thy servant is in the midst of thy people which thou hast chosen, a great people, that cannot be numbered nor counted for multitude."

Solomon might have stayed his tongue there. He had spoken the weight of that which he bore — a young king, unproved, set over a nation he could not number. He might have asked for any thing. Strength for battle. Gold for his treasury. Years piled upon years. The overthrow of them that might rise against his throne. Any king would have understood such asking. Any counselor would have given his approval.

He asked for none of these.

"Give therefore thy servant an understanding heart to judge thy people, that I may discern between good and bad. For who is able to judge this thy so great a people?"

The request settled into the stillness of the dream. An understanding heart. Not dominion over enemies, but clarity between right and wrong. Not wealth, but the wisdom to serve justly those he had been given to lead.

And the speech pleased the Lord, that Solomon had asked this thing. And God said unto him, "Because thou hast asked this thing, and hast not asked for thyself long life, neither hast asked riches for thyself, nor hast asked the life of thine enemies, but hast asked for thyself understanding to discern judgment, behold, I have done according to thy words. Lo, I have given thee a wise and an understanding heart, so that there was none like thee before thee, neither after thee shall any arise like unto thee."

But God was not finished.

"And I have also given thee that which thou hast not asked, both riches and honour, so that there shall not be any among the kings like unto thee all thy days. And if thou wilt walk in my ways, to keep my statutes and my commandments, as thy father David did walk, then I will lengthen thy days."

The dream ended. And Solomon awoke. The night air at Gibeon lay cool upon his skin. He could hear the wind moving through the scrub upon the hillside and the last embers of the altar shifting against their stones. He lay still for a time, the weight of that which had been given settling into him as water into dry ground.

He arose and came to Jerusalem. He stood before the ark of the covenant of the Lord and offered up burnt offerings and made peace offerings. And he made a feast to all his servants. The tables were set in the open air, and they did eat and drink together, the young king among them.
