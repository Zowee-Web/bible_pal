Solomon loved the Lord, walking in the statutes of David his father. He sacrificed and made offerings at the high places, for no house had yet been built for the name of the Lord.

The king went to Gibeon to sacrifice there, for that was the great high place. Solomon offered a thousand burnt offerings on that altar. The smoke rose thick and steady in the still air, spreading over the hilltop and drifting into the valley below. The priests tended the fires from morning through evening. The sky darkened over Gibeon, and the stars came out one by one until the whole sky was filled with them.

That night, the Lord appeared to Solomon in a dream. God said, "Ask what I shall give you."

The words hung in the dream like something you could reach out and touch. Ask what I shall give you. No condition named. No limit set. The God who made the heavens and the earth stood before a young king in the quiet of his sleep and said: ask.

Solomon answered. He spoke in the dream as a man speaks who has measured his words carefully and knows their weight.

"You have shown great and steadfast love to your servant David my father, because he walked before you in faithfulness, in righteousness, and in uprightness of heart toward you. You have kept for him this great and steadfast love, and have given him a son to sit on his throne this day."

He paused. The dream held still around him, as still as the air over Gibeon before dawn. No sound moved through it. Only his voice, and the presence that listened.

"And now, O Lord my God, you have made your servant king in place of David my father, although I am but a little child. I do not know how to go out or come in. Your servant is in the midst of your people whom you have chosen, a great people, too many to be numbered or counted for multitude."

Solomon could have stopped there. He had stated the weight of what he carried — a young king, untested, set over a nation he could not number. He could have asked for anything. Strength for battle. Gold for his treasury. Years piled on years. The destruction of those who might rise against him. Any king would have understood such requests. Any counselor would have approved them.

He asked for none of these.

"Give your servant therefore an understanding heart to judge your people, that I may discern between good and evil. For who is able to judge this your great people?"

The request settled into the silence of the dream. An understanding heart. Not power over enemies, but clarity between right and wrong. Not wealth, but the wisdom to serve justly those he had been given to lead.

It pleased the Lord that Solomon had asked this thing. God said to him, "Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, I now do according to your word. I give you a wise and discerning mind, so that none like you has been before you, and none like you shall arise after you."

But God was not finished.

"I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in my ways, keeping my statutes and my commandments, as your father David walked, then I will lengthen your days."

The dream ended. Solomon awoke. The night air at Gibeon lay cool on his skin. He could hear the wind moving through the scrub brush on the hillside and the last embers of the altar shifting against their stones. He lay still for a time, the weight of what had been given settling into him like water into dry ground.

He rose and returned to Jerusalem. He stood before the ark of the covenant of the Lord and offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings. Then he made a feast for all his servants. The tables were laid in the open air, and they ate and drank together, the young king among them.
