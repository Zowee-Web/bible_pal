Far to the east, wise men studied the night sky. They knew the movements of the stars as a shepherd knoweth his flock — each one familiar, each one in its place. Then a new star appeared. It rose where no star had been, bright and steady against the darkness.

The wise men gathered themselves together. They spake one to another concerning that which they had seen. They made preparations — loading camels with provisions, skins of water, and treasures wrapped with great care. Then they set forth upon the long road toward the west, toward the land of Judæa.

The journey was many days. They crossed dry plains where the wind lifted sand against their faces. They passed through rocky hill country where the camels picked their way along narrow paths. At night they watched the sky. In the morning they pressed onward.

They arrived in Jerusalem.

The city was thronged, full of merchants and travellers and soldiers of Rome. The wise men went through the streets asking a single question. "Where is he that is born King of the Jews?" they said. "For we have seen his star in the east, and are come to worship him."

The people stopped and hearkened. The question passed from mouth to mouth through the marketplaces and courtyards. It reached the palace.

When Herod the king had heard these things, he was troubled. The words "born King of the Jews" were not words a sitting king wished to hear from foreign visitors. And all Jerusalem was troubled with him. The unease spread through the city as smoke through a house.

Herod gathered together all the chief priests and scribes of the people. He demanded of them where Christ should be born. They stood before him in the hall and gave their answer.

"In Bethlehem of Judæa," they said. They quoted the prophet Micah: out of Bethlehem should come a Governor, that shall rule my people Israel.

Herod dismissed them. Then he called the wise men privily. The room was quiet. He sat forward and enquired of them diligently what time the star appeared. They told him. He hearkened carefully, his fingers resting upon the arm of his chair.

"Go and search diligently for the young child," Herod saith. "And when ye have found him, bring me word again, that I may come and worship him also."

The wise men departed from the palace. They passed through the gate of Jerusalem and took the road southward toward Bethlehem. It was not far — a few miles through the Judæan hills. The evening air was cool, and the road was quiet.

Then the star appeared again. The same star which they had seen in the east went before them now. It moved ahead through the darkening sky, low and steady. It stood still. It came and stood over where the young child was.

When they saw that the star had stood still, they rejoiced with exceeding great joy. They spake one to another, their voices rising. They pointed upward at the light that held its place, unmoving, above the rooftops of the small town.

They came to the house. The doorway was low. They bowed their heads and entered in. And when they were come in, they saw the young child with Mary his mother. The room was simple — clay walls, a floor of packed earth, the smell of oil from a small lamp.

The wise men knelt. They fell down before the child and worshipped him.

Then they rose and brought forward that which they had carried across the long miles. They opened their treasures one by one. Gold — heavy, gleaming in the lamplight. Frankincense — its sharp, clean scent rising straightway in the small space. Myrrh — dark resin, wrapped with care, rich and bitter.

The gifts lay spread upon the floor before the child and his mother.

That night the wise men slept. And being warned of God in a dream that they should not return to Herod, the warning was plain. They questioned it not.

In the morning they packed their camels. They cinched the saddles and checked the ropes. They took not the road north again through Jerusalem. They turned eastward and departed into their own country another way. The dust rose behind them and settled slowly as they went along the unfamiliar road, the hills of Bethlehem growing small behind them.
