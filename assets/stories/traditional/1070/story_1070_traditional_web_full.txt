Far to the east, wise men studied the night sky. They knew the movements of stars the way a shepherd knows his flock — each one familiar, each one in its place. Then a new star appeared. It rose where no star had been, bright and steady against the dark.

The wise men gathered. They spoke to one another about what they had seen. They made preparations — loading camels with provisions, water skins, and carefully wrapped treasures. Then they set out on the long road toward the west, toward the land of Judea.

The journey took many days. They crossed dry plains where the wind lifted sand against their faces. They moved through rocky hill country where the camels picked their way along narrow paths. At night they watched the sky. In the morning they pressed on.

They arrived in Jerusalem.

The city was crowded, full of merchants and travelers and Roman soldiers. The wise men moved through the streets asking a single question. "Where is he who has been born king of the Jews?" they said. "We saw his star when it rose, and we have come to worship him."

People stopped and listened. The question passed from mouth to mouth through the marketplaces and courtyards. It reached the palace.

When King Herod heard this, he was troubled. The words "born king of the Jews" were not words a sitting king wished to hear from foreign visitors. All Jerusalem was troubled with him. The unease spread through the city like smoke through a house.

Herod gathered together all the chief priests and scribes of the people. He demanded to know where the Christ was to be born. They stood before him in the hall and gave their answer.

"In Bethlehem of Judea," they said. They quoted the prophet Micah: out of Bethlehem would come a ruler who would shepherd the people of Israel.

Herod dismissed them. Then he summoned the wise men in secret. The room was quiet. He sat forward and asked them the exact time the star had appeared. They told him. He listened carefully, his fingers resting on the arm of his chair.

"Go and search diligently for the child," Herod said. "And when you have found him, bring me word, that I too may come and worship him."

The wise men left the palace. They passed through the gate of Jerusalem and took the road south toward Bethlehem. It was not far — a few miles through the Judean hills. The evening air was cool, and the road was quiet.

Then the star appeared again. The same star they had seen when it rose in the east went before them now. It moved ahead through the darkening sky, low and steady. It stopped. It stood still over one place.

When they saw that the star had stopped, they rejoiced with exceedingly great joy. They spoke to one another, their voices rising. They pointed upward at the light that held its position, unmoving, above the rooftops of the small town.

They came to the house. The doorway was low. They ducked their heads and entered. Inside, they saw the young child with Mary his mother. The room was simple — clay walls, a packed dirt floor, the smell of oil from a small lamp.

The wise men knelt. They fell down before the child and worshiped him.

Then they rose and brought forward what they had carried across the long miles. They opened their treasures one by one. Gold — heavy, gleaming in the lamplight. Frankincense — its sharp, clean scent rising immediately in the small space. Myrrh — dark resin, wrapped carefully, rich and bitter.

The gifts lay spread on the floor before the child and his mother.

That night the wise men slept. And God warned them in a dream not to return to Herod. The warning was clear. They did not question it.

In the morning, they packed their camels. They cinched the saddles and checked the ropes. They did not take the road north back through Jerusalem. They turned east and departed for their own country by another way. The dust rose behind them and settled slowly as they moved along the unfamiliar road, the hills of Bethlehem growing small behind them.
