Jesus had come up out of the country of Tyre and Sidon and traveled inland to the lake of Galilee through the country of the Decapolis — the ten cities east of the lake, where the herd of swine had run down into the water some months before. A crowd had gathered on his arrival. They brought a man to him who could not hear and could not speak clearly, and they begged Jesus to lay his hand on the man. Jesus took him aside.

Again he departed from the borders of Tyre and Sidon, and came to the sea of Galilee, through the middle of the region of Decapolis.

They brought to him one who was deaf and had an impediment in his speech. They begged him to lay his hand on him.

He took him aside from the multitude, privately, and put his fingers into his ears, and he spat, and touched his tongue.

Looking up to heaven, he sighed, and said to him,

"Ephphatha!"

that is,

"Be opened!"

Immediately his ears were opened, and the impediment of his tongue was released, and he spoke clearly.

He commanded them that they should tell no one, but the more he commanded them, so much the more widely they proclaimed it.

They were astonished beyond measure, saying, "He has done all things well. He makes even the deaf hear, and the mute speak!"

And Jesus turned and walked back toward the lake with the disciples after him. The crowd in the Decapolis stood for a moment in the place watching him go, the stunned silence of the first instant giving way at last to the shouting of the news through the streets behind him; and the man who had been deaf and dumb walked with his neighbors back toward his own house, hearing them speak and speaking back to them, in the late sun of the country east of the lake of Galilee.
