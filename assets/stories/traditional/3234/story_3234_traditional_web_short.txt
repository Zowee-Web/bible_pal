Nehemiah saw men treading the wine presses in Judah on the Sabbath. There were sheaves being brought in and donkeys being loaded. Along with the sheaves came wine, grapes, figs, and burdens of every kind, carried into Jerusalem on the Sabbath. On the day the people sold their food, Nehemiah testified against them.

Men from Tyre were living there as well. They brought fish and all kinds of goods, selling them in Jerusalem to the people of Judah on the Sabbath.

Nehemiah confronted the nobles of Judah. “What evil thing is this that you do, and profane the Sabbath day?” he asked them. He reminded the nobles of their fathers, who had done the same. Had their God not brought disaster upon them and upon this city? Now, Nehemiah charged, the nobles were bringing still more wrath upon Israel by profaning the Sabbath.

Before the Sabbath, when Jerusalem’s gates began to grow dark, Nehemiah gave his command: the doors of the city were to be shut. He also forbade their reopening until the Sabbath was over. Some of his own servants received posts at the gates, so that no burden would be brought through into Jerusalem on the Sabbath day.

The merchants and sellers of all kinds of goods camped outside Jerusalem once or twice. Nehemiah testified against them, demanding to know why they remained around the wall. If they did so again, he warned, he would lay hands on them. After that warning, the traders no longer came on the Sabbath.

Nehemiah commanded the Levites to purify themselves and come to guard the gates, so that the Sabbath day would be kept holy. He ended with a prayer to his God: “Remember me for this also, my God, and spare me according to the greatness of your loving kindness.”
