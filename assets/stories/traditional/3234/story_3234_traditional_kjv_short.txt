Nehemiah saw men at work in Judah upon the sabbath, treading the wine presses. Others brought in sheaves and loaded asses. With the sheaves came wine, grapes, figs, and burdens of every sort, all brought into Jerusalem upon the sabbath day. When the people sold their victuals, Nehemiah bore witness against them.

Among those dwelling there were men of Tyre. These brought fish and wares of every kind to sell unto the children of Judah in Jerusalem, even upon the sabbath.

Nehemiah took the matter to the nobles of Judah and contended with them. “What evil thing is this that ye do, and profane the sabbath day?” he asked. Had not their fathers done the same, and had not their God brought evil upon them and upon their city? He charged them with bringing yet more wrath upon Israel by profaning the sabbath.

As darkness began to fall upon Jerusalem’s gates before the sabbath, Nehemiah gave orders concerning the city gates: they were to be shut. He charged that they remain unopened until after the sabbath. At those entrances he stationed some of his own servants, so that no burden should be brought in on the sabbath day.

Once or twice the merchants and sellers of all kinds of wares lodged outside Jerusalem. Nehemiah challenged them about their lodging by the wall. He warned that, should they do this again, he would lay hands upon them. From that time onward, the merchants ceased coming on the sabbath.

Nehemiah then charged the Levites that they should cleanse themselves. They were to come and keep the gates, that the sabbath day might be sanctified. Having given these commands, he addressed his God in prayer: “Remember me, O my God, concerning this also, and spare me according to the greatness of thy mercy.”
