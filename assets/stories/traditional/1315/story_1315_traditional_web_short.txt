The day of Pentecost had come and gone. The Holy Spirit had been poured out. Peter had stood in the upper room and then in the courts of the temple, and he had spoken to the crowd that had gathered, and three thousand had been added to the number of the disciples on that day. The streets of Jerusalem were full of new believers — pilgrims who had come from far countries for the feast, and Jerusalem residents who had heard the word and turned. The work of the first church had begun.

They continued steadfastly in the apostles' teaching and fellowship, in the breaking of bread, and prayer.

Fear came on every soul, and many wonders and signs were done through the apostles.

All who believed were together, and had all things in common.

They sold their possessions and goods, and distributed them to all, according as anyone had need.

Day by day, continuing steadfastly with one accord in the temple, and breaking bread at home, they took their food with gladness and singleness of heart,

praising God, and having favor with all the people. The Lord added to the assembly day by day those who were being saved.

And the days went on as the days had begun to go. The bread was broken in the houses. The prayers rose in the temple courts. The new believers were gathered. The poor were fed. And so it was in Jerusalem in those days, when the church was first being built.
