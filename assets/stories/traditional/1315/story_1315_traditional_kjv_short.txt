The day of Pentecost had come and gone. The Holy Ghost had been poured out. Peter had stood in the upper room and then in the courts of the temple, and he had spoken unto the multitude that had gathered, and three thousand souls had been added unto the number of the disciples on that day. The streets of Jerusalem were full of new believers — pilgrims which had come from far countries for the feast, and dwellers in Jerusalem which had heard the word and turned. The work of the first church had begun.

And they continued stedfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers.

And fear came upon every soul: and many wonders and signs were done by the apostles.

And all that believed were together, and had all things common;

And sold their possessions and goods, and parted them to all men, as every man had need.

And they, continuing daily with one accord in the temple, and breaking bread from house to house, did eat their meat with gladness and singleness of heart,

Praising God, and having favour with all the people. And the Lord added to the church daily such as should be saved.

And the days went on as the days had begun to go. The bread was broken in the houses. The prayers rose in the temple courts. The new believers were gathered. The poor were fed. And so it was in Jerusalem in those days, when the church was first being built.
