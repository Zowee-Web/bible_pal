In the year that King Uzziah died, Isaiah came into the temple of the Lord.

The temple was large and dim. Stone columns lined the great hall, rising from the polished floor into the high ceiling where the light barely reached. The air smelled of incense — thick, layered, the residue of many offerings burned on the altar over many days. The stones underfoot were cool and worn smooth by years of sandaled feet.

Then Isaiah saw the Lord.

He was seated on a throne, high and lifted up, far above the floor of the temple. The train of his robe filled the space below, spreading out in vast folds of heavy fabric that covered the stones from wall to wall, cascading down the steps of the throne and pooling across the entire breadth of the temple.

Above the throne stood seraphim.

Each one had six wings. With two wings each covered its face. With two wings each covered its feet. With the remaining two, each one flew. They hovered above the throne, their wings moving in steady rhythm, the sound of them like wind through a narrow pass — constant and low.

One called to another. The voice filled the temple from floor to ceiling, pressing against the stone walls, vibrating in the columns.

"Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts. The whole earth is full of his glory."

The doorposts shook. The massive stone thresholds of the temple trembled in their fittings, grinding against the floor. Dust fell from the crevices between the stones overhead. The foundations themselves seemed to shift beneath the weight of the sound.

Smoke filled the temple. It rolled in from the altar, white and dense, curling between the columns and rising in thick clouds until the far walls disappeared behind it. The seraphim above moved in and out of the haze, their wings catching the faint light that still filtered through the smoke. The edges of the throne blurred. The folds of the robe on the floor became shapes in a white sea.

Isaiah cried out. His voice broke sharp against the stone and smoke.

"Woe is me! I am undone!"

He stood in the middle of the temple, small beneath the throne, surrounded by smoke. His hands were open at his sides. His face was turned upward toward the seraphim and the throne above.

"I am a man of unclean lips," he said. "And I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips — for my eyes have seen the King, the Lord of hosts."

One of the seraphim moved. It left its place above the throne and flew toward him, descending through the smoke with its wings spread wide. Two still covered its face. Two still covered its feet. The other two carried it forward, beating the heavy air and scattering the smoke in trailing wisps.

In its hand it held a live coal.

The coal glowed bright against the dimness — orange and white at its center, pulsing with heat. The seraph had taken it from the altar with tongs, lifting it from among the burning embers where the sacrifices were consumed. The heat of it pushed against the air ahead of it, and the smoke nearest the coal thinned and curled away.

The seraph reached Isaiah. It extended the coal toward his face. The glow lit the skin of his cheeks and the bridge of his nose. Then the seraph pressed the coal against his mouth.

The burning touched his lips.

"Behold," the seraph said. "This has touched your lips. Your iniquity is taken away, and your sin is purged."

The seraph withdrew the coal. The glow pulled back from Isaiah's face. The seraph rose again, climbing through the smoke on steady wings, returning to its place above the throne.

Isaiah stood where he was. The taste of fire sat on his lips. The smoke continued to fill the temple, drifting slowly between the columns, thinning near the floor and thickening near the ceiling. The train of the robe still covered the stones beneath his feet. The doorposts had stopped shaking, but the echo of the trembling still seemed to live in the walls.

Then a voice came through the smoke. It was the voice of the Lord, speaking from the throne above.

"Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?"

The smoke swirled faintly in the stillness that followed.

Isaiah opened his mouth and spoke.

"Here am I. Send me."
