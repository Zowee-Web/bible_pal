In the year that King Uzziah died, Isaiah came into the temple of the Lord.

The air inside was still. Stone columns rose on either side, tall and smooth, reaching upward into shadow. The smell of incense hung in the space between them, faint and sharp at once.

Then Isaiah saw the Lord, seated on a throne, high and lifted up. The train of his robe filled the temple, spreading across the floor in folds of heavy cloth, covering the stones from wall to wall.

Above the throne stood seraphim. Each one had six wings. With two wings each covered its face. With two each covered its feet. With the remaining two, each one flew.

One called to another. The voice shook the air.

"Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts. The whole earth is full of his glory."

The doorposts of the temple shook at the sound. The great stone thresholds trembled in their fittings. Smoke filled the temple, thick and white, rolling between the columns and rising toward the ceiling until the walls could barely be seen.

Isaiah cried out. His voice broke against the smoke and stone.

"Woe is me! I am undone! I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips — for my eyes have seen the King, the Lord of hosts."

One of the seraphim flew toward him. Its wings beat the smoky air. In its hand it held a live coal, glowing bright, taken with tongs from the altar. The heat of it pulsed against the dimness.

The seraph reached out and touched the coal to Isaiah's mouth. The burning pressed against his lips.

"Behold," the seraph said. "This has touched your lips. Your iniquity is taken away, and your sin is purged."

The coal was withdrawn. The smoke still filled the temple. The throne remained high and lifted up, and the train of the robe still covered the floor.

Then the voice of the Lord came through the smoke.

"Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?"

Isaiah stood in the temple, the taste of fire still on his lips, the smoke curling around his feet. He opened his mouth and spoke.

"Here am I. Send me."
