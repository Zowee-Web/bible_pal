In the year that king Uzziah died, Isaiah came into the temple of the Lord.

The temple was great and dim. Pillars of stone stood in rows along the great hall, rising up from the polished floor unto the high ceiling where the light scarce reached. The air was heavy with incense — thick, and layered, the residue of many offerings burned upon the altar over many days. The stones underfoot were cool, and worn smooth by the years of sandaled feet that had trodden upon them.

Then Isaiah saw the Lord.

He was seated upon a throne, high and lifted up, far above the floor of the temple. The train of his robe filled the space beneath, spreading forth in vast folds of heavy raiment that covered the stones from wall to wall, cascading down the steps of the throne and pooling across the whole breadth of the temple.

Above the throne stood the seraphim.

Each one had six wings. With twain each covered his face. With twain each covered his feet. And with twain each did fly. They hovered above the throne, their wings moving in steady measure, and the sound of them was as wind through a narrow pass — constant and low.

And one cried unto another. The voice filled the temple from floor unto ceiling, pressing against the walls of stone, and the pillars trembled therewith.

"Holy, holy, holy, is the Lord of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory."

The posts of the door did shake. The great thresholds of stone trembled in their fittings, grinding against the floor. Dust fell from the crevices between the stones overhead. The very foundations seemed to shift beneath the weight of the sound.

And the temple was filled with smoke. It rolled in from the altar, white and dense, curling betwixt the pillars and rising in thick clouds until the far walls were hid behind it. The seraphim above moved to and fro within the haze, their wings catching the faint light that yet filtered through the smoke. The edges of the throne were made dim. The folds of the robe upon the floor became as shapes in a white sea.

And Isaiah cried out. His voice brake sharp against the stone and smoke.

"Woe is me! for I am undone!"

He stood in the midst of the temple, small beneath the throne, compassed about with smoke. His hands were open at his sides. His face was turned upward toward the seraphim and the throne above.

"I am a man of unclean lips," he said, "and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips — for mine eyes have seen the King, the Lord of hosts."

Then one of the seraphim moved. It departed from its place above the throne and flew toward him, descending through the smoke with its wings spread wide. Twain still covered its face. Twain still covered its feet. And with the other twain it bare itself forward, beating the heavy air and scattering the smoke in trailing wisps.

And in its hand it held a live coal.

The coal glowed bright against the dimness — orange and white at the centre thereof, pulsing with heat. The seraph had taken it from off the altar with the tongs, lifting it from among the burning embers where the sacrifices were consumed. The heat thereof pressed against the air before it, and the smoke that was nearest the coal grew thin and curled away.

The seraph reached Isaiah. It stretched forth the coal toward his face. The glow lit the skin of his cheeks and the bridge of his nose. Then the seraph laid the coal upon his mouth.

The burning touched his lips.

"Lo," saith the seraph, "this hath touched thy lips; and thine iniquity is taken away, and thy sin purged."

The seraph withdrew the coal. The glow drew back from the face of Isaiah. The seraph rose again, ascending through the smoke upon steady wings, returning unto its place above the throne.

And Isaiah stood where he was. The taste of fire sat upon his lips. The smoke continued to fill the temple, drifting slowly betwixt the pillars, growing thin near the floor and thick near the ceiling. The train of the robe still covered the stones beneath his feet. The posts of the door had ceased their shaking, but the echo of the trembling seemed yet to dwell within the walls.

Then came a voice through the smoke. It was the voice of the Lord, speaking from the throne above.

"Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?"

The smoke swirled faintly in the stillness that followed.

And Isaiah opened his mouth and spake.

"Here am I; send me."
