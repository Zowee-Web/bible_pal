Mordecai rent his clothes in the street. He poured ashes upon his head and bound sackcloth about his body, and he went out into the midst of the city of Shushan, and he cried with a loud and a bitter cry. The people stepped aside in the doorways of their shops. The children stopped where they were and watched him pass. He came even before the king's gate. He did not pass through it. None clothed in sackcloth was permitted past that threshold, and he stood instead in the open square before it, in plain sight of every officer and runner who came and went.

In every province, whithersoever the king's commandment and his decree came, the same scene repeated itself. There was great mourning among the Jews, and fasting, and weeping, and wailing, and many lay in sackcloth and ashes upon the floors of their houses.

Within the palace, Esther's maids came unto her. Her chamberlains came after them. They told her what was happening at the gate, and the queen was exceedingly grieved. She sent raiment out unto Mordecai, fresh robes folded over a servant's arms, to clothe him and to take away his sackcloth from him. He received it not. The servant returned with the cloth still folded.

Then called Esther for Hatach, one of the king's chamberlains, whom he had appointed to attend upon her. She gave him a commandment. Go unto Mordecai. Know what it is, and why it is.

Hatach went forth. He passed through the corridors of the palace and out beyond the king's gate, into the city square where Mordecai stood. He came near. Mordecai told him of all that had happened unto him. He told him what Haman had done. He told him the exact sum of the money that Haman had promised to pay to the king's treasuries for the destruction of the Jews. He gave into Hatach's hands a copy of the writing of the decree itself, the one that was given at Shushan. He charged him: shew this unto Esther. Declare it unto her. Charge her that she should go in unto the king. To make supplication unto him. To make request before him for her people.

Hatach went back the way he had come. He came unto Esther and he told her the words of Mordecai.

She answered not at once. Then she sent him forth again, and through him she sent her answer. All the king's servants, and the people of the king's provinces, do know, that whosoever, whether man or woman, shall come unto the king into the inner court, who is not called, there is one law of his to put him to death, except such to whom the king shall hold out the golden sceptre, that he may live. I have not been called to come in unto the king these thirty days.

Hatach carried her words across the gate, and out into the square, and he told them unto Mordecai.

Mordecai stood in the ashes and gave him his answer to bring back. "Think not with thyself," he said, "that thou shalt escape in the king's house, more than all the Jews. For if thou altogether holdest thy peace at this time, then shall there enlargement and deliverance arise to the Jews from another place; but thou and thy father's house shall be destroyed: and who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?"

Hatach went back across the threshold. He spake the words into the chamber where Esther waited.

She was quiet for a long while. The words sat in the chamber with her. Without, the cry that had been the sound of the city all day was now also the sound of her own house.

She gave Hatach her last message. "Go," she said. "Gather together all the Jews that are present in Shushan, and fast ye for me, and neither eat nor drink three days, night or day: I also and my maidens will fast likewise; and so will I go in unto the king, which is not according to the law.

"And if I perish, I perish."

Hatach received her words and turned and went forth. He crossed the gate one last time. He came unto Mordecai in the square, and he gave him the message. Mordecai listened. He bowed his head. He went his way, and did according to all that Esther had commanded him.
