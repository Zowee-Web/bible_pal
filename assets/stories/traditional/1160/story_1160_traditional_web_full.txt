Mordecai tore his clothes in the street. He poured ashes over his head and bound sackcloth around his body, and he went out into the middle of the city of Susa, and he cried a loud and bitter cry. People stepped aside in the doorways of their shops. Children stopped where they were and watched him pass. He came as far as the king's gate. He did not go through it. No one clothed in sackcloth was permitted past that threshold, and he stood instead in the open square before it, in plain sight of every official and runner who came and went.

In every province where the decree had been read aloud, the same scene repeated itself. There was great mourning among the Jews, and fasting, and weeping, and wailing, and many lay in sackcloth and ashes on the floors of their houses.

Inside the palace, Esther's maids came to her. Her eunuchs came after them. They told her what was happening at the gate, and the queen was greatly grieved. She sent garments out to Mordecai, fresh robes folded over a servant's arms, to clothe him and to take the sackcloth from him. He did not receive them. The servant returned with the cloth still folded.

Then Esther called for Hatach, one of the king's eunuchs whom the king himself had appointed to attend her. She gave him a command. Go to Mordecai. Find out what this is, and why it is.

Hatach went out. He passed through the corridors of the palace and out beyond the king's gate, into the city square where Mordecai stood. He came close. Mordecai told him everything. He told him what Haman had done. He told him the exact sum of money Haman had promised to pay into the king's treasuries for the destruction of the Jews. He put into Hatach's hands a copy of the writing of the decree itself, the one that had been given out at Susa. He charged him: show this to Esther. Declare it to her. Charge her to go in to the king. To make supplication. To make request for her people.

Hatach went back the way he had come. He came to Esther and he told her the words of Mordecai.

She did not answer at once. Then she sent him out again, and through him she sent her answer. All the king's servants and the people of the king's provinces know what happens to anyone, whether man or woman, who comes to the king into the inner court without being called. There is one law for them, that they be put to death, except those to whom the king might hold out the golden scepter. I have not been called to come in to the king these thirty days.

Hatach carried her words across the gate, and out into the square, and he told them to Mordecai.

Mordecai stood in the ashes and gave him his answer to bring back. "Don't think to yourself," he said, "that you will escape in the king's house any more than all the Jews. If you remain silent now, relief and deliverance will come to the Jews from another place. But you and your father's house will perish. And who knows whether you have come to the kingdom for such a time as this."

Hatach went back across the threshold. He spoke the words into the chamber where Esther waited.

She was quiet for a long while. The words sat in the room with her. Outside, the cry that had been the sound of the city all day was now also the sound of her own house.

She gave Hatach her last message. "Go," she said. "Gather together all the Jews who are present in Susa. Fast for me. Don't eat or drink three days, night or day. I and my maidens will also fast the same way. Then I will go in to the king, which is against the law.

"And if I perish, I perish."

Hatach received her words and turned and went out. He crossed the gate one last time. He came to Mordecai in the square, and he gave him the message. Mordecai listened. He nodded. He went his way, and he did according to all that Esther had commanded him.
