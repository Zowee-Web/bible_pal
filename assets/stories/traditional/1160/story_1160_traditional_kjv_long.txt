When Mordecai perceived all that was done, he rent his clothes in the street where he stood. He took ashes from the nearest hearth and poured them upon his head, and he bound sackcloth about his body in place of the robes he had let fall, and he went out into the midst of the city of Shushan. He cried with a loud and a bitter cry as he walked. The cry rose above the rooftops. The people stepped aside in their doorways. The merchants stayed their counting and looked up. The children stilled where they were playing. A man in sackcloth and ashes was a man whose grief had broken the small order of the day, and the day made room for him.

He came even before the king's gate. He did not pass through it. None clothed in sackcloth was permitted past that threshold, and Mordecai knew it. So he stood in the open square before the gate, in plain sight of every officer and runner and merchant who came and went, and there he waited, and there he wept.

He was not alone in his grief. In every province of the kingdom, whithersoever the king's commandment and his decree came, the same scene was unfolding. There was great mourning among the Jews. There was fasting. There was weeping and wailing. Many lay in sackcloth and ashes upon the floors of their houses, and the houses themselves were silent save for that sound.

Within the palace, in the quiet of the inner chambers, Esther knew none of it as yet. The walls of the palace were thick. The voice of the city did not pass through them. Then her maids came in. Her chamberlains came after her maids. They told her what was happening at the gate, and the queen was exceedingly grieved.

Her first response was practical. She sent raiment out unto Mordecai. Fresh robes folded over the arms of a servant, robes such as a man of the court might wear, to clothe him and to take away the sackcloth from him. The servant carried them out through the gate to the square. He held them out unto Mordecai. Mordecai received them not. The servant came back with the cloth still folded as it had been.

Then called Esther for Hatach. He was one of the king's chamberlains, whom the king himself had appointed to attend upon her, and he stood ready in her presence. She gave him a commandment. Go unto Mordecai. Know what it is, and why it is.

Hatach went forth.

He passed through the inner corridors of the palace. He passed the guards at the king's gate and went out into the city square that lay before it. He came unto Mordecai where he stood among the ashes. He came near. Mordecai told him of all that had happened unto him. He told him what Haman had done. He told him the exact sum of the money that Haman had promised to pay to the king's treasuries for the destruction of the Jews. He drew out a copy of the writing of the decree itself, the one that was given at Shushan, and he placed it into Hatach's hands. Shew this unto Esther, he said. Declare it unto her. Charge her that she should go in unto the king. To make supplication unto him. To make request before him for her people.

Hatach took the writing and went back the way he had come. He passed again through the king's gate. He came unto Esther in her chamber and he told her the words of Mordecai, and he gave into her hands the copy of the decree.

She read it. She answered not at once.

Then she spake unto Hatach, and through him she sent her answer back. All the king's servants, and the people of the king's provinces, do know, that whosoever, whether man or woman, shall come unto the king into the inner court, who is not called, there is one law of his. They are put to death. There is one exception. The king may hold out the golden sceptre, and the one to whom he holdeth it out may live. I have not been called to come in unto the king these thirty days.

Hatach received her words. He turned and went forth.

He passed the guards. He crossed the gate. He came into the square and he came unto Mordecai where Mordecai still stood, and he told him what the queen had said.

Mordecai listened. He looked toward the gate. He looked away from it. Then he gave Hatach his answer to bring back.

"Think not with thyself," he said, "that thou shalt escape in the king's house, more than all the Jews. For if thou altogether holdest thy peace at this time, then shall there enlargement and deliverance arise to the Jews from another place; but thou and thy father's house shall be destroyed: and who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?"

Hatach turned again. He went back across the square. He passed the guards. He came into the palace, and he came unto the chamber where Esther waited, and he spake the words into the room.

She was quiet for a long while. The words sat in the chamber with her. She rose not. She paced not. Without her window, the cry that had been the sound of the city all day was now also the sound of her own house. The light through the lattice fell upon the floor in narrow bars. A maiden somewhere in an outer room set down a jar.

After a long time, Esther spake. Her voice was low, and Hatach leaned to hear it. Then it steadied.

"Go," she said. "Gather together all the Jews that are present in Shushan, and fast ye for me, and neither eat nor drink three days, night or day: I also and my maidens will fast likewise; and so will I go in unto the king, which is not according to the law."

She paused. Then she finished.

"And if I perish, I perish."

Hatach bowed his head. He received her words. He turned and went forth from the chamber. He crossed the gate one last time. He came into the square where Mordecai still stood among the ashes, and he gave him the message that the queen had sent.

Mordecai listened. He bowed his head. He went his way, and did according to all that Esther had commanded him.
