There is a mine for silver and a place where gold is refined. Iron is drawn from the earth, and copper is smelted from ore. A miner carries his search to the furthest boundary of darkness. Far from where people live, he opens a shaft. Forgotten by passing feet, men hang and swing below.

Bread comes from the surface of the earth, while beneath it the ground is turned as by fire. Sapphires lie in its rocks, and its dust holds gold. No bird of prey knows that hidden path. The falcon has not seen it, proud animals have not trodden it, and the fierce lion has not passed there.

Man lays his hand on flinty rock and overturns mountains by their roots. He cuts channels among the stones. His eye finds precious things; he binds streams from trickling and brings what was hidden into light.

But where will wisdom be found? Where is the place of understanding? Man does not know its price, and it is not found in the land of the living. The deep answers, “It is not in me.” The sea answers, “It is not with me.” Gold cannot buy it. Silver cannot be weighed as its price. The gold of Ophir, onyx, sapphire, glass, coral, crystal, rubies, and Ethiopian topaz cannot equal its value.

Again the question rises: where does wisdom come from? It remains hidden from every living eye and even from the birds of the sky. Destruction and Death have heard only a rumor of it.

God understands wisdom’s way and knows its place. He looks to the ends of the earth and sees beneath the whole sky. He established the force of the wind and measured the waters. When he appointed the rain and made a way for the lightning of thunder, he saw wisdom, declared it, established it, and searched it out.

To man he said, “Behold, the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom. To depart from evil is understanding.”
