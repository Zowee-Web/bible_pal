Peter came to Jesus and stood before him. The other disciples were near. Peter asked a question. "Lord, how many times shall my brother sin against me and I forgive him? Up to seven times?"

Jesus looked at Peter. "I do not say to you up to seven times," he said, "but up to seventy times seven."

Then he told them a story. He said the kingdom of heaven is like a certain king who decided to settle accounts with his servants.

When the king had begun to reckon, they brought one servant before him. The man owed ten thousand talents. The number was staggering. A man could work his whole life and not earn a fraction of it. The debt was beyond all possibility of repayment.

The servant had nothing to give. The king commanded that the man be sold, along with his wife and his children and everything he owned, so that payment could be made. The order was given in the hearing of the court. The servants standing along the walls heard it. The man's family would be taken from him. His house, his goods, his body — all of it given over.

The servant fell down before the king. His knees hit the stone floor. His forehead pressed against the ground. "Lord," he said, "have patience with me, and I will pay you all of it."

The king looked at the man on the floor. Something moved in the king's chest. He was moved with compassion. He ordered the man released. He forgave the servant the entire debt. Ten thousand talents. Every coin of it, erased. The weight of it lifted from the man's back like a hand pulling a stone off his shoulders.

The servant rose. He walked out of the king's hall. His sandals crossed the courtyard stones. He passed through the gate and into the open streets beyond the palace.

There he found one of his fellow servants. This man owed him a hundred pence. It was a real debt, but small. A working man's wages for a few months. Nothing close to ten thousand talents.

The forgiven servant seized the man. He grabbed him by the throat with both hands and pressed. His fingers tightened on the man's neck. "Pay me what you owe," he said.

The fellow servant dropped to his knees. His hands came up and gripped the other man's wrists, trying to loosen the hold on his throat. His voice came out strained and desperate. "Have patience with me," he said, "and I will pay you all of it."

The same words. The same posture on the ground. The same plea — nearly identical to the one spoken minutes before in the king's hall.

The servant would not listen. He released the man's throat and dragged him away. He had his fellow servant thrown into prison until the debt was paid in full. The cell door closed. The lock turned.

Other servants in the household saw what had happened. They watched the whole thing unfold in the street. They were deeply grieved. Some of them had been present in the king's court when the enormous debt was forgiven. They had heard the servant beg. They had seen the king's face soften. And now they had seen the same man's hands around another man's throat.

They went together to the king. They told him everything. Every detail of what the servant had done.

The king sent for the servant. The man was brought back into the same hall where he had knelt. The same stone floor. The same high walls. But the king's face had changed. The compassion was gone.

"You wicked servant," the king said. His voice carried through the room. "I forgave you all that debt because you begged me. Should you not also have had compassion on your fellow servant, the same way I had pity on you?"

The king was angry. He handed the servant over to the torturers. The man would remain with them until he paid everything that was owed. Every last coin of the original debt, restored in full. The guards took hold of his arms. They led him out of the hall.

Jesus finished the story. The disciples stood around him. The air was still.

"So also my heavenly Father will do to every one of you," Jesus said, "if you do not forgive your brother from your heart."
