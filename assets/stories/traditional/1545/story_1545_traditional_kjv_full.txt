And Peter drew nigh unto Jesus and stood before him. The other disciples were gathered close. And Peter put forth a question. "Lord, how many times shall my brother trespass against me, and I forgive him? Shall it be unto seven times?"

Jesus looked upon Peter. "I say not unto thee seven times," he answered, "but unto seventy times seven."

Then he told them a parable. He said the kingdom of heaven is likened unto a certain king who purposed to settle his accounts with his servants.

And when the king had begun to reckon, they brought before him one servant. The man was indebted ten thousand talents. The sum was past all reckoning. A man might labour the whole of his days and not earn so much as a portion of it. The debt was beyond all hope of repayment.

The servant had nothing wherewith to pay. And the king commanded that the man be sold, and his wife, and his children, and all that he possessed, that the debt might be satisfied. The decree was spoken in the hearing of the court. The servants standing along the walls heard it. The man's household would be taken from him. His dwelling, his goods, his very body — all given over.

And the servant fell down before the king. His knees struck the stone floor. His brow pressed against the ground. "Lord," he said, "have patience with me, and I shall repay thee the whole of it."

The king looked upon the man where he lay upon the floor. And something stirred within the king's breast. He was moved with deep compassion. He commanded that the man be loosed. He forgave the servant the whole of that debt. Ten thousand talents. Every coin of it, blotted out. And the weight of it was lifted from the man's back as a hand that rolleth a stone from off a man's shoulders.

And the servant rose up. He went forth from the king's hall. His sandals crossed the courtyard stones. He passed through the gate and out into the open streets beyond the palace.

And there he found one of his fellow servants. This man owed him an hundred pence. It was a true debt, yet small. A labouring man's wages for a few months. Nothing nigh unto ten thousand talents.

And the forgiven servant laid hold upon him. He seized him by the throat with both hands and pressed hard. His fingers tightened upon the man's neck. "Pay me that which thou owest," he said.

The fellow servant fell down upon his knees. His hands rose up and gripped the other man's wrists, striving to loosen the hold upon his throat. His voice came forth strained and desperate. "Have patience with me," he said, "and I shall repay thee the whole of it."

The selfsame words. The selfsame posture upon the ground. The selfsame plea — near unto the very words spoken but minutes before in the king's hall.

But the servant would not hear him. He loosed the man's throat and dragged him away. He caused his fellow servant to be cast into prison until the debt should be paid in full. The door of the cell was shut. The lock was turned.

Now other servants of that household beheld what had been done. They had watched the whole of it come to pass in the street. And they were grieved exceedingly. Certain of them had stood present in the king's court when the great debt was forgiven. They had heard the servant make his supplication. They had seen the king's countenance grow tender. And now they had seen those same hands closed about another man's throat.

And they went together unto the king. They told him all things. Every particular of what the servant had done.

Then the king sent for the servant. And the man was brought again into the selfsame hall where he had knelt. The selfsame stone floor. The selfsame high walls. But the countenance of the king was changed. The compassion was departed from it.

"Thou wicked servant," the king said. And his voice carried through all that chamber. "I forgave thee the whole of that debt, because thou didst beseech me." He paused, and the hall was silent. "Shouldest thou not also have had compassion upon thy fellow servant, even as I had pity upon thee?"

And the king was wroth. He delivered the servant over unto the tormentors. The man would abide with them until he should pay all that was owed. Every last coin of the first debt, restored in full. And the guards took hold of his arms. They led him forth from the hall.

And Jesus finished the parable. The disciples stood round about him. The air was still.

"So likewise shall my heavenly Father do also unto every one of you," Jesus said, "if ye forgive not each one his brother from the heart."
