A long time ago, in a faraway land called Babylon, there was a very powerful king named Nebuchadnezzar. He was the most powerful person in the whole world. Everyone had to do what he said.

One day, King Nebuchadnezzar decided to build a giant statue made entirely of gold. It was enormous — about ninety feet tall, which is taller than most buildings! He had it placed on a big, flat field called the Plain of Dura, where everyone could see it gleaming in the sunlight.

Then the king made a rule. He called together every important person in his kingdom — all the governors, all the judges, all the leaders from every part of the land. They came and stood in long rows on the dusty plain, looking up at the golden statue.

A man with a very loud voice shouted out the king's command: "Listen up, everyone! When you hear the music start playing — the horns, the flutes, the harps — you MUST fall down on your face and worship the golden statue. And if anyone doesn't bow down... they will be thrown into a burning fiery furnace!"

The music started. Horns blared. Flutes whistled. Harps strummed. And every single person on that plain fell flat on the ground.

Well, almost every single person.

Three young men kept standing. Their names were Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. They were Jewish — which meant they believed in the one true God. And God had told them never to worship anyone or anything else. So they stood straight up while everyone around them lay on the ground.

Some men who didn't like them went running to the king. "Your Majesty! Those three Jewish men you gave important jobs to? They won't bow down! They won't worship your statue!"

The king was FURIOUS. His face turned red with anger. He called the three men before him.

"Is this TRUE?!" he shouted. "I'm going to give you ONE more chance. When the music plays, you'd BETTER bow down. Because if you don't, you're going into the furnace. And there is NO god who can save you from me!"

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego stood there calmly. They weren't shaking. They weren't looking at each other for help. They looked right at the king and said something incredibly brave.

"O King, we don't even need to think about this. Our God is able to save us from the furnace. He CAN do it."

Then they said the most important part.

"But even if he DOESN'T save us — we still will not worship your golden statue. Ever."

Even if he doesn't.

Those are some of the bravest words ever spoken.

The king was so angry that his whole face changed. He screamed at his soldiers: "Heat the furnace SEVEN TIMES HOTTER than normal!" The strongest soldiers in the whole army grabbed Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego — they were still wearing all their regular clothes — and threw them right into the fire.

The furnace was SO incredibly hot that the soldiers who threw them in were killed just by being near the flames!

But then something happened that changed everything.

The king leaned forward on his throne and looked into the furnace. His eyes went wide. He jumped to his feet.

"Wait a minute," he said to his helpers. "Didn't we throw THREE men into the fire?"

"Yes, O King. Three men."

"Then why do I see FOUR people walking around in there?! They're not tied up! They're not hurt! And the fourth one — he looks like... like a son of the gods!"

The king ran to the opening of the furnace. Even from the doorway he could feel the incredible heat. But inside, four figures were walking around calmly, like they were taking a walk in a garden.

"Shadrach! Meshach! Abednego!" the king shouted. "Come OUT! You servants of the Most High God — come HERE!"

And they did. They walked right out of the fire, through the doorway, and onto the dusty plain.

Everyone crowded around them, looking them over from head to toe. Their hair wasn't burned. Not even a little. Their clothes weren't burned. Their skin wasn't red or hurt at all.

And here's the most amazing part: they didn't even SMELL like smoke.

King Nebuchadnezzar stood before all his officials and said something nobody expected to hear from him:

"Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego! He sent his angel and rescued his servants because they trusted in him. They were willing to give up their lives rather than worship any god except their own God."

The golden statue still stood gleaming on the plain. But three men stood beside it — alive, untouched, unburned — and everyone who saw them knew that there was a God more powerful than any king.
