King Nebuchadnezzar built a golden image on the plain of Dura, in the province of Babylon. It was enormous — ninety feet tall — and it gleamed in the sun like a pillar of fire. He set it up in the open ground where everyone could see it, and he summoned every official in his kingdom to come and stand before it.

They came. Governors and captains, judges and treasurers, counselors and rulers — all of them gathered on the plain, standing in rows before the great image. A herald cried out in a loud voice: "When you hear the sound of the horn, the flute, the harp, the lyre, and every kind of music, you shall fall down and worship the golden image. Whoever does not fall down and worship shall be thrown into a burning fiery furnace."

The music played. The people fell to the ground. Every head bowed. Every knee bent.

But three men did not bow.

Their names were Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. They were Jews, exiles from Jerusalem, appointed to serve in the king's government. They stood upright on the plain while everyone around them lay flat on the ground.

When Nebuchadnezzar heard, he was furious. He summoned the three men. "Is it true?" he demanded. "I will give you one more chance. When the music plays, fall down and worship. If you do not, you will be thrown into the furnace. And what god will be able to rescue you from my hand?"

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego answered the king. "We do not need to defend ourselves before you in this matter," they said. "If we are thrown into the furnace, the God we serve is able to deliver us. But even if he does not — we want you to know, O king, that we will not serve your gods or worship the golden image you have set up."

Nebuchadnezzar's face twisted with rage. He ordered the furnace heated seven times hotter than usual. Soldiers bound the three men and threw them into the flames. The heat was so fierce that the soldiers who carried them were killed by it.

But then the king leaped to his feet. He stared into the furnace with wide eyes. "Did we not throw three men into the fire?" he said. "I see four men walking around in the flames, unbound, unharmed. And the fourth looks like a son of the gods."

Nebuchadnezzar approached the door of the furnace. "Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego — servants of the Most High God — come out!" he called.

They walked out. The governors and captains and officials crowded around them. Their hair was not singed. Their robes were not burned. There was no smell of fire on them.

Nebuchadnezzar said, "Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, who sent his angel and delivered his servants who trusted in him."
