Long ago in the land of Babylon, King Nebuchadnezzar built a giant statue made of gold. It was really tall — about ninety feet high — and it shined so brightly in the sun that you could see it from far away. The king put it out on a big flat field called the Plain of Dura.

Then the king made a rule. A man shouted it out so everyone could hear: "When the music starts playing, everyone in the whole kingdom has to bow down and worship the golden statue. If anyone doesn't bow down, they'll be thrown into a burning hot furnace!"

The music played. Horns and flutes and harps all sounded together. And everyone fell to the ground and bowed.

Well, almost everyone.

Three young men stood up straight. Their names were Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. They were Jewish, and they believed in God — the real God — and they would not bow to any statue, no matter what.

Some people noticed and told the king. Nebuchadnezzar was furious! He brought the three men before him.

"Is this true?" he shouted. "You won't worship my statue? I'll give you ONE more chance. When the music plays again, bow down. If you don't, into the furnace you go. And no god can rescue you from me!"

The three friends looked at each other. Then they looked at the king.

"O King," they said, "we don't need to think about this. Our God CAN save us from your furnace. But even if he DOESN'T — we want you to know — we will never worship your statue."

The king was SO angry his face changed color. He ordered the furnace heated up seven times hotter than normal. Strong soldiers tied up the three men and threw them into the fire. The furnace was so hot that the soldiers who threw them in were killed by the heat!

But then something amazing happened.

The king jumped to his feet. He stared into the furnace. "Wait," he said. "Didn't we throw THREE men in there?"

"Yes, three men," his helpers said.

"Well, I see FOUR people walking around in there! They're not tied up. They're not hurt. And the fourth one — he looks like a son of the gods!"

The king ran to the opening of the furnace. "Shadrach! Meshach! Abednego!" he called. "Come out!"

And they walked right out of the fire.

Everyone crowded around them. Their hair wasn't burned. Their clothes weren't burned. They didn't even SMELL like smoke!

King Nebuchadnezzar stood in front of everyone and said, "Praise the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego! He sent his angel and saved his servants because they trusted in him!"
