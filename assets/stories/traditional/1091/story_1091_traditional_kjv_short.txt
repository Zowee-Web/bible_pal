Nebuchadnezzar the king made an image of gold upon the plain of Dura, in the province of Babylon. It was exceeding great — ninety feet in height — and it shone in the sun as a pillar of fire. He set it up in the open ground where all might behold it, and summoned every official in his kingdom to stand before it.

They came — governors and captains, judges and treasurers, counsellors and rulers — and gathered in long rows upon the plain. A herald cried aloud: "When ye hear the sound of the cornet, flute, harp, sackbut, and all kinds of musick, ye shall fall down and worship the golden image. Whoso falleth not down and worshippeth shall be cast into the midst of a burning fiery furnace."

The musick sounded. The people fell to the ground. Every head bowed. Every knee bent.

But three men bowed not.

Their names were Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. They were Jews, captives from Jerusalem, set over the affairs of the province. They stood upright upon the plain whilst all about them lay prostrate in the dust.

When Nebuchadnezzar heard thereof, he was full of fury. He summoned the three men. "Is it true?" he demanded. "I will give you one chance more. When the musick playeth, fall down and worship. If ye do not, ye shall be cast into the furnace. And who is that God that shall deliver you out of my hands?"

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego answered the king. "O Nebuchadnezzar, we are not careful to answer thee in this matter. If it be so, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace. But if not — be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up."

The king's visage was changed. He commanded the furnace heated seven times more. Soldiers bound the three men and cast them into the flames. The heat was so fierce that it slew the men who bore them.

Then the king rose in haste. He looked into the furnace. "Did not we cast three men bound into the fire?" he said. "Lo, I see four men loose, walking in the midst of the fire, and they have no hurt; and the form of the fourth is like the Son of God."

Nebuchadnezzar came near the mouth of the furnace. "Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, ye servants of the most high God — come forth!" he cried.

They came forth. Their hair was not singed. Their coats were not burned. There was no smell of fire upon them.

Nebuchadnezzar spake: "Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, who hath sent his angel, and delivered his servants that trusted in him."
