For three successive years in David’s reign, famine continued. David sought Yahweh, who answered that it was for Saul and for his bloody house, because Saul had put the Gibeonites to death.

David called the Gibeonites. They were a remnant of the Amorites, not Israelites. Israel had sworn an oath to them, but Saul had sought to kill them in his zeal for Israel and Judah.

The king asked what he could do to make atonement, that they might bless Yahweh’s inheritance. They answered that silver and gold could not settle the matter with Saul’s house, nor was it for them to put any man in Israel to death. David promised to do what they asked.

They spoke of Saul’s attempt to destroy them from Israel’s borders. They requested seven of his sons to hang before Yahweh at Gibeah of Saul, Yahweh’s chosen. “I will give them,” David said.

The king spared Jonathan’s son Mephibosheth because of the oath before Yahweh between David and Jonathan. He took Armoni and Mephibosheth, the two sons whom Rizpah daughter of Aiah had borne to Saul. He also took the five sons Merab, Saul’s daughter, had borne to Adriel son of Barzillai the Meholathite.

David delivered the seven to the Gibeonites. They hanged them on the mountain before Yahweh, and all seven fell together, at the beginning of the barley harvest.

Rizpah spread sackcloth for herself on the rock. From the beginning of harvest until water poured down from the sky, she kept birds from resting on the bodies by day and animals from them by night.

David heard what Rizpah, Saul’s concubine, had done. He went to the men of Jabesh Gilead and took the bones of Saul and Jonathan. Those men had stolen the bones from the street of Beth Shan, where the Philistines had hanged them when they killed Saul at Gilboa.

David brought those bones up, and the bones of the seven who had been hanged were also gathered. Saul and Jonathan were buried in Benjamin, at Zela, in the tomb of Saul’s father Kish. Everything the king commanded was performed. After that, God answered prayer for the land.
