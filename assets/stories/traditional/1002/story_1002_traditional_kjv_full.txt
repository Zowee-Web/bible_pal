Then was Nebuchadnezzar filled with fury, and the form of his visage was changed against Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. He commanded that they be brought before him, and they were brought — three men standing upon the stone floor of that great hall, the king seated high above them, his officers arrayed on every side.

He spoke unto them, his voice carrying through the pillared chamber. He demanded whether it was true — did they refuse to serve his gods? Did they refuse to fall down before the golden image he had set up? He gave them one chance more. When they should hear the sound of the cornet, the flute, the harp, the sackbut, the psaltery, the dulcimer, and all manner of music, they were to fall down and worship the image he had made. And if they would not, they should be cast that very hour into the midst of a burning fiery furnace.

He paused. His voice rose higher still. Who, he asked, was the god that should deliver them out of his hands?

The three stood together upon that floor. They answered the king plainly. They told him they were not careful to answer him in this matter. If it be so, they said, their God whom they served was able to deliver them from the burning fiery furnace, and he would deliver them out of the king's hand. But if not — they paused, and every man in the hall could hear the silence — but if not, let the king know that they would not serve his gods, nor worship the golden image which he had set up.

Then was Nebuchadnezzar full of fury. His face twisted against them. He commanded that the furnace be heated seven times more than it was wont to be heated. He called forth the most mighty men of his army and commanded them to bind Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. The soldiers seized them. They bound them in their coats, their hosen, their hats, and their other garments — bound them fast, just as they were — and carried them toward the mouth of the furnace.

The furnace roared. The heat poured forth in waves that bent the air above its opening. The flames leapt high, and the stone around the mouth glowed with fierce light. The king's commandment was urgent, and the furnace exceeding hot. The mighty men who carried the three up to the furnace's edge — the flame slew those soldiers. The heat took them before they could step back. Their bodies fell at the furnace's mouth.

And Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, bound fast, fell down into the midst of the burning fiery furnace.

Then Nebuchadnezzar the king rose up in haste from his seat. He turned to his counsellors, and astonishment was upon his face. He asked them — did they not cast three men bound into the fire? They answered him and said it was true, O king.

He spoke again, and his voice carried across that hall. He said he saw four men loose, walking in the midst of the fire, and they had no hurt upon them. The form of the fourth, he said, was like the Son of God.

Then Nebuchadnezzar came near to the mouth of the burning fiery furnace. The heat pressed against him. He called out with a loud voice. He called them by name — Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, ye servants of the most high God — and he commanded them to come forth.

They came forth. Out of the midst of the fire, the three men walked. Their feet crossed the threshold of that furnace and they stood again upon the stone floor.

The princes gathered close. The governors drew near. The captains and the king's counsellors crowded round and looked upon them. They examined them, every man pressing forward to see. The fire had no power upon their bodies. Not a hair of their heads was singed. Their coats were not changed. Their garments bore no mark of flame. The smell of fire had not passed upon them.

The princes turned to one another. The governors looked again. The counsellors leaned closer still. Three men stood whole and unmarked in that great hall, the furnace still roaring behind them, the bodies of the mighty soldiers still lying at its mouth. The heat still poured from the opening, bending the air, casting its fierce glow across the stone — and yet these three bore nothing of it upon their flesh, nor upon their clothing, nor upon a single thread.

Nebuchadnezzar spoke before all who were assembled. He blessed the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, who had sent his angel and delivered his servants that trusted in him. They had yielded their bodies, he declared, rather than serve or worship any god except their own God. They had changed the king's word.

Therefore, he decreed before every prince and governor and captain gathered there: every people, every nation, every language that should speak anything amiss against the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego should be cut in pieces, and their houses made a dunghill. For there was no other god, he declared, that could deliver after this sort.

The furnace still burned behind them. The three men stood in the hall, unbound, whole, and silent.
