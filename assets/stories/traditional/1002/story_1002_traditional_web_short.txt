Nebuchadnezzar's face darkened. He spoke sharply, and soldiers moved through the crowd, seizing Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. They dragged the three men forward and stood them before the king's seat.

"Is it true," Nebuchadnezzar said, his voice carrying across the open plain, "that you do not serve my gods or worship the golden image I have set up?" He leaned forward. "Now if you are ready, when you hear the sound of the horn, flute, harp, lyre, psaltery, and all kinds of music, fall down and worship the image I have made. But if you do not worship, you will be cast immediately into the middle of a burning fiery furnace." He paused. "And who is the god who will deliver you out of my hands?"

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego stood before him. Their answer came without hesitation. "Nebuchadnezzar, we have no need to answer you in this matter. If it be so, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of your hand, O king. But if not, let it be known to you, O king, that we will not serve your gods or worship the golden image which you have set up."

The king's expression changed. His mouth tightened. He ordered the furnace heated seven times hotter than it was usually heated. He commanded certain mighty men of his army to bind Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. The soldiers tied them — still wearing their coats, trousers, turbans, and other garments — and carried them toward the furnace.

Heat poured from the opening. The flames roared. The soldiers who carried the three men stepped close to the mouth of the furnace. The fire leaped outward and killed those soldiers where they stood. Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego fell down, bound, into the middle of the burning fire.

Nebuchadnezzar rose suddenly from his royal seat. "Didn't we cast three men bound into the middle of the fire?" he asked his counselors.

They answered, "True, O king."

He stared at the furnace. "I see four men loose, walking in the middle of the fire, and they are not hurt. The appearance of the fourth is like a son of the gods."

Nebuchadnezzar approached the mouth of the furnace. The heat pressed against him. He called out, "Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, you servants of the Most High God, come out and come here!"

The three men walked out of the fire. The satraps, prefects, governors, and the king's counselors gathered around them. They looked closely. The fire had not touched their bodies. Not a hair on their heads was singed. Their garments were unchanged. The smell of smoke was not on them.
