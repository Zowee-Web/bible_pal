Then was Nebuchadnezzar full of fury, and the form of his visage was changed against Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. He commanded that they be brought before him, and they were brought, standing upon the stone floor of that great hall, the king upon his seat above them.

He spoke, and his voice carried through the pillared chamber. He demanded whether it was true — did they refuse to serve his gods and bow before the golden image he had raised upon the plain of Dura? He gave them one chance more. When they heard the sound of the cornet, flute, harp, sackbut, psaltery, dulcimer, and all manner of music, they were to fall down and worship. If they would not, they would be cast that very hour into a burning fiery furnace. And he asked them plainly what god could deliver them out of his hands.

The three stood together and answered the king. They told him they were not careful to answer him in this matter. Their God whom they served was able to deliver them from the furnace and from his hand. But even if he did not, they declared before the king that they would not serve his gods nor worship the golden image he had set up.

At this, the king's face twisted with rage. He commanded that the furnace be heated seven times more than it was wont to be heated. He ordered the mightiest soldiers in his army to bind the three men. They were bound in their coats, their hosen, their hats, and their other garments, and they were cast into the midst of the burning fiery furnace. The heat of that furnace was so exceeding fierce that the flames slew the soldiers who carried them to the door.

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego fell down bound into the midst of the fire.

Then Nebuchadnezzar rose up in haste from his seat. He spoke to his counsellors and asked whether they had not cast three men bound into the fire. They answered him and confirmed it. The king spoke again, his voice altered. He said he saw four men loose, walking in the midst of the fire, unhurt. The form of the fourth, he said, was like the Son of God.

Nebuchadnezzar came near to the mouth of the furnace. Heat poured from its opening. He called out to them — servants of the most high God — and bade them come forth. Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego walked out from the midst of the fire.

The princes, governors, captains, and counsellors gathered close and looked upon them. The fire had no power upon their bodies. Not a hair of their heads was singed. Their coats were not changed. The smell of fire had not passed upon them.
