The sound of Nebuchadnezzar's voice carried through the great hall like the crack of a struck drum. He gave the order, and his servants moved quickly through the corridors. Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego were brought before him, their sandals scraping against the polished stone floor as guards positioned them in front of the throne.

Nebuchadnezzar leaned forward. "Is it true, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego? You don't serve my gods and you won't worship the golden image I've set up?" His fingers gripped the arm of his seat. He spoke again, his voice filling the wide chamber: "Now if you're ready, at the moment you hear the sound of the horn, flute, zither, lyre, harp, pipe, and all kinds of music, fall down and worship the image I've made. But if you don't worship, you'll be cast that very hour into the middle of a burning fiery furnace." He paused. "And who is the god who will deliver you out of my hands?"

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego stood before him. They answered the king: "Nebuchadnezzar, we have no need to answer you in this matter. If it be so, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of your hand, O king. But if not, let it be known to you, O king, that we will not serve your gods or worship the golden image which you have set up."

Nebuchadnezzar's face changed. The muscles along his jaw tightened. His expression toward them shifted into fury. He gave the command: heat the furnace seven times more than it was usually heated. Then he ordered certain mighty men of his army to bind Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego and cast them into the burning fiery furnace.

The soldiers moved forward. They bound the three men — still wearing their trousers, their tunics, their cloaks, and their other garments. Ropes pulled tight around their arms. The soldiers gripped them and marched them toward the furnace.

The furnace roared. Its mouth opened wide, and the heat that poured from it rippled the air above the opening. The stone around its base was scorched dark. The flames inside were visible even from a distance, white and orange, climbing and folding over themselves. The sound was a deep, steady bellow, like wind forced through a narrow canyon.

The mighty men who carried Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego pushed them toward the opening. The heat struck the soldiers as they drew close. The flames leaped out. Those men — the ones who threw the three into the furnace — were killed by the intense heat. Their bodies fell near the base of the furnace.

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego fell down, bound, into the middle of the burning fiery furnace.

Nebuchadnezzar watched from his seat. Then he rose up in haste. He turned to his counselors. "Didn't we cast three men bound into the middle of the fire?"

They answered, "True, O king."

He answered, "Look, I see four men loose, walking in the middle of the fire, and they are not hurt. The appearance of the fourth is like a son of the gods."

The king stepped closer to the mouth of the furnace. The heat pressed against his skin and clothing. He stopped at the door of the furnace and called out: "Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, you servants of the Most High God, come out! Come here!"

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego walked out of the middle of the fire. Their feet stepped from the furnace floor onto the ground outside.

The satraps gathered around them. The deputies, the governors, and the king's counselors crowded close, pressing in to see. They looked at the three men. The fire had no power over their bodies. Not a hair on their heads was singed. Their trousers were unchanged. Their cloaks hung as they had before. The smell of fire had not even come on them.

Nebuchadnezzar spoke. "Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, who has sent his angel and delivered his servants who trusted in him, and have changed the king's word, and have yielded their bodies, that they might not serve nor worship any god except their own God."

The officials stood around the three men in the open air near the furnace, which still roared behind them. Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego stood among the gathered crowd of governors and counselors, their garments whole, their skin unburned. The heat from the furnace continued to press outward, but where the three men stood, not even the faintest trace of smoke clung to their clothing.
