The prophet had spoken of Assyria coming up against the land like a flood. He had spoken of the cedars of Lebanon falling under the axe. He had spoken of the day of judgment that lay ahead. And then, in the midst of those oracles, he turned to another vision — a vision of a child to be born, of a branch growing from a stump that had seemed long dead, of a king who would rule with righteousness, of a day when the wolf and the lamb would lie down together.

And after he had seen all of that, the prophet gave the song that the people would one day sing.

In that day you will say, "I will give thanks to you, Yahweh; for though you were angry with me, your anger has turned away and you comfort me.

Behold, God is my salvation. I will trust, and will not be afraid; for Yah, Yahweh, is my strength and song; and he has become my salvation."

Therefore with joy you will draw water out of the wells of salvation.

In that day you will say, "Give thanks to Yahweh! Call on his name. Declare his doings among the peoples. Proclaim that his name is exalted!

Sing to Yahweh, for he has done excellent things! Let this be known in all the earth!

Cry aloud and shout, you inhabitant of Zion; for the Holy One of Israel is great among you!"

And the prophet set the song down, that it might be remembered. The day he had seen was not yet. But the song was already given. And so it was kept in Israel, against the day when the people would draw water with joy from the wells of salvation.
