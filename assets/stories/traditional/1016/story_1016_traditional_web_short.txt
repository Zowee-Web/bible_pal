The word of the Lord came to Abram in Haran. Leave your country. Leave your relatives. Leave your father's house. Go to the land that I will show you.

No map was given. No name for the destination. Only a promise: I will make of you a great nation. I will bless you and make your name great. You will be a blessing. I will bless those who bless you and curse the one who treats you with contempt. In you all the families of the earth will be blessed.

Abram was seventy-five years old. He had lived in Haran among people he knew — the wells, the streets, the smell of bread from the ovens near the gate.

And the Lord said: go.

So Abram went, as the Lord had spoken to him. He took Sarai his wife and Lot his brother's son. He gathered their possessions and the people they had acquired in Haran. Servants loaded the donkeys. The tents were struck and rolled and tied with rope. They set out toward the land of Canaan.

The road was long. Dust rose from their feet and from the hooves of their livestock. The sun pressed against the open ground. Sarai covered her head against the glare. Lot walked beside the cattle. Abram led the way south, through country he had never crossed before. No familiar landmark met his eye.

They came into the land of Canaan. Abram passed through it to the place of Shechem, to the oak of Moreh. The Canaanite was in the land then. These were not his people. The altars along the road were built to other gods.

The Lord appeared to Abram there. He said, "I will give this land to your offspring."

Abram built an altar to the Lord who had appeared to him. He gathered stones from the ground and stacked them one upon another. No priest attended. No congregation watched. Just a man, rough stone, and a word spoken by God in a land full of strangers.

He moved from there to the mountain east of Bethel and pitched his tent, with Bethel on the west and Ai on the east. The wind came down from the hills. There he built another altar to the Lord and called on the name of the Lord.

Then Abram journeyed on, stage by stage, moving toward the Negev. Behind him two altars stood in a land not yet his own. Ahead the road stretched into brown hills. He walked between the promise already spoken and the fulfillment not yet seen, an old man carrying nothing but the word of God and the dust of the road beneath his feet.
