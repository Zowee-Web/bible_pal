The word of the Lord came unto Abram in Haran. Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father's house, unto a land that I will shew thee.

No map was given him. No name for the place appointed. Only a promise: I will make of thee a great nation, and I will bless thee, and make thy name great. Thou shalt be a blessing. I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee. In thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.

Abram was five and seventy years old. He had dwelt in Haran among people he knew — the wells, the streets, the smell of bread from the ovens near the gate.

And the Lord said: go.

So Abram departed, as the Lord had spoken unto him. He took Sarai his wife and Lot his brother's son. He gathered all their substance and the souls they had gotten in Haran. Servants laden the asses. The tents were struck and bound with cord. They went forth to go into the land of Canaan.

The road was long. Dust rose from their feet and from the hooves of their cattle. The sun pressed upon the open ground. Sarai covered her head against the glare. Lot walked beside the herd. Abram led the way southward through country he had never crossed. No familiar mark met his eye.

They came into the land of Canaan. Abram passed through the land unto the place of Sichem, unto the plain of Moreh. The Canaanite was then in the land. These were not his people. The altars along the road were builded unto other gods.

The Lord appeared unto Abram there. He said, "Unto thy seed will I give this land."

Abram builded an altar unto the Lord, who appeared unto him. He gathered stones from the ground and set them one upon another. No priest attended. No congregation looked on. Only a man, rough stone, and a word spoken by God in a land full of strangers.

He removed from thence unto a mountain on the east of Bethel and pitched his tent, having Bethel on the west and Hai on the east. The wind came down from the hills. There he builded an altar unto the Lord and called upon the name of the Lord.

Then Abram journeyed, going on still toward the south. Behind him two altars stood in a land not yet his own. Before him the road stretched into brown hills. He walked between the promise already spoken and the fulfilment not yet seen, an old man bearing naught but the word of God and the dust of the road beneath his feet.
