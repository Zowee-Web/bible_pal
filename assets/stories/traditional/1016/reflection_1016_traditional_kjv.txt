There is something that striketh the heart in the account of a man who leaveth behind all that is known unto him upon the strength of a single word. Abram had no proof — no contract, no bearing upon a map, no surety beyond the voice that spake. He gathered his tents and walked into a land of strangers, and when he arrived, he builded altars. Not houses. Altars.

It may be that the most honest answer unto uncertainty is not to resolve it, but to mark the ground whereon one standeth, and to own that something greater than one's own understanding hath spoken. The altars Abram raised were small things — rough stones heaped in the open air. Yet they were real, and they remained after he had passed on.
