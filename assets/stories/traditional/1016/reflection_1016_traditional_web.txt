There is something striking about a person who leaves behind everything familiar on the strength of a single word. Abram had no proof — no contract, no coordinates, no guarantee beyond the voice that spoke. He packed his tents and walked into a land full of strangers, and when he arrived, he built altars. Not houses. Altars.

Sometimes the most honest response to uncertainty is not to resolve it but to mark the place where you stand and acknowledge that something larger than your understanding has spoken. The altars Abram built were small things — rough stones stacked in open air. But they were real, and they remained after he moved on.
