The word of the Lord came unto Abram in Haran. It came without warning, without prelude, without the comfort of explanation. Get thee out of thy country. Leave thy kindred. Depart from thy father's house. Go unto a land that I will shew thee.

No map was given him. No name for the place appointed. The Lord said not how far, nor how long, nor what the road should cost. He gave only a promise, layered and vast: I will make of thee a great nation. I will bless thee, and make thy name great. Thou shalt be a blessing. I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee. In thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.

Abram was five and seventy years old. He had dwelt in Haran with his household, among people he knew. The streets were familiar to him. The wells were mapped in his memory. He knew which merchants set their wares near the gate, which flocks grazed the hills east of town. His father's house was there. His kindred were there. All he had gathered or earned lay within the reach of a morning's walk.

And the Lord said: go.

So Abram departed. As the Lord had spoken unto him, he departed.

He told Sarai. He told Lot. The household began to stir. Servants took down the tents and folded them into bundles. Sarai wrapped jars of oil and grain in heavy cloth. Lot numbered the livestock — sheep, goats, the few cattle they possessed. Asses were laden with rolled leather and stacked provisions. Ropes were tried and drawn tight. The last of the stores were divided and bound.

Abram took Sarai his wife and Lot his brother's son. He gathered all their substance and the souls they had gotten in Haran. They went forth to go into the land of Canaan.

The road was dry and long. Dust rose behind the company in a low brown cloud that settled not. The sun climbed and pressed down upon the open ground. The air tasted of earth and dry grass. Sarai walked beside the foremost ass, her head covered against the glare, her sandals raising small clouds of dust with every step. Lot rode at the rear, watching the cattle stretch into a slow uneven line behind them. A servant called after a straying goat. A child set atop a bundle of tents shielded her eyes and looked ahead upon nothing but more road.

Abram led them forward. He knew not the way. He had no mark to steer by, no friend awaiting at the journey's end. He had a word from God. He walked toward it.

They came into the land of Canaan. The ground changed — rocky hillsides covered in low scrub, valleys where wild grasses bent before the wind, ridgelines of pale stone. Abram passed through the land unto the place of Sichem, unto the plain of Moreh. It was a broad tree, old and thick of trunk, standing alone upon the open ground. The Canaanite was then in the land. Their settlements stood upon the hills. Their tongue filled the marketplaces. Their altars rose beside the roads, builded unto gods Abram knew not.

This was not his country. These were not his people.

The Lord appeared unto Abram there. He said, "Unto thy seed will I give this land."

The land about him — the hills, the oak, the valleys stretching southward — all of it promised unto a future Abram could not yet behold. He had no seed. Sarai had borne no child. The promise reached past the present hour into that which did not yet exist.

Abram builded an altar unto the Lord, who appeared unto him. He gathered stones from the ground, rough and unhewn, and set them one upon another. The labour was simple. No priest attended. No congregation looked on. Only a man kneeling in the dirt of a foreign place, setting stone upon stone in answer to a voice he alone had heard.

From thence he removed. He journeyed unto a mountain on the east of Bethel and pitched his tent, having Bethel on the west and Hai on the east. The ground was hard and stony. The wind came down from the hills and pressed the tent cloth flat against its poles. At night the air grew cold, and the stars spread thick across the heavens.

There Abram builded another altar unto the Lord, and called upon the name of the Lord. A second altar. A second answer. The same God, the same promise, marked once more in stone upon foreign ground.

Then Abram journeyed, going on still toward the south. The wilderness stretched before him — brown and wide, shimmering beneath the heat. Behind him stood two altars in a land not yet his own. Before him the road offered naught that was certain. He walked between the promise already spoken and the fulfilment not yet given, an old man in a new land, bearing the word of God and the dust of a long road beneath his feet.
