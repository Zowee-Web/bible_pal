Jacob lived in Canaan and loved Joseph more than all his sons, because he was the son of his old age. He made him a coat of many colors.

His brothers saw the coat. They hated him. They could not speak peaceably to him.

Joseph dreamed a dream and told his brothers. "We were binding sheaves in the field. My sheaf arose and stood upright. Your sheaves gathered around and bowed down to my sheaf."

His brothers stared at him. "Will you indeed reign over us?" They hated him all the more for his dreams.

He dreamed again. The sun, the moon, and eleven stars bowed down to him. His father rebuked him. "Shall I and your mother and your brothers come to bow down before you?" His brothers envied him. His father kept the saying in mind.

His brothers went to feed the flock in Shechem. Jacob said to Joseph, "Go now. See whether it is well with your brothers." Joseph walked north. A man told him they had gone to Dothan. Joseph followed.

They saw him from far off. The colored coat was unmistakable against the brown hillside. "Here comes the dreamer," they said. "Let us kill him and throw him into a pit."

Reuben said, "Shed no blood. Throw him into this pit but lay no hand on him." He meant to rescue Joseph later.

When Joseph arrived, they seized him and stripped his coat. They threw him into an empty pit. No water was in it. The walls were dry stone.

They sat down to eat bread. They saw Ishmaelite traders coming from Gilead with camels bearing spices, bound for Egypt.

Judah said, "What profit if we kill our brother? Let us sell him." They drew Joseph up and sold him for twenty pieces of silver. The traders took him toward Egypt.

Reuben returned to the pit. Joseph was gone. He tore his clothes. "The boy is gone. Where shall I go?"

They killed a young goat and dipped Joseph's coat in blood. They brought it to their father. "We found this. Is it your son's coat?"

Jacob recognized it. "My son's coat. A fierce animal has devoured him. Joseph is without doubt torn in pieces." He tore his garments. He put sackcloth on his waist. He mourned many days.

All his family rose to comfort him, but he refused. "I will go down to Sheol to my son, mourning." Thus his father wept for him.
