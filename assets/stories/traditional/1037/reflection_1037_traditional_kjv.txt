The coat changed hands twice. First when the brethren stripped it from Joseph's body. Then when they dipped it in the blood of a kid and brought it unto their father. The same garment that had been a sign of love became the instrument of grief.

What Jacob mourned was not only the loss of a son. It was the loss of the particular son he had loved most visibly, most openly, in a manner that all around him could see and resent. The favouring that made the coat also made the hatred, and the hatred made the pit.

Hurt seldom arriveth from a single quarter. It layereth — the brethren's envy, Joseph's solitude, Jacob's grief that would not be comforted, Reuben's failed deliverance. Every soul in this account carrieth a wound he helped to make. The pit is empty and dry, and none cometh away whole.
