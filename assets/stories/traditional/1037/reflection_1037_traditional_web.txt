The coat changed hands twice. First when the brothers stripped it from Joseph's body. Then when they dipped it in goat's blood and brought it to their father. The same garment that had been a sign of love became the instrument of grief.

What Jacob mourned was not only the loss of a son. It was the loss of the particular son he had loved most visibly, most openly, in a way that everyone around him could see and resent. The favoritism that made the coat also made the hatred, and the hatred made the pit.

Hurt rarely arrives from a single direction. It layers — the brothers' envy, Joseph's isolation, Jacob's inconsolable grief, Reuben's failed rescue. Everyone in this story carries a wound they helped to make. The pit is empty and dry, and no one comes away whole.
