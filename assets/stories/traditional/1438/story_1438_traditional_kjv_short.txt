These be the names of the mighty men whom David had: the Tachmonite that sat in the seat, chief among the captains — the same was Adino the Eznite: he lift up his spear against eight hundred, whom he slew at one time. And after him was Eleazar the son of Dodo the Ahohite.

The Philistines came up in a great host. They gathered themselves together to battle. The men of Israel were gone away. The line was broken. Only the three mighty men remained upon the field with David.

The dust came up off the plain. The men of Israel were running, looking back at the line which they had left. The shout of the Philistines came forward.

Eleazar fled not. He arose. He set his face into the wind.

He drew his sword. He stepped into them.

The first man came. He smote. The second man came. He smote. He turned, and he smote the third. He moved through the line of them. His feet held the ground. His arm arose, and his arm came down.

The sun moved across the field. The shouting went on. He smote again. He smote again. He stayed not.

The men of Israel beheld him from the place where they had fled. They beheld the one figure in the dust, swinging, swinging, not falling back.

The Lord wrought a great victory that day.

Eleazar smote the Philistines until his hand was weary, and his hand clave unto the sword. The fingers would not open. The grip would not let go.

The men of Israel returned unto him then, only to spoil. They walked among the bodies. They spake not.

He stood in the midst of the field. His hand had not moved from the sword. The dust was settling, and his fingers would not open.
