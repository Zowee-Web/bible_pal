These are the names of the mighty men whom David had: Josheb Basshebeth, a Tahchemonite, chief of the captains — the same was Adino the Eznite, against eight hundred slain at one time. And after him was Eleazar, the son of Dodai the son of an Ahohite.

The Philistines came up in a great host. They gathered themselves together to battle. The men of Israel had gone away. The line had broken. Only the three mighty men remained on the field with David.

The dust came up off the plain. The men of Israel were running, looking back at the line they had left. The Philistine shout came forward.

Eleazar did not run. He stood up. He set his face into the wind.

He drew his sword. He stepped into them.

The first man came. He struck. The second man came. He struck. He turned, and he struck the third. He moved through the line of them. His feet held the ground. His arm rose, and his arm came down.

The sun moved across the field. The shouting went on. He struck again. He struck again. He did not stop.

The men of Israel saw him from the place where they had fled. They saw the one figure in the dust, swinging, swinging, not falling back.

Yahweh worked a great victory that day.

Eleazar struck the Philistines until his hand was weary, and his hand froze to the sword. The fingers would not open. The grip would not let go.

The men of Israel came back to him then, only to take the plunder. They walked among the bodies. They did not speak.

He stood in the middle of the field. His hand had not moved from the sword. The dust was settling, and his fingers would not open.
