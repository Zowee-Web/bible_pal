Paul’s struggle for believers he had not seen had a clear purpose: that their hearts would be encouraged, that they would be joined together in love, and that they would possess the assurance of understanding. The mystery they needed to know centered on Christ, in whom every treasure of wisdom and knowledge is hidden. Persuasive speech must not draw them away from him.

They had received Christ Jesus as Lord; now they were to continue walking in him. Like a plant taking root and a building rising on its foundation, they were to become established in the faith they had been taught and overflow with thanksgiving. Philosophy and vain deceit, following the tradition of men and the elemental spirits of the world, could rob them because they were not according to Christ.

All the fullness of deity dwells bodily in Christ, and believers are made full in him. He is head over every principality and power. Their true circumcision was not performed by human hands. They had been buried with Christ in baptism and raised with him through faith in God’s work. Though dead in trespasses, they were made alive with Christ and forgiven. The handwriting in ordinances which was against them was taken away and nailed to the cross. He stripped the principalities and the powers and triumphed over them openly.

Therefore no one was to judge them over food, drink, festivals, new moons, or Sabbaths. Such things were shadows; the body is Christ’s. Neither self-abasement nor worship of angels should rob them. Those practices came from a mind swollen without cause and failed to hold firmly to Christ the Head. From him the whole body receives its supply, is joined together, and grows with God’s growth.

Since they had died with Christ to the elemental spirits of the world, they must not submit again to commands such as “Do not handle, taste, or touch.” Things that perish through use cannot give life. Human rules may appear wise through self-imposed worship, humility, and severity toward the body, but they have no power against fleshly indulgence. Fullness, forgiveness, growth, and victory are found in Christ.
