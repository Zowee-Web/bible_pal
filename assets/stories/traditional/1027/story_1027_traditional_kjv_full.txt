Moses kept the flock. They belonged to Jethro his father in law, the priest of Midian. Every morning, Moses led the flock out to find grass and water. He bare a shepherd's staff — a long and sturdy rod for guiding the sheep and for leaning upon through the long and hot days.

On this day, Moses led the flock farther than was his wont. Past the green patches near the wells. Past the rocky hills where wild goats climbed. Even unto the backside of the desert, to a mountain called Horeb. Some called it the mountain of God, though Moses may not have known wherefore.

The sheep spread out, grazing quietly. The sun was hot. The air bore the savour of dry dust and sage. Then Moses beheld something strange.

A bush was on fire.

That of itself was not so unusual. Bushes did at times kindle in the dry heat. But this fire was unlike any other. The bush burned and burned, yet was not consumed. The flames flickered orange and gold through the branches, but the leaves stayed green. The wood did not turn to ash. The fire just kept going, steady and bright, as though the bush and the flame were one.

Moses stayed his walking. He gazed upon the bush. "I will now turn aside," he said, "and see this great sight. Wherefore is the bush not burnt?"

He took a step nearer. Then another.

When the Lord saw that he turned aside to see, God called unto him out of the midst of the bush. "Moses! Moses!"

The voice came forth from the fire itself.

"Here am I," Moses said.

"Draw not nigh hither," God said. "Put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground."

Moses pulled off his shoes quickly and set them upon the dirt. He stood barefoot upon the warm earth.

"I am the God of thy father," the voice said, "the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob."

Moses covered his face with his hands. He was afraid to look upon God. His heart beat hard against his ribs. The fire crackled softly in the bush before him.

Then God spake of something that had been coming to pass far away, in the land of Egypt. "I have surely seen the affliction of my people which are in Egypt," God said. "I have heard their cry by reason of their taskmasters. I know their sorrows."

God's people — the children of Israel — were bondmen in Egypt. They made bricks and bare heavy burdens. Their masters smote them and worked them till they could scarce stand. And God had been watching. God had been hearkening.

"I am come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians," God said, "and to bring them up out of that land unto a good land and a large, unto a land flowing with milk and honey."

A good land. A land with plenty of food and water and room to dwell. God would deliver his people and bring them thither.

Then God spake the words that made Moses tremble. "Come now therefore. I will send thee unto Pharaoh, that thou mayest bring forth my people the children of Israel out of Egypt."

Send Moses? Unto Pharaoh? Moses was a shepherd keeping another man's sheep in the midst of the wilderness. How could he walk into the palace of the mightiest king in the world and demand anything?

"Who am I," Moses said, "that I should go unto Pharaoh, and that I should bring forth the children of Israel out of Egypt?"

It was an honest question. Moses felt small and uncertain.

God's answer was short and sure. "Certainly I will be with thee."

Not a long answer. Not a list of reasons wherefore Moses was the right man. Only a promise. I will be with thee.

Then God gave Moses a token to hold onto. "When thou hast brought forth the people out of Egypt, ye shall serve God upon this mountain." This very mountain, Horeb. They should come again hither.

Moses had yet another question. "When I come unto the children of Israel and shall say unto them, 'The God of your fathers hath sent me unto you,' and they shall say to me, 'What is his name?' — what shall I say unto them?"

God answered from the burning bush. "I AM THAT I AM."

The words hung in the air, mighty and full of mystery. Then God said, "Thus shalt thou say unto the children of Israel, 'I AM hath sent me unto you.'"

God spake again. "Thus shalt thou say unto the children of Israel: 'The Lord God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, hath sent me unto you.' This is my name for ever, and this is my memorial unto all generations."

The bush yet burned. The flames yet danced. Moses stood barefoot upon the holy ground, his shoes beside him and his shepherd's staff in his hand, hearkening to the God who had seen, who had heard, and who was sending him to set a whole nation free.
