Moses was taking care of sheep for his father-in-law Jethro. The flock moved slowly through the dry grass, nibbling and bleating. Moses led them far out into the wilderness, past the usual pastures, all the way to a mountain called Horeb. Some people called it the mountain of God.

Something caught his eye. A bush was on fire. Orange flames danced through the branches. But something was strange — the bush was not burning up. The leaves did not shrivel. The branches did not turn black. The fire just kept burning, bright and steady.

"I will turn aside and see this amazing sight," Moses said. "Why isn't the bush burning up?"

When God saw that Moses had come closer to look, God called to him from the middle of the bush. "Moses! Moses!"

"Here I am," Moses answered.

"Do not come closer," God said. "Take off your sandals. The place where you are standing is holy ground."

Moses pulled off his sandals and stood barefoot on the warm dirt.

"I am the God of your father," the voice said, "the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob." Moses hid his face. He was afraid to look at God.

Then God said something that changed everything. "I have seen how my people are suffering in Egypt. I have heard them crying out. I know their pain. I have come down to rescue them and bring them to a good land, a land flowing with milk and honey."

Moses listened, his heart pounding.

"Come now," God said. "I am sending you to Pharaoh to bring my people out of Egypt."

Moses could barely believe it. "Who am I," he asked, "that I should go to Pharaoh?"

God answered simply. "I will be with you."

Then Moses asked, "When the people ask me your name, what should I tell them?"

God said, "I AM WHO I AM. Tell them, 'I AM has sent me to you.'" Then God added, "Tell them, 'The Lord, the God of your fathers — the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob — has sent me to you.' This is my name forever."
