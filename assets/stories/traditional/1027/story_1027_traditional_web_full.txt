Moses was watching sheep. They belonged to his father-in-law Jethro, a priest in the land of Midian. Every morning, Moses led the flock out to find grass and water. He carried a shepherd's staff — a long, sturdy stick for guiding the sheep and leaning on during the long, hot days.

On this day, Moses led the flock farther than usual. Past the green patches near the wells. Past the rocky hills where wild goats climbed. All the way to the far side of the wilderness, to a mountain called Horeb. Some people called it the mountain of God, though Moses may not have known why.

The sheep spread out, grazing quietly. The sun was hot. The air smelled like dry dust and sage. Then Moses saw something strange.

A bush was on fire.

That was not so unusual by itself. Bushes sometimes caught fire in the dry heat. But this fire was different. The bush burned and burned, and it was not destroyed. The flames flickered orange and gold through the branches, but the leaves stayed green. The wood did not turn to ash. The fire just kept going, steady and bright, as if the bush and the flame were one thing.

Moses stopped walking. He stared at the bush. "I will turn aside," he said, "and see this great sight. Why is the bush not burned up?"

He took a step closer. Then another.

When God saw that Moses had turned aside to look, God called to him out of the middle of the bush. "Moses! Moses!"

The voice came from the fire itself.

"Here I am," Moses said.

"Do not come any closer," God said. "Take off your sandals from your feet, for the place on which you are standing is holy ground."

Moses pulled off his sandals quickly and set them on the dirt. He stood barefoot on the warm earth.

"I am the God of your father," the voice said, "the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob."

Moses covered his face with his hands. He was afraid to look at God. His heart beat hard against his ribs. The fire crackled softly in the bush before him.

Then God spoke about something that had been happening far away, in the land of Egypt. "I have surely seen the affliction of my people who are in Egypt," God said. "I have heard their cry because of their taskmasters. I know their sufferings."

God's people — the Israelites — were slaves in Egypt. They made bricks and carried heavy loads. Their masters beat them and worked them until they could barely stand. And God had been watching. God had been listening.

"I have come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians," God said, "and to bring them up out of that land to a good and large land, a land flowing with milk and honey."

A good land. A land with plenty of food and water and room to live. God wanted to rescue his people and take them there.

Then God said the words that made Moses tremble. "Come now. I will send you to Pharaoh, that you may bring my people, the children of Israel, out of Egypt."

Send Moses? To Pharaoh? Moses was a shepherd watching someone else's sheep in the middle of nowhere. How could he walk into the palace of the most powerful king in the world and demand anything?

"Who am I," Moses said, "that I should go to Pharaoh, and that I should bring the children of Israel out of Egypt?"

It was an honest question. Moses felt small and unsure.

God's answer was short and sure. "Certainly I will be with you."

Not a long explanation. Not a list of reasons why Moses was the right person. Just a promise. I will be with you.

Then God gave Moses a sign to hold onto. "When you have brought the people out of Egypt, you shall serve God on this mountain." This very mountain, Horeb. They would come back here.

Moses had another question. "When I come to the children of Israel and say, 'The God of your fathers has sent me to you,' and they ask me, 'What is his name?' — what shall I tell them?"

God answered from the burning bush. "I AM WHO I AM."

The words hung in the air, powerful and mysterious. Then God said, "Tell the children of Israel, 'I AM has sent me to you.'"

God spoke again. "Say this to the children of Israel: 'The Lord, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you.' This is my name forever. This is how I am to be remembered for all generations."

The bush still burned. The flames still danced. Moses stood barefoot on the holy ground, his sandals beside him and his shepherd's staff in his hand, listening to the God who had seen, who had heard, and who was sending him to set a whole nation free.
