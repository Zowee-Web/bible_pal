Moses kept the flock of Jethro his father in law. The sheep moved slowly through the dry grass, nibbling and bleating. Moses led them far out into the wilderness, past the wonted pastures, even unto a mountain called Horeb. Some called it the mountain of God.

Something caught his eye. A bush was on fire. Orange flames danced through the branches. But something was strange — the bush was not consumed. The leaves did not wither. The branches did not turn black. The fire just kept burning, bright and steady.

"I will now turn aside, and see this great sight," Moses said. "Wherefore is the bush not burnt?"

When the Lord saw that he turned aside to see, God called unto him out of the midst of the bush. "Moses! Moses!"

"Here am I," Moses answered.

"Draw not nigh hither," God said. "Put off thy shoes from off thy feet. The place whereon thou standest is holy ground."

Moses pulled off his shoes and stood barefoot upon the warm earth.

"I am the God of thy father," the voice said, "the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob." Moses hid his face. He was afraid to look upon God.

Then God spake words that changed everything. "I have surely seen the affliction of my people in Egypt. I have heard their cry. I know their sorrows. I am come down to deliver them, and to bring them up unto a good land, a land flowing with milk and honey."

Moses hearkened, his heart pounding.

"Come now," God said. "I will send thee unto Pharaoh, that thou mayest bring my people out of Egypt."

Moses could scarce believe it. "Who am I," he said, "that I should go unto Pharaoh?"

God answered simply. "Certainly I will be with thee."

Then Moses said, "When the people ask me thy name, what shall I say unto them?"

God said, "I AM THAT I AM. Tell them, 'I AM hath sent me unto you.'" Then God added, "Tell them, 'The Lord God of your fathers — the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob — hath sent me unto you.' This is my name for ever."
