The Lord Jesus lifted up his voice unto the multitude, his words gentle, his countenance kind. He called unto them that laboured under heavy burdens, that were wearied with cares which bowed down their backs and troubled their souls.

"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

His gaze swept across the multitude — men and women with furrowed brows, children clinging unto the robes of their mothers, old men stooped with the weight of years and toil. His voice was borne over them, a balm more strong than the cool wind beneath the shade of the olive trees.

"Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls."

He stood before the assembly, unadorned with riches or earthly pride, his presence inviting peace. No whip was in his hand; no harsh word came forth of his lips. Only the promise of rest, only an invitation to learn of him whose path was marked with kindness. The burdens pressed upon the people — the burdens of the law and of labour, the weariness of striving — yet Jesus did offer something other.

With authority and humility he continued, "For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light."

The people hearkened. Some shifted as though a load had been lifted, leaning in to catch every word. In these sayings the Lord spake of a burden that did not break the back, a yoke that did not wound the shoulders. He called the burden "light" and the yoke "easy" — not as the world giveth, but as only he could bestow. There was no striving, only the call to come, and the open promise of rest unto troubled souls.

So Jesus spake, and the call hung in the air as a song at evening, offered freely unto all that would hear. The sunlight fell upon his countenance, and his words tarried, soft and strong, summoning the weary and the heavy laden unto himself, where rest waited — full and gentle — at the feet of the Master.
