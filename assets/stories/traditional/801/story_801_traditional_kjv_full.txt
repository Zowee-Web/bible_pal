Come near and hear the words of Jesus, as it is told in the Gospel according to Matthew. In those days Jesus went about throughout the towns and villages of Galilee, teaching in their synagogues, preaching the good tidings of the kingdom, and healing every sickness and every disease among the people. Multitudes came unto him — men and women, the old and the young, the weary labourers, and the curious-hearted seekers, some bowed down with burdens, others craving hope.

In the midst of this multitude, Jesus paused and turned his gaze upon them. His eyes beheld their weary faces and stooped shoulders. The sun shone down steadily, glinting upon the brow of every labourer that had toiled in the fields since the dawn, and the market-makers that had cried out their wares under the weight of cares. Some bare heavy baskets; others bare secret troubles unseen, yet just as weighty.

Standing there under the open sky, Jesus lifted up his voice that all might hear. The multitudes were silent — children paused in their play, elders leaned upon their staves, mothers quieted their children. The winds settled, and a holy hush descended.

He called out, his words both invitation and promise: "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

The people hearkened. Husbandmen that had laboured under the yoke of plows, fishermen that had passed weary nights casting their nets, merchants jostling in busy markets, scribes bent over their scrolls, all heard him. The weight which they bare — whether it were the toil of their hands, the trouble in their hearts, or the demands of law and custom — he beheld every burden.

Again Jesus spake, "Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls."

The word "yoke" was familiar unto every ear in Galilee. The oxen in the fields bare wooden yokes across their backs, drawing heavy plows at the husbandman's call. For many, life felt much the same — a task always at hand, a weight upon the shoulders, an expectation never satisfied.

Jesus spake of a different yoke, even his own, and offered it in the place of all other burdens. "Take my yoke upon you," said he, "and learn of me." His voice was patient, marked with the calm of one that doth not bruise the reed nor quench the smoking flax. "For I am meek and lowly in heart," he proclaimed, and the people knew by his words and by his ways that he spake in truth.

About them, with sandals dusty from the road, faces marked by the sun, they waited and beheld as Jesus did invite them not into labour, but into rest. "And ye shall find rest unto your souls," he promised.

Some clutched their staves a little tighter, feeling the ache in their bones. Others pressed their hands over anxious hearts. His promise was not only for the heaviness of the body, but for the weariness within that no sleep or food could wholly refresh — the rest, said he, should settle deep within their souls.

He ended not there. His voice rang out over the multitude, clear as water from a spring. "For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light."

The hearers cast their eyes upon him. They beheld, not a rabbi clothed in silks, but a humble man clothed in simple raiment, sandal-shod, dust upon his feet — a man that walked with them upon their roads, did eat at their tables, touched the sick, spake with the children. He had yoke and burden, as all men do, but his was not hard. His presence brought peace, his words brought ease.

Standing in the midst of them, he gave invitation unto all: the righteous and the unrighteous, the known and the forgotten. "Come unto me," said he, and the words tarried in the midday air. The light round about them grew gentler, shadows shortening as the multitudes lingered in hope.

The people, moved by these words, called to remembrance times of struggle and burdens borne alone. Here was a promise spoken plainly — that to come unto him, to hear and to follow, was to exchange the heavy weight of their lives for a yoke that brought calmness and peace, a burden that might be borne without despair.

In those moments some drew nearer, taking courage from the gentleness in his voice. Others stood and pondered. The old man leaning upon his staff found his spirit steadied; the weary mother felt a lightness enter her wearied limbs.

Thus in word and in presence Jesus extended his rest unto any that would come — an offer ringing out across the fields and villages, echoing in hearts open to hear. The promise stood for all: "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light."

So were the words spoken, and so did they rest in the hearts of them that hearkened, an invitation unending unto all the weary and heavy laden, wheresoever they might be found.
