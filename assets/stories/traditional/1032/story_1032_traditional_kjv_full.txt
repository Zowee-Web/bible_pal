And it came to pass after these things that God did tempt Abraham. He said, "Abraham!" And he said, "Behold, here I am."

And God said, "Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest, and get thee into the land of Moriah. Offer him there for a burnt offering upon one of the mountains which I shall tell thee of."

And Abraham rose up early in the morning, whilst the sky was yet dark. He saddled his ass and clave the wood for the burnt offering, laying each piece with care. He woke two of his young men and Isaac his son. They went forth unto the place which God had told him of.

The first day, the road ran southward through dry hills. Dust rose with each footfall. Isaac walked beside his father. The young men led the ass behind them, the wood shifting upon its back. None spake much. The sun passed over them, and they walked beneath it.

They made camp that night beside the road. Abraham built a small fire. Isaac lay near it and slept. Abraham sat with the flames before him and watched them pull apart and join again.

The second day was as the first. Dust upon the road. Sun above them. The hills grew steeper. Abraham's countenance gave nothing away. He walked as a man walketh who hath made his purpose and will not turn from it.

On the third day, Abraham lifted up his eyes. He saw the place afar off — a ridge set sharp against the heavens. He stayed and said unto his young men, "Abide ye here with the ass. I and the lad will go yonder and worship, and come again to you."

He took the wood of the burnt offering and laid it upon Isaac his son. The lad bore the weight without murmuring. Abraham took the fire in his hand, and the knife. And they went both of them together.

The path climbed upward. Loose stones shifted beneath their feet. The scrub grew thin. Wind came over the ridge and pressed against them. Isaac brake the silence.

"My father?"

"Here am I, my son."

"Behold the fire and the wood, but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?"

Abraham's hands tightened upon the knife. The wind moved across the hillside. He said, "My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering." So they went both of them together.

And they came to the place which God had told him of. Abraham gathered stones from the hillside. He built an altar and laid the wood in order upon it. He placed each piece as he had placed a hundred offerings before — steady, unhurried. Then he bound Isaac his son and laid him on the altar, upon the wood.

The lad lay still. The wood pressed against his back.

Abraham stretched forth his hand. He took the knife. He raised it.

And the angel of the Lord called unto him out of heaven. "Abraham! Abraham!"

And he said, "Here am I."

"Lay not thine hand upon the lad, neither do thou anything unto him. For now I know that thou fearest God, seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son, from me."

Abraham lowered the knife. He lifted up his eyes and looked. Behind him a ram was caught in a thicket by his horns. It had not been there before. Abraham went and took the ram and offered him up for a burnt offering in the stead of his son.

He loosed Isaac. The lad stood. The marks from the cord were yet upon his wrists.

And Abraham called the name of that place Jehovah-jireh. As it is said to this day, "In the mount of the Lord it shall be seen."

And the angel of the Lord called unto Abraham out of heaven a second time. He said, "By myself have I sworn, saith the Lord. For because thou hast done this thing, and hast not withheld thy son, thine only son, I will bless thee greatly. I will multiply thy seed as the stars of the heaven and as the sand which is upon the sea shore. Thy seed shall possess the gate of his enemies. And in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed, because thou hast obeyed my voice."

So Abraham returned unto his young men. They rose up and went together to Beersheba. And Abraham dwelt at Beersheba.
