After these things, God tested Abraham. He said, "Abraham!" And Abraham said, "Here I am."

God said, "Take your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and go to the land of Moriah. Offer him there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains that I will show you."

Abraham did not answer. The voice had ended, and the tent was as it had been. A lamp burned low at his feet. Sarah slept on the other side of the curtain. Through the open flap he could see the stars over the plain of Beersheba, the same stars he had been told would be the count of his children. He sat very still. He did not weep. He did not move toward the curtain to look at her. After a long while, he rose.

Abraham rose early in the morning. The sky was still dark. He went out to the donkey and saddled it, drawing the girth tight with hands that did not shake. He took up the axe and split the wood for the burnt offering, each stroke clean, stacking the pieces in a careful bundle. He did not pause between strokes. He did not look toward the tent. He woke two of his young men and his son Isaac, quietly, so as not to wake the camp. They set out before the first light, walking south, with the place God had named ahead of them and Beersheba falling behind.

The first day, the road stretched south through dry hills. Dust rose with each step. Isaac walked beside his father. The young men led the donkey behind them, the wood shifting and creaking on its back. Abraham watched the road in front of his feet. When Isaac spoke to him about a hawk overhead, he answered. When the young men laughed at something between themselves, he did not turn. The sun moved across the sky, and they walked beneath it.

They camped that night beside the road. Abraham built a small fire. Isaac ate, and lay near the flames, and slept. The young men slept beyond him. Abraham sat with the fire between his knees and watched the flames pull apart and rejoin. He did not lie down. When the fire burned low, he fed it. When the night turned cold, he drew his cloak over his shoulders and did not move.

The second day was the same. Dust on the road. Sun overhead. The hills grew steeper. Abraham's face gave nothing away. He walked as a man walks who has made his decision and will not turn from it. At midday they stopped at a well, and Abraham held the cup while Isaac drank. The boy looked up at him over the rim. Abraham took the cup back and drank in his turn. They went on.

That second night, Isaac slept with his head on his father's knee for a while before he settled by the fire. Abraham did not move his leg. He laid his palm once on the boy's hair, and then he took it away.

On the third day, Abraham lifted his eyes. He saw the place far off — a ridge rising sharp against the morning sky. He stopped on the road. The young men stopped behind him. He turned to them. "Stay here with the donkey. The boy and I will go over there. We will worship, and we will come back to you."

He lifted the wood from the donkey and laid it on Isaac's shoulders. The boy adjusted the weight without complaint. Abraham took the fire in one hand and the knife in the other. The two of them walked on together.

The path climbed. Loose stones shifted underfoot. The brush grew thin. Wind came over the ridge and pressed against them. Their shadows ran short beside their feet as the sun rose higher. Abraham's grip on the knife did not loosen. The fire pot was warm against his palm. Behind him, he could hear the boy's steady breathing under the weight of the wood. He did not look back. Isaac broke the silence.

"My father?"

"Here I am, my son."

"I see the fire and the wood. But where is the lamb for the burnt offering?"

Abraham's hands tightened on the knife. The wind moved across the hillside. A bird called once from somewhere below them and was answered by no other bird. He said, "God will provide himself the lamb for a burnt offering, my son."

The two of them walked on together.

They came to the place God had told him. Abraham set down the fire and the knife. He gathered stones from the hillside, one by one, and carried them to a level spot, and laid them in a square. He fitted them together as he had fitted a hundred altars before — steady, deliberate. When the stones were in place, he lifted the wood from Isaac's shoulders and laid it in order across the top. He arranged each piece the way the wood is arranged.

Then he bound Isaac his son. He drew the cords around the boy's wrists, and around his ankles, and his hands did not falter. The boy did not pull away. He looked at his father, and his father looked at him, and Abraham lifted him and laid him on the altar, on top of the wood.

The boy lay still. The wood pressed against his back. The ridge was quiet. The wind had dropped.

Abraham stretched out his hand. He took the knife. He raised it above his son.

The angel of the Lord called to him out of heaven. "Abraham! Abraham!"

He said, "Here I am."

"Do not lay your hand on the boy. Do not do anything to him. Now I know that you fear God, seeing that you have not withheld your son, your only son, from me."

Abraham lowered the knife. He stood with it in his hand and did not move for a long moment. His chest rose and fell. He lifted his eyes and looked. Behind him, caught in a thicket by his horns, was a ram. It had not been there before. The animal struggled once in the brush, and was held. Abraham went and took the ram. He laid it on the altar in place of his son, and he offered it up as a burnt offering. The smoke rose into the clear sky above the ridge.

He unbound Isaac. The cords fell away from the boy's wrists. The marks of the rope were still on his skin. The boy stood, and his father set a hand on his shoulder, and neither of them spoke.

Abraham called the name of that place "The Lord Will Provide." As it is said to this day, "On the Lord's mountain, it will be provided."

The angel of the Lord called to Abraham a second time out of heaven. He said, "By myself I have sworn, declares the Lord. Because you have done this thing and have not withheld your son, your only son, I will surely bless you. I will greatly multiply your offspring as the stars of the heavens and as the sand that is on the seashore. Your offspring shall possess the gate of his enemies. In your offspring shall all the nations of the earth be blessed, because you have obeyed my voice."

Abraham took up the fire pot and the knife. Isaac walked at his side, the empty cords still in his hand. They came down off the ridge together. The young men were waiting where they had been left. They rose up, and they went together to Beersheba. And Abraham lived at Beersheba, under the same stars he had been shown.
