After these things, God tested Abraham. He said, "Abraham!" And Abraham said, "Here I am."

God said, "Take your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and go to the land of Moriah. Offer him there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains that I will show you."

Abraham rose early in the morning. The sky was still dark. He saddled his donkey and split the wood for the burnt offering, stacking each piece with care. He woke two of his young men and his son Isaac. They set out for the place God had named.

The first day, the road stretched south through dry hills. Dust rose with each step. Isaac walked beside his father. The young men led the donkey behind them, the wood shifting and creaking on its back. No one spoke much. The sun moved across the sky, and they walked beneath it.

They camped that night beside the road. Abraham built a small fire. Isaac lay near it and slept. Abraham sat with the fire between his knees and watched the flames pull apart and rejoin.

The second day was the same. Dust on the road. Sun overhead. The hills grew steeper. Abraham's face gave nothing away. He walked as a man walks who has made his decision and will not turn from it.

On the third day, Abraham lifted his eyes. He saw the place far off — a ridge rising sharp against the sky. He stopped and turned to the young men. "Stay here with the donkey. The boy and I will go over there. We will worship, and we will come back to you."

He lifted the wood from the donkey and laid it on Isaac's shoulders. The boy adjusted the weight without complaint. Abraham carried the fire in one hand and the knife in the other. The two of them walked on together.

The path climbed. Loose stones shifted underfoot. The brush grew thin. Wind came over the ridge and pressed against them. Isaac broke the silence.

"My father?"

"Here I am, my son."

"I see the fire and the wood. But where is the lamb for the burnt offering?"

Abraham's hands tightened on the knife. The wind moved across the hillside. He said, "God will provide himself the lamb for a burnt offering, my son."

The two of them walked on together.

They came to the place God had told him. Abraham gathered stones from the hillside. He built an altar and laid the wood in order across the top. He arranged each piece as he had arranged a hundred offerings before — steady, deliberate. Then he bound Isaac his son and laid him on the altar, on top of the wood.

The boy lay still. The wood pressed against his back.

Abraham stretched out his hand. He took the knife. He raised it.

The angel of the Lord called to him out of heaven. "Abraham! Abraham!"

He said, "Here I am."

"Do not lay your hand on the boy. Do not do anything to him. Now I know that you fear God, seeing that you have not withheld your son, your only son, from me."

Abraham lowered the knife. He lifted his eyes and looked. Behind him, caught in a thicket by his horns, was a ram. It had not been there before. Abraham went and took the ram. He offered it up as a burnt offering instead of his son.

He unbound Isaac. The boy stood. The marks from the rope were still on his wrists.

Abraham called the name of that place "The Lord Will Provide." As it is said to this day, "On the Lord's mountain, it will be provided."

The angel of the Lord called to Abraham a second time out of heaven. He said, "By myself I have sworn, declares the Lord. Because you have done this thing and have not withheld your son, your only son, I will surely bless you. I will greatly multiply your offspring as the stars of the heavens and as the sand that is on the seashore. Your offspring shall possess the gate of his enemies. In your offspring shall all the nations of the earth be blessed, because you have obeyed my voice."

Abraham returned to his young men. They rose up and went together to Beersheba. And Abraham lived at Beersheba.
