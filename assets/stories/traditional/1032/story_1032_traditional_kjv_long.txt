And it came to pass after these things that God did tempt Abraham. He said, "Abraham!" And Abraham said, "Behold, here I am."

And God said, "Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest, and get thee into the land of Moriah. Offer him there for a burnt offering upon one of the mountains which I shall tell thee of."

Abraham answered not. The voice had ceased, and the tent was as it had been. A lamp burned low at his feet. Sarah slept on the other side of the curtain. Through the open flap he could see the stars over the plain of Beersheba, the same stars he had been told would be the number of his children. He sat very still. He wept not. He moved not toward the curtain to look upon her. After a long while, he rose.

And Abraham rose up early in the morning. The sky was yet dark. He went out unto the ass and saddled it, drawing the girth tight with hands that did not shake. He took up the axe and clave the wood for the burnt offering, each stroke clean, stacking the pieces in a careful bundle. He paused not between strokes. He looked not toward the tent. He woke two of his young men and Isaac his son, quietly, that the camp should not be wakened. They set forth before the first light, walking southward, with the place God had named before them and Beersheba falling behind.

The first day, the road ran southward through dry hills. Dust rose with each footfall. Isaac walked beside his father. The young men led the ass behind them, the wood shifting and creaking upon its back. Abraham watched the road before his feet. When Isaac spake to him of a hawk overhead, he answered. When the young men laughed at somewhat between themselves, he turned not. The sun passed across the sky, and they walked beneath it.

They made camp that night beside the road. Abraham built a small fire. Isaac ate, and lay near the flames, and slept. The young men slept beyond him. Abraham sat with the fire between his knees and watched the flames pull apart and join again. He lay not down. When the fire burned low, he fed it. When the night turned cold, he drew his cloak over his shoulders and moved not.

The second day was as the first. Dust upon the road. Sun above them. The hills grew steeper. Abraham's countenance gave nothing away. He walked as a man walketh who hath made his purpose and will not turn from it. At midday they stayed at a well, and Abraham held the cup whilst Isaac drank. The lad looked up at him over the rim. Abraham took the cup back and drank in his turn. They went on.

That second night, Isaac slept with his head upon his father's knee for a season before he settled by the fire. Abraham moved not his leg. He laid his palm once upon the lad's hair, and then he took it away.

On the third day, Abraham lifted up his eyes. He saw the place afar off — a ridge set sharp against the morning sky. He stayed upon the road. The young men stayed behind him. He turned unto them. "Abide ye here with the ass. I and the lad will go yonder and worship, and come again to you."

He took the wood from off the ass and laid it upon Isaac his son. The lad shifted the weight without murmuring. Abraham took the fire in one hand and the knife in the other. And they went both of them together.

The path climbed upward. Loose stones shifted beneath their feet. The scrub grew thin. Wind came over the ridge and pressed against them. Their shadows ran short beside their feet as the sun rose higher. Abraham's grip upon the knife loosened not. The fire pot was warm against his palm. Behind him, he could hear the lad's steady breathing under the weight of the wood. He looked not back. Isaac brake the silence.

"My father?"

"Here am I, my son."

"Behold the fire and the wood, but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?"

Abraham's hands tightened upon the knife. The wind moved across the hillside. A bird cried once from somewhere below them and was answered by no other bird. He said, "My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering."

And they went both of them together.

And they came to the place which God had told him of. Abraham set down the fire and the knife. He gathered stones from the hillside, one by one, and bare them to a level place, and laid them in a square. He fitted them together as he had fitted an hundred altars before — steady, unhurried. When the stones were in their places, he lifted the wood from off Isaac's shoulders and laid it in order across the top. He set each piece in the manner the wood is set.

Then he bound Isaac his son. He drew the cords about the lad's wrists, and about his ankles, and his hands faltered not. The lad drew not back. He looked upon his father, and his father looked upon him, and Abraham lifted him and laid him upon the altar, upon the wood.

The lad lay still. The wood pressed against his back. The ridge was quiet. The wind had dropped.

Abraham stretched forth his hand. He took the knife. He raised it above his son.

And the angel of the Lord called unto him out of heaven. "Abraham! Abraham!"

And he said, "Here am I."

"Lay not thine hand upon the lad, neither do thou anything unto him. For now I know that thou fearest God, seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son, from me."

Abraham lowered the knife. He stood with it in his hand and moved not for a long moment. His breast rose and fell. He lifted up his eyes and looked. Behind him, caught in a thicket by his horns, was a ram. It had not been there before. The beast struggled once in the brush, and was held. Abraham went and took the ram. He laid him upon the altar in the stead of his son, and he offered him up for a burnt offering. The smoke rose into the clear sky above the ridge.

He loosed Isaac. The cords fell away from the lad's wrists. The marks of the rope were yet upon his skin. The lad stood, and his father set a hand upon his shoulder, and neither of them spake.

And Abraham called the name of that place Jehovah-jireh. As it is said to this day, "In the mount of the Lord it shall be seen."

The angel of the Lord called unto Abraham a second time out of heaven. He said, "By myself have I sworn, saith the Lord. Because thou hast done this thing, and hast not withheld thy son, thine only son, I will bless thee greatly. I will multiply thy seed as the stars of the heaven and as the sand which is upon the sea shore. Thy seed shall possess the gate of his enemies. And in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed, because thou hast obeyed my voice."

Abraham took up the fire pot and the knife. Isaac walked at his side, the empty cords yet in his hand. They came down off the ridge together. The young men were waiting where they had been left. They rose up and went together to Beersheba. And Abraham dwelt at Beersheba, under the same stars he had been shown.
