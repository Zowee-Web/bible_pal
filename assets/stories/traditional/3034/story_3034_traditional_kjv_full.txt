Peter began not with the trials of his hearers, but with blessing. He blessed the God and Father of the Lord Jesus Christ. According to the abundant mercy of God, believers had been begotten again unto a lively hope. The fountain of that hope was the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead.

The hope reached unto an inheritance. Peter described it by those things which cannot befall it: it cannot corrupt, it cannot be defiled, and it fadeth not away. That inheritance is reserved in heaven for them. They who await it are themselves kept by the power of God through faith. This keeping looketh unto a salvation prepared to be revealed in the last time.

Within this promise Peter spake of great rejoicing. He also spake plainly of heaviness. For a season, if need be, the believers had been grieved through manifold temptations. Peter supplied no names, places, or histories for those trials. Neither did he declare that grief had ceased to be grief. He set the temptations beside the proving of their faith.

Gold perisheth, although it be tried with fire. Faith, Peter said, is much more precious than such gold. The trial thereof shall be found unto praise, honour, and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ. Thus the trial received not the final word; Peter directed his testimony unto that which shall be found when Christ appeareth.

Christ remained unseen by those whom Peter addressed. They had not seen him, yet they loved him. They saw him not now, yet believed in him. Peter joined that believing unto exceeding joy: joy unspeakable and full of glory. While they believed without sight, they were receiving the end of their faith, even the salvation of their souls.

Then Peter enlarged the testimony only so far as the prophets who had spoken already of this grace. The salvation announced unto believers had been the matter of diligent inquiry. Prophets had prophesied concerning the grace that should come unto them, and those same prophets enquired and searched with care.

Their search bore a particular question. The Spirit of Christ within them had signified beforehand the sufferings of Christ and the glory that should follow. The prophets sought what person the Spirit indicated, or what manner of time was shown. Peter affirmed not that they possessed a full account of every circumstance. Their inquiry remained inquiry, governed by that which the Spirit had testified beforehand.

This had been revealed unto them: their ministry in these things was not for themselves, but for those who should afterward hear the report. The former prophecy and the present proclamation belonged unto one course of testimony, yet Peter kept their offices distinct. The prophets searched and ministered; others now preached the gospel.

Those heralds declared the message with the Holy Ghost sent down from heaven. What the prophets had foretold concerning the sufferings of Christ and the glory following had now been reported in the gospel. Peter ended the movement by looking beyond mortal hearers and mortal messengers. These are things into which the angels desire to look.

The blessing therefore proceeded from the mercy of God unto the resurrection of Christ, from resurrection unto lively hope, and from hope unto an inheritance kept in heaven. It acknowledged present heaviness without inventing its form. It described faith tried and the honour awaiting the appearing of Christ. It spake of love and belief directed unto him who is unseen, and of salvation received. At the last it remembered prophets who searched, ministers who preached by the Holy Ghost, and angels who desire to look. The hope remained lively throughout, for Peter had established it upon Jesus Christ raised from the dead.
