Peter opened with blessing to the God and Father of the Lord Jesus Christ. God's great mercy had given believers new birth into a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead. That hope looked toward an inheritance that cannot decay, become defiled, or fade. It is reserved in heaven for them. Meanwhile, God's power guards them through faith for a salvation ready to be revealed in the last time.

Within that promise, Peter acknowledged both rejoicing and grief. They greatly rejoice, though for a little while, if necessary, various trials have grieved them. He did not name the trials or add circumstances to them. He described their purpose through the proving of faith. Faith is more precious than perishing gold, even when gold is tested by fire. Its proof will be found to result in praise, glory, and honor when Jesus Christ is revealed.

The believers loved Christ without having known him, and though they did not see him now, they believed in him. Their rejoicing was great, filled with a joy beyond speech and full of glory. They were receiving the result of their faith: the salvation of their souls.

Peter then looked backward to the prophets who had spoken about this grace. They had searched and inquired diligently concerning the promised salvation. The Spirit of Christ within them pointed beforehand to the sufferings of Christ and the glories that would follow. They searched for whom or what kind of time the Spirit indicated. Peter did not say that they mastered every detail. He said that they searched.

It was revealed to those prophets that their ministry served not themselves but the people who now heard the announcement. The things foretold had been proclaimed through those who preached the Good News by the Holy Spirit sent from heaven. Even angels desire to look into these things.

Thus Peter's blessing held the whole movement together: mercy, resurrection, living hope, a guarded inheritance, present trials, tested faith, unseen Christ, promised salvation, searching prophets, proclaimed Good News, and angels looking in. Hope remained living because its beginning was the resurrection, and its inheritance remained reserved with God.
