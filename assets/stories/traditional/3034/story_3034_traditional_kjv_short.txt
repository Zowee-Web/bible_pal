Peter began with blessing unto the God and Father of the Lord Jesus Christ. According to abundant mercy, God hath begotten believers again unto a lively hope by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead. That hope looked unto an inheritance incorruptible, undefiled, and unfading, reserved in heaven for them. In the meantime, the power of God keepeth them through faith unto salvation, ready to be revealed in the last time.

Within that promise Peter acknowledged both rejoicing and heaviness. Therein they greatly rejoice, though for a season, if need be, manifold temptations bring grief. He named not those trials, neither did he add circumstances unto them. He declared their purpose through the proving of faith. Faith is more precious than gold that perisheth, though gold be tried with fire. The trial of faith shall be found unto praise, honour, and glory when Jesus Christ appeareth.

The believers loved Christ whom they had not seen; and though they saw him not now, yet they believed. Their rejoicing was exceeding, with joy unspeakable and full of glory. They were receiving the end of their faith, even the salvation of their souls.

Then Peter turned unto the prophets who had spoken of this grace. They had enquired and searched diligently concerning the promised salvation. The Spirit of Christ within them signified beforehand the sufferings of Christ and the glory that should follow. They searched what person or what manner of time the Spirit did indicate. Peter said not that every particular was comprehended. He testified that they searched.

It was revealed unto those prophets that their ministry served not themselves, but the people who afterward heard the report. The things foretold had now been declared by them that preached the gospel with the Holy Ghost sent down from heaven. Even the angels desire to look into these things.

Thus Peter's blessing joined the whole movement: mercy, resurrection, lively hope, a kept inheritance, present trials, faith proved, Christ unseen, salvation promised, prophets searching, the gospel proclaimed, and angels looking in. Hope abideth lively because its beginning is the resurrection, and its inheritance remaineth reserved in heaven.
