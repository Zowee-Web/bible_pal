They laid hold upon one Simon, a Cyrenian, coming out of the country. He had not been part of the crowd. He had not been at the trial. They drew him from the road, laid the cross upon his back, and compelled him to bear it after Jesus.

The street was thronged. A great company of people followed — pressing against the walls, standing upon steps, leaning from doorways. Women among them beat their breasts and lamented. The sound carried above the noise of the soldiers and the multitude.

Jesus turned unto the women. His face was bloodied. His voice was steady. "Daughters of Jerusalem, weep not for me, but weep for yourselves, and for your children. For if they do these things in a green tree, what shall be done in the dry?"

They came unto the place which is called Calvary. The ground was bare and rocky. The sky was open above them.

There they crucified him. The soldiers drove the nails. They raised the cross. Two malefactors were crucified with him — one on the right hand, the other on the left.

Jesus spake. "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do."

The soldiers sat beneath the crosses. They parted his raiment and cast lots. The people stood beholding. The rulers derided him. "He saved others; let him save himself, if he be Christ, the chosen of God."

The soldiers also mocked him. They came unto him, offering him vinegar. "If thou be the king of the Jews, save thyself."

A superscription was set above his head: THIS IS THE KING OF THE JEWS.

One of the malefactors which were hanged railed on him. "If thou be Christ, save thyself and us."

The other turned his head and rebuked him. "Dost not thou fear God, seeing thou art in the same condemnation? And we indeed justly; for we receive the due reward of our deeds: but this man hath done nothing amiss." He looked toward Jesus. "Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom."

Jesus turned unto him. "Verily I say unto thee, today shalt thou be with me in paradise."

About the sixth hour, darkness fell over all the earth. It lasted three hours, until the ninth. The sun was darkened. The light went out of the sky. The multitude grew quiet. The mocking ceased.

The veil of the temple was rent in the midst — from the top to the bottom.

Jesus cried with a loud voice. "Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit."

Having said thus, he gave up the ghost. His head fell forward. His body hung still.

The centurion standing at the foot of the cross beheld what was done. He glorified God, saying, "Certainly this was a righteous man."

The people that had gathered began to depart. They walked back toward the city, smiting their breasts. The road that had been full of clamour was quiet now.

All his acquaintance stood afar off. The women that followed him from Galilee were there. They stood and beheld. They departed not.
