They seized a man named Simon, a Cyrenian, coming out of the countryside. He had not been part of the crowd. He had not been at the trial. They pulled him from the road, laid the cross on his back, and made him carry it behind Jesus.

The street was crowded. A great multitude of people followed — pressing against the walls, standing on steps, leaning from doorways. Women among them beat their chests and wailed. The sound carried above the noise of the soldiers and the crowd.

Jesus turned to the women. His face was bloodied. His voice was steady. "Daughters of Jerusalem, don't weep for me, but weep for yourselves and for your children. For if they do these things in the green tree, what will be done in the dry?"

They came to the place called The Skull. The ground was bare and rocky, pale dirt between the stones. The sky was open above them.

There they crucified him. The soldiers drove the nails. They raised the cross. Two criminals were crucified with him — one on his right, one on his left.

Jesus spoke. "Father, forgive them, for they don't know what they are doing."

The soldiers sat beneath the crosses. They divided his garments among themselves, casting lots to see who would get what. The people stood watching. The rulers sneered. "He saved others. Let him save himself, if this is the Christ of God, his chosen one."

The soldiers joined in. They came up to him, offered him sour wine. "If you are the King of the Jews, save yourself."

A sign was placed above his head: THIS IS THE KING OF THE JEWS.

One of the criminals hanging beside him hurled insults. "Aren't you the Christ? Save yourself and us."

The other criminal turned his head. "Don't you even fear God, seeing you are under the same condemnation? We indeed justly, for we receive the due reward for our deeds, but this man has done nothing wrong." He looked toward Jesus. "Jesus, remember me when you come into your Kingdom."

Jesus turned to him. "Assuredly I tell you, today you will be with me in Paradise."

About noon, darkness fell over the whole land. It lasted three hours. The sun failed. The light went out of the sky. The crowd grew quiet. The mocking stopped.

The veil of the temple was torn in two — from top to bottom.

Jesus cried out with a loud voice. "Father, into your hands I commit my spirit."

He breathed his last. His head fell forward. His body hung still.

The centurion standing at the foot of the cross watched. He said, "Certainly this was a righteous man."

The crowds who had gathered began to leave. They walked back toward the city, beating their breasts. The road that had been full of noise was quiet now.

All his acquaintances stood at a distance. The women who had followed him from Galilee were there. They stood and watched. They did not leave.
