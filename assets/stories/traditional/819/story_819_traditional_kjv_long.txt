Esther dwelt in the great palace of king Ahasuerus, compassed about with shining golden pillars and walls of bright royal colours. Heavy curtains of purple hung before tall windows. Servants moved with quiet feet, and the chambers bare the savour of fresh flowers. Esther was clothed in fair raiment and wore a glittering crown. Yet her heart bare a secret hope for her people, the Jews, that dwelt in many cities throughout the land. Mordecai her cousin, who had brought her up as his own daughter, dwelt without the gates of the palace. Often he sent word unto Esther, putting her in remembrance that she was not alone.

On a day, tidings swept through the palace as a cold wind. Haman, who served the king and wore costly robes, was wroth. He would have all things after his own will. Because Mordecai bowed not unto him, Haman devised a plot to hurt all the Jewish people far and near, even them whom Esther loved. With cunning words, Haman persuaded king Ahasuerus to make a new decree. The king took off his royal ring and gave Haman power to write whatsoever he would. Haman wrote a decree and sent it abroad into every province. The new decree dismayed the people. When Mordecai heard it, he rent his clothes, and put on sackcloth with ashes, and went out into the midst of the city, and cried with a loud and a bitter cry. He sat without the king's gate, weeping. Many others put on sackcloth likewise, shewing their sorrow and dread.

Esther's maidens hastened unto her and said, "Mordecai is grieved! He sitteth at the gate in sackcloth." Esther was sore troubled. She sent clean raiment unto Mordecai, but he would not put it on. So Esther sent Hatach, her servant, to inquire what ailed him. Mordecai declared all unto him. He gave a copy of the new decree unto Hatach for Esther. He besought her to go in unto the king and to make supplication for her people. Hatach brought back the words of Mordecai unto Esther.

Esther considered that which Mordecai had said. She said unto Hatach, "There is a law in the palace. None may come in unto the king except he be called. Whosoever cometh uncalled, the king may not receive him. But if the king hold out unto him the golden sceptre, then may he come in and live. I have not been called to come in unto the king these thirty days." Hatach ran to Mordecai to tell him.

Mordecai sent back wise words. "Think not with thyself that thou shalt escape in the king's house, more than all the Jews. For if thou altogether holdest thy peace at this time, then shall there enlargement and deliverance arise to the Jews from another place; but thou and thy father's house shall be destroyed: and who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?" Esther hearkened carefully unto Mordecai's words. Her hands trembled a little, yet a new boldness was growing within her.

She sent answer unto Mordecai. "Go, gather together all the Jews that are present in the city, and fast ye for me, and neither eat nor drink three days, night or day. I also and my maidens will fast likewise; and so will I go in unto the king, which is not according to the law: and if I perish, I perish." Mordecai did as Esther bade him. There was a stillness in the city, as the people fasted and prayed.

On the third day, Esther put on her royal apparel. She drew her breath deeply. The hall of the palace was long and silent, filled with the glow of the morning sun. Golden lamps shone upon red carpets. As she drew nigh, she beheld the king sitting upon his lofty throne of gold. The king was compassed about with guards arrayed in shining armour, standing perfectly still.

Esther stood just within the doorway, holding her breath. Her heart beat quickly, even as she waited, still and brave. The king lifted up his eyes. For a moment, the chamber was wholly silent. Esther neither spake nor moved. The king saw her, and she obtained favour in his sight: and the king held out to Esther the golden sceptre that was in his hand. So Esther drew near, and touched the top of the sceptre. The king spake kindly, "What wilt thou, queen Esther? It shall be even given thee to the half of the kingdom."

Esther bowed low. She answered, "If it seem good unto the king, let the king and Haman come this day unto the banquet that I have prepared for him." The king assented, and called for Haman. At the banquet, the meat was savoury, and dishes of gold did shine upon the table. The king asked again, "What is thy petition, queen Esther?"

Esther gently answered, "I pray thee, come yet to another banquet on the morrow, and let Haman come also." The king consented, and so did Haman, who went home that evening, lifted up with pride. Yet when Haman beheld Mordecai at the gate and saw that Mordecai still bowed not unto him, Haman was filled with indignation. At home, Haman gathered his friends and told them how great and honoured he was. He purposed to have Mordecai punished. Howbeit, he tarried for the banquet on the morrow.

That night, the king could not sleep, and commanded that the chronicles of his kingdom be read aloud. As they read, the king learned that Mordecai had once saved his life by warning him of a secret plot. The king asked what honour had been done unto Mordecai, and was told that nothing had been done for him. Then came Haman into the hall. The king said, "What shall be done unto the man whom the king delighteth to honour?" Haman thought the king meant himself, and answered that royal apparel and the king's horse should be brought, and a noble prince should lead such a man through the city, proclaiming, "Thus shall it be done unto the man whom the king delighteth to honour!"

The king then said, "Make haste, and do even so to Mordecai. Let nothing fail of all that thou hast spoken." So Haman must needs lead Mordecai through the city, though it grieved him sore. Afterward, Haman hasted home, troubled and dismayed. Soon the servants of the king came to bring Haman unto Esther's banquet.

The second banquet was peaceful, with the soft light of lamps. Delicate meats filled the table, and Esther sat in her place. King Ahasuerus said, "What is thy petition, queen Esther? and it shall be granted thee: and what is thy request? and it shall be performed, even to the half of the kingdom." Esther answered with care and courage, "If I have found favour in thy sight, O king, and if it please the king, let my life be given me at my petition, and my people at my request." The king said, "Who is he, and where is he, that durst presume in his heart to do so?" Esther answered, "The adversary and enemy is this wicked Haman."

The king arose in his wrath, and went into the palace garden. Haman was sore afraid. He made supplication unto Esther for his life, but soon the king returned. The king commanded that Haman be taken away. Then was the king's wrath pacified. After this, the king gave the house of Haman unto Esther. Mordecai was honoured of the king and was arrayed in royal apparel of blue and white, with a great crown of gold. The Jewish people had joy and peace throughout the kingdom. They kept a feast with gladness, for hope was come. Esther's courage and Mordecai's wisdom kept the people safe, and the care of God was shewed unto all.

In quiet moments, Esther looked forth from her window, giving thanks. She knew that courage might grow even in the most uncertain night.
