Jacob had come back to the land of Canaan with his wives and his children and his flocks. He had bought a parcel of ground at Shechem and pitched his tent there; and the city had brought violence on his household in the matter of Dinah, and Simeon and Levi had taken sword and gone in and brought back vengeance, and Jacob had said to them, "You have troubled me, to make me a stench among the inhabitants of the land." The household had stayed at Shechem in the trouble of those days. And then the word of God came to Jacob.

God said to Jacob, "Arise, go up to Bethel, and live there. Make there an altar to God, who appeared to you when you fled from the face of Esau your brother."

Then Jacob said to his household, and to all who were with him, "Put away the foreign gods that are among you, purify yourselves, change your garments.

Let us arise, and go up to Bethel. I will make there an altar to God, who answered me in the day of my distress, and was with me on the way which I went."

They gave to Jacob all the foreign gods which were in their hands, and the rings which were in their ears; and Jacob hid them under the oak which was by Shechem.

They traveled, and a terror of God was on the cities that were around them, and they didn't pursue the sons of Jacob.

So Jacob came to Luz (that is, Bethel), which is in the land of Canaan, he and all the people who were with him.

He built an altar there, and called the place El Beth El; because there God was revealed to him, when he fled from the face of his brother.

And the altar of El Beth El stood among the stones of Luz where it had been built. The oak by Shechem kept its secret — the buried gods of the household — and the cities of the country around Bethel kept the terror that had fallen on them and did not pursue Jacob's people; and Jacob renewed at the altar what had been spoken to him under the open sky on the way to Haran twenty years before. And the household of Israel stayed at Bethel.
