The road into Jericho lay thick with dust, and the press of the crowd was great upon every side. Men and women stood shoulder to shoulder along the way, for word had come that Jesus of Nazareth drew nigh unto the city. Vendors left their stalls. Children climbed upon low walls. The noise of many voices filled the narrow street.

Among the throng moved a man of small stature, Zacchaeus by name, chief among the publicans of that region and exceedingly rich. He pressed forward through the crowd, but the bodies before him stood close together, and he could not see past them. He pushed again toward the edge of the road, but taller men blocked his view entirely.

So Zacchaeus ran ahead, his sandals striking the packed earth, past the line of onlookers, past the merchants and the beggars, until he came to a sycamore tree that grew beside the road. He took hold of the lower branches and pulled himself upward, climbing until he sat among the broad leaves, his legs braced against the trunk. From there, the road lay open beneath him.

The crowd parted as Jesus came walking through. Dust rose from his steps. The voices around him grew louder, calling out, reaching. Then Jesus stopped beneath the sycamore tree and lifted his face upward.

He spoke directly to the man in the branches. He called him by name — Zacchaeus — and bade him come down with haste, for he must abide at his house that day.

Zacchaeus came down from the tree quickly, his hands gripping the bark as he lowered himself branch by branch. His feet touched the ground, and he received Jesus with visible gladness, standing before him in the open road.

The crowd murmured. Voices passed from mouth to mouth — that Jesus had gone to be guest with a man who was a sinner. The words carried sharp and plain through the warm air.

Then Zacchaeus stood and spoke aloud before them all. He declared unto the Lord that the half of his goods he would give to the poor. And if he had taken anything from any man by false accusation, he would restore it fourfold.

Jesus answered him there in the hearing of the people. He declared that salvation had come to that house on this day, forasmuch as Zacchaeus also was a son of Abraham. He said plainly that the Son of man was come to seek and to save that which was lost.

The crowd stood still upon the road. The sycamore tree cast its shadow across the dust. Zacchaeus remained standing before Jesus in the open light of the afternoon, and the murmuring voices along the street fell quiet.
