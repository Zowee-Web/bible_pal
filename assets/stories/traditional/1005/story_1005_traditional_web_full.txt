The road into Jericho was dry and packed hard from the traffic of many feet. Dust hung in the air where sandals scuffed the ground. Along the edges of the road, merchants called out from their stalls, and donkeys stood tethered to posts, flicking their ears against flies. The city sat low in the Jordan valley, warm even when the hills above caught cooler winds.

Word had moved through the streets that Jesus was passing through. People left their doorways and their work, gathering along the road in clusters that thickened as the minutes passed. Some stood on the stone curbs. Others pressed forward into the street itself, craning their necks to see down the road where a group of travelers was approaching.

Among the crowd was a man named Zacchaeus. He was a chief tax collector, and he was wealthy. His robes were finer than those around him, the fabric dyed and stitched well. But he was short in stature, and the crowd packed tightly together ahead of him. He moved to one side, then the other, stepping up on his toes, but the backs and shoulders of taller men blocked his view completely. No one shifted to make room for him. No one turned to let him through.

Zacchaeus moved ahead of the crowd, walking quickly along the road in the direction Jesus was coming from. A sycamore fig tree stood there, its trunk thick and low-branching, its broad leaves casting a wide circle of shade onto the ground below. He reached up, gripped one of the lower limbs, and pulled himself up. His sandals scraped against the bark as he climbed higher, settling himself on a branch where the leaves partially screened him but the road below was fully visible.

From that perch, he could see everything. The crowd stretched back along the road in both directions. Children darted between adults. Vendors paused mid-transaction, looking toward the approaching group. The sound of many voices talking at once filled the air — questions, shouts, murmured conversations.

Then Jesus came near. He walked at the center of a group of disciples and followers, his pace unhurried. People along the road called out to him. Some reached toward him. He moved through the crowd steadily, and then he stopped directly beneath the sycamore fig tree.

Jesus looked up.

"Zacchaeus, hurry and come down," he said, "for today I must stay at your house."

The words carried clearly. People nearby turned to look — first at Jesus, then up into the tree where Zacchaeus sat on the branch, his legs hanging above the road. He moved immediately, gripping the limb and lowering himself down, dropping the last short distance to the ground. His feet hit the packed dirt and he straightened, standing before Jesus. He received him with visible gladness, his face open, his posture quick and attentive as he gestured toward the road leading to his home.

The crowd shifted. Voices rose. People standing nearby spoke to one another, and their words were sharp and audible.

"He has gone in to lodge with a man who is a sinner," they said. The complaint passed from mouth to mouth through the gathered people. Heads shook. Arms folded. The murmuring continued as Jesus and Zacchaeus walked together away from the tree, the disciples following, moving toward the part of the city where Zacchaeus lived.

At the house, Zacchaeus stood before Jesus. The room around them held the signs of wealth — carved furniture, oil lamps burning steadily, woven textiles on the walls. Zacchaeus straightened and spoke aloud, his voice firm and clear enough for those present to hear every word.

"Lord, half of my goods I give to the poor," he said. He paused, then continued. "If I have wrongfully exacted anything from anyone, I restore four times as much."

Jesus spoke in response, and his voice filled the room.

"Today, salvation has come to this house, because he also is a son of Abraham." He paused. The lamplight flickered against the walls. "For the Son of Man came to seek and to save that which was lost."

The room was still. Zacchaeus stood where he was, his hands at his sides, his declaration still hanging in the air. The disciples stood nearby. Outside, the sounds of Jericho continued — a distant voice calling from a market stall, the clatter of a cart wheel on stone. Inside the house, Jesus remained with Zacchaeus, and the evening settled around them like the cooling air that came down from the hills as the sun moved lower over the valley.
