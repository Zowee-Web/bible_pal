The road into Jericho was packed. Dust hung low over the crowd, stirred by hundreds of sandaled feet pressing toward the city gate. Vendors along the roadside called out prices for dried figs and oil, but their voices were swallowed by the noise of the gathering throng. Word had spread that Jesus of Nazareth was passing through.

Zacchaeus pushed through the edge of the crowd. He was a chief tax collector, a wealthy man, and short in stature. The wall of shoulders and backs in front of him blocked his view entirely. He moved along the fringe, craning his neck, but it was no use. The press of bodies was too thick and too tall.

He ran ahead, his sandals slapping the packed earth, past clusters of people still jostling for position. A sycamore-fig tree stood near the road ahead, its broad trunk leaning slightly, its thick branches spreading wide and low enough to reach. Zacchaeus gripped the lowest limb, pulled himself up, and climbed until he sat on a branch overlooking the road. Bark scraped against his palms. Leaves brushed his face. From there, the approaching procession came into full view.

Jesus walked at the center of a loose group of followers. The crowd parted unevenly around him, some pressing close, others stepping back to let him pass. When he reached the sycamore tree, he stopped. He looked up.

"Zacchaeus, hurry and come down," Jesus said, "for today I must stay at your house."

Zacchaeus climbed down quickly, dropping the last few feet to the ground. He received Jesus gladly, and the two of them turned together toward the road leading into the city.

Murmuring spread through the crowd. Voices carried clearly in the dry air. "He has gone in to lodge with a man who is a sinner," people said, one group to another, the words repeated and passed back through the press of onlookers.

Zacchaeus stood before Jesus. He spoke aloud, his voice steady and audible to those nearby. "Lord, half of my goods I give to the poor. If I have wrongfully exacted anything from anyone, I restore four times as much."

Jesus answered him, and his words carried over the heads of the gathered crowd.

"Today, salvation has come to this house, because he also is a son of Abraham. For the Son of Man came to seek and to save that which was lost."

The crowd stood still along the dusty road. Zacchaeus turned and walked with Jesus toward his house, their footsteps fading past the sycamore tree, past the vendors and the onlookers, into the streets of Jericho.
