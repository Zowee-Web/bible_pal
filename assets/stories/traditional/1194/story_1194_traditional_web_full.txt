The word of the Lord came to Joel the son of Pethuel. It was the time of the locust. The fields outside the city had been stripped to the bare earth. The vines and the fig trees had been cut down to the wood. The food in the houses was running low. The prophet stood in Zion. He lifted his voice and gave the word that had been given to him.

"Blow the trumpet in Zion. Sanctify a fast. Call a solemn assembly."

The trumpet was sounded. The voice of it carried across the city and out into the country roads. The men in the fields heard it where they had been bent over the broken ground. They lifted their faces toward Zion. The mothers heard it inside their houses. The old men heard it in the doorways where they sat. The trumpet sounded a second time, and a third time, and the people began to come.

They came from the fields. They came from the hill country. They came along the dusty roads with their children at their sides. The bridegroom came out of his room. The bride came out of her chamber. The mothers carried their infants wrapped in cloth. The old men leaned upon their staffs and would not be left behind. By the morning of the next day, the courts of the temple were full.

The priests, the ministers of the Lord, came up between the porch and the altar. They stood between the two. They wept. They lifted their voices.

"Spare your people, Lord," they said. "Don't give your heritage to reproach, that the nations should rule over them. Why should they say among the peoples, 'Where is their God?'"

The people stood behind the priests in the assembled silence. The lamps of the temple burned in the morning light. The wind moved over the city. The prophet lifted his face. He spoke the word that had been given for this hour.

"Yet even now," says the Lord, "turn to me with all your heart, and with fasting, and with weeping, and with mourning. Tear your heart and not your garments, and turn to the Lord, your God; for he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and abundant in loving kindness, and relents from sending calamity. Who knows? He may turn and relent, and leave a blessing behind him."

The people heard. The priests bowed their faces between the porch and the altar. The lamps burned on.

Then the Lord was jealous for his land. He had pity on his people.

The word came back through the prophet, and his voice changed as he spoke it.

"Behold, I will send you grain, new wine, and oil, and you will be satisfied with them. I will no more make you a reproach among the nations. Land, do not be afraid. Be glad and rejoice, for the Lord has done great things. Do not be afraid, you animals of the field, for the pastures of the wilderness spring up, for the tree bears its fruit. The fig tree and the vine yield their strength."

The prophet looked across the people. He looked at the children. He looked at the old men. He let the next words settle slowly.

"Be glad then, you children of Zion, and rejoice in the Lord, your God; for he gives you the early rain in just measure, and he causes the rain to come down for you, the early rain and the latter rain, as before. The threshing floors will be full of wheat, and the vats will overflow with new wine and oil."

The morning was bright over Zion. The trumpet did not sound a fourth time. The priests came down from the porch. The mothers gathered up their children. The men turned back toward their fields. The wind moved across the city and out along the country roads, and the words of the prophet went with them.
