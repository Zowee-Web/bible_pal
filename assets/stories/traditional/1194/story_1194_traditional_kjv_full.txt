The word of the Lord came unto Joel the son of Pethuel. It was the time of the locust. The fields without the city had been stripped to the bare earth. The vines and the fig trees had been cut down to the wood. The food in the houses was waning. The prophet stood in Zion. He lifted up his voice and gave the word that had been given unto him.

"Blow the trumpet in Zion. Sanctify a fast. Call a solemn assembly."

The trumpet was sounded. The voice thereof carried across the city and out into the country roads. The men in the fields heard it where they had been bent over the broken ground. They lifted their faces toward Zion. The mothers heard it within their houses. The old men heard it in the doorways where they sat. The trumpet sounded a second time, and a third time, and the people began to come.

They came from the fields. They came from the hill country. They came along the dusty roads with their children at their sides. The bridegroom came forth of his chamber. The bride came forth of her closet. The mothers carried their infants wrapped in cloth. The old men leaned upon their staves and would not be left behind. By the morning of the next day, the courts of the temple were full.

The priests, the ministers of the Lord, came up between the porch and the altar. They stood between the two. They wept. They lifted up their voices.

"Spare thy people, O Lord," they said. "Give not thine heritage to reproach, that the heathen should rule over them. Wherefore should they say among the people, Where is their God?"

The people stood behind the priests in the assembled silence. The lamps of the temple burned in the morning light. The wind moved over the city. The prophet lifted his face. He spake the word that had been given for that hour.

"Therefore also now, saith the Lord, turn ye even to me with all your heart, and with fasting, and with weeping, and with mourning. And rend your heart, and not your garments, and turn unto the Lord your God: for he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and repenteth him of the evil. Who knoweth if he will return and repent, and leave a blessing behind him?"

The people heard. The priests bowed their faces between the porch and the altar. The lamps burned on.

Then the Lord was jealous for his land. He pitied his people.

The word came back through the prophet, and his voice changed as he spake it.

"Yea, the Lord will answer and say unto his people, Behold, I will send you corn, and wine, and oil, and ye shall be satisfied therewith. I will no more make you a reproach among the heathen. Fear not, O land; be glad and rejoice: for the Lord will do great things. Be not afraid, ye beasts of the field: for the pastures of the wilderness do spring, for the tree beareth her fruit, the fig tree and the vine do yield their strength."

The prophet looked across the people. He looked upon the children. He looked upon the old men. He let the next words settle slowly.

"Be glad then, ye children of Zion, and rejoice in the Lord your God: for he hath given you the former rain moderately, and he will cause to come down for you the rain, the former rain, and the latter rain in the first month. And the floors shall be full of wheat, and the fats shall overflow with wine and oil."

The morning was bright over Zion. The trumpet sounded not a fourth time. The priests came down from the porch. The mothers gathered up their children. The men turned back toward their fields. The wind moved across the city and out along the country roads, and the words of the prophet went with them.
