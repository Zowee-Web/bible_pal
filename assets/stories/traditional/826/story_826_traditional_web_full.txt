The armies of Israel and the Philistines drew up their battle lines in the wide Valley of Elah. On one hill camped the armies of Israel, their banners fluttering in the mist of morning. Across the valley, on the opposite slope, the Philistine host gathered, armor gleaming, the air thick with tension. Between them lay open ground, silent but for the distant clink of weapons and the mutter of watchful men.

From the Philistine camp stepped their champion, a giant named Goliath of Gath. He stood over nine feet tall, clad in a coat of bronze mail that shimmered like fire in the sunrise. A massive helmet shaded his face. He bore a heavy spear like a weaver's beam, its iron tip alone weighed more than a man's hand could grasp. An armor-bearer walked before him, carrying a shield to complete the spectacle.

Goliath marched forward to the center of the valley. His voice thundered across the space. “Why have you come out to set your battle in array? Am I not a Philistine, and you servants of Saul? Choose a man for yourselves, and let him come down to me!” He paced as he spoke, eyes scouring the Israelite line. “If he is able to fight with me and kill me, then we will be your servants. But if I prevail against him and kill him, then you will be our servants and serve us.” He lifted his head, his shout ringing clear. “I defy the armies of Israel this day! Give me a man, that we may fight together.”

No one answered him. The men of Israel, hearing Goliath’s challenge, shrank back, dismayed and afraid. Day after day, Goliath returned—morning and evening—uttering the same taunt. For forty days, the echo of his challenge troubled Israel’s camp.

Back in Bethlehem, Jesse sent his youngest son, David, to visit his older brothers, who served in Saul’s army. He brought bread and roasted grain, along with a gift for their commander. David left the sheep with a keeper and made his way to the camp just as the armies formed their lines, shouting the war cry. Israel and the Philistines faced each other, ranks glimmering in the dawn.

David hurried to his brothers at the battle line. As he greeted them, Goliath stepped forward again, repeating his boast. David watched as Israel’s men flinched, turning away in fear. He heard them speak, their voices shaking, “Have you seen this man who has come up? Surely to defy Israel has he come up! The king will enrich the man who kills him with great riches and will give him his daughter. His father’s house will be free in Israel.”

David gazed across the valley at the giant. He asked the men, “What shall be done for the man who kills this Philistine and takes away the reproach from Israel? For who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?” The men told him the same—riches, the king’s daughter, and freedom for his family.

David’s words reached his oldest brother, Eliab, who burned with anger. “Why have you come down here? With whom have you left those few sheep in the wilderness? I know your pride and the naughtiness of your heart; for you have come down to see the battle.”

David replied, “What have I now done? Is there not a cause?” He turned to another, repeating his question about Goliath. Word spread through the camp until it reached King Saul. Saul sent for him.

David stood before the king, a young shepherd, strong and full of courage. “Let no man’s heart fail because of him. Your servant will go and fight with this Philistine.”

Saul looked at him, surprised. “You are not able to go against this Philistine to fight with him. You are but a youth, and he has been a man of war from his youth.”

David answered, “Your servant kept his father’s sheep. When a lion or a bear came and took a lamb out of the flock, I went out after it, struck it, and delivered the lamb from its mouth. When it turned against me, I struck it and killed it. Your servant has struck both the lion and the bear. This uncircumcised Philistine shall be as one of them, since he has defied the armies of the living God.” David declared boldly, “Yahweh, who delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine.”

Saul relented. “Go, and Yahweh be with you.” He gave David his own armor—a bronze helmet and a heavy coat. David strapped Saul’s sword over the tunic and tried walking, but he could not move freely.

“I can’t go with these,” he said. “I have not tested them.” He took the armor off and, with his staff in hand, chose five smooth stones from the brook. Placing them in his shepherd’s bag, with sling in hand, David approached the Philistine.

Goliath advanced, shield-bearer in front, and saw young David drawing near. He disdained him, for he was little more than a youth, ruddy and handsome in appearance. Goliath roared, “Am I a dog, that you come to me with sticks?” He cursed David by his gods. “Come to me, and I will give your flesh to the birds of the sky and to the beasts of the field.”

David answered, his voice ringing with courage. “You come to me with a sword, with a spear, and with a javelin. But I come to you in the name of Yahweh of armies, the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied. Today, Yahweh will deliver you into my hand. I will strike you down and take your head from you. I will give the bodies of the host of the Philistines this day to the birds and to the wild animals. All the earth will know that there is a God in Israel. All this assembly shall know that Yahweh doesn’t save with sword and spear—for the battle is Yahweh’s, and he will give you into our hand.”

Goliath moved forward, closing the distance. David ran quickly toward the battle line to meet him. He reached into his bag, took a stone, and slung it. The stone struck Goliath on his forehead, sinking deep. The giant fell facedown with a thunderous crash.

Silence fell across the valley.

David stood over the fallen Philistine. He took Goliath’s own sword, drew it, and cut off his head. When the Philistines saw their champion was dead, they turned and fled. The men of Israel and Judah rose with a shout and pursued the Philistines. The rout stretched from the valley to the gates of Ekron, the slain lying on the road. The people of Israel returned, plundering the Philistine camp.

David brought Goliath’s head to Jerusalem, but he kept the giant’s armor in his own tent.
