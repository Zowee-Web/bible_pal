The Philistines gathered their armies together for battle. They came and camped at Ephes-dammim, between Socoh and Azekah, and spread out along the slope. Across the wide valley, the men of Israel assembled with King Saul. The Israelites stood on one hill, and the Philistines encamped opposite them, a great distance yawning between their lines. In the morning light, mists hovered low over the rocky earth. Armor clinked. Breathless silence lingered before the day’s noise began.

From the Philistine ranks, there stepped forth a champion named Goliath, out of Gath. He towered above every man, his height enormous—six cubits and a span. Goliath’s bronze helmet shone, his massive coat of mail glinting bronze with each movement. Greaves covered his legs, and a javelin of bronze slung between his shoulders. The staff of his spear was like a weaver's beam, the spearhead alone weighing six hundred shekels of iron. A shield-bearer marched before him, setting the measure of Goliath’s strength.

He called out across the valley, his voice steady and proud. “Why have you come out to set your battle in array? Am I not a Philistine, and you servants of Saul? Choose a man for yourselves, and let him come down to me!” The challenge echoed in the air. “If he is able to fight with me and kill me, then we will be your servants. But if I prevail and kill him, then you will be our servants and serve us.” Again, he shouted, “I defy the armies of Israel this day! Give me a man, that we may fight together!”

The words of the giant sent a ripple of fear through the Israelites. Saul and all the men heard every taunt, and their courage withered. No man dared answer him. Day after day, Goliath strode forth with his challenge. Both armies faced each other across the valley, each morning met by the Philistine’s voice, and each day the hope of Israel dimmed.

Now, there lived in Bethlehem a man named Jesse, who had eight sons. In Saul’s camp, Jesse’s three eldest sons fought among Israel’s troops. David, the youngest, would come from tending his father’s sheep to bring food to his brothers at the battlefield, then return home swiftly.

One day, Jesse called David to him as the armies were arrayed. “Take this roasted grain and these ten loaves to your brothers. Also bring these ten cheeses to the captain of their thousand. See how your brothers are, and bring me word.”

Before dawn, David rose and left his sheep in a keeper’s care. He carried the provisions and traveled to the encampment. As he approached, the sound of the shouts and battle cry of Israel rose into the air. The armies faced off, rank against rank, gripping weapons, stamping feet against the rocks.

David left the food with the keeper and ran toward the lines to greet his brothers. As he spoke with them, behold—Goliath, the Philistine champion, strode forward again. His vast figure cast a long shadow on the dew-covered ground. The same words roared from his lips. Every soldier of Israel shrank back.

The men around David whispered, “Have you seen this man who has come up? Surely he comes to defy Israel. The king will enrich the man who strikes him—he will give him great wealth, his daughter, and make his father’s house free in Israel.”

David asked those nearby, “What will be done for the man who kills this Philistine, and takes away the reproach from Israel? Who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?”

Some spoke sharply to David. His eldest brother Eliab heard his words and burned with anger. “Why have you come down? With whom have you left those few sheep in the wilderness? I know your pride, and the evil of your heart—for you came down to see the battle.”

David replied, “What have I now done? Is there not a cause?” He turned to others and repeated his question. Word spread through the camp.

Soon they reported David’s words to Saul. He sent for the shepherd boy. David came before the king and spoke plainly, “Let no man’s heart fail because of him. Your servant will go and fight with this Philistine.”

Saul eyed David, noting his youth. He said, “You are not able to go against this Philistine to fight with him. You are but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth.”

But David answered with courage. “Your servant kept his father’s sheep. When a lion or a bear came and took a lamb out of the flock, I went after him, and struck him, and delivered it out of his mouth. When he arose against me, I caught him by his beard, and struck him, and killed him. Your servant struck both the lion and the bear. This uncircumcised Philistine shall be as one of them—seeing he has defied the armies of the living God.” David’s eyes shone with conviction. “Yahweh who delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine.”

Saul regarded him long, then said, “Go, and Yahweh be with you.”

Saul placed his own armor upon David: a bronze helmet, a coat of mail, his sword strapped across the boy’s clothing. David tried to walk, but the unfamiliar weight stifled him. He said softly, “I can’t go with these; I have not tested them.” He laid aside the armor.

Instead, David took his staff in hand and chose five smooth stones from the brook. He placed them in the shepherd's bag along with his sling. Thus, dressed as a shepherd, he stepped out into the valley, courage in every step.

Goliath saw the boy approach. He loomed over David, his armor flaming gold, and his shield-bearer at his side. The Philistine looked David over and sneered. “Am I a dog,” he laughed, “that you come to me with sticks?” Cursing David by his gods, Goliath thundered, “Come to me. I will give your flesh to the birds of the sky and the beasts of the field!”

David answered, his voice ringing through the hush. “You come to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a javelin. But I come to you in the name of Yahweh of Armies, the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied. Today Yahweh will deliver you into my hand. I will strike you, and take your head from off you. I will give the dead bodies of the host of the Philistines today to the birds of the sky, and to the wild animals of the earth, that all the earth may know that there is a God in Israel. All this assembly shall know that Yahweh doesn’t save with sword and spear—for the battle is Yahweh’s, and he will give you into our hand.”

Without waiting, Goliath moved forward, heavy feet pounding the ground, shield raised. David ran quickly toward the battle line to meet him. He reached into his bag, took one stone, set it in his sling. He whirled it around and let it fly with practiced aim. The stone sped through the quiet air and struck Goliath upon his forehead. The blow sank deep.

Goliath’s legs faltered. The giant tottered and crashed facedown upon the earth. For a moment, a deep silence filled the valley.

David hurried and stood over Goliath. He took the Philistine’s sword and drew it from its sheath. With a single motion, he slew Goliath and cut off his head.

When the Philistines saw that their champion was dead, panic seized them. They turned and fled. The men of Israel and of Judah rose with a shout and pursued the Philistines to the gates of Ekron. Along the way, the wounded and slain lay strewn across the road to Shaaraim, even to Gath and Ekron.

The Israelites returned from chasing the Philistines, cries of victory rising. They plundered the Philistine camp. David took the head of Goliath and brought it to Jerusalem, keeping the giant’s armor within his own tent.

Thus ended the days of Goliath’s challenge, and the Lord delivered Israel by the hand of a shepherd boy, before the watching armies and the valley thick with morning mist.
