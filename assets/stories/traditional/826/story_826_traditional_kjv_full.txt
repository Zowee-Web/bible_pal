The armies of Israel and the Philistines drew up their battle lines in the wide Valley of Elah. Upon one hill encamped Israel, their banners moving in the morning mist. Across the valley, upon the opposite slope, the host of the Philistines was gathered, armour gleaming, the air thick with watchfulness. Between them lay open ground, silent save for the distant clinking of weapons.

From the camp of the Philistines there went out a champion, a giant named Goliath of Gath. His height was six cubits and a span, clad in a coat of bronze mail that shimmered like fire in the sunrise. A massive helmet shaded his face. He bare a heavy spear, the staff whereof was like a weaver's beam, its iron head alone weighed more than a man's hand could grasp. An armour-bearer went before him, carrying a shield.

Goliath strode forth unto the midst of the valley. His voice thundered across the space. "Why are ye come out to set your battle in array? Am I not a Philistine, and ye servants to Saul? Choose you a man for you, and let him come down to me!" He paced as he spake, his eyes searching the ranks of Israel. "If he be able to fight with me, and to kill me, then will we be your servants: but if I prevail against him, and kill him, then shall ye be our servants, and serve us." He lifted up his head, his shout ringing clear. "I defy the armies of Israel this day; give me a man, that we may fight together."

No man answered him. The men of Israel, when they heard Goliath's challenge, shrank back, dismayed and sore afraid. Day after day Goliath came forth, morning and evening, uttering the same taunt. For forty days the echo of his challenge troubled the camp of Israel.

In Bethlehem, Jesse sent his youngest son David to visit his elder brethren, which served in Saul's army. He bare bread and parched corn, and a gift unto their captain. David left the sheep with a keeper and made his way unto the camp, even as the armies set their lines in array, shouting the war cry. Israel and the Philistines stood against each other, their ranks glimmering in the dawn.

David hasted unto his brethren at the battle line. And as he greeted them, Goliath stepped forth again, repeating his boast. David beheld the men of Israel flinching, turning away in fear. He heard them speak, their voices trembling, "Have ye seen this man that is come up? Surely to defy Israel is he come up: and it shall be, that the man who killeth him, the king will enrich him with great riches, and will give him his daughter, and make his father's house free in Israel."

David gazed across the valley at the giant. He said unto the men, "What shall be done to the man that killeth this Philistine, and taketh away the reproach from Israel? for who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?" And the men told him the same — riches, the king's daughter, and freedom for his father's house.

David's words came unto Eliab his eldest brother, and Eliab's anger was kindled against him. "Why camest thou down hither? and with whom hast thou left those few sheep in the wilderness? I know thy pride, and the naughtiness of thine heart; for thou art come down that thou mightest see the battle."

David answered, "What have I now done? Is there not a cause?" He turned unto another, and asked again concerning Goliath. Word of his speech spread through the camp, until it came unto King Saul. And Saul sent for him.

David stood before the king, a young shepherd, strong and full of courage. "Let no man's heart fail because of him; thy servant will go and fight with this Philistine."

Saul looked upon him, marvelling. "Thou art not able to go against this Philistine to fight with him: for thou art but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth."

David answered, "Thy servant kept his father's sheep, and there came a lion, and a bear, and took a lamb out of the flock: and I went out after him, and smote him, and delivered it out of his mouth. When he arose against me, I caught him by his beard, and smote him, and slew him. Thy servant slew both the lion and the bear: and this uncircumcised Philistine shall be as one of them, seeing he hath defied the armies of the living God." And David said boldly, "The Lord that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine."

Saul relented. "Go, and the Lord be with thee." He put his own armour upon David — a helmet of brass and a heavy coat of mail. David girded the sword of Saul upon the armour, and essayed to go, but he could not move freely.

"I cannot go with these," he said; "for I have not proved them." He put them off; and, with his staff in his hand, he chose him five smooth stones out of the brook. He put them in a shepherd's bag, and his sling was in his hand, and he drew near unto the Philistine.

Goliath came on, his shield-bearer before him, and beheld the youth drawing nigh. He disdained him, for he was but a youth, and ruddy, and of a fair countenance. Goliath roared, "Am I a dog, that thou comest to me with staves?" And he cursed David by his gods. "Come to me, and I will give thy flesh unto the fowls of the air, and to the beasts of the field."

David answered, his voice ringing with courage. "Thou comest to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield: but I come to thee in the name of the Lord of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom thou hast defied. This day will the Lord deliver thee into mine hand; and I will smite thee, and take thine head from thee. And I will give the carcases of the host of the Philistines this day unto the fowls of the air, and to the wild beasts of the earth; that all the earth may know that there is a God in Israel. And all this assembly shall know that the Lord saveth not with sword and spear: for the battle is the Lord's, and he will give you into our hands."

Goliath went forward, closing the distance. And David ran quickly toward the battle line to meet him. He put his hand in his bag, and took thence a stone, and slang it, and smote the Philistine in his forehead, that the stone sunk into his forehead; and he fell upon his face to the earth.

Silence fell upon the valley.

David stood over the fallen Philistine. He took Goliath's own sword, and drew it out of the sheath thereof, and cut off his head therewith. When the Philistines saw their champion was dead, they turned and fled. The men of Israel and of Judah arose with a shout, and pursued the Philistines. The rout stretched from the valley even unto the gates of Ekron, the slain lying upon the way. The men of Israel returned, and spoiled the camp of the Philistines.

David bare the head of Goliath unto Jerusalem; but he put his armour in his own tent.
