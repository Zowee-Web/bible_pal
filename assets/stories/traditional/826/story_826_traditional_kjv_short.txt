The Philistines gathered their armies for battle. They stood upon a mountain on the one side, and Israel stood upon a mountain on the other side: and there was a valley between them. The morning mist clave unto the ground, and the sound of armour clashed from both camps. From the ranks of the Philistines, there went out a champion named Goliath, of Gath. He was a giant of a man, his height six cubits and a span, his bronze helmet and coat of mail shining in the rising sun. His armour clanked as he moved, and a mighty spear, heavy and long, rested in his hand.

Goliath cried out unto the armies of Israel, his voice borne across the valley. "Why are ye come out to set your battle in array? Am I not a Philistine, and ye servants to Saul? Choose you a man for you, and let him come down to me! If he be able to fight with me and to kill me, then will we be your servants. But if I prevail, then shall ye be our servants, and serve us!" His challenge rang forth, and day by day he taunted Israel, but no man durst answer him.

King Saul and all Israel heard the words of Goliath. The people were dismayed and sore afraid. Time passed, and Goliath came forth morning and evening, forty days in all.

Now there was a young man named David, who kept his father Jesse's sheep, and his father sent him to bear food unto his brethren upon the field of battle, and to bring word again. David came thither as the armies drew up, shouting for the battle. He left his carriage with the keeper, and ran unto the front of the host. As he spake with his brethren, Goliath uttered his challenge again. The men of Israel fled in fear.

David said, "Who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?"

His words came unto King Saul. David was brought before the king, and said, "Let no man's heart fail because of him. Thy servant will go and fight with this Philistine." Saul beheld how young he was, but David answered, "Thy servant hath slain both lion and bear. This Philistine shall be as one of them, seeing he hath defied the armies of the living God. The Lord that delivered me shall deliver me out of his hand."

Saul put his own armour upon David, but David could not go in them. He laid them aside, taking only his staff, five smooth stones from the brook, and his sling in his hand.

David went forth into the valley. On the other side, Goliath strode nigh, clad in shining armour, mocking, "Am I a dog, that thou comest to me with staves?" And he cursed David by his gods. David answered, "Thou comest to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield: but I come to thee in the name of the Lord of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom thou hast defied. This day will the Lord deliver thee into mine hand. All the earth shall know that there is a God in Israel."

The armies looked on in silence. David ran quickly toward the Philistine. He put his hand in his bag, and took thence a stone, and slang it. The stone smote Goliath in his forehead. The giant fell upon his face to the earth. A hush fell upon the valley. David took Goliath's sword and cut off his head.

When the Philistines saw that their champion was dead, they fled. The men of Israel arose with a shout, and pursued the Philistines, and returned to spoil their camp. David bare the head of Goliath unto Jerusalem, and the courage of Israel was restored.
