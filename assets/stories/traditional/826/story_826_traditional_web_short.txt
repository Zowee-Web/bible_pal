The Philistines gathered their armies for battle and stood on a mountain on one side, while the Israelites stood on another, with only the wide Valley of Elah between them. Morning mist clung to the ground as the sound of armor clashed from both camps. From the ranks of the Philistines, a champion named Goliath of Gath stepped forward. He was a giant of a man, over nine feet tall, his bronze helmet and coat of mail shining in the rising sun. Armor clanked as he moved, and a mighty spear, heavy and long, rested in his hand.

Goliath called out to Israel, his voice carrying across the valley. “Why have you come out to set your battle in array? Am I not a Philistine, and you servants to Saul? Choose a man for yourselves, and let him come down to me! If he wins against me and kills me, we will be your servants. But if I prevail… then you shall be our servants and serve us!” His challenge echoed, and day after day he taunted Israel, but no man dared to answer him.

King Saul and all Israel heard Goliath’s words. The people were dismayed and greatly afraid. Time passed, and Goliath came forth morning and evening, forty days in all.

Meanwhile, a young man named David, shepherd to his father Jesse’s sheep, was sent by his father to deliver food to his brothers on the battlefield and bring news home. David arrived just as the armies drew up, shouting for battle. He left his supplies with the keeper and ran to the front lines. As he spoke with his brothers, Goliath issued his challenge again. The men of Israel fled in fear.

David asked, “Who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?”

His words reached King Saul. David was brought before the king and said, “Let no man’s heart fail. Your servant will go and fight this Philistine.” Saul saw how young David was, but David replied, “Your servant has killed both lion and bear. This Philistine shall be like one of them, for he has defied the armies of the living God. The LORD who delivered me will deliver me out of his hand.”

Saul gave David his own armor, but David was not used to it. He laid it aside, taking only his staff, five smooth stones from the brook, and his sling in hand.

David went forth into the valley. On the other side, Goliath strode closer, clad in shining armor, mocking, “Am I a dog, that you come to me with sticks?” He cursed David by his gods. David answered, “You come to me with a sword, spear, and javelin. But I come to you in the name of the LORD of Hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied. The LORD will deliver you into my hand. All the earth will know there is a God in Israel.”

The armies watched in silence. David ran quickly toward the Philistine. He reached into his bag, took a stone, and slung it. The stone struck Goliath’s forehead. The giant fell, face first, to the ground. A hush fell over the valley. David took Goliath’s sword and cut off his head.

When the Philistines saw that their champion was dead, they fled. The men of Israel rose with a shout, pursuing the Philistines, and returned to plunder their camp. David took the head of Goliath to Jerusalem, and Israel's courage was restored.
