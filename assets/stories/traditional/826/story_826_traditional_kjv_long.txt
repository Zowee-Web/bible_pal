The Philistines gathered their armies together for battle. They came and pitched at Ephes-dammim, between Shochoh and Azekah, and spread themselves along the slope. Across the wide valley, the men of Israel were assembled with King Saul. The Israelites stood upon one hill, and the Philistines encamped over against them, a great distance yawning between their lines. In the morning light, mists hovered low upon the rocky earth. Armour clinked. A breathless silence lingered ere the noise of the day began.

From the ranks of the Philistines there went forth a champion named Goliath, out of Gath. He towered above every man, his height enormous — six cubits and a span. Goliath's helmet of brass shone, his massive coat of mail glinting bronze with every movement. Greaves of brass were upon his legs, and a javelin of brass between his shoulders. The staff of his spear was like a weaver's beam, the head of his spear alone weighing six hundred shekels of iron. A shield-bearer marched before him, declaring the measure of Goliath's strength.

He cried out across the valley, his voice steady and proud. "Why are ye come out to set your battle in array? Am I not a Philistine, and ye servants to Saul? Choose you a man for you, and let him come down to me!" The challenge echoed in the air. "If he be able to fight with me, and to kill me, then will we be your servants: but if I prevail against him, and kill him, then shall ye be our servants, and serve us." Again he shouted, "I defy the armies of Israel this day; give me a man, that we may fight together!"

The words of the giant sent a ripple of fear through the Israelites. Saul and all the men heard every taunt, and their courage withered within them. No man durst answer him. Day after day, Goliath strode forth with his challenge. Both armies stood facing one another across the valley, each morning met by the Philistine's voice, and each day the hope of Israel was dimmed.

Now there dwelt in Bethlehem a man named Jesse, which had eight sons. In Saul's camp, the three eldest sons of Jesse fought among the troops of Israel. David, the youngest, would come from tending his father's sheep to bear food unto his brethren upon the battlefield, and then return home swiftly.

One day, Jesse called David unto him as the armies were set in array. "Take now for thy brethren this parched corn and these ten loaves. Carry also these ten cheeses unto the captain of their thousand. Look how thy brethren fare, and bring me word again."

Before the morning, David arose and left his sheep with a keeper. He took the provisions and journeyed unto the encampment. As he drew near, the sound of the shouting and the battle cry of Israel rose into the air. The armies stood rank against rank, gripping their weapons, their feet stamping upon the rocks.

David left the carriage with the keeper, and ran unto the lines to greet his brethren. As he spake with them, behold — Goliath, the champion of the Philistines, strode forward again. His vast figure cast a long shadow upon the dewy ground. The same words roared from his lips. Every man of Israel shrank back.

The men round about David whispered, "Have ye seen this man that is come up? Surely to defy Israel is he come up: and it shall be, that the man that killeth him, the king will enrich him with great riches, and will give him his daughter, and make his father's house free in Israel."

David said unto them that stood by, "What shall be done to the man that killeth this Philistine, and taketh away the reproach from Israel? For who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?"

Some spake sharply unto David. Eliab his eldest brother heard his words, and his anger was kindled against him. "Why camest thou down hither? and with whom hast thou left those few sheep in the wilderness? I know thy pride, and the naughtiness of thine heart; for thou art come down that thou mightest see the battle."

David answered, "What have I now done? Is there not a cause?" He turned unto others, and asked his question again. Word of his speech spread through the camp.

Soon they reported David's words unto Saul. And the king sent for the shepherd lad. David came before the king, and spake plainly, "Let no man's heart fail because of him; thy servant will go and fight with this Philistine."

Saul looked upon David, marking his youth. He said, "Thou art not able to go against this Philistine to fight with him: for thou art but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth."

But David answered with courage. "Thy servant kept his father's sheep, and there came a lion, and a bear, and took a lamb out of the flock: and I went out after him, and smote him, and delivered it out of his mouth. And when he arose against me, I caught him by his beard, and smote him, and slew him. Thy servant slew both the lion and the bear: and this uncircumcised Philistine shall be as one of them, seeing he hath defied the armies of the living God." David's eyes shone with conviction. "The Lord that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine."

Saul regarded him long, then said, "Go, and the Lord be with thee."

Saul put his armour upon David: a helmet of brass, a coat of mail, his sword girded over the lad's clothing. David essayed to go, but the unfamiliar weight stifled him. He said softly, "I cannot go with these; for I have not proved them." And he laid the armour aside.

Instead, David took his staff in his hand, and chose him five smooth stones out of the brook. He put them in a shepherd's bag, along with his sling. Thus, clothed as a shepherd, he went out into the valley, courage in every step.

Goliath beheld the lad approaching. He loomed over David, his armour flaming like gold, his shield-bearer at his side. The Philistine looked David over and disdained him. "Am I a dog," he scoffed, "that thou comest to me with staves?" And he cursed David by his gods. Goliath thundered, "Come to me, and I will give thy flesh unto the fowls of the air, and to the beasts of the field!"

David answered, his voice ringing through the hush. "Thou comest to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield: but I come to thee in the name of the Lord of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom thou hast defied. This day will the Lord deliver thee into mine hand; and I will smite thee, and take thine head from thee. And I will give the carcases of the host of the Philistines this day unto the fowls of the air, and to the wild beasts of the earth; that all the earth may know that there is a God in Israel. And all this assembly shall know that the Lord saveth not with sword and spear: for the battle is the Lord's, and he will give you into our hands."

Without tarrying, Goliath came on, his heavy feet pounding the ground, his shield raised. David ran quickly toward the battle line to meet him. He put his hand in his bag, took thence one stone, and set it in his sling. He whirled it round about, and let it fly with practiced aim. The stone sped through the quiet air, and smote Goliath in his forehead. The blow sank deep.

Goliath's legs faltered. The giant tottered, and fell upon his face to the earth. For a moment, a deep silence filled the valley.

David ran, and stood over Goliath. He took the Philistine's sword, and drew it out of the sheath thereof. And with a single motion, he slew him, and cut off his head therewith.

When the Philistines saw their champion was dead, fear seized them. They turned and fled. The men of Israel and of Judah arose with a shout, and pursued the Philistines, even unto the gates of Ekron. Along the way, the wounded and the slain lay strewn upon the way to Shaaraim, even unto Gath and Ekron.

The Israelites returned from chasing after the Philistines, with cries of victory rising. They spoiled the tents of the Philistines. David took the head of Goliath, and brought it to Jerusalem; but he put his armour in his own tent.

Thus ended the days of Goliath's challenge, and the Lord delivered Israel by the hand of a shepherd lad, before the watching armies and the valley thick with morning mist.
