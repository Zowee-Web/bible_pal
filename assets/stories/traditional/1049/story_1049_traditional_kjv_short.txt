The twelve spies returned after forty days. They came again unto Moses and unto Aaron and unto all the congregation of the children of Israel at Kadesh in the wilderness of Paran. Two men walked before, bearing betwixt them upon a staff a single cluster of grapes so great it bent the wood. The skins of the grapes were dark and tight with juice. Behind them came others bearing pomegranates and figs, the rinds smooth, the figs soft in their hands.

The congregation gathered nigh. Dust rose beneath hundreds of sandaled feet. The spies gave their report.

"We came unto the land whither thou sentest us, and surely it floweth with milk and honey; and this is the fruit of it." They held the grapes on high. The people pressed forward, their eyes wide. Children pushed betwixt their parents to behold. The scent of ripe fruit carried upon the dry desert air, sweet against the familiar smell of sand and goatskin.

Then the voices changed.

"Nevertheless the people be strong that dwell in the land, and the cities are walled and very great. And moreover we saw the children of Anak there." The Amalekites in the south. The Hittites and the Jebusites and the Amorites in the mountains. The Canaanites by the sea and by the coast of Jordan.

The murmuring began low and spread through the multitude as wind through dry grass. Men shifted upon their feet. Women drew their children nigh. Hands that had reached toward the fruit fell back to their sides.

And Caleb stilled the people before Moses. He stepped forward and stood where they might see him, his sandals set firm in the dust. He spake over the murmuring. "Let us go up at once, and possess it; for we are well able to overcome it."

But the men that went up with him said, "We be not able to go up against the people; for they are stronger than we."

And they brought up an evil report of the land unto the children of Israel, saying, "The land, through which we have gone to search it, is a land that eateth up the inhabitants thereof; and all the people that we saw in it are men of a great stature. And there we saw the giants, the sons of Anak, which come of the giants: and we were in our own sight as grasshoppers, and so we were in their sight."

The grapes hung yet from the staff, bending the wood with their weight. The pomegranates lay in the dust where they had been set down. The figs softened in the heat, their skins splitting. None reached for them.
