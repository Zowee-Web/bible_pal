The people of Israel had their backs to the sea. They had marched out of Egypt with flocks and herds and children and bread wrapped in cloth. They had followed the pillar of cloud by day and the pillar of fire by night. Now they stood at the water's edge with nowhere left to go.

Then someone looked back.

Pharaoh's army was coming. Chariots spread across the horizon — six hundred chosen chariots and every other chariot in Egypt. The dust cloud behind them covered the western sky. The sound of wheels reached them first, a low rolling thunder that did not stop.

The people were terrified. They cried out to the Lord. Then they turned on Moses. "Were there no graves in Egypt that you have taken us away to die in the wilderness? What have you done to us? Did we not say, leave us alone? It would have been better to serve the Egyptians than to die here."

Moses faced them. The army behind. The sea behind that. The people's fear thick as smoke.

"Do not be afraid," he said. "Stand firm and see the salvation of the Lord, which he will work for you today. The Egyptians you see today you will never see again. The Lord will fight for you. You have only to be still."

The Lord spoke to Moses. "Why do you cry to me? Tell the people to go forward. Lift your staff. Stretch out your hand over the sea and divide it, that the people may go through on dry ground."

The angel of God who had been going before the camp moved behind them. The pillar of cloud shifted from front to rear and stood between the army of Egypt and the people of Israel. Darkness on the Egyptian side. Light on the Israelite side. The two camps did not come near each other through the night.

Moses stretched out his hand over the sea.

The Lord drove the sea back by a strong east wind that blew all through the night. The wind came hard and steady, pressing the water aside. It did not gust or relent. Hour after hour it pushed. The water parted. The seabed appeared — packed sand, smooth stones, broken shells — wet at first, then dry as the wind scoured the ground.

Walls of water rose on either side. They stood like cliffs, dark and heavy, shifting inside themselves but held in place. The corridor between them stretched forward into the darkness.

The people of Israel went into the sea on dry ground. Mothers held children's hands. Fathers drove donkeys loaded with supplies. The elderly walked with staffs, their sandals firm on the hard floor of the sea. Livestock moved between the walls of water. No wave touched them.

The Egyptians followed. Pharaoh's chariots and horsemen pursued them into the corridor. The wheels rolled across the dry ground. The soldiers pressed forward in ranks.

In the morning watch, the Lord looked down through the pillar of fire and cloud upon the Egyptian forces. He threw them into confusion. He clogged their chariot wheels so they drove heavily. The chariots lurched and dragged. Wheels snapped from axles. Horses stumbled.

The Egyptians said, "Let us flee from Israel. The Lord fights for them against Egypt."

The Lord said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the sea, that the water may come back upon the Egyptians, upon their chariots, and upon their horsemen."

Moses stretched out his hand. The sea returned to its normal course at the break of dawn. The Egyptians tried to flee into it. The Lord threw them into the sea. The waters came back and covered the chariots, the horsemen, all the host of Pharaoh. Not one remained.

But the people of Israel walked on dry ground through the sea. The water was a wall to them on right and left. The Lord saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians.

The morning light spread across the water. The bodies of the Egyptians lay along the shore — chariots overturned, horses still in harness, bronze armor half-buried in wet sand. Israel stood on the far bank with the wind falling quiet around them and the sea settling back into place.
