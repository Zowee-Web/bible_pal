Pharaoh's chariots came behind them. The dust rose first, then the sound — wheels and hooves and the clatter of bronze on leather. The people of Israel stood at the edge of the sea and saw the army of Egypt closing the distance.

They cried out to Moses. "Were there no graves in Egypt? Why have you brought us into the wilderness to die?"

Moses stood before them. The wind pulled at his robes. Behind him the sea stretched flat and gray.

"Do not be afraid," Moses said. "Stand firm and see the salvation of the Lord. The Egyptians you see today, you will never see again. The Lord will fight for you. You need only to be still."

The Lord said to Moses, "Tell the people to go forward. Lift up your staff and stretch your hand over the sea and divide it."

Moses raised his staff over the water. The Lord drove the sea back by a strong east wind all through the night. The wind roared across the surface and pushed the water apart. Walls of water rose on the left and on the right. Between them the seabed lay exposed — dry ground, firm underfoot.

The people of Israel walked into the sea on dry land. Women carried children on their hips. Men drove livestock between the walls of water. The ground held firm beneath them.

The Egyptians followed. Every chariot, every horseman pressed forward into the corridor of the sea.

At morning watch the Lord looked down through the pillar of fire and cloud. He threw the Egyptian army into confusion. Their chariot wheels locked and dragged in the sand. "Let us flee," they said. "The Lord fights for them against Egypt."

The Lord told Moses to stretch out his hand. Moses stretched his hand over the sea. The waters returned at the break of dawn. They covered the chariots and the horsemen and all of Pharaoh's army that had followed into the sea. Not one of them remained.

But the people of Israel had walked on dry ground through the middle of the sea. The water was a wall to them on the right and on the left.

The sea lay flat again under the morning sun. The Egyptian dead washed against the shore. Israel stood on the far side, their sandals still dry, the wind dying down around them.
