The children of Israel had their backs unto the sea. They had marched forth out of Egypt with their flocks and their herds and their little ones and their bread bound in their kneading troughs. They had followed the pillar of the cloud by day and the pillar of fire by night. Now they stood at the water's edge with no place remaining to go.

Then one looked back.

Pharaoh's army was coming. Chariots spread across the horizon — six hundred chosen chariots and all the chariots of Egypt. The dust behind them covered the western sky. The sound of wheels reached them first, a low rolling thunder that ceased not.

The people were sore afraid. They cried out unto the Lord. Then they turned upon Moses. "Because there were no graves in Egypt, hast thou taken us away to die in the wilderness? Wherefore hast thou dealt thus with us? Did we not say unto thee, let us alone that we may serve the Egyptians? For it had been better for us to serve the Egyptians than that we should die in the wilderness."

Moses faced them. The army behind. The sea behind that. The people's fear thick as smoke.

"Fear ye not," said he. "Stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord, which he will shew to you today. For the Egyptians whom ye have seen today, ye shall see them again no more forever. The Lord shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace."

And the Lord spake unto Moses. "Wherefore criest thou unto me? Speak unto the children of Israel, that they go forward. But lift thou up thy rod, and stretch out thine hand over the sea, and divide it, and the children of Israel shall go on dry ground through the midst of the sea."

And the angel of God, which went before the camp of Israel, removed and went behind them. And the pillar of the cloud went from before their face and stood behind them. It came between the camp of the Egyptians and the camp of Israel. It was a cloud and darkness unto the Egyptians, but it gave light by night unto Israel. So that the one came not near the other all the night.

And Moses stretched out his hand over the sea.

And the Lord caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all that night, and made the sea dry land. The wind came hard and steady, and drove the waters apart. It gusted not, it relented not. Hour upon hour it blew. The waters were divided. The floor of the sea appeared — packed sand and smooth stones and broken shells — wet at the first, then dry as the wind scoured the ground.

And the waters were a wall unto them on their right hand and on their left. They stood as cliffs, dark and heavy, shifting within themselves but held fast in their place. The passage between them stretched forward into the darkness.

And the children of Israel went into the midst of the sea upon the dry ground. Mothers held their children's hands. Fathers drave donkeys laden with goods. The aged walked with their staves, their sandals firm upon the hard floor of the sea. Cattle passed between the walls of water. No wave touched them.

And the Egyptians pursued and went in after them. All Pharaoh's horses, his chariots, and his horsemen entered into the midst of the sea. The wheels rolled upon the same dry ground. The soldiers pressed forward in their ranks.

And it came to pass in the morning watch that the Lord looked unto the host of the Egyptians through the pillar of fire and of the cloud, and troubled the host of the Egyptians. And took off their chariot wheels, that they drave them heavily. The chariots lurched and dragged. Wheels brake from their axles. Horses stumbled.

And the Egyptians said, "Let us flee from the face of Israel, for the Lord fighteth for them against the Egyptians."

And the Lord said unto Moses, "Stretch out thine hand over the sea, that the waters may come again upon the Egyptians, upon their chariots, and upon their horsemen."

And Moses stretched forth his hand over the sea. And the sea returned to his strength when the morning appeared. And the Egyptians fled against it. And the Lord overthrew the Egyptians in the midst of the sea. And the waters returned, and covered the chariots, and the horsemen, and all the host of Pharaoh that came into the sea after them. There remained not so much as one of them.

But the children of Israel walked upon dry land in the midst of the sea. And the waters were a wall unto them on their right hand and on their left. Thus the Lord saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians.

The morning light spread across the water. The dead of Egypt lay upon the shore — chariots overturned, horses yet in harness, armour of bronze half buried in the wet sand. Israel stood upon the far bank with the wind falling still about them and the sea settling back into its place.
