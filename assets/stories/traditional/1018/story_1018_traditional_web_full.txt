Jacob went out from Beersheba and went toward Haran. He traveled alone. The road ran north through dry country, past low hills and open scrubland where the wind moved through thorn bushes and the only sound was the scuff of sandals on packed earth. Behind him lay his father's tents, his mother's counsel, and a brother whose anger he had earned.

He came to a certain place as the sun went down. The light was failing. The sky turned copper at the horizon and deepened to blue overhead. He would go no farther that night. He stopped and looked around the open ground — no shelter, no walls, no fire. Just the bare earth and the stones scattered across it.

He took one of the stones of the place and put it under his head. He lay down on the hard ground. The stone was rough against his skull, unshapen, cold. The night air settled around him. Stars appeared, one by one, then in great sweeps across the black sky. He closed his eyes.

He dreamed.

In the dream, a ladder was set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven. It rose from the very ground where he lay, climbing upward through the darkness into a light beyond seeing. The angels of God were ascending and descending on it — not in haste, not in confusion, but in steady, ordered procession. They moved between heaven and earth as though this traffic were constant, as though the connection between the two had always been open.

The Lord stood above it. He spoke, and his voice carried the weight of covenant.

"I am the Lord, the God of Abraham your father and the God of Isaac. The land on which you lie, I will give to you and to your offspring. Your offspring will be like the dust of the earth. You will spread abroad to the west, to the east, to the north, and to the south. In you and in your offspring all the families of the earth will be blessed."

The promise was vast — the same words spoken to Abraham, now renewed to Jacob on this bare patch of ground, beneath this open sky. And then God added something more.

"I am with you. I will keep you wherever you go. I will bring you back to this land. I will not leave you until I have done what I have promised you."

Not a conditional promise. Not a reward for good behavior. A declaration. I am with you. I will keep you. I will bring you back. I will not leave you.

Jacob awoke out of his sleep. The stars were still overhead. The stone was still under his head. The ground was still hard, the air still cold. But something had changed.

He said, "Surely the Lord is in this place, and I did not know it."

He was afraid. Not the fear of danger but the fear of standing in a place far more significant than he had realized. He said, "How awesome is this place! This is none other than the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven."

He rose early in the morning. The light came pale across the eastern hills. He took the stone that had served as his pillow and set it up on end as a pillar. He poured oil on the top of it — a small act of consecration, marking the ordinary stone as something set apart. He called the name of that place Bethel, which means "house of God." Before that, the city had been called Luz.

Then Jacob made a vow. He spoke it into the morning air, standing beside the anointed stone. "If God will be with me and will keep me in this way that I go, and will give me bread to eat and clothing to wear, so that I come again to my father's house in peace, then the Lord will be my God. This stone which I have set up as a pillar will be God's house. Of all that you give me, I will surely give a tenth to you."

The vow was simple. Bread and clothing and safe return. A tenth of everything given back. The pillar stood in the morning light, dark with oil, a single stone on open ground — marking the place where heaven had touched the earth while a man slept on the bare ground with his head on a rock.
