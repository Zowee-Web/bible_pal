Jacob did not go looking for God that night. He was running from a problem he had caused, and he stopped only because the sun went down. The stone under his head was not a sacred object. The ground was not a holy site. It was just a place on the road — until it wasn't.

There is something quietly unsettling about the idea that the gate of heaven might be located in the last place a person would think to look for it. That the ground beneath ordinary exhaustion, ordinary failure, ordinary flight might turn out to be the very place where something speaks.
