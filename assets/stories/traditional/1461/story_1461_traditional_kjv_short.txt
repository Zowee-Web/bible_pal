The king's men brought Daniel up out of the palace at sundown.

They led him unto the mouth of the lions' den without the city wall. The lions were within. They could be heard moving in the dark behind the iron gate.

The king came down unto the den himself. He stood at the mouth of it. He spake unto Daniel before they cast him in.

"Thy God whom thou servest continually," said he, "he will deliver thee."

The men cast Daniel into the den.

A stone was rolled across the mouth of the den. It was set firm. The king pressed his signet into the soft clay which they had spread along the seam. The lords pressed their signets in beside it. No man could open the den that night without breaking the seal.

The king went back unto his palace.

He passed the night fasting. He would not eat. He would not drink. No instruments of musick were brought before him. He went unto his chamber and lay down upon his bed, but his sleep fled from him. He could hear nothing in the dark of his rooms but his own breath.

He arose very early in the morning, before the light had come over the wall.

He went in haste back unto the den of lions. He came to the mouth of it. He stood there in the gray hour.

He cried out with a lamentable voice. "O Daniel, servant of the living God, is thy God, whom thou servest continually, able to deliver thee from the lions?"

The voice came back from within the den.

"O king, live for ever. My God hath sent his angel, and hath shut the lions' mouths, that they have not hurt me: forasmuch as before him innocency was found in me; and also before thee, O king, have I done no hurt."

The king was exceeding glad. He commanded that they should take Daniel up out of the den.

The seal was broken. The stone was rolled away. Daniel was brought up into the morning.

No manner of hurt was found upon him. The lions were quiet behind the stone.

Daniel had trusted in his God, and the morning was upon the wall of Babylon.
