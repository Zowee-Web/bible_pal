The king's men brought Daniel up out of the palace at sundown.

They led him to the mouth of the lions' den outside the city wall. The lions were inside. They could be heard moving in the dark behind the iron gate.

The king came down to the den himself. He stood at the mouth of it. He spoke to Daniel before they cast him in.

"Your God whom you serve continually," he said, "he will deliver you."

The men cast Daniel into the den.

A stone was rolled across the mouth of the den. It was set firm. The king pressed his signet into the soft clay that they had spread along the seam. The lords pressed their signets in beside it. No man could open the den that night without breaking the seal.

The king went back to his palace.

He passed the night fasting. He would not eat. He would not drink. No instruments of music were brought before him. He went to his chamber and lay down on his bed, but his sleep fled from him. He could hear nothing in the dark of his rooms but his own breath.

He rose very early in the morning, before the light had come over the wall.

He went in haste back to the den of lions. He came to the mouth of it. He stood there in the gray hour.

He cried out with a lamentable voice. "Daniel, servant of the living God — is your God, whom you serve continually, able to deliver you from the lions?"

The voice came back from inside the den.

"O king, live forever. My God has sent his angel and has shut the lions' mouths, and they have not hurt me — because before him innocence was found in me, and also before you, O king, I have done no hurt."

The king was exceedingly glad. He commanded that they take Daniel up out of the den.

The seal was broken. The stone was rolled away. Daniel was brought up into the morning.

No harm was on him. The lions were quiet behind the stone.

Daniel had trusted in his God, and the morning was on the wall of Babylon.
