Again there came a day when the sons of God presented themselves before the Lord. Satan came in among them, and presented himself also.

"From whence comest thou?" the Lord said unto him.

"From going to and fro in the earth," Satan answered, "and from walking up and down in it."

"Hast thou considered my servant Job?" said the Lord. "There is none like him in the earth, a perfect and an upright man, one that feareth God and escheweth evil. And still he holdeth fast his integrity, though thou movedst me against him, to destroy him without cause."

"Skin for skin," Satan answered. "All that a man hath will he give for his life. But put forth thine hand now, and touch his bone and his flesh, and he will curse thee to thy face."

"Behold, he is in thine hand," said the Lord. "But save his life."

So Satan went out from the presence of the Lord, and he smote Job with sore boils. They covered him from the sole of his foot unto his crown. Job took up a potsherd. He scraped himself with it, and he sat down among the ashes.

"Dost thou still retain thine integrity?" his wife said unto him. "Curse God, and die."

"Thou speakest as one of the foolish women speaketh," he answered her. "What? Shall we receive good at the hand of God, and shall we not receive evil?"

In all this did not Job sin with his lips.

News of all this evil came to three friends of Job — Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite. They came every one from his own place, having appointed a time together to mourn with him and to comfort him.

They saw him while they were yet afar off, and they knew him not. Then they lifted up their voice and wept. Every man rent his mantle. They cast dust upon their heads toward heaven.

And they sat down with him upon the ground. Seven days and seven nights they sat there, and none spake a word unto him, for they saw that his grief was very great.
