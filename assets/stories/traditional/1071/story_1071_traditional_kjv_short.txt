The prophet spake unto a people who had grown weary.

They had borne their burdens a long time. The roads were dusty and cracked beneath sandaled feet. The sun pressed down upon bent shoulders. Hands that once were lifted up in praise now hung limp at their sides. Their legs ached. Their breath came short upon the hills. They moved as old men, even the young among them.

And unto these people, the prophet lifted up his voice.

Hast thou not known? Hast thou not heard?

The words carried across dry ground, past the low stone walls where men sat with their heads bowed. Past the women drawing water in silence. Past the children who no longer ran.

The Lord is the everlasting God, the prophet saith. The Creator of the ends of the earth.

A warm wind stirred the dust at his feet.

He fainteth not. He groweth not weary.

The people hearkened. Some looked up. Some did not.

His understanding is unsearchable, the prophet continued. No man can measure it. No man can trace the edges thereof.

A woman shifted the clay jar upon her hip. An old man leaned forward upon his staff. The wind moved through dry grass along the roadside.

He giveth power to the faint, the prophet saith. And to him that hath no might, he increaseth strength.

The words hung in the still air. Somewhere a goat bleated from behind a wall. A child turned to look upon its mother.

Even the youths shall faint and be weary, the prophet saith. And the young men shall utterly fall.

This was a thing they had seen. The strongest among them stumbling upon level ground. The youngest gasping for breath before the day's work was done. Legs giving way. Shoulders dropping under loads they once bare without thought.

But they that wait upon the Lord shall renew their strength.

The prophet's voice rose. The dust settled. The people stood where they were upon the hard-packed road, in the doorways, beside the well.

They shall mount up with wings as eagles.

The prophet lifted his face toward the open sky. High above, where the heat shimmered against pale blue, a broad-winged bird turned in a slow circle, riding the warm air without effort, without strain, its wings wide and still.

They shall run, and not be weary.

They shall walk, and not faint.
