The prophet spoke to a people who had grown weary.

They had carried their burdens a long time. The roads were dusty and cracked beneath sandaled feet. The sun pressed down on bent shoulders. Hands that once lifted in praise now hung limp at their sides. Their legs ached. Their breath came short on the hills. They moved like old men, even the young among them.

And to these people, the prophet lifted his voice.

Have you not known? Have you not heard?

The words carried across dry ground, past the low stone walls where men sat with their heads bowed. Past the women drawing water in silence. Past the children who no longer ran.

The Lord is the everlasting God, the prophet said. The Creator of the ends of the earth.

A warm wind stirred the dust at his feet.

He does not faint. He does not grow weary.

The people listened. Some looked up. Some did not.

His understanding is unsearchable, the prophet continued. No one can measure it. No one can trace its edges.

A woman shifted the clay jar on her hip. An old man leaned forward on his staff. The wind moved through dry grass along the roadside.

He gives power to the faint, the prophet said. And to him who has no might, he increases strength.

The words hung in the still air. Somewhere a goat bleated from behind a wall. A child turned to look at its mother.

Even youths shall faint and be weary, the prophet said. Young men shall fall exhausted.

This was a thing they had seen. The strongest among them stumbling on level ground. The youngest gasping for breath before the day's work was done. Legs giving way. Shoulders dropping under loads they once carried without thought.

But they who wait for the Lord shall renew their strength.

The prophet's voice rose. The dust settled. The people stood where they were on the hard-packed road, in the doorways, beside the well.

They shall mount up with wings like eagles.

The prophet lifted his face toward the open sky. High above, where the heat shimmered against pale blue, a broad-winged bird turned in a slow circle, riding the warm air without effort, without strain, its wings wide and still.

They shall run and not be weary.

They shall walk and not faint.
