The prophet spoke to a people who had grown weary.

They had carried their burdens a long time. The roads were dusty and cracked beneath sandaled feet. The sun pressed down on bent shoulders and bare heads. Hands that once lifted in praise now hung limp at their sides. Legs ached. Breath came short on the hills. Men and women moved like the aged, even the young among them. The whole land seemed to drag itself forward, step by labored step.

And to these people, the prophet lifted his voice.

Have you not known?

The words crossed the dry ground, past low stone walls where men sat with their heads bowed and their hands between their knees. Past the women drawing water in silence at the well, their ropes fraying, their jars chipped. Past the children who no longer ran in the streets.

Have you not heard?

A few faces turned toward the sound. A man standing in the shade of a fig tree straightened. A woman set her jar down on the rim of the well and held still. The breeze had died. The air was thick and warm.

The Lord is the everlasting God, the prophet said. The Creator of the ends of the earth.

His voice was steady. It carried without strain across the packed dirt, between the mud-brick houses, over the low rooftops where laundry hung motionless in the heat.

He does not faint, the prophet continued. He does not grow weary.

A man near the back of the gathering shifted his weight from one foot to the other. His tunic was stained with sweat across the shoulders. His sandals were worn through at the heel. He looked at the ground. He looked up again.

His understanding is unsearchable, the prophet said. No one can measure it. No one can find where it begins or where it ends. No one can trace its edges.

The woman at the well pressed her palm against the warm stone rim. An old man leaned forward on his staff, the wood smooth and dark where his hands had gripped it for years. A boy sat on a low wall, his legs dangling, his bare feet dusty. The wind moved through dry grass at the edge of the road, a faint rustling, then nothing.

He gives power to the faint.

The prophet paused. The silence held.

And to him who has no might, he increases strength.

A dog lay in the shadow of a doorway, its ribs showing, its tongue resting on the dirt. Flies circled above a clay pot. The heat pressed close against every surface.

Even youths shall faint and be weary, the prophet said. His voice was clear. Young men shall fall exhausted.

This was a thing they had seen with their own eyes. The strongest among them stumbling on level ground. The youngest gasping for breath before the day's work was half done. Legs giving way beneath loads once carried without thought. Young men sitting down in the middle of a field, unable to rise. Boys dropping their tools at midday, their arms trembling, their faces pale under brown skin.

The people stood in doorways. They sat on stones. They leaned against walls. No one spoke. The prophet's words moved through them like water moving through cracked earth.

But they who wait for the Lord shall renew their strength.

The prophet's voice lifted. Not louder, but fuller, the way a wind fills a sail.

They shall mount up with wings like eagles.

He raised his face toward the open sky. The people followed his gaze. High above the rooftops, where the heat shimmered against pale blue, a broad-winged bird turned in a wide, slow circle. It rode the warm air rising from the valley floor. Its wings were spread full. They did not beat. They did not strain. The bird tilted and climbed, tilted and climbed, carried upward by something unseen, something beneath and around it.

They shall run and not be weary.

The old man's grip tightened on his staff. The woman at the well drew a breath and let it out slowly. The boy on the wall stopped swinging his legs and watched the bird.

They shall walk and not faint.

The eagle turned once more, high and distant, its shadow passing over the dry ground far below. The prophet stood with his face lifted, the sun on his skin, the words still hanging in the warm air above the silent crowd.
