The prophet spoke to a people who had grown weary.

They had carried their burdens a long time. Longer than some of them could remember. The roads they traveled were dusty and cracked beneath sandaled feet, the earth splitting open in pale seams where no rain had come. The sun pressed down on bent shoulders and bare heads from the moment it cleared the eastern hills until it dropped behind the ridgeline in the west. In between, the hours stretched like the flat land itself, endless and dry.

Hands that once lifted in praise now hung limp at their sides. Legs ached from walking. Breath came short on the hills. Men and women moved like the very old, even the young among them. Mothers carried water jars on their hips with arms that shook. Fathers stood in fields with hoes and could not bring them down. Boys who should have been chasing goats sat instead in patches of shade, their chests heaving, their eyes dull.

The whole land seemed to drag itself forward, step by labored step.

And to these people, the prophet lifted his voice.

Have you not known?

The words crossed the dry ground. They passed the low stone walls where men sat with their heads bowed and their hands between their knees. They passed the women at the well, drawing water in silence, the ropes fraying in their palms, the clay jars chipped along the rims. They passed the children who no longer ran in the streets, who sat instead in doorways with their knees pulled up.

Have you not heard?

A few faces turned toward the sound. A man standing in the shade of a fig tree straightened his back, slowly, the way a man does when his muscles have been locked in one position too long. A woman set her jar down on the stone rim of the well and held still, her wet hands motionless at her sides. The breeze had died. The air was thick and warm and tasted of dust.

The Lord is the everlasting God, the prophet said.

His voice was steady. It carried without strain across the packed dirt road, between the mud-brick houses with their flat rooftops, over the walls where laundry hung without moving in the still air.

The Creator of the ends of the earth.

A lizard darted across a stone and vanished into a crack. Somewhere in the distance, beyond the last houses, a donkey brayed once and fell silent. The sky above was pale, bleached by heat, with no cloud from one horizon to the other.

He does not faint.

The prophet stood in the open, the sun on his head and shoulders, speaking to people who understood faintness in their bones. Who felt it in the trembling of their knees when they stood too quickly. Who tasted it in the dryness of their mouths at the end of the day.

He does not grow weary.

A man near the back of the gathering shifted his weight from one foot to the other. His tunic was stained dark with sweat across the shoulders and chest. His sandals were worn through at the heel, the leather cracked, the stitching pulling loose. He looked at the ground. He rubbed his face with the back of his hand. He looked up.

His understanding is unsearchable, the prophet continued.

The word hung in the air. Unsearchable. No one could measure it. No one could find where it began or where it ended. No one could walk to its border and look across to the other side. It was beyond the reach of tired minds and weary hands.

The woman at the well pressed her palm flat against the warm stone rim. The heat of it seeped into her skin. An old man who had come to listen leaned forward on his staff, a piece of acacia wood worn smooth and dark where his hands had gripped it every day for years. The knot at the top was polished to a shine. His knuckles were swollen. His fingers curled around the wood like roots around a stone.

A boy sat on a low wall nearby, his legs dangling, his bare feet dusty from the road. A fly landed on his arm and he did not brush it away. The wind stirred the dry grass at the edge of the road — a faint rustling like someone whispering — and then nothing.

He gives power to the faint, the prophet said.

He paused. The silence gathered. A goat bleated from behind a wall and its kid answered, thin and high.

And to him who has no might, he increases strength.

The words settled over the gathering like shade. A dog lay in the shadow of a doorway, its ribs visible beneath its thin coat, its tongue resting on the packed dirt. Flies circled above a clay pot left uncovered. The heat pressed close against every surface — against the stone, against the mud walls, against the skin of every man, woman, and child who stood or sat or leaned in the open air.

The prophet drew a breath and spoke again.

Even youths shall faint and be weary.

His voice was clear and unhurried. The words fell into the quiet like stones into still water.

Young men shall fall exhausted.

This was a thing they had seen with their own eyes. Not a warning about some distant time. Not a description of some other place. They had watched the strongest among them stumble on level ground. They had seen the youngest gasping for breath before the day's work was half done. They had seen legs buckle beneath loads that once were carried without thought, the way a man carries his own arms, without noticing.

Young men sitting down in the middle of a harvested field, unable to rise. Boys dropping their tools at midday, their arms shaking, their faces pale beneath sun-darkened skin. Girls carrying bundles of sticks half the size they carried the year before, and stopping twice on the path home to set them down and rest. Even the animals — the oxen standing still in the furrow, the donkeys refusing the hill.

The people stood in doorways. They sat on stones. They leaned against walls with their arms folded or hanging loose. No one spoke. No one moved to leave. The prophet's words moved through them like water through cracked earth, finding every seam.

But they who wait for the Lord shall renew their strength.

The prophet's voice lifted. Not louder, not sharper, but fuller. The way a wind fills a loosened sail.

They shall mount up with wings like eagles.

He raised his face toward the open sky. The motion was slow and deliberate, his chin tilting upward, his eyes narrowing against the brightness. The people followed his gaze. One by one, heads turned upward. The old man with the staff. The woman at the well. The boy on the wall. The man with the worn sandals. The mothers in the doorways. Even the children, who had not been listening, looked up because everyone else was looking up.

High above them, where the heat shimmered against pale blue, a broad-winged bird turned in a wide, slow circle. An eagle. It rode the warm air that rose from the valley floor in invisible columns. Its wings were spread to their full reach, each feather distinct at the tips, fanned wide like fingers. The wings did not beat. They did not strain. They did not labor. The bird tilted and climbed, tilted and climbed, carried upward by something beneath it and around it that could not be seen.

They shall run and not be weary.

The old man's grip tightened on his staff. His knuckles whitened around the dark wood. The woman at the well drew a long breath through her nose and let it out slowly through parted lips. The boy on the wall stopped swinging his legs and sat perfectly still, watching the bird.

They shall walk and not faint.

The eagle turned once more, high and distant now, a dark shape against the bleached sky. Its shadow passed over the dry ground far below, moving across the road, across the low wall, across the feet of the people gathered there. The prophet stood with his face lifted, the sun full on his skin, the dust settling around his sandals. The people watched the bird climb. Its wings were wide and still and open.
