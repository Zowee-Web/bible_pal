By night, the woman sought him whom her soul loved, but found him not. She rose and went through the streets and broad ways of the city, still seeking. The watchmen found her, and she asked whether they had seen her beloved. It was but a little after she passed them that she found him. She held him and would not let him go until she had brought him into her mother's house. Then came the charge unto the daughters of Jerusalem: stir not up, nor awake love, till it please.

From the wilderness arose a sight like pillars of smoke, perfumed with myrrh, frankincense, and the merchant's powders. Solomon's bed appeared, surrounded by threescore valiant men of Israel, every one expert in war and armed because of fear in the night. The king had made a chariot of Lebanon wood, with pillars of silver, a bottom of gold, a covering of purple, and the midst paved with love. The daughters of Zion were called to behold Solomon crowned in the day of his gladness.

The beloved praised the woman's fairness through images of doves, flocks, scarlet, pomegranate, and David's tower hung with shields. To him she was all fair. He called to his spouse from Lebanon and spoke of love better than wine, ointments sweeter than spices, and lips that dropped as the honeycomb.

She was a garden inclosed, a fountain sealed, an orchard of pleasant fruits and chief spices, and a well of living waters with streams from Lebanon. The woman called for north and south winds to blow upon her garden, that its spices might flow, and she invited her beloved to enter and eat its pleasant fruit.

He answered that he had come into his garden, gathered myrrh and spice, eaten honey with honeycomb, and drunk wine with milk. Friends were called to eat and drink abundantly together in love's gladness.
