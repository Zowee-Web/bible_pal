By night, the woman sought the one her soul loved, but he was not there. She rose and went through the city's streets and squares, still searching. The watchmen found her, and she asked whether they had seen him. Scarcely had she passed them when she found her beloved. She held him and would not let him go until she had brought him to her mother's house. Then came the charge to the daughters of Jerusalem: do not stir up or awaken love until it desires.

From the wilderness rose a sight like pillars of smoke, fragrant with myrrh, frankincense, and merchants' spices. Solomon's carriage appeared, surrounded by sixty mighty men of Israel, each trained for war and armed against the fear of night. The king had made the carriage from Lebanon wood, with silver pillars, a gold base, a purple seat, and a midst paved with love. The daughters of Zion were called to behold Solomon crowned on the day of his gladness.

The beloved praised the woman's beauty through images of doves, flocks, scarlet, pomegranate, and David's tower hung with shields. To him she was altogether beautiful. He called to his bride from Lebanon and spoke of love better than wine, fragrance richer than spices, and lips like honeycomb. Her garments carried Lebanon's fragrance.

She was a garden enclosed, a sealed fountain, an orchard of precious fruits and spices, and a well of living waters flowing from Lebanon. The woman called for north and south winds to blow upon her garden so its spices would flow, and she invited her beloved to enter and enjoy its precious fruit.

He answered that he had come into his garden, gathered myrrh and spice, eaten honey with honeycomb, and drunk wine with milk. Friends were invited to eat and drink abundantly together, sharing in love's gladness.
