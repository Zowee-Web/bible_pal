The sun, a golden crown resting upon the hills, cast long shadows as two weary disciples went heavily along the dusty path. Their hearts were as weathered stones, burdened with grief. The Master was gone, taken up into heaven, leaving them with naught but whispers and remembrance.

They had beheld him teach with gentle words and work mighty signs with a touch. He had shared parables that took hold of their minds as precious jewels. But now, with his throne in heaven and their lives unchanged, doubt gnawed at their souls as hungry wolves.

Was their faith in vain? Was their obedience not enough? The whispers seemed to echo their fears, calling to remembrance their failings. But in their despair, a memory yet flickered.

It was the day they had first met him, gathered together in the shadows of the temple. He had looked upon them with eyes of compassion, offering a warm embrace that put their fear away. He had broken bread with them as though it were the most precious thing in the world.

His words, though softly spoken, held a power that surpassed their understanding. He spake of love that crossed every border, of forgiveness that knew no end, of a kingdom wherein hearts should be made whole and tears should be wiped away.

Their tears flowed freely that day, tears of sorrow but also of hope. Hope in a promise that death could not overcome, that love could never be lost. The Master had shewn them that even in their grief, they were not alone.

As they walked, their hearts began to be made whole. The whispers grew faint, and were turned into the melody of their own remembrance. They remembered the laughter, the wisdom, the love. The Master's love, as a gentle breeze, lifted their burdens and bare them forward.

They came unto their destination, a hilltop bathed in the warm glow of the setting sun. There they found others awaiting, their faces like unto theirs, full of grief and doubting. But in the midst of them, they beheld a young child, his eyes filled with unshaken faith.

The child drew near unto the followers of the Master, and spake with a voice of conviction. "Blessed are ye that mourn, for your faith is strong. Remember, the greatest love is the love that never fadeth, the love that goeth beyond death."

His words rang out as a song, calling to remembrance the love that never departeth, the love that abideth even when the body is gone. And in that love they found their hope, their purpose, their way forward.

The voice of the child echoed through the gathering, kindling a fire of conviction within the hearts of the grieving disciples. His words called to mind the love that bound them unto their Master, a love that surpassed the things seen and lingered within the substance of their souls.

As they stood upon the hilltop, the disciples perceived that their grief was no token of weakness, but a witness of the depth of their love. They remembered the teachings of the Master, his compassion, and his steadfast love toward his followers.

Moved by the words of the child, they began to tell their stories one to another, tales of how the Master had touched their lives. They spake of the wonders they had seen, the wisdom he had shared, and the love he had poured into their hearts.

Their stories rang together, weaving a tapestry of shared remembrance. They perceived that their grief was a common thing, a witness of the deep work that their Master had wrought in their lives.

A sense of calling began to rise out of their shared sorrow. They knew they must carry forward the work of the Master, sharing his love and his teachings with the world. They must keep his memory living, kindling others to walk in his way.

Led by the words of the child, the disciples set forth upon a journey of faith and service. They visited the sick, comforted them that mourned, and shared the love of the Master with such as had need. They became as lamps of hope, putting all in remembrance that even in their darkest hours, they were not alone.

As the days passed, their grief was turned into a deep thankfulness for the privilege of having known their Master. They learned that love fadeth not, even when the body is gone. It is a timeless song that soundeth through the hearts of them that have loved and been loved.

And so the disciples went forward, led by the love of their Master and the steadfast faith of a young child. They became living witnesses of the power of love to heal, to kindle, and to make all things new.
