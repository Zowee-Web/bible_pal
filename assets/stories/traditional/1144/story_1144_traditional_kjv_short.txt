When Ahab returned to Jezreel, he told Jezebel all that Elijah had done, and how the prophets of Baal had been put to the sword. Then Jezebel sent a messenger unto Elijah. She said, "So let the gods do to me, and more also, if I make not thy life as the life of one of them by to morrow about this time."

When Elijah heard her words, he arose and went for his life. He came to Beersheba in Judah, and left his servant there. But he himself went a day's journey into the wilderness, and the sun was hot above him.

He came and sat down under a juniper tree, and he was alone. He requested for himself that he might die, and said, "It is enough; now, O Lord, take away my life; for I am not better than my fathers." Then he was silent under the open sky, and the wind moved across the sand.

He lay down and slept under the juniper tree. And behold, an angel of the Lord touched him, and said unto him, "Arise and eat."

He looked, and lo, there was a cake baken on the coals at his head, and a cruse of water. He did eat and drink, and laid him down again upon the dry ground. He slept once more.

The angel of the Lord came again the second time, and touched him, and he opened his eyes. The angel said, "Arise and eat; because the journey is too great for thee."

So he arose, and did eat and drink, and went in the strength of that meat forty days and forty nights, unto Horeb the mount of God. The wilderness was vast about him, and he walked alone. The days passed slowly under the sun, and the rocks were warm beneath his feet.
