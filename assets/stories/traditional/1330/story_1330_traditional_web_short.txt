The Psalm was the only one of David's writings that he called by the name "praise." He had written it in his old age, when his hand was no longer steady on the harp, but his voice still rose in the morning and in the evening. He had set it down as an acrostic, line by line through the letters of the alphabet, so that no part of his praise would be left unsaid. And on the day he finished it, he stood, and he sang it.

I will exalt you, my God, the King. I will praise your name forever and ever.

Every day I will praise you. I will extol your name forever and ever.

Great is Yahweh, and greatly to be praised! His greatness is unsearchable.

One generation will commend your works to another, and will declare your mighty acts.

Of the glorious majesty of your honor, of your wondrous works, I will meditate.

Men will speak of the might of your awesome acts. I will declare your greatness.

They will utter the memory of your great goodness, and will sing of your righteousness.

And the Psalm was given to the singers. The harp was set down. The old king sat in the late light. The words went out into Israel, generation after generation — fathers teaching sons, mothers teaching daughters, the people of Yahweh declaring his mighty acts. And so it was kept in Israel from then on.
