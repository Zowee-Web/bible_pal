The Psalm was the only one of David's writings that he called by the name "praise." He had written it in his old age, when his hand was no longer steady upon the harp, but his voice yet arose in the morning and in the evening. He had set it down as an acrostic, line by line through the letters of the alphabet, that no part of his praise should be left unsaid. And on the day he finished it, he arose, and he sang it.

I will extol thee, my God, O king; and I will bless thy name for ever and ever.

Every day will I bless thee; and I will praise thy name for ever and ever.

Great is the Lord, and greatly to be praised; and his greatness is unsearchable.

One generation shall praise thy works to another, and shall declare thy mighty acts.

I will speak of the glorious honour of thy majesty, and of thy wondrous works.

And men shall speak of the might of thy terrible acts: and I will declare thy greatness.

They shall abundantly utter the memory of thy great goodness, and shall sing of thy righteousness.

And the Psalm was given unto the singers. The harp was set down. The old king sat in the late light. The words went forth into Israel, generation after generation — fathers teaching sons, mothers teaching daughters, the people of the Lord declaring his mighty acts. And so it was kept in Israel from then on.
