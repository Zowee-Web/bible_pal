Paul wrote of the earthly body as a tent that could be dissolved, yet he set beside it a building from God, eternal in the heavens and not made with hands. In the present tent there was groaning and burden, with a longing to be clothed with the heavenly dwelling so that mortality might be swallowed up by life. Paul wrote that God had “made us for this very thing” and also gave to them the down payment of the Spirit.

Therefore Paul spoke with courage. While at home in the body, believers were absent from the Lord, walking by faith rather than sight. Paul was willing rather to be absent from the body and to be at home with the Lord. Whether at home or absent, his aim was to be well pleasing to Christ. Everyone would be revealed before Christ’s judgment seat, and each would receive according to what had been done in the body, whether good or bad.

Knowing the fear of the Lord, Paul persuaded men. He wrote that “we are revealed to God,” and hoped they were revealed also in the Corinthians’ consciences. He was not commending himself again, but giving them an answer for those who boasted in appearance and not in heart. If they were beside themselves, it was for God. If they were of sober mind, it was for the Corinthians.

The love of Christ compelled them because one died for all; therefore all died. Christ died so that those who lived would no longer live for themselves, but for the one who died and rose for them. From then on, they would know no one according to the flesh. If anyone was in Christ, he is a new creation. Old things had passed away, and all things had become new.

All things are of God. Through Jesus Christ, God reconciled them to himself and gave to them the ministry of reconciliation. God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself, not reckoning to them their trespasses, and committed to them the word of reconciliation. Paul wrote, “We are therefore ambassadors on behalf of Christ, as though God were entreating by us.” Their message was: be reconciled to God. The one who knew no sin was made sin on their behalf, so that in him they might become God’s righteousness.
