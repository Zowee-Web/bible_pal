Paul wrote of the earthly body as a tabernacle that might be dissolved, yet he set beside it a building of God, eternal in the heavens and not made with hands. In this tabernacle there was groaning and burden, with an earnest desire to be clothed upon with the house from heaven, that mortality might be swallowed up of life. God had wrought them for the selfsame thing and had given unto them the earnest of the Spirit.

Therefore Paul spoke with confidence. Whilst at home in the body, believers were absent from the Lord, walking by faith and not by sight. Paul was willing rather to be absent from the body and present with the Lord. Whether present or absent, he laboured to be accepted of Christ. All must appear before the judgment seat of Christ, that every one might receive according to the things done in the body, whether good or bad.

Knowing the terror of the Lord, Paul persuaded men. He was made manifest unto God and trusted that he was manifest also in the Corinthians’ consciences. He commended not himself again, but gave them an answer for those who gloried in appearance and not in heart. If they were beside themselves, it was to God; if they were sober, it was for the Corinthians’ cause.

The love of Christ constrained them because one died for all; therefore all were dead. Christ died that they which lived should not henceforth live unto themselves, but unto him which died for them and rose again. They henceforth knew no man after the flesh. If any man was in Christ, he was a new creature: old things had passed away, and all things had become new.

All things were of God. By Jesus Christ, God had reconciled them to himself and had given unto them the ministry of reconciliation. God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto himself, not imputing their trespasses unto them, and had committed unto them the word of reconciliation. Therefore they were ambassadors for Christ, as though God did beseech by them: be ye reconciled to God. He who knew no sin was made sin for them, that they might be made the righteousness of God in him.
