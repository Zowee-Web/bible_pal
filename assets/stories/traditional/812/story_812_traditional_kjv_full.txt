On a bright and gentle morning, a shepherd led his flock of an hundred sheep across the green hillside. The sun arose above the hills, pouring forth light upon the scattered stones and fresh grass. The sheep moved together, making soft bleating sounds. The shepherd knew each one by its face, by its wool, even by the sound of its steps upon the ground. The wind was merry, blowing softly through the grass and stirring the wool of the sheep. The quiet bleats rose up into the still air, as the sheep nibbled and wandered in the open land.

But as the shepherd kept watch over his flock, one little sheep drifted away. It wandered past a heap of stones and followed a butterfly across the field. It marked not how far it went. Anon, the lost sheep lifted up its eyes and saw no other sheep round about. The gentle breeze bare no familiar bleats unto its ears. Instead, it heard only the rustling grass and a bird singing from a tall bush. The hillside that had felt so safe and warm now stretched wide and empty before the small, woolly sheep.

Back with the main flock, the shepherd counted his sheep, one by one. He counted each soft back and each pair of bright eyes. But when he had finished counting, there were but ninety and nine. The shepherd's heart leaped within him—one was missing. He searched the flock again, parting the sheep with careful hands. Still, there was a space where that one small sheep should have been. The rest of the sheep huddled together, not knowing what had befallen their brother. The shepherd stood very still. The grass brushed gently against his sandals. Then, without tarrying, he made his choice.

He left the ninety and nine sheep upon the open hillside, trusting that together, they should be safe. The shepherd's eyes searched the rough land. He hearkened for any faint bleat that might be borne over the rocks and grass. He went into the wild places where the grass grew thin and the ground became rocky and steep. His feet felt each pebble, and his cloak caught the wind as he searched high and low. Birds scattered as he drew near, and thorny bushes brushed against his legs. Still, he hearkened and searched, calling out with a warm and gentle voice, that the sheep might know he was near.

At length, from behind a great stone, he heard a weak bleat. It was the lost sheep, sore afraid yet alive. Its wool was tangled with seeds and its face covered with dust, but the moment it beheld the shepherd, it ceased its crying. The shepherd kneeled down and gently took it up. He brushed the dust from its nose and wrapped his arms about its trembling body. The lost sheep felt the safety of the shepherd's strong arms and laid its head upon his shoulder. Then, with joy filling his heart, the shepherd arose. He laid the sheep upon his shoulders, where it might feel his warmth and savour the familiar scent of home.

Step by step, the shepherd bare the sheep back over the hills, through the grass, and returned unto the others. His heart was full of gladness. When he came home, the shepherd called together his friends and neighbours. His voice was loud and glad as he said, "Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost." The others gathered, smiling and clapping in delight. Far greater was his joy over finding that one lost sheep than for the ninety and nine that abode safe together upon the hillside.

Likewise, joy shall be in heaven over one that is found, more than over many who went not astray. Even as the shepherd rejoiced for his lost sheep, so great is the gladness when even one that wandereth far away is brought back again.

That evening, as twilight softened the hills, the flock gathered close beneath the shepherd's watchful gaze. The little sheep nestled safely beside its companions, soothed by the warmth of familiar bodies and the shepherd's gentle hand. In the golden quiet, peace settled over them all. The shepherd smiled, thankful for every woolly head among the flock. In that tender hush, hope lingered, promising that none should ever be forgotten.
