Upon a gentle hillside covered with green grass, a shepherd kept watch over his flock as the morning sun arose above the world. The fields stretched out as far as any sheep could wander. In every quarter, the air was filled with the sound of wind rustling through tall grass and the gentle bleating of an hundred sheep. The shepherd loved each and every one of his sheep. He counted them under his breath, that he might be sure they were all near at hand and safe. They nibbled upon sweet plants under the wide open sky, their woolly backs glowing soft and white. Beyond the grassy fields stood quiet hills, dotted with stones and wildflowers, where bushes hid in the shadows and the ground became uneven and rough. The shepherd's heart was peaceful as he kept watch, his eyes ever moving, that no sheep should drift too far from the rest.

In the flock, one little sheep grew curious concerning the world beyond the soft, safe grass. The sheep had ever abode near the others, sometimes nudging close unto its friends, other times exploring the shade of a tree or the edge of the flock. But on this day, as the shepherd counted and the other sheep grazed, the little sheep felt something calling from beyond the meadow. Mayhap it was the bright blue of a distant flower, or a patch of fresh grass that seemed even softer than where it stood. Quietly, almost unmarked, the little sheep began to wander away from the ninety and nine. Its tiny hooves bare it through patches of wildflowers and then onto rocky ground, where the grass grew thinner.

Step by step, the little sheep moved further and further away. The comfort of familiar voices faded behind it. In their place came new sounds—the whisper of wind through tall, dry grass, the chirping of birds hidden among the stones, and the crunch of pebbles beneath its feet. The path was no longer smooth. Stones poked at its soft ankles, and bushes tugged at its wool. The sun shone bright, but here the shadows seemed longer, the world larger and more strange. The little sheep looked back, but it could not see the others. For the first time, its heart skipped with worry.

In that moment, as the flock grazed peacefully, the shepherd began to count again. He called quietly, hearkening for a returning bleat. Ninety and eight…ninety and nine…but there was no hundredth sheep. His face grew grave, and he looked round about the hillside. The wind bare only the sounds of the grass and the faintest echo. He turned his head, his eyes searching the horizon, and called again. Still, the little sheep answered not. The shepherd's heart beat faster, for every sheep in his care mattered much unto him, and he knew one was missing.

Leaving the rest of the flock in the peaceful meadow, the shepherd stepped carefully away from the ninety and nine, following the narrow trail where the grass was pressed down by tiny hooves. He passed blooming flowers and stones warm from the sun. The sheep in the flock watched him go, their tails swishing and their eyes gentle, safe together upon the soft field. The shepherd's sandals brushed against the pebbles. His staff helped him walk along the steep, uneven ground. He hearkened as the breeze whispered and waited, hoping to hear the familiar sound of the lost sheep. Though the way was rough, and the ground sometimes hard to cross, he did not stop. His strong arms were ready to bear the little sheep home again.

Then, out upon the wild hillside, he heard it—a faint bleat, borne upon the wind. The shepherd's face lit with hope. He called the lost sheep's name again, moving faster toward the sound. He climbed over stones and through prickly bushes, never giving over. At length, in a patch of shade beneath a little tree, he beheld the lost sheep standing still, its wool a little rumpled. The sheep's eyes showed worry, but as it beheld the shepherd, it trusted him straightway. The shepherd smiled with great joy, his warm hands reaching out gently. He lifted the little sheep and laid it upon his strong shoulders, where it felt most safe. The lost sheep could feel the shepherd's kindness and the steady beat of his heart through the soft wool.

With the lost sheep safely upon his shoulders, the shepherd turned back toward the sunlit hills and green grass. Each step felt lighter than before, though he bare the added weight, for his heart was filled with gladness. The shepherd walked with care, as the little sheep nuzzled against his neck, warm and safe. The breeze brushed against them as they returned unto the meadow, where the ninety and nine sheep stood waiting. The other sheep beheld and gave soft, merry bleats when they saw their friend was come home. The shepherd was overjoyed. He called unto his neighbours and friends, his voice ringing merrily across the fields, saying, "Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost."

The birds sang, and the day seemed brighter as the flock was whole again. The shepherd's laughter rose up like a song. His love for each sheep was plain, and his joy could not be hid. He cared deeply for every single one, and not one was forgotten nor left behind. The little lost sheep was safely returned, held by the gentle arms of the shepherd, safe among the green hills and its friends.

Even as the shepherd's heart was filled with gladness over finding his lost sheep, so likewise joy shall be in heaven over one that cometh back, more than over ninety and nine that needed no finding. The flock was together again, and the hillside echoed with gladness, for the shepherd had found his lost sheep, and brought it home.

The sun rose higher and warmed the grass, casting long golden stripes across the hillside. The little sheep nestled closer unto its friends, still feeling the gentle touch of the shepherd's arms. It remembered the rough ground beyond the meadow, but now the comfort of the flock soothed its worry away. Round about it, the others grazed quietly, calm and unafraid, with the shepherd's eyes watching over them all.

The shepherd kneeled by his flock, resting for a moment as the breeze fluttered through his hair. He touched each sheep as he moved among them, offering a pat here, a soft word there. Even the rambunctious lambs gathered close, drawn by his warmth and steady voice. He lifted up his wooden flute and played a soft tune, letting the gentle notes dance across the meadow. The flock hearkened and nibbled at the grass, safe beneath the open sky.

Later, as the afternoon turned the hills dusky blue, the shepherd led his flock toward home. The little sheep kept close unto his side, no longer curious for distant places, only content to follow whither he walked. Night birds called from the shadows, but the gentle ring of the shepherd's staff promised safety with every step. The flock moved together, each sheep finding its place, all of them secure.

When they were come unto the fold, the shepherd led his sheep one by one through the gate, that he might be sure none was missing this time. The little sheep trotted through last, pausing to look back upon the wide hills. It felt a new peace, knowing the kindness of its shepherd could always find a lost soul, no matter how far it might wander.

As the stars glimmered above, the shepherd settled at the gate, smiling as he watched over his flock. Safe and whole, the sheep nestled in the sweet hay, and the quiet hope of the hillside wrapped itself round them as a soft blanket. In that quiet night, every heart—shepherd and sheep—rested easy, knowing they belonged and should never be lost in the fields alone.
