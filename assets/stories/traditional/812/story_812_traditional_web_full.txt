On a bright, gentle morning, a shepherd guided his flock of one hundred sheep across the green hillside. The sun rose above the hills, pouring light over the scattered rocks and fresh grass. The sheep moved together, making soft bleating sounds. The shepherd knew each one by its face, by its wool, even by the sound of its steps on the ground. The wind was playful, blowing softly through the grass and ruffling the sheep’s wool. The quiet bleats lifted into the still air, as the sheep nibbled and wandered in the open land.

But as the shepherd watched over his flock, one little sheep drifted away. It wandered past a cluster of rocks and followed a butterfly across the field. It did not notice how far it went. Soon, the lost sheep looked up and saw no other sheep around. The gentle breeze carried no familiar bleats to its ears. Instead, it heard only the rustling grass and a bird singing from a tall bush. The hillside that had felt so safe and warm now stretched wide and empty before the small, woolly sheep.

Back with the main flock, the shepherd counted his sheep, one by one. He counted each soft back and each pair of bright eyes. But when he finished counting, there were just ninety-nine. The shepherd’s heart leaped—one was missing. He searched the flock again, parting the sheep with careful hands. Still, there was a space where that one small sheep should be. The rest of the sheep huddled together, not knowing what had happened to their brother. The shepherd stood very still. The grass brushed gently against his sandals. Then, without waiting, he made a choice.

He left the ninety-nine sheep on the open hillside, trusting that together, they would be safe. The shepherd’s eyes scanned the rough land. He listened for any faint bleat that might carry over the rocks and grass. He walked into the wild places where the grass grew thin and the ground became rocky and steep. His feet felt each pebble, and his cloak caught the wind as he searched high and low. Birds scattered as he walked near, and thorny bushes brushed against his legs. Still, he listened and searched, calling out with a warm and gentle voice, making sure the sheep would know he was near.

At last, from behind a large stone, he heard a weak bleat. It was the lost sheep, frightened but alive. Its wool was tangled with seeds and its face covered in dust, but the moment it saw the shepherd, it stopped crying. The shepherd knelt down and gently picked it up. He brushed the dust from its nose and wrapped his arms around its trembling body. The lost sheep felt the safety of the shepherd’s strong arms and rested its head against his shoulder. Then, with joy filling his heart, the shepherd stood. He placed the sheep on his shoulders, where it could feel his warmth and smell the familiar scent of home.

Step by step, the shepherd carried the sheep back over the hills, through the grass, and returned to the others. His heart was full of happiness. When he reached his home, the shepherd called to his friends and neighbors. His voice was loud and glad as he said, “Rejoice with me! I have found my sheep which was lost!” The others gathered, smiling and clapping in delight. Far greater was his joy over finding that one lost sheep than for the ninety-nine that stayed safe together on the hillside.

Even so, there is more joy in heaven over one who is found than over many who did not stray. Just as the shepherd rejoiced for his lost sheep, so great is the happiness when even one who wanders far away is brought back again.

That evening, as twilight softened the hills, the flock huddled close under the shepherd’s watchful gaze. The little sheep nestled safely beside its companions, soothed by the warmth of familiar bodies and the shepherd’s gentle hand. In the golden quiet, peace settled over them all. The shepherd smiled, grateful for every woolly head among the flock. In that tender hush, hope lingered, promising that none would ever be forgotten.
