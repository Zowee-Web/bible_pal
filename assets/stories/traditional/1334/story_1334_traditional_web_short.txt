The servant had taken the road from Hebron in the south up to Aram-naharaim in the north — a journey of many weeks, with ten camels and their drivers, and with the gifts of his master Abraham. He had stopped at the well outside the city of Nahor, and he had made the camels kneel down, and he had stood by the well in the evening when the women came out to draw water. And he had spoken in his heart to Yahweh, the God of his master Abraham, and he had said, "Let the one whom you have appointed for Isaac come, and let her give me water, and offer to water the camels also." And before he had finished praying, Rebekah had come out, the daughter of Bethuel, the son of Milcah, the wife of Nahor, the brother of Abraham. She had filled her pitcher. She had given him to drink. She had drawn water for all the camels until they had finished drinking. And then he had asked her whose daughter she was, and she had told him.

The man bowed his head, and worshiped Yahweh.

He said, "Blessed be Yahweh, the God of my master Abraham, who has not forsaken his loving kindness and his truth toward my master. As for me, Yahweh has led me on the way to the house of my master's relatives."

And the servant lifted up his head. The camels were finished drinking. The evening was deepening over the well. Rebekah stood near him, her pitcher empty now. The bracelets on her wrist caught the last of the light. And the servant stood there in the gratitude of one whose journey had been answered. And so it was at the well outside Nahor, in the days when Yahweh led the servant of Abraham to the house of Rebekah.
