The servant had taken the road from Hebron in the south up unto Aram-naharaim in the north — a journey of many weeks, with ten camels and their drivers, and with the gifts of his master Abraham. He had stayed at the well without the city of Nahor, and he had made the camels to kneel down, and he had stood by the well in the evening when the women came out to draw water. And he had spoken in his heart unto the Lord, the God of his master Abraham, and he had said, Let her that thou hast appointed for Isaac come, and let her give me drink, and offer to water the camels also. And before he had done speaking, behold, Rebekah came out, the daughter of Bethuel, the son of Milcah, the wife of Nahor, Abraham's brother. She had filled her pitcher. She had given him to drink. She had drawn water for all the camels until they had done drinking. And then he had asked her whose daughter she was, and she had told him.

And the man bowed down his head, and worshipped the Lord.

And he said, Blessed be the Lord God of my master Abraham, who hath not left destitute my master of his mercy and his truth: I being in the way, the Lord led me to the house of my master's brethren.

And the servant lifted up his head. The camels had done drinking. The evening was deepening over the well. Rebekah stood near him, her pitcher empty now. The bracelets upon her wrist caught the last of the light. And the servant stood there in the gratitude of one whose journey had been answered. And so it was at the well without Nahor, in the days when the Lord led the servant of Abraham unto the house of Rebekah.
