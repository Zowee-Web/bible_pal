A voice came unto Isaiah, and it told him what he should say. "Comfort ye, comfort ye my people," it said. "Speak ye comfortably to Jerusalem. Cry unto her, that her warfare is accomplished, that her iniquity is pardoned, that she hath received of the LORD's hand double for all her sins."

The voice told him what should be said in the years to come. There should be one crying in the wilderness. He should say: "Prepare ye the way of the LORD. Make straight in the desert a highway for our God. Every valley shall be exalted. Every mountain and hill shall be made low. The crooked shall be made straight. The rough places shall be made plain. And the glory of the LORD shall be revealed. All flesh shall see it together."

Then the voice bade him speak again, and the words it gave him were sharp and were carried upon a wind. "All flesh is grass. All the goodliness thereof is as the flower of the field. The grass withereth. The flower fadeth. The Spirit of the LORD bloweth upon it. But the word of our God shall stand for ever."

Then the voice softened, and the last part of what was said was the part that should be remembered longest.

"O Zion, that bringest good tidings, get thee up into the high mountain. Lift up thy voice with strength. Be not afraid. Say unto the cities of Judah: Behold your God."

"Behold," the voice said, "the Lord GOD will come with strong hand. His arm shall rule for him. His reward is with him. He shall feed his flock like a shepherd. He shall gather the lambs with his arm. He shall carry them in his bosom. He shall gently lead those that are with young."

The voice fell silent at the end of that. Isaiah stood where he had been standing. The country opened in front of him — the long road unto the city, the houses by the highway, the lambs in the small flocks set out across the hills. He saw the shepherds among them. He saw that which the voice had told him should be seen.
