A voice came to Isaiah, and it told him what to say. "Comfort, comfort my people," it said. "Speak comfortably to Jerusalem. Cry to her that her warfare has been finished, that her iniquity has been pardoned, that she has received from the LORD's hand double for all her sins."

The voice told him what would be said in the years to come. There would be one crying out in the wilderness. He would say: "Prepare the way of the LORD. Make a highway in the desert for our God. Every valley shall be lifted up. Every mountain and hill shall be made low. The crooked shall be made straight. The rough places shall be made plain. And the glory of the LORD shall be revealed. All flesh together shall see it."

Then the voice told him to speak again, and the words it gave him were sharp and were carried on a wind. "All flesh is grass. All its glory is as the flower of the field. The grass withers. The flower fades. The breath of the LORD blows upon it. But the word of our God shall stand forever."

Then the voice softened, and the last part of what was said was the part that would be remembered longest.

"Get up onto a high mountain, O Zion, you that bring good news. Lift up your voice with strength. Be not afraid. Say to the cities of Judah: Behold your God."

"Behold," the voice said, "the Lord GOD will come as a mighty one. His arm rules for him. His reward is with him. He will feed his flock like a shepherd. He will gather the lambs with his arm. He will carry them in his bosom. He will gently lead those that have their young."

The voice fell silent at the end of that. Isaiah stood where he had been standing. The country opened in front of him — the long road to the city, the houses by the highway, the lambs in the small flocks set out across the hills. He saw the shepherds among them. He saw what the voice had told him should be seen.
