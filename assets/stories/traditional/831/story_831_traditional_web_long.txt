Two years had passed since Joseph last spoke to Pharaoh’s cupbearer. The underground prison where Joseph lived was dark and heavy with the scent of damp stone. Far above him, in the sunlit halls of Egypt’s palace, Pharaoh rested restlessly, plagued by dreams that would not leave him.

One night, Pharaoh slept and dreamed. He remembered each vision clearly, as though it burned into his mind. He stood beside the Nile, the river’s surface shimmering under the Egyptian dawn. From the river rose seven cows, sleek and healthy, feeding among the reeds. Behind them, seven other cows emerged—thin, gaunt, and ugly. These stood beside the first. Suddenly, the seven lean cows devoured the seven fat cows, yet remained as thin as before. Pharaoh awoke. Confused, he drifted back to sleep.

He dreamed again. This time he saw seven ears of grain, plump and good, growing on a single stalk. After them sprang up seven thin, scorched ears. The thin ears swallowed up the full ones. Pharaoh woke, his spirit troubled and breath uneasy.

Morning soon dawned. Sunlight slanted through carved windows into the great halls. Pharaoh’s servants moved quietly, but the king’s restlessness was unmistakable. Summoning magicians and wise men from across Egypt, Pharaoh told his dreams to them in turn. They listened carefully, yet none could give an answer that satisfied. The golden court, normally full of certainty and confidence, was now heavy with anxious whispers. No one understood what the dreams meant.

Then, a voice interrupted the distressed murmurs. The chief cupbearer, having served Pharaoh faithfully since his own release from prison, remembered something. A memory from two years past returned and pressed on his conscience. Carefully, he stepped forward and spoke with humility.

“I remember my faults today,” the cupbearer said to Pharaoh. “When Pharaoh was angry with his servants, you put me and the chief baker in custody in the captain of the guard’s house. We dreamed a dream in one night—he and I—each with a dream of its own meaning. There was with us a young man, a Hebrew, servant to the captain of the guard. We told him our dreams, and he interpreted them for us. As he interpreted, so it happened. I was restored to my office, and the baker was hanged.”

The cupbearer’s words sent a ripple through the chamber. Pharaoh heard and acted at once. He sent for Joseph without delay.

In the lower depths of the palace, Joseph was quickly summoned from his cell. Men came to him and told him to prepare at once. He washed, shaved, and changed his clothing. The linen was bright and clean compared to prison rags. The light above ground was sharp in his eyes after so long in shadow. At last, he was led into Pharaoh’s throne room—a place flooded with sunlight, gilded pillars, and the cool fragrance of myrrh.

Joseph stood before Pharaoh.

Pharaoh looked at the young Hebrew, dressed now in foreign garments, and said, “I have dreamed a dream, and there is no one who can interpret it. I have heard it said of you that you can hear a dream and interpret it.”

Joseph answered directly, his voice steady. “It is not in me. God will give Pharaoh an answer of peace.”

Pharaoh leaned forward and recounted his dreams. “In my dream, I stood on the bank of the Nile. Out of the river came seven cows, fat and attractive, and they fed in the reeds. Then seven other cows came up behind them, poor and very ugly—leaner than any I have seen in Egypt. The thin cows ate up the first seven cows, yet after they had eaten them, no one would have known they had done so. They were still thin and ugly, as at the beginning. Then I awoke.”

He continued, “I dreamed again and saw in my dream seven ears of grain, full and good, growing on one stalk. After them came up seven ears, withered, thin, and scorched by the east wind. The thin ears devoured the seven good ears. I have told this to the magicians, but there is no one who can explain it to me.”

The throne room was still. The gold and marble seemed colder with waiting. Joseph listened to every word. Then he spoke.

“The dreams of Pharaoh are one. God has shown Pharaoh what he is about to do. The seven good cows are seven years. The seven good ears are also seven years. The dreams are one.”

Joseph’s words moved slowly through the hall, echoed by the silent courtiers.

“The seven thin and ugly cows that came up after them are seven years. So also are the seven empty ears, blighted by the east wind—they represent seven years of famine.”

Joseph looked directly at Pharaoh. “It is as I told you—God has shown Pharaoh what he is about to do. Behold, there come seven years of great plenty throughout all the land of Egypt. After them will arise seven years of famine. All plenty will be forgotten in the land of Egypt, and the famine will consume the land. The plenty will not be known in the land by reason of the famine that will follow, for it shall be very grievous.”

Stepping forward, Joseph’s words were clear and sure. “The dream was repeated to Pharaoh twice, because the thing is established by God, and God will shortly bring it to pass.”

Now Joseph counseled Pharaoh with wisdom from above. “Let Pharaoh look for a discreet and wise man and set him over the land of Egypt. Let Pharaoh do this: appoint overseers over the land and take up the fifth part of the produce of the land of Egypt during the seven plentiful years. Let them gather all the food of these good years that come, and lay up grain under Pharaoh’s hand, to be kept in the cities. That food shall be for a reserve against the seven years of famine which shall be in the land of Egypt, so that the land does not perish through the famine.”

The air lingered between the spoken words and the awaiting reply. The magicians and servants looked to their king. Pharaoh consulted with all his servants and considered the matter. The throne room, once harsh with tension, now filled with a new, cautious hope.

Pharaoh turned to Joseph. “Can we find such a one as this, a man in whom is the Spirit of God?”

He looked upon Joseph with favor and spoke clearly so all could hear. “Because God has shown you all of this, there is no one so discreet and wise as you. You shall be over my house, and according to your word shall all my people be ruled. Only in the throne I will be greater than you.”

Pharaoh lifted his signet ring, the sign of royal authority, and placed it on Joseph’s hand. The ring was heavy and cold, its gold glinting in the sunlight. Servants watched as the exchange was made, the symbols of power passing from one hand to another.

The scent of incense drifted through the room. Pharaoh declared to all those present: “See, I have set you over all the land of Egypt.”
