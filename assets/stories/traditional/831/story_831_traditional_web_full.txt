In the land of Egypt, years passed, and Pharaoh dreamed two dreams. In the first dream, he stood by the river. Out of the river came seven cows, well-favored and fat, grazing among the reeds. Then, seven other cows came up after them, poor and very thin, such as he had never seen in all Egypt for badness. The thin cows ate up the seven fat cows. Pharaoh woke, troubled.

He slept again and dreamed a second time. Seven heads of grain, full and good, grew on one stalk. Then, seven heads, thin and blasted by the east wind, sprung up after them. The thin heads swallowed up the seven good heads of grain. Pharaoh awoke; it was a dream.

In the morning, his spirit was troubled. Pharaoh summoned all the magicians of Egypt and all its wise men. He told them his dreams, but none could interpret them for him. The court was restless; whispers moved through the golden halls, but no answer brought relief.

Then the chief cupbearer remembered. He stepped forward and spoke to Pharaoh. “I remember my faults today,” he said. “When Pharaoh was angry with his servants and put me and the chief baker in the custody of the captain of the guard’s house, we each dreamed a dream one night, he and I. There was with us there a young man, a Hebrew, servant to the captain of the guard. We told him our dreams, and he interpreted them for us, giving each an interpretation. And exactly as he interpreted, so it happened. I was restored to my office, and the baker was hanged.”

Pharaoh listened. He gave orders at once, and they hurried to bring Joseph out of the prison. Joseph was quickly shaved and given clean clothing. He was brought from the dimness and stone walls of the prison into the light of Pharaoh’s hall, where pillars gleamed and servants stood attentive.

Pharaoh spoke to Joseph. “I have dreamed a dream, and there is no one who can interpret it. I have heard it said of you that when you hear a dream you can interpret it.”

Joseph answered Pharaoh, calmly, “It is not in me. God will give Pharaoh an answer of peace.”

Pharaoh began to recount his dreams. “In my dream I stood upon the bank of the river. Out of the river came seven cows, fat and well-favored; they fed in the reeds. Seven other cows came up after them, poor and thin, such as I never saw in all the land of Egypt. The thin cows ate up the seven fat cows. When they had eaten them, it could not be known that they had eaten them, for they were still just as thin and poor as at the beginning. So I awoke.

“I saw in my dream, and, behold, seven heads of grain, full and good, sprang up on one stalk. Then, seven heads, withered, thin, blasted by the east wind, grew up after them. The thin heads swallowed up the seven good heads. I told this unto the magicians, but none could explain it to me.”

Joseph listened. The golden court seemed to wait with him. Then he spoke. “The dreams of Pharaoh are one. God has shown Pharaoh what he is about to do. The seven good cows are seven years, and the seven good heads are seven years. The dream is one. The seven thin and ugly cows that came up after them are seven years, and the seven empty heads of grain, blasted by the east wind, are also seven years of famine. This is the thing I have spoken to Pharaoh: What God is about to do he shows to Pharaoh.

“Behold, there come seven years of great plenty throughout all the land of Egypt. After them shall arise seven years of famine, and all the plenty shall be forgotten in the land of Egypt. The famine will consume the land. The plenty will not be known in the land because of that famine which follows, for it will be very grievous. The dream was doubled to Pharaoh, because the thing is established by God, and God will shortly bring it to pass.”

Joseph continued, his voice steady in the bright hall. “Now therefore let Pharaoh look for a discreet and wise man, and set him over the land of Egypt. Let Pharaoh appoint officers over the land and take up the fifth part of the produce of the land of Egypt in the seven plentiful years. Let them gather all the food of these good years and store grain under the hand of Pharaoh, food in the cities for preservation. That food shall be for a store, against the seven years of famine which shall be in the land of Egypt, so that the land does not perish through the famine.”

This saying pleased Pharaoh and all his servants. They regarded Joseph and said, “Can we find such a one as this, a man in whom is the Spirit of God?”

Pharaoh spoke to Joseph, “Since God has shown you all of this, there is no one as discreet and wise as you. You shall be over my house, and all my people shall be ruled according to your word. Only in the throne will I be greater than you.” Pharaoh said to Joseph, “See, I have set you over all the land of Egypt.”
