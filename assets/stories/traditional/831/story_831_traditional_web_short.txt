Two years passed. In the land of Egypt, Pharaoh had two troubling dreams. In the first, he stood by the river. Seven fat, healthy cows came up from the water and grazed in the grass. Then, seven other cows—thin and ugly—came up behind them, standing by the first ones. The thin cows ate up the plump cows, but still looked just as thin as before. Pharaoh woke, disturbed.

He slept again and dreamed a second time. This time, seven full heads of grain, healthy and good, grew on a single stalk. Then, seven thin, scorched heads sprang up and devoured the full ones. Pharaoh woke again. In the morning, his spirit was troubled. He called all his magicians and wise men, but none could interpret the dreams for him.

The chief cupbearer stood in Pharaoh's golden hall. He remembered a man he once met in prison. Speaking to Pharaoh, he said, "Today I remember my offenses. When Pharaoh was angry with his servants, I and the chief baker were in custody. We each had dreams in the same night, and a young Hebrew, servant to the captain of the guard, interpreted them. For each, as he interpreted, so it happened. I was restored to my office. The baker was hanged."

Quickly, Pharaoh sent for Joseph. They brought him out of the prison. He shaved, changed his clothes, and stood before Pharaoh—stepping from cold stone into the brilliance of Pharaoh’s court.

Pharaoh said, "I have dreamed a dream. No one can interpret it. I have heard it said of you, that if you hear a dream, you can interpret it." Joseph replied, "It isn’t in me. God will give Pharaoh an answer of peace."

Pharaoh recounted his dreams—the cows, the grain, the devouring, the emptiness after. Joseph listened, then spoke. "The dreams of Pharaoh are one. God has shown Pharaoh what he is about to do. The seven good cows and the seven good heads are seven years of abundance. The seven thin, ugly cows and the seven empty heads are seven years of famine."

He continued. "There will come seven years of great plenty throughout Egypt, followed by seven years of famine. The famine will be severe. God has shown this to Pharaoh twice, for it is certain and God will soon bring it to pass. Now let Pharaoh select a wise and discerning man, set him over the land, and let overseers gather food during the years of plenty to store against the years of famine."

Pharaoh and his servants agreed it was good. Pharaoh asked, "Can we find a man like this, in whom is the Spirit of God?" He turned to Joseph. "Since God has shown you all this, there is none as discerning and wise as you. You shall be over my house. By your word my people will be ruled. Only in regard to the throne will I be greater than you." Pharaoh said to Joseph, "See, I have set you over all the land of Egypt."
