The prophet stood before the people. The people were a long way from home. They were they that had said the Lord did not see them.

The prophet spake.

"Who hath measured the waters in the hollow of his hand?"

He held out his own hand. A man's hand. The size of it. The hollow of it. Then he set the question down beside that hand and let it lie.

"And meted out heaven with the span? Who hath comprehended the dust of the earth in a measure? Who hath weighed the mountains in scales, and the hills in a balance?"

The questions did not require an answer. They were not the kind of questions that sought an answer. They were the kind of questions that opened a door within him that heard them, and let the size of the room behind the door begin to show.

"Who hath directed the Spirit of the Lord, or being his counsellor hath taught him? With whom took he counsel, and who instructed him, and taught him in the path of judgment?"

None. The prophet said not, None. He let the silence say it.

Then he turned unto the nations. The great kingdoms. The men that sat upon thrones in distant courts and decreed the fates of peoples like the prophet's own.

"Behold, the nations are as a drop of a bucket, and are counted as the small dust of the balance. Behold, he taketh up the isles as a very little thing. And Lebanon is not sufficient to burn, nor the beasts thereof sufficient for a burnt offering. All nations before him are as nothing; and they are counted to him less than nothing, and vanity."

The drop of a bucket. The small dust upon the scales. The isles taken up as pebbles in a man's hand. The cedars of Lebanon — the forests that crowned the north and roofed the temples of kings — not sufficient fuel for the altar of the One that sat above all of it.

"To whom then will ye liken God? Or what likeness will ye compare unto him?"

The prophet let the question stand. Then he answered it himself, in the voice that was not his own.

"To whom then will ye liken me, or shall I be equal? — saith the Holy One."

The Holy One had not ceased speaking. The voice came on through the prophet.

"Lift up your eyes on high, and behold who hath created these things, that bringeth out their host by number. He calleth them all by names. By the greatness of his might, for that he is strong in power — not one faileth."

The prophet pointed. He pointed upward. The stars above the people's tents. The great host of light marching forth each night across the sky. Each one called by name. Each one accounted for.

Then the prophet turned unto the voice that had been muttering within the camp — the voice that had brought him hither in the first place.

"Why sayest thou, O Jacob, and speakest, O Israel — 'My way is hid from the Lord, and my judgment is passed over from my God'? Hast thou not known? Hast thou not heard?"

He said it twice. He said it the way a teacher repeateth a thing that should already have been remembered.

"The everlasting God, the Lord, the Creator of the ends of the earth, fainteth not, neither is weary. There is no searching of his understanding. He giveth power to the faint; and to them that have no might he increaseth strength."

Then he brought it down unto the bodies of them that heard him.

"Even the youths shall faint and be weary, and the young men shall utterly fall. But they that wait upon the Lord shall renew their strength. They shall mount up with wings as eagles. They shall run, and not be weary. They shall walk, and not faint."

The last word fell upon the still air. The camp held it. The stars went on burning above them.
