The prophet stood before the people. The people were a long way from home. They were the ones who had said that the Lord did not see them.

The prophet spoke.

"Who has measured the waters in the hollow of his hand?"

He held out his own hand. A man's hand. The size of it. The hollow of it. Then he set the question down beside that hand and let it lie.

"Who has marked off the sky with his span? Who has calculated the dust of the earth in a measure? Who has weighed the mountains in scales, and the hills in a balance?"

The questions did not need an answer. They were not the kind of questions that wanted an answer. They were the kind of questions that opened a door inside the one who heard them, and let the size of the room behind the door begin to show.

"Who has directed the Spirit of the Lord? Or has taught him as his counselor? Who did he take counsel with, and who instructed him, and taught him in the path of justice?"

No one. The prophet did not say no one. He let the silence say it.

Then he turned to the nations. The great kingdoms. The men who sat on thrones in distant courts and decided the fates of peoples like the prophet's own.

"Behold, the nations are like a drop in a bucket. They are regarded as a speck of dust on a balance. Behold, he lifts up the islands like a very little thing. Lebanon is not sufficient to burn, nor its animals sufficient for a burnt offering. All the nations are like nothing before him. They are regarded by him as less than nothing, and vanity."

The drop in the bucket. The speck of dust on the scales. The islands lifted like pebbles in a man's hand. The cedars of Lebanon — the forests that crowned the north and roofed the temples of kings — not enough fuel for the altar of the One who sat above all of it.

"To whom then will you liken God? Or what likeness will you compare to him?"

The prophet let the question stand. Then he answered it himself, in the voice that was not his own.

"To whom then will you liken me, that I should be equal to him? — says the Holy One."

The Holy One was not done speaking. The voice came on through the prophet.

"Lift up your eyes on high, and see who has created these. Who brings out their army by number. He calls them all by name. By the greatness of his might, and because he is strong in power — not one is lacking."

The prophet pointed. He pointed upward. The stars above the people's tents. The great army of light marching out each night across the sky. Each one called by name. Each one accounted for.

Then the prophet turned to the voice that had been muttering inside the camp — the voice that had brought him here in the first place.

"Why do you say, Jacob, and speak, Israel — 'My way is hidden from the Lord, and the justice due me is disregarded by my God'? Have you not known? Have you not heard?"

He said it twice. He said it the way a teacher repeats a thing that should already have been remembered.

"The everlasting God, the Lord, the Creator of the ends of the earth, doesn't faint. He isn't weary. His understanding is unsearchable. He gives power to the weak. He increases the strength of him who has no might."

Then he brought it down to the bodies of those who were hearing him.

"Even the youths faint and get weary, and the young men utterly fall. But those who wait for the Lord will renew their strength. They will mount up with wings like eagles. They will run, and not be weary. They will walk, and not faint."

The last word fell on the still air. The camp held it. The stars went on burning above them.
