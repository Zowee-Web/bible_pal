The Lord gave unto Moses the appointed feasts that Israel was to proclaim as holy convocations. Each week began with the order of six days for work and a seventh day as a sabbath of solemn rest in all their dwellings.

The yearly appointments opened in the first month. The Lord's passover came on the fourteenth day at even, followed by seven days of unleavened bread. The first and seventh days were holy convocations, with servile work set aside and offerings presented unto the Lord.

When Israel entered the land and reaped its harvest, the first sheaf belonged before the Lord. The priest was to wave it on the morrow after the sabbath, together with the appointed burnt, meat, and drink offerings. No bread, parched corn, or green ears were to be eaten before this gift was brought.

From that day Israel counted seven complete sabbaths, numbering fifty days in all even unto the morrow after the seventh sabbath. At the Feast of Weeks they brought two loaves as firstfruits with the commanded offerings and assembled in holy convocation. Even at harvest, the corners and gleanings of the field were to remain for the poor and the stranger.

The seventh month held further appointed times. Its first day was a sabbath, a memorial of blowing trumpets, and an holy convocation. On the tenth day came the Day of Atonement. The people were to afflict their souls and cease from every manner of work from even unto even while atonement was made before the Lord.

On the fifteenth day the Feast of Tabernacles began. For seven days Israel offered unto the Lord and rejoiced before him, with a sabbath on the first day and again on the eighth. Israelites born were to dwell in booths so later generations would know that the Lord made Israel dwell in booths when he brought them out of Egypt. Moses declared these feasts unto the people.
