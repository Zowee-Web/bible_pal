Yahweh gave Moses the appointed times that Israel was to proclaim as holy convocations. Each week began with the pattern of six days for work and a seventh day of solemn Sabbath rest in every dwelling.

The yearly appointments opened in the first month. Passover came on the fourteenth day at evening, followed by seven days of Unleavened Bread. The first and seventh days were holy convocations, with regular work set aside and offerings presented to Yahweh.

When Israel entered the land and reaped its harvest, the first sheaf belonged before Yahweh. The priest was to wave it on the day after the Sabbath, together with the appointed burnt, meal, and drink offerings. No bread or grain from that harvest was to be eaten before this gift was brought.

From that day Israel counted seven complete Sabbaths, fifty days in all, reaching the day after the seventh Sabbath. At the Feast of Weeks they brought two loaves as firstfruits with the prescribed offerings and gathered in holy convocation. This statute continued throughout their generations. Even at harvest, the field's corners and gleanings were to remain for the poor and the foreigner.

The seventh month held further appointed times. Its first day was a solemn rest, a trumpet memorial, and a holy convocation. On the tenth day came the Day of Atonement. The people were to deny themselves and cease from every kind of work from evening to evening while atonement was made before Yahweh.

On the fifteenth day the Feast of Booths began. For seven days Israel offered to Yahweh and rejoiced before him, carrying fruit and branches from majestic trees, with solemn rest on the first day and again on the eighth. Native-born Israelites lived in temporary shelters so later generations would know that Yahweh made Israel dwell in shelters when he brought them out of Egypt. Moses declared these appointed feasts to the people.
