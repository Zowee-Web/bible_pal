Jeremiah had stood in the court of the Lord's house and spoken what the Lord had given him to speak — that if the people did not amend their ways, the Lord would make this house like Shiloh, and the city would be a curse unto all the nations of the earth. When he had made an end of speaking, the priests and the prophets and all the people had laid hold on him, saying, "Thou shalt surely die." And the princes of Judah had come up from the king's house to sit in judgment at the entry of the new gate. And Jeremiah stood before them. And the priests and the prophets called for his death; and the prophet answered them.

Then spake Jeremiah unto all the princes and to all the people, saying, "The Lord sent me to prophesy against this house and against this city all the words that ye have heard.

Therefore now amend your ways and your doings, and obey the voice of the Lord your God; and the Lord will repent him of the evil that he hath pronounced against you.

As for me, behold, I am in your hand: do with me as seemeth good and meet unto you.

But know ye for certain, that if ye put me to death, ye shall surely bring innocent blood upon yourselves, and upon this city, and upon the inhabitants thereof: for of a truth the Lord hath sent me unto you to speak all these words in your ears."

And the princes and all the people heard him. They turned unto the priests and the prophets and said, "This man is not worthy to die; for he hath spoken unto us in the name of the Lord our God." And certain of the elders of the land arose and remembered Micah the Morasthite, which had prophesied in the days of Hezekiah; and Jeremiah was given back his life. And so it was in the days when Jeremiah stood at the new gate of the Lord's house and was not put to death.
