In the days after the day of Pentecost, the believers in Jerusalem were many. They met together daily in the courts of the temple, and in their houses, and in the streets near the porch of Solomon. The multitude of those who believed were of one heart and soul.

Not one of them claimed that anything of the things which he possessed was his own. They had all things in common. When a man had wheat, it was carried into the houses of those who had no wheat. When a woman had oil, she filled the lamps of those who had no oil. The believers continued together.

With great power, the apostles gave their testimony of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus. They spoke in the courts where the Sanhedrin had warned them not to speak. They spoke in the streets. They spoke at the well in the morning when the women came down for water. Great grace was on them all.

There was no one among them who lacked.

Those who were owners of lands or houses sold them. They brought the proceeds of the things that were sold, and they laid them at the apostles' feet. Distribution was made to each, according as anyone had need.

One of those who did this had a name they had given him in those days.

He had been born Joses, a Levite, a man of Cyprus by race. He had come up to Jerusalem with the others and had heard the apostles' testimony. When he had come into the assembly, the apostles had seen in him a quiet steadiness. He came alongside the new believers when they were afraid. His voice lifted the discouraged. He spoke kindly when others did not know what to say.

They had called him Barnabas, which is, being interpreted, Son of Encouragement.

Barnabas had a field somewhere outside the city. It had belonged to his people. It was the kind of inheritance a Levite would have been expected to keep — a piece of ground that connected him to his fathers' land.

He sold the field.

He brought the money to Jerusalem. He came into the place where the apostles met. He laid the money down at their feet, on the floor where the other proceeds had been laid down.

He did not speak much about what he had done. He went back to the work of encouragement.

The money lay at the apostles' feet, ready to be given to those who had need. And the man who had brought it was now known among them by the name the apostles had given him in the assembly at Jerusalem.
