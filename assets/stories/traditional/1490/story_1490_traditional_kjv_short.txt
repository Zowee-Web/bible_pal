In the days after the day of Pentecost, the believers in Jerusalem were many. The multitude of them that believed were of one heart and of one soul.

Neither said any of them that ought of the things which he possessed was his own. They had all things common.

And with great power gave the apostles witness of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus. And great grace was upon them all.

Neither was there any among them that lacked.

For as many as were possessors of lands or houses sold them, and brought the prices of the things that were sold, and laid them down at the apostles' feet. And distribution was made unto every man according as he had need.

One of them that did this had a name they had given him in those days.

He had been born Joses, a Levite of Cyprus. When he had come into the assembly in Jerusalem, the apostles had seen in him a quiet steadiness, a willingness to come alongside the new believers when they were afraid, a voice that lifted up them that were cast down. They had surnamed him Barnabas — the son of consolation.

Barnabas had land somewhere without the city. It had belonged to his people. It was the manner of inheritance a Levite would have been expected to keep.

He sold the land.

He brought the money unto the apostles in Jerusalem. He laid it down at their feet, upon the floor where the other prices had been laid down.

He spake not much about what he had done. He went back unto the work of encouragement. The believers continued together.

The money lay at the apostles' feet, ready to be given unto them that had need. And the man who had brought it became known by the name that had been given him — the son of consolation.
