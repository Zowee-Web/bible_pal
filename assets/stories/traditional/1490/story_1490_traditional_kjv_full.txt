In the days after the day of Pentecost, the believers in Jerusalem were many. They met together daily in the courts of the temple, and in their houses, and in the streets near the porch of Solomon. The multitude of them that believed were of one heart and of one soul.

Neither said any of them that ought of the things which he possessed was his own. They had all things common. When a man had wheat, it was carried into the houses of them that had no wheat. When a woman had oil, she filled the lamps of them that had no oil. The believers continued together.

And with great power gave the apostles witness of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus. They spake in the courts where the Sanhedrin had warned them not to speak. They spake in the streets. They spake at the well in the morning when the women came down for water. And great grace was upon them all.

Neither was there any among them that lacked.

For as many as were possessors of lands or houses sold them, and brought the prices of the things that were sold, and laid them down at the apostles' feet. And distribution was made unto every man according as he had need.

One of them that did this had a name they had given him in those days.

He had been born Joses, a Levite, and of the country of Cyprus. He had come up unto Jerusalem with the others and had heard the apostles' witness. When he had come into the assembly, the apostles had seen in him a quiet steadiness. He came alongside the new believers when they were afraid. His voice lifted up them that were cast down. He spake kindly when others knew not what to say.

They had surnamed him Barnabas, which is, being interpreted, The son of consolation.

Barnabas had land somewhere without the city. It had belonged to his people. It was the manner of inheritance a Levite would have been expected to keep — a piece of ground that connected him unto his fathers' land.

He sold the land.

He brought the money unto Jerusalem. He came into the place where the apostles met. He laid the money down at their feet, upon the floor where the other prices had been laid down.

He spake not much about what he had done. He went back unto the work of encouragement.

The money lay at the apostles' feet, ready to be given unto them that had need. And the man who had brought it was now known among them by the name the apostles had given him in the assembly at Jerusalem.
