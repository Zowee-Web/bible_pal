In the days after the day of Pentecost, the believers in Jerusalem were many. The multitude of those who believed were of one heart and soul.

Not one of them claimed that anything of the things which he possessed was his own. They had all things in common.

With great power, the apostles gave their testimony of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus. Great grace was on them all.

There was no one among them who lacked.

Those who were owners of lands or houses sold them. They brought the proceeds of the things that were sold, and they laid them at the apostles' feet. Distribution was made to each, according as anyone had need.

One of those who did this had a name they had given him in those days.

He had been born Joses, a Levite of Cyprus. When he had come into the assembly in Jerusalem, the apostles had seen in him a quiet steadiness, a willingness to come alongside the new believers when they were afraid, a voice that lifted the discouraged. They had called him Barnabas — Son of Encouragement.

Barnabas had a field somewhere outside the city. It had belonged to his people. It was the kind of inheritance a Levite would have been expected to keep.

He sold the field.

He brought the money to the apostles in Jerusalem. He laid it down at their feet, on the floor where the other proceeds had been laid down.

He did not speak much about what he had done. He went back to the work of encouragement. The believers continued together.

The money lay at the apostles' feet, ready to be given to those who had need. And the man who had brought it was now known among them by the name the apostles had given him in the assembly at Jerusalem.
