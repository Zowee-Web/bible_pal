Paul had departed out of Ephesus to return unto Antioch and Jerusalem, and the household of Priscilla and Aquila — the tentmakers which had come with him from Corinth — abode in the city to keep the work. They went up unto the synagogue on the sabbath day as the believers did, and they returned after unto the tent-shop in the lower part of the city, where they cut and stitched the heavy cloth and the leather. On a certain sabbath they heard a new voice teaching out of the scriptures.

And a certain Jew named Apollos, born at Alexandria, an eloquent man, came to Ephesus, and he was mighty in the scriptures.

This man was instructed in the way of the Lord; and being fervent in the spirit, he spake and taught diligently the things of the Lord, knowing only the baptism of John.

And he began to speak boldly in the synagogue: whom when Aquila and Priscilla had heard, they took him unto them, and expounded unto him the way of God more perfectly.

And when he was disposed to pass into Achaia, the brethren wrote, exhorting the disciples to receive him: who, when he was come, helped them much which had believed through grace:

For he mightily convinced the Jews, and that publickly, shewing by the scriptures that Jesus was Christ.

And Apollos sailed over unto Achaia in the days that followed with the letters of the brethren in his hand. The synagogue of Ephesus kept the memory of the eloquent man that had been taken aside privately rather than corrected publickly; Priscilla and Aquila returned unto the tent-shop and unto the cutting of the leather; and the way of God that had been expounded gently in the back room of their shop bare Apollos forward into Corinth, in the quiet weeks of the late Ephesian summer.
