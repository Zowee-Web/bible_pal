Laban answered Jacob by claiming the daughters, children, flocks, and everything before him as his own. Yet he also asked what he could do that day to his daughters or to the children they had borne. Instead of continuing the dispute, Laban proposed that he and Jacob make a covenant. Their agreement would stand as a witness between them.

Jacob took a stone and set it upright as a pillar. Then he told his relatives to gather stones. They brought them together into a heap, and the families ate there beside it. Laban called the heap Jegar Sahadutha, while Jacob called it Galeed. Both names marked the stones as a witness to the covenant they were making.

Laban also called the place Mizpah. He said that Yahweh would watch between them when they were absent from one another. He warned Jacob not to afflict his daughters or take additional wives. Even if no other person stood nearby to observe them, God remained witness between the two men.

Before both households, the stones marked where each man had promised not to cross for the purpose of harm.

Laban pointed again to the heap and the pillar. He declared that neither man would cross beyond those stones to harm the other. He called on the God of Abraham, the God of Nahor, and the God of their father to judge between them. Jacob then swore by the fear of his father, Isaac.

Jacob offered a sacrifice on the mountain and invited his relatives to eat bread. They shared the meal and remained there through the night. Early the next morning, Laban rose, kissed his sons and daughters, and blessed them. Then he departed and returned to his own place. The pillar and heap remained behind as the boundary both men had accepted.
