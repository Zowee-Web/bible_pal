Laban answered Jacob and claimed the daughters, children, cattle, and all that Jacob could see as his own. Yet he also asked what he could do that day unto his daughters or unto the children they had born. Rather than continue the dispute, Laban said, “Come thou, let us make a covenant, I and thou.” Their agreement would remain for a witness between him and Jacob.

Jacob took a stone and set it up for a pillar. Then he told his brethren to gather stones. They brought the stones together and made an heap, and they ate there upon it. Laban named it Jegar-sahadutha, but Jacob called it Galeed. The heap stood before them while they declared the terms of their covenant.

Laban also called the place Mizpah, saying, “The Lord watch between me and thee, when we are absent one from another.” He warned Jacob not to afflict his daughters or take other wives beside them. Though no man might be present with them, God would still be witness betwixt the two men.

Before their brethren, the stones marked the bound neither man was to cross unto the other for harm.

Laban pointed again to the heap and the pillar cast betwixt them. He declared that he would not pass over the heap unto Jacob for harm, and Jacob would not pass over it unto him for harm. He called upon the God of Abraham, the God of Nahor, and the God of their father to judge between them. Jacob then sware by the fear of his father Isaac.

Jacob offered sacrifice upon the mount and called his brethren to eat bread. They ate and tarried all night in the mount. Early in the morning, Laban rose, kissed his sons and daughters, and blessed them. He then departed and returned unto his place. The heap and pillar remained as the witness both men had named.
