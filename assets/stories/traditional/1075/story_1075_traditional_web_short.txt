In the year that King Uzziah died, Isaiah saw the Lord.

He sat on a throne, high and lifted up. The hem of His robe filled the entire temple. It spread across the stone floor like a river of fabric, flowing into every corner.

Above the throne stood angels. They were called seraphim. Each one had six wings. Two wings covered the face. Two wings covered the feet. And with two wings, each one flew.

They called out to one another. Their voices rang through the temple like thunder rolling between mountains.

"Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts! The whole earth is full of His glory!"

The doorposts shook at the sound of their voices. The stone foundations of the temple trembled. Smoke filled the room, thick and white, curling around the pillars and drifting across the floor.

Isaiah cried out.

"Woe is me!" he said. "I am ruined! I am a man of unclean lips, and I live among a people of unclean lips. And my eyes have seen the King, the Lord of hosts!"

Then one of the seraphim flew toward him. It moved fast, wings beating the smoky air. In its hand it held a burning coal. The angel had taken the coal with tongs from the altar, lifting it from the fire that burned there.

The seraphim reached out and touched the coal to Isaiah's mouth. The hot coal pressed against his lips.

"See," the angel said. "This has touched your lips. Your guilt is taken away. Your sin is forgiven."

The smoke still swirled through the temple. The great throne still rose above. The seraphim still flew on their wide wings.

Then a voice spoke. It was the voice of the Lord.

"Whom shall I send?" the voice said. "And who will go for us?"

Isaiah stood in the temple, smoke drifting past his shoulders, the taste of fire still on his lips. He opened his mouth.

"Here am I," Isaiah said. "Send me."
