In the year that King Uzziah died, Isaiah saw the Lord.

He sat upon a throne, high and lifted up. The hem of his robe filled the whole temple. It spread across the stone floor like a river of fabric, flowing into every corner.

Above the throne stood angels. They were called seraphim. Each one had six wings. With twain he covered his face. With twain he covered his feet. And with twain he did fly.

They cried one unto another. Their voices rang through the temple like thunder rolling betwixt the mountains.

"Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts! The whole earth is full of his glory!"

The posts of the door shook at the sound of their voices. The stone foundations of the temple trembled. Smoke filled the house, thick and white, curling round about the pillars and drifting across the floor.

Isaiah cried out.

"Woe is me!" he said. "For I am undone! For I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips. For mine eyes have seen the King, the Lord of hosts!"

Then one of the seraphim flew unto him. He moved swiftly, his wings beating the smoky air. In his hand he held a live coal. The angel had taken the coal with the tongs from off the altar, lifting it from the fire that burned there.

The seraphim reached out and laid the coal upon Isaiah's mouth. The burning coal pressed against his lips.

"Lo," the angel said. "This hath touched thy lips. Thine iniquity is taken away. Thy sin is purged."

The smoke yet swirled through the temple. The great throne yet rose above. The seraphim yet flew upon their wide wings.

Then a voice spake. It was the voice of the Lord.

"Whom shall I send?" the voice said. "And who will go for us?"

Isaiah stood in the temple, smoke drifting past his shoulders, the taste of fire yet upon his lips. He opened his mouth.

"Here am I," Isaiah said. "Send me."
