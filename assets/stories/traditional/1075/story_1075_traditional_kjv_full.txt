In the year that King Uzziah died, Isaiah saw the Lord.

King Uzziah had ruled in Judah for a very long time. He had sat upon a throne in Jerusalem for many, many years. But now Uzziah was no more. And in that year, Isaiah beheld a thing no man could have looked for.

He saw the Lord sitting upon a throne.

It was high. It was lifted up far above the floor. The hem of his robe filled the whole temple. The fabric spread across the stone like a river without end, flowing over the steps and pooling against the walls. It reached into every corner. It covered all things.

Above the throne stood angels. They were called seraphim. They were tall and bright, and each one had six wings. With twain he covered his face. With twain he covered his feet. And with the last twain, each one did fly. They hovered above the throne, their wings beating slowly, holding them in the smoky air.

They cried one unto another.

Their voices were loud. They were louder than any sound Isaiah had ever heard. They echoed off the stone walls. They shook the ceiling beams.

"Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts!" they cried. "The whole earth is full of his glory!"

The posts of the door trembled at the sound. The thick wooden frames rattled in their stone sockets. The foundations of the temple shook beneath Isaiah's feet. He could feel the floor moving. The ground itself seemed to answer the angels' cry.

Smoke filled the house.

It rose from the altar and curled round about the tall pillars. It drifted past the seraphim and their wide, moving wings. It rolled across the floor and swallowed the walls. The temple became a place of smoke and sound and shaking stone.

Isaiah cried out.

"Woe is me!" he said. His voice brake in the smoky air. "For I am undone! For I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips."

He stood there, small in the great room, his chest rising and falling.

"For mine eyes," he said, "have seen the King — the Lord of hosts!"

Then one of the seraphim flew down from above. His wings cut through the smoke. He came swiftly, moving toward Isaiah with purpose. In his hand he carried a live coal. The coal glowed orange and red. The seraphim had taken it with the tongs from off the altar — lifted it straight from the fire that burned there among the stones.

The angel reached out.

He laid the burning coal against Isaiah's mouth. The hot coal touched his lips. Isaiah felt it there, the heat against his skin.

"Lo," the angel said. His voice was clear and close. "This hath touched thy lips. Thine iniquity is taken away. Thy sin is purged."

The seraphim lifted the coal away and flew back upward, his wings spreading wide. The smoke yet moved through the temple. The great throne yet rose above all things. The other seraphim yet hovered and flew, their six wings moving in the haze.

Isaiah stood still. The heat lingered upon his lips.

Then a voice spake. It came from the throne. It filled the whole temple as the robe filled the floor.

"Whom shall I send?" the voice said. "And who will go for us?"

The words hung in the smoky air. The seraphim were quiet now. The shaking had ceased. The temple was still, save for the slow drift of smoke betwixt the pillars.

Isaiah lifted up his face toward the throne. His mouth opened — the same mouth the coal had touched, the same lips the fire had cleansed.

"Here am I," Isaiah said. "Send me."
