In the year that King Uzziah died, Isaiah saw the Lord.

King Uzziah had ruled in Judah for a very long time. He had sat on a throne in Jerusalem for many, many years. But now Uzziah was gone. And in that year, Isaiah saw something no one could have expected.

He saw the Lord sitting on a throne.

It was high. It was lifted up far above the floor. The hem of God's robe filled the whole temple. The fabric spread across the stone like a river with no end, flowing over the steps and pooling against the walls. It reached into every corner. It covered everything.

Above the throne stood angels. They were called seraphim. They were tall and bright, and each one had six wings. Two wings covered the face. Two wings covered the feet. And with the last two wings, each one flew. They hovered above the throne, their wings beating slowly, holding them in the smoky air.

They called out to one another.

Their voices were loud. They were louder than anything Isaiah had ever heard. They echoed off the stone walls. They shook the ceiling beams.

"Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts!" they cried. "The whole earth is full of His glory!"

The doorposts trembled at the sound. The thick wooden frames rattled in their stone sockets. The foundations of the temple shook beneath Isaiah's feet. He could feel the floor moving. The ground itself seemed to answer the angels' cry.

Smoke filled the room.

It rose from the altar and curled around the tall pillars. It drifted past the seraphim and their wide, moving wings. It rolled across the floor and swallowed the walls. The temple became a place of smoke and sound and shaking stone.

Isaiah cried out.

"Woe is me!" he said. His voice broke in the smoky air. "I am ruined! I am a man of unclean lips, and I live among a people of unclean lips."

He stood there, small in the great room, his chest rising and falling.

"And my eyes," he said, "have seen the King — the Lord of hosts!"

Then one of the seraphim flew down from above. Its wings cut through the smoke. It came quickly, moving toward Isaiah with purpose. In its hand it carried a burning coal. The coal glowed orange and red. The seraphim had taken it with tongs from the altar — lifted it straight from the fire that burned there among the stones.

The angel reached out.

It pressed the burning coal against Isaiah's mouth. The hot coal touched his lips. Isaiah felt it there, the heat against his skin.

"See," the angel said. Its voice was clear and close. "This has touched your lips. Your guilt is taken away. Your sin is forgiven."

The seraphim lifted the coal away and flew back upward, its wings spreading wide. The smoke still moved through the temple. The great throne still rose above everything. The other seraphim still hovered and flew, their six wings moving in the haze.

Isaiah stood still. The heat lingered on his lips.

Then a voice spoke. It came from the throne. It filled the whole temple the way the robe filled the floor.

"Whom shall I send?" the voice said. "And who will go for us?"

The words hung in the smoky air. The seraphim were quiet now. The shaking had stopped. The temple was still, except for the slow drift of smoke between the pillars.

Isaiah lifted his face toward the throne. His mouth opened — the same mouth the coal had touched, the same lips the fire had cleaned.

"Here am I," Isaiah said. "Send me."
