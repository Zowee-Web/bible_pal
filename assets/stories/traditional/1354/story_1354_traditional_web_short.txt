The Sabbath was coming. The cross stood on Golgotha and the body of Jesus had been there for some hours. The disciples had scattered. Peter had wept bitterly in the dark of the courtyard and was now hidden somewhere in the city. The women who had followed Jesus from Galilee stood at a distance and watched. And in the gathering of the council, one of the prominent members who had not consented to the death of Jesus, a man named Joseph of Arimathea, looked for the Kingdom of God. The hour was late, and what he was about to do would mark him before all of Jerusalem. And he went in to Pilate.

When evening had now come, because it was the Preparation Day, that is, the day before the Sabbath,

Joseph of Arimathaea, a prominent council member who also himself was looking for God's Kingdom, came. He boldly went in to Pilate, and asked for Jesus' body.

Pilate marveled if he were already dead; and summoning the centurion, he asked him whether he had been dead long.

When he found out from the centurion, he granted the body to Joseph.

He bought a linen cloth, and taking him down, wound him in the linen cloth, and laid him in a tomb which had been cut out of a rock. He rolled a stone against the door of the tomb.

And Joseph went home. The tomb in the rock was sealed by the stone he had rolled against it, and the body that had been left on the cross now lay wrapped in the linen cloth he had bought, and the Sabbath was beginning in the city. The man who had not consented to the council's verdict had taken the verdict upon himself in a different way. And so it was in the days when Joseph of Arimathea boldly asked Pilate for the body of Jesus.
