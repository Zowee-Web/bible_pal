After the city of Jerusalem had fallen, and the people had been carried away into captivity, they that remained gathered together among the broken stones of the city. They lifted up their voices in lamentation before the Lord, and the words of their grief were written down.

They prayed, "Remember, O Lord, what is come upon us: consider, and behold our reproach. Our inheritance is turned to strangers, our houses to aliens."

"We are orphans and fatherless, our mothers are as widows. We have drunken our water for money; our wood is sold unto us."

"Our necks are under persecution: we labour, and have no rest. We have given the hand to the Egyptians, and to the Assyrians, to be satisfied with bread."

"Our fathers have sinned, and are not; and we have borne their iniquities. Servants have ruled over us: there is none that doth deliver us out of their hand."

"We gat our bread with the peril of our lives because of the sword of the wilderness. Our skin was black like an oven because of the terrible famine."

"The crown is fallen from our head: woe unto us, that we have sinned! For this our heart is faint; for these things our eyes are dim. Because of the mountain of Zion, which is desolate, the foxes walk upon it."

Then they that remained lifted up their voices together: "Thou, O Lord, remainest for ever; thy throne from generation to generation. Wherefore dost thou forget us for ever, and forsake us so long time?"

"Turn thou us unto thee, O Lord, and we shall be turned; renew our days as of old."

The wind moved through the broken walls. The stones of the temple lay where they had fallen, and the watchmen of the city were no more. The men of Jerusalem sat upon the ground, and they arose not.

The day waxed warm above the ruined houses. The words of their lamentation went up unto the Lord, and the people which remained kept the prayer in their hearts.
