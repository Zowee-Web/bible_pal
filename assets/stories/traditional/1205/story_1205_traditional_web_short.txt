After the city of Jerusalem had fallen, and the people had been carried away into exile, those who remained gathered among the broken stones of the city. They lifted up their voices in lament before the Lord, and the words of their grief were written down.

They prayed, "Remember, Lord, what has come on us. Look, and see our reproach. Our inheritance has been turned over to strangers, and our houses to aliens."

"We have become orphans, and our fathers are no more. Our mothers are as widows. We must pay for water to drink. Our wood is sold to us."

"Our pursuers are on our necks. We are weary, and have no rest. We have given our hand to the Egyptians, and to the Assyrians, to be satisfied with bread."

"Our fathers sinned, and are no more. We have borne their iniquities. Servants rule over us. There is no one to deliver us out of their hand."

"We get our bread at the peril of our lives, because of the sword of the wilderness. Our skin is hot like an oven, because of the burning heat of famine."

"The crown has fallen from our head. Woe to us, for we have sinned. For this our heart is faint. For these things our eyes are dim. For the mountain of Zion lies desolate. The foxes walk on it."

Then those who had remained lifted their voices together: "You, Lord, remain forever. Your throne is from generation to generation. Why do you forget us forever, and forsake us for so long a time?"

"Turn us to yourself, Lord, and we shall be turned. Renew our days as of old."

The wind moved through the broken walls. The stones of the temple lay where they had fallen, and the watchmen of the city were gone. The men of Jerusalem sat upon the ground, and they did not rise.

The day grew warm above the ruined houses. The words of their lament went up to the Lord, and the people who had remained kept the prayer in their hearts.
