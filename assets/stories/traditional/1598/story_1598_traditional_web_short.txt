Ehud was a left-handed man, and that was how he carried a hidden sword.

Israel had done evil in the sight of Yahweh, and for eighteen years they served Eglon, the king of Moab. But when they cried out to Yahweh, he raised up a deliverer: Ehud son of Gera, of the tribe of Benjamin. When Israel sent their tribute to Eglon, they sent it by Ehud's hand.

Ehud made himself a two-edged sword, a cubit long, and strapped it under his clothes on his right thigh. He brought the tribute to Eglon, who was a very fat man. Then he sent away the men who had carried it.

But Ehud himself turned back at the carved idols near Gilgal. "I have a secret message for you, O king," he said. The king called for silence, and all his attendants left him. Ehud came to him as he sat alone in his cool upper room. "I have a message from God for you," Ehud said. As the king rose from his seat, Ehud reached with his left hand, drew the sword from his right thigh, and thrust it into his belly. The fat closed over the blade, and he did not pull it out.

Ehud went out to the porch, shut the doors of the upper room, and locked them. When the servants came and found the doors locked, they thought the king was relieving himself. They waited until they were embarrassed, and still the doors stayed shut. At last they took the key and opened them, and there was their lord, fallen dead on the floor.

By then Ehud had escaped. He passed the idols and came to Seirah, and there he blew a trumpet in the hill country of Ephraim. Israel came down with him. "Follow me," he said, "for Yahweh has delivered your enemies the Moabites into your hand." They seized the fords of the Jordan and let no one cross, and they struck down about ten thousand Moabites, every strong and able man; not one escaped. So Moab was subdued that day, and the land had rest for eighty years.
