After five days, Ananias the high priest descended with elders and an orator named Tertullus. They brought their case against Paul before the governor Felix.

Tertullus opened with praise for Felix, then accused Paul of being a pestilent fellow, a mover of sedition among Jews throughout the world, a ringleader of the sect of the Nazarenes, and one who had gone about to profane the temple. He also spoke of the chief captain Lysias taking Paul from their hands. The Jews assented, saying these things were so.

Felix beckoned unto Paul to speak. Paul answered, “I know that thou hast been of many years a judge unto this nation.” Only twelve days had passed since he went up to Jerusalem to worship. His accusers had not found him disputing in the temple or raising up the people in the synagogues or the city. Neither could they prove their charges.

Paul did confess that after the way they called heresy, he worshipped the God of his fathers. He believed all things written in the law and the prophets and had hope toward God that there would be a resurrection of the dead, both just and unjust. He exercised himself to keep a conscience void of offence toward God and men.

Paul had returned after many years to bring alms to his nation and offerings. Certain Jews from Asia had found him purified in the temple, neither with multitude nor tumult. Those men were not there to object. The men before Felix could name only Paul’s cry before the council: “Touching the resurrection of the dead I am called in question by you this day.”

Felix had more perfect knowledge of that way and deferred the case until Lysias came. He commanded a centurion to keep Paul, to let him have liberty, and to forbid none of his acquaintance to minister unto him.

Later Felix and his wife Drusilla heard Paul concerning faith in Christ. As Paul reasoned of righteousness, temperance, and judgment to come, Felix trembled and sent him away until a convenient season. Felix also expected money to be given him of Paul, so he called for him often and communed with him.

After two years, Porcius Festus came into Felix’s room. Felix, willing to shew the Jews a pleasure, left Paul bound.
