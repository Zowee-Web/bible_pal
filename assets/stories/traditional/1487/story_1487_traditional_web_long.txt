The three men had come up to Abraham in the heat of the day, by the oaks of Mamre. Abraham had run to meet them when he saw them standing by his tent. He had bowed himself to the earth. He had brought water, and bread, and a calf tender and good, and butter and milk. Sarah had laughed inside the tent at what she heard them say to Abraham about the son she would bear at her old age.

The men had eaten. The afternoon had moved on.

Now the men rose up from the place where Abraham had given them food. They looked toward Sodom in the valley below the hills. Abraham walked out with them to see them on their way.

Yahweh did not hide from Abraham what he was about to do.

For Abraham would surely become a great and mighty nation, and all the nations of the earth would be blessed in him. He had been known by Yahweh, that he might command his children and his household after him to keep the way of Yahweh, to do righteousness and justice. So that Yahweh might bring on Abraham what he had spoken concerning him.

Yahweh said, "Because the cry of Sodom and Gomorrah is great, and because their sin is very grievous, I will go down now, and see whether their deeds are as bad as the reports which have come to me. If not, I will know."

The two men who had been with Yahweh turned from there. They went on down the slope of the hills, toward the city of Sodom in the valley below. Abraham could see them going as he stood.

But Abraham stood yet before Yahweh.

He did not turn back to his tent. He did not call after the two men. He stood where he had been standing, with the road of Sodom in front of him and the road of Mamre behind him, and the hills of his sojourning around him.

Abraham came near.

He said, "Will you sweep away the righteous with the wicked? What if there are fifty righteous within the city? Will you consume and not spare the place for the fifty righteous who are in it? Be it far from you to do things like that, to kill the righteous with the wicked, so that the righteous should be like the wicked. May that be far from you. Shouldn't the Judge of all the earth do right?"

The question had been bigger than the count of fifty. The question had been about what Yahweh was. Abraham had said it once, and it could not be unsaid.

Yahweh said, "If I find in Sodom fifty righteous within the city, then I will spare the whole place for their sake."

Abraham heard the answer.

He took a breath. He looked at the two men still moving toward Sodom in the valley.

He spoke again. This time more carefully. The thing he had asked had been granted. He had not asked it once and gone home. He had decided to ask further. And he had decided to ask in a smaller voice, the voice of a man who knew he had already received more than he had any right to receive.

He said, "See now, I have taken it on myself to speak to the Lord, although I am dust and ashes. What if there will lack five of the fifty righteous? Will you destroy all the city for lack of five?"

The smaller drop. The careful step downward. Not from fifty to twenty in a single move. From fifty to forty-five. As if to ask whether the answer would hold for the lost five.

He said, "I will not destroy it if I find forty-five there."

Abraham spoke to him yet again. "What if there are forty found there?"

He said, "I will not do it for the forty's sake."

There was a silence after that answer. The kind of silence in which a man knows he is asking again, and knows he should not be asking again, and knows that he must.

The two men were further down the slope now. Abraham could just see them where the road bent.

He said, "Oh don't let the Lord be angry, and I will speak. What if there are thirty found there?"

He had crossed a number now. He had crossed from forty into thirty. The qualifier before the question was longer. Abraham had felt the lengthening himself, and he had spoken it out.

He said, "I will not do it if I find thirty there."

Abraham did not stop. He gathered the next ask before he could lose his nerve.

"See now, I have taken it on myself to speak to the Lord. What if there are twenty found there?"

He said, "I will not destroy it for the twenty's sake."

The silence after this one was longer.

Abraham knew he would ask once more. He knew it would be the last. He said it in the voice of a man who knew he was at the edge of what he could ask, and who had decided that the edge was where he should make his last stand.

"Oh don't let the Lord be angry, and I will speak just once more. What if ten are found there?"

He said, "I will not destroy it for the ten's sake."

Abraham did not ask for fewer than ten. He did not ask for one righteous man. He stopped at ten. There was a line he could not cross, and he had found it.

Then Yahweh went his way, as soon as he had finished communing with Abraham.

Abraham did not call after him.

He stood for a long moment in the place where Yahweh had been, with the wind in the high grass of the plains of Mamre, and the two men still walking toward Sodom in the valley below — smaller now, almost at the edge of what his eyes could hold.

Then he turned. He returned to his place — to his tent, to Sarah within it, to the work of the evening that always came after the day.

The road back to the tent was longer than the road out had been.

He did not yet know what the morning would show him. He did not yet know whether the ten would be found. He had asked for what he could ask for, and the asking was finished, and now the matter was in the hand of the One he had stood before.
