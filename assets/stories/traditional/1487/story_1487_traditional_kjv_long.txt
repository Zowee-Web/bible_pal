The three men had come up unto Abraham in the heat of the day, by the oaks of Mamre. Abraham had run to meet them when he saw them standing by his tent. He had bowed himself toward the ground. He had brought water, and bread, and a calf tender and good, and butter and milk. Sarah had laughed within the tent at what she heard them say unto Abraham about the son she should bear in her old age.

The men had eaten. The afternoon had moved on.

Now the men rose up from the place where Abraham had given them food. They looked toward Sodom in the valley below the hills. Abraham went with them to bring them on the way.

The Lord did not hide from Abraham that thing which he was about to do.

For Abraham should surely become a great and mighty nation, and all the nations of the earth should be blessed in him. He had been known of the Lord, that he might command his children and his household after him to keep the way of the Lord, to do justice and judgment. That the Lord might bring upon Abraham that which he had spoken of him.

And the Lord said, "Because the cry of Sodom and Gomorrah is great, and because their sin is very grievous, I will go down now, and see whether they have done altogether according to the cry of it, which is come unto me. And if not, I will know."

The two men who had been with the Lord turned their faces from thence. They went on down the slope of the hills, toward the city of Sodom in the valley below. Abraham could see them going as he stood.

But Abraham stood yet before the Lord.

He turned not back unto his tent. He called not after the two men. He stood where he had been standing, with the road of Sodom before him and the road of Mamre behind him, and the hills of his sojourning round about him.

Abraham drew near.

He said, "Wilt thou also destroy the righteous with the wicked? Peradventure there be fifty righteous within the city. Wilt thou also destroy and not spare the place for the fifty righteous that are therein? That be far from thee to do after this manner, to slay the righteous with the wicked. And that the righteous should be as the wicked, that be far from thee. Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?"

The question had been larger than the count of fifty. The question had been about what the Lord was. Abraham had said it once, and it could not be unsaid.

And the Lord said, "If I find in Sodom fifty righteous within the city, then I will spare all the place for their sakes."

Abraham heard the answer.

He drew breath. He looked at the two men yet moving toward Sodom in the valley.

He spake again. This time more carefully. The thing he had asked had been granted. He had not asked it once and gone home. He had purposed to ask further. And he had purposed to ask in a smaller voice — the voice of a man who knew he had already received more than he had any right to receive.

He said, "Behold now, I have taken upon me to speak unto the Lord, which am but dust and ashes. Peradventure there shall lack five of the fifty righteous. Wilt thou destroy all the city for lack of five?"

The smaller drop. The careful step downward. Not from fifty unto twenty in a single move. From fifty unto forty and five. As if to ask whether the answer would hold for the lost five.

And he said, "If I find there forty and five, I will not destroy it."

And Abraham spake unto him yet again. "Peradventure there shall be forty found there."

And he said, "I will not do it for forty's sake."

There was a silence after that answer. The kind of silence in which a man knows he is asking again, and knows he ought not to be asking again, and knows that he must.

The two men were further down the slope now. Abraham could just see them where the road bent.

He said, "Oh let not the Lord be angry, and I will speak. Peradventure there shall thirty be found there."

He had crossed a number now. He had crossed from forty into thirty. The qualifier before the question was longer. Abraham had felt the lengthening himself, and he had spoken it out.

And he said, "I will not do it, if I find thirty there."

Abraham did not stop. He gathered the next asking before he could lose his nerve.

"Behold now, I have taken upon me to speak unto the Lord. Peradventure there shall be twenty found there."

And he said, "I will not destroy it for twenty's sake."

The silence after this one was longer.

Abraham knew he would ask once more. He knew it would be the last. He said it in the voice of a man who knew he stood at the edge of what he could ask, and who had purposed that the edge was where he should make his last stand.

"Oh let not the Lord be angry, and I will speak yet but this once. Peradventure ten shall be found there."

And he said, "I will not destroy it for ten's sake."

Abraham asked not for fewer than ten. He asked not for one righteous man. He stopped at ten. There was a line he could not cross, and he had found it.

And the Lord went his way, as soon as he had left communing with Abraham.

Abraham called not after him.

He stood for a long moment in the place where the Lord had been, with the wind in the high grass of the plains of Mamre, and the two men still walking toward Sodom in the valley below — smaller now, almost at the edge of what his eyes could hold.

Then he turned. He returned unto his place — unto his tent, unto Sarah within it, unto the work of the evening that always came after the day.

The road back unto the tent was longer than the road out had been.

He knew not yet what the morning should shew him. He knew not yet whether the ten should be found. He had asked for what he could ask for, and the asking was finished, and now the matter was in the hand of the One he had stood before.
