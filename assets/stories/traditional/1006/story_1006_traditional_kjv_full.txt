And it came to pass, as he journeyed toward Jerusalem, that he walked along the border country between Samaria and Galilee. The road was dry and the dust rose beneath his feet, and his disciples walked with him, their sandals scraping against the packed earth. The land stretched out on either side — terraced hillsides, low stone walls marking the edges of fields, olive trees standing in their silver-green stillness.

They drew near unto a certain village. The rooftops came into view, and the sound of voices carried from within the settlement. But before they reached the gate, there arose a cry from a distance — not from the village itself, but from the open ground beyond its edge, where ten men stood together in a ragged cluster.

They stood far off.

Their garments were torn, their skin marked and discolored with the disease that separated them from all others. Some had cloth wound about their hands. One leaned upon a staff, his legs unsteady beneath him. They kept their distance, as the law required, and none from the village stood near them.

They lifted up their voices together, and the sound carried across the dry ground between them and the road. It was loud and uneven, ten voices calling at once, some hoarse, some cracked with effort.

They cried out, saying that Jesus, Master, would have mercy upon them.

He stopped upon the road. The dust settled around his feet. He looked toward them — ten figures standing apart from the walls of the village, their faces turned toward him. The wind moved through the grass between them.

He called back to them. His voice was clear across the distance. He told them to go and show themselves unto the priests.

No hand was laid upon them. No word of healing was spoken over their flesh. He gave them only this instruction — to go.

And they turned. All ten of them turned from where they stood and began to walk. Some moved slowly, their bodies stiff. Others walked with quicker steps. They moved together along the road, heading in the direction of the priests, as he had commanded.

And it came to pass that as they went, their skin changed. The marks upon their hands faded. The roughness smoothed. The discoloration drew back like water receding from stone. One man looked down at his arm and stopped walking. Another touched his own face. Their flesh was restored — clean, whole, as it had been before the disease had come upon them.

Nine of them continued forward. Their pace quickened. Their voices rose among themselves as they pressed on toward the priests, toward the confirmation of what had already taken place upon their bodies.

But one of them, when he saw that he was healed, turned back.

He did not continue toward the priests. He turned his body around on the road and began to walk in the opposite direction, back toward the place where Jesus stood. As he drew nearer, his voice rose. He glorified God with a loud voice — not a whisper, not a murmur, but a cry that rang across the open ground, audible to the disciples and to any who stood near the village gate.

He came to where Jesus was and fell down on his face at his feet. His forehead pressed against the dust of the road. His hands lay flat upon the earth. He gave thanks unto him, his voice breaking against the ground beneath him.

And this man was a Samaritan.

Jesus spoke. He did not speak softly. Those standing near could hear every word. He asked whether there were not ten cleansed. He asked where the nine were. He asked whether none had returned to give glory to God save this stranger — this one, this Samaritan, who was not of the house of Israel.

The man remained upon the ground, his face still low, his body still bent in the posture of thanksgiving. The dust clung to his garments and to his restored skin.

Jesus spoke to him directly. He told him to arise and go his way, and he declared that the man's faith had made him whole.

The man rose from the earth. He stood upright, his face now lifted, the road stretching before him and behind him. The dust still hung faintly in the air where he had fallen. His garments were torn as before, the garments of a leper, though his flesh beneath them was clean.

He stood before Jesus for a moment, and then he went his way.

The road remained. The village stood quiet behind its walls. The nine were gone, already distant, already moving toward the priests. The border country between Samaria and Galilee lay open and still under the broad sky. Jesus turned again toward Jerusalem, and his disciples walked with him, and the dust rose once more beneath their feet as they continued on.
