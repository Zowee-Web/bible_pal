The road ran between Samaria and Galilee, a dusty track cutting through dry hills where low scrub clung to the rocky ground. Jesus walked with his disciples toward Jerusalem, their sandals scuffing against the packed earth. The afternoon air carried the smell of sun-warmed stone and wild thyme.

As they drew near a village, ten men stood at a distance along the roadside. Their clothes hung loose and stained. Patches of discolored skin marked their faces and hands — lepers, kept apart from the rest of the village. They clustered together on the far side of a low stone wall, their voices carrying across the open ground.

They lifted their voices together and called out. "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us!"

He stopped on the road and turned toward them. The wind stirred the dust between where he stood and where they waited. He looked at them — all ten, standing in a rough line, some leaning on staffs, others with cloth wrapped around their hands.

He called back to them. "Go and show yourselves to the priests."

They turned and began walking. One steadied himself against another's arm. Their feet moved along the path leading toward the village, and as they walked, the skin on their hands and faces changed. The discoloration faded. The rough, thickened patches smoothed. By the time they had gone a short distance, their skin was clean.

Nine of them kept walking, their pace quickening, their voices rising as they spoke to one another and moved toward the village.

One man stopped. He stood still on the path, looking down at his hands. He turned them over, then turned back. He came toward Jesus with a loud voice, glorifying God. When he reached the place where Jesus stood, he fell on his face at Jesus' feet, pressing his forehead to the dusty ground. He thanked him.

This man was a Samaritan.

Jesus spoke, and his words were clear enough for the disciples and anyone nearby to hear. "Weren't the ten cleansed? But where are the nine? Were there none found who returned to give glory to God, except this foreigner?"

The Samaritan remained on the ground, his hands flat against the earth on either side of his head. The dust clung to his clean skin, to his knees and the front of his robe.

Jesus spoke to him. "Get up and go your way. Your faith has made you well."

The man rose to his feet. He stood before Jesus for a moment, then turned and walked down the road. His steps were steady on the packed earth. Behind him, the village sat quiet in the afternoon light, and the road stretched on toward Jerusalem where Jesus and his disciples continued their journey.
