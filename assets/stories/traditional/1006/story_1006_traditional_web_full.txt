The road stretched between Samaria and Galilee, dry and rutted from the passage of carts and feet. Dust hung in the low places where the wind did not reach. Jesus walked with his disciples toward Jerusalem, their sandals scuffing against packed earth, the hems of their garments brushing the stones that lined the path. The sun stood high, and the shadows were short beneath the scattered trees along the roadside.

As they drew near a village, the outline of low mudbrick walls and flat rooftops came into view. Smoke from cooking fires rose thin and pale against the open air. But before Jesus and his group reached the village entrance, ten figures appeared at a distance. They stood together in a loose cluster on the bare ground off the road, well away from the path where travelers walked. Their clothes were ragged, some torn at the edges, and several had cloth wrapped around their hands or faces. Patches of discolored skin showed on their arms and necks. They were lepers.

They did not approach. They stood where they were, keeping the distance that was required of them. But they lifted their voices together, and the sound carried clearly across the open ground.

"Jesus, Master, have mercy on us!"

The cry was loud, raw, and unmistakable. A few of the disciples turned their heads toward the group. Jesus stopped walking. He looked at the ten men standing there in the dust, their faces turned toward him, their arms raised or clasped together.

He called out to them, his voice steady and clear enough to cross the distance between them.

"Go and show yourselves to the priests."

That was all he said. No gesture toward them, no command to come closer, no touch. Just those words, spoken plainly across the dry air.

The ten men turned. They began walking, some quickly, some with halting steps, moving together in the direction of the village and the road beyond it. Their sandals stirred up small clouds of dust. One leaned on another's arm. They moved as a group, heading toward the priests as they had been told.

And as they walked, it happened. The discolored patches on their skin began to change. One man stopped and looked down at his hands, turning them over. The lesions were gone. His skin was smooth and whole. Another pulled the wrapping from his face, and the flesh beneath was clean. All ten of them, as they went, were made clean.

Nine of them kept going. Their pace quickened. Their voices rose in scattered exclamations to one another as they moved down the road, heading toward the priests, toward the confirmation of what had happened, toward the life that had been closed off to them.

But one of them stopped.

He stood still on the road for a moment. Then he turned around. He began walking back the way he had come, back toward Jesus and the disciples. As he walked, he lifted his voice. It rang out loud and strong, praising God. The words echoed off the low walls of the nearest buildings and carried across the open ground. He was not quiet about it. His voice was full, filling the space around him as he moved.

When he reached Jesus, he did not remain standing. He dropped to the ground and fell on his face at Jesus' feet. His forehead pressed against the dust. His hands lay flat on the earth beside him. He stayed there, giving thanks, his voice muffled now against the ground but still audible.

He was a Samaritan.

Jesus looked at the man lying at his feet, and then he spoke. His words were directed not only to the man but to those standing nearby — the disciples, anyone within earshot.

"Weren't the ten cleansed? But where are the nine?"

No one answered. The road was quiet except for the distant sounds of the village and the faint noise of the other nine men continuing on their way.

Jesus spoke again.

"Were there none found who returned to give glory to God, except this foreigner?"

The Samaritan remained on the ground, his body still, his breathing visible in the rise and fall of his back. The dust beneath him was dry and warm.

Then Jesus spoke directly to him.

"Get up, and go your way. Your faith has made you well."

The man lifted his head. He pushed himself up from the ground, brushing the dust from his clothes and his hands. He stood before Jesus, his face clean, his skin whole, his eyes on the one who had spoken. Then he turned and walked away down the road, his steps steady on the packed earth.

Jesus and his disciples resumed their journey toward Jerusalem. The road ahead curved between low hills, and the village fell behind them. The sound of footsteps on dry ground was the only noise for a while — sandals on stone, the rustle of cloth, the ordinary rhythm of people walking together under the open sky.
