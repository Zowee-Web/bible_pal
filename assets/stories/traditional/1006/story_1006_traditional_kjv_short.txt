And it came to pass, as he journeyed toward Jerusalem, that he passed along the border between Samaria and Galilee. The road was dry and pale with dust, and the wind carried the smell of sun-warmed stone across the open ground.

He drew near unto a certain village, and before he reached the gate, ten men stood at a distance. They stood together upon the bare slope beyond the road, their garments torn and stained, their skin marked with the signs of leprosy. They kept apart from the travelers and the merchants and the women drawing water, as the law required.

They lifted their voices together, and the sound carried across the open space between them. They called out to him by name — Jesus, Master — and they asked him to have mercy upon them.

He stopped upon the road. The dust settled around his feet. He looked upon them where they stood, and he spoke plainly. He told them to go and show themselves unto the priests.

They turned and went. Their sandals scraped against the dry ground as they walked, all ten of them moving together along the path that led away from the village. And as they went, their skin changed. The marks upon their flesh cleared. The disease departed from them, every one.

One of the ten stopped. He stood still in the road. Then he turned back, and his voice rose loud, glorifying God so that those nearby could hear him. He came to the place where Jesus stood, and he fell down upon his face at his feet. His forehead pressed against the dust of the road. He gave thanks.

This man was a Samaritan.

Jesus spoke then, and those standing near could hear every word. He asked where the other nine were. He asked whether none had returned to give glory to God save this stranger alone.

The man remained upon the ground, his hands spread flat against the earth.

Jesus spoke to him directly. He told him to arise and go his way, and he declared that the man's faith had made him whole.

The Samaritan rose from the dust. He stood upon his feet. The road stretched out before him in both directions — toward the village behind and the country beyond. The sun bore down upon the road, and the wind moved across the dry grass at the edges of the path.

He walked. Behind him, Jesus continued his journey toward Jerusalem. The nine were already gone from sight, somewhere ahead on the road to the priests, their voices no longer heard. The dust settled where the Samaritan had knelt, and the village carried on its commerce and its noise beyond the gate.
