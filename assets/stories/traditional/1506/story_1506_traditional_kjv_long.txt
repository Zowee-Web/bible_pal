In the first year of Belshazzar king of Babylon, in the great city by the river, Daniel lay upon his bed. The night was quiet around the palace. The lamps in the outer rooms had been brought low. Daniel slept. And while he slept, a dream came to him.

The dream was not a small dream.

He saw visions of his head as he lay there in the night. When he waked, he sat up upon his bed in the dark, and the dream was still within him, and he let it not go. He took up his pen. He wrote the dream down. He told the sum of the matters that had passed before him. This is what he wrote.

He had seen the four winds of the heaven striving upon the great sea.

The waters had not been still. The winds had come at them from the four quarters at once — out of the north and out of the south, out of the east and out of the west — and the surface of the sea had risen and broken under the pressure of them. The sound of the wind upon the water had filled the dream.

Then, out of the sea, the beasts had come up. Four of them. Each one diverse from the others.

The first was like a lion.

It had eagle's wings. Daniel beheld, in the dream, while the wings thereof were plucked. The feathers were drawn out one and then another. The lion was lifted up from the earth. It was made to stand upon the feet as a man. A man's heart was given to it. The lion that had been a lion was no longer only a lion. Something had been put within it that had not been within it before.

The second was like to a bear.

It raised up itself on one side. Three ribs were in the mouth of it between the teeth of it. A voice spake over it from somewhere above the dream. "Arise. Devour much flesh." The bear arose. Its weight shifted. Its slow body moved forward.

The third was like a leopard.

It had upon the back of it four wings of a fowl. It had four heads. Dominion was given to it. The leopard moved with a swiftness the eye could not keep pace with. The wings opened. The four heads turned in four directions at once.

Then came the fourth.

The fourth beast was dreadful and terrible. It was strong exceedingly. The teeth thereof were of iron. It devoured. It brake in pieces what it could not swallow. It stamped the residue with the feet of it. It was diverse from all the beasts that were before it. It had ten horns. Daniel beheld the horns. While he beheld, there came up among them another little horn. Three of the first horns were plucked up by the roots before it. The little horn had eyes like the eyes of man. It had a mouth. And the mouth spake. The mouth spake great things.

Daniel beheld till the thrones were cast down.

Then the Ancient of Days did sit.

His garment was white as snow. The hair of his head was like the pure wool. His throne was like the fiery flame. The wheels thereof as burning fire. A fiery stream issued and came forth from before him. The light of it ran across the floor of the place where the throne had been set. Thousand thousands ministered unto him. Ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him in their ranks. The judgment was set. The books were opened.

The little horn still spake the great words when Daniel turned his eyes back to it.

The voice had not ceased. The mouth went on. The eyes within the horn looked out at the throne, and the throne was the answer the voice had not looked for. Daniel beheld until the fourth beast was slain because of the words the little horn spake. The body was destroyed and given to the burning flame. The fire took it. As concerning the rest of the beasts, they had their dominion taken away, yet their lives were prolonged for a season and time.

Then Daniel saw, in the night visions, one like the Son of man coming with the clouds of heaven.

He came with the clouds. He came not upon the ground. The clouds bore him forward. He came to the Ancient of Days. He was brought near before him. The throne with the fiery wheels was there, and the one like the Son of man stood before it.

There was given him dominion and glory and a kingdom — that all people, nations, and languages should serve him. The dominion was laid into his hand. The kingdom was set upon him.

His dominion was an everlasting dominion. It should not pass away. His kingdom was one that should not be destroyed.

Daniel waked.

The night was still around him. The lamps in the outer rooms were still low. He lay there with the dream still within his head — the beasts coming up out of the sea, the throne with the fiery wheels, the books opened, the one like the Son of man walking upon the clouds, the kingdom that should not pass away.

He sat up upon his bed. He took up his pen. He set the dream down in writing, as the angel had once charged him to do, that the words should not be lost. He wrote until he had told the sum of the matters. Then he laid down the pen.

The dream had been given. The dream had been written. The kingdom that was coming would come.
