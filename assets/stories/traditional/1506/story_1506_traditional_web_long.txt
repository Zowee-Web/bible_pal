In the first year of Belshazzar king of Babylon, in the great city by the river, Daniel lay upon his bed. The night was quiet around the palace. The lamps in the outer rooms had been brought low. Daniel slept. And while he slept, a dream came to him.

The dream was not a small dream.

He saw visions of his head as he lay there in the night. When he woke, he sat up on his bed in the dark, and the dream was still inside him, and he did not let it go. He took up his pen. He wrote the dream down. He told the sum of the matters that had passed before him. This is what he wrote.

He had seen the four winds of heaven striving on the great sea.

The waters had not been still. The winds had come at them from the four quarters at once — out of the north and out of the south, out of the east and out of the west — and the surface of the sea had risen and broken under the pressure of them. The sound of the wind on the water had filled the dream.

Then, out of the sea, the beasts had come up. Four of them. Each one different from the others.

The first was like a lion.

It had the wings of an eagle. Daniel watched, in the dream, while the wings were plucked from it. The feathers were drawn out one and then another. The lion was lifted up from the earth. It was made to stand on its feet like a man. A man's heart was given to it. The lion that had been a lion was no longer only a lion. Something had been put inside it that had not been inside it before.

The second was like a bear.

It raised itself up on one side. Three ribs were in its mouth, held between its teeth. A voice spoke over it from somewhere above the dream. "Arise. Devour much flesh." The bear arose. Its weight shifted. Its slow body moved forward.

The third was like a leopard.

It had four wings of a bird on its back. It had four heads. Dominion was given to it. The leopard moved with a swiftness the eye could not keep up with. The wings opened. The four heads turned in four directions at once.

Then came the fourth.

The fourth beast was dreadful and terrible. It was strong exceedingly. Its teeth were of iron. It devoured. It broke in pieces what it could not swallow. It stamped what was left of its prey beneath its feet. It was different from all the beasts that had come before it. It had ten horns. Daniel watched the horns. While he watched, another horn came up among them — a little one. Three of the first horns were plucked up by the roots before it. The little horn had eyes like the eyes of a man. It had a mouth. And the mouth was speaking. The mouth was speaking great things.

Daniel beheld in his dream until the thrones were cast down.

Then the Ancient of Days sat.

His garment was white as snow. The hair of his head was like pure wool. The throne where he sat was like the fiery flame. The wheels of the throne were burning fire. A fiery stream issued and came out from before him. The light of it ran across the floor of the place where the throne had been set. Thousand thousands ministered to him. Ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him in their ranks. The judgment was set. The books were opened.

The little horn was still speaking its great words when Daniel turned his eyes back to it.

The voice had not stopped. The mouth went on. The eyes in the horn looked out at the throne, and the throne was the answer the voice had not expected. Daniel watched until the fourth beast was slain because of the words the little horn spoke. The body was destroyed and given to the burning flame. The fire took it. The other three beasts had their dominion taken away from them, but their lives were prolonged for a season and a time.

Then Daniel saw, in the night visions, one like the Son of Man coming with the clouds of heaven.

He came on the clouds. He did not come on the ground. The clouds carried him forward. He came to the Ancient of Days. He was brought near before him. The throne with the fiery wheels was there, and the one like the Son of Man stood before it.

There was given to him dominion and glory and a kingdom — that all peoples and nations and languages should serve him. The dominion was placed into his hand. The kingdom was set upon him.

His dominion was an everlasting dominion. It would not pass away. His kingdom was one that would not be broken.

Daniel woke.

The night was still around him. The lamps in the outer rooms were still low. He lay there with the dream still inside his head — the beasts coming up out of the sea, the throne with the fiery wheels, the books opened, the one like the Son of Man walking on the clouds, the kingdom that would not pass away.

He sat up on his bed. He took up his pen. He set the dream down in writing, the way the angel had once told him to, so the words would not be lost. He wrote until he had told the sum of the matters. Then he laid down the pen.

The dream had been given. The dream had been written. The kingdom that was coming would come.
