Joseph lived all his days in Egypt.

He had been brought down to that land as a young man in chains, in the shame of his brothers. He had risen in the house of Pharaoh. He had stored the grain that fed the world through the years of famine. He had brought his father Jacob and the whole household of Israel down into the land of Goshen, and he had buried his father in the cave of Machpelah, back in the land of Canaan, when his father had asked him.

After that he had lived on in Egypt, he and his brothers, in the house that Pharaoh had given them.

Joseph lived one hundred ten years.

He saw Ephraim's children to the third generation. The children of Machir, the son of Manasseh, were born on Joseph's knees. He held them when they were small. He could feel their weight on his lap, and the years of his life in the chair where he sat.

Then his strength began to fail him.

He called his brothers to him in his house.

He said to them, "I am dying."

He said, "But God will surely visit you. He will bring you up out of this land to the land which he swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob."

He took an oath from the children of Israel before he died.

He said, "God will surely visit you. When that day comes, you shall carry up my bones from here."

So Joseph died, being one hundred ten years old.

They embalmed him in the manner of the Egyptians. They put him in a coffin. They set the coffin in a house in Egypt, in the land that was not the land of the promise.

The coffin stayed there.

It would stay through the years that the children of Israel grew into a great people in Goshen. It would stay through the years they were brought low into bondage under another Pharaoh. It would stay through the four hundred years of waiting before God visited his people through the hand of Moses.

The bones of Joseph were carried up out of Egypt on that day.

But on the day Joseph died, the coffin stood quiet in the house in Egypt, and the promise stood quiet with him.
