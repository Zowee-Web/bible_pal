Four kings of the east had come up against five kings of the plain, and the five had fled into the slime pits of the Vale of Siddim; and the four had taken all the goods of Sodom and Gomorrah, and the people of those cities, and they had taken also Lot, the son of Abram's brother, who lived in Sodom. The armies of the east had turned northward with the spoil. Abram dwelt by the oaks of Mamre, in alliance with Mamre the Amorite and his brothers Eshcol and Aner; and a man came running.

One who had escaped came and told Abram, the Hebrew. At that time, he lived by the oaks of Mamre, the Amorite, brother of Eshcol, and brother of Aner; and they were allies of Abram.

When Abram heard that his relative was taken captive, he led out his trained men, born in his house, three hundred and eighteen, and pursued as far as Dan.

He divided himself against them by night, he and his servants, and struck them, and pursued them to Hobah, which is on the left hand of Damascus.

He brought back all the goods, and also brought back his relative, Lot, and his goods, and the women also, and the other people.

And Abram came down again to the Vale of Shaveh, which is the king's vale. The men who had been carried away were restored to their houses; the goods that had been taken were brought back into the cities of the plain; and Melchizedek the priest of God Most High came out from Salem to meet him in the valley with bread and wine. And so it was in the days when Abram pursued the kings of the east as far as Hobah.
