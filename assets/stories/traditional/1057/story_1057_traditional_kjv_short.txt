Moses had sent twelve men to search out the promised land. They had been gone forty days — almost six weeks! — and the people of Israel were waiting and wondering what they should find. Cook fires burned low in the evenings while families spake of what the spies might bring back.

Then the spies returned. And they bare somewhat wondrous.

Two of the men bare a great cluster of grapes upon a staff between them. The grapes were dark purple, fat and round, and so heavy that it took two grown men to bear but one cluster! Other spies bare pomegranates — smooth and red as sunset — and figs that were soft and sweet. The little ones ran up to look. Parents pointed and whispered. The juice from the grapes dripped upon the dusty ground and made dark spots in the sand.

"We came unto the land whither thou sentest us," the spies said. "It is wondrous indeed! Behold the fruit thereof — the land is full of good things, even as the Lord said!"

All were glad. But then the countenance of the spies was changed.

"Nevertheless the people that dwell therein are strong," they said. "The cities are walled and very great. And we saw giants there — even the sons of Anak! They are exceeding tall."

The multitude was silent. Then the whispering began. The people looked afraid. Giants? Great walls? Mothers caught hold of their children's hands. A babe cried somewhere at the back of the crowd.

"We were in our own sight as grasshoppers," the spies said. "So small did we seem."

Grasshoppers. The word went through the camp like a cold wind. All began to be afraid. Some sat down upon the ground and wept. Others cried out against Moses. The gladness that had come of the fruit drained away like water spilling forth from a cracked vessel.

But one man stood forth. His name was Caleb. He had seen the same giants. He had walked the same land. He had stood in the shadow of the same high walls. Dust of the long journey yet clave unto his sandals.

"Let us go up at once, and possess it!" Caleb said, his voice strong and clear. "For we are well able to overcome it."

The multitude gazed upon him. The great grapes yet hung heavy upon the staff, dripping. The pomegranates and figs yet sat in the dust — sweet, true proof of the promise.

And Caleb stood there, his feet planted, his chin lifted up, waiting.
