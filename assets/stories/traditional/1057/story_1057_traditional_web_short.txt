Moses had sent twelve men to explore the promised land. They had been gone for forty days — almost six weeks! — and the people of Israel were waiting and wondering what they would find. Cook fires burned low in the evenings while families talked about what the spies might bring back.

Then the spies came back. And they brought something amazing.

Two of the men carried a giant cluster of grapes on a pole between them. The grapes were dark purple, fat and round, and so heavy that it took two grown men to carry just one bunch! Other spies carried pomegranates — smooth and red as sunset — and figs that were soft and sweet. Kids ran up to look. Parents pointed and whispered. The juice from the grapes dripped onto the dusty ground and made dark spots in the sand.

"We went to the land you sent us to," the spies said. "It really is wonderful! Look at this fruit — the land is full of good things, just like God said!"

Everyone was excited. But then the spies' faces changed.

"But the people who live there are very strong," they said. "The cities have huge walls around them. And we saw giants — actual giants — the sons of Anak! They are enormous."

The crowd went quiet. Then the whispering started. People looked scared. Giants? Huge walls? Mothers reached for their children's hands. A baby cried somewhere at the back of the crowd.

"We looked like grasshoppers compared to them," the spies said. "That's how small we felt."

Grasshoppers. That word went through the camp like a cold wind. Everyone started to be afraid. Some people sat down on the ground and cried. Others shouted at Moses. The happy feeling from the fruit drained away like water spilling from a cracked jar.

But one man stepped forward. His name was Caleb. He had seen the same giants. He had walked the same land. He had stood in the shadow of the same tall walls. Dust from the long journey still clung to his sandals.

"Let's go right now!" Caleb said, his voice strong and clear. "We can take the land. We are well able!"

The crowd stared at him. The giant grapes still hung heavy on the pole, dripping. The pomegranates and figs still sat in the dust — sweet, real proof of the promise.

And Caleb stood there, feet planted, chin up, waiting.
