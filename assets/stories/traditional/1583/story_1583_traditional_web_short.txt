Moses was grown now, and one day he went out to his own people and looked on their heavy labor. He saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, one of his own brothers. He looked one way and then the other, and when he saw that no one was watching, he struck the Egyptian down and killed him, and hid the body in the sand.

The next day he went out again, and this time it was two Hebrews fighting each other. He said to the one in the wrong, "Why are you striking your companion?" But the man turned on him. "Who made you a prince and a judge over us? Do you mean to kill me the way you killed the Egyptian?" Then Moses was afraid, and he thought, Surely the thing is known.

It was. Pharaoh heard of it and sought to kill him, and Moses fled from Pharaoh's face, far from Egypt, into the land of Midian. There he sat down beside a well.

Now the priest of Midian had seven daughters, and they came to draw water and fill the troughs for their father's flock. But shepherds came and drove them away. Moses stood up and came to their defense, and watered their flock himself. When the daughters came home, their father asked, "How is it you have come back so soon today?" They said, "An Egyptian delivered us from the shepherds — and he even drew the water for us, and watered the flock." "And where is he?" their father said. "Why did you leave the man out there? Call him in, that he may eat bread."

So Moses was content to stay with the man, and he gave him Zipporah his daughter. She bore a son, and Moses named him Gershom; for he said, "I have been a foreigner in a foreign land."
