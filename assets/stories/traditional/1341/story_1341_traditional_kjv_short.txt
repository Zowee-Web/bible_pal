The messengers had come up from Sennacherib king of Assyria. They had stood at the conduit of the upper pool and called out unto the people on the wall, and they had spoken in the Jews' language so that all might hear; and they had said that no god of any nation had delivered its people out of the hand of the king of Assyria. The words had come unto the king. Hezekiah had taken the letter and gone up unto the temple.

And Hezekiah received the letter of the hand of the messengers, and read it. Then Hezekiah went up into the house of the Lord, and spread it before the Lord.

And Hezekiah prayed before the Lord, and said, "O Lord God of Israel, which dwellest between the cherubims, thou art the God, even thou alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth; thou hast made heaven and earth.

Lord, bow down thine ear, and hear: open, Lord, thine eyes, and see: and hear the words of Sennacherib, which hath sent him to reproach the living God.

Of a truth, Lord, the kings of Assyria have laid waste the nations and their lands,

and have cast their gods into the fire: for they were no gods, but the work of men's hands, wood and stone: therefore they have destroyed them.

Now therefore, O Lord our God, I beseech thee, save thou us out of his hand, that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that thou art the Lord God, even thou only."

And the king finished his prayer and arose. The letter lay open before the Lord in the temple, and the city of Jerusalem went on with its watching, and the army of Sennacherib went on with its waiting, and the matter had been placed in the hands where the king had placed it. And so it was in the days when Hezekiah received the letter of Sennacherib and brought it up unto the house of the Lord.
