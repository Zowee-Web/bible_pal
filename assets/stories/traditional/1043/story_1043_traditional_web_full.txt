The Midianites were terrible to the people of Israel. Every year, they swarmed over the land like locusts — stealing grain, taking sheep and cattle and donkeys, trampling crops flat. When they came, the Israelites ran. They hid in mountain caves and dens and strongholds. They were afraid of everything.

In the little town of Ophrah, a young man named Gideon was threshing wheat. But he was not threshing it on a threshing floor out in the open, the way you normally would. He was doing it by the winepress — a low stone pit hidden behind walls. He was hiding. If the Midianites saw his wheat, they would take it.

Gideon beat the stalks against the stone. Dust floated in the warm air. Sweat ran down his face. He worked fast, glancing up every few seconds to make sure no one was coming.

Then someone was there.

The angel of the Lord appeared and sat down under the oak tree that grew nearby. Its shade was cool and dark. Gideon did not notice him at first. Then the angel spoke.

"The Lord is with you, you mighty man of valor."

Gideon stopped. He looked at this stranger sitting under the tree. Mighty man of valor? Gideon was hiding in a winepress. He did not feel mighty at all.

"Oh my lord," Gideon said, "if the Lord is with us, why then has all this happened to us? Where are all his wondrous deeds that our fathers told us about? They said, 'Did not the Lord bring us up from Egypt?' But now the Lord has cast us off and delivered us into the hand of Midian."

Gideon's voice had an edge to it. He was frustrated. He had heard the old stories about God doing amazing things. But all he could see was his people hiding in caves.

The Lord turned and looked straight at Gideon. "Go in this your strength and save Israel from the hand of Midian. Have I not sent you?"

Gideon blinked. Save Israel? Him? "O Lord, how shall I save Israel?" he said. "My clan is the weakest in all of Manasseh. And I am the least in my father's house."

The youngest. The smallest family. The most unlikely person anyone would pick.

"Surely I will be with you," the Lord said. His voice was calm and sure. "And you shall strike the Midianites as one man."

Gideon stood there with wheat dust on his hands. He wanted to believe it. But he needed to know this was real.

"If now I have found favor in your sight," Gideon said, "then show me a sign that it is you who talks with me. Please do not go away until I come back to you and bring out my offering."

The Lord said, "I will wait until you come back."

Gideon went inside his house. He took a young goat and killed it. He cooked the meat. He took flour and made unleavened bread — flat bread without yeast, baked quickly over the fire. He put the meat in a basket. He poured the broth into a pot. He carried it all outside, under the oak tree, and set it before the angel.

The angel of God said, "Take the meat and the unleavened bread and lay them on this rock. Pour out the broth."

Gideon did it. He set the meat and the flat bread on the big rock. He poured the warm broth over it all. Then he stepped back.

The angel of the Lord reached out the end of his staff — a long walking stick — and touched the tip to the meat and bread.

Fire burst up from the rock. It came from inside the stone itself, hot and bright. The flames swallowed the meat and the bread in seconds. Gideon felt the heat on his face. The broth sizzled and steamed. Everything was consumed.

When Gideon looked up, the angel of the Lord was gone. The oak tree's branches swayed in the breeze. The rock was scorched black. The food was gone.

Gideon's heart hammered. "Alas, O Lord God!" he cried. "I have seen the angel of the Lord face to face!"

But the Lord spoke to him again — gently this time, like a father calming a frightened child. "Peace be to you. Do not be afraid. You shall not die."

Gideon stood still. His breathing slowed. The fear drained away and something else took its place — a deep, quiet peace.

He built an altar right there, on that spot, using the stones around him. He stacked them carefully and named the altar "The Lord is Peace." Because that is what God had given him. Not just a mission, not just a sign. Peace.

The young man who had been hiding in a winepress, afraid of everything — God had called him a mighty warrior. And God meant it.
