The word of the Lord came unto Ezekiel with a charge against the shepherds of Israel. They had fed themselves when shepherds ought to have fed the flock. They ate the fat, clothed themselves with wool, and killed them that were fed, but left the flock unfed.

Their neglect reached every wounded part of the flock. They had not strengthened the diseased or healed the sick. They had not bound up the broken, brought again the driven away, or sought the lost. Instead, they ruled with force and cruelty. The sheep scattered because there was no shepherd. They wandered over mountains and high hills, spread across the face of the earth, and became meat for the beasts of the field. None searched or sought after them.

The Lord God swore that he was against those shepherds. Because they fed themselves and did not search for his flock, he would require his sheep at their hand. Their feeding of the flock would cease, and they would no longer feed themselves from it. The Lord would deliver his sheep from their mouth so they could not be consumed.

The Lord himself would do what the shepherds had refused to do. As a shepherd seeketh out his scattered flock when he is among his sheep, the Lord would search for his own. He would deliver them from every place where they had been scattered in the cloudy and dark day. He would bring them out from the people, gather them from the countries, and return them to their own land.

There he would feed them upon Israel's mountains, by the rivers, and throughout the inhabited places. Their pasture would be good and fat, and their fold would be upon the high mountains of Israel. The Lord himself would feed them and cause them to lie down. He would seek the lost, bring again the driven away, bind up the broken, and strengthen the sick. The fat and strong who had abused others would face his judgment, for he would feed the flock with judgment.

The flock itself was not beyond judgment. Some had eaten the good pasture and then trodden down what remained. They had drunk the deep waters and fouled the rest, leaving other sheep to eat and drink what their feet had spoiled. The fat cattle had thrust with side and shoulder and pushed the diseased with their horns until they were scattered abroad.

The Lord would judge between the fat cattle and the lean. He would save his flock so they should no more be a prey. He would set one shepherd over them—even his servant David—to feed them. David would be their shepherd and prince, and the Lord would be their God.

With his flock the Lord would make a covenant of peace. Evil beasts would cease out of the land, and his people would dwell safely even in the wilderness and the woods. He would make them and the places round about his hill a blessing. Rain would come in its season as showers of blessing. The tree would yield her fruit, the earth would yield her increase, and the people would be safe in their land.

They would know the Lord when he broke the bands of their yoke and delivered them from those who had served themselves of them. The heathen would no longer take them as prey, and beasts would not devour them. They would dwell safely with none to make them afraid. The Lord would raise up a plant of renown for them; hunger and the shame of the heathen would no longer consume them.

The house of Israel would know that the Lord their God was with them and that they were his people. They were men, yet he called them his sheep, the flock of his pasture. He was their God.
