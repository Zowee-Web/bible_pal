Yahweh's word came to Ezekiel with a charge against the shepherds of Israel. They had fed themselves when shepherds should have fed the sheep. They ate the fat, clothed themselves with wool, and killed the fatlings, but left the flock unfed.

Their neglect reached every wounded part of the flock. They had not strengthened the diseased or healed the sick. They had not bound up the broken, brought back the driven away, or sought the lost. Instead, they ruled with force and rigor. The sheep scattered because there was no shepherd. They wandered over mountains and high hills, spread across the earth, and became food for wild animals. No one searched or sought them.

The Lord Yahweh swore that he was against those shepherds. Because they fed themselves and did not search for his flock, he would require his sheep at their hand. Their feeding of the flock would cease, and they would no longer feed themselves from it. Yahweh would deliver his sheep from their mouths so they could not be consumed.

Yahweh himself would do what the shepherds had refused to do. As a shepherd seeks a scattered flock when he is among his sheep, Yahweh would search for his own. He would rescue them from every place where they had been scattered in the cloudy and dark day. He would bring them out from the peoples, gather them from the countries, and return them to their own land.

There he would feed them upon Israel's mountains, beside the watercourses, and throughout the inhabited places. Their pasture would be good and rich, and their fold would be on the heights of Israel. Yahweh himself would shepherd them and cause them to lie down. He would seek the lost, bring back the driven away, bind up the broken, and strengthen the sick. The fat and strong who had abused others would face his judgment, for he would feed the flock in justice.

The flock itself was not beyond judgment. Some had eaten good pasture and then trampled what remained. They had drunk clear water and fouled the rest, leaving other sheep to eat and drink what their feet had spoiled. The fat sheep had thrust with side and shoulder and pushed the diseased with their horns until they were scattered.

Yahweh would judge between the fat sheep and the lean. He would save his flock so they would no longer be prey. He would set one shepherd over them—his servant David—to feed them. David would be their shepherd and prince, and Yahweh would be their God.

With his flock Yahweh would make a covenant of peace. Evil animals would cease from the land, and his people would dwell securely even in wilderness and woods. He would make them and the places around his hill a blessing. Rain would come in its season as showers of blessing. Trees would yield fruit, the earth would yield its increase, and the people would be secure in their land.

They would know Yahweh when he broke the bars of their yoke and delivered them from those who had enslaved them. Nations would no longer take them as prey, and animals would not devour them. They would dwell without anyone making them afraid. Yahweh would raise up a renowned plantation for them; famine and the shame of the nations would no longer consume them.

The house of Israel would know that Yahweh their God was with them and that they were his people. They were human beings, yet he called them his sheep, the flock of his pasture. He was their God.
