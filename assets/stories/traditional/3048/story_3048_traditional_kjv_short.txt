The Lord sent Ezekiel to prophesy against the shepherds of Israel. They fed themselves instead of the flock. They took the fat and wool, but did not strengthen the diseased, heal the sick, bind up the broken, bring again the driven away, or seek the lost. They ruled with force and cruelty. Without a shepherd, the Lord's sheep scattered over mountains and hills and became prey, while none searched for them.

Therefore the Lord God stood against those shepherds. He would require his flock at their hand, cause them to cease from feeding themselves, and deliver the sheep from their mouth.

Then the Lord declared what he himself would do. As a shepherd seeketh his scattered sheep, he would search for his flock and deliver them from every place where they had been scattered in the cloudy and dark day. He would gather them from the people and countries, bring them into their own land, feed them in good pasture upon Israel's mountains, and cause them to lie down safely. He would seek the lost, bring again the driven away, bind up the broken, and strengthen the sick. He would feed them with judgment.

The Lord would also judge within the flock. Strong sheep had trodden down pasture, fouled water, and pushed the diseased aside. He would judge between cattle and cattle so that his flock would no more be a prey.

He would set one shepherd over them, his servant David, to feed them, while the Lord would be their God. He would make a covenant of peace, remove danger, and let his people dwell safely. Showers would come in season, trees would bear fruit, and the earth would yield her increase. The Lord would break the bands of their yoke and deliver them from those who enslaved them. No heathen would prey upon them, none would make them afraid, and they would know that the Lord was with them. They were his people, the sheep of his pasture, and he was their God.
