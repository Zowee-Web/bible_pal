Yahweh sent Ezekiel to prophesy against Israel's shepherds. They fed themselves instead of the sheep. They took the fat and wool, but did not strengthen the diseased, heal the sick, bind up the broken, bring back the driven away, or seek the lost. They ruled with force and rigor. Without a shepherd, Yahweh's sheep scattered over mountains and hills and became prey, while no one searched for them.

Therefore the Lord Yahweh opposed those shepherds. He would require his sheep from their hands, stop them from feeding themselves, and rescue the flock from their mouths.

Then Yahweh declared what he himself would do. As a shepherd seeks scattered sheep, he would search for his flock and deliver them from every place where they had been scattered in the cloudy and dark day. He would gather them from peoples and countries, bring them into their own land, feed them in good pasture on Israel's mountains, and cause them to lie down safely. He would seek the lost, bring back the driven away, bind up the broken, and strengthen the sick. He would feed them with justice.

Yahweh would also judge within the flock. Strong sheep had trampled pasture, fouled water, and pushed the weak aside. He would judge between sheep and sheep so that his flock would no longer be prey.

He would set one shepherd over them, his servant David, to feed them, while Yahweh would be their God. He would make a covenant of peace, remove danger, and let his people dwell securely. Showers would come in season, trees would bear fruit, and the earth would yield its increase. Yahweh would break their yoke and deliver them from those who enslaved them. No nation would prey upon them, no one would make them afraid, and they would know that Yahweh was with them. They were his people, the sheep of his pasture, and he was their God.
