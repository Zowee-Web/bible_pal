Jehu son of Jehoshaphat guarded the conspiracy formed at Ramoth Gilead. King Joram had defended that place against Hazael of Syria, but wounds received in the fighting had brought him back to Jezreel. Jehu ordered that no one leave the city to carry news ahead of him. Then he drove his chariot toward Jezreel, where Ahaziah king of Judah had come to visit Joram.

The watchman on Jezreel's tower saw Jehu's company approaching. Joram sent a horseman to ask, “Is it peace?” Jehu ordered the messenger to fall in behind him. A second horseman received the same answer and also failed to return. The watchman reported that the driving resembled Jehu's, for he drove furiously.

Joram and Ahaziah each took a chariot and went out. They met Jehu on the land of Naboth the Jezreelite. Joram asked whether there was peace. Jehu answered that peace could not remain while Jezebel's prostitution and witchcraft abounded. Joram turned to flee and warned Ahaziah of treason. Jehu drew his bow with full strength and struck Joram through the heart.

Jehu told Bidkar to cast the body onto Naboth's field. He recalled that while they had ridden behind Ahab, Yahweh had pronounced judgment for the blood of Naboth and his sons: repayment would come in that very plot. Ahaziah fled by the garden house. Jehu's men struck him near Ibleam; he reached Megiddo and died there. His servants carried him to Jerusalem for burial with his fathers.

When Jehu entered Jezreel, Jezebel looked down from a window and addressed him as a murderer of his master. Jehu called for those on his side. Two or three eunuchs looked out, and at his command they threw her down. After Jehu had eaten and drunk, he ordered that she be buried because she was a king's daughter. Those who went found only her skull, feet, and palms. Jehu identified what had happened with Yahweh's word through Elijah concerning Jezebel on Jezreel's land.

Seventy sons of Ahab were being raised by leading men in Samaria. Jehu wrote to the city's authorities, telling them to place the strongest son on Ahab's throne and fight. They were terrified because two kings had not stood before him. They submitted and promised to do whatever he commanded. His second letter required them to send the heads of Ahab's sons. They killed the seventy and sent their heads in baskets.

Jehu ordered the heads laid in two heaps at Jezreel's gate. In the morning he acknowledged his own conspiracy against Joram and asked who had killed all these. His conclusion was that none of Yahweh's word concerning Ahab's house would fail. He then struck all who remained of that house in Jezreel. On the road to Samaria, he met forty-two relatives of Ahaziah and had them killed at the shearing-house pit.

Jehu next met Jehonadab son of Rechab. After asking whether his heart was right, Jehu took him by the hand into his chariot and invited him to see his zeal for Yahweh. In Samaria, Jehu destroyed those who remained to Ahab, according to the word spoken to Elijah.

Then Jehu announced that he would serve Baal greatly. He called every prophet, priest, and worshiper of Baal to a solemn assembly. Jehu did this deceptively, intending to destroy Baal's worshipers. The house of Baal filled from end to end. Robes were distributed, and Jehu ordered a search to ensure that only Baal's worshipers were present. Eighty guards waited outside under penalty of life for life if anyone escaped.

When the offering ended, Jehu commanded the guards and captains to strike. They killed the worshipers and entered the inner place. The pillars were brought out and burned; Baal's pillar and house were broken down, and the site was made a latrine. In this way Jehu destroyed Baal worship out of Israel.
