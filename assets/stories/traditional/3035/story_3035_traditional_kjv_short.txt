Jehu kept the conspiracy at Ramoth-gilead and rode unto Jezreel, where king Joram lay wounded and Ahaziah king of Judah had come to see him. From the tower a watchman beheld Jehu's company. Twice an horseman went forth asking, Is it peace? Neither came again. Then the watchman knew Jehu by his furious driving.

Joram and Ahaziah rode out and met him in the portion of Naboth the Jezreelite. When Joram asked of peace, Jehu answered that there could be none while Jezebel's whoredoms and witchcrafts were many. Joram fled, but Jehu smote him with an arrow. He commanded that Joram be cast into Naboth's field, remembering the word of the Lord that Naboth's blood should be requited there. Ahaziah also fled, was smitten, and died at Megiddo.

At Jezreel, Jezebel looked from a window and challenged Jehu. At his word, attendants threw her down. Afterward, they that went to bury her found only part of her remains. Jehu declared that this fulfilled the word of the Lord spoken by Elijah.

Ahab's seventy sons were in Samaria. Jehu challenged their guardians to set up a king and fight, but they submitted. At his second letter, they slew the seventy and sent their heads unto Jezreel. Jehu declared that nothing of the word of the Lord concerning Ahab's house should fall to the earth. He slew those who remained of Ahab's house in Jezreel. On the way to Samaria he also slew two and forty brethren of Ahaziah.

Jehu met Jehonadab the son of Rechab, took him into his chariot, and brought him to behold his zeal. In Samaria, Jehu destroyed those who remained unto Ahab, according to the word spoken by Elijah. Then Jehu gathered Baal's worshippers by proclaiming a great sacrifice. He did it in subtilty to destroy them. The house of Baal was filled; vestments marked the worshippers, and Jehu made sure no servant of the Lord was among them. After the offering, his guard slew Baal's worshippers. They burned the images, brake down Baal's image and house, and made it a draught house. Thus Jehu destroyed Baal out of Israel.
