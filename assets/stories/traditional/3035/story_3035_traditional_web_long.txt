Jehu son of Jehoshaphat son of Nimshi kept the conspiracy at Ramoth Gilead from being reported ahead of him. King Joram had been defending Ramoth Gilead with Israel against Hazael king of Syria, but Syrian wounds had sent him back to Jezreel to recover. Jehu told those with him that if they agreed with his course, no one should leave the city and carry word to Jezreel. He then mounted his chariot and rode there. Ahaziah king of Judah was also in Jezreel, visiting the wounded king.

From the tower of Jezreel, a watchman saw Jehu's company coming. Joram ordered a horseman sent to ask whether the approach meant peace. The messenger reached Jehu and delivered the question, but Jehu answered, “What do you have to do with peace? Fall in behind me!” The watchman saw that the horseman did not return. Joram sent a second messenger, and the same exchange followed. When that man also remained with the company, the watchman added one more detail: the driving looked like the driving of Jehu, for he drove furiously.

Joram ordered his chariot prepared. He and Ahaziah each rode out, and the place where they met Jehu was Naboth the Jezreelite's land. Joram asked Jehu directly, “Is it peace?” Jehu answered that there could be no peace while the prostitution of Joram's mother Jezebel and her witchcraft continued. Joram turned to flee and warned Ahaziah that it was treason. Jehu drew his bow with his full strength. The arrow struck Joram between the arms, passed through his heart, and he sank in his chariot.

Jehu told his captain Bidkar to lift the body and cast it onto Naboth's field. He recalled the day when he and Bidkar had ridden together behind Ahab and heard Yahweh's burden against him. Yahweh had seen the blood of Naboth and the blood of his sons and had said that repayment would come in this plot. Jehu connected Joram's death with that word and ordered the body placed there.

Ahaziah fled by the way of the garden house. Jehu pursued and ordered that he also be struck in his chariot. He was wounded at the ascent of Gur by Ibleam, fled to Megiddo, and died there. His servants carried him to Jerusalem and buried him in his tomb with his fathers in David's city.

News of Jehu's arrival reached Jezebel. She painted her eyes, arranged her head, and looked from a window. As Jehu passed through the gate, she challenged him by invoking Zimri, the murderer of his master. Jehu looked up and called, “Who is on my side? Who?” Two or three eunuchs looked out. At his command they threw her down. Her blood struck the wall and the horses, and she was trampled.

Jehu went inside to eat and drink. Afterward he ordered that the woman be buried, since she was a king's daughter. Those sent for the burial found no more than her skull, feet, and the palms of her hands. When they returned, Jehu named what had occurred as Yahweh's word spoken through Elijah the Tishbite: Jezebel's flesh would be consumed on Jezreel's plot, and her body would become like refuse on the field, leaving no place where people could point and say, “This is Jezebel.”

The judgment then extended to Ahab's seventy sons in Samaria. Jehu wrote to the rulers, elders, and guardians who had raised them. Because they had the king's sons, chariots, horses, armor, and a fortified city, he challenged them to choose the best of Ahab's sons, place him on his father's throne, and fight for their master's house. The authorities were exceedingly afraid. Two kings had already failed to stand before Jehu; they asked how they could stand.

The overseers of the household and city, the elders, and the guardians answered that they were Jehu's servants. They would appoint no king and would do what he told them. Jehu's second letter tested that submission with a severe demand: if they were truly on his side and would listen to him, they were to bring the heads of their master's sons to Jezreel by the next day. The seventy sons were in the care of the city's great men. When the letter arrived, those men killed them, placed their heads in baskets, and sent them to Jehu.

A messenger announced their arrival. Jehu ordered that the heads be set in two heaps at the gate until morning. Then he stood before the people. He called them righteous, acknowledged that he himself had conspired against his master and killed him, and asked who had killed all these. Jehu declared that none of Yahweh's word concerning Ahab's house would fall to the ground. Yahweh had done what he spoke through Elijah. Jehu proceeded against everyone remaining to Ahab in Jezreel—great men, acquaintances, and priests—until no one remained.

Jehu set out for Samaria. At the shearing house along the road, he met relatives of Ahaziah. They identified themselves and said they were going to greet the royal children. Jehu ordered that they be taken alive, and then the forty-two men were killed at the pit. None was left.

Farther on, Jehu met Jehonadab son of Rechab coming toward him. Jehu greeted him and asked whether Jehonadab's heart was right with his. When Jehonadab answered that it was, Jehu asked for his hand and lifted him into the chariot. “Come with me, and see my zeal for Yahweh,” he said. With Jehonadab beside him, Jehu entered Samaria and destroyed all who remained there to Ahab, in accord with Yahweh's word to Elijah.

Jehu then gathered the people and announced that although Ahab had served Baal a little, he would serve Baal much. He demanded the presence of every prophet, worshiper, and priest of Baal for a great sacrifice, threatening death to anyone absent. Jehu acted deceptively because he intended to destroy Baal's worshipers.

A solemn assembly was proclaimed throughout Israel. The worshipers came until the house of Baal was filled from end to end. Jehu ordered the keeper of the wardrobe to provide robes for them all. He and Jehonadab entered and required the worshipers to search carefully, making certain that no servant of Yahweh was among them. Outside, Jehu stationed eighty men and required life for life if any of those delivered into their hands escaped.

The sacrifices and burnt offerings proceeded. As soon as the burnt offering was finished, Jehu ordered the guards and captains to go in, strike the worshipers, and let no one escape. They killed them with the sword, cast the bodies out, and pressed into the inner shrine. They brought out the pillars and burned them. They broke down Baal's pillar, demolished Baal's house, and turned the place into a latrine. Thus Jehu destroyed Baal worship out of Israel.
