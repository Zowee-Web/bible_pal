Jehu the son of Jehoshaphat the son of Nimshi kept the conspiracy at Ramoth-gilead from being declared before him. King Joram had kept Ramoth-gilead with Israel against Hazael king of Syria, but wounds given by the Syrians had caused him to return unto Jezreel for healing. Jehu told them with him that, if the purpose pleased them, none should go forth to bear tidings unto Jezreel. Then he entered his chariot and rode thither. Ahaziah king of Judah was likewise in Jezreel, having come down to see the wounded king.

From the tower of Jezreel, a watchman saw Jehu's company coming. Joram commanded that an horseman should meet them and ask, Is it peace? The messenger came unto Jehu and spoke the king's question, but Jehu answered, What hast thou to do with peace? Turn thee behind me. The watchman perceived that the horseman came not again. Joram sent a second messenger, and the same answer was given. When he also remained with the company, the watchman added that the driving was like Jehu's driving, for he drove furiously.

Joram commanded that his chariot be made ready. He and Ahaziah went forth, each in his chariot, and they met Jehu in the portion of Naboth the Jezreelite. Joram asked Jehu, Is it peace? Jehu answered that there could be no peace while the whoredoms of Joram's mother Jezebel and her witchcrafts were so many. Joram turned his hands to flee and cried unto Ahaziah that there was treachery. Jehu drew a bow with his full strength. The arrow smote Joram between his arms, went out at his heart, and he sank down in his chariot.

Jehu charged Bidkar his captain to take up the body and cast it in Naboth's field. He remembered the time when he and Bidkar rode together after Ahab and the Lord laid a burden upon him. The Lord had seen the blood of Naboth and the blood of his sons and had said that he would requite Ahab in this same plat. Jehu joined Joram's death unto that word and commanded that the body be cast there.

Ahaziah fled by the way of the garden house. Jehu pursued and ordered that he also be smitten in his chariot. He was wounded at the going up to Gur by Ibleam, fled unto Megiddo, and died there. His servants carried him unto Jerusalem and buried him in his sepulchre with his fathers in the city of David.

Jezebel heard that Jehu had come to Jezreel. She painted her face, tired her head, and looked out at a window. As Jehu entered the gate, she spoke of Zimri, who slew his master. Jehu lifted his face and asked, Who is on my side? Who? Two or three eunuchs looked out unto him. At his command they threw her down. Her blood was sprinkled upon the wall and horses, and she was trodden under foot.

Jehu entered, ate, and drank. Afterward he ordered that the woman be buried, for she was a king's daughter. They that went to bury her found no more than the skull, the feet, and the palms of her hands. When they returned, Jehu declared that this was the word of the Lord spoken by Elijah the Tishbite: dogs should eat Jezebel's flesh in the portion of Jezreel, and her carcase should be as dung upon the field, so that none could say, This is Jezebel.

The judgment then came upon Ahab's seventy sons in Samaria. Jehu wrote unto the rulers, elders, and them that brought up the children. Because they had their master's sons, chariots, horses, armour, and a fenced city, he challenged them to choose the best and meetest son, set him upon his father's throne, and fight for their master's house. The authorities feared exceedingly. Two kings had not stood before Jehu; how then should they stand?

He that was over the household, he that was over the city, the elders, and the bringers up answered that they were Jehu's servants. They would make no king and would obey whatsoever he bade them. Jehu's second letter proved that submission with a grievous command: if they were his and would hearken unto his voice, they should bring the heads of their master's sons unto Jezreel by the morrow. The seventy sons were in the keeping of the great men. When the letter came, those men slew them, put their heads in baskets, and sent them unto Jehu.

A messenger told Jehu that the heads had arrived. He ordered them laid in two heaps at the entering of the gate until morning. Then he stood before the people. He called them righteous, acknowledged that he had conspired against his master and slain him, and asked who had slain all these. Jehu declared that nothing of the word of the Lord concerning Ahab's house should fall unto the earth. The Lord had done that which he spake by Elijah. Jehu then slew all that remained unto Ahab in Jezreel—great men, kinsfolk, and priests—until none remained.

Jehu departed toward Samaria. At the shearing house in the way, he met the brethren of Ahaziah. They named themselves and said they went to salute the children of the king and queen. Jehu commanded that they be taken alive; afterward the two and forty men were slain at the pit. He left none of them.

Thereafter Jehu met Jehonadab the son of Rechab coming to meet him. Jehu saluted him and asked whether his heart was right with Jehu's heart. When Jehonadab answered that it was, Jehu asked for his hand and took him up into the chariot. Come with me, and see my zeal for the Lord, he said. With Jehonadab beside him, Jehu entered Samaria and destroyed all that remained unto Ahab, according to the saying of the Lord spoken unto Elijah.

Jehu gathered the people and declared that, whereas Ahab had served Baal a little, he would serve Baal much. He required every prophet, servant, and priest of Baal to appear for a great sacrifice, threatening death unto any that was wanting. Jehu did it in subtilty, intending to destroy Baal's worshippers.

A solemn assembly was proclaimed through Israel. The worshippers came until the house of Baal was full from one end unto another. Jehu commanded the keeper of the vestry to bring forth vestments for them all. He and Jehonadab entered and required the worshippers to search diligently, lest any servant of the Lord should be among them. Without, Jehu appointed fourscore men and required life for life if any committed to their keeping escaped.

The sacrifices and burnt offerings proceeded. As soon as the burnt offering was ended, Jehu commanded the guard and captains to go in, slay the worshippers, and let none come forth. They smote them with the sword, cast them out, and went into the inner place. They brought forth the images and burned them. They brake down the image of Baal, brake down his house, and made the place a draught house. Thus Jehu destroyed Baal out of Israel.
