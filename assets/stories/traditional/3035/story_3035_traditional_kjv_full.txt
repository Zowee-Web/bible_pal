Jehu the son of Jehoshaphat kept close the conspiracy made at Ramoth-gilead. King Joram had kept that place against Hazael of Syria, but wounds received in battle had brought him again unto Jezreel. Jehu charged that none should escape from the city to carry tidings before him. Then he drove his chariot toward Jezreel, where Ahaziah king of Judah was come to visit Joram.

The watchman upon the tower of Jezreel saw Jehu's company drawing nigh. Joram sent an horseman to ask, Is it peace? Jehu bade the messenger turn behind him. A second horseman received the same answer and likewise came not again. The watchman reported that the driving was like Jehu's, for he drove furiously.

Joram and Ahaziah made ready their chariots and went forth. They met Jehu in the portion of Naboth the Jezreelite. Joram asked concerning peace. Jehu answered that peace could not be while Jezebel's whoredoms and witchcrafts were so many. Joram turned his hands to flee and warned Ahaziah of treachery. Jehu drew his bow with full strength and smote Joram through the heart.

Jehu commanded Bidkar to cast the body into Naboth's field. He remembered how, when they had ridden after Ahab, the Lord laid a burden upon him concerning the blood of Naboth and his sons: it should be requited in that same plat. Ahaziah fled by the garden house. Jehu's men smote him near Ibleam; he came to Megiddo and died there. His servants carried him unto Jerusalem and buried him with his fathers.

When Jehu entered Jezreel, Jezebel looked from a window and spoke of Zimri, who slew his master. Jehu called for them that were on his side. Two or three eunuchs looked out, and at his command they threw her down. After Jehu had eaten and drunk, he ordered that she be buried, for she was a king's daughter. They found only her skull, feet, and palms. Jehu declared that this was the word of the Lord spoken by Elijah concerning Jezebel in the portion of Jezreel.

Seventy sons of Ahab were with the great men of Samaria who brought them up. Jehu wrote unto the rulers and elders, commanding them to set the best of Ahab's sons upon the throne and fight. They feared greatly, for two kings had not stood before Jehu. They submitted themselves and promised to obey him. His second letter required the heads of their master's sons. They slew the seventy and sent their heads in baskets.

Jehu commanded that the heads be laid in two heaps at the gate of Jezreel. In the morning he confessed his conspiracy against Joram and asked who had slain all these. He declared that nothing of the word of the Lord concerning Ahab's house should fall unto the earth. He then slew all that remained of that house in Jezreel. Upon the way to Samaria, he met two and forty brethren of Ahaziah and slew them at the pit of the shearing house.

Jehu afterward met Jehonadab the son of Rechab. Having asked whether his heart was right, Jehu took him by the hand into his chariot and called him to behold his zeal for the Lord. In Samaria, Jehu destroyed all that remained unto Ahab, according to the word spoken unto Elijah.

Then Jehu said that he would serve Baal much. He summoned every prophet, servant, and priest of Baal unto a solemn assembly. Jehu did this in subtilty, to the intent that he might destroy the worshippers of Baal. The house of Baal was full from one end unto another. Vestments were brought forth, and Jehu required a search so that no servant of the Lord should be among them. Fourscore guards waited without, under sentence of life for life if any escaped.

When the burnt offering was ended, Jehu commanded the guard and captains to smite. They slew the worshippers and went into the inner place. The images were brought forth and burned; the image and house of Baal were broken down, and the place became a draught house. Thus Jehu destroyed Baal out of Israel.
