Jehu guarded the conspiracy at Ramoth Gilead and rode to Jezreel, where wounded King Joram lay and King Ahaziah of Judah was visiting him. From Jezreel's tower, a watchman saw Jehu's company. Two horsemen went out asking whether there was peace; neither returned. The watchman recognized Jehu by his furious driving.

Joram and Ahaziah rode out and met Jehu on Naboth's land. When Joram asked about peace, Jehu answered that there could be none while Jezebel's prostitution and witchcraft continued. Joram fled, but Jehu struck him with an arrow. He ordered Joram's body cast onto Naboth's field, recalling Yahweh's word that Naboth's blood would be repaid there. Ahaziah also fled, was struck, and died at Megiddo.

At Jezreel, Jezebel looked from a window and challenged Jehu. At his command, attendants threw her down. Later, those sent to bury her found only part of her remains. Jehu recognized the fulfillment of Yahweh's word spoken through Elijah.

Ahab's seventy sons remained in Samaria. Jehu challenged their guardians to establish a king and fight, but they submitted. At his second letter, they killed the seventy and sent their heads to Jezreel. Jehu declared that none of Yahweh's word against Ahab's house would fall to the ground. He struck those who remained of Ahab's house in Jezreel. On the way to Samaria he killed forty-two relatives of Ahaziah.

Jehu met Jehonadab son of Rechab, took him into his chariot, and brought him to witness his zeal. In Samaria, Jehu destroyed those who remained of Ahab's house, according to Yahweh's word through Elijah. Then Jehu gathered Baal's worshipers by announcing a great sacrifice. He acted deceptively, intending to destroy them. The house of Baal filled; special robes marked the worshipers, and Jehu ensured that no servant of Yahweh was among them. After the offering, his guards killed Baal's worshipers. They burned the pillars, broke down Baal's pillar and house, and made the place a latrine. Thus Jehu destroyed Baal worship out of Israel.
