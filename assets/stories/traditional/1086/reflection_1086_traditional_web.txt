He was born into darkness, and it was the only world he knew. No one asked if he wanted to see. No one explained why he couldn't. The darkness was simply there — from the beginning, without cause, without apology.

What changed was not his effort. He didn't find a cure. He didn't earn the healing. A stranger knelt beside him, put mud on his eyes, and told him to wash. He went. He washed. And everything was different.

The hardest part may not have been the blindness. It may have been what came after — the questions, the doubt, the people who could not accept what had happened. He was asked to explain something he barely understood himself, over and over, to people who did not want to believe.

And when they threw him out, Jesus came and found him.

Sometimes the ones who see most clearly are the ones who have the least reason to believe — and believe anyway.
