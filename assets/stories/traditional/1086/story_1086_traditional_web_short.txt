As Jesus walked through Jerusalem, he passed a man who had been blind from birth. The man sat by the side of the road, his hands resting on his knees, his face turned toward the warmth of the sun he had never seen. His world was darkness — not a darkness he had fallen into, but the only one he had ever known.

The disciples looked at the man and asked Jesus a question. "Rabbi, who sinned — this man or his parents — that he was born blind?"

Jesus answered, "Neither this man nor his parents sinned. He was born blind so that the works of God might be displayed in him."

He knelt down. He spat on the ground and made clay with the dust and the moisture, working it between his fingers. Then he spread the clay over the blind man's eyes, pressing it gently against his closed lids.

"Go," Jesus said. "Wash in the pool of Siloam."

The man rose. He walked through the streets of Jerusalem with the clay still on his face, guided by the sounds around him — footsteps, voices, the bleating of animals near the market. He reached the pool. He knelt at the edge, cupped water in his hands, and washed his eyes.

And he could see.

Light flooded in. Shapes sharpened into faces. Colors he had no names for filled the air around him. He stood at the edge of the pool, blinking, turning his head slowly, looking at everything — the water, the stone, the sky, the trees along the wall.

His neighbors were the first to notice. "Isn't this the man who used to sit and beg?" they said. Some said it was him. Others said no, it was only someone who looked like him. But the man said, "I am the one."

"Then how were your eyes opened?" they asked.

He told them. "A man called Jesus made clay and put it on my eyes, and told me to wash. I went and washed, and now I can see."

They brought him to the Pharisees, who questioned him closely. They questioned his parents. They called him back again and pressed him further. Some of the Pharisees said Jesus could not be from God because he had healed on the Sabbath. Others said, "How can a sinner do such signs?" They were divided.

They turned to the man again. "What do you say about him, since he opened your eyes?"

The man answered simply. "He is a prophet."

They did not like this answer. They argued with him. They threatened him. And in the end, they cast him out.

When Jesus heard that the man had been thrown out, he found him.

"Do you believe in the Son of God?" Jesus asked.

The man looked at him — looked at him with the eyes that had been opened. "Who is he, Lord," he said, "that I might believe in him?"

Jesus said, "You have seen him. He is the one speaking with you."

The man said, "Lord, I believe."

And he worshiped him.
