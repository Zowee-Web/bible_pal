As Jesus passed through Jerusalem with his disciples, he came upon a man which had been blind from his birth. The man sat beside the way near the temple, his hands upon his knees, his face turned toward the sun. He had never beheld it — had never seen aught. Not the stones of the temple, not the faces of his mother or his father, not the sky above the city. His world was darkness, and it had ever been darkness.

His disciples asked him, saying, "Master, who did sin, this man, or his parents, that he was born blind?"

Jesus answered, "Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents: but that the works of God should be made manifest in him."

He ceased walking. He kneeled down in the dust beside the man. He spat upon the ground and mingled the dirt with the moisture, working it with his fingers into a smooth clay. Then he anointed the eyes of the blind man with the clay, pressing it gently upon his closed lids.

"Go," said he, "wash in the pool of Siloam."

The man rose to his feet. He stood a moment in the road, the clay drying upon his face, the warmth of the sun upon his skin. Then he turned and made his way through the streets of Jerusalem, guided by sound — the press of bodies, the slope of the ground beneath his feet. He came unto the pool of Siloam. The water was cool and still. He kneeled at the stone edge, cupped water in his hands, and washed the clay from his eyes.

And he received his sight.

Light poured in upon him. The world sharpened round about — the blue of the water, the grey of the stone, the green of the trees along the wall. He saw his own hands, wet and trembling, for the first time in his life. He saw the sky. He stood at the edge of the pool, turning slowly, taking in the astonishing flood of shapes and colours that was the world.

He walked again through the streets, and his neighbours were first to mark him. "Is not this he that sat and begged?" said some. Others said, "Nay, he is like him." But the man said, "I am he."

"How then were thine eyes opened?"

He told them all. "A man that is called Jesus made clay, and anointed mine eyes, and said unto me, Go to the pool of Siloam, and wash. I went and washed, and I received sight."

They brought him before the Pharisees. The Pharisees questioned him straitly — how it had come to pass, who had done it, what had been spoken. Some of the Pharisees said, "This man is not of God, because he keepeth not the sabbath day." Others said, "How can a man that is a sinner do such miracles?" And there was a division among them.

They turned to the man. "What sayest thou of him, that he hath opened thine eyes?"

He answered plainly. "He is a prophet."

The Pharisees would not receive this. They called his parents and questioned them. "Is this your son? Was he born blind? How doth he now see?"

His parents were afraid. "We know that this is our son," they said, "and that he was born blind: but by what means he now seeth, we know not. He is of age; ask him."

The Pharisees called the man back a second time. "Give God the praise," said they. "We know that this man is a sinner."

He that had been blind answered them. "Whether he be a sinner or no, I know not: one thing I know, that, whereas I was blind, now I see."

They pressed him. They accused him. And because he would not change his testimony, they cast him out.

When Jesus heard that they had cast him out, he went and found him.

"Dost thou believe on the Son of God?" he asked.

The man looked upon him — looked upon him with the eyes that had been opened, eyes that had never beheld a face until that morning. "Who is he, Lord," he said, "that I might believe on him?"

Jesus saith unto him, "Thou hast both seen him, and it is he that talketh with thee."

Something settled in the man's countenance. Not surprise, but recognition — as though the light which had entered his eyes had now reached something deeper still.

"Lord, I believe," he said.

And he worshipped him.
