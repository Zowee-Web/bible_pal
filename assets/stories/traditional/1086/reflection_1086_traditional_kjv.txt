He was born into darkness, and it was the only world he knew. No man asked whether he desired sight. No man explained why he lacked it. The darkness was simply there — from the beginning, without cause, without remedy.

What changed was not his own doing. He found no cure. He earned not the healing. A stranger kneeled beside him, set clay upon his eyes, and bade him wash. He went. He washed. And all was changed.

The hardest part was perhaps not the blindness itself. It was what followed — the questions, the doubt, the men who could not receive what had come to pass. He was made to explain that which he scarce understood, again and again, to those who would not believe.

And when they cast him out, Jesus came and found him.

Sometimes those who see most clearly are those who have the least cause to believe — and believe notwithstanding.
