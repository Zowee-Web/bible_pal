A clear morning brake over Jerusalem, with the dust of long roads settling upon the worn stones and the broken walls drying in the sun. They that were returned had brought the foundation of the new temple unto completion. The priests went forth into their places upon the open ground in white linen with the silver trumpets in their hands; the Levites of the sons of Asaph bare the cymbals of brass; the singers gathered with their faces toward the place where the great house had once stood. The people of the city came out to behold.

When the builders laid the foundation of the temple of the Lord, they set the priests in their apparel with trumpets, and the Levites the sons of Asaph with cymbals, to praise the Lord, after the ordinance of David king of Israel.

And they sang together by course in praising and giving thanks unto the Lord, saying, "For he is good, for his mercy endureth for ever toward Israel." And all the people shouted with a great shout, when they praised the Lord, because the foundation of the house of the Lord was laid.

The shout went forth past the ruined walls and over the slopes. But among the throng stood ancient men — priests, Levites, and chief of the fathers — who had seen the first house in their youth, before the burning, before the carrying away. And when the foundation of the new house was laid before their eyes, they wept aloud, even as the shout of joy went up round about them.

But many of the priests and Levites and chief of the fathers, who were ancient men, that had seen the first house, when the foundation of this house was laid before their eyes, wept with a loud voice; and many shouted aloud for joy,

so that the people could not discern the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping of the people: for the people shouted with a loud shout, and the noise was heard afar off.

The sun stood higher now over the open ground. The trumpets had been let down, the cymbals hung still at the Levites' sides, and the two sounds that had risen together — the shout of joy and the weeping of remembrance — went forth beyond all hearing.
