A clear morning broke over Jerusalem, with the dust of long roads settling on the worn stones and the broken walls drying in the sun. The returners had brought the foundation of the new temple to completion. The priests moved into their positions on the open ground in white linen with the silver trumpets in their hands; the Levites of the sons of Asaph carried the bronze cymbals; the singers gathered with their faces toward the place where the great house had once stood. The people of the city came out to watch.

When the builders laid the foundation of Yahweh's temple, they set the priests in their clothing with trumpets, with the Levites the sons of Asaph with cymbals, to praise Yahweh, according to the directions of David king of Israel.

They sang to one another in praising and giving thanks to Yahweh, "For he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever toward Israel." All the people shouted with a great shout, when they praised Yahweh, because the foundation of Yahweh's house had been laid.

The shout travelled out past the ruined walls and over the slopes. But among the crowd stood old men — priests, Levites, and heads of fathers' houses — who had seen the first house in their youth, before the burning, before the carrying away. And when the foundation of the new house was laid before their eyes, they wept aloud, even as the shout of joy went up around them.

But many of the priests and Levites and heads of fathers' households, the old men who had seen the first house, when the foundation of this house was laid before their eyes, wept with a loud voice. Many also shouted aloud for joy,

so that the people could not discern the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping of the people; for the people shouted with a loud shout, and the noise was heard far away.

The sun stood higher now over the open ground. The trumpets had been lowered, the cymbals hung quiet at the Levites' sides, and the two sounds that had risen together — the shout of joy and the weeping of memory — moved out beyond all hearing.
