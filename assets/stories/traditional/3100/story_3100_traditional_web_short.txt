Kish, a mighty man of valor from the tribe of Benjamin, had a son named Saul. There was not among the children of Israel a more handsome person than he, and from his shoulders and upward he was taller than any of the people.

The donkeys of Kish were lost. Kish said to Saul, “Now take one of the servants with you, and arise, go look for the donkeys.” Saul passed through the hill country of Ephraim and the land of Shalishah without finding them. He crossed the land of Shaalim and the land of the Benjamites, and still the donkeys were not found.

In the land of Zuph, Saul turned to his servant. “Come! Let’s return,” he said, “lest my father stop caring about the donkeys and be anxious for us.” But the servant spoke of a man of God in that city, a man held in honor, whose every word surely happened. Perhaps he could tell them which way to go. The bread in their sacks was spent, but the servant still had the fourth part of a shekel of silver for the man of God. “Well said,” Saul answered. “Come! Let’s go.”

Young maidens going out to draw water told them the seer had come that day, for the people had a sacrifice in the high place, and he would bless the sacrifice before the invited guests ate. As Saul and his servant came within the city, Samuel came out toward them.

Yahweh had revealed to Samuel a day before, “Tomorrow about this time I will send you a man out of the land of Benjamin, and you shall anoint him to be prince over my people Israel.” When Samuel saw Saul, Yahweh said to him, “Behold, the man of whom I spoke to you! He will have authority over my people.”

Saul approached Samuel in the gateway and asked where the seer’s house was. “I am the seer,” Samuel answered. The donkeys lost three days ago had been found. For whom did all Israel desire? Was it not Saul and all his father’s house? Saul answered that he was a Benjamite from the smallest of the tribes of Israel, his family the least in Benjamin. Why then did Samuel speak to him like this?

Yet Samuel seated Saul and his servant in the best place among about thirty invited guests, and the portion that had been set aside was placed before Saul, kept for the appointed time. Saul ate with Samuel that day, and that evening Samuel talked with him on the housetop. At daybreak Samuel sent the servant on ahead and said, “But stand still first, that I may cause you to hear God’s message.”
