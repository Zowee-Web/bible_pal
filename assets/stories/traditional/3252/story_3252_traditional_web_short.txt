King Solomon commanded Abiathar the priest to go to his own fields at Anathoth. He told Abiathar that he deserved death, but would not be killed at that time. Solomon gave two reasons: Abiathar had carried the Lord Yahweh's ark before David, Solomon's father, and had shared all David's afflictions.

Solomon removed Abiathar from serving as priest to Yahweh. Thus Yahweh's word concerning Eli's house, spoken in Shiloh, was fulfilled.

The news reached Joab. He had followed Adonijah, though he had not followed Absalom. Joab fled to Yahweh's Tent and grasped the horns of the altar.

Solomon was told where Joab had gone and that he was beside the altar. The king sent Benaiah, Jehoiada's son, with the command to fall on him.

At the Tent, Benaiah delivered the king's summons: “Come out!”

Joab refused. “No; but I will die here.”

Benaiah returned to Solomon and reported Joab's answer. The king told him to do as Joab had said: kill him and bury him. Solomon said this would remove from himself and his father's house the blood Joab had shed without cause.

Solomon declared that Yahweh would return Joab’s blood upon his own head because he had killed two men with the sword, men more righteous and better than himself. The king said David had not known of their killing. Solomon named them: Abner, son of Ner, commander of Israel’s army, and Amasa, son of Jether, commander of Judah’s army.

The king went on to say that their blood would rest upon Joab and his offspring forever. For David, his offspring, his house, and his throne, Solomon declared there would be peace forever from Yahweh.

Benaiah went up and killed Joab. Joab was buried in his own house in the wilderness.

Solomon appointed Benaiah over the army in Joab's place. He appointed Zadok as priest in Abiathar's place.
