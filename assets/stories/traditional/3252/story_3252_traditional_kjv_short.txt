King Solomon commanded Abiathar the priest to go to his own fields at Anathoth. He told Abiathar that he was worthy of death, but would not be put to death at that time. Solomon gave his reasons: Abiathar had borne the ark of the Lord God before David, Solomon's father, and had suffered in all David's afflictions.

Solomon removed Abiathar from the priesthood of the Lord. Thus the Lord's word concerning the house of Eli, spoken in Shiloh, was fulfilled.

Tidings reached Joab. He had turned after Adonijah, though he had not turned after Absalom. Joab fled to the Lord's tabernacle and caught hold of the altar's horns.

Solomon heard where Joab had gone and that he was by the altar. The king sent Benaiah, son of Jehoiada, commanding him to fall upon Joab.

At the tabernacle Benaiah gave the king's command: “Come forth.”

Joab answered, “Nay; but I will die here.”

Benaiah returned to the king and reported what Joab had said. Solomon commanded him to do as Joab had spoken: slay him and bury him. The king said this would take away from himself and his father's house the innocent blood that Joab had shed.

Solomon declared that the Lord would return Joab’s blood upon his own head, for he had slain two men with the sword, men more righteous and better than himself. The king said David had not known of their killing. Solomon named them: Abner, son of Ner, captain of Israel’s host, and Amasa, son of Jether, captain of Judah’s host.

The king further declared that their blood would rest upon Joab and his seed for ever. For David, his seed, his house, and his throne, Solomon said there would be peace for ever from the Lord.

Benaiah went up and slew Joab. Joab was buried in his own house in the wilderness.

Solomon set Benaiah over the host in Joab's place. He placed Zadok in the priesthood that Abiathar had held.
