The servants of David and Joab returned from pursuing a troop, bringing great spoil with them. Abner was no longer with David in Hebron, for David had sent him away in peace. When Joab heard that Abner had come unto the king and departed safely, he went to David and challenged what had been done. He asked, “What hast thou done?” Joab said that Abner had come to deceive the king and to know all his going out and coming in.

After Joab came out from David, he sent messengers after Abner. They brought Abner again from the well of Sirah, but David knew it not. When Abner returned to Hebron, Joab took him aside in the gate to speak with him quietly. There Joab smote him under the fifth rib, and Abner died for the blood of Asahel his brother.

When David heard it, he said that he and his kingdom were guiltless before the Lord for ever from the blood of Abner. He pronounced judgment upon the house of Joab. Joab and Abishai his brother had slain Abner because Abner had slain Asahel in battle at Gibeon.

David commanded Joab and all the people with him, “Rend your clothes, gird you with sackcloth, and mourn before Abner.” King David himself followed the bier. They buried Abner in Hebron, and the king lifted up his voice and wept at the grave. All the people wept with him.

David lamented, “Died Abner as a fool dieth? Thy hands were not bound, nor thy feet put into fetters. As a man falleth before wicked men, so fellest thou.” The people wept again over him.

While it was yet day, the people came to cause David to eat meat. David sware that he would taste neither bread nor ought else until the sun went down. The people took notice, and the king’s mourning pleased them. That day all Israel understood that it was not of the king to slay Abner.

David told his servants that a prince and a great man had fallen that day in Israel. Though anointed king, David said he was weak and that the sons of Zeruiah were too hard for him. He ended by declaring that the Lord would reward the doer of evil according to his wickedness.
