David’s servants and Joab returned from a raid with great plunder. Abner was no longer with David in Hebron, because David had sent him away in peace. When Joab heard that Abner had visited the king and departed safely, he went to David and challenged the decision. Joab claimed that Abner had come to deceive the king and learn his movements and actions.

After leaving David, Joab sent messengers after Abner. They brought Abner back from the well of Sirah, but David did not know about it. When Abner returned to Hebron, Joab drew him aside into the middle of the gate to speak with him quietly. There Joab struck him in the body, and Abner died for the blood of Asahel, Joab’s brother.

When David heard what had happened, he declared that he and his kingdom were forever guiltless before Yahweh concerning Abner’s blood. He pronounced judgment upon Joab’s house. Joab and his brother Abishai had killed Abner because Abner had killed Asahel in battle at Gibeon.

David commanded Joab and all the people with him to tear their clothes, put on sackcloth, and mourn before Abner. David himself followed the bier. They buried Abner in Hebron, and David lifted his voice and wept at the grave. All the people wept with him.

David lamented, “Should Abner die as a fool dies? Your hands weren’t bound, and your feet weren’t put into fetters. As a man falls before the children of iniquity, so you fell.” The people wept again over him.

While it was still day, the people urged David to eat, but he swore that he would taste no food until sunset. The people noticed his mourning and were pleased. That day all Israel understood that the king had not ordered Abner’s death.

David told his servants that a prince and a great man had fallen in Israel. Though anointed king, David said he was weak that day and that the sons of Zeruiah were too hard for him. He ended by asking Yahweh to repay the evildoer according to his wickedness.
