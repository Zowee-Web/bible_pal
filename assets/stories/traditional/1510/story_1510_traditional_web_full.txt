Naaman was captain of the host of the king of Syria. He was a great man with his master. He was honorable in the sight of all that knew him. By him the LORD had given deliverance to Syria more than once. He was a mighty man in valor. He was a leper.

A little maid had been carried captive out of the land of Israel in one of the raids. She waited on Naaman's wife. She knew the trouble that was in the house. She had spoken of her own country to the wife many times. One day she said to her mistress: would that my lord were with the prophet who is in Samaria. He would recover him of his leprosy.

The wife told Naaman. Naaman told the king of Syria what the maid had said. The king of Syria said: go to. Go, and I will send a letter to the king of Israel. Naaman went, with him ten talents of silver and six thousand pieces of gold and ten changes of raiment. He carried the letter to the king of Israel.

The king of Israel rent his clothes when he read it. He said: am I God, to kill and to make alive, that this man sends to me to recover a man of his leprosy? See how he seeks a quarrel against me.

Elisha the man of God heard. He sent to the king: let him come to me, and he shall know that there is a prophet in Israel.

Naaman came up to Samaria with his horses and with his chariots. The chariots stopped at the door of the house of Elisha. The horses stood waiting in the road outside. The dust of the journey was still on the wheels.

Elisha did not come out. He sent a messenger to him. The messenger said: go and wash in the Jordan seven times. Your flesh shall come again to you. You shall be clean.

Naaman was angry. He turned to go away. He said: I thought he would come out to me. I thought he would stand and call on the name of the LORD his God. I thought he would strike his hand over the place and recover the leper. Are not Abana and Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? May I not wash in them and be clean?

He went away in a rage. His chariots turned back from Elisha's door. They went out of Samaria the way they had come.

His servants came near to him on the road. They spoke to him with respect. They said: my father, if the prophet had asked you to do some great thing, would you not have done it? How much rather then, when he says to you, wash and be clean?

Naaman heard them. He did not answer at once. Then he turned the chariots back toward the river.

He went down to the Jordan. He took off his garments. He stepped into the water. He dipped himself once. He dipped himself twice. He dipped himself three times. He dipped himself four times. He dipped himself five times. He dipped himself six times. He dipped himself the seventh time.

His flesh came again to him like the flesh of a little child. He was clean.

He came up out of the water. The servants helped him on with his clothes. He stood on the bank a long while. The Jordan went on past him. The chariots stood waiting on the bank.

He went back to Elisha's door. The dust of the river was on his sandals.
