Saul spoke unto Samuel, acknowledging his sin. “I have sinned: for I have transgressed the commandment of the Lord, and thy words: because I feared the people, and obeyed their voice.”

He sought pardon and entreated Samuel to accompany him: “Now therefore, I pray thee, pardon my sin, and turn again with me, that I may worship the Lord.”

Samuel would not return. He told Saul that, because Saul had rejected the word of the Lord, the Lord had rejected him from being king over Israel.

Samuel turned to depart. Saul laid hold upon the skirt of his mantle, and it rent. At the tearing of the mantle, Samuel declared that the Lord had rent Israel's kingdom from Saul that day and given it to a neighbour better than he.

Samuel also spoke of the Strength of Israel, who would neither lie nor repent, for he was not a man that he should repent.

“I have sinned,” Saul said again. Yet he entreated Samuel to honour him before the elders of his people and before Israel. He asked Samuel to turn again with him, that he might worship the Lord, Samuel's God.

Samuel turned back after Saul, and Saul worshipped the Lord.

Then Samuel commanded that Agag, king of the Amalekites, be brought before him. Agag came delicately. “Surely the bitterness of death is past,” he said.

Samuel answered, “As thy sword hath made women childless, so shall thy mother be childless among women.” Before the Lord at Gilgal, Samuel hewed Agag in pieces.

Samuel went his way to Ramah, while Saul returned to his house in Gibeah of Saul. Until the day of his death, Samuel came no more to see Saul. Nevertheless, he mourned for Saul; and the Lord repented that he had made Saul king over Israel.
