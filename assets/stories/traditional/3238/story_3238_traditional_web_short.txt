Saul spoke to Samuel and confessed his sin. “I have sinned; for I have transgressed the commandment of Yahweh and your words, because I feared the people and obeyed their voice.”

He asked for pardon and for Samuel's company, saying, “Now therefore, please pardon my sin, and turn again with me, that I may worship Yahweh.”

Samuel refused to go back. Saul had rejected Yahweh's word, he said, and Yahweh had rejected Saul as king over Israel.

As Samuel turned to leave, Saul caught the skirt of his robe. The cloth tore. Samuel spoke again, declaring that Yahweh had torn the kingdom of Israel from Saul that day and given it to a neighbor better than he. He added that the Strength of Israel would neither lie nor repent, for he was not a man who should repent.

Saul answered with another confession: “I have sinned.” Then he asked Samuel to honor him before the elders of his people and before Israel. He pleaded with Samuel to come back with him, that he might worship Yahweh, Samuel's God.

This time Samuel returned with Saul, and Saul worshiped Yahweh.

Samuel then called for Agag, king of the Amalekites, to be brought to him. Agag approached cheerfully and said, “Surely the bitterness of death is past.”

Samuel answered the king by speaking of the women whom Agag's sword had left childless. As Agag had done to them, Samuel declared, so his own mother would be childless among women. There in Gilgal, before Yahweh, Samuel cut Agag in pieces.

Samuel left for Ramah. Saul went up to his own house at Gibeah of Saul. Samuel did not come again to see Saul until the day of his death. Yet Samuel mourned for Saul, and Yahweh grieved that he had made Saul king over Israel.
