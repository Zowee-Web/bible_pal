Ephraim’s men chided Gideon sharply because he had not called them to fight the Midianites. He asked what his deeds were beside theirs. Was not Ephraim’s gleaning better than the vintage of Abi–ezer? God had delivered Oreb and Zeeb, princes of Midian, into their hands. Their anger abated at his answer.

With three hundred men, Gideon passed over Jordan, faint yet pursuing. At Succoth he asked bread for those who followed him, for they were faint. He pursued Zebah and Zalmunna, kings of Midian. Succoth’s princes asked whether the kings’ hands were already in his hand, that they should feed his army.

Gideon said that when the Lord delivered those kings unto him, he would tear the flesh of Succoth’s princes with wilderness thorns and briers. Penuel answered his request as Succoth had. Gideon warned them, “When I come again in peace, I will break down this tower.”

At Karkor the kings had about fifteen thousand men remaining from the hosts of the east; an hundred and twenty thousand swordsmen had fallen. By the way of the tent dwellers, east of Nobah and Jogbehah, Gideon came upon the secure host and smote it. He pursued the fleeing kings, took both, and discomfited all the host.

Gideon son of Joash returned before the sun was up. He caught a young man of Succoth and enquired of him. The youth described the city’s princes and elders, threescore and seventeen men.

Gideon presented Zebah and Zalmunna to Succoth, recalling its taunt about bread for his weary men. He took the elders of the city, and with wilderness thorns and briers taught the men of Succoth. He beat down Penuel’s tower and slew the men of that city.

He asked the kings what manner of men they had slain at Tabor. They answered, “As thou art, so were they; each one resembled the children of a king.”

“They were my brethren, even the sons of my mother,” Gideon said. “As the Lord liveth, if ye had saved them alive, I would not slay you.”

He bade Jether his firstborn slay them. The youth drew no sword, for he feared, being yet a youth.

Zebah and Zalmunna called upon Gideon to rise and fall upon them: as the man was, so was his strength. Gideon arose and slew the two kings, then took the ornaments from their camels’ necks.
