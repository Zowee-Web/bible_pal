The men of Ephraim sharply rebuked Gideon for not calling them to fight Midian. Gideon asked what he had done compared with them. Wasn’t Ephraim’s gleaning better than Abiezer’s vintage? God had delivered Oreb and Zeeb, Midian’s princes, into their hands. At his answer, their anger subsided.

Gideon crossed the Jordan with his three hundred men, faint but still pursuing. He asked Succoth for bread, naming Zebah and Zalmunna, the kings of Midian he pursued. Succoth’s princes asked whether he already held the kings’ hands, that they should feed his army.

Gideon answered that when Yahweh delivered the kings to him, he would tear the flesh of Succoth’s princes with wilderness thorns and briers. At Penuel he made the same request and received the same answer. He warned them, “When I come again in peace, I will break down this tower.”

The kings were at Karkor with about fifteen thousand men, the remnant of the eastern army. One hundred twenty thousand swordsmen had fallen. Gideon approached east of Nobah and Jogbehah by the tent dwellers’ way. He struck while the army felt secure. The kings fled; Gideon pursued, captured both, and threw the whole army into confusion.

Returning from the ascent of Heres, Gideon son of Joash caught a young man from Succoth. Questioned by Gideon, he described the city’s princes and elders, seventy-seven men.

Gideon brought the captured kings before Succoth and recalled its taunt about feeding his weary men. He took the elders, and with wilderness thorns and briers taught the men of Succoth. He also broke down Penuel’s tower and killed the men of that city.

Then Gideon asked the kings what kind of men they had killed at Tabor. They answered, “They were like you. They all resembled the children of a king.”

“They were my brothers, the sons of my mother,” Gideon said. “As Yahweh lives, if you had saved them alive, I would not kill you.”

He commanded Jether, his firstborn, to kill them. But Jether did not draw his sword. He was still a youth, and he was afraid.

Zebah and Zalmunna called on Gideon himself to strike them, saying that a man’s strength was according to the man. Gideon rose and killed both kings. He took the crescents from their camels’ necks.
