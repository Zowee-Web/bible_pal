The third year of Belshazzar was full of feasting, and the night of the feast was the loudest of them all. A thousand of his lords reclined along the long tables. The lamps stood in rows above their heads. The wine went around. The king sat at the high seat with his cup in his hand, and his cup was never empty for long.

While he tasted the wine, he gave the command. Servants ran for the storerooms. They came back through the hall slowly, their arms full of the gold and silver vessels of the house of God, which Nebuchadnezzar his father had taken out of the temple in Jerusalem. The cups of the sanctuary were set among the cups of the feast. The king lifted one of them. His lords lifted theirs. They drank the wine, and they praised the gods of gold and of silver, of bronze, of iron, of wood, and of stone.

In the same hour the fingers of a man's hand came forth.

They came out beside the lampstand. They moved against the plaster of the wall in the lamplight. There was no arm behind them. There was no body. There were only the fingers, writing.

The king saw the part of the hand that wrote. His cup stayed at his lip and did not move. Then his face changed in him. The flush of the wine drained out of it. His hands began to shake against the gold of the cup, and a drop of wine ran down the back of his wrist. The joints of his hips were loosened beneath his robe. His knees struck one against another. He could not hear them strike above the music, but he heard it in his own body.

He cried aloud. The music broke off mid-note. The harpists set their harps against the columns. The pipers lowered their pipes. "Bring in the wise men," he said, and his voice came thin against the quiet. "Bring in the enchanters and the soothsayers. Bring them now."

Servants ran. The lords stayed on their couches with the holy vessels still in their hands. None of them looked at the king. They looked at the wall. The fingers were no longer there. Only the letters remained.

The wise men of Babylon came in. They came in from their houses and their watch-rooms, blinking against the lamps of the hall. They gathered before the wall. They lifted their hands toward the letters. They turned to one another. They turned back. They could not read them. They could not make the interpretation known to the king. One after another they stepped away and stood with their heads bowed.

The king was greatly troubled. His face was changed in him a second time. His lords were perplexed. The wine in the holy vessels was untouched now, and the lords nearest the king began to lower their eyes from his face to the table, and from the table to the floor, and they did not lift them again.

Then the queen came into the banquet house.

She crossed the long hall between the tables of the lords. Her step was even against the trembling of his. "O king, live forever," she said. "Let not your thoughts trouble you, nor your face be changed. There is a man in your kingdom in whom is the spirit of the holy gods. In the days of your father, light and understanding and wisdom were found in him. Let Daniel be called."

The king sent for him.

Daniel was brought up through the wine-stained hall. He came as an old man, unhurried, and he stood before the young king. He looked at the vessels on the table. He looked at the lampstand. Then he looked at the king.

"Let your gifts be to yourself," he said. "Give your rewards to another. Yet I will read the writing to the king, and make known to him the interpretation."

He spoke of Nebuchadnezzar, of greatness given and humbling taken, and of the lesson the father had been made to learn. Then he turned his face to Belshazzar.

"You have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this. They have brought the vessels of his house before you, and you and your lords have drunk wine from them. The God in whose hand your breath is, you have not glorified. Therefore the part of the hand was sent, and this writing was inscribed."

He stepped toward the wall.

"Mene. Mene. Tekel. Upharsin." His voice carried into the hush. "God has numbered your kingdom, and brought it to an end. You are weighed in the balances, and found wanting. Your kingdom is divided, and given to the Medes and Persians."

The king heard him. The hall heard him. The lords did not lift their faces from the floor.

He gave the order. They clothed Daniel with purple. They put a chain of gold around his neck. The proclamation was made concerning him. The king sat at the high seat among the holy vessels, and the lamps burned on above them, and the letters were still on the wall when the night came down on Babylon.
