The third year of Belshazzar was full of feasting, and the night of the feast was the loudest of them all. A thousand of his lords reclined along the long tables. The lamps stood in rows above their heads. The wine went round. The king sat at the high seat with his cup in his hand, and his cup was never empty for long.

While he tasted the wine, he gave the commandment. Servants ran unto the storehouses. They came back through the hall slowly, their arms full of the gold and silver vessels of the house of God, which Nebuchadnezzar his father had taken out of the temple in Jerusalem. The cups of the sanctuary were set among the cups of the feast. The king lifted up one of them. His lords lifted theirs. They drank the wine, and praised the gods of gold, and of silver, of brass, of iron, of wood, and of stone.

In the same hour came forth fingers of a man's hand.

They came out beside the candlestick. They moved against the plaister of the wall in the lamplight. There was no arm behind them. There was no body. There were only the fingers, writing.

The king saw the part of the hand that wrote. The cup stayed at his lip and moved not. Then his countenance was changed in him. The flush of the wine drained away. His hands began to shake against the gold of the cup, and a drop of wine ran down the back of his wrist. The joints of his loins were loosed beneath his robe. His knees smote one against another. He could not hear them smite above the music, yet he heard it in his own body.

He cried aloud. The music brake off mid-note. The harpers laid their harps against the columns. The pipers lowered their pipes. "Bring in the wise men," he said, and his voice came thin against the quiet. "Bring in the enchanters and the soothsayers. Bring them now."

Servants ran. The lords stayed upon their couches with the holy vessels yet in their hands. None of them looked upon the king. They looked upon the wall. The fingers were no more there. Only the letters remained.

The wise men of Babylon came in. They came in from their houses and their watch-chambers, blinking against the lamps of the hall. They gathered before the wall. They lifted up their hands toward the letters. They turned one to another. They turned back. They could not read them. They could not make known the interpretation unto the king. One after another they stepped away and stood with their heads bowed.

The king was greatly troubled. His countenance was changed in him a second time. His lords were astonied. The wine in the holy vessels was untouched now, and the lords nearest the king began to lower their eyes from his face to the table, and from the table to the floor, and they lifted them not again.

Then came the queen into the banquet house.

She crossed the long hall between the tables of the lords. Her step was even against the trembling of his. "O king, live for ever," she said. "Let not thy thoughts trouble thee, nor thy countenance be changed. There is a man in thy kingdom in whom is the spirit of the holy gods. In the days of thy father, light and understanding and wisdom were found in him. Let Daniel be called."

The king sent for him.

Daniel was brought up through the wine-stained hall. He came as an old man, unhurried, and he stood before the young king. He looked upon the vessels on the table. He looked upon the candlestick. Then he looked upon the king.

"Let thy gifts be to thyself," he said. "Give thy rewards to another. Yet I will read the writing unto the king, and make known unto him the interpretation."

He spake of Nebuchadnezzar, of greatness given and humbling taken, and of the lesson the father had been made to learn. Then he turned his face unto Belshazzar.

"Thou hast not humbled thine heart, though thou knewest all this. They have brought the vessels of his house before thee, and thou and thy lords have drunk wine therein. The God in whose hand thy breath is, hast thou not glorified. Therefore was the part of the hand sent, and this writing was inscribed."

He stepped toward the wall.

"Mene. Mene. Tekel. Upharsin." His voice carried into the hush. "God hath numbered thy kingdom, and brought it to an end. Thou art weighed in the balances, and found wanting. Thy kingdom is divided, and given to the Medes and Persians."

The king heard him. The hall heard him. The lords lifted not their faces from the floor.

He gave the commandment. They clothed Daniel with scarlet. They put a chain of gold about his neck. The proclamation was made concerning him. The king sat at the high seat among the holy vessels, and the lamps burned on above them, and the letters were yet upon the wall when the night came down on Babylon.
