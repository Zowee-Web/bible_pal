The feast was in the third year of the reign of Belshazzar.

A thousand of the lords of the realm were in the hall of the king. The wine was being poured. The lamps were many. The king sat at the high table, lifting a cup in his hand.

Then Belshazzar gave commandment.

"Bring the vessels of gold and silver which Nebuchadnezzar my father took out of the temple at Jerusalem. The king, and his princes, his wives, and his concubines, shall drink therein."

The servants brought them up out of the storehouses. The gold of the temple was set upon the tables of the feast. The silver bowls. The chalices of the house of the Lord. The vessels stood among the wine.

The king drank. The princes drank. The wives drank. The concubines drank.

They praised the gods of gold, and of silver, of brass, of iron, of wood, and of stone.

Then the hand appeared.

In the same hour came forth fingers of a man's hand out of the air over against the candlestick. The hand moved across the plaister of the wall. The fingers wrote letters in the lamp-light.

The king saw it before the rest.

His countenance was changed. The cup fell from his hand. His knees smote one against another. The colour went out of his face.

He cried aloud for the astrologers. He cried aloud for the Chaldeans. He cried aloud for the soothsayers of Babylon.

They came. They stood before the wall in the lamp-light. They could not read that which was written there.

The princes held their peace. The thousand held their peace. The wine was yet upon the tables, but no man lifted a cup.

One of the princes drew back his chair from the table. He stood against the wall. The man at his side did likewise. No man in the hall came forward toward the writing. The writing abode where the fingers had set it.

Then came the queen into the hall.

"O king, live for ever," she said. "There is a man in thy kingdom, in whom is the spirit of the holy gods. In the days of thy father light and understanding were found in him. Let Daniel be called."

Daniel was brought before the king in his hoary hair. He read the writing upon the wall.

He said, "This is the writing that was written: MENE, MENE, TEKEL, UPHARSIN. This is the interpretation of the thing: God hath numbered thy kingdom, and finished it. Thou art weighed in the balances, and art found wanting. Thy kingdom is divided, and given to the Medes and Persians."

Belshazzar heard. The hall heard.

The lamps in the hall went on burning over the silver vessels of Jerusalem.

In that night was Belshazzar the king of the Chaldeans slain.
