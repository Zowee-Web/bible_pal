The feast was in the third year of Belshazzar's reign.

A thousand of the great men of the realm were in the hall of the king. The wine was being poured. The lamps were many. The king sat at the high table, lifting a cup in his hand.

Then Belshazzar gave the command.

"Bring the vessels of gold and silver that Nebuchadnezzar my father took out of the temple in Jerusalem. The king and his princes, his wives and his concubines, will drink from them."

The servants brought them up out of the storerooms. The gold of the temple was set on the tables of the feast. The silver bowls. The chalices of the house of Yahweh. The vessels stood among the wine.

The king drank. The princes drank. The wives drank. The concubines drank.

They praised the gods of gold, and silver, and bronze, and iron, and wood, and stone.

Then the hand appeared.

In the same hour, the fingers of a man's hand came forth out of the air over the lamp-stand. The hand moved across the plaster of the wall. The fingers traced letters in the lamp-light.

The king saw it before the others.

His face changed. The cup fell from his hand. His knees began to strike one against the other. The color went out of his face.

He cried out for the astrologers. He cried out for the Chaldeans. He cried out for the soothsayers of Babylon.

They came. They stood before the wall in the lamp-light. They could not read what was written there.

The princes were silent. The thousand were silent. The wine was still on the tables, but no man lifted a cup.

One of the princes pushed his chair back from the table. He stood against the wall. The man at his side did the same. No one in the hall came forward toward the writing. The writing stayed where the fingers had set it.

Then the queen came into the hall.

"O king, live forever," she said. "There is a man in your kingdom in whom is the spirit of the holy gods. In the days of your father, light and understanding were found in him. Let Daniel be called."

Daniel was brought before the king in his white hair. He read the writing on the wall.

He said, "This is the writing that was inscribed: MENE, MENE, TEKEL, UPHARSIN. The interpretation is this: God has numbered your kingdom and brought it to an end. You have been weighed in the balances and found wanting. Your kingdom is divided and given to the Medes and Persians."

Belshazzar heard. The hall heard.

The lamps in the hall went on burning over the silver vessels of Jerusalem.

In that night Belshazzar the Chaldean king was slain.
