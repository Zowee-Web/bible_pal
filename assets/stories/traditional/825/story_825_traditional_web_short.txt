Elijah went into a cave. He spent the night there, alone and restless. As darkness pressed in, a word from Yahweh came to him.

“What are you doing here, Elijah?” the Lord asked.

Elijah answered quickly, his voice trembling, “I have been very jealous for Yahweh, the God of Armies. The children of Israel have forsaken your covenant, thrown down your altars, and slain your prophets with the sword. I, even I only, am left, and they seek my life to take it away.”

The Lord replied, “Go out, and stand on the mountain before Yahweh.”

Elijah rose and stepped forward. The mountain air was tense and still. Suddenly, Yahweh passed by. A great and strong wind tore the mountains apart, shattering rocks before Yahweh. But Yahweh was not in the wind.

After the wind came an earthquake. Stones shifted. The ground rumbled beneath Elijah’s feet. But Yahweh was not in the earthquake.

Once the shaking stopped, fire came. Bright flames licked across the mountain, scorching the darkness. Yet Yahweh was not in the fire.

Then, after the fire, silence settled — a gentle whisper, a still small voice.

When Elijah heard it, he wrapped his face in his cloak. He stepped out and stood at the entrance of the cave. Again, he heard a voice, quiet but clear. “What are you doing here, Elijah?”

Elijah answered as before, sorrow pressing in. “I have been very jealous for Yahweh, the God of Armies. The children of Israel have forsaken your covenant, thrown down your altars, and slain your prophets with the sword. I, even I only, am left, and they seek my life to take it away.”

Yahweh spoke to him, patient and resolute. “Go, return on your way to the wilderness of Damascus. When you arrive, anoint Hazael to be king over Syria. Anoint Jehu, the son of Nimshi, to be king over Israel. Anoint Elisha, the son of Shaphat of Abel Meholah, to be prophet in your place.”

He continued, “He who escapes from the sword of Hazael, Jehu will kill. He who escapes from the sword of Jehu, Elisha will kill. Yet I have reserved seven thousand in Israel, all the knees which have not bowed to Baal, and every mouth which has not kissed him.”

Elijah listened in the anxious quiet and prepared to obey all that the Lord had spoken.
