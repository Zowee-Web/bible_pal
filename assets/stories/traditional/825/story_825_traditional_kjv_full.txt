Elijah went up the long, stony slopes of Horeb, the mount of God. The rocks pressed beneath his feet. The air grew thin and cool as he ascended. He came thither unto a cave hewn into the mountain's side. There, shadows claimed the entering in, and the world seemed far off.

Elijah went into the cave, bearing the burden of dread and weariness. Darkness pressed round about him. Without, the wind moaned along the barren slopes. He sat in stillness, alone. Days of fleeing, of fear, weighed heavily upon him. The tidings of Jezebel's threats echoed in his memory as a warning bell. His heart pounded with uncertainty. He drew his mantle close and waited.

And, behold, the word of the Lord came unto Elijah. "What doest thou here, Elijah?" The question lingered in the air of the cave, gentle yet insistent. Elijah's voice was brittle and weary: "I have been very jealous for the Lord God of hosts: for the children of Israel have forsaken thy covenant, thrown down thine altars, and slain thy prophets with the sword; and I, even I only, am left; and they seek my life, to take it away."

Silence followed. The cave grew colder. Elijah's voice faded into the stones round about him. His hands trembled. He durst not hope.

Then the Lord spake again, charging him, "Go forth, and stand upon the mount before the Lord." Elijah arose slowly. His legs shook as he stepped toward the mouth of the cave. The world without was harsh and wild.

And, behold, a great and strong wind rent the mountains. Stones brake in pieces, and dust spun into the air. The blast howled past Elijah, fierce and wild, breaking the rocks before him. But the Lord was not in the wind.

The wind fell silent. The mount itself began to shake beneath Elijah's feet. The ground heaved and rumbled. Boulders came crashing down the slope. Elijah braced himself against the trembling earth. But the Lord was not in the earthquake.

The shaking ceased. Then fire flashed across the hillside. Flames crackled and surged in the sky, devouring the dry grasses and brush. Breathless heat pulsed through the air. Elijah shielded his face from the blistering light. But the Lord was not in the fire.

The fire abated. A deep hush fell over the mount. Silence, heavy as stone, descended. Into that stillness came a still small voice. It slipped across the rocks and settled in Elijah's soul.

Elijah drew his mantle over his face. The weight of awe and holy fear filled him. He stepped carefully toward the sound, his heart pounding as quietly as the whisper itself. In the hush, the Lord spake unto him again: "What doest thou here, Elijah?"

Again Elijah answered, his voice small but earnest, "I have been very jealous for the Lord God of hosts: because the children of Israel have forsaken thy covenant, thrown down thine altars, and slain thy prophets with the sword; and I, even I only, am left; and they seek my life, to take it away."

The voice of God answered, calm and clear, "Go, return on thy way to the wilderness of Damascus. And when thou comest, anoint Hazael to be king over Syria. And Jehu the son of Nimshi shalt thou anoint to be king over Israel. And Elisha the son of Shaphat of Abel-meholah shalt thou anoint to be prophet in thy room. And it shall come to pass, that him that escapeth the sword of Hazael shall Jehu slay; and him that escapeth from the sword of Jehu shall Elisha slay."

The words bare both commission and reassurance. Elijah hearkened as God continued, "Yet I have left me seven thousand in Israel, all the knees which have not bowed unto Baal, and every mouth which hath not kissed him."

Elijah stood upon the quiet, windswept heights and hearkened. The fear and uncertainty had not vanished, but God's whisper cut through the loneliness. In the gentlest voice, Elijah learned he was not alone.

Elijah let the whisper settle deep within him, gentler than wind, quieter than fire. He looked out across the jagged peaks. The world felt no less wild, yet its emptiness was now mingled with somewhat new—remembrance that hope, like water, sometimes runneth just beneath the rock. He stood unmoving for a long moment, mantle yet drawn up to his face, the cool air licking his brow.

Slowly, his shoulders relaxed. His hands unclenched. There was work waiting for him, difficult and uncertain, yet it was not for him alone. The Lord had preserved thousands that held fast unto truth, even if Elijah could not behold them from this lonely ridge. The chasm betwixt his dread and his purpose grew narrower.

He stepped carefully forth from the cave, feeling the sharp bite of gravel beneath his sandals. The sky was soft with evening light now, and the memory of the fire glowed upon distant stones. Elijah let his hand brush along the rock as he walked, steadying himself, and found unlooked-for strength in the rough surface.

As he went down, the slopes of the mount fell away to scattered brush and the winding path below. The way ahead seemed long, yet with every step, Elijah bare a little less fear. He could almost see beyond his smallness—see a tapestry threaded with unknown hands and brave hearts scattered through the land, stedfast and silent, kindred in spirit.

When the night settled and the wind once again arose across Horeb, Elijah rested along the trail, hearkening to the hush. He whispered a prayer, not loud or grand, but true. The answer was only quiet—yet within his heart, it was enough. And so, wrapped in the cool breath of the wilderness, Elijah arose again to follow the path given unto him, the whisper of God echoing quietly in his soul.
