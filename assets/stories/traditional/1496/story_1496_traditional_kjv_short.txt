Hannah stood in the court of the house of the LORD with the boy beside her. The boy was small. He held the hem of her robe. They had come up from Ramah with the offering — the bullock, and an ephah of flour, and the wine — and Eli the priest had been told who the woman was and wherefore she was come. She was come to give the child unto the LORD, as she had vowed in the day when she stood weeping in this same court and could not eat.

Then she began to speak. She wept not. The hem was held in the small hand of the child, and her hand was lifted up, and the song came forth out of her that none had heard before.

"My heart rejoiceth in the LORD," she said. "Mine horn is exalted in the LORD."

She spake of the bows of the mighty broken, and of the feeble girded with strength. She spake of the full that had hired themselves out for bread, and of the hungry that had ceased. She spake of the barren that had borne seven, and of her that had many children that was waxed feeble.

"The LORD killeth, and maketh alive," she said. "He bringeth down to the grave, and bringeth up. The LORD maketh poor, and maketh rich. He raiseth up the poor out of the dust, and lifteth up the beggar from the dunghill, to set them among princes, and to make them inherit the throne of glory."

The boy stood at her side. He understood not the song. His hand was yet upon the hem of her robe.

She came to the end of it. "He shall give strength unto his king, and exalt the horn of his anointed."

She lowered her arm. She laid her hand upon the head of the boy and held it there. The day went on round about them in the court — the smoke of the offerings going up, the priests moving among the altars. Hannah and Elkanah turned and went down the long road home unto Ramah, and the song abode with her along the way.
