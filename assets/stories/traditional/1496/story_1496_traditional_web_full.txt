The walk up to Shiloh was long. Hannah and Elkanah had come up from Ramah with the offering — the bull for the sacrifice, the ephah of flour, the wine. The boy walked between them. He was very small. He had only just been weaned. He had not been to Shiloh before.

The road went up through the hill country. They stopped to rest in the middle of the day under an oak tree that had been on the road as long as anyone could remember. Hannah sat with the boy in her lap. He fell asleep against her shoulder. She did not move for a while. Elkanah watched her without saying anything.

She was thinking, as she sat there, of the last time she had come to this place. She had come up to Shiloh that year not able to eat. She had stood in the court of the house of the LORD and wept until she could not weep anymore. She had spoken in her heart. She had not spoken aloud. Eli had thought she was drunk. She had answered him that no, she had been pouring out her soul before the LORD. He had blessed her and let her go in peace.

The boy in her lap now had come of that day. She had named him Samuel — "I have asked him of the LORD." She had told Elkanah, when the boy was born, that she would bring him to the LORD when he was weaned. The day had come.

They went on in the afternoon. They came to Shiloh by evening. They stayed the night with friends. In the morning they brought the offering to the priest. Eli was told who the woman was and why she had come. She had come to give the child to the LORD as she had said she would give him.

Then she began to speak. She did not weep. The boy was holding the edge of her robe. Her hand was lifted. The song came up out of her that no one had heard before.

"My heart rejoices in the LORD," she said. "My horn is exalted in the LORD."

She spoke of the bows of the mighty broken. She spoke of the weak girded with strength. She spoke of the full who had hired themselves out for bread, and of the hungry who had ceased to hunger. She spoke of the barren bearing seven, and of the one who had many children grown feeble.

"The LORD kills and makes alive. He brings down to the grave and brings up. The LORD makes poor and makes rich. He raises the poor out of the dust. He lifts the needy out of the dunghill, to set them among princes, to make them inherit the throne of glory."

The boy stood at her side. He did not understand the song. His hand was still on the edge of her robe.

She came to the end of it. "He will give strength unto his king and exalt the horn of his anointed."

She lowered her arm. She put her hand on the boy's head and held it there. The day went on around them in the court. The smoke of the offerings was rising. The priests were moving among the altars. Eli stood watching her.

Then she and Elkanah turned. They went down the long road home to Ramah. The boy was left with Eli. The song stayed with Hannah along the way.

She would come back every year to bring him a little coat. She knew that. She would see him grow up in the courts of the house. She did not weep on the way home that day. She had wept her weeping already, the year before. This year she sang what she had been given to sing instead.
