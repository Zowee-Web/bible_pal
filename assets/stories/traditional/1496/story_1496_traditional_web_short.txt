Hannah was on her feet in the court of the house of the LORD with the boy beside her. The boy was small. He stood holding the edge of her robe. They had walked up from Ramah with the offering — the bull, and the ephah of flour, and the wine — and Eli the priest had been told who the woman was and why she had come. She had come to give the child to the LORD as she had said she would give him on the day she had stood weeping in this same court and could not eat.

Then she began to speak. She did not weep. The robe was held in the boy's small hand, and her own hand was lifted, and the song came up out of her that no one had heard before.

"My heart rejoices in the LORD," she said. "My horn is exalted in the LORD."

She spoke of the bows of the mighty broken, and of the weak girded with strength. She spoke of the full who had hired themselves out for bread, and of the hungry who had ceased to hunger. She spoke of the barren bearing seven, and of the one who had many children grown feeble.

"The LORD kills and makes alive," she said. "He brings down to the grave and brings up. The LORD makes poor and makes rich. He raises the poor out of the dust. He lifts the needy out of the dunghill, to set them among princes, and to make them inherit the throne of glory."

The boy stood at her side. He did not understand the song. His hand was still on the edge of her robe.

She came to the end of it. "He will give strength unto his king and exalt the horn of his anointed."

She lowered her arm. She put her hand on the boy's head and held it there. The day went on around them in the court — the smoke of the offerings rising, the priests moving among the altars. Hannah and Elkanah turned and went down the long road home to Ramah, and the song stayed with her along the way.
