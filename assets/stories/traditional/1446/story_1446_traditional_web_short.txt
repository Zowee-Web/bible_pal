The two men lay flat on the roof of Rahab's house, under stalks of flax she had spread over them. The flax was dry. It scratched the side of the face.

Below, in the street, the king's captain came up the slope toward her door.

Rahab heard the butt of his spear strike the wood. Once. Twice.

She went down with her hair covered. She opened the door only part of the way.

"There came men in to me," she said, "but I didn't know where they came from. About the time of the shutting of the gate, when it was dark, the men went out. Where the men went, I don't know. Pursue them quickly. You will overtake them."

The captain's eye went past her into the house. The lamp burned on the table where she had been sitting. The bread had been set out for three.

He did not look long.

"Go," she said again. "They are on the road already."

The captain turned. The spears turned with him. The gate of the city was shut behind them.

She climbed back up to the roof. She knelt down beside the flax.

"I know that Yahweh has given you the land," she said. "The terror of you has fallen on us. The inhabitants of the land melt before you. We have heard how Yahweh dried up the water of the Red Sea when you came out of Egypt. Our hearts melted, and no spirit remained in any man. For Yahweh your God is God in heaven above and on earth beneath.

Now therefore, swear to me by Yahweh — since I have dealt kindly with you, deal kindly with my father's house. Give me a true sign. Save alive my father and my mother, and my brothers, and my sisters, and all that they have. Deliver our lives from death."

The men spoke from under the flax. "Our life for yours," they said, "if you don't talk about this business of ours. When Yahweh gives us the land, we will deal kindly and truly with you."

She brought them to the window.

The window was set in the wall of the city, above the slope. Beyond the window was open air.

She tied a scarlet cord to the wood of the frame. The cord hung outside the wall.

She let the men down by a second cord. Their feet found the stones of the wall, then the slope, then the dirt of the road below.

"Go to the mountain," she said down to them. "Hide yourselves there three days, until the pursuers have returned. Then go your way."

They went up into the mountain.

The cord stayed at the window. The slope was empty. The dust their feet had stirred was already settling in the silence.
