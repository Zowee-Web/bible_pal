The road ascended. Dust lay upon all things — upon the stones beneath their feet, upon the borders of their garments, upon sandals worn through by many days of journeying. A company of pilgrims moved upward along the ridge, spread forth in small gatherings, their burdens heavy upon their shoulders. The sun stood high and the air was parched and the hills rose before them in long brown folds against a pale sky.

A woman near the midst of the company lifted her head. She had watched the ground before her feet the whole of the morning. Her water skin was nigh unto empty. Sweat darkened the cloth about her neck. She raised her eyes beyond the road, beyond the turning ahead, beyond the shoulders of them that walked before her, and she looked upon the hills.

She opened her mouth and began to sing.

"Unto the hills do I raise mine eyes. From whence shall my help arise?"

Her voice was rough with the dust. It carried across the stones. A man walking some paces before her turned his head, yet he did not stay his step.

"My help cometh from the Lord, who hath made the heavens and the earth."

Another voice joined with hers. Then two more. And the words passed backward through the company, as a thing given from one pair of hands unto the next.

"He shall not suffer thy foot to be moved. He that keepeth watch over thee shall not slumber."

The road turned about a rocky outcrop. Loose gravel shifted beneath their feet. A child stumbled, and a hand took hold of the child's arm and set the child upright, and the stride was not broken.

"He that guardeth Israel shall not slumber. He shall not close his eyes."

The voices grew. They were not strong voices. They were the voices of weary folk — folk whose lips were cracked and whose knees ached and whose hair was thick with dust. Yet the song kept climbing.

"The Lord is thy keeper. The Lord is thy shade upon thy right hand. The sun shall not smite thee by day, neither the moon by night."

The woman drew the back of her hand across her face. She kept walking. The hill kept rising.

"The Lord shall preserve thee from all evil. He shall keep thy going forth and thy coming in, from this hour unto the ages of ages."

The last word hung in the dry air. The company moved on. Sandals scraped upon stone. The hills stood before them, brown and vast, and the road wound upward between them, and the pilgrims climbed.
