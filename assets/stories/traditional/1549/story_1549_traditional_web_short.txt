The road climbed. Dust covered everything — the stones underfoot, the hems of robes, the sandals worn thin from days of walking. A line of pilgrims moved upward along the ridge, spaced out in small clusters, their packs heavy on their shoulders. The sun was high and the air was dry and the hills rose ahead of them in long brown folds against a white sky.

A woman near the middle of the column lifted her head. She had been watching her feet for most of the morning. Her water skin was nearly empty. Sweat darkened the cloth at her neck. She looked up past the road, past the next bend, past the shoulders of the travelers ahead of her, and she looked at the hills.

She opened her mouth and began to sing.

"I will lift up my eyes to the hills. Where does my help come from?"

Her voice was rough from the dust. It carried across the stones. A man walking a few paces ahead turned his head but did not stop.

"My help comes from the LORD, who made heaven and earth."

Another voice joined hers. Then two more. The words passed back through the column like something handed from one set of hands to the next.

"He will not let your foot slip. He who watches over you will not sleep."

The road bent around a rocky outcrop. Loose gravel shifted under their feet. A child stumbled and a hand caught the child's arm and pulled the child upright without breaking stride.

"The one who watches over Israel will not sleep. He will not close his eyes."

The voices grew. They were not strong voices. They were the voices of tired people, people with cracked lips and sore knees and dust in their hair. But the song kept climbing.

"The LORD is your keeper. The LORD is your shade at your right hand. The sun will not strike you by day, nor the moon by night."

The woman wiped her face with the back of her hand. She kept walking. The hill kept rising.

"The LORD will keep you from all harm. He will watch over your life. The LORD will watch over your going out and your coming in, from this time on, and forever."

The last word hung in the dry air. The column moved on. Sandals scraped against stone. The hills stood ahead of them, brown and vast, and the road wound upward between them, and the pilgrims climbed.
