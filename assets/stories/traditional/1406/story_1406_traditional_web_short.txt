The eastern tribes had been about to go home. Reuben and Gad and the half-tribe of Manasseh had taken their inheritance east of the Jordan, in the lands of Gilead and Bashan, and they had been dismissed by Joshua with his blessing; and on their way back, near the river, they had built a great altar — like the altar of Yahweh in front of the tent of meeting. The other tribes had heard, and gathered for war, and sent Phinehas the priest with ten chiefs of the tribes to go down and ask what this altar meant. The eastern tribes had explained: not for sacrifice, but as a witness between them and Israel that Yahweh was their God too. Phinehas heard.

When Phinehas the priest, and the princes of the congregation, even the heads of the thousands of Israel that were with him, heard the words that the children of Reuben and the children of Gad and the children of Manasseh spoke, it pleased them well.

Phinehas the son of Eleazar the priest said to the children of Reuben, to the children of Gad, and to the children of Manasseh, "Today we know that Yahweh is among us, because you have not committed this trespass against Yahweh. Now you have delivered the children of Israel out of Yahweh's hand."

Phinehas the son of Eleazar the priest, and the princes, returned from the children of Reuben, and from the children of Gad, out of the land of Gilead, to the land of Canaan, to the children of Israel, and brought them word again.

The thing pleased the children of Israel; and the children of Israel blessed God, and spoke no more of going up against them to war, to destroy the land in which the children of Reuben and the children of Gad lived.

The children of Reuben and the children of Gad named the altar "A Witness Between Us that Yahweh is God."

And the altar by the bank of the Jordan in the country of Gilead stood with its name carved into the great stones. The men of the east turned back to their towns east of the river; the men of the west put away their weapons in Shiloh; and the gathering for war that had almost been a war among brothers ended on the slope above the Jordan, witness to the peace that held among the tribes.
