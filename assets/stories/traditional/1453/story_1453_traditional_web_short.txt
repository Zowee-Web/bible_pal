In the tenth year of Zedekiah king of Judah, the army of Babylon was around the walls of Jerusalem.

Jeremiah was shut up in the court of the guard, in the house of the king. He was not free to walk in the streets. The siege went on outside. He sat in the court with the other prisoners and waited.

Then the word of Yahweh came to him there.

He spoke of it later, when his cousin had gone home —

"The word came to me, saying: Behold, Hanamel the son of Shallum your uncle shall come to you, saying, Buy my field that is in Anathoth; for the right of redemption is yours to buy it."

The next day Hanamel came into the court of the guard.

He stood before Jeremiah where Jeremiah was sitting. He had a deed in his hand. He had walked a long way to get to Jerusalem.

"Please buy my field that is in Anathoth," he said to Jeremiah, "which is in the land of Benjamin. The right of inheritance is yours. The redemption is yours. Buy it for yourself."

Anathoth was held by the army of Babylon. The field could not be walked in. The roads to it were closed. The vineyards there had been trampled.

Jeremiah bought the field.

He weighed out seventeen shekels of silver in the balances. He counted them into Hanamel's hand. He drew up the deed. He sealed one copy. He left the other copy open. He called the witnesses to sign.

The men who sat in the court of the guard signed as witnesses. Hanamel signed. Baruch the scribe stood by.

Jeremiah gave the deeds to Baruch the son of Neriah.

He said before all of them — Hanamel, the witnesses, the Jews who sat in the court — "Take these deeds, this deed of the purchase which is sealed, and this deed which is open. Put them in an earthen vessel, that they may continue many days. For Yahweh of Armies, the God of Israel, says: Houses and fields and vineyards shall yet again be bought in this land."

Baruch took the two scrolls.

He took an earthen vessel from beside the wall of the court. He folded the scrolls and put them down inside the vessel. He set the lid on it and pressed it firm.

The vessel stayed in the court of the guard, the deed of a field in Anathoth sealed inside it, while the army of Babylon kept the siege of Jerusalem.
