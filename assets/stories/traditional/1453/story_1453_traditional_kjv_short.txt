In the tenth year of Zedekiah king of Judah, the army of Babylon was round about the walls of Jerusalem.

Jeremiah was shut up in the court of the prison, which was in the house of the king. He was not free to walk in the streets. The siege went on without. He sat in the court with the other prisoners, and waited.

Then the word of the Lord came unto him there.

He spake of it afterward, when his cousin had gone home —

"The word came unto me, saying: Behold, Hanameel the son of Shallum thine uncle shall come unto thee, saying, Buy thee my field that is in Anathoth; for the right of redemption is thine to buy it."

The next day Hanameel came into the court of the prison.

He stood before Jeremiah where Jeremiah was sitting. He had an evidence in his hand. He had walked a long way to come unto Jerusalem.

"I pray thee, buy my field that is in Anathoth," he said unto Jeremiah, "which is in the country of Benjamin. The right of inheritance is thine. The redemption is thine. Buy it for thyself."

Anathoth was held by the army of Babylon. The field could not be walked in. The ways unto it were shut up. The vineyards there had been trodden down.

Jeremiah bought the field.

He weighed out seventeen shekels of silver in the balances. He counted them into the hand of Hanameel. He drew up the evidence. He sealed one copy. He left the other copy open. He called the witnesses to subscribe.

The men that sat in the court of the prison subscribed as witnesses. Hanameel subscribed. Baruch the scribe stood by.

Jeremiah gave the evidences unto Baruch the son of Neriah.

He spake before them all — Hanameel, the witnesses, the Jews that sat in the court — "Take these evidences, this evidence of the purchase which is sealed, and this evidence which is open. Put them in an earthen vessel, that they may continue many days. For thus saith the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel: Houses and fields and vineyards shall be possessed again in this land."

Baruch took the two scrolls.

He took an earthen vessel from beside the wall of the court. He folded the scrolls and laid them within the vessel. He set the lid thereon and pressed it firm.

The vessel abode in the court of the prison, the evidence of a field in Anathoth sealed therein, while the army of Babylon kept the siege of Jerusalem.
