The Philistines gathered their armies to battle at Socoh, which belongs to Judah. Saul and the men of Israel encamped in the valley of Elah. The Philistines stood on the mountain on one side. Israel stood on the mountain on the other side. There was a valley between them. The ground was dry and open. A brook ran through the bottom, its water moving over smooth stones.

For forty days the Philistine drew near morning and evening and presented himself. At sunrise the watchmen of Israel saw him first. They turned and called down into the camp, and the camp lifted its head. At dusk the call came again. Men set down their bread. Men set down their cups. They climbed the slope to look down, and then they came back to their tents without speaking.

A champion went out of the camp of the Philistines, named Goliath, of Gath. His height was six cubits and a span. He had a helmet of bronze on his head and wore a coat of mail weighing five thousand shekels. He had bronze shin armor on his legs and a javelin between his shoulders. The staff of his spear was like a weaver's beam. A shield bearer went before him.

He cried to the armies of Israel, "Choose a man for yourselves, and let him come down to me. If he kills me, we will be your servants. But if I prevail against him, you will serve us. I defy the armies of Israel this day. Give me a man, that we may fight together."

When Saul and all Israel heard those words, they were dismayed and greatly afraid. The voice carried across the valley and broke against the rock of the far slope and came back thinner. The men of Israel did not answer it. They looked at the ground at their feet, or at the king, or at no one at all.

David was the youngest son of Jesse the Bethlehemite, who had eight sons. The three oldest followed Saul to the battle. David went back and forth to feed his father's sheep at Bethlehem.

Jesse said to David, "Take this grain and these loaves to your brothers at the camp. Bring these cheeses to their captain. Bring back word how your brothers are doing."

David rose early. He left the sheep with a keeper. He came to the place of the wagons as the army was going out, shouting the war cry. The shout rose around him as he walked, and he left the supplies with the baggage keeper and ran to find his brothers. He greeted them. As he talked with them, Goliath came up out of the ranks of the Philistines and said the same words.

David heard them.

His face changed. He looked across the valley. He looked at the men around him. All the men of Israel, when they saw the giant, fled from him and were terrified.

David spoke to the men who stood by him. "Who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?"

Eliab his oldest brother heard him speak. Eliab's anger burned. "Why have you come down? With whom have you left those few sheep in the wilderness? I know your pride and the evil of your heart. You have come down that you might see the battle."

David said, "What have I now done? Is there not a cause?" He turned away from his brother to another man, and spoke the same words again, and the people answered him the same way. The word of what he had said traveled back through the camp, and they rehearsed his words before Saul, and Saul sent for him.

David walked through the camp to the king's tent. The soldiers parted for him as he passed. They had seen him only as a boy with grain and bread. Now they watched him go.

David said to Saul, "Let no man's heart fail because of him. Your servant will go and fight with this Philistine."

Saul looked at him. "You are not able. You are but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth."

David said, "Your servant kept his father's sheep. A lion came and took a lamb. I went after it, struck it, and delivered the lamb from its mouth. When it arose against me, I caught it by its beard and killed it. I killed both the lion and the bear. This uncircumcised Philistine shall be as one of them, seeing he has defied the armies of the living God. The Lord who delivered me from the lion and the bear will deliver me from this Philistine."

Saul said, "Go. The Lord will be with you."

Saul put his own armor on David. He set the helmet of bronze on the boy's head. He clad him in the coat of mail. He strapped the sword on the cloak. David tried to take a step. The weight pulled at his shoulder. He took another step, and the strap shifted across his chest. He stood still. He said, "I cannot go with these, for I have not tested them." He unstrapped the sword. He pulled off the coat. He set the helmet down on the ground beside the king's seat.

He took his staff in his hand and walked down the slope alone.

The brook ran clear at the bottom of the valley. The water moved over rounded rocks. David knelt at the edge. He looked into the water. He chose a stone. He chose another. He chose three more. Five smooth stones lay in his palm before he put them in the pouch of his shepherd's bag. His sling was in his hand.

He stood up and turned toward the Philistine.

On the far slope, the men of Israel were watching. They saw a boy with a staff stepping out into the open ground. On the near slope, the Philistines were watching too. Goliath came on with his shield bearer ahead of him. The shield was wide and bright. The spear stood up behind the shield like a beam of wood balanced against the sky.

When the Philistine looked around and saw David, he disdained him, for he was but a youth, and ruddy, and had a good looking face. He said, "Am I a dog, that you come to me with sticks?" His voice carried across the open ground. He cursed David by his gods. "Come to me, and I will give your flesh to the birds of the sky and to the animals of the field."

David's answer came back across the valley clear and steady.

"You come to me with a sword and a spear and a javelin, but I come to you in the name of the Lord of Hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied. This day the Lord will deliver you into my hand. I will strike you. All the earth shall know that there is a God in Israel. The battle is the Lord's, and he will give you into our hand."

The Philistine rose up and walked and came near to meet him. David did not wait. He hurried and ran toward the army to meet the Philistine. His feet moved across the dry ground. His hand was already in his bag.

He took a stone. He fit it to the leather. The sling rose. It whirled once. It whirled again. The stone left the leather.

The stone struck the Philistine in his forehead and sank in. The giant's head jerked back. His knees gave. The shield bearer ahead of him stepped aside as he came down. The bronze helmet rang against the stones of the valley floor. Goliath fell on his face to the earth.

So David prevailed over the Philistine with a sling and a stone. He struck the Philistine and killed him. There was no sword in his hand.

The valley was still for one breath. Then Israel began to shout. The shout rose along the slope of the mountain, and the men who had not answered the giant for forty days were answering now. They came down from the mountain and pursued the Philistines back across the open ground. The shield bearer was already running. The line of the Philistine camp was already breaking.

David stood over the fallen giant. The sling hung at his side. The four stones he had not used were still in his shepherd's bag. The brook ran on at the bottom of the valley behind him.

And on that ground, in the cry of Israel coming down off the mountain, the boy from Bethlehem stood unmoved, the staff in his hand, the name of the Lord still ringing in the open air over the valley of Elah.
