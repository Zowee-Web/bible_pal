The Philistines gathered their armies to battle at Shochoh, which belongeth to Judah. Saul and the men of Israel pitched by the valley of Elah. The Philistines stood on a mountain on the one side, and Israel stood on a mountain on the other side, and there was a valley between them. The ground was dry and open. A brook ran through the bottom, its water moving over smooth stones.

For forty days the Philistine drew near morning and evening, and presented himself. At sunrise the watchmen of Israel saw him first. They turned and called down into the camp, and the camp lifted up its head. At dusk the call came again. Men set down their bread. Men set down their cups. They climbed the slope to look down, and then they returned unto their tents without speaking.

There went out a champion of the Philistines, named Goliath, of Gath. His height was six cubits and a span. He had an helmet of brass upon his head, and was armed with a coat of mail weighing five thousand shekels. He had greaves of brass upon his legs, and a target of brass between his shoulders. The staff of his spear was like a weaver's beam. And one bearing a shield went before him.

And he cried unto the armies of Israel, "Choose you a man for you, and let him come down to me. If he be able to kill me, then will we be your servants. But if I prevail against him, then shall ye serve us. I defy the armies of Israel this day; give me a man, that we may fight together."

When Saul and all Israel heard those words, they were dismayed, and greatly afraid. The voice carried across the valley, and brake against the rock of the far slope, and came back thinner. The men of Israel answered him not. They looked at the ground at their feet, or at the king, or at no man at all.

Now David was the youngest son of Jesse the Bethlehemite, who had eight sons. And the three eldest followed Saul to the battle. But David went and returned from Saul to feed his father's sheep at Bethlehem.

Jesse said unto David, "Take now for thy brethren this parched corn and these loaves, and carry them unto the camp. Carry these cheeses unto their captain. Bring me word again how thy brethren fare."

David rose up early. He left the sheep with a keeper. He came to the trench as the host went forth, shouting for the battle. The shout rose round about him as he walked, and he left his carriage with the keeper, and ran unto his brethren. He saluted them. As he talked with them, Goliath came up out of the ranks of the Philistines, and spake according to the same words.

David heard them.

His countenance was changed. He looked across the valley. He looked upon the men round about him. And all the men of Israel, when they saw the man, fled from him, and were sore afraid.

David spake to the men that stood by him, saying, "Who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?"

Eliab his eldest brother heard him speak. Eliab's anger was kindled. "Why camest thou down hither? And with whom hast thou left those few sheep in the wilderness? I know thy pride, and the naughtiness of thine heart. For thou art come down that thou mightest see the battle."

David said, "What have I now done? Is there not a cause?" He turned from his brother toward another, and spake again after the same manner; and the people answered him after the former manner. The words which he spake were heard back through the camp, and they rehearsed them before Saul; and Saul sent for him.

David walked through the camp unto the king's tent. The soldiers parted before him as he passed. They had seen him only as a stripling with corn and bread. Now they watched him go.

David said unto Saul, "Let no man's heart fail because of him; thy servant will go and fight with this Philistine."

Saul looked upon him. "Thou art not able. Thou art but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth."

David said, "Thy servant kept his father's sheep, and there came a lion, and a bear, and took a lamb out of the flock. And I went out after him, and smote him, and delivered it out of his mouth. And when he arose against me, I caught him by his beard, and smote him, and slew him. Thy servant slew both the lion and the bear. And this uncircumcised Philistine shall be as one of them, seeing he hath defied the armies of the living God. The LORD that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine."

Saul said, "Go, and the LORD be with thee."

Saul armed David with his armour, and put an helmet of brass upon his head. He clad him with a coat of mail. He girded the sword upon his armour. David assayed to go. The weight pulled at his shoulder. He went another step, and the strap shifted across his breast. He stood still. He said, "I cannot go with these; for I have not proved them." He loosed the sword. He put off the coat. He set the helmet down upon the ground beside the king's seat.

He took his staff in his hand, and went down the slope alone.

The brook ran clear at the bottom of the valley. The water moved over rounded rocks. David knelt at the edge. He looked into the water. He chose him a stone. He chose another. He chose three more. Five smooth stones lay in his palm before he put them in the pouch of his shepherd's bag. His sling was in his hand.

He stood up, and turned toward the Philistine.

On the far slope, the men of Israel were watching. They saw a youth with a staff stepping out into the open ground. On the near slope, the Philistines were watching too. Goliath came on with his shield bearer before him. The shield was wide and bright. The spear stood up behind the shield like a beam of wood balanced against the sky.

When the Philistine looked about and saw David, he disdained him; for he was but a youth, ruddy, and of a fair countenance. He said, "Am I a dog, that thou comest to me with staves?" His voice carried across the open ground. The Philistine cursed David by his gods. "Come to me, and I will give thy flesh unto the fowls of the air, and to the beasts of the field."

David's answer came back across the valley clear and steady.

"Thou comest to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield: but I come to thee in the name of the LORD of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom thou hast defied. This day will the LORD deliver thee into mine hand; and I will smite thee. And all the earth shall know that there is a God in Israel. For the battle is the LORD's, and he will give you into our hands."

When the Philistine arose and drew nigh to meet David, David hasted toward the army to meet him. His feet moved across the dry ground. His hand was already in his bag.

He took thence a stone. He fitted it to the leather. The sling rose. It whirled once. It whirled again. The stone left the leather.

The stone sunk into his forehead; and he fell upon his face to the earth. The giant's head was driven back. His knees gave. The shield bearer before him stepped aside as he came down. The helmet of brass rang against the stones of the valley floor. Goliath fell upon his face to the earth.

So David prevailed over the Philistine with a sling and with a stone, and smote the Philistine, and slew him. But there was no sword in the hand of David.

The valley was still for one breath. Then Israel began to shout. The shout rose along the slope of the mountain, and the men that had not answered the giant for forty days were answering now. They came down from the mountain and pursued the Philistines back across the open ground. The shield bearer was already running. The line of the Philistine camp was already broken.

David stood over the fallen giant. The sling hung at his side. The four stones he had not used were yet in his shepherd's bag. The brook ran on at the bottom of the valley behind him.

Upon that ground, in the cry of Israel coming down off the mountain, the stripling of Bethlehem stood unmoved, the staff in his hand, the name of the LORD yet ringing in the open air over the valley of Elah.
