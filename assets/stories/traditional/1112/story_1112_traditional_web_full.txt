The Philistines gathered their armies to battle at Socoh, which belongs to Judah. Saul and the men of Israel encamped in the valley of Elah. The Philistines stood on the mountain on one side. Israel stood on the mountain on the other side. There was a valley between them. The ground was dry and open. A brook ran through the bottom, its water moving over smooth stones.

A champion went out of the camp of the Philistines, named Goliath, of Gath. His height was six cubits and a span. He had a helmet of bronze on his head and wore a coat of mail weighing five thousand shekels. He had bronze shin armor on his legs and a javelin between his shoulders. The staff of his spear was like a weaver's beam. A shield bearer went before him.

He cried to the armies of Israel, "Choose a man for yourselves, and let him come down to me. If he kills me, we will be your servants. But if I prevail against him, you will serve us. I defy the armies of Israel this day. Give me a man, that we may fight together."

When Saul and all Israel heard those words, they were dismayed and greatly afraid.

For forty days the Philistine drew near morning and evening and presented himself.

David was the youngest son of Jesse the Bethlehemite, who had eight sons. The three oldest followed Saul to the battle. David went back and forth to feed his father's sheep at Bethlehem.

Jesse said to David, "Take this grain and these loaves to your brothers at the camp." David rose early, left the sheep with a keeper, and went. He came to the camp as the army was going out, shouting the war cry. He left the supplies with the baggage keeper and ran to his brothers. As he talked with them, Goliath came out and spoke according to the same words. David heard them.

All the men of Israel fled from him.

David said to Saul, "Let no man's heart fail. Your servant will fight this Philistine."

Saul said, "You are not able. You are but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth."

David said, "Your servant kept his father's sheep. A lion came and took a lamb. I went after it, struck it, and delivered the lamb from its mouth. When it arose against me, I caught it by its beard and killed it. I killed both the lion and the bear. This uncircumcised Philistine shall be as one of them, seeing he has defied the armies of the living God. The Lord who delivered me from the lion and the bear will deliver me from this Philistine."

Saul put his armor on David — helmet, coat of mail. David strapped on the sword, tried to walk, and said, "I cannot go with these, for I have not tested them." He took them off.

He took his staff and went down to the brook. The water ran clear over the rocks at the bottom of the valley. He chose five smooth stones and put them in his shepherd's bag. His sling was in his hand. He drew near to the Philistine.

The Philistine came on with his shield bearer. When he saw David, he disdained him, for he was but a youth, ruddy and of a fair face. He said, "Am I a dog, that you come to me with sticks?" And he cursed David by his gods.

David said, "You come to me with a sword, a spear, and a javelin, but I come to you in the name of the Lord of Hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied. This day the Lord will deliver you into my hand."

David put his hand in his bag, took a stone, and slung it. The stone struck the Philistine in his forehead and sank in. He fell on his face to the earth.

So David prevailed over the Philistine with a sling and a stone. He struck the Philistine and killed him. There was no sword in his hand.

The giant lay face down in the dirt of the valley.
