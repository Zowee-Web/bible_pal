The city of Jerusalem had been broken open. The temple had been burned, and the walls were thrown down, and the people had been carried away in chains. And one who walked through the ruins of the city — the one whom tradition has named Jeremiah, though the lament itself does not name him — sat among the broken stones and lifted up a voice. He spoke in the first person, the man speaking for the city, and for himself, and for the people; and the words of the man were these.

"I am the man who has seen affliction
by the rod of his wrath.

He has led me and caused me to walk in darkness,
and not in light.

Surely he turns his hand against me
again and again all day long.

He has made my flesh and my skin old.
He has broken my bones.

He has built against me,
and surrounded me with bitterness and hardship.

He has made me dwell in dark places,
as those who have been long dead.

He has walled me about, so that I can't go out.
He has made my chain heavy.

Yes, when I cry, and call for help,
he shuts out my prayer.

He has walled up my ways with cut stone.
He has made my paths crooked."

And the words went down into the writing. The poet who had spoken them set them in the gathering of laments, in the third of five that would be kept among the songs of Israel; and the affliction did not lift in the speaking of it, but it had been spoken, and the words were no longer locked in his bones. And so it was in the days after the breaking of the city.
