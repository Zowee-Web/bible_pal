The city of Jerusalem had been broken open. The temple had been burned, and the walls were thrown down, and the people had been carried away in chains. And one who walked through the ruins of the city — the one whom tradition hath named Jeremiah, though the lamentation itself nameth him not — sat among the broken stones and lifted up his voice. He spake in the first person, the man speaking for the city, and for himself, and for the people; and the words of the man were these.

"I am the man that hath seen affliction
by the rod of his wrath.

He hath led me, and brought me into darkness,
but not into light.

Surely against me is he turned;
he turneth his hand against me all the day.

My flesh and my skin hath he made old.
He hath broken my bones.

He hath builded against me,
and compassed me with gall and travail.

He hath set me in dark places,
as they that be dead of old.

He hath hedged me about, that I cannot get out.
He hath made my chain heavy.

Also when I cry and shout,
he shutteth out my prayer.

He hath inclosed my ways with hewn stone.
He hath made my paths crooked."

And the words went down into the writing. The poet who had spoken them set them in the gathering of lamentations, in the third of five that would be kept among the songs of Israel; and the affliction lifted not in the speaking of it, but it had been spoken, and the words were no longer locked in his bones. And so it was in the days after the breaking of the city.
