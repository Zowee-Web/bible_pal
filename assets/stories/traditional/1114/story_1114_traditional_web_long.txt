It pleased Darius to set over the kingdom a hundred and twenty satraps, who should be throughout the whole kingdom, and over them three presidents, of whom Daniel was one. The satraps gave account to them, so that the king should suffer no loss. The work moved through Daniel's hands without slipping. The ledgers came in at evening and went out at morning. The king watched him do it.

Then this Daniel was distinguished above the presidents and the satraps, because an excellent spirit was in him, and the king thought to set him over the whole realm.

Word of the king's intention reached the other presidents. It reached the satraps. It traveled along the corridors of the palace at a quiet pace, from one robe to another, and where it landed it took root. They began to watch Daniel as he passed. They marked the hour he came to the council. They marked the hour he went home.

Then the presidents and the satraps sought to find occasion against Daniel concerning the kingdom. They sent men into the records. They opened the ledgers. They followed the accounts back through the years. They looked for a coin out of place, a name out of place, a tribute unaccounted for. They could find no occasion or fault, because he was faithful. There was no error or fault found in him.

They gathered in a low room with the lamps trimmed down. The conspiracy turned in upon itself. One of them spoke at last what the others had been holding.

"We will not find any occasion against this Daniel, unless we find it against him concerning the law of his God."

The room received the words. No one objected. The lamps were carried out, and the men dispersed into the corridors, and the plan went forward.

They came together before the king the next day in numbers. The presidents, the governors, the satraps, the counselors, the captains, robed and ranked, filled the throne room with the weight of consensus. They bowed low. They lifted their faces.

"King Darius, live forever. All the presidents of the kingdom, the governors, and the satraps, the counselors, and the captains, have consulted together to establish a royal statute, and to make a firm decree, that whoever shall ask a petition of any god or man for thirty days, except of you, O king, shall be cast into the den of lions. Now, O king, establish the decree and sign the writing, that it not be changed, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot be altered."

The hall was still. Darius looked over the gathered ranks. He saw his whole administration in one mind. The flattery sat warm in the room. He did not see, in the back rows, the one face that was not there. He called for the writing. He took the reed. He signed.

When Daniel knew that the writing was signed, he went into his house. The upper room was as he had kept it. The windows were open in his room toward Jerusalem. He kneeled on his knees three times that day, and prayed, and gave thanks before his God, as he did before. The shadow of the lattice fell across his hands. His voice did not rise. His voice did not change.

Then these men assembled and found Daniel at it. They had posted themselves where they could see the upper window. They had counted the hours. They came up the stairs in a body and stood in the open doorway and watched him kneel. He did not stop. He did not turn. They had what they had come for.

They went down. They went to the king.

They came near and spoke before the king concerning the king's decree. "Have you not signed a decree, that every man who shall ask a petition of any god or man within thirty days, except of you, O king, shall be cast into the den of lions?"

The king answered, "The thing is true, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot be altered."

Then they answered and said before the king, "That Daniel, who is of the children of the captivity of Judah, does not regard you, O king, nor the decree that you have signed, but makes his petition three times a day."

The trap closed. The king heard the names of his own words coming back to him fitted to a man he had not meant them for. He was very displeased. He set his heart on Daniel to deliver him. He called for the writing again. He called for his counselors. He searched the wording for a hinge. He labored till the going down of the sun.

But the men returned to him. "Know, O king, that it is a law of the Medes and Persians, that no decree nor statute which the king establishes may be changed."

The sun went down. The king commanded.

They brought Daniel out into the open air. The king came out also, into the torchlight at the mouth of the den. He looked at Daniel and could not turn away. "Your God whom you serve continually, he will deliver you."

A stone was brought and laid on the mouth of the den. The king sealed it with his own signet ring, and with the signet of his lords, that nothing might be changed concerning Daniel. The stone sat heavy over the opening. The seal pressed into the wax and held.

Then the king went to his palace. He passed the night fasting. No food was brought before him, and no instruments of music. His sleep fled from him. The corridors were dark. The servants kept their distance. The king walked from one window to another and back. Below him, in the city, the lamps went out one by one. The hours of the night were long.

Then the king arose very early in the morning, and went in haste to the den of lions. He took the steps too quickly. His robe caught at his ankles. Servants ran behind him with torches. The seal on the stone was still unbroken. The king came near the opening. He could not wait for the men to break it.

He cried with a troubled voice. "O Daniel, servant of the living God, is your God, whom you serve continually, able to deliver you from the lions?"

The silence held. The torchlight wavered against the stone.

Then a voice came back from inside, steady, undiminished. "O king, live forever. My God has sent his angel and has shut the lions' mouths, and they have not hurt me, because before him innocence was found in me, and also before you, O king, I have done no harm."

The king was exceedingly glad. He commanded that they should take Daniel up out of the den. The seal was broken. The stone was rolled. Hands reached down. Daniel was taken up out of the den, and no manner of harm was found on him, because he had trusted in his God.

Then the king commanded, and they brought those men who had accused Daniel. They brought them with their wives and their children. The conspiracy was gathered at the mouth of the den where it had hoped to leave Daniel. They were cast in. The lions took them before they reached the bottom.

Then King Darius wrote to all the peoples, nations, and languages who dwelt in all the earth. "Peace be multiplied to you. I make a decree that in all the dominion of my kingdom men tremble and fear before the God of Daniel. For he is the living God, and steadfast forever. His kingdom is that which will not be destroyed. His dominion will be even to the end. He delivers and rescues. He works signs and wonders in heaven and in earth, who has delivered Daniel from the power of the lions."

So this Daniel prospered in the reign of Darius and in the reign of Cyrus the Persian. The windows of his upper room stayed open toward Jerusalem. He went on kneeling.
