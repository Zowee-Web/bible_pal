It pleased Darius to set over the kingdom an hundred and twenty princes, which should be throughout the whole kingdom, and over these three presidents, of whom Daniel was first. The princes gave accounts unto them, that the king should have no damage. The work moved through Daniel's hands without slipping. The ledgers came in at evening and went out at morning. The king watched him do it.

Then this Daniel was preferred above the presidents and the princes, because an excellent spirit was in him, and the king thought to set him over the whole realm.

Word of the king's intention reached the other presidents. It reached the princes. It travelled along the corridors of the palace at a quiet pace, from one robe unto another, and where it landed it took root. They began to watch Daniel as he passed. They marked the hour he came unto the council. They marked the hour he went home.

Then the presidents and the princes sought to find occasion against Daniel concerning the kingdom. They sent men into the records. They opened the ledgers. They followed the accounts back through the years. They looked for a coin out of place, a name out of place, a tribute unaccounted for. They could find none occasion nor fault, forasmuch as he was faithful. Neither was there any error or fault found in him.

They gathered in a low chamber with the lamps trimmed down. The conspiracy turned in upon itself. One of them spake at last what the others had been holding.

"We shall not find any occasion against this Daniel, except we find it against him concerning the law of his God."

The chamber received the words. No man objected. The lamps were carried out, and the men dispersed into the corridors, and the plan went forward.

They came together before the king the next day in numbers. The presidents, the governors, the princes, the counsellors, the captains, robed and ranked, filled the throne room with the weight of consent. They bowed low. They lifted their faces.

"King Darius, live for ever. All the presidents of the kingdom, the governors, and the princes, the counsellors, and the captains, have consulted together to establish a royal statute, and to make a firm decree, that whosoever shall ask a petition of any God or man for thirty days, save of thee, O king, he shall be cast into the den of lions. Now, O king, establish the decree, and sign the writing, that it be not changed, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which altereth not."

The hall was still. Darius looked over the gathered ranks. He saw his whole administration in one mind. The flattery sat warm in the chamber. He saw not, in the back rows, the one face that was not there. He called for the writing. He took the reed. He signed.

Now when Daniel knew that the writing was signed, he went into his house. The upper chamber was as he had kept it. His windows being open in his chamber toward Jerusalem, he kneeled upon his knees three times a day, and prayed, and gave thanks before his God, as he did aforetime. The shadow of the lattice fell across his hands. His voice did not rise. His voice did not change.

Then these men assembled, and found Daniel at it. They had posted themselves where they could see the upper window. They had counted the hours. They came up the stairs in a body and stood in the open doorway and watched him kneel. He stopped not. He turned not. They had what they had come for.

They went down. They went unto the king.

They came near, and spake before the king concerning the king's decree. "Hast thou not signed a decree, that every man that shall ask a petition of any God or man within thirty days, save of thee, O king, shall be cast into the den of lions?"

The king answered and said, "The thing is true, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which altereth not."

Then answered they and said before the king, "That Daniel, which is of the children of the captivity of Judah, regardeth not thee, O king, nor the decree that thou hast signed, but maketh his petition three times a day."

The trap closed. The king heard the names of his own words coming back unto him fitted to a man he had not meant them for. He was sore displeased with himself. He set his heart on Daniel to deliver him. He called for the writing again. He called for his counsellors. He searched the wording for a hinge. He laboured till the going down of the sun.

But the men returned unto him. "Know, O king, that the law of the Medes and Persians is, That no decree nor statute which the king establisheth may be changed."

The sun went down. The king commanded.

They brought Daniel forth into the open air. The king came forth also, into the torchlight at the mouth of the den. He looked upon Daniel and could not turn away. "Thy God whom thou servest continually, he will deliver thee."

And a stone was brought, and laid upon the mouth of the den, and the king sealed it with his own signet, and with the signet of his lords, that the purpose might not be changed concerning Daniel. The stone sat heavy over the opening. The seal pressed into the wax and held.

Then the king went to his palace, and passed the night fasting. Neither were instruments of musick brought before him, and his sleep went from him. The corridors were dark. The servants kept their distance. The king walked from one window to another and back. Below him, in the city, the lamps went out one by one. The hours of the night were long.

Then the king arose very early in the morning, and went in haste unto the den of lions. He took the steps too quickly. His robe caught at his ankles. Servants ran behind him with torches. The seal upon the stone was yet unbroken. The king came near the opening. He could not tarry for the men to break it.

He cried with a lamentable voice. "O Daniel, servant of the living God, is thy God, whom thou servest continually, able to deliver thee from the lions?"

The silence held. The torchlight wavered against the stone.

Then a voice came back from within, steady, undiminished. "O king, live for ever. My God hath sent his angel, and hath shut the lions' mouths, that they have not hurt me, forasmuch as before him innocency was found in me, and also before thee, O king, have I done no hurt."

Then was the king exceeding glad, and commanded that they should take Daniel up out of the den. The seal was broken. The stone was rolled. Hands reached down. So Daniel was taken up out of the den, and no manner of hurt was found upon him, because he believed in his God.

And the king commanded, and they brought those men which had accused Daniel, and they cast them into the den of lions, them, their children, and their wives. The conspiracy was gathered at the mouth of the den where it had hoped to leave Daniel. They were cast in. The lions took them or ever they came at the bottom of the den.

Then king Darius wrote unto all people, nations, and languages, that dwell in all the earth. "Peace be multiplied unto you. I make a decree, That in every dominion of my kingdom men tremble and fear before the God of Daniel: for he is the living God, and stedfast for ever, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed, and his dominion shall be even unto the end. He delivereth and rescueth, and he worketh signs and wonders in heaven and in earth, who hath delivered Daniel from the power of the lions."

So this Daniel prospered in the reign of Darius, and in the reign of Cyrus the Persian. The windows of his upper chamber stayed open toward Jerusalem. He went on kneeling.
