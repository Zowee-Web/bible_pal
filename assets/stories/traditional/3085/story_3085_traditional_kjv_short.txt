When Hiram king of Tyre heard that Solomon had been anointed king in the room of his father, he sent his servants unto him. Hiram had ever loved David, and Solomon returned a message unto the king.

Solomon declared that David his father could not build an house unto the name of the Lord his God. Wars had compassed David about, until the Lord put his enemies under the soles of his feet. But now the Lord had given Solomon rest on every side, so that there was neither adversary nor evil occurrent to hinder the labour.

Therefore Solomon purposed to build the house of which the Lord had spoken unto David. David's son should sit upon his throne, and that son should build an house unto the Lord's name. Solomon asked Hiram to command that cedar trees be hewn for him out of Lebanon. His own servants would labour with Hiram's servants, and he would give the hire that Hiram appointed. For Solomon knew that none among Israel could skill to hew timber like unto the Sidonians.

When Hiram heard Solomon's words, he rejoiced greatly. He blessed the Lord, who had given unto David a wise son over so great a people. Then Hiram sent his answer. He would perform all Solomon's desire concerning timber of cedar and timber of fir. His servants would bring the logs down from Lebanon unto the sea, convey them by floats, and loose them at the place Solomon appointed. Solomon should receive them there and provide food for Hiram's household.

Thus Hiram gave Solomon cedar trees and fir trees according to all his desire. Solomon gave Hiram twenty thousand measures of wheat for food and twenty measures of pure oil, and he gave this provision year by year. The Lord gave Solomon wisdom, as he had promised him. Peace continued between Hiram and Solomon, and the two kings made a league together.
