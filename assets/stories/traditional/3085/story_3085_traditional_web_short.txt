When Hiram king of Tyre heard that Solomon had been anointed king in place of his father, he sent servants to him. Hiram had always loved David, and Solomon answered his greeting with a message.

Solomon told Hiram that David had not been able to build a house for the name of Yahweh his God. Enemies had surrounded David until Yahweh put them under his feet. Now Yahweh had given Solomon rest on every side. There was no enemy and no evil occurrence to hinder the work.

Solomon said that he intended to build the house Yahweh had spoken of to David. A son of David would sit on the throne, and that son would build the house for Yahweh's name. Solomon therefore asked Hiram to command that cedar trees be cut for him from Lebanon. Solomon's servants would work beside Hiram's servants, and Solomon would pay whatever wages Hiram appointed. The Sidonians, Solomon acknowledged, knew how to cut timber better than anyone in Israel.

Hiram rejoiced greatly when he heard Solomon's words. He blessed Yahweh for giving David a wise son to rule so great a people. Then he sent his answer. He would supply all the cedar and cypress timber Solomon desired. Hiram's servants would bring the logs down from Lebanon to the sea, bind them into rafts, and float them to the place Solomon named. There the rafts would be broken apart for Solomon to receive. In return, Solomon would provide food for Hiram's household.

Hiram supplied cedar and cypress according to Solomon's desire. Solomon supplied Hiram with twenty thousand cors of wheat for food and twenty cors of pure oil, giving this amount year by year. Yahweh gave Solomon wisdom as he had promised. Peace remained between Hiram and Solomon, and the two kings made a covenant together.
