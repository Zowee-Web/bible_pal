Something happened the day David came back from fighting the giant. Prince Jonathan — King Saul's son — met David, and right away something special connected them. The Bible says Jonathan's soul was knit to David's soul. That means they became the closest friends you can imagine, as fast as a heartbeat.

Jonathan was a prince. He lived in the palace. He wore fine clothes and carried a real sword and a real bow. He was the king's son — the most important young man in all of Israel.

David was a shepherd. He came from a small town called Bethlehem. His hands were rough from working with sheep. His clothes smelled like wool and grass and open sky.

They couldn't have been more different. But Jonathan loved David as much as he loved himself.

And then Jonathan did the most surprising thing.

He took off his robe — the special robe that told everyone "I am the prince" — and he gave it to David. Just handed it over. The fabric was dyed and woven, and it was heavier than anything David had ever worn.

Then he took off his armor and gave that to David too. The leather and bronze that protected him in battle.

Then his sword. The real, sharp, heavy sword that hung at his side. He placed it in David's hands.

Then his bow. The weapon he had practiced with since he was a little boy.

Then his belt. The wide leather strap that held everything together — sword, quiver, all the tools of a warrior.

Jonathan stood there. No robe. No armor. No sword. No bow. No belt.

David held Jonathan's robe and sword and bow and belt. The robe was too long for him. The sword belt would need tightening.

Jonathan stood with empty hands and bare shoulders. David's arms were full. Neither of them moved, and the court was quiet.
