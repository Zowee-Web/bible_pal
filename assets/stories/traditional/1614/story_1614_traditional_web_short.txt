The city was spared, and it displeased Jonah exceedingly. He was angry.

He prayed to Yahweh. "Please, Yahweh — was this not what I said when I was still in my own country? That is why I hurried to flee to Tarshish. I knew that you are a gracious God and merciful, slow to anger, and abundant in loving kindness, and that you relent of doing harm."

Then he made his request. "Take my life from me. It is better for me to die than to live."

Yahweh said, "Is it right for you to be angry?"

Jonah went out of the city and sat down on its east side. He made himself a booth there and sat under it in the shade, to see what would become of the city.

Yahweh God prepared a vine. It came up over Jonah and made a shade over his head, to deliver him from his discomfort.

Jonah was exceedingly glad because of the vine.

At dawn the next day God prepared a worm. It chewed on the vine, and the vine withered.

When the sun arose, God prepared a sultry east wind. The sun beat on Jonah's head until he was faint, and he asked again for his own death.

"It is better for me to die than to live."

God said to Jonah, "Is it right for you to be angry about the vine?"

"I am right to be angry," Jonah said. "Even to death."

Then Yahweh said, "You have been concerned for the vine, for which you have not labored, and which you did not make grow. It came up in a night and perished in a night.

"Should I not be concerned for Nineveh, that great city, in which are more than one hundred twenty thousand persons who cannot discern between their right hand and their left hand — and also many animals?"
