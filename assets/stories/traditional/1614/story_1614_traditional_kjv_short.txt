The city was spared. It displeased Jonah exceedingly, and he was very angry. He prayed unto the Lord.

"O Lord, I pray thee — was not this my saying, while I was yet in mine own country? Therefore I fled before unto Tarshish. For I knew what thou art: a gracious God, and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness. And I knew that thou repentest thee of the evil.

"Therefore now, O Lord, take my life from me, I beseech thee. For it is better for me to die than to live."

The Lord answered him. "Doest thou well to be angry?"

So Jonah went out of the city. He sat down on the east side of it, and there he made him a booth. Under its shadow he sat, and waited to see what would become of the city.

Then the Lord God prepared a gourd. It came up over Jonah and made a shadow over his head. That shadow delivered him from his grief.

Jonah was exceeding glad of that gourd.

But when the morning rose the next day, God prepared a worm. The worm smote the gourd, and the gourd withered.

And when the sun did arise, God prepared a vehement east wind. The sun beat upon the head of Jonah till he fainted. Again he wished in himself to die.

"It is better for me to die than to live."

And God said unto Jonah, "Doest thou well to be angry for the gourd?"

"I do well to be angry," said Jonah. "Even unto death."

Then said the Lord unto Jonah, "Thou hast had pity on the gourd. Thou hast not laboured for it, neither madest it grow. It came up in a night, and it perished in a night.

"And in Nineveh, that great city, are more than sixscore thousand persons. They cannot discern between their right hand and their left hand. And there is much cattle also.

"Should not I spare Nineveh?"
