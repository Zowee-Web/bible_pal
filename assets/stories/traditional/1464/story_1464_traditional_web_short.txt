The servant of Elisha rose early.

He went out from the house where Elisha was staying in Dothan. The morning had only just come over the hills.

He looked toward the city wall, and beyond the wall, and he saw what had not been there the night before.

The army of Aram was around the city.

The horses stood in their lines. The chariots stood behind them. The captains in their armor were on the field already. The light of the morning was on the iron of their spears.

The servant turned and ran back into the house.

He came to Elisha where Elisha was sitting. He could not steady his voice when he spoke.

"Alas, my master," he said. "What shall we do?"

Elisha looked at him.

"Don't be afraid," he said. "For those who are with us are more than those who are with them."

The servant did not understand. There were many of them outside the city wall. There were two men inside the house.

Then Elisha prayed.

"Yahweh," he said, "please open his eyes, that he may see."

Yahweh opened the young man's eyes.

The servant turned and looked again toward the wall.

The mountain that stood above Dothan was on fire.

There were horses of fire on the slopes of it. There were chariots of fire on the ridge. The hillside above the city was an army of fire, and the army of fire was standing around Elisha. The fire moved as fire does, but it did not burn the grass of the hillside, and it did not heat the morning air.

The servant did not speak.

He saw what had been around them all along. The fire stood on the hillside above Dothan.
