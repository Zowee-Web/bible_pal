David had fled from Jerusalem in the night, with his servants and his fighting men. He had crossed the brook Kidron with the people weeping around him. He had climbed the mount of Olives barefoot, with his head covered, and the people with him barefoot and weeping. He had heard the word that Ahithophel was among the conspirators, and his heart had grown heavy. The road had taken him over the Jordan and into the country beyond — into the wilderness of Gilead, into the camp at Mahanaim. And the men with him were hungry, and they were tired, and they had no provisions.

When David had come to Mahanaim, Shobi the son of Nahash of Rabbah of the children of Ammon, and Machir the son of Ammiel of Lodebar, and Barzillai the Gileadite of Rogelim,

brought beds, basins, earthen vessels, wheat, barley, meal, parched grain, beans, lentils, parched grain,

honey, butter, sheep, and cheese of the herd, for David, and for the people who were with him, to eat; for they said, "The people are hungry, weary, and thirsty in the wilderness."

And the king ate, and the people ate. The fires were lit in the camp at Mahanaim, and the bread was set out, and the meat was set out, and the men slept that night under cover. And so it was in the days when David was driven out, while the friends of the king brought food to him in the wilderness beyond the Jordan.
