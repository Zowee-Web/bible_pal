David had fled from Jerusalem in the night, with his servants and his men of war. He had passed over the brook Kidron with the people weeping round about him. He had gone up the mount of Olives barefoot, with his head covered, and the people with him barefoot and weeping. He had heard the word that Ahithophel was among the conspirators, and his heart had grown heavy. The way had taken him over Jordan and into the country beyond — into the wilderness of Gilead, into the camp at Mahanaim. And the men with him were hungry, and they were weary, and they had no provision.

And it came to pass, when David was come to Mahanaim, that Shobi the son of Nahash of Rabbah of the children of Ammon, and Machir the son of Ammiel of Lodebar, and Barzillai the Gileadite of Rogelim,

brought beds, and basons, and earthen vessels, and wheat, and barley, and flour, and parched corn, and beans, and lentiles, and parched pulse,

and honey, and butter, and sheep, and cheese of kine, for David, and for the people that were with him, to eat: for they said, "The people is hungry, and weary, and thirsty, in the wilderness."

And the king did eat, and the people did eat. The fires were kindled in the camp at Mahanaim, and the bread was set forth, and the meat was set forth, and the men slept that night under cover. And so it was in the days when David was driven out, while the friends of the king brought food unto him in the wilderness beyond Jordan.
