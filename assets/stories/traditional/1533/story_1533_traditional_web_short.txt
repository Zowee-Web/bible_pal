Hathach came back through the palace corridors and stood before Queen Esther. He repeated every word Mordecai had spoken at the king's gate — the exact sum of silver, the decree, the copy of the written order for the destruction of all the Jews in every province. Mordecai had charged him to lay it all before the queen and urge her to go to the king, to plead for her people.

Esther listened. Then she sent Hathach back to Mordecai with her answer.

"Any man or woman who comes to the king in the inner court without being called — there is one law for them. Death. Unless the king holds out the golden scepter. Then they live." She paused. "I have not been called to come to the king these thirty days."

Hathach carried these words across the open courtyards and through the gate to where Mordecai waited. Mordecai heard her reply. His face did not change.

He sent his answer back.

"Do not think to yourself that you will escape in the king's house any more than all the other Jews. If you stay silent at this time, relief and rescue will come to the Jews from another place. But you and your father's house will be destroyed." He stopped. Then he said, "And who knows whether you have come to the kingdom for such a time as this?"

Hathach returned through the corridors again. The afternoon light fell through high windows onto the stone floors. He delivered Mordecai's words to the queen, every one.

Esther stood still for a long moment. The room was quiet around her. Her attendants waited nearby, watching her face.

Then she gave her command, and Hathach carried it to Mordecai.

"Go. Gather all the Jews in Shushan. Fast for me. Do not eat. Do not drink. Three days. Night and day." Her voice was steady and clear. "My young women and I will fast the same way. And then I will go in to the king. It is not according to the law. And if I perish, I perish."

Mordecai received her words at the gate. The sun was moving lower over the city rooftops. The streets of Shushan stretched out around him, full of people who did not yet know what had been decided in that exchange of messages through the palace walls.

He turned and went his way and did everything Esther had commanded him.
