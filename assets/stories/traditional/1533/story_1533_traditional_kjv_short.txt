Hathach came again through the corridors of the palace and stood before Esther the queen. And he told her all that Mordecai had spoken at the king's gate — the full sum of silver, the decree that had gone forth, and the copy of the written command for the destroying of all the Jews throughout every province. For Mordecai had charged him to lay the matter before the queen and to beseech her that she would go in unto the king and make supplication for her people.

And Esther heard him. Then she sent Hathach again unto Mordecai with her answer.

"Whosoever cometh before the king into the inner court, man or woman, and hath not been summoned — for that one there is but one law. They shall be put to death." She was silent a moment. "Save only if the king extend unto them the golden sceptre; then shall they live." And she said, "These thirty days I have not been called to come before the king."

So Hathach went forth across the open courtyards and through the gate unto the place where Mordecai waited. And Mordecai heard the words of the queen. His countenance did not change.

And he sent his answer back.

"Think not within thyself that thou shalt find safety in the house of the king above all other Jews. For if thou hold thy peace at this time, deliverance and aid shall arise for the Jews from some other quarter." He stayed his tongue. Then he said, "But thou and all thy father's house shall perish. And who can tell whether thou art come unto the kingdom for the sake of such an hour as this?"

And Hathach returned once more through the corridors. The light of the afternoon fell through the high windows and lay upon the stone floors. He delivered unto the queen every word that Mordecai had spoken, withholding nothing.

And Esther stood still a long while. The chamber was quiet about her. Her attendants waited nigh, and watched her face.

Then she gave her commandment, and Hathach bore it unto Mordecai.

"Go. Gather together all the Jews that are found in Shushan. Fast ye for me. Neither eat nor drink. Three days. By night and by day." Her voice was steady and did not waver. "I also and my maidens will fast in like manner. And afterward I will go in unto the king, though it be contrary to the law." She drew breath. "And if I perish, I perish."

And Mordecai received her words at the gate. The sun was moving low over the rooftops of the city. And the streets of Shushan stretched wide about him, filled with people who knew not yet what had been determined in that passing of words through the walls of the palace.

And he turned and went his way, and did according to all that Esther had commanded him.
