A certain woman in the press had been sick twelve years.

The issue of her blood had not stayed in all that time. She had suffered many things of many physicians. She had spent all that she had. Each year she had grown worse, and not bettered.

She had heard of Jesus.

She came up behind him through the press.

She cried not out unto him. She told not her name. She only reached forth for his garment.

She touched the hem of his cloak. She said within her own heart, "If I may but touch his clothes, I shall be whole."

The fountain of her blood was dried up.

She felt in her body that she was healed of her plague.

Jesus stayed his step.

He felt that virtue had gone out from him. He turned him about in the press.

"Who touched my clothes?" said he.

His disciples beheld him. "Thou seest the multitude thronging thee," said they, "and sayest thou, 'Who touched me?'"

He moved not. He looked round about in the press to see her that had done this thing.

The woman saw that she could not be hid.

She, fearing and trembling, knowing what was done in her, came forward.

She fell down at his feet upon the way. She told him all of it — the twelve years, and the physicians, and the spending of all that she had, and what she had said within herself when she reached for the hem.

He said unto her, "Daughter, thy faith hath made thee whole. Go in peace. Be whole of thy plague."

She arose where she had fallen. She went back through the press unto her own house. The bleeding was gone. She walked alive into her own door.
