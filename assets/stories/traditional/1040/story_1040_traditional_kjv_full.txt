After these things, God did tempt Abraham. "Abraham!" God called out.

"Behold, here I am," Abraham answered.

God said, "Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest, and get thee into the land of Moriah. Offer him there for a burnt offering upon one of the mountains which I will tell thee of."

That night, Abraham could find no sleep. He lay in the dark, staring up at nothing. Isaac was his boy — the son for whom he had waited so many years. The son God had promised him when Abraham was already old. Abraham loved Isaac more than any thing in the world.

But Abraham trusted God. He had trusted him before, when God told him to get out of his country and journey unto a new land. He had trusted him when God promised him a son, even when it seemed impossible. And the Lord had ever kept his word. Every single promise.

Early on the morrow, before the sun was up, Abraham rose from his bed. He went without in the cool grey light and clave the wood for the burnt offering. The axe thudded against the logs. He laded the wood upon the ass and bound it tight. Then he wakened Isaac and two of his young men. They set out together.

They journeyed three days. Three long days of dusty roads, sleeping under the stars, and eating simple meals beside the fire. Isaac talked and laughed and asked questions as boys are wont to do. He chased lizards from the path and cast stones at bushes. Abraham watched him and kept on walking. He said not much.

On the third day, Abraham lifted up his eyes and saw the place afar off. It rose up dark against the bright sky. His heart beat hard within him.

"Abide ye here with the ass," Abraham said unto the two young men. "I and the lad will go yonder and worship, and come again to you."

Abraham took the wood of the burnt offering and laid it upon Isaac's back. The lad bore it without complaint — the rough sticks pressing against his shoulders. Abraham bore the fire in a clay vessel and the knife in his hand. And they went, both of them together, up the mountain side by side, their sandals crunching upon the rocky ground.

Isaac looked upon his father. Somewhat felt amiss. They had every thing for an offering save one. "My father?" he said.

"Here am I, my son."

"Behold the fire and the wood," Isaac said slowly. "But where is the lamb for a burnt offering?"

Abraham swallowed hard. His throat was tight. "My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering," he said softly.

So they went both of them together. The wind blew across the rocky hillside. Birds wheeled high above them in the blue sky. Neither of them spake.

When they came to the place which God had told him of, Abraham built an altar there. He found flat stones and laid them one upon another. He laid the wood in order upon the stones, piece by piece. His hands were steady, though his heart was not.

Then he bound Isaac his son, and laid him on the altar upon the wood.

Abraham stretched forth his hand. He took the knife. The metal was cold in his grip.

"Abraham, Abraham!" The voice came out of heaven — the angel of the Lord, loud and clear.

"Here am I," Abraham said. His hand stayed in the air.

"Lay not thine hand upon the lad," the angel said. "Neither do thou any thing unto him. For now I know that thou fearest God, seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son, from me."

Abraham's whole body shook. Relief poured through him like warm water. He laid the knife down upon the stones. His eyes were filled with tears. He lifted them up.

And behold, behind him was a ram — a great male sheep — caught in a thicket by his horns. He struggled and bleated. He had not been there before. The Lord had set him there, at the very moment of need.

Abraham went unto the ram and loosed him from the thicket. He offered him up for a burnt offering in the stead of his son. The fire crackled. The smoke rose up into the blue sky, curling and climbing higher and higher.

Abraham called the name of that place "The Lord Will Provide." Because in the mount of the Lord it shall be seen.

The angel of the Lord called unto Abraham out of heaven a second time. "Because thou hast done this thing, and hast not withheld thy son, thine only son, in blessing I will bless thee. I will multiply thy seed as the stars of the heaven, and as the sand which is upon the sea shore."

Abraham loosed Isaac. The lad stood up, alive and safe. Father and son looked one upon another. Then they went down the mountain together. The young men awaited them at the foot thereof. The ass swished his tail in the warm sun. And they all went home.
