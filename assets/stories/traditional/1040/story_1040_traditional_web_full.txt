After these things, God tested Abraham. "Abraham!" God called out.

"Here I am," Abraham answered.

God said, "Take now your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and go to the land of Moriah. Offer him there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains that I will tell you about."

That night, Abraham could not sleep. He lay in the dark, staring up at nothing. Isaac was his boy — the son he had waited so many years for. The son God had promised him when Abraham was already old. Abraham loved Isaac more than anything in the world.

But Abraham trusted God. He had trusted God before, when God told him to leave his home and travel to a new land. He had trusted God when God promised him a son, even though it seemed impossible. And God had always kept his promises. Every single one.

Early the next morning, before the sun came up, Abraham got out of bed. He went outside in the cool gray light and split wood for the burnt offering. The ax thudded against the logs. He loaded the wood onto the donkey and strapped it tight. Then he woke Isaac and two of his servants. They set out together.

They walked for three days. Three long days of dusty roads, sleeping under the stars, and eating simple meals by the fire. Isaac talked and laughed and asked questions the way boys do. He chased lizards off the path and threw stones at bushes. Abraham watched him and kept walking. He did not say much.

On the third day, Abraham looked up and saw the mountain in the distance. It rose up dark against the bright sky. His heart beat hard in his chest.

"Stay here with the donkey," Abraham told the two servants. "The boy and I will go over there. We will worship and come back to you."

Abraham took the wood for the burnt offering and put it on Isaac's back. The boy carried it without complaining — the rough sticks pressing against his shoulders. Abraham carried the fire in a clay pot and the knife in his hand. The two of them walked up the mountain together, side by side, their sandals crunching on the rocky ground.

Isaac looked at his father. Something felt wrong. They had everything for an offering except one thing. "Father?" he said.

"Here I am, my son."

"I see the fire and the wood," Isaac said slowly. "But where is the lamb for the burnt offering?"

Abraham swallowed hard. His throat was tight. "God will provide himself the lamb for a burnt offering, my son," he said quietly.

They walked on together. The wind blew across the rocky hillside. Birds circled high above them in the blue sky. Neither of them spoke.

When they came to the place God had told him about, Abraham built an altar. He found flat stones and laid them one on top of another. He arranged the wood on top of the stones, piece by piece. His hands were steady even though his heart was not.

Then he bound Isaac his son and laid him on the altar, on top of the wood.

Abraham reached out his hand. He picked up the knife. The metal was cold in his grip.

"Abraham! Abraham!" The voice came from heaven — the angel of the Lord, loud and clear.

"Here I am," Abraham said. His hand stopped in the air.

"Do not lay your hand on the boy," the angel said. "Do not do anything to him. For now I know that you fear God, seeing that you have not withheld your son, your only son, from me."

Abraham's whole body shook. Relief poured through him like warm water. He set the knife down on the stones. His eyes blurred with tears. He looked up.

There, right behind him, was a ram — a big male sheep — caught in a thorny bush by its curly horns. It struggled and bleated. It had not been there before. God had put it there, at exactly the right moment.

Abraham went to the ram and untangled it from the thorns. He offered it as a burnt offering instead of his son. The fire crackled. The smoke rose up into the blue sky, curling and climbing higher and higher.

Abraham named that place "The Lord Will Provide." Because on the mountain of the Lord, it will be provided.

The angel of the Lord called to Abraham a second time from heaven. "Because you have done this thing, and have not withheld your son, your only son, I will surely bless you. I will multiply your descendants like the stars of the sky and like the sand on the seashore."

Abraham untied Isaac. The boy stood up, alive and safe. Father and son looked at each other. Then they walked back down the mountain together. The servants were waiting at the bottom. The donkey swished its tail in the warm sun. And they all went home.
