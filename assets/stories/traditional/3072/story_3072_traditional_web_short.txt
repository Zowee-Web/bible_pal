Sarah lived one hundred twenty-seven years. She died in Kiriath Arba, also called Hebron, in the land of Canaan. Abraham came to mourn for Sarah and to weep for her.

Then Abraham rose from before his dead and spoke to the children of Heth. He described himself as a stranger and a foreigner living among them and asked for possession of a burial place where he could bury his dead.

The children of Heth called Abraham a prince of God among them. They invited him to choose from their finest tombs, saying that none of them would withhold a tomb from him. Abraham stood and bowed before the people of the land.

He asked them to entreat Ephron son of Zohar on his behalf. Abraham named the cave of Machpelah at the end of Ephron’s field and asked that Ephron sell it to him for the full price as a possession for a burial place.

Ephron was sitting among the children of Heth. In the hearing of all who entered the gate of his city, Ephron answered Abraham. He offered Abraham the field and the cave within it in the presence of his people and told him to bury his dead.

Abraham bowed again before the people. Speaking to Ephron where everyone could hear, he asked Ephron to accept the price of the field so that he could bury Sarah there. Ephron answered that the land was worth four hundred shekels of silver and again told Abraham to bury his dead.

Abraham listened to Ephron. In the hearing of the children of Heth, he weighed out the amount Ephron had named: four hundred shekels of silver according to the standard current among the merchants.

The transfer included Ephron’s field in Machpelah before Mamre, the cave within it, and all the trees inside the field’s borders. These were deeded to Abraham as a possession in the presence of the children of Heth and all who entered the city gate.

Afterward, Abraham buried Sarah his wife in the cave of the field of Machpelah before Mamre, which is Hebron, in Canaan. The children of Heth confirmed the field and its cave to Abraham as a possession for a burial place.
