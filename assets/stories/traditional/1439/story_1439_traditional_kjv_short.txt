That day Saul had laid a great oath upon the men. He had said, "Cursed be the man that eateth any food until evening, until I have avenged me of mine enemies." So none of the men had tasted food, and the host was faint.

But Jonathan had not heard the oath of his father.

In the forest he had come unto a place where honey was dropping from a comb upon the ground. He had put forth the end of the rod that was in his hand. He had dipped it into the honey, and he had put his hand unto his mouth, and his eyes were enlightened.

When the lot fell against him in the evening, Jonathan stood before his father in the camp. The men of Israel stood round about them in the gathering of the day. The lamps were lit. The faces were watching.

Saul said to Jonathan, "Tell me what thou hast done."

Jonathan told him, and said, "I did but taste a little honey with the end of the rod that was in mine hand, and, lo, I must die."

Saul answered, "God do so and more also: for thou shalt surely die, Jonathan."

The people held not their peace.

They came forward unto Saul, all of them — the men that had fought through the day, the men that had seen Jonathan break the Philistines upon the rock. They spake as one voice.

"Shall Jonathan die," the people said, "who hath wrought this great salvation in Israel? God forbid: as the Lord liveth, there shall not one hair of his head fall to the ground; for he hath wrought with God this day!"

So the people rescued Jonathan, that he died not.

He went among them in the camp, and they were round about him in the lamplight. The rod was yet in his hand. The honey was upon his fingers when he wiped them. The men of Israel stood close unto him in the gathering of the evening.
