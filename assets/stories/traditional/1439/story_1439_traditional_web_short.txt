That day Saul had laid a great oath on the men. He had said, "Cursed is the man who eats any food until the evening, until I have avenged myself of my enemies." So none of the men had tasted food, and the army was faint.

But Jonathan had not heard the oath of his father.

In the forest he had come to a place where honey was dripping from a comb on the ground. He had put out the end of the rod that was in his hand. He had dipped it into the honey, and he had put his hand to his mouth, and his eyes were brightened.

When the lot fell against him in the evening, Jonathan stood before his father in the camp. The men of Israel stood around them in the gathering of the day. The lamps had been lit. The faces were watching.

Saul said to Jonathan, "Tell me what you have done."

Jonathan told him, and said, "I certainly did taste a little honey with the end of the rod that was in my hand; and behold, I must die."

Saul said, "God do so and more also; for you shall surely die, Jonathan."

The people did not stay silent.

They came forward to Saul, all of them — the men who had fought through the day, the men who had seen Jonathan break the Philistines on the rock. They spoke as one voice.

"Shall Jonathan die," the people said, "who has worked this great salvation in Israel? Far from it! As Yahweh lives, there shall not one hair of his head fall to the ground; for he has worked with God today!"

So the people rescued Jonathan, that he didn't die.

He went among them in the camp, and they were around him in the lamp-light. The rod was still in his hand. The honey was on his fingers when he wiped them. The men of Israel stood close to him in the gathering of the evening.
