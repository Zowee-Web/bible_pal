It was the night before the feast of the passover. Jesus and his disciples had gathered in an upper room in Jerusalem for the evening meal. The room was simple — a low table, cushions upon the floor, oil lamps casting a warm light against the walls. The sounds of the city rose from the streets below.

Jesus knew what was to come. He knew that his hour was at hand. He knew who would betray him. He knew what the morrow held. But he spake not of it. Instead, he rose from supper.

The disciples watched as he laid aside his garments and took a towel and girded himself. He poured water into a bason. Then he kneeled upon the floor and began to wash the disciples' feet.

He went from one to the next. He took each foot in his hands — dusty feet, calloused feet — and washed them gently, and dried them with the towel wherewith he was girded. No man spake. The room was still save the sound of water.

He cometh to Simon Peter. Peter drew back his feet. "Lord," said he, "dost thou wash my feet?"

Jesus answered, "What I do thou knowest not now; but thou shalt know hereafter."

Peter saith unto him, "Thou shalt never wash my feet."

Jesus said, "If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me."

Peter's countenance changed. "Lord," he said quickly, "not my feet only, but also my hands and my head."

Jesus saith unto him, "He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit: and ye are clean, but not all." For he knew who should betray him.

When he had washed their feet, he took his garments and sat down again. The lamps flickered. The disciples sat in silence.

Jesus looked upon them.

"Know ye what I have done to you?" he said. "Ye call me Master and Lord: and ye say well; for so I am. If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another's feet. For I have given you an example, that ye should do as I have done to you."

He paused. The room was still.

"The servant is not greater than his lord," he said. "If ye know these things, happy are ye if ye do them."
