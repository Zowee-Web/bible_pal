It was the night before a very important holiday. Jesus and his twelve closest friends gathered together in a room upstairs for dinner. Bread and wine were on the table. Oil lamps gave the room a warm glow.

Then Jesus did something no one expected.

He got up from the table. He took off his outer robe and wrapped a towel around his waist. He poured water into a big bowl.

Then he knelt down on the floor and started washing his friends' feet.

In those days, people wore sandals and walked on dusty roads. Their feet got very dirty. Usually a servant washed the guests' feet. But here was Jesus — the teacher, the leader — doing the servant's job.

He washed one person's feet, then the next, then the next. No one knew what to say.

When he got to Peter, Peter pulled his feet away. "No way!" Peter said. "You shouldn't be washing MY feet!"

Jesus said, "If I don't wash your feet, you can't really be part of what I'm doing."

Peter changed his mind fast. "Then wash my hands and my head too!"

Jesus smiled. "You just need your feet washed," he said.

When he finished, he put his robe back on and sat down.

"Do you understand what I just did?" he asked. "I'm your teacher, and I washed your feet. That means you should do the same for each other. I've shown you how to treat people — by serving them."

He paused.

"If you know this, you'll be happy when you do it."
