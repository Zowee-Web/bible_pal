It was the night before the feast of the passover. Jesus and his disciples were gathered together in an upper room in Jerusalem for the evening meal. The room was simple — a low table set with bread and wine, cushions upon the floor, oil lamps burning in small niches along the walls. The warm light moved with every draught of air. The sounds of the city came up through the windows — voices, footsteps, the distant bleating of lambs led to slaughter for the feast.

Jesus knew what was at hand. He knew that his hour was come — that he should depart out of this world unto the Father. He knew that Judas, the son of Simon Iscariot, should betray him ere the night was spent. He knew what the morrow held. But he spake not of it. Not yet.

He rose from supper.

The disciples watched as he laid aside his garments and took a towel and girded himself. He poured water into a bason — the sound of it filling the silent room. Then he kneeled upon the floor and began to wash the disciples' feet.

He went from one to the next. He took each foot in his hands — dusty, calloused feet, the feet of fishermen and labourers — and washed them gently, carefully, running the water over the skin and between the toes. Then he wiped them with the towel wherewith he was girded. The disciple said nothing. Jesus moved on to the next.

He washed them one by one. The room was still. The only sound was the water — the dipping, the pouring, the soft falling of it back into the bason. Some of the disciples turned their faces aside, ill at ease. Some watched in silence, their hands still in their laps.

He cometh to Simon Peter. Peter drew back his feet. "Lord," said he, "dost thou wash my feet?"

Jesus looked up at him from the floor. "What I do thou knowest not now; but thou shalt know hereafter."

Peter shook his head. "Thou shalt never wash my feet."

Jesus saith unto him, "If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me."

Peter's countenance changed. "Lord," said he quickly, "not my feet only, but also my hands and my head."

A gentle look crossed the face of Jesus. "He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit," he said. "And ye are clean, but not all." For he knew who should betray him; therefore said he, Ye are not all clean.

When he had washed all their feet, he set the bason aside. He dried his hands upon the towel, took his garments again, and sat down. The lamps flickered. The bread sat untouched. The wine remained in the cups. The disciples sat in silence.

Jesus looked about the room, his eyes resting upon each face.

"Know ye what I have done to you?" said he. "Ye call me Master and Lord: and ye say well; for so I am. If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another's feet. For I have given you an example, that ye should do as I have done to you."

He paused. The room was perfectly still.

"Verily, verily, I say unto you, The servant is not greater than his lord; neither he that is sent greater than he that sent him. If ye know these things, happy are ye if ye do them."

The oil lamps burned low. The bason of water sat near the door, the towel draped upon its edge. Without, the city of Jerusalem settled into the quiet of the evening — the last night ere all things should change.
