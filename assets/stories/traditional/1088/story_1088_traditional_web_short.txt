It was the night before Passover. Jesus and his disciples had gathered in an upper room in Jerusalem for the evening meal. The room was simple — a low table, cushions on the floor, oil lamps casting warm light against the walls. The food was laid out. The bread was broken. The sounds of the city drifted up through the windows from the streets below.

Jesus knew what was coming. He knew that the hour had come for him to leave this world. He knew who would betray him. He knew what the next day held. But he did not speak of it — not yet. Instead, he rose from the table.

The disciples watched as he took off his outer garment and wrapped a towel around his waist. He poured water into a basin. Then he knelt down on the floor and began to wash the disciples' feet.

He moved from one to the next. He took each foot in his hands — dusty feet, calloused feet, feet that had walked the same roads he had walked. He washed them gently, carefully, and dried them with the towel around his waist. No one spoke. The room was quiet except for the sound of water.

When he came to Simon Peter, Peter pulled his feet back. "Lord," he said, "are you going to wash my feet?"

Jesus looked up at him. "You don't understand now what I am doing," he said, "but afterward you will understand."

Peter shook his head. "You will never wash my feet."

Jesus said to him, "If I don't wash you, you have no part with me."

Peter's face changed. "Lord," he said quickly, "then not just my feet — wash my hands and my head too!"

Jesus smiled gently. "The one who has bathed only needs to wash his feet," he said. "You are clean — but not all of you." He said this because he knew who was going to betray him.

When he had finished washing all their feet, he put his garment back on and returned to his place at the table. The lamps flickered. The disciples sat quietly, unsure what to say.

Jesus looked around the room at their faces.

"Do you understand what I have done for you?" he said. "You call me Teacher and Lord, and you are right, for that is what I am. If I then, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet. I have given you an example, that you should do as I have done to you."

He paused. The room was still.

"A servant is not greater than his master," he said. "If you know these things, blessed are you if you do them."

The oil lamps burned low. The bread sat on the table. The basin of water rested on the floor near the door, the towel draped beside it. Outside, the city of Jerusalem settled into the quiet of the evening, unaware of what was to come.
