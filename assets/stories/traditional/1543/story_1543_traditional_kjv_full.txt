The high place at Gibeon stood upon a hill above the town, and it was the greatest of all the high places in the land. Thither Solomon had gone up with his company, and upon the altar he offered a thousand burnt offerings. The smoke rose thick and white throughout the day, lifting above the stones of the platform and drifting over the hills round about. And the fires burned through the afternoon and into the evening, the priests attending them without rest, the wood cracking and splitting in the heat thereof.

And night came upon Gibeon. The fires upon the high place burned low. The stars appeared above the ridgeline. And Solomon slept.

And the LORD appeared unto him in a dream by night.

And God spake, saying, "Ask of me what I shall give thee."

Solomon answered. He did not hasten. He spake as a man speaketh who knoweth well before whom he standeth.

He said that the LORD had dealt in great mercy with his father David, even according as David had walked before him in faithfulness and in righteousness and in uprightness of heart. And he said that God had kept this great lovingkindness toward David, that he had set a son upon his throne, even as it was that very day.

He continued.

He said that now the LORD his God had made his servant king in the place of David his father, and that he was but a young child, knowing not how to go out nor how to come in.

The young king's words were plain and without adornment. He described what he beheld around him.

He said that his servant stood in the midst of the people whom God had chosen — a great people, a multitude that could not be numbered nor counted for greatness.

Then he made his request. One request alone. He asked not for any second thing.

He asked that the LORD would give unto his servant a heart of understanding, that he might judge the people aright and discern between the good and the evil. For who, he said, is sufficient to judge so great a people as this?

And the speech was pleasing in the sight of the Lord. It pleased him that Solomon had asked this thing.

And God spake again. The words came with the sureness of a thing already determined, a thing already accomplished.

He declared that because Solomon had asked this and had not sought for himself length of days, neither had sought riches for himself, nor had sought the destruction of his enemies, but had sought rather understanding whereby to render just judgment — behold, it was done according to his words.

God said unto him, "I have bestowed upon thee a heart both wise and discerning, such that none before thee hath possessed its likeness, neither shall any arise after thee to equal it."

And there was more. God gave beyond that which had been sought.

He told Solomon that he had given him also that which he had not asked — both riches and honour in abundance — so that among all the kings of the earth there should not be found his equal, all the days of his life.

Then God set a condition. One condition, and it was clear as all the rest.

He said that if Solomon would walk in his ways and keep his statutes and his commandments, even as David his father had walked, then he would lengthen Solomon's days upon the earth.

And Solomon awoke. He was at Gibeon. The night air lay upon his face. It had been a dream.

He rose and departed from Gibeon unto Jerusalem. The road descended from the high place and wound southward through the hills. The dust was pale beneath the morning light. And his men walked with him.

And when he came unto Jerusalem, he went before the ark of the covenant of the LORD. The ark rested within the tent that David had pitched for it. And Solomon stood before it, and he offered up burnt offerings upon the altar there. The smoke rose within the tent and curled toward the open sky above. He offered peace offerings also. And the fat burned upon the fire, and the scent of the roasting flesh filled all the space about the ark.

Then Solomon made a feast for all his servants. The tables were set forth in the open courts. Bread and meat and wine were brought out in abundance. His officers and his household gathered about the tables, and the servants sat and did eat together in the presence of the king. And the sounds of the feast carried across the courtyard — the voices of men, the touching of cups one against another, the low murmur of those who eat together after an offering hath been made.

And Solomon sat among them. The feast was spread before him, the plates full, the cups lifted, the servants gathered on every side.
