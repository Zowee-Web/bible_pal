For years the reports had come to the queen of Sheba along the trade roads, carried by merchants who had heard them from other merchants. A king in the west. Wiser than any man living. Rich beyond reckoning, and favoured by the God of Israel, whose name was upon everything he did.

She did not believe it. She had sat upon a throne long enough to know how a report grows. A true thing leaves a man's mouth and travels a thousand miles, and by the time it arrives it has doubled twice over. So she made up her mind to go and prove the man herself.

She came to Jerusalem with a very great train. Camels in a long line, bearing spices and much gold and precious stones, more wealth than most kings would see in a lifetime. But the weight she carried that mattered most could not be laid upon any animal. She carried questions. Hard ones. The riddles and the deep matters that had stumped the wise men of her own court, the things no one in her land had been able to answer. She had come to prove him with them.

She communed with Solomon of all that was in her heart. She held nothing back. She asked him the questions she had carried so long that she had half stopped believing they had answers at all.

Solomon told her all her questions. Nothing was too hard for him. There was not any thing hid from the king which he told her not. Whatsoever she set before him, he opened it to her, clear and unhurried, as a man explains a thing he has known all his life. The questions she had guarded like treasure, he turned over in the light and handed back to her, solved.

And when the questions ran out, she began to look.

She saw the house that he had built, stone fitted to stone. She saw the meat of his table. She saw the sitting of his servants, each in his appointed place, and the attendance of his ministers, and his cupbearers, and the apparel that they wore. She saw the order of it, the whole house moving like one body. And she saw the ascent by which the king went up unto the house of the LORD.

There was no more spirit in her.

She stood there with nothing left to say. Every question was answered. Every doubt was spent.

When her voice returned, it was not the voice of a queen proving a rival. It was something quieter.

"It was a true report," she said, "that I heard in mine own land of thy acts and of thy wisdom. Howbeit I believed not the words, until I came, and mine eyes had seen it. And behold, the half was not told me. Thy wisdom and prosperity exceedeth the fame which I heard."

She looked upon the men gathered about him, standing each in his place. "Happy are these thy servants, which stand continually before thee, and that hear thy wisdom."

Then she lifted her eyes past the king, past the house, past all of it.

"Blessed be the LORD thy God, which delighted in thee, to set thee on the throne of Israel. Because the LORD loved Israel for ever, therefore made he thee king, to do judgment and justice."

She gave the king an hundred and twenty talents of gold, and of spices very great store, such as never came into Israel again, and precious stones beyond counting. And Solomon, for his part, gave unto the queen of Sheba all her desire, whatsoever she asked, beside that which he gave her of his royal bounty.

Then she turned and went unto her own country, she and her servants, the long train of camels moving east into the morning.
