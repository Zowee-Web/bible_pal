The queen of Sheba had heard the reports for years. A king in the west, they said. Wiser than any man alive, and favored by the God of Israel. She did not believe it. Reports travel far, and they grow on the road. So she loaded a caravan and went to weigh the man herself.

She came to Jerusalem with a long line of camels behind her, bearing spices and gold and precious stones. In her heart she carried something heavier than any of it. Hard questions. The kind that had gone unanswered in her own court for years. She had come to test him.

She sat with Solomon and laid it all out, everything that was in her heart. The riddles. The deep matters. The questions she had held so long that she half believed they had no floor beneath them.

Solomon answered every one. Nothing was too hard for him. Nothing was hidden. Whatever she asked, he opened it to her plainly, the way a man tells you what he plainly knows.

Then she stopped asking, and she looked.

She saw the house he had built. She saw the food laid along his table, the servants standing each in his place, the cup bearers at their stations, the cut and color of their robes. She watched the king go up by his own ascent to the house of the LORD.

And the breath went out of her.

She stood with nothing left to say. The questions were gone. The doubt was gone. There was no more spirit in her.

When her voice came back, it had changed.

"It was a true report," she said. "All of it. Everything I heard in my own land of your deeds and your wisdom. And I did not believe a word of it — not until I came, and saw it with my own eyes." She shook her head. "The half was not told me. Your wisdom and your wealth run past anything that ever reached my country."

She looked at the men around him. "Happy are these, your servants, who stand here day after day and hear what you know."

Then she lifted her eyes higher than the king.

"Blessed be the LORD your God, who delighted in you and set you on the throne of Israel. Because he loved Israel forever, he made you king, to do justice and righteousness."

She gave him gold, a hundred and twenty talents of it, and spices in such abundance that none like it ever came again, and precious stones besides. And Solomon gave her back all she desired, and more, out of his own royal hand.

Then she turned for home, she and her servants, the long line of camels moving east into the morning.
