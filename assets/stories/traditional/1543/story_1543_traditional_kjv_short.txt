The queen of Sheba had heard the reports for years. A king in the west, they said. Wiser than any man alive, and favoured by the God of Israel. She did not believe it. Reports travel far, and they grow upon the road. So she made ready a train of camels and went to prove the man herself.

She came to Jerusalem with a very great train, camels bearing spices and much gold and precious stones. Yet in her heart she carried something heavier than any of it. Hard questions. The kind that had gone unanswered in her own court for years. She had come to prove him.

She communed with Solomon of all that was in her heart. The riddles. The deep matters. The questions she had held so long that she half believed they had no floor beneath them.

Solomon told her all her questions. Nothing was too hard for him. There was not any thing hid from the king which he told her not. Whatsoever she asked, he opened it to her plainly, as a man tells what he surely knows.

Then she ceased her asking, and she looked.

She saw the house that he had built. She saw the meat of his table, the sitting of his servants, the attendance of his ministers, the apparel they wore, and his cupbearers in their places. And she saw the ascent by which he went up unto the house of the LORD.

And there was no more spirit in her.

She stood with nothing left to say. The questions were gone. The doubt was gone.

When her voice returned, it was changed.

"It was a true report," she said, "that I heard in mine own land of thy acts and of thy wisdom. Howbeit I believed not the words, until I came, and mine eyes had seen it. And behold, the half was not told me. Thy wisdom and prosperity exceedeth the fame which I heard."

She looked upon the men about him. "Happy are these thy servants, which stand continually before thee, and that hear thy wisdom."

Then she lifted her eyes higher than the king.

"Blessed be the LORD thy God, which delighted in thee, to set thee on the throne of Israel. Because the LORD loved Israel for ever, therefore made he thee king, to do judgment and justice."

She gave the king an hundred and twenty talents of gold, and of spices very great store, and precious stones. And Solomon gave unto the queen of Sheba all her desire, whatsoever she asked, beside that which he gave her of his royal bounty.

Then she turned and went unto her own country, she and her servants, the long train of camels moving east into the morning.
