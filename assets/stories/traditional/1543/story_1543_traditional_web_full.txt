For years the reports had come to the queen of Sheba along the trade roads, carried by merchants who had heard them from other merchants. A king in the west. Wiser than any man living. Rich beyond reckoning, and favored by the God of Israel, whose name was on everything he did.

She did not believe it. She had sat on a throne long enough to know how a report grows. A true thing leaves a man's mouth and travels a thousand miles, and by the time it arrives it has doubled twice over. So she made up her mind to go and weigh the man herself.

She came to Jerusalem with a great caravan. Camels in a long line, bearing spices and gold and precious stones, more wealth than most kings would see in a lifetime. But the weight she carried that mattered most could not be loaded on any animal. She carried questions. Hard ones. The riddles and the deep matters that had stumped the wise men of her own court, the things no one in her land had been able to answer. She had come to test him with them.

She sat with Solomon and laid it all before him, everything that was in her heart. She held nothing back. She asked him the questions she had carried so long that she had half stopped believing they had answers at all.

Solomon answered every one. Nothing was too hard for him. Nothing was hidden from him. Whatever she set before him, he opened it to her, clear and unhurried, the way a man explains a thing he has known all his life. The questions she had guarded like treasure, he turned over in the light and handed back to her, solved.

And when the questions ran out, she began to look.

She saw the house he had built, stone fitted to stone. She saw the food laid out along his table. She saw his servants, each standing in his appointed place, and the men who waited on his cup, and the cut and the color of the robes they wore. She saw the order of it, the whole house moving like one body. And she watched the king rise and go up by his own ascent to the house of the LORD.

The breath went out of her.

She stood there with nothing left to say. Every question was answered. Every doubt was spent. There was no more spirit in her.

When her voice returned, it was not the voice of a queen testing a rival. It was something quieter.

"It was a true report," she said. "Everything I heard in my own land of your deeds and your wisdom was true. And still I did not believe it — not until I came here and saw it with my own eyes." She shook her head slowly. "The half was not told me. Your wisdom and your wealth go far past anything that ever reached my country."

She looked at the men gathered around him, standing each in his place. "Happy are these, your servants, who stand before you day after day and hear your wisdom."

Then she lifted her eyes past the king, past the house, past all of it.

"Blessed be the LORD your God, who delighted in you and set you on the throne of Israel. Because the LORD loved Israel forever, he has made you king, to do justice and righteousness."

She gave him a hundred and twenty talents of gold. She gave him spices in such abundance that no such store ever came to Israel again, and precious stones beyond counting. And Solomon, for his part, gave her everything she desired, whatever she asked, and more besides, out of the bounty of his own hand.

Then she turned and went back to her own land, she and her servants, the long line of camels moving east into the morning.
