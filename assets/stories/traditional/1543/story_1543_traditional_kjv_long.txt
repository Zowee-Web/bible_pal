The high place at Gibeon stood upon a hill above the town, and it was the chief of all the high places in the land. Thither went Solomon with them that attended him, and he offered a thousand burnt offerings upon the altar. The smoke rose thick and white through the day, lifting above the stone platform and drifting over the hills round about. The wood was laid on, and kindled, and laid on again. The priests moved about the altar with their robes drawn close, and their faces were dark with soot. The fires burned through the afternoon and into the evening. The wood cracked and split in the heat. Ash drifted down upon the stones and gathered in the seams.

The hills about Gibeon went red, and then gray, and then dark. The town beneath drew her shutters close. Lamps were lit in the houses one by one, and one by one were quenched. Night came upon Gibeon. The fires on the high place burned low, the coals red and pulsing beneath a thin shell of ash. The stars appeared above the ridge. The air grew cool. And Solomon slept.

And the LORD appeared unto him in a dream by night.

And God said, "Ask what I shall give thee."

Solomon answered. He hasted not. He spake as a man speaketh when he knoweth who heareth him.

"Thou hast shewed unto thy servant David my father great mercy," he said, "according as he walked before thee in truth and in righteousness and in uprightness of heart with thee. And thou hast kept for him this great kindness, that thou hast given him a son to sit upon his throne, as it is this day."

And he went on.

"And now, O LORD my God, thou hast made thy servant king in the room of David my father. And I am but a little child. I know not how to go out or to come in."

The words of the young king were plain, and without ornament. He spake of that which he saw round about him.

"And thy servant is in the midst of thy people which thou hast chosen, a great people that cannot be numbered nor counted for multitude."

Then made he his request. One request. He asked not a second thing.

"Give thy servant an understanding heart to judge thy people, that I may discern between good and bad. For who is able to judge this thy so great a people?"

And the saying pleased the Lord. It pleased him that Solomon had asked this thing.

And God spake again. The words came with the clearness of a thing determined, a thing already done.

"Because thou hast asked this thing, and hast not asked for thyself long life, neither hast asked riches for thyself, nor hast asked the life of thine enemies, but hast asked for thyself understanding to discern judgment — behold, I have done according to thy words."

And God said, "I have given thee a wise and an understanding heart, so that there hath been none like thee before thee, neither after thee shall any arise like thee."

And there was more. God gave beyond that which had been asked.

"And I have also given thee that which thou hast not asked, both riches and honour, so that there shall not be any among the kings like unto thee all thy days."

Then God set a condition. One condition, clear as the rest.

"And if thou wilt walk in my ways, to keep my statutes and my commandments, as thy father David did walk, then will I lengthen thy days."

And Solomon awoke. He was at Gibeon. The night air was upon his face. The coals upon the altar yet glowed somewhere below the ridge, faint and red. The stars were moved out of their places. It was a dream.

He lay still a space. Then arose he up. He called his servants. The horses were brought, and the men gathered themselves, and in the early light he journeyed from Gibeon unto Jerusalem. The road went down from the high place and wound southward through the hills. The dust was pale under the morning light. Goats moved upon the slopes. A few husbandmen were already in their fields, bowed over the stones at the edges of the terraces. His men walked beside him. None spake much. The sun arose higher, and the heat came down upon the way.

And when he was come to Jerusalem, he went unto the ark of the covenant of the LORD. The ark stood in the tent which David had pitched for it. The flaps were drawn back, and the inside was dim and cool after the road. Solomon stood before it. He tarried a moment, beholding the ark, and the carved cherubim, and the staves set in the rings upon the sides thereof. Then offered he up burnt offerings upon the altar there. The fire was kindled and the wood took, and the smoke rose within the tent and curled toward the open sky above. And he offered peace offerings also. The fat was laid upon the coals. It hissed, and ran, and burned. The savour of the roasting meat filled the place about the ark and drifted out through the tent flaps into the court.

Then made Solomon a feast unto all his servants. The word went forth, and the cooks set to their labour, and the tables were spread in the open courts. Bread was carried out in baskets. The flesh of the offerings was borne in upon platters, yet steaming. Wine was poured into cups, and the cups went down the tables. His princes came in from their chambers. His household was gathered. Captains and stewards and scribes took their places. And the servants sat and did eat together in the presence of the king.

The noise of the meal carried across the court. Voices rose and were mingled. Cup rang against cup. Men laughed, and leaned one toward another, and reached across the tables for the bread. A low hum of speech settled upon the courts, the hum of men eating together after an offering hath been made. The afternoon sun came down over the walls and lit upon the white stones.

And Solomon sat in the midst of them. The feast stretched before him, the plates full, the cups lifted up, the servants gathered on every side.
