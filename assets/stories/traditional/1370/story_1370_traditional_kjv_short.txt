Paul and Barnabas had stood up in the synagogue of Antioch in Pisidia on the second sabbath of their preaching. On the first sabbath, the rulers of the synagogue had bidden them bring some word of exhortation, and Paul had arisen and told them the story from David through the resurrection of Jesus. The Jews which had gathered then had besought that the same words might be spoken unto them again the next sabbath. And on the second sabbath, almost the whole city was come together to hear the word of God. There had been contention. The leaders of the Jews had contradicted Paul and blasphemed. And then Paul had turned unto the Gentiles that were gathered there, and had spoken.

And when the Gentiles heard this, they were glad, and glorified the word of the Lord: and as many as were ordained to eternal life believed.

And the word of the Lord was published throughout all the region.

But the Jews stirred up the devout and honourable women, and the chief men of the city, and raised persecution against Paul and Barnabas, and expelled them out of their coasts.

But they shook off the dust of their feet against them, and came unto Iconium.

And the disciples were filled with joy, and with the Holy Ghost.

And the road unto Iconium opened before them. The dust of Antioch in Pisidia fell from their sandals as they walked, and behind them the new believers — who had not been there when the morning began — abode in the houses of Antioch with the word that had been given unto them. And the two that had been driven out went on with the work, and the joy that had filled them lessened not on the road unto Iconium.
