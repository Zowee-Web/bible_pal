Paul and Barnabas had stood up in the synagogue of Pisidian Antioch on the second Sabbath of their preaching. The first Sabbath, the rulers of the synagogue had asked them to bring some word of exhortation, and Paul had stood and told them the story from David through the resurrection of Jesus. The Jews who had gathered then had begged that the same words be spoken to them again the next Sabbath. And on the second Sabbath, almost the whole city had come together to hear the word of God. There had been argument. The Jewish leaders had contradicted Paul and blasphemed. And then Paul had turned to the Gentiles who were gathered there, and had spoken.

As the Gentiles heard this, they were glad, and glorified the word of God. As many as were appointed to eternal life believed.

The Lord's word was spread abroad throughout all the region.

But the Jews stirred up the devout and prominent women and the chief men of the city, and stirred up a persecution against Paul and Barnabas, and threw them out of their borders.

But they shook off the dust of their feet against them, and came to Iconium.

The disciples were filled with joy and with the Holy Spirit.

And the road to Iconium opened before them. The dust of Pisidian Antioch fell from their sandals as they walked, and behind them the new believers — who had not been there when the morning began — stayed in the houses of Antioch with the word that had been given to them. And the two who had been driven out went on with the work, and the joy that had filled them did not lessen on the road to Iconium.
