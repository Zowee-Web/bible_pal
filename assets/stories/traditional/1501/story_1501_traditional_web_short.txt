The servant spoke of what had been given to him, and the way he spoke of it was very quiet. "The Lord GOD has given me," he said, "the tongue of the learned, that I should know how to speak a word in season to him that is weary."

He paused on that word — weary — and then he went on.

"He wakens my ear morning by morning," the servant said. "He wakens it to hear as the learned. The Lord GOD has opened my ear. And I was not rebellious. I did not turn away backward."

He told what he had given in exchange for the opened ear. "I gave my back to those who struck me, and my cheeks to those who plucked off the hair. I did not hide my face from shame and spitting."

He named what held him steady through it. "For the Lord GOD will help me. Therefore I have not been ashamed. Therefore I have set my face like a flint, and I know I shall not be confounded. He is near who justifies me. Who will contend with me? Let us stand together. Who is mine adversary? Let him come near."

The servant fell quiet again. The morning was coming on around him, the same morning in which his ear had been opened that day, the way it had been opened every other day since the first. The light came across the floor of the room where he had been speaking. The doves outside the high window had begun to call.

The servant sat where he had been sitting. The day would have its weight, and it would press on him as the days had pressed. The tongue that had been given him was for the weary, and there would be weary ones in the country before the sun was very high.

He waited for them in the small light at the table.
