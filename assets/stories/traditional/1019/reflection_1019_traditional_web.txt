The bush was ordinary. It was the kind of scrub that grew in every gully of the wilderness, passed without a second glance by every shepherd who walked those hills. What made it extraordinary was not its nature but what inhabited it. The fire did not consume. It revealed.

Moses asked for a name, and the answer he received was unlike any name he had encountered. Not a title. Not a function. Just existence itself — I AM. There is something about that name that resists being domesticated or filed away. It remains, like the bush, burning and unconsumed, as present now as it was on that quiet hillside.
