Now Moses kept the flock of Jethro his father in law, the priest of Midian. The sheep moved slowly across the dry ground, their hooves stirring dust. Moses led the flock to the backside of the desert, away from the settled pastures, into the rocky country near Horeb, the mountain of God.

The land was rough and still. Stones lay in heaps between sparse patches of thorn. The air stirred not. Heat rose from the ground in waves that bent the shape of distant rocks, and the sky was pale and flat from one edge unto the other. There was no sound save the bleating of sheep and the clatter of loose stone underfoot.

The angel of the Lord appeared unto him in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush. Moses beheld it — a thorny bush, such as grew wild in every gully of the wilderness, burning with fire. The flames moved through the branches, bright and steady, orange and white at the heart. But the bush was not consumed. The wood blackened not. The leaves curled not, nor fell. The fire burned, and the bush endured, whole in the midst of the flame.

Moses said, "I will now turn aside and see this great sight, why the bush is not burnt."

He drew nearer. The ground beneath his feet was the same dry earth he had trod all the morning. The sheep scattered behind him, left for the moment unwatched. He moved toward the bush, drawn by the sight of fire that destroyed not what it touched.

When the Lord saw that he turned aside to see, God called unto him out of the midst of the bush. "Moses, Moses."

He said, "Here am I."

"Draw not nigh hither." The voice was clear, bearing an authority that stayed him where he stood. "Put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground."

Moses stooped and loosed his shoes. The earth pressed against the bare soles of his feet — the same ground, but now named as other than common.

"I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob."

Moses hid his face, for he was afraid to look upon God.

The Lord spake on. "I have surely seen the affliction of my people which are in Egypt, and have heard their cry by reason of their taskmasters, for I know their sorrows. I am come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians, and to bring them up out of that land unto a good land and a large, unto a land flowing with milk and honey — unto the place of the Canaanites, and the Hittites, and the Amorites, and the Perizzites, and the Hivites, and the Jebusites."

The names rolled forth — nations and territories, a map of promise. God had seen. God had heard. God was come down.

"Now therefore, behold, the cry of the children of Israel is come unto me; and I have also seen the oppression wherewith the Egyptians oppress them. Come now therefore, and I will send thee unto Pharaoh, that thou mayest bring forth my people the children of Israel out of Egypt."

Moses said unto God, "Who am I, that I should go unto Pharaoh, and that I should bring forth the children of Israel out of Egypt?"

The question was plain. A shepherd in the waste places, far from the courts of Egypt, far from any seat of power. Who am I?

God answered, "Certainly I will be with thee. And this shall be a token unto thee, that I have sent thee: when thou hast brought forth the people out of Egypt, ye shall serve God upon this mountain."

The token pointed forward. It would not be given beforehand. It would be confirmed only when the work was done — worship upon this same mountain, Horeb, where the bush burned without burning.

Moses said unto God, "When I come unto the children of Israel and shall say unto them, 'The God of your fathers hath sent me unto you,' and they shall say to me, 'What is his name?' — what shall I say unto them?"

God said unto Moses, "I AM THAT I AM."

Three words. No title, no lineage, no likening unto other gods. Pure being. Pure presence. Self-existent, self-named, answerable unto no class or kind.

He said, "Thus shalt thou say unto the children of Israel, 'I AM hath sent me unto you.'" God said moreover unto Moses, "Thus shalt thou say unto the children of Israel, 'The Lord God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, hath sent me unto you.' This is my name for ever, and this is my memorial unto all generations."

The bush yet burned. The fire yet held. Moses stood barefoot upon the holy ground, his face hidden, the voice of God filling the air about him — and the sheep grazed, undisturbed, upon the slope below.
