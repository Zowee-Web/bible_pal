Moses was keeping the flock of Jethro, his father-in-law, the priest of Midian. The sheep moved slowly across the dry ground, their hooves stirring dust. Moses led them to the far side of the wilderness, away from the settled pastures, into the rocky terrain near Horeb, the mountain of God.

The land was rough and quiet. Stones lay in heaps between sparse patches of brush. The air was still. Heat rose from the ground in visible waves, and the sky was a flat, pale blue from edge to edge. There was no sound but the bleating of sheep and the occasional clatter of loose rock underfoot.

The angel of the Lord appeared to him in a flame of fire out of the middle of a bush. Moses saw it — a thorny bush, the kind that grew wild in every gully of the wilderness, burning with fire. The flames moved through the branches, bright and steady, orange and white at the core. But the bush was not consumed. The wood did not blacken. The leaves did not curl or fall. The fire burned and the bush remained, unchanged, alive in the midst of it.

Moses said, "I will turn aside now and see this great sight, why the bush is not burned."

He stepped closer. The ground beneath his feet was the same dry earth he had walked all morning. The sheep scattered behind him, unattended for the moment. He moved toward the bush, drawn by the sight of fire that did not destroy what it touched.

When the Lord saw that he turned aside to look, God called to him out of the middle of the bush. "Moses, Moses."

He said, "Here I am."

"Do not come close." The voice was clear, immediate, carrying an authority that stopped him where he stood. "Take off your sandals, for the place where you are standing is holy ground."

Moses reached down and removed his sandals. The ground pressed against the bare soles of his feet — the same ground, but now named as something other than ordinary.

"I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob."

Moses hid his face. He was afraid to look at God.

The Lord spoke on. "I have surely seen the affliction of my people who are in Egypt. I have heard their cry because of their taskmasters, for I know their sufferings. I have come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians, and to bring them up out of that land to a good and large land, to a land flowing with milk and honey — to the place of the Canaanite, the Hittite, the Amorite, the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite."

The names rolled out — nations and territories, a geography of promise. God had seen. God had heard. God had come down.

"Now the cry of the children of Israel has come to me. I have also seen the oppression with which the Egyptians oppress them. Come now, therefore, and I will send you to Pharaoh, that you may bring my people, the children of Israel, out of Egypt."

Moses said to God, "Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh, and that I should bring the children of Israel out of Egypt?"

The question was direct. A shepherd in the wilderness, far from the courts of Egypt, far from power. Who am I?

God answered, "Certainly I will be with you. And this will be the sign to you that I have sent you: when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you shall serve God on this mountain."

The sign pointed forward. It would not be given in advance. It would be confirmed only after the deed was done — worship on this same mountain, Horeb, where the bush burned without burning.

Moses said to God, "When I come to the children of Israel and tell them, 'The God of your fathers has sent me to you,' and they ask me, 'What is his name?' — what shall I say to them?"

God said to Moses, "I AM WHO I AM."

Three words. No title, no genealogy, no comparison to other gods. Pure being. Pure presence. Self-existent, self-defined, answerable to no category.

He said, "You shall tell the children of Israel, 'I AM has sent me to you.'" God said further to Moses, "You shall tell the children of Israel, 'The Lord, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you.' This is my name forever, and this is my memorial to all generations."

The bush still burned. The fire still held. Moses stood barefoot on the holy ground, his face hidden, the voice of God filling the air around him — and the sheep grazed, unbothered, on the slope below.
