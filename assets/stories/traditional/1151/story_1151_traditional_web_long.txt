Adoni-Zedek the king of Jerusalem heard, and his hands grew cold.

He heard how Joshua had taken Ai and utterly destroyed it. He heard how the inhabitants of Gibeon had made peace with Israel and were among them now. Gibeon was a great city. It was as one of the royal cities. It was greater than Ai. Its men were mighty. And it had bent the knee. The king of Jerusalem stood in his hall and the news rang against the walls and would not settle.

He sent runners that same day. He sent to Hoham king of Hebron, and to Piram king of Jarmuth, and to Japhia king of Lachish, and to Debir king of Eglon. The word he sent was short. "Come up to me and help me. Let us strike Gibeon. For they have made peace with Joshua and with the children of Israel."

The four kings came. They brought all their hosts with them. Five armies poured up out of the hill country and gathered in one camp before the walls of Gibeon. The chariots stood in long lines on the plain. The cooking fires of the captains burned at evening. The smoke rose along the ridge above the city. And then the kings of the Amorites began the war.

Inside Gibeon, the men of the city sent for help.

The messenger went south by foot. He went the long way, down the wadis, around the watches of the five kings. He did not stop. He came at last to the camp of Israel at Gilgal, in the plain of Jericho, at the falling of the night. He came to the tent of Joshua. He fell to his knees on the dust. His chest was heaving. His lips were cracked.

"Do not abandon your servants," he said. "Come up to us quickly. Save us. Help us. For all the kings of the Amorites that dwell in the hill country are gathered together against us."

Joshua heard him. He did not sit down. He did not call for water. He called for his captains.

The captains came. They stood in the lamplight of the tent. Joshua gave the order in a voice that did not lift. Every man of war. Every mighty man of valor. The whole army would go up that night. He named the time. They turned and went out to make the camp ready.

Then, before he stepped from the tent, the word of the Lord came to him.

"Do not fear them. For I have delivered them into your hand. Not a man of them shall stand before you."

Joshua bowed his head where he stood. He laid his hand on the haft of his sword. He stepped out into the dark.

The camp at Gilgal moved by torch and by quiet command. The men strapped on their sandals where they had been sitting. They took up their spears. They took up their bows. They tied their cloaks close against the night air. The line formed in the dust between the tents. The line began to climb.

They marched all night.

The road from Gilgal rises hard. It comes up out of the Jordan plain and bends into the hills of the Amorites by sharp climbing turns. The moon was high. The limestone showed pale. The dust the front ranks raised drifted back over the men behind. The captains held them at a steady pace. The mighty men of valor went at the head, and Joshua went with them. Stones turned underfoot. A cloak caught on the rough brush of a slope and was pulled free without breaking stride. A water skin slapped softly against a thigh. A man behind coughed once into his sleeve and the line went on. Higher and higher the road went, switching back along the ridges, the air growing thinner, the night growing late.

They came over the last ridge near Gibeon at the breaking of the day.

The Amorite camp was spread on the plain below and had not yet woken. The five kings had not believed that any host could march that road in one night. They had not set their watches against the south. Their fires were low. The tents stood in long quiet rows. Joshua looked down at them and the light was beginning to come on behind him.

He came on them suddenly.

The line of Israel poured down off the ridge. The first cry of an Amorite watch went up too late. The Lord confused them before Israel. He smote them with a great slaughter at Gibeon. The host of the five kings broke. They could not hold. They could not form. They turned and ran along the way that goes up to Beth Horon, and Israel pursued. They were chased to Azekah. They were chased as far as Makkedah.

The descent of Beth Horon is a narrow road. It falls between rock walls. The fleeing men poured down it in a current that could not be turned aside. The chariots that had stood in long lines on the plain were left behind. The captains who had marched up out of Jerusalem and Hebron and Jarmuth and Lachish and Eglon were running on foot now among their own men. And as they ran, the sky changed.

The clouds that had stood low over the hills since the dawn gathered tighter and grew dark. A wind ran ahead through the brush, bending it flat. Then the Lord cast down great stones from heaven upon them. The hail came down in stones the size of a man's fist, and then larger. They struck the men on the descent of Beth Horon. They struck the men along the road to Azekah. The hail did not strike Israel. It struck the host that fled before Israel. The hailstones killed more of them than the sword of Israel killed. Where the men of Israel came, the road was already strewn.

The wind passed. The hail stopped. The work of the day was not yet done.

Joshua came up onto a high place above Gibeon, where the men around him could see his face. He turned and looked west. The sun was past the height of noon. It was leaning down toward the valley of Aijalon. The fleeing host was still on the road. The pursuit had not yet reached its end. There were hours of work that needed daylight, and the daylight would not last.

He stood there in the sight of Israel. He spoke to the Lord in the day when the Lord delivered up the Amorites before the children of Israel. He lifted up his voice so that the men around him could hear.

"Sun, stand still on Gibeon. You, moon, stop in the valley of Aijalon."

The sun stood still. The moon stayed.

The shadow on the stone at Joshua's feet did not lengthen. The light over Gibeon held where it had been. The light over the valley of Aijalon held. A captain near Joshua turned his face up to the sky and held it there a long moment. The sun stayed in the middle of the sky, and did not hurry to go down about a whole day. Israel went on along the road. The pursuit was carried through to its end. The kings of the five cities were sought out. The avenging of the people upon their enemies was finished. And the sun did not move from above them until it was done.

There was no day like that before it or after it. The Lord listened to the voice of a man. For the Lord fought for Israel.

When the day at last let go, Joshua returned, and all Israel with him, to the camp at Gilgal. The men came down the same road they had climbed in the dark. They came down with the dust of Beth Horon on their sandals. The watchfires in the plain of Jericho were burning ahead of them. The camp received them in. The wounded were laid down on cloaks beside the tents. Water was brought. A long quiet settled over Gilgal. Out beyond the camp the night had finally come, the moon was where it should be, and a man somewhere in the tents began to tell what he had seen.
