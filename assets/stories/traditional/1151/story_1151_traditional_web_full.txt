Word came up from Gibeon at first dark. A runner stumbled into the camp at Gilgal with the dust of the lowlands still on his face. He fell to his knees before Joshua's tent. His chest was heaving. He could not speak for a moment. Then he found the words.

"Do not abandon your servants. Come up to us quickly. Save us. Help us. For all the kings of the Amorites that dwell in the hill country have gathered together against us."

Five kings had come up. The king of Jerusalem, the king of Hebron, the king of Jarmuth, the king of Lachish, the king of Eglon. They had brought all their armies. They were encamped before Gibeon. They had begun the war against the city.

Joshua heard the runner out. He did not sit. He turned to the captains who stood near him in the lamplight. He gave the order. Every man of war. Every mighty man of valor. They would go up that night.

Then the word of the Lord came to him in the tent before he went out. "Do not fear them, for I have delivered them into your hands. Not a man of them shall stand before you."

Joshua bowed his head. He took up his sword. He stepped out into the dark.

The camp at Gilgal moved by torch and by quiet command. Men strapped on their sandals where they had been sitting. They took up their spears. They took up their bows. The line formed. The line began to climb.

They marched all night. The road from Gilgal rises hard. It climbs out of the Jordan plain and up into the hills, switching back along the limestone, the dust silver under the moon. The men did not speak. The captains held them at a steady pace. The mighty men of valor went at the head. Joshua went with them. Stones turned underfoot. A cloak caught on the rough brush of a slope and was pulled free without breaking stride. The line stretched long behind him, men climbing in the dark, the column rising into the hill country one ridge at a time.

They came over the last ridge near Gibeon at the breaking of the day.

The Amorite camp was spread before them and had not woken. The five kings had not set their watches against the road from Gilgal. They had not believed that any host could march that road in the night. Joshua came on them suddenly. He came on them as the light came.

The Lord confused them before Israel. He smote them with a great slaughter at Gibeon. The host of the five kings broke. They turned and ran along the way that goes up to Beth Horon, and Israel pursued. They were chased to Azekah and to Makkedah. The descent of Beth Horon is steep and narrow, and the fleeing men poured down it in a current that could not be turned.

And as they fled, the sky changed.

The clouds that had stood low over the hills since the morning gathered tighter and grew dark. A wind ran ahead through the brush. Then the Lord cast down great stones from heaven upon them. The hail came down in stones the size of a man's fist, then larger. They struck the men on the descent of Beth Horon. They struck the men along the road to Azekah. The hailstones killed more of them than the sword of Israel killed. Where Israel passed, the road was already strewn.

Joshua came up onto a high place above Gibeon. He looked west. The sun was past the height of noon and was beginning to lean toward the valley of Aijalon. The fleeing host was still on the road. The work of that day was not yet done. He stood in the sight of Israel, and he spoke to the Lord. And then he lifted up his voice so that the men around him could hear.

"Sun, stand still on Gibeon. You, moon, stop in the valley of Aijalon."

The sun stood still. The moon stayed. The shadow on the stone at Joshua's feet did not lengthen. The light over Gibeon held. The light over the valley of Aijalon held. The sun stayed in the middle of the sky, and did not hurry to go down about a whole day. Israel went on along the road, the hail had spent itself, the pursuit was carried through to its end, and the sun did not move from above them until the avenging was finished.

There was no day like that before it or after it. The Lord listened to the voice of a man. The Lord fought for Israel.

When it was done, Joshua returned, and all Israel with him, to the camp at Gilgal.
