There was a man named Job who lived in a place called Uz. Job was a good man — one of the best. He loved God. He was honest and kind. He stayed away from anything wrong.

Job had a big, wonderful family. Seven sons and three daughters. They loved each other and got together for meals and celebrations all the time.

And Job was rich. He had seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred teams of oxen, and five hundred donkeys. He had lots of workers to take care of it all. He was the most successful man in the whole area.

Every morning, Job would wake up early and pray for his children. He wanted to make sure they were close to God. That's the kind of father he was.

Then came the worst day of Job's life.

A messenger ran in, out of breath. "The Sabeans attacked! They stole all your oxen and donkeys! They killed the workers! I'm the only one who got away!"

Before that man finished talking, another messenger came running. "Fire fell from the sky! It burned up all the sheep and the shepherds! I'm the only one left!"

Before that man finished, another came. "Raiders stole all the camels! They killed your servants! I'm the only one who escaped!"

And before that man finished — one more messenger. This one could barely speak. His voice shook.

"Your sons and daughters were eating together at your oldest son's house. A terrible wind came and knocked the house down. They're all gone. I'm the only one who made it out."

One message after another after another after another. Everything — the animals, the workers, and worst of all, his ten children — gone. All in one afternoon.

Job stood up. He tore his robe. He shaved his head. Those were ways people showed the deepest sadness in those days. Then he fell to the ground.

And he said something that people have been repeating for thousands of years:

"I came into this world with nothing. I will leave with nothing. The Lord gave, and the Lord has taken away. Blessed be the name of the Lord."

Then the room was quiet. Job knelt on the torn fabric of his robe, his head bare, his hands open on the ground.
