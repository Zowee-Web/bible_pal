The Farmer and the Seeds

One day, by the shore of a wide blue lake, such a great crowd gathered to hear Jesus that there was hardly room to stand. So Jesus climbed into a little fishing boat, and his friends pushed it out a short way onto the water. There Jesus sat down, rocking gently on the waves, where everyone on the shore could see him and hear him. And he taught them by telling stories. Here is one of the stories he told.

Once there was a farmer. And early one morning, when the air was still cool, he went out to his field to plant his crop. Over his shoulder he carried a big bag, heavy and full of seeds. He reached his hand inside, took out a great handful, and began to walk up and down the field, swinging his arm wide and scattering the seeds this way and that, letting them fly out all across the ground.

Now, as the farmer scattered, the seeds landed in all sorts of different places. He could not know, as he walked along, just where each seed would come down, or what would become of it. He simply swung his arm and let them fly, and then he would have to wait — through long days of sun and rain — to see what would grow. And what happened to each little seed depended a great deal on where it landed.

Some of the seeds fell on the hard, beaten path that ran along the edge of the field. So many feet had walked there that the dirt was packed flat and hard as a floor. The seeds could not sink in at all. They just lay there on top, out in the open. And it was not long before the birds spotted them, and came swooping down, and gobbled up every last one.

Some of the other seeds fell on rocky ground, where only a thin layer of dirt lay over a shelf of hard stone. And oh, how quickly those seeds sprouted! Up they shot, fast and green and eager, faster than all the rest. But down below, where no one could see, their little roots pushed down and hit solid rock, and could go no deeper. They had no place to dig in, and no way to reach the cool water far below. So when the sun climbed high and beat down hot upon the field, those eager little plants had nothing to drink. They wilted, and they withered, and soon they dried right up.

Still other seeds fell among the weeds and the prickly thorns. The little plants did sprout, and they did try their very best to grow up toward the light. But the weeds had gotten there first, and the weeds were faster and stronger and greedier. They grew up thick and tall and tangled, crowding in on every side, stealing all the room and all the sunlight, until the poor little plants were so squeezed and shaded that they could not grow at all.

But then — ah, but then — some of the seeds fell on good soil. Soft, dark, rich soil, just right for growing. And what a wonderful difference that made!

Those seeds sank gently down into the warm brown earth. They pushed their roots down deep, where there was plenty of room to spread, and plenty of cool water to drink. They lifted up little green shoots toward the sun. And they grew, and they grew, and they grew — up green and strong and tall, swaying in the wind.

And when at last the harvest time came, those plants were heavy with grain. So much grain! Far, far more than the farmer had ever scattered in the first place. There was enough to fill his hands, and fill his baskets, and fill his barns to the brim, until they very nearly overflowed. The farmer must have laughed out loud for joy to see it, and gathered it all in with a glad and thankful heart. All of that — from a few small seeds that found the good, soft ground.

When Jesus had finished telling the story, he looked out at all the people sitting quietly on the shore, listening. And he smiled.

"A heart that listens," he said gently, "is like that good, soft soil. When God's love comes and settles there, it can grow down deep, and grow up strong and tall, and bring more good and wonderful things than you could ever dream."
