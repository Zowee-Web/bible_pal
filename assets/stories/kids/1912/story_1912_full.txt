The Farmer and the Seeds

So many people crowded along the shore to hear Jesus that he climbed into a little fishing boat, pushed out a little way onto the lake, and sat down. And from there, rocking gently on the water, he told the whole crowd a story. It went like this.

Once there was a farmer, and one morning he went out to his field to plant. He had a big bag of seeds slung over his shoulder. He reached in, took out a great handful, and began to scatter the seeds this way and that, swinging his arm wide as he walked, letting them fly all across the ground.

Now, some of the seeds fell on the hard, beaten path that ran along the edge of the field, where the dirt was packed down flat and tight from many feet walking on it. Those seeds just sat there on top, with nowhere to go. And before long, the birds came swooping down and gobbled every one of them up.

Some of the other seeds fell on rocky ground, where there was only a thin little layer of dirt over the stones. Oh, those seeds sprang up quickly — fast and green and eager! But down below, their tiny roots hit hard rock and had nowhere to grow. So when the hot sun climbed high and beat down, the little plants had no deep roots to drink with, and they dried right up and withered away.

Still other seeds fell among the prickly weeds and thorns. The little plants did try their best to grow. But the weeds were faster and stronger, and they grew up thick and tall all around the little plants and crowded them out, until there was no room and no light left for them at all.

But then — ah — some of the seeds fell on good soil. Soft, dark, rich soil, just right for growing things. And what a difference that made!

Those seeds sank gently down into the warm brown earth, and there they found everything they needed. They pushed their roots down deep, where there was plenty of room to spread out and plenty of cool water to drink. They lifted up little green shoots toward the sky. They drank the rain. They soaked up the warm sunshine. And day after day, all on their own, they grew — up green, and strong, and tall, swaying happily in the wind.

And when at last the harvest time came, those plants were heavy and golden with grain. So much grain! Far, far more than the farmer had ever scattered in the first place. There was enough to fill his hands, and fill his baskets, and fill his barns to the very top, until they very nearly overflowed with good golden grain. How the farmer must have laughed for joy to see it.

When Jesus finished the story, he smiled at all the people listening. "A heart that listens," he said, "is like that good, soft soil. Wonderful things can grow there."
