The Farmer and the Seeds

So many people crowded around to hear Jesus that he climbed into a little boat, pushed out a little way onto the lake, and from there he told them all a story. It went like this.

A farmer went out to his field to plant. He reached into his bag, took a big handful of seeds, and scattered them this way and that, all across the ground.

Some of the seeds fell on the hard path, where the dirt was packed down tight. Those seeds just sat there on top — and the birds swooped down and gobbled them up.

Some of the seeds fell on rocky ground, where there was hardly any dirt at all. They sprouted up fast and green! But their little roots had nowhere to go, and when the hot sun rose high, they dried right up.

Some of the seeds fell among prickly weeds and thorns. The little plants did try to grow, but the weeds grew up thick all around them and crowded them right out.

But some of the seeds — ah, some of the seeds fell on good, soft soil. And those seeds pushed their roots down deep, and grew up green and strong and tall, and made grain — so much grain! Far more than the farmer had ever planted. Enough to fill his hands, and his baskets, and his barns.

When Jesus finished the story, he smiled. "A heart that listens," he said, "is like that good, soft soil. Wonderful things can grow there."
