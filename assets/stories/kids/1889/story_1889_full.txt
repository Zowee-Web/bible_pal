The Woman at the Well

It was the middle of a hot, sunny day, and Jesus was tired. He had been walking a long way, and now he stopped to rest beside an old, deep well, out in the countryside. His friends had gone off to a nearby town to buy some food, so Jesus sat there by the well, all by himself, thirsty in the heat.

After a little while, a woman came walking up to the well, carrying an empty jar to fill with water. Now, most people came to the well in the cool of the morning or the evening, all together, chatting as they drew their water. But this woman came alone, in the hottest part of the day, when no one else was there. For she was the kind of person that others looked down on, and whispered about, and left out. So she came when she could be by herself.

But Jesus did not look down on her, and he did not whisper, and he did not leave her out. Instead, he looked right at her, with kind and gentle eyes. "Would you give me a drink of water?" he asked.

The woman was so surprised she hardly knew what to say. A man like this, speaking to a woman like her? It was not the sort of thing that ever happened. "Why are you even talking to me?" she asked.

And so Jesus began to talk with her — kindly, and patiently, as though she were the most important person in the world. He told her about a wonderful gift he had to give. He called it living water. "Anyone who drinks ordinary water," Jesus said, "will get thirsty again. But the water I give is different. It is like a spring of gladness, deep inside your heart — and it never, ever runs dry."

As they talked on, the woman began to understand something that filled her with wonder. This man knew her. He knew all about her life — all the things she had done, even the things she felt ashamed of. And yet — and this was the most amazing part of all — he was not unkind to her, not even a little. He looked at her with nothing but love.

She had spent so very long being passed by, and left out, and looked down upon. And now, here was someone who saw her — really, truly saw her — and cared. She had never felt anything like it in all her life.

The woman was so overjoyed that she left her water jar sitting right there by the well, and she ran — all the way back to her town. "Come!" she called out to everyone she met. "Come and see! There is a man who told me everything I ever did — and he was kind to me! Could he be the one God promised to send?" And the people put down what they were doing, and came hurrying out to meet Jesus for themselves.

So remember this, little one: there is no one — no one at all — that Jesus passes by. He sees you. He sees every part of you, and he loves you, completely. To him, you are never, ever invisible.
