The Jar That Never Ran Empty

For a long, long time, no rain had fallen on the land. The streams dried up, the crops would not grow, and food became very, very hard to find. People everywhere were hungry.

Now, God told the prophet Elijah to travel to a little town by the sea, where a poor widow lived with her young son. So Elijah set off, tired and hungry from his long journey.

When he reached the town gate, he saw a woman there, gathering a few small sticks to make a fire. It was the very widow God had spoken of. Elijah called to her kindly. "Excuse me," he said. "Could I trouble you for a drink of water? And — could you please make me a little bread to eat? I have come a very long way."

At that, the woman's eyes filled up with tears. "Oh, sir," she said softly, "I wish that I could. But I have almost nothing left in all the world. There is just a small handful of flour in the bottom of my jar, and a few last drops of oil in my jug. That is all. I am gathering these little sticks right now to make one final meal — just a small one — for my son and me. And after we have eaten it... there will be nothing left at all."

Elijah's heart was kind toward her. He did not want to take her last food away. Instead, he brought her a gentle promise — a promise straight from God.

"Do not be afraid," Elijah said. "Go home, and make your meal, just as you planned. But first, would you make one small loaf for me, and bring it out? For this is what God has promised you: your jar of flour will not run empty, and your jug of oil will not run dry — not until the day God sends the rain again."

Now, that was a great deal to ask of a woman with so little. But something in her heart decided to trust God. So she went home. And she took her last little bit of flour, and her last few drops of oil, and she made a small loaf — and she shared it with Elijah first of all.

And then the wonderful thing happened. When the woman went back to her flour jar — there was still flour inside it! And when she tipped her jug — there was still oil! She made another loaf, and then another, and still the jar was not empty, and still the oil did not run dry. However much she poured out, there was always enough left for the next meal, and the next day, and the next.

And so it went, day after day after day. There was always food — enough for the woman, and for her dear son, and for Elijah too. Through all the long, dry time, that little jar and that little jug never, ever ran empty.

The woman had trusted God, and shared the very last of what she had. And God had seen it, and God had cared for her, and made sure she always had just enough.

So when you share what you have, little one — even when it does not feel like very much at all — you can trust God with it. He always sees what you give. And he loves, more than anything, to fill an empty jar right back up again.
