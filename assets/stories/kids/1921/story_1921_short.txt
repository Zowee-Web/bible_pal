The Queen Who Came to See

Far, far away, in a land called Sheba, a queen sat in her palace and listened to travelers' stories. They told her about a king named Solomon, who lived in a land beyond the deserts. God had made him the wisest man in all the world, they said. He knew the answer to every hard question. His palace was beautiful, and his people were glad.

The stories sounded almost too good to be true. And the more the queen heard, the more one thought grew inside her, bright and certain: "I must go and see for myself."

So that is exactly what she did.

Picture it. A great caravan of camels, stepping out across the wide, golden desert — camel after camel after camel, swaying along under the hot sun. Some carried sweet-smelling spices. Some carried shining gold. And in the middle rode the queen, looking far ahead toward the land of King Solomon, coming such a very long way just to see if it was really true.

Day after day they traveled, over the sand and the faraway roads. And at last, the long journey ended. The queen of Sheba had arrived.

Now she could ask her questions — and oh, she had brought so many! Every riddle she could think of. Every puzzle. Every hard, tricky question she had been saving up for years. One by one, she asked them all.

And one by one, Solomon answered them. Every single one. Nothing was too hard for him. Nothing at all.

The queen walked through his beautiful palace. She saw how wisely he ruled, and how happy his people were. And she stood very still, and her heart filled up to the brim with wonder.

"It is true," she said softly. "It is all true. And the half was not even told to me! It is far more wonderful than anything I heard."

Then the queen of Sheba praised God, who had made Solomon so wise. She had come such a very long way to see if the stories were true. And she discovered that God's wisdom and goodness were even greater and more wonderful than all the stories she had ever known.
