Jesus and the Children

It had been a long and busy day. Jesus had been teaching crowds of people from morning until evening, and his friends, the disciples, were tired and ready to rest.

Then, up the road, came some mothers and fathers, and they were bringing their children to Jesus. Little ones toddled along holding tight to their hands. Babies bounced on their shoulders. Older children skipped on ahead. They had all come for one reason: they wanted Jesus to hold their children, and to place his hands on them, and to bless them.

But the disciples stepped in front of them to send them away. "Not now," they said. "Can't you see the Teacher is tired? Jesus is far too busy for little children today. Come back another time."

The children's faces fell.

But when Jesus saw what was happening, he stopped his friends at once. "No," he said firmly. "Let the children come to me. Do not ever turn them away." And he opened his arms wide.

The children did not need to be asked twice. They ran to him! Some climbed right up into his lap. Little hands reached for his face, and little eyes looked up into his. Jesus laughed out loud with the joy of it. He listened to their chattering stories. One little girl showed him a smooth stone she had found, and he turned it over in his hand and told her it was beautiful. A small boy grew so comfortable that he fell asleep against Jesus's arm, and Jesus held him a little more carefully so he would not wake.

He noticed the shy ones, too, peeking out from behind their mothers, and he smiled at them so warmly that soon they crept close as well. One by one, he gathered them up, placed his hands gently on their heads, and blessed each one. "God's kingdom belongs to little children," Jesus said.

The grown-ups stood quietly, watching. They had been so sure that Jesus was too important and too busy for the little ones. But they had been wrong. Jesus was never, ever too busy for a child.

He stayed with them as the afternoon stretched long and the sun sank low and golden. And when at last it was time for everyone to go home, each child went away knowing they had sat right beside Jesus — and that Jesus had been glad, so very glad, that they came.
