The First Garden

When the world was brand new, God planted a beautiful garden, and it was the loveliest place that has ever been.

In the garden grew tall green trees, heavy with sweet, ripe fruit. Cool rivers wound through it, sparkling in the sunshine. There were soft mossy places to rest, and bright flowers of every color, and the air was full of the songs of birds.

And into this beautiful garden, God placed the very first man. God loved him, and gave him a happy and important job: to take care of the garden, and to look after every living thing in it.

Then God did something wonderful. One by one, God brought all the animals to the man, so that he could give each one a name.

The big, strong animal with the golden mane padded up, and the man said, "I will call you... lion." A tall creature with a very long neck came nibbling the treetops, and the man smiled and said, "And you shall be... giraffe." A tiny, buzzing thing zipped past his ear, quick as a blink, and he laughed and said, "Hummingbird!"

On and on came the animals — the big ones and the small ones, the ones that galloped and the ones that hopped and the ones that swam — and the man gave every single one its very own name.

And when the day was done, God looked at the man, and the animals, and the whole beautiful garden, and God was very pleased. For everything God had made was good, and lovely, and altogether beautiful.
