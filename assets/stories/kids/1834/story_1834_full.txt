The First Garden

When the world was still brand new, God planted a beautiful garden, and it was the loveliest place that has ever been, anywhere.

Oh, what a garden it was! Tall green trees grew there, hanging heavy with sweet, ripe fruit you could simply reach up and pick. Cool, clear rivers wound their way between them, sparkling and gurgling in the warm sunshine. There were soft, mossy banks just right for resting on, and bright flowers of every color you can imagine, red and gold and purple and blue. And everywhere, all through the leaves, the air was filled with the happy songs of birds.

Into this beautiful garden, God placed the very first man. God loved him dearly. And God gave him a happy and important job to do: to take care of the garden, and to watch over every living thing that lived there.

Then God did something truly wonderful. One by one, God began to bring all the animals of the world to the man, so that the man could look at each one and give it a name of its very own.

First came a big, strong animal with a shaggy golden mane and a mighty roar. The man looked at him and said, "I will call you... lion." Then came a tall, gentle creature with a neck so long it could nibble leaves from the very tops of the trees. "And you," said the man with a smile, "shall be a giraffe." A tiny, shimmering thing came zipping past his ear, so fast it was almost a blur. The man laughed out loud. "Hummingbird!" he said.

And still they came, more and more of them. Great lumbering elephants and tiny scurrying mice. Animals that galloped, and animals that hopped, and animals that slithered, and animals that splashed in the rivers. Stripy ones and spotty ones and woolly ones and smooth ones. And to each and every one, the man gave its own special name.

It took a long, long while, for there were ever so many animals. But the man did not mind one bit. It was the best and happiest job in all the world.

And when at last the day was done, and the sun was setting golden over the garden, God looked at everything — at the man, and the animals, and the rivers, and the trees, and the flowers, and the whole beautiful, peaceful garden. And God was very, very pleased. For everything God had made was good, and lovely, and altogether beautiful.
