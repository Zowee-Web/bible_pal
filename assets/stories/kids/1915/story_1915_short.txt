The God Who Sees Me

Far out in the dry desert, a mother named Hagar walked along with her little boy. The sun was hot. The sand stretched on and on. And the water in their bottle was almost gone.

Hagar and her son were all alone out there. No home. No friends nearby. No one, it seemed, who even knew where they were. And when the very last drop of water was gone, Hagar's heart filled up with sorrow. She was so afraid for her boy. Gently she set him down in the little shade of a bush, and she went and sat down a small way off, and she began to cry. She had never felt so alone in all her life.

But she was not alone. Someone saw her. Someone heard.

God heard the little boy. And God called out to Hagar, softly and kindly, from heaven. "Hagar, do not be afraid," he said. "I have heard your son. Get up, and lift him up, and hold him close. I am going to take care of you both."

And then God opened Hagar's eyes — and there, close by, right where she had not seen it before, was a well of cool, clear water.

Hagar could hardly believe it. She hurried over and filled their bottle right to the top, and gave her thirsty boy a long, wonderful drink. They were going to be all right after all.

Hagar had thought there was nothing out there but hot, dry sand. But God had seen her the whole time — and a well of cool water had been waiting close by, all along.

Sometimes, when all we can see is the dry, dry sand, God helps us see the well that was there all along.
