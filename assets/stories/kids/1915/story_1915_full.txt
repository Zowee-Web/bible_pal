The God Who Sees Me

Far out in the dry desert, a mother named Hagar walked slowly along, with her little boy beside her. The sun was hot overhead. The sand stretched on and on in every direction, with no end in sight. And the water in their leather bottle was almost gone.

Hagar and her son were all alone out there. They had no home to go to. They had no friends nearby. There was no one, it seemed, in all the wide world who even knew where they were, or that they were in trouble.

They walked, and they walked. And at last, the very last drop of water was gone.

Hagar's heart filled up with sorrow. She was so afraid for her boy out in that hot, empty place. Very gently, she helped him into the little patch of shade beneath a desert bush. Then she went and sat down a small way off, because she could not bear to watch him hurting. And there, all alone, Hagar put her face in her hands and began to cry. She had never in her whole life felt so alone.

But Hagar was not as alone as she felt. Someone saw her. Someone heard.

God heard the little boy where he lay. And God called out to Hagar — softly, and kindly, from heaven. "Hagar," he said, "what is the matter? Do not be afraid. I have heard your son crying, right where he is. Get up now. Go to him, and lift him up, and hold him close by the hand. For I am going to take care of you both, and I am going to make your boy into a great people one day."

And then God opened Hagar's eyes. And there — close by, right nearby, where somehow she had not seen it before — was a well of cool, clear water. How had she walked right past it? She did not know. But there it was, sparkling in the sunlight, right when she needed it most of all.

Oh, Hagar could hardly believe her eyes! She jumped up and hurried over to the well. She filled their empty bottle all the way to the top. And she brought it to her thirsty boy and gave him a long, long, wonderful drink. And then she drank too. They were going to be all right after all.

And God stayed close to that little boy as the years went by. He was with him as he grew up, big and strong and happy, out in the wide desert lands. God never left the two of them — not once, and not ever.

Hagar had truly believed there was nothing left out there but hot, dry sand and sorrow. But God had seen her the whole time. He had heard every single tear that fell. And a well of cool, clear water had been waiting close by, all along.

Sometimes, when all we can see is the dry, dry sand, God helps us see the well that was there all along.
