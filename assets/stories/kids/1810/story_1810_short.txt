Baby Moses

By the edge of a great river, a mother held her baby boy close. She loved him more than anything in the world. But it was a dangerous time for baby boys in the land of Egypt, and she wanted, more than anything, to keep him safe.

So she made a plan. She wove a little basket out of reeds and covered it well so it would float like a tiny boat, dry and snug inside. She kissed her baby, laid him gently in, and set the basket among the tall reeds at the river's edge. And she asked God to watch over him.

The baby's big sister, Miriam, hid nearby to see what would happen.

Soon a princess — the daughter of the king himself — came down to the river. She saw the little basket floating in the reeds and sent her servant to fetch it. When she opened it, there was the baby, looking up at her. And he began to cry.

The princess looked into his little face and knew she wanted to help him. "This is one of the Hebrew babies," she said softly. "I will keep him as my own."

Then brave Miriam stepped forward. "Shall I find someone to help care for the baby?" she asked. And the princess said yes.

So Miriam ran and brought back the baby's very own mother! The princess did not know — and the mother got to hold her own son again, and care for him, safe and sound.

The princess named the baby Moses. And though no one could see it, God had been watching over that little basket the whole time.
