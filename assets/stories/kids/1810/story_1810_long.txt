Baby Moses

Long ago, in the land of Egypt, there lived a mother who had a brand-new baby boy. He had ten tiny fingers and ten tiny toes, and when he wrapped his little hand around her finger, her heart melted with love. She loved him more than anything in all the world.

But it was a frightening time to have a baby boy in Egypt. The king, who was called Pharaoh, had grown afraid of God's people, because there were so many of them. And he had made a cruel rule that put the baby boys in danger. So this mother had only one wish, by day and by night: to keep her precious little son safe.

For three whole months, she kept him hidden away at home. But as he grew bigger, he grew louder too, and it became harder and harder to keep him secret. So the mother thought, and she prayed, and at last she made a plan — a brave and clever plan.

Down by the great river, tall green reeds grew along the water's edge. The mother gathered armfuls of them, and she wove them together, round and round, into a little basket — just the right size for a baby. Then she coated it all over with sticky tar, so that not one drop of water could get inside, and so that it would float upon the water like a tiny boat.

When the basket was ready, she lined it soft and snug. She kissed her baby's warm little head. And gently, oh so gently, she laid him down inside. Then she carried the little basket down to the river, and she set it ever so carefully among the tall reeds, where the water was calm and still. And she whispered a prayer, asking God to watch over her little boy. And then — because she had to — she turned and walked away, though it was the very hardest thing she had ever done.

But the baby was not left all alone. For his big sister, whose name was Miriam, had crept down to the river too. She tucked herself behind the tall green reeds, close by, where no one could see her. And there she waited, and watched over that little basket with all her might, to see what would happen to her baby brother.

Miriam did not have to wait very long. For soon, down to the river came a grand and beautiful lady — none other than the princess of Egypt, the daughter of Pharaoh himself! She had come to bathe in the cool water, and her servants walked all around her.

As the princess looked out across the river, she noticed the little basket, bobbing gently among the reeds. "What is that, out there?" she said. "Go and bring it to me." So one of her servants waded out into the water, and carried the basket carefully back to the princess.

The princess lifted the lid and peeked inside. And there, looking right up at her, was the baby. And just then, he scrunched up his little face, and he began to cry.

Well — the princess's heart melted at once. "Oh, you poor little thing," she said softly, and she lifted him up into her arms. "You must be one of the Hebrew babies." And though she knew it, she did not mind one bit. "I will keep him," she decided, "and care for him, and raise him as my very own son."

Now this was the moment that brave Miriam had been waiting for! She stepped out from behind the reeds, her heart pounding, and she spoke up. "Excuse me," she said, as politely as she could. "Would you like me to go and find one of the Hebrew women, to help nurse the baby for you?"

"Why, yes," said the princess, pleased. "Please do."

So Miriam ran — oh, how she ran! — all the way home. And who do you think she brought back to the princess? The baby's very own mother!

The princess did not know it was the baby's mother, of course. "Take this child," she said, "and care for him for me. And I will see that you are paid for it." And so — can you even imagine the joy? — the mother was given back her very own dear son, to hold, and to feed, and to love, and to keep safe and sound.

The princess named the baby Moses, and he grew up safe and cared for. But here is the most wonderful thing of all. Through every single part of that story — the basket, and the river, and the princess, and the sister waiting in the reeds — God had been watching over that little baby the whole time. Not for one moment had he taken his loving eyes away.

And one day, when Moses was all grown up, God would use him to do something greater than anyone could ever have dreamed. But that is a story for another time.
