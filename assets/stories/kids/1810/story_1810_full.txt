Baby Moses

By the edge of a great river, a mother held her baby boy close and rocked him gently. She loved him more than anything in all the world. But it was a dangerous time for baby boys in the land of Egypt, and there was nothing this mother wanted more than to keep her little son safe.

So she thought, and she prayed, and she made a plan. She gathered tall reeds from the riverbank and wove them into a little basket. She covered the basket all over, so that not a drop of water could get in, and so it would float like a tiny boat. Then she kissed her baby's soft head, laid him gently inside, and carried the basket down to the river. She set it among the tall green reeds at the water's edge — and she asked God to watch over her little boy.

The baby's big sister, Miriam, did not go far. She hid herself nearby, behind the reeds, to watch and see what would happen to her baby brother.

She did not have to wait long. Soon, down to the river came a princess — the daughter of the king of Egypt himself — with all her servants walking beside her. As the princess strolled along the water, she saw the little basket bobbing among the reeds. "What is that?" she said, and she sent her servant wading out to bring it to her.

The princess lifted the lid. And there, inside, was the baby, looking up at her. And he began to cry.

The princess looked into his little face and knew she wanted to help him. "This is one of the Hebrew babies," she said softly. "I will keep him, and care for him, as my very own son."

That was the moment brave Miriam had been waiting for. She stepped out from her hiding place. "Shall I go and find someone to help care for the baby for you?" she asked.

"Yes," said the princess. "Please do."

So Miriam ran as fast as she could — and brought back the baby's very own mother! The princess never knew. And so the mother was given back her own dear son, to hold him and feed him and care for him, safe and sound.

The princess named the baby Moses. And though not one person there could see it, God had been watching over that little basket, and that little baby, the whole time.
