The Holy Spirit Comes

After Jesus rose from the dead, alive again, he stayed with his friends for a little while. And then, one day, he went back up to heaven, to be with his Father. But before he went, Jesus had given his friends a wonderful promise.

"Do not go away just yet," he had told them. "Wait here in the city of Jerusalem. For I am going to send you a special helper — the Holy Spirit. He will come to be with you always. He will comfort you, and he will fill you with courage."

So Jesus' friends did exactly as he had said. They stayed together in Jerusalem, all of them, in one house. And while they waited, they prayed. Day after day they prayed together, and they wondered: when would the helper come? And what would it be like when he did? They did not have to wonder for very long.

Then, one morning, it happened.

All at once, out of nowhere, came a sound from heaven. It was like a great, rushing wind — a mighty roaring, like the strongest gust of wind you have ever heard. And it filled the whole house, from the floor right up to the ceiling, even though not a single window was open. The friends looked up in astonishment.

And then they saw something even more wonderful. Little flames of fire appeared — soft, and bright, and warm — and they came to rest, ever so gently, one upon each person in the room. One small, glowing flame for every single friend. But the fire did not hurt anyone, and it did not burn. Instead, it filled each of them, right down to their toes, with a great rush of joy, and gladness, and courage.

The Holy Spirit had come — just exactly as Jesus had promised!

And then the most surprising thing of all happened. Jesus' friends opened their mouths to speak — and out came languages they had never even learned! Words and words, in all sorts of different tongues, flowing out as easily as their very own.

Now, that very day, the city of Jerusalem was packed full of visitors. People had traveled there from many faraway lands, near and far, and each of them spoke a different language of their own. When they heard that strange and marvelous sound, a great crowd came hurrying to see what could possibly be happening.

And then they all stopped, amazed. For every single person in that crowd — no matter what country they had come from — heard Jesus' friends telling about the wonderful things God had done, each one in his very own language, the language he had grown up speaking at home!

"How can this be?" the people asked one another, their eyes wide with wonder. "These are just ordinary people. So how is it that we each hear them speaking our own language, the language from our own faraway home?"

Then one of Jesus' friends, a man named Peter, stood up tall in front of the whole enormous crowd.

Now, this was the very same Peter who, not so long before, had been so frightened that he would not even admit that he knew Jesus. But Peter was not afraid anymore. The Holy Spirit had filled him with courage, right up to the very brim. And so he lifted up his voice, and he spoke to all those thousands of people, with joy and with boldness. He told them the wonderful good news: that Jesus, who had died, was alive again forevermore — and that Jesus loved each and every one of them.

Peter spoke so beautifully, and with such love, that the people's hearts were opened wide. And that very day, thousands and thousands of people came to believe in Jesus, and to become his friends.

And do you know what that day was? It was the day the very first church was born. Not a church made of stones, and wood, and windows — but a church made of people! A great, big, happy family, from every land and every language, all loving God, and all belonging to one another.

And the very best part of all was this. God's own Spirit had come to live right inside them, just as Jesus had promised. He would comfort them whenever they were sad, and help them whenever things were hard, and fill them with courage whenever they were afraid. And he would never, ever leave them on their own again — not for a single moment.

And that very same Holy Spirit is still with God's people today — comforting them, and helping them, and giving them courage — even now, even you.
