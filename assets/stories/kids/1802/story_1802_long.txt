Jonah and the Big Fish

In a land beside the sea, there lived a man named Jonah. One quiet day, God spoke to him.

"Jonah," said God, "go to the great city of Nineveh. The people there have forgotten me and are doing wrong. Go and tell them to turn back to me."

But Jonah did not want to go. Nineveh was far, far away. And deep down in his heart, Jonah did not think those people deserved another chance. So Jonah made up his mind to do something he never should have tried. He decided to run away from God.

He hurried down to the harbor, where the boats rocked on the water and the sailors loaded their ships. He found a ship sailing far in the other direction, away from Nineveh. He paid for his place, climbed aboard, and went all the way down into the bottom of the ship. There, deep inside, rocked by the gentle waves, Jonah curled up and fell fast asleep.

But you cannot sail away from God.

While Jonah slept, God sent a great wind sweeping across the sea. The sky went dark as night. The waves rose up like rolling hills, lifting the little ship high and dropping it low, high and low. Rain lashed the deck. The sailors had never, in all their years, seen a storm so wild. They held on tight and cried out, sure the ship would break to pieces.

Then someone remembered the passenger asleep below. They shook him awake. "How can you sleep?" they shouted over the roaring wind. "Get up! Pray to your God to save us!"

Jonah climbed up onto the heaving deck, and he looked out at the wild, dark storm. And he knew. "This storm is my fault," he said quietly. "I ran away from God. If you lift me up and put me into the sea, the water will grow calm again."

The sailors did not want to do it — they were kind men, and they did not want to harm anyone. So they grabbed the oars and rowed with all their might, trying to reach the shore. But the storm only grew wilder and stronger. At last, there was nothing else they could do. Gently, carefully, they lowered Jonah over the side of the ship and into the waves.

And the very moment Jonah touched the water — the sea went calm. The wind stopped. The waves lay down flat. The dark clouds melted away, and the sky grew quiet and still.

Down, down, down Jonah sank, into the deep, dark, cold water — down where it was quiet, and far from the light.

But God had not forgotten Jonah. Not for one single moment.

For God had a surprise waiting in the deep. He sent an enormous fish swimming through the sea — a fish bigger than the ship itself — and it opened its great wide mouth and swallowed Jonah up, all in one gulp.

Inside the fish, everything was dark. Jonah opened his eyes, and he could not see a single thing. But slowly, as he sat there in the dark, he began to understand something wonderful. He was safe. It was quiet inside the fish, and it was warm, and Jonah was not alone. God was right there with him — even here, in the middle of the sea, in the deep, in the dark, inside a fish.

So Jonah began to pray. "Thank you, God, for saving me," he said. "I am sorry I ran away. I should have trusted you all along." For three whole days and three whole nights, Jonah prayed there in the deep. And God heard every single word.

Then God spoke to the fish. And the great fish swam up, and up, and up — all the way to the shore — and it gently laid Jonah down on the warm, dry sand.

Jonah blinked in the bright sunshine. He was free. And God spoke to him once more, as kind as the very first time. "Jonah," said God, "go to Nineveh."

And this time, Jonah stood up, brushed the sand from his clothes, and went.

It was a long, long road, but Jonah walked every step of it. When at last he reached the great city of Nineveh, he called out the message God had given him. "Turn back to God!" he said. "God is giving you another chance!"

And do you know what? The people of Nineveh listened. From the tallest grown-up to the smallest child, all the way up to the king himself, every one of them was sorry for the wrong they had done. And God, who is full of love, forgave them — every single one.

Afterward, Jonah sat down in the sunshine outside the city, and he thought about everything that had happened. God had been with him in the storm. God had been with him in the deep, dark water. God had been with him inside the fish. God had given Jonah another chance — and then God had given a whole city another chance, too.

And there in the warm sunshine, Jonah understood something he would carry in his heart forever. God's love was bigger than the wild storm. It was bigger than the deep blue sea. It was bigger than the great big fish. It was big enough for a whole city full of people — and it was even big enough to chase after one man who had tried to run away, and to bring him gently home.
