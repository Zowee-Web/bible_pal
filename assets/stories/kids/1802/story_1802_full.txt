Jonah and the Big Fish

A long time ago, there was a man named Jonah. One day, God spoke to him. "Go to the great city of Nineveh," God said. "The people there have forgotten me. Go and tell them to turn back."

But Jonah did not want to go. Nineveh was far away, and deep down, Jonah did not think those people deserved another chance. So Jonah decided to run.

He hurried down to the sea, where the ships came and went. He found one sailing far in the other direction, paid his way aboard, and climbed down into the bottom of the ship. There, rocked by the waves, he fell fast asleep.

But you cannot sail away from God.

While Jonah slept, a great wind came sweeping across the water. The sky turned dark. The waves rose up like hills, and the little ship climbed and dropped, climbed and dropped. The sailors had never seen a storm so wild. They called out, afraid the ship would break apart.

Down below, they found Jonah sleeping. "How can you sleep?" they cried. "Wake up! Pray to your God!"

Jonah looked at the storm, and he knew. "This is my fault," he said quietly. "I ran away from God. Lift me up and put me into the water, and it will grow calm again."

The sailors did not want to do it. They were kind men, and they tried to row to shore. But the storm only grew stronger. At last, gently, they lowered Jonah over the side and into the waves. And the very moment he touched the water, the sea went calm. The wind stopped. The sky grew quiet and still.

Down, down Jonah sank into the deep, dark water. But God had not forgotten him. Not for a single moment. God sent a great big fish swimming through the sea, and the fish opened its wide mouth and swallowed Jonah whole.

Inside the fish, it was dark. Jonah opened his eyes. He could not see a thing — but slowly he understood that he was safe. It was quiet there, and it was warm, and Jonah was not alone. God was with him, even here, in the middle of the sea, in the dark, inside a fish.

So Jonah began to pray. "Thank you, God, for rescuing me. I should have trusted you all along." For three whole days and nights, Jonah prayed in the deep. And God heard every word.

Then the great fish swam up through the water, all the way to the shore, and gently set Jonah down on the warm, dry sand.

God spoke to Jonah once more. "Go to Nineveh," God said. And this time, Jonah got up, brushed off the sand, and went.

It was a long road, but Jonah walked it. When he reached the great city, he told the people what God had said: "Turn back. God is giving you another chance." And the people of Nineveh listened. From the smallest child to the king himself, every one of them was sorry. And God, who is full of love, forgave them all.

Jonah sat down in the sunshine outside the city, and he thought about everything that had happened. God had been with him in the storm. God had been with him in the deep, dark water. God had been with him inside the fish. God had given Jonah another chance — and then God had given the whole city another chance, too.

And Jonah understood something he would never forget. God's love was bigger than the sea. It was bigger than the great fish. It was big enough for a whole city of people — and it was even big enough to find one man who had tried to run away.
