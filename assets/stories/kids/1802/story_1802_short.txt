Jonah and the Big Fish

A long time ago, God spoke to a man named Jonah. "Go to the big city of Nineveh," God said. "Tell the people there to turn back to me."

But Jonah did not want to go. So he did something he thought would work — he ran the other way. He found a ship sailing far across the sea, climbed aboard, and went down below to sleep.

But you cannot sail away from God.

A great wind came rushing over the water. The waves grew tall, and the little ship rocked and groaned. The sailors were frightened. They woke Jonah and asked what they should do.

Jonah knew. "I ran away from God," he said gently. "Lift me up and put me into the sea, and it will grow calm again." The sailors did not want to. But at last they lowered Jonah into the waves — and at once the sea grew still and quiet.

Down, down Jonah sank into the deep. But God had not forgotten him. God sent a great big fish, and the fish opened its mouth and swallowed Jonah whole.

Inside the fish, it was dark. Jonah opened his eyes. It was quiet, and it was warm, and Jonah was not alone — God was right there with him. For three days Jonah prayed. "Thank you, God, for rescuing me. I should have trusted you all along."

Then the great fish swam to the shore and gently set Jonah down on the dry sand.

God spoke to Jonah again. "Go to Nineveh," God said. And this time, Jonah went.

When Jonah told the people of Nineveh to turn back to God, they listened. Every one of them was sorry. And God, who is full of love, forgave them all.

Jonah sat in the sunshine and thought about it. God had been with him in the storm. God had been with him in the deep. God had given him another chance — and God's love was big enough for the whole city, too.

It was bigger than the sea. It was bigger than the great fish. It was even big enough to find a man who tried to run away.
