Jesus Washes Their Feet

One evening, Jesus and his friends were sharing supper together in a quiet upstairs room. Now, in those days, everyone walked from place to place in sandals, along dusty, dirty roads. So by the end of the day, people's feet were very dusty indeed. Usually, the lowest servant in the house would kneel down and wash the dust from each person's feet.

But that night, there was no servant in the room. And not one of the friends offered to do it — for who would want such a lowly, humble job?

Then Jesus stood up from the table. He took off his outer robe, and wrapped a towel around his waist. He poured water into a basin. And then Jesus — their teacher, their leader, the most important one in the whole room — got down on his knees, and began to wash his friends' dusty feet, one by one, gently and carefully.

When it was Peter's turn, he pulled his feet back, surprised. "Lord!" he said. "You should never wash my feet! You are far too important for that!" But Jesus looked up at him kindly. "Let me," he said softly. "This is how much I love you." And so Peter let him.

When Jesus had finished, he put his robe back on and sat down again. "Do you understand what I have done?" he asked. "I am your teacher — and yet I knelt down and washed your feet. Love does not wait to be served. Love gets down low, and helps."

And his friends never, ever forgot the sight of it: the greatest one of all, kneeling on the floor, washing their feet, because he loved them.
