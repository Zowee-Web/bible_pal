Jesus Washes Their Feet

One evening, Jesus and his closest friends gathered to share a supper together, in a quiet room upstairs. Soft lamplight glowed on the table, and they were all together, the way they loved to be.

Now, in those days, there were no cars and no buses. People walked everywhere they went, in open sandals, along dusty, dirty roads. So by the time anyone arrived at a house, their feet were always tired, and always very dusty. And so it was the custom that when guests came in, the lowest, humblest servant of the house would kneel down by the door and gently wash the dust off each person's feet.

But on this evening, in this room, there was no servant at all. The friends looked at the basin of water and the towel sitting by the door. And not a single one of them offered to pick it up. For washing feet was the lowliest job there was — and each of them thought he was far too important for such a thing.

Then Jesus did something that surprised them all.

Quietly, Jesus stood up from the table. He took off his outer robe, and he wrapped a towel around his waist, just like a servant would. He poured water into the basin. And then Jesus — their teacher, their master, the greatest and most important one in the whole entire room — knelt down on the floor.

And one by one, he began to wash his friends' dusty, tired feet. Gently, and carefully, he washed each foot, and dried it with the towel around his waist. The room grew very quiet. No one could quite believe what they were seeing.

When it was Peter's turn, the surprised disciple quickly pulled his feet back. "Lord!" he said. "You must never wash my feet! You are our teacher — you are far too important for a job like this!"

But Jesus looked up at him, with eyes full of kindness. "Please, let me," he said softly. "This is how much I love you." And so, gently, Peter let him.

When Jesus had finished washing every friend's feet, he stood up, put his robe back on, and returned to his place at the table. And he asked them a question.

"Do you understand what I have just done for you?" he said. "You call me your Teacher, and your Lord — and that is right, because that is who I am. And yet, see — I knelt down and washed your feet. I did it so you would understand something, and never forget it. Love does not sit up high and wait to be served. Love gets down low. Love kneels. Love helps. That is what real love looks like."

And his friends never, ever forgot that evening — or the sight of it. The greatest one of all, the one they loved most, down on his knees on the floor, washing their dusty feet, one by one, simply because he loved them.
