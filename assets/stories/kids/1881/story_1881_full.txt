Gideon's Three Hundred

Long ago, there lived a man named Gideon. Gideon was not a soldier, and he did not feel brave at all. But God had a very big job for him. A huge enemy army had come marching into the land, to hurt God's people and to take everything they had. And God chose Gideon — quiet, ordinary Gideon — to lead his people to safety.

So Gideon called together an army of men to help him. But when he saw the enemy camped down in the valley, his heart sank. The enemy soldiers were everywhere, more than anyone could count, spread across the land like a great swarm of grasshoppers. Gideon's army seemed so very small beside them. He was afraid.

And then God said something that surprised Gideon very much. "Gideon," God said, "your army is too big."

Too big? Gideon thought his army was far too small! But God explained. If Gideon won with a great big army, the people might think they had done it all by themselves. God wanted them to know, deep in their hearts, that the victory came from him. So, little by little, God made Gideon's army smaller. And smaller. And smaller still — until only three hundred men were left.

Just three hundred men — to face thousands and thousands of enemy soldiers!

But God was with them, and God had a clever plan. He told Gideon to give every single one of his three hundred men a trumpet to blow, and a clay jar, with a bright burning torch hidden down inside it. It was a strange sort of plan — no swords, and no fighting, just trumpets and jars and torches. But Gideon trusted God, and he did exactly as God had told him.

So that night, while it was dark, the three hundred men crept softly all the way around the edge of the enemy camp, as quiet as they could possibly be. The enemy soldiers were fast asleep. And then, when everyone was in place, Gideon gave the signal.

All at once, the three hundred men smashed their clay jars on the ground — CRASH! — and suddenly the night was full of blazing torches, shining out everywhere! They lifted up their trumpets and blew them as loud as they could. And they shouted out together, all three hundred voices at once, ringing through the dark.

The sleeping enemy soldiers woke up in a terrible fright. All around them were lights, and trumpets, and shouting! They could not tell what was happening. They were so frightened, and so muddled and confused, that they jumped up and ran away into the night — every last one of them. And they did not come back.

When morning came, God's people were safe and sound. And everyone knew it was not because of a big, strong army. It was because God had been with them all along.

So whenever something feels too big, or whenever you feel far too small, remember Gideon and his three hundred. You do not have to be the biggest, or the strongest, or the bravest. You only need to know that God is right there beside you.
