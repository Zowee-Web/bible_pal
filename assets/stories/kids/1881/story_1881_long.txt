Gideon's Three Hundred

Long ago, there lived a man named Gideon. Gideon was not a soldier, and he did not feel brave at all. When he looked at himself, he saw someone small and ordinary — the kind of person nobody picks for a big job. But God had a very big job for him. A huge enemy army had come marching into the land, to hurt God's people and to take everything they had. And God chose Gideon — quiet, ordinary Gideon — to lead his people to safety.

So Gideon called together an army of men to help him. They came from all over, picking up their packs and gathering on the hillside, until Gideon had quite a few men standing beside him. For a little while, he felt better. He was not all alone anymore.

But then he climbed up where he could see the valley below, and his heart sank right down. The enemy soldiers were everywhere. They filled the whole valley, more than anyone could ever count, spread across the land like a great swarm of grasshoppers. Their tents went on and on. Gideon's army seemed so very small beside them — like a handful of pebbles next to a mountain. He was afraid, and he did not feel one bit braver than before.

And then God said something that surprised Gideon very much. "Gideon," God said, "your army is too big."

Too big? Gideon could hardly believe his ears. He thought his army was far too small! How could it ever be too big? But God explained. If Gideon won the battle with a great big army, the people might look at all those soldiers and think, "We did it ourselves. We were strong enough." God did not want them to think that. God wanted them to know, deep down in their hearts, that the victory came from him.

So God began to make Gideon's army smaller. He let the men who felt frightened go home — and a great many of them turned and went. Gideon watched them walk away over the hills until far fewer were left. Then God made the army smaller still, and smaller, by the little stream where the men stopped to drink. Little by little, group by group, the army shrank. And shrank. And shrank some more — until at last, when Gideon counted, only three hundred men were left standing with him.

Just three hundred men — to face thousands and thousands of enemy soldiers!

Now, you might think Gideon would feel more afraid than ever. And he did wonder, deep inside, how this could ever work. But God was kind to Gideon. That very night, God let him creep close to the enemy camp and listen, just for a moment. And there in the dark, Gideon heard something wonderful: even the enemy soldiers were whispering that God was going to give the victory to Gideon. When Gideon heard it, hope rose up warm inside him. He bowed his head and thanked God. And he was not so afraid anymore.

Then God gave Gideon a clever plan. He told Gideon to give every single one of his three hundred men a trumpet to blow, and a clay jar, with a bright burning torch hidden down inside it. It was a strange sort of plan — no swords swinging, no fighting at all, just trumpets and jars and hidden torches. Gideon looked at the trumpet in one hand and the clay jar in the other, and surely it seemed too simple to ever work. But Gideon trusted God now. So he did exactly as God had told him.

He divided the three hundred into little groups and gave each man his trumpet and his jar. "Watch me," Gideon told them quietly, "and do just as I do." And then they waited. They waited in the dark for the right moment, hearts beating, torches glowing softly inside the clay, until the camp grew still and the enemy was fast asleep.

When the time came, the three hundred men crept softly all the way around the edge of the enemy camp, stepping as quiet as they could possibly be. Not one of them made a sound. They spread themselves out, here and there, all around the sleeping soldiers. And then, when every man was finally in his place, Gideon lifted his trumpet — and gave the signal.

All at once, the three hundred men smashed their clay jars on the ground — CRASH! — and suddenly the night was full of blazing torches, shining out everywhere at once! They lifted up their trumpets and blew them as loud as ever they could. And they shouted out together, all three hundred voices ringing through the dark, "For the Lord, and for Gideon!"

The sleeping enemy soldiers woke up in a terrible fright. All around them were lights, and trumpets, and shouting! They could not tell what was happening, or how many were out there in the dark. They were so frightened, and so muddled and confused, that they jumped up and ran away into the night — every last one of them. And they did not come back.

When morning came, the sun rose over a quiet valley, and the great enemy army was gone. God's people were safe and sound. And everyone knew — every man, woman, and child — that it had not happened because of a big, strong army. It could not have. There had only been three hundred, with trumpets and jars and torches. It had happened because God had been with them all along.

So whenever something feels too big, or whenever you feel far too small, remember Gideon and his three hundred. You do not have to be the biggest, or the strongest, or the bravest. God is not looking for the mightiest army. He is looking for a heart that will trust him. And he is right there beside you.
