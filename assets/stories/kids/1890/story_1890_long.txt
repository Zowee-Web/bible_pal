Singing in the Dark

Paul and his good friend Silas loved Jesus with all their hearts. Together they traveled, mile after mile, from town to town and from city to city. Everywhere they went, they told the people they met the wonderful good news about Jesus. Many people were glad to hear it. Their faces lit up like windows at sunrise, and they wanted to hear more and more.

But in one faraway city, some people did not like what Paul and Silas were saying. They were not glad at all. And so the two friends were arrested, and they were marched away, and they were put into prison.

It was not a nice prison. The guards took Paul and Silas deep inside, down where the air was cold and the light could not reach. They were led into the darkest cell of all, far at the back. There, their feet were locked tightly into heavy wooden blocks, so they could not move them even a little. The big door was swung shut. A heavy bolt slid across with a clang. And then the footsteps walked away, and faded, and were gone.

Now there was no way out at all.

Paul and Silas sat in the dark. They could not see each other's faces. They could only hear the quiet drip of water, somewhere far off, and the slow breathing of the other prisoners around them in the gloom.

Now, you might think that this is where Paul and Silas would sit feeling sad, and frightened, and sorry for themselves. And no one in the whole world would have blamed them for it. Their feet hurt. The night was long. Morning felt very far away.

But that is not what they did. Not at all.

The hours went by, slow and dark, until it was the very middle of the night. Midnight. Everything was still. Everything was quiet. And it was right then, in the deepest dark of all, that Paul and Silas began to sing.

That's right! They prayed out loud to God, the two of them together, right there in the gloomy prison. And then they began to sing him songs. Happy songs. Thankful songs. Their voices rose up softly at first, and then stronger, floating up through the darkness, full of trust.

All around them, the other prisoners woke up. One by one, they lifted their heads. They lay very still in the dark, listening, hardly daring to breathe. They had never in all their lives heard anything like it. Two men, locked in chains, in a place as dark and cold as this — singing? How could it be? How could anyone be glad in here?

But Paul and Silas knew a secret. They knew that God was right there with them, even in the darkness. God had not stayed behind at the prison door. God had come all the way inside, down into the deepest cell, and he was closer than the dark itself. And so the two friends were not afraid. And so they kept right on singing, and their song wrapped around the listening prisoners like a warm blanket in the cold.

And then — it happened.

All at once, deep down inside the earth, came a low rumble. The rumble grew. And then came a mighty shaking, a great earthquake that made the whole prison tremble from the floor to the very top. The walls shook. The chains rattled. And then, with a great crash, every prison door flew wide open — and the heavy locks let go, and the chains fell right off every single prisoner, all at once.

The singing stopped. For one long moment, nobody moved at all.

Now, far away at the front of the prison, the jailer woke up with a fright. He was the man whose job was to guard the prison and keep watch over the doors. He came running, and when he saw all the doors hanging open in the dark, his heart sank all the way down to his feet. He was sure, quite sure, that every prisoner had run far away into the night, and that he would be in terrible trouble.

But Paul saw him there, lost and afraid in the doorway. And Paul called out to him, kind and strong, right through the dark: "Do not worry! We are all still here! Every one of us!"

For it was true. Paul and Silas had not run. The doors stood open. The chains lay broken on the ground. They could have slipped away into the night and been free. But they had stayed, calm and kind, right where they were. And not one of the other prisoners had run either, for the song still held them all.

The jailer could hardly believe it. He called for a light, and when it came, he saw their faces, peaceful in the glow. He came in, trembling all over, and he knelt right down before Paul and Silas. "What must I do?" he asked them, in a small and wondering voice. "What must I do?"

So Paul and Silas told him. They told him all about Jesus, and how Jesus had come for him, and how much God loved him — yes, even him, the jailer, here in the middle of the night. The jailer listened, and his fear melted away, and something brand new and warm grew up inside him in its place.

And that very night, the jailer believed. He gave his whole heart to God. And his family believed too — every single one of them, all together, in the lamplight.

Then the jailer did a kind and gentle thing. He brought water, and he washed the places where his new friends were hurting, soft and careful. And he took them up out of the cold prison and into his own warm home. He sat them at his table and gave them a good meal, and all of them ate together. The whole house was glad. They had begun the night in the dark, and now they were full of light and joy, like a lamp burning bright until morning.

So remember this, little one, whenever your own night feels long and dark. You are never, ever alone in it. God does not wait at the door. He comes all the way in, and he stays right beside you, just as he stayed beside Paul and Silas in the very deepest cell. He is the God who is closer than the dark — and he is the One who fills the night with singing. And like Paul and Silas, even when you cannot see the way out, you can always find a song to sing.
