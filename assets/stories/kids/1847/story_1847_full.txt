Elijah Fed by Ravens

A long, dry time had come to the land. Day after day, the sun rose hot in a wide, cloudless sky, and no rain fell at all — not a single drop. The streams grew thin, and then thinner still. The green grass turned brown and crackly. And little by little, food became hard to find for everyone in the land.

But God had not forgotten his helper Elijah. One quiet day, God spoke to him, with a plan all ready.

"Go and hide by the little brook out in the hills," God told him. "It is a peaceful place, far from the dusty roads and the busy towns. There you can drink the cool, clear water whenever you are thirsty. And do not worry about food — I have a surprise for you. I have told the ravens to bring it to you."

So Elijah did exactly as God said. He walked out into the hills until he found the little brook, bubbling and trickling softly over the smooth stones. And there, beside the water, he made his home.

Now, ravens are big black birds, with glossy, shining feathers and wide, flapping wings. And everything God had said came true. The very next morning, just as the sun peeked over the hills, Elijah heard the sound of beating wings. He looked up — and down came the ravens! And what do you think they were carrying in their beaks? Bread, and good food! They set it down gently on the ground beside him, and then up they flew again, into the brightening sky.

And that very evening, as the sun was going down, the ravens came again, carrying more bread and more food. Elijah ate until he was full and warm and glad.

The next morning, they came again. And again that evening. Morning and evening, evening and morning, day after day after day, the faithful birds brought Elijah exactly what he needed. And whenever he grew thirsty, he simply knelt down at the edge of the brook and drank the cool, clear water.

As the days went by, Elijah stopped worrying altogether. He knew the ravens would come at sunrise, and he knew they would come again at sunset. So even in that lonely place, far from everyone, he was not really alone at all — because God was taking care of him, morning and evening, just as he had promised.

So Elijah was never hungry, and he was never thirsty, even in that long, dry, difficult time. He sat by his quiet brook in the hills. He listened to the gentle water. He watched the wide sky for the dark wings of his feathered friends. And every time they came, his heart filled up with thankfulness all over again.

For God knew exactly where Elijah was. God knew exactly what he needed — and exactly when he needed it. And God took care of him in a way that no one in the whole world would ever have guessed: with a quiet little brook, and a bit of bread, and a flock of faithful black birds.
