The Good Samaritan

One day, a man came to Jesus with a question. "Teacher," he asked, "who is my neighbor? Who am I supposed to love?" And Jesus, who loved to teach with stories, answered him with one.

Once, Jesus said, a man was traveling alone down a long, lonely, winding road. It was a dangerous road. And partway along it, some robbers jumped out, and they hurt the man, and took all his things, and left him lying by the side of the road, too weak even to lift his head.

After a while, footsteps came down that road. It was an important man, a leader from the temple. He saw the hurt man lying there in the dust. But instead of helping, he crossed to the far side of the road and hurried right past.

A little later, a second man came along. He too saw the hurt man lying there. And he too hurried past on the other side, and left him all alone.

The poor hurt man must have wondered if anyone would ever stop to help him.

And then, at last, a third traveler came down the road. He was from a faraway country called Samaria. When he saw the hurt man, he did not hurry past. His heart filled with kindness, and he stopped right away.

Gently, the Samaritan knelt down in the dust beside him. He carefully cleaned the man's hurts, and poured on a little oil to soothe them, and wrapped them up with strips of soft cloth. He gave the man a sip of cool water, and spoke to him kindly. Then he lifted the hurt man up onto his own donkey, and walked slowly beside him, holding him steady, all the way to an inn where he could rest and grow strong again.

The kind traveler even stayed the whole night, watching over him. And in the morning, before he had to leave, he gave the innkeeper some money. "Please take good care of this man," he said. "And if it costs any more than this, I will pay you back when I return."

The hurt man was a stranger. The Samaritan had never met him before in his life. But he had stopped, and helped, and cared for him, just like a true friend.

When Jesus had finished the story, he looked at the people who were listening to him. "Now you know," he said softly. "Go and do the same."
