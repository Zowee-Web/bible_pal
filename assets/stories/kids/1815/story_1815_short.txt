The Good Samaritan

One day, someone asked Jesus a question: "Who is my neighbor?" And Jesus answered with a story.

Once, a man was walking down a long, lonely road. On the way, some robbers came and hurt him and took his things, and they left him lying by the side of the road, too weak to get up.

After a while, an important man came walking down the same road. He saw the hurt man lying there — but he hurried past on the other side and did not stop.

Then a second man came along. He too saw the hurt man — but he also hurried past and left him all alone.

At last, a third man came down the road. He was from a faraway place called Samaria. When he saw the hurt man, he did not hurry past. He felt sorry for him, and he stopped.

Gently, the kind man knelt down. He cleaned the hurt man's wounds and wrapped them up with soft cloth. Then he lifted him onto his own donkey and walked beside him, all the way to an inn, where he could rest and get better.

The kind man even stayed to care for him through the night. And in the morning, before he left, he gave the innkeeper some money. "Please take good care of him," he said. "And if it costs any more, I will pay you when I come back."

The hurt man was a stranger. The kind man did not know him at all. But he had stopped, and helped, and cared for him like a true friend.

Jesus looked at the people listening to him. "Now you know," he said softly. "Go and do the same."
