David and the Giant

In the green hills near Bethlehem, there lived a shepherd boy named David. He was the youngest of all his brothers. While they grew tall and went off to do important things, David stayed out on the wide hillsides, watching over his father's sheep.

David did not mind one bit. He loved those quiet hills. He knew every sheep by name, and he could count them all without missing a single one. When the sun was warm on his back, he played his harp and sang soft songs to God. And the sheep grazed safely, because David was always watching.

One day, a lion came creeping out of the hills, looking for a lamb to carry off. Another day, it was a bear. Both times, David's heart pounded hard — but he did not run. He stood up tall, and with God's help, he drove the wild animals away and kept his little flock safe. "God was with me," David said softly afterward, his heart still thumping. And he never, ever forgot it.

Now, far from David's peaceful hills, the king's great army had gathered for a war. David's three oldest brothers were soldiers there. One morning, David's father called him in. "Take this bread to your brothers," he said, "and see how they are." So David left his sheep with a helper and set off down the long road.

But when David reached the army, something was terribly wrong. The soldiers were not marching. They were not training. They were crouched down behind the rocks, peeking out, and their faces were pale with fear.

Then David heard it. A sound came rolling across the valley — heavy footsteps. Thud. Thud. Thud. A giant came striding out, taller than any man David had ever seen, with armor that flashed in the sun. His name was Goliath. "Send out a man to fight me!" he roared, so loudly that the ground seemed to tremble. Every soldier ducked lower behind the rocks. Not one of them moved.

And David learned that the giant came out every morning and every evening to shout the very same thing — and every single time, the whole brave army shook with fear.

David turned to the men beside him. "Why is everyone so afraid?" he asked.

"He is too big," they whispered. "No one can stand against him. No one at all."

But David thought of the lion. He thought of the bear. He thought of how God had been with him every single time. "God kept my sheep safe," David said quietly. "God will help us now."

Word of the brave shepherd boy reached the king, and David was brought before him.

"You are only a boy," the king said, shaking his head. "And Goliath has been a fighter his whole life."

"I have kept my father's sheep," David answered. "When the lion came, and when the bear came, God rescued me. And God will rescue me now."

The king looked into David's steady eyes for a long moment. And at last, he agreed.

The king wanted to help, so he dressed David in his own armor. He set a heavy bronze helmet on David's head. He hung a great coat of metal over his shoulders. He gave him a long, heavy sword. But it was all far, far too big. David could barely stand, let alone walk. He took one wobbly step, and then another.

"I cannot move in these," David said kindly. So he took the armor off, every heavy piece, and set it all down in a pile.

Instead, David walked down to a little stream that ran sparkling through the valley. He knelt at the water's edge, and the water ran cool and clear over his fingers. Carefully, he chose five smooth stones — round, and cool, and just the right size — and slipped them into his shepherd's bag. Then he took his sling in his hand, the same sling he had used to protect his sheep, and he walked out to meet the giant.

Goliath looked down at the shepherd boy, with no sword and no armor, and he laughed a great, booming laugh. "Am I a dog," he sneered, "that you come at me with a stick?"

But David looked up at the giant, and his voice did not shake, not even a little. "You come to me with a sword and a spear," he said. "But I come in the name of the living God — and he is with me."

David reached into his bag and drew out one smooth stone. He fitted it into his sling. Then round and round it swung over his head, faster and faster, whirring through the air — and David let it fly. The stone sped across the valley, straight and true.

The giant swayed. He teetered. And then the great giant came tumbling down to the ground — and the whole valley went still.

For one breathless moment, nobody moved at all. Then, slowly, the soldiers rose up from behind their rocks. They could hardly believe their eyes. The fear that had held them for so many long days was simply gone. They lifted their voices and cheered until the hills rang, because they had seen, with their own eyes, that God is greater than the very greatest giant.

And David? The shepherd boy who trusted God went back, in time, to his hills, and his sheep, and his songs — the same boy he had always been, with the same God beside him, who had been with him all along.
