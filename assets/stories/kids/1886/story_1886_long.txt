Bread from Heaven

Have you ever felt hungry, and looked all around, and there was nothing to eat anywhere? Not in the cupboard, not on the table, not anywhere at all? That is how God's people felt, a long, long time ago, out in the middle of a great, dry desert.

For God had set his people free from Egypt, and now he was leading them home. Day after day they walked, with sand all around them and the hot sun above. They walked over hills and down into valleys, farther and farther from any town. And out there, far from anywhere, there were no shops, and no gardens, and no food to be found.

Before long, the people grew very hungry. And when people are hungry and tired, they sometimes begin to grumble. "What are we going to eat out here?" they said to one another. "There is nothing at all! Not a crumb, not a berry, not a single bite." They were frightened, and just for a moment, they forgot how much God loved them.

But God had not forgotten them. Not for a single moment. God heard every worried word, and he was not angry. He simply said, "Do not be afraid. I will send you bread from heaven. There will be enough for everyone, every single day."

Bread from heaven? The people had never heard of such a thing. How could bread come from the sky? But the sun was going down now, and the desert was growing dark, and there was nothing to do but go to bed and wait for morning.

So the people lay down in their tents, hungry and wondering. The little ones snuggled close. "Will the bread really come?" they whispered. And their mothers and fathers said, "God promised. So we will wait, and we will see." And one by one, all through the camp, the people fell asleep under the desert stars.

While they slept, the night grew quiet. The stars wheeled slowly overhead. And the people did not know it, but God was already keeping his promise.

When the first light crept over the desert, a child woke up and peeked out of the tent — and stopped, and stared, and rubbed her eyes. "Mother!" she called. "Father! Come and look! Come and look right now!"

For all across the ground, covering everything like a soft white frost, lay thousands of thin little flakes. They were small, and round, and white, and they sparkled in the morning light. The whole desert glittered, as far as the eye could see, as though the ground had been sprinkled all over with tiny stars.

Out of every tent the people came, blinking in the morning sun. And nobody knew what the little flakes were. They bent down low and looked closely. They turned to one another with wide eyes.

"What is it?" they asked. "What in the world is it?" For they had never seen anything like it in all their lives.

Then Moses came out among them, and he was smiling. "It is the bread the Lord has given you to eat," he said. "Just as he promised. Go on — gather it up!"

So the people began to gather. Down they bent, scooping the flakes up by the handful, filling their baskets to the brim. The children laughed and ran here and there, chasing each other through the sparkling field, gathering the wonderful surprise. And when at last they tasted it — oh, it was delicious! It was sweet, a little like wafers made with honey. The whole camp was soon full of happy, munching sounds.

The people gave the bread a name. Because nobody had known what it was, they called it manna — which means, "What is it?" And from then on, that was its name. Manna. The bread that came from heaven.

Now, that morning, God told his people something important. "Gather just enough for today," he said. "Do not try to keep great piles of it for tomorrow. Trust me — there will be fresh manna again in the morning."

Some of the people found that hard to believe. "Just enough for today?" they said. "But what if there is none tomorrow?" And there were one or two who did not want to trust. Quietly, when no one was looking, they scooped up extra, and filled a jar, and hid it away inside their tent. "Now we will have plenty," they said. "Now we do not have to worry."

But when the next morning came — what do you think they found? When they opened the jar of manna they had hidden away, the old manna was no good at all. It had spoiled in the night. The people who had not trusted hung their heads, a little sad and a little sorry.

But oh — when they looked outside! There on the ground, sparkling in the morning light, was fresh manna again. New bread from heaven, soft and sweet and waiting, exactly as God had promised. There was plenty for everyone. There always was. Nobody had to keep great hidden piles, because every single morning, God sent fresh bread, brand new with the dawn.

And so it went. Morning after morning after morning, the people woke and peeked outside, and there it was — the desert sparkling with bread from heaven, always enough, and never too little. They gathered, and they ate, and they grew strong for the long walk ahead.

The little ones learned something on that journey, something they would remember all their lives. They did not have to worry about what they would eat tomorrow. They did not have to hide great jars away. When tomorrow came, God would be there, and the bread would be there, sparkling on the ground in the morning sun. All they had to do was trust him, and wake up, and look.

God fed his people that way all the long way through the desert. Through every hill, through every valley, all the way home. He did not forget them for even one day. Not one single day.

So you never have to worry about tomorrow, little one. The very same God who sent the manna sees you, right now, and he knows exactly what you need. He is not far away in the sky. He is close, and he is faithful, and he loves to give good gifts to his children. And he gives us what we need, gently, one new morning at a time.
