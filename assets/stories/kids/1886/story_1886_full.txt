Bread from Heaven

After God set his people free from Egypt, he led them on a long journey through a great, dry desert. Day after day they walked, with sand all around and the hot sun above. And out there, far from any town, there were no shops, no gardens, and no food to be found anywhere.

Before long, the people grew very hungry. And when people are hungry and tired, they sometimes begin to grumble. "What are we going to eat out here?" they said. "There is nothing at all!" They were frightened, and just for a moment, they forgot how much God loved them. But God had not forgotten them. Not for a single moment.

But God heard them, and he was not angry. He simply said, "Do not be afraid. I will send you bread from heaven. There will be enough for everyone, every single day."

That night, the people went to sleep, hungry and wondering. But when they woke the next morning and peeked out of their tents — what a surprise! All across the ground, covering everything like a soft white frost, lay thousands of thin little flakes. They were small, and round, and white, and they sparkled in the morning light. The whole desert glittered, as far as the eye could see, as though the ground had been sprinkled all over with tiny stars.

The people came out and stared. "What is it?" they asked one another, for they had never seen anything like it in all their lives. "What in the world is it?"

"It is the bread the Lord has given you to eat," said Moses, with a smile. "Just as he promised. Go on — gather it up!"

So the people gathered it, scooping it up by the basketful. The children laughed and ran here and there, filling their baskets with the wonderful surprise. And when they tasted it — oh, it was delicious! It was sweet, a little like wafers made with honey. They gave it a name. They called it manna.

Now, God told them something important. "Gather just enough for today," he said. "Do not try to keep great piles of it for tomorrow. Trust me — there will be fresh manna again in the morning."

Some of the people found that hard to believe at first. But sure enough, when the next morning came, the manna was right there again, sparkling on the ground, exactly as God had promised.

And there always was. Every single morning, when the people woke and looked outside, the ground was covered with fresh bread from heaven, sparkling and waiting — always enough, and never too little. God fed his people that way, morning after morning after morning, all the long way through the desert. He did not forget them for even one day.

So you never have to worry about tomorrow, little one. The very same God who sent the manna sees you, and he knows exactly what you need. And he gives us what we need, gently, one day at a time.
