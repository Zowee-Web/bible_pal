When Moses' Arms Grew Tired

It was a hard, frightening day for God's people. Trouble had come to them in the wilderness, and they would have to be brave. So while the people gathered down in the valley, their leader Moses climbed up to the top of a high hill, where he could see them all. And he took two good friends along with him — Aaron and Hur.

In his hand Moses carried a tall wooden staff — the very same staff God had used to do great wonders. And up on the hill, Moses lifted that staff high, raising both his hands toward heaven, praying for his people down below.

And something amazing happened. As long as Moses kept his hands lifted up, God helped his people, and all went well for them. They were strong. They were safe.

But after a while, Moses' arms began to grow tired. Have you ever held your arms up high for a long, long time? They start to ache. They start to shake. Slowly, slowly, Moses' arms began to droop and sink down toward the ground. And the moment they came down — oh no — things grew hard for his people again.

So Moses lifted them back up. And his people grew strong again. But his arms were so very tired now. His shoulders burned, and his hands began to shake. Up and down, up and down they went — and the harder Moses tried, the heavier his arms seemed to grow. He simply could not keep them up all by himself any longer.

That is when his two friends had an idea.

First, Aaron and Hur found a big smooth stone, and they rolled it over behind Moses so that he could sit down and rest his tired legs. Then Aaron stood on one side of Moses, and Hur stood on the other side. And gently, carefully, each friend reached out and took hold of one of Moses' arms — and lifted it up, and held it there.

One friend on the left. One friend on the right. And Moses in the middle, sitting on the stone, his hands held high and steady against the sky.

And there they stayed. The sun climbed high, and then began to sink low, and still those three did not move. Aaron and Hur held Moses' hands up, hour after hour after hour. Their own arms surely grew tired too — but they did not let go. Not once. They held on for their friend all the way until the sun went down. And God brought his people safely, gently through that hard, long day.

Moses did not have to do it alone. When he was too weary to hold his own hands up, God gave him friends to hold them up for him.

And God still does that today. When we are tired, and a day feels too heavy to carry all by ourselves, God so often sends us people — people who will come and stand beside us, one on each side, and help us hold on.
