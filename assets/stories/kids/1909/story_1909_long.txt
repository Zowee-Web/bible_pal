When Moses' Arms Grew Tired

It was a hard, frightening day for God's people. They were out in the wide wilderness, far from any home, when trouble came marching toward them. They were afraid. This was a day when they would have to be very brave.

Their leader Moses knew just what to do. "I will go up to the top of the hill," he said, "and I will hold up my hands to God, and pray for you the whole time." So while the people gathered down in the valley below, Moses began to climb. And he did not go alone — he took two good friends with him, Aaron and Hur, one walking on either side.

Up they went, up the steep, rocky path, until they stood together at the very top, where they could see everyone down below.

In his hand Moses carried a tall wooden staff. It was the same staff God had used to do great and wonderful things. Moses lifted that staff high above his head, raising both of his hands toward heaven, praying and praying for his people.

And something amazing began to happen. As long as Moses kept his hands lifted up high, God helped his people down in the valley, and all went well for them. They stood strong. They were safe. You could see it from the hilltop.

But the morning stretched on, and then it was noon, and Moses' arms began to grow tired. Have you ever tried to hold your arms up high for a long, long time? At first it is easy. But soon they begin to ache. They begin to tremble and shake. And little by little, no matter how hard you try, they start to sink.

That is what happened to Moses. Slowly, slowly, his tired arms drooped down toward the ground. And the very moment they came down — oh no — things grew hard for his people again, down in the valley.

So Moses lifted his arms back up as high as he could. And at once his people grew strong again. Up went his hands, and all was well. Down they sank, and the trouble returned. Up, and down. Up, and down. Moses tried with all his might, gritting his teeth, lifting and lifting — but his arms were simply too tired now. He could not hold them up by himself any longer, no matter how badly he wanted to.

That is when his two friends had an idea — a wonderful, simple idea.

First, Aaron and Hur looked around and found a big smooth stone. Together they rolled it over, right behind Moses, so that he could sit down and rest his tired, shaky legs.

Then Aaron went and stood close on one side of Moses. And Hur went and stood close on the other side. And gently — oh so gently and carefully — each friend reached out, and took hold of one of Moses' heavy, tired arms, and lifted it up high, and held it there for him.

One friend on the left. One friend on the right. And Moses sitting in the middle, on his stone, his hands held high and steady against the wide blue sky.

And there the three of them stayed. The sun climbed up high overhead, and then slowly, slowly began to sink down toward evening — and still those three did not move from their places. Aaron and Hur held Moses' hands up, hour after hour after hour. Their own arms surely grew tired too, aching just like his had. But they did not let go. Not once. Not for a single moment. They held on for their dear friend, steady and sure, all the way until the sun went down at the end of the day.

And because his hands stayed lifted up, God brought all his people safely and gently through that long, hard day.

When evening came at last, Moses did not say, "Look what I did." Because Moses had not done it alone. When he was far too weary to hold his own hands up another minute, God had given him friends to hold them up for him.

And God still does that very thing today. When we are tired, and a day feels too heavy and too hard to carry all by ourselves, God so often sends us people — a friend on one side, a friend on the other — who will come and stand beside us, and help us hold on, all the way to the end of the day.
