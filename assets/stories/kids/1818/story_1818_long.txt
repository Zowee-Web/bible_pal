The Walls of Jericho

After many long years of traveling through the wilderness, God's people came, at last, right to the edge of the beautiful land that God had promised to give them. They could hardly wait to go in. But there was one enormous problem standing in their way. And its name was Jericho.

Jericho was a great city, and all around it ran the tallest, thickest walls that anyone had ever seen. They were so high that you had to tip your head all the way back, and back, just to see the very top. They were so thick and so wide that whole houses were built right on top of them. And the great wooden gates were shut up tight, and barred, and locked. There was no way over those walls, and no way through them. However in all the world would God's people ever get inside?

But God was with his people. And God had a plan — though it was just about the most surprising plan that anyone had ever heard.

God spoke to Joshua, the leader of his people. "You will not need to fight for this city," God said. "Here is what you must do instead. Have all the people march around the walls of Jericho — one time around, each day, for six days. Send the priests out in front, carrying trumpets made from rams' horns. But tell the people to stay completely quiet. No talking. No shouting. Not yet. Just march, and trust me, and wait."

Well! That was a strange sort of plan for taking a great city. But God's people had learned, over many years, to trust their God. So they did exactly as he said.

On the very first day, they all lined up, and they marched around the great walls of Jericho — one time, all the way around — and then they went quietly back to their camp. The only sounds were the soft tramp, tramp, tramp of their feet, and the long, low call of the trumpets. High up on the walls, the people of Jericho leaned over, and watched, and scratched their heads, and wondered what in the world these strange marchers could possibly be doing.

On the second day, God's people did it again. Around the city, one time, quiet and steady, and then home. And nothing happened. The walls stood just as tall, and just as strong, as ever.

On the third day, they marched again. And on the fourth day. And the fifth. And the sixth. Around, and around, once each day, calm and quiet, while the trumpets sang their long, low song. And still — nothing. The mighty walls did not so much as wobble. But God's people did not give up, and they did not lose heart. They kept right on marching, and they kept right on trusting God, and they waited to see what he would do.

Then came the seventh day. And this day was different.

This time, God's people did not march around the city only once. They marched around it seven whole times! Around, and around, and around, and around — quietly, and steadily, while the trumpets called out across the bright morning. The people of Jericho watched from the walls, more and more puzzled with every single lap.

And when God's people had finished marching around for the seventh time, everything went suddenly still, and silent. Not a sound. The people stood, and they waited, and they held their breath.

Then Joshua lifted up his voice, and he gave the signal. "Now!" he cried. "Shout! For the Lord has given us the city! God is with us!"

The priests blew their trumpets as loud as ever they could — a great, blaring blast that rang out across the whole land. And all of God's people opened their mouths and shouted together — one enormous, mighty shout, all at the very same moment — so loud that it seemed to fill the whole wide sky.

And then it happened.

The mighty walls of Jericho — those huge, tall, thick walls that no one could ever climb, and no one could ever break — began to tremble. And then to shake. And then, with a great and thunderous CRASH, they came tumbling, and rumbling, and crashing all the way down — flat to the ground!

God's people could hardly believe their eyes. They stood there in the morning light, looking at the great heaps of fallen stone, where the impossible walls had stood only a moment before. The walls had not come down because the people were strong, or because they had mighty weapons. They had come down because God was with his people, and because his people had trusted him, and waited, and obeyed.

And there in the bright morning, God's people lifted up their hearts, full of wonder, and full of thanks, at all the amazing things their God could do.

So whenever something stands in your way that seems far too big, little one — too tall, too strong, impossible — remember the walls of Jericho. And remember that nothing at all is too big for the God who loves you.
