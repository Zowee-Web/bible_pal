The Cross

Of all the days in the whole story of Jesus, this is the one his friends would never forget. It was the day the world seemed to hold its breath.

Jesus was led up a hill, just outside the city. His friends could only watch from a little way off — and among them stood his own mother, Mary, with the women who had followed him, weeping softly. Their hearts were heavy and aching, because they loved him with all that they had.

You see, Jesus had never once done anything wrong. He had healed the sick and helped the poor. He had taken little children up in his arms and blessed them. He had loved every single person he ever met, even the ones nobody else loved. But there were some who did not understand him, and were afraid of him, and they put Jesus on a wooden cross.

And even then — even there, in the hardest moment of all — Jesus did not stop loving. He looked down at the people below him, the very ones who had hurt him, and he prayed for them. "Father," he said, "forgive them." That is how much love was in his heart. There was no room in it for anything but love.

Then, in the middle of the day, the sky grew dark. All across the land it grew dark, as if the whole world had gone quiet and sad together. And in the darkness, Jesus looked up to God, his Father. "Into your hands," he said softly, "I give myself." And he closed his eyes, and his breath grew still.

His friends wept. It was the saddest day they had ever known. They did not understand why this had happened. They did not know yet what God was doing, hidden in the dark.

But here is what they could not see — and it is the most important part. God had not left. God had not gone away, not even for a moment. God was right there in the dark, holding everything, holding Jesus, holding the whole sad world close. And this was not the end of the story.

For Jesus had not been taken. Jesus had chosen this. He had walked this path on purpose, because he loved the whole world — he loved every person who had ever lived, and every person still to come. He loved you. And a love as strong as that, the strongest love there has ever been, could never, ever stay buried in the dark.

His friends did not know it yet, as they wiped their tears that evening. But Sunday was coming.
