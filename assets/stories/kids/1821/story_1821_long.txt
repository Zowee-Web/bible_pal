The Cross

Of all the days in the whole story of Jesus, this is the one his friends would never forget. It was the day the world seemed to hold its breath.

Jesus was led up a hill, just outside the city. And though it was a hard and sorrowful day, there is something to remember all the way through it — something that matters more than anything else: Jesus was not alone.

His friends were there. They stood a little way off, because their hearts were aching so, but they did not go away, and they did not leave him. And his own mother, Mary, was there too, close by — the way a mother stays near her child, no matter what. The people who loved Jesus most had come to be with him, so that even on this day, he would not be by himself.

They could not make the hard day go away. They could not change what was happening. But they could stay. And so they stayed — close, and loving, and near. For sometimes, when someone we love is hurting, the most loving thing in all the world is simply to be there beside them, and not to leave.

You see, Jesus had never once done anything wrong. He had healed the sick and helped the poor. He had taken little children up into his arms and blessed them. He had loved every single person he ever met — even the ones that nobody else would love. But there were some who did not understand him, and were afraid of him. And they put Jesus on a wooden cross.

And even then — even there, on the hardest day of all — Jesus did not stop loving. He looked down at the people below him, the very ones who had hurt him, and he prayed for them. "Father," he said, "forgive them." There was no room in his heart for anything but love. Not even on that day. And his friends, watching through their tears, heard him pray it, and they wondered at a love so deep and so strong.

Then, in the very middle of the day, the sky grew dark. All across the land it grew soft and dark, as if the whole world had gone quiet and still and sad together, keeping watch alongside Jesus' friends.

But the darkness was not empty. This is the most important part of all — the part to hold onto with both hands. God was there. God had not left, not for one single moment. He was right there in the dark, closer than ever before, holding Jesus, and holding Mary, and holding the weeping friends, and holding the whole sad and quiet world gently in his hands. No one on that hill was alone. Love was still there. Love was holding everything. Even when the day felt dark, and sad, and wrong, God was holding it all — and God's love never lets go. Not on the hardest day. Not ever.

And in the darkness, Jesus looked up to God, his Father. "Into your hands," he said softly, "I give myself." And he closed his eyes, and his breath grew still and quiet, like a little child falling gently asleep, safe in his father's arms.

His friends wept. It was the saddest evening they had ever known. But even in their sorrow they were together, leaning on one another, and they did not have to carry it all alone. They did not understand why this had happened, or what God was doing, there in the quiet dark.

But here is what they could not see yet. Jesus had not simply been taken from them. Jesus had chosen this. He had walked up that hill on purpose, because he loved the whole world so very much — he loved every person who had ever lived, and every person still to come. He loved his friends. He loved Mary. He loved you. And a love that strong, the very strongest love there has ever been, could never, ever stay buried in the dark. Somewhere deep down, though they were far too sad to feel it yet, that great love was already quietly at work — the way the first light of morning is already on its way, long before the sky ever turns pink.

So as his friends held one another close that evening, and dried each other's tears, they did not know it yet — but the story was not over. Not nearly. The darkest night was not the end.

Because Sunday was coming.
