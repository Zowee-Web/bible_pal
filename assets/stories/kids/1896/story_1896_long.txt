A House for God

Long ago, King David — the shepherd boy who had grown up to become a great king — had a longing deep in his heart. "Here I live in a fine palace made of cedar," he thought, "while the holy ark of God still rests in a simple tent. That is not right. I will build a beautiful house for God."

But God said to David, gently, "Your heart is good, David. But you will not be the one to build it. Your son will build my house." And so David spent his last years gathering all that would be needed — piles of cedar, and stone, and gold, and silver — so that when the time came, his son would have everything ready.

And now that time had come. David's son, Solomon, was king. And Solomon had not forgotten his father's dream.

"God has been so good to us," Solomon said. "He gave us this land, and he has watched over us every single day. Now I will do what my father longed to do. I will build the Lord a temple — a house more beautiful than any house in all the world."

To find the finest wood, Solomon sent a message to King Hiram, a friendly king who ruled a land of great cedar forests by the sea. "Will you help me build a house for God?" Solomon asked. And Hiram was glad to help. His workers cut down the tall, sweet-smelling cedar trees, tied them into great rafts, and floated them all the way down the coast. In return, Solomon sent Hiram wheat and good olive oil, and the two kings became friends.

Then the work began in earnest. Thousands and thousands of workers set to the task. Up in the hills, skilled stonecutters carved enormous blocks of fine white stone, shaping each one perfectly. And here is a wonderful thing. Every block was cut and finished far away, up at the quarry, before it was ever carried to the temple. So all through the long years of building, at the temple itself there was never the sound of a hammer, or a chisel, or any iron tool at all. The great house of God rose up quietly, peacefully, each stone fitting silently against the next.

And oh, how beautiful it became. Inside, the craftsmen carved pictures into every wall — flowers opening in bloom, and tall palm trees, and angels with their wings spread wide. Then they covered it all — the carvings, the walls, even the floor — with pure, shining gold, until the whole inside glowed and gleamed like the morning sun. At the entrance stood two great pillars of gleaming bronze. And in the most special room of all, two tall golden angels stretched their wings from one wall to the other, to watch over the holy ark of God.

It took seven long years to build. But Solomon never once rushed. He wanted every single piece to be just right — for nothing less than the very best would do, for the house of God.

And at last — at last — the great temple was finished.

Then Solomon gathered all the people of the land together, from every town and village, for the greatest celebration anyone had ever seen. The priests lifted up the holy ark of God, ever so carefully, and carried it into the temple, and set it down in its special place, beneath the wings of the golden angels. Then the trumpets blew, and the singers lifted their voices, and the music swelled, and all the people praised God together with one great, joyful sound.

And then it happened. As the people worshiped, a bright cloud came down and filled the whole temple — a cloud so full of the glory of God that the priests could hardly even stand. Everyone grew quiet with wonder. For they all understood what it meant. God himself had come. He had come to make his home there, right in the middle of his people, in the beautiful house they had built for him.

Solomon's heart was so full it could hardly hold it all. He stood up before all the people, and then he knelt down, and he spread out his hands toward heaven, and he prayed a long and beautiful prayer. "O Lord, the God of our people," he said, "there is no one in all the heavens or the earth like you. You always keep your promises, and your love never, ever runs out. Thank you for your goodness to us. And thank you, most of all, for coming to be here, so close to the people who love you."

And then there was a great feast, and the celebration went on for days and days, and everyone went home with glad and thankful hearts.

So whatever you have to give to God, little one — your time, or your songs, your kindness, or your love — give it gladly, and give it your very best, the way Solomon gave his best. For of all the things in heaven and earth, there is nothing God loves more than to come close, and to make his home with those who love him.
