The Fiery Furnace

Long ago, in a faraway land, there lived three friends who loved God with all their hearts. Their names were Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. They lived far from the home they had grown up in, in the land of a powerful king. But even there, so far away, they never once forgot to love and worship God.

Now, the king of that land had built an enormous golden statue. It was tall as a tree and shining in the sun, and the king was very, very proud of it. He gathered all the people of the land together in one great crowd, and a herald called out the king's command in a loud voice. "When you hear the music play," he said, "every one of you must bow down low to the king's golden statue. And anyone who will not bow will be thrown into a blazing-hot furnace!"

Then the music played. And all across that wide plain, as far as the eye could see, the people bowed down low to the ground.

But three people did not. Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego would only ever worship God, and no statue of gold could ever take God's place. So while everyone else was bowing down, the three friends quietly stayed standing — tall, and still, and together.

It did not take long for some people to notice, and to run and tell the king. The king was furious. He called the three friends to stand before him. "I will give you just one more chance," he warned them. "When the music plays again, bow down to my statue. But if you will not — then into the blazing furnace you will go. And what god could ever save you then?"

Now, here is the bravest part of the whole story. The three friends were not angry, and they did not run away, and they did not even need a long time to think about it. They stood together before the mighty king, and they gave their answer, calm and clear.

"O king," they said, "our God is well able to save us from the fire. And we believe that he will. But even if he does not — even then — we want you to know this: we will still worship only him. We will never bow down to your statue of gold."

Even if he does not. They would be faithful to God whether they were rescued or not. That was how much they loved and trusted him. They did not yet know what was about to happen. They only knew the One they trusted. And so, whatever came next, their hearts were settled and quiet and at peace.

Well, this made the king angrier than ever. He ordered the furnace to be made hotter still, and the three friends were placed inside.

But then the king looked into the fire — and what he saw made him leap up from his throne. He could hardly believe his own eyes. "Did we not throw three men into the fire?" he cried out to his helpers. "But look — I see four! Four men, walking around in the middle of the flames, and they are not hurt at all! And the fourth one shines like an angel!"

For God had not left his friends alone in the fire. God was right there in the flames with them, walking beside them, keeping them safe and calm and unafraid.

"Come out!" the king called to them. "Come out, servants of the Most High God!" And Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego walked out of the furnace, safe and sound and well. Everyone crowded close to see — and it was true: their hair was not burned, their clothes were not scorched, and there was not even the smell of smoke upon them. The whole crowd stared in amazement, hardly able to believe their eyes. Three friends had been put into the blazing fire — and three friends had walked back out again, alive and well and unafraid, without so much as a single burn.

The king looked at the three friends, and then he praised their God in front of everyone. "Wonderful is the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego," he said. "He sent his help, and he stayed right beside them, and he kept them safe." And he made a new rule that day — that everyone in the land should honor the great God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego: the God who had saved his three faithful friends, and who stays right beside his people, even in the fire.
