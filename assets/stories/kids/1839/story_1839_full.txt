The Gentle Whisper

Elijah was one of God's helpers, and he was very, very tired.

For a long time he had been brave. He had stood up for God when it was hard, and he had done everything God asked. But now Elijah felt worn out and afraid, and so he ran far away — across the dry land, over the hills — until he came to a tall and lonely mountain. There he found a cave, and he crept inside, and he lay down. He felt as though he could not take one more step.

But God had not forgotten Elijah. While he slept, an angel woke him with a soft touch. "Get up and eat," the angel said gently. And there beside him was warm bread and cool water. The angel let him rest, and fed him again, until Elijah had the strength he needed. God was taking care of his tired helper, even out there all alone.

Then, in the quiet of the cave, God spoke. "Elijah," God asked kindly, "why are you here?"

"I have tried so hard," Elijah answered. "And now I feel all alone, and I am so tired."

"Come outside," said God. "Stand on the mountain. I am going to pass by."

So Elijah stood at the mouth of the cave and waited. And then it began.

A mighty wind came howling up the mountainside — so strong it split the rocks and sent them tumbling. But God was not in the wind.

When the wind had gone, the ground began to shake and rumble beneath Elijah's feet. But God was not in the shaking.

And after the shaking came a crackling, leaping fire. But God was not in the fire either.

Then, at last, when all the loud and powerful things had passed away, there came a sound so small that Elijah had to hold his breath just to hear it.

A gentle whisper.

Soft as a feather floating down. Quiet as a breath against your cheek.

And the moment he heard it, Elijah knew, deep down in his heart, that this was God. He pulled his coat up over his face, the way you might if something is almost too wonderful to look at, and he stepped closer. For God was very, very near.

God had not needed the roaring wind, or the shaking ground, or the crackling fire. God had come close in a whisper — to tell his tired, lonely helper the truest thing of all: You are loved. You are not alone. I am right here with you, and I always have been.
