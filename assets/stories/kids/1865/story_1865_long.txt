The Friends at Emmaus

On the very same day that Jesus' tomb was found empty, two of his friends set out walking along a road that led away from the city, toward a village called Emmaus. It was a long, dusty walk, and as they went, their heads hung low and their hearts were heavy and sad. For Jesus — their teacher, their friend, the one they had loved and followed and hoped in — had died. And though some of the women had come running that very morning with a strange and wonderful tale, that his tomb was empty and that he was alive, the two friends did not understand it at all. So they walked along together, feeling lost, and confused, and full of sorrow.

As they walked, talking quietly about everything that had happened, a stranger came up the road and began to walk along beside them. "What are you two talking about as you walk?" the stranger asked them kindly. "And why do you both look so very sad?"

The friends stopped, surprised. "Are you the only person around here who hasn't heard?" they said. And then it all came pouring out of them. They told the stranger about Jesus — what a wonderful teacher he was, and all the amazing things he had done, and how he had helped so many people. They told how he had died, and how they had so hoped he was the one who would save them. And they told him, too, about the empty tomb that morning, and how they simply did not know what to make of any of it.

The stranger listened to every word. And then he began to speak. Starting from the very oldest stories, from long, long ago, he gently explained all the promises God had made down through the years — all the clues, hidden in the old tellings, about a Savior who would one day come. On and on he talked as the three of them walked together. And the more the stranger spoke, the more something warm and glowing began to stir inside the two friends' hearts — though they could not have told you why.

At last they came near to the village of Emmaus, where the two friends were headed. The stranger looked as though he meant to keep walking on, further down the road. But by now the friends did not want him to leave. "Please, stay with us tonight," they begged him. "Look — it is nearly evening, and the day is almost gone. Come inside and share some supper with us." And so, kindly, the stranger agreed, and came in.

They sat down together at the table. And then the stranger did something simple, and ordinary — and yet it changed everything. He reached out and picked up the loaf of bread. He held it in his hands, and gave thanks to God. And then he broke the bread, and handed a piece to each of them.

And in that very instant — their eyes were opened.

They knew him! This was no stranger at all. It was Jesus — their own dear Jesus — alive again, sitting right there at their own table, breaking bread with them just the way he had done so many times before!

But the moment they recognized him — Jesus was gone from their sight.

The two friends turned and stared at one another, hearts pounding, eyes shining. "It was Jesus!" they said. "It was Jesus, all along! Didn't our hearts feel warm inside, while he talked with us back on the road? He was walking right there beside us the whole time — and we never even knew it was him!" To think — Jesus himself had been right there with them in their sadness, walking step for step beside them along the whole long road, even when their eyes were too tired and sad to see that it was him.

Well, they could not possibly sit still after that. Supper was forgotten. Even though darkness had fallen outside, they leaped up from the table and hurried right back out the door — back down the long road they had only just walked, half walking and half running the whole way to the city — to find their friends and tell them the most wonderful news in all the world: Jesus is alive! We have seen him! He walked beside us, all the way home. He had been close to them the whole time — and he had never truly left them at all.
