God Calls Abraham

In a faraway land, long ago, an old man named Abraham lived quietly in the only home he had ever known, with his wife Sarah, in the very place where he had grown up. He knew every path there. He knew the well where the water was cold and sweet. He knew the faces of his neighbors, and the way the evening light fell on the hills. It was familiar, and it was safe, and it was the only home he had ever known.

One quiet day, when the world was still, God spoke to Abraham.

"Abraham," God said, "I want you to do something big and brave. I want you to leave your home, and your town, and the land you have always known. Go to a new land — a land that I will show you."

Abraham listened. A new land? He did not even know where it was.

"And do not be afraid," God said gently, "for I will take care of you. I will bless you, and I will make your name great. I will give you a great big family, with more children and grandchildren than you could ever count — as many as the stars in the sky. And through your family, I will send a blessing to every person in the whole wide world."

It was a wonderful promise. The biggest, kindest promise anyone had ever heard. But there was one part Abraham did not know. God did not tell him exactly where the new land was. He did not point to it on any map, or show him the whole long road, or tell him how many days it would take. God simply said, "Come. Follow me. And I will show you the way as you go."

Now, Abraham sat very still and thought about that. It is not an easy thing — to set out when you cannot see where you are going. He could not picture the new land. He did not know if the journey would be long or short, easy or hard. He only had a voice, and a promise, and the kindness in it.

But here is the thing about Abraham. He did not need to see the whole road. He looked up at the sky, and he remembered that the One who was speaking to him had made every star up there. And he thought, "If God is leading me, then I do not have to be afraid. I do not know the way ahead — but I know the One who is leading me."

And so Abraham trusted God.

The next morning, he told Sarah. "We are going," he said. "God has called us to a new land." And though they did not know where, they began to get ready. They gathered their animals, the gentle sheep and the swaying camels. They packed up everything they owned, and folded their tents, and tied their bundles tight.

Then came the hardest moment of all — the goodbye. Abraham looked one last time at the home he had always known, the well and the hills and the evening light. He breathed it all in. And then, holding tight to God's promise, he turned, and he stepped out the door, and he set off down a road he had never walked before.

So Abraham and Sarah began to travel. They walked over rolling hills. They crossed wide, open lands, where the wind moved the grass like waves. Some days the sun was warm on their faces. Some days they were tired, and their feet were sore, and Abraham wondered, "Is it much farther now?" But each time the worry crept in, he remembered. He did not know the way — but he knew the One who was leading him.

When the sky grew dark, they would stop and set up their tents. And night after night, Abraham stepped out under the great wide sky. He tipped his head back and looked up, and there they were — more stars than he could ever, ever count. Thousands and thousands of tiny lights, shining and shining. And every single time, he remembered God's promise. "A family as many as the stars," God had said. Standing there beneath all that shining, Abraham smiled, and his heart grew quiet and sure.

In the morning, the sun would rise over the hills, and they would fold up the tents again, and load the animals, and follow God a little farther down the road. One step. Then another. Then another. They did not have to see the whole journey. They only had to take the next step, and trust that God was out in front, leading the way.

And so the days went by, and the miles went by, and still they followed.

And at last, after their long, long journey, they came to the place God had been leading them to all along. Abraham stood at the top of a rise and looked out, and there it was spread before him — a good land, green and wide, with room to grow. He knew it the moment he saw it. This was the place.

"This is the land," God told him gently. "I am giving it to you, and to your family after you."

Abraham looked around at the new land that would become his home, and his heart was full, full and thankful. He thought back over the whole long road — every hill, every tired evening, every night beneath the counting-stars. He had not been able to see any of it ahead of time. Not one single step. He had walked the whole way without knowing.

But God had known. God had seen the road from the very beginning, all the way to the end. And God had never left him for a moment — not on the hard days, not in the dark nights, not for one single step of the journey. God had been right there beside him, the whole time, all along the way.

For that is who God is. He is the One who goes before you, and the One who walks beside you, when you cannot yet see the way. He holds the road in his hands, and he holds you, and he says, ever so gently, "Come. Follow me. I will show you."
