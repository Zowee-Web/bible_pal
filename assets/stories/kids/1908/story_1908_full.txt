The Shepherd Boy Becomes King

When David was still a shepherd boy, out on the hills with his father's sheep, God had already chosen him for something big. The prophet Samuel had come and poured oil over David's head, right there in front of his older brothers, because God had looked past all of them and chosen the youngest. One day, God had said, this shepherd boy would be the king.

But it did not happen all at once. David had to wait — for years and years and years.

So he kept living his life, and kept trusting God, while he waited. He cared for his sheep on the quiet hills, leading them to green grass and cool water. He played his harp so beautifully that he was called to play for the king himself, and his gentle music could calm a troubled heart. When a giant frightened the whole army, David stepped forward and was brave, because he knew God was with him. He made a dear and faithful friend. And through all his days, the happy ones and the hard ones, he kept singing his songs to God.

But there were hard days too. For a long while, David even had to run and hide, far from home, because the old king grew jealous and wanted to harm him. Through all of it — the happy days and the frightening ones — David held on to God's promise. He kept on trusting, even when the waiting felt very, very long.

And through all those years, God stayed close to David — just as God had stayed close to him out among the sheep. God was never in a hurry. He was getting everything ready, quietly, in his own good time.

Then at last, the day came.

From every corner of the land, the people gathered together — more than David could count, a great glad crowd of them, family after family streaming in until the whole place was full. They remembered the shepherd boy. They remembered the songs he sang, and the giant he had faced, and how God had been with him all his life. They remembered how he had cared for them, the way a good shepherd cares for his sheep. And all together, with one voice, they said, "You will be our king."

So they placed the crown upon David's head. The shepherd boy — the one who used to carry little lambs in his arms and sing to God beneath the stars — was now the king of all God's people. Imagine the cheering that day. Imagine the music and the dancing.

And David did not forget where he had come from, or who had brought him there. The same God who had been with him among the sheep was with him still, now that he was king. The long wait was over. And David could see it so clearly now: God had made that promise to a shepherd boy long ago — and God had kept it. God always keeps his promises.
