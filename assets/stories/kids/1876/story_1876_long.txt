Jesus Shines Like the Sun

One day, Jesus said to three of his closest friends — Peter, and James, and John — "Come with me." And he led them away from all the others, toward a tall mountain, so that they could be quiet, and pray, just the four of them together.

It was a long climb to the top. Up, and up, and up they went, along the winding path, higher and higher above the towns, and the fields, and the little villages far below. By the time they reached the very top, the three friends were tired out from the climb, and the evening was coming on, and everything was hushed and still up there, with the wide sky stretching out all around them. Jesus went off a little way, to pray. And the three friends, weary from the long climb, felt their eyes growing heavy.

But then — all at once — something happened that woke them right up. Something so amazing that they would remember it for the rest of their whole lives.

Jesus began to shine.

It started in his face. It grew brighter, and brighter, and brighter still — until it was shining like the sun itself. And then his clothes began to shine too. They turned a dazzling, gleaming white — whiter than fresh fallen snow, whiter than anything in all the world, glowing as though they were woven out of pure light. The three friends scrambled up and stared, their eyes wide and their mouths open. The whole mountaintop seemed to be filled with the light that poured out of him. They had never, in all their lives, seen anything half so beautiful, or half so wonderful.

And that was not all. For suddenly, the friends saw that Jesus was not alone anymore. Two other people were there now, standing beside him, talking quietly with him, the way old friends do. And somehow, the three friends knew just who they were: they were Moses, and Elijah — two of God's great and faithful friends, from long, long ago. And here they were, on the mountaintop, speaking with shining Jesus.

Peter was so amazed, and so excited, and so full of wonder, that he could hardly think straight at all. And so he blurted out the very first thing that popped into his head. "Lord!" he said. "It is so good to be here! This is wonderful! Here — let me build three little tents, one for each of you. One for you, and one for Moses, and one for Elijah — so that we can all just stay up here together!"

But even while Peter was still talking, something gentle and wonderful happened. A bright, shining cloud came drifting softly down from above, and it settled all around them, and covered them over. The friends could hardly see. And then, from inside that bright cloud, there came a voice.

It was the voice of God himself — God, the Father.

It was not a loud or a frightening voice, not at all. It was warm, and deep, and gentle, and full to the very brim with love. And this is what the voice said:

"This is my Son, the one I love. I am so very pleased with him. Listen to him."

When the three friends heard the voice of God, they were filled with such awe that they fell down with their faces to the ground. And there they lay, hardly daring to move at all.

But then — as softly, and as gently, as it had come — the bright cloud lifted, and floated away. And everything was quiet once more. The friends felt a gentle hand resting upon their shoulders. They looked up. And there was Jesus — just Jesus, all by himself now, looking exactly as he always did. Moses and Elijah were gone. And Jesus was smiling down at them, kindly.

"Get up," he said softly. "Do not be afraid." So the three friends stood up, their hearts still pounding with wonder. And together, the four of them turned, and began the long walk back down the mountain.

All the way down, the three friends were very quiet. Their hearts were full, right up to the brim, with everything they had just seen, and everything they had just heard. And for the rest of their lives, whenever they closed their eyes, they could still see it — the dazzling light, and the bright cloud — and they could still hear that gentle, loving voice from heaven.

They did not tell anyone about it, not for a long, long while. They simply kept it, treasured up safe inside them. But not one of those three friends ever, ever forgot the day they climbed a tall mountain with Jesus — and saw him shine as bright as the sun. And most of all, they never forgot what God himself had said, up there in the shining cloud, about his own dear Son:

"This is my Son, the one I love. Listen to him."
