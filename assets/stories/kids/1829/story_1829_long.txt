The Night in Bethlehem

Not long after the angel had come to visit Mary, with his wonderful news, the time drew near for her baby to be born — the very special baby that God had promised, his own dear Son.

Now, just at that time, far away in a grand palace, a powerful ruler named Caesar made a brand-new rule. He wanted to count every single person in all the land. And so he said that everyone must travel back to the town their family had come from, long ago, to have their names written down in his great big book.

For Mary and her husband, Joseph, that meant a long, long journey — all the way to a little hillside town called Bethlehem.

It was a hard way to travel, especially for Mary, with her baby coming so very soon. There were no cars in those days, and no fast roads. So Mary rode gently on the back of a patient little donkey, and Joseph walked along beside her, leading the way and watching over her. The road climbed up the hills, and wound back down again, mile after dusty mile. The days were long and tiring, and at night they slept beneath the open stars. Mary grew very weary. But Joseph took good care of her, and step by step, day by day, they came closer.

At last, as the sun was setting on the very last day, they saw it: the little town of Bethlehem, perched upon its hill, with its lamps beginning to twinkle on, one by one, in the gentle dusk.

But oh — what a crowd! Because of Caesar's new rule, people had come from far and wide, and Bethlehem was fuller than it had ever, ever been. Every house was packed. Every room was taken. And poor Mary was so very tired now, and the baby was coming very, very soon.

Joseph knocked on the first door he came to. "I am so sorry," said the man inside, with a tired shake of his head. "We have no room." So Joseph tried the next house, and then the next, and then the next. But everywhere he knocked, the answer was the same. "No room here. No room. I am so sorry — there is simply no room."

Whatever would they do? Mary needed somewhere to rest, and to rest soon.

Then, at last, one kind person looked at the weary travelers, and his heart was moved. "I have no room inside my house," he said gently. "But I do have a stable, where the animals sleep. It is warm in there, and dry, and out of the cold night air. You are welcome to stay in it." And Mary and Joseph thanked him with all their hearts, for it was just exactly what they needed.

So they made their way into the little stable. And it was cozy inside — warm, and snug, with the gentle cows chewing slowly, and the woolly sheep nestled down in the corners, and the soft, sweet smell of fresh hay all around. And there, in that humble little stable, on that quiet and holy night, Mary's baby was born.

She wrapped him up carefully in soft strips of cloth, snug and warm, the way mothers did in those days, to keep their babies cozy and safe. And because there was no little bed for him anywhere, Mary looked about, and her eyes fell upon the manger — the low wooden box where the animals' hay was kept. She filled it up with clean, soft hay, and there she gently laid her baby down to sleep. It made a perfect little bed.

And she named him Jesus, just exactly as the angel had told her she would.

Outside, the night was deep, and still, and peaceful. The stars shone down, bright and clear, over the quiet rooftops of Bethlehem. And inside the little stable, all was calm. The animals rested. Joseph kept his loving watch over them both. And Mary looked down at her tiny, sleeping baby — so small, and so new, and yet God's own Son, come at last into the world — and she held him close to her heart.

She did not say very much at all. She simply gazed down at his little sleeping face, and listened to his soft, gentle breathing, and treasured up every single moment, deep down inside her heart. And she wondered at it all, filled with a quiet and peaceful joy.

It was the most wonderful night the world had ever known. And it had come not in a grand palace, and not in a great city — but in a little stable, on a quiet hill, in the small town of Bethlehem. Where a tiny baby slept softly in the hay, watched over by his mother, and his father, and the gentle animals, and every shining star in all of heaven.

So sleep gently now, little one, just as the baby Jesus slept — wrapped up warm and safe, on that first holy night, so very long ago.
