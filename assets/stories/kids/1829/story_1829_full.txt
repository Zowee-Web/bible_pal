The Night in Bethlehem

Not long after the angel had visited her, the time came near for Mary to have her baby — the special baby God had promised, his very own Son. And just at that time, Mary and her husband, Joseph, had to make a long journey to a faraway little town called Bethlehem.

It was a long, slow way to travel. Mary rode gently on the back of a little donkey, and Joseph walked beside her, leading the way. The road wound up the hills and down again, over many dusty miles, and the journey took days and days. Mary was tired, but Joseph took good care of her, and at last, as evening fell, they saw the little town of Bethlehem ahead.

But oh, what a crowd! Bethlehem was full to bursting, more crowded than anyone had ever seen it. People had come from far and wide, and every house and every room was already full.

Joseph knocked on a door. "I am sorry," said the person inside. "There is no room here." So Joseph knocked on another, and another, and another. But every time, the answer was the same. "No room. No room. I am so sorry — there is no room."

Poor Mary was so weary, and the baby was coming very soon. Whatever would they do?

Then, at last, one kind person had an idea. "I have no room in my house," he said gently. "But you may stay in my stable, where the animals sleep. It is warm and dry, and you will be out of the cold." And Mary and Joseph were thankful, for it was just what they needed.

So they went into the little stable. It was warm there, and snug, with the gentle cows and the woolly sheep, and the sweet smell of fresh hay. And that night — that quiet, holy night — in the stable in Bethlehem, Mary's baby was born.

She wrapped him up warm and snug in soft cloths, to keep him cozy. And because there was no little bed for him, she laid him down to sleep in a manger — the low wooden box where the animals' hay was kept. It made a perfect little bed, soft and sweet.

And she named him Jesus, just exactly as the angel had said she would.

Outside, the night was still, and the stars shone down bright and clear over the little town. Inside the stable, all was quiet and peaceful. The animals rested. Joseph watched over them both. And Mary looked down at her tiny, sleeping baby — God's own Son, come at last into the world — and she held it all gently in her heart, full of quiet wonder and love.
