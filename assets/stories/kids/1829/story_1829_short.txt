The Night in Bethlehem

Mary was going to have her baby very soon — the special baby the angel had promised, God's own Son. And just at that time, she and her husband, Joseph, had to take a long journey to a little town called Bethlehem.

It was a long, slow way. Mary rode gently on a donkey, and Joseph walked beside her, and the road went up and down the hills for many miles.

When they finally reached Bethlehem, the little town was crowded, full of people. Joseph knocked on door after door, looking for a place to stay. But every house was full. "I am sorry," each person said. "There is no room."

At last, a kind person took pity on them, and offered them the only place left in all of Bethlehem — a stable, where the animals were kept, warm and snug. So that is where Mary and Joseph stayed, with the gentle cows and sheep, on the soft, sweet hay.

And there, in that quiet stable, on that holy night, Mary's baby was born. She wrapped him up warm and snug, and she laid him down to sleep in a manger — the little wooden box where the animals' hay was kept.

She named him Jesus, just as the angel had said.

The animals were quiet. The stars shone bright outside. And Mary looked at her tiny, sleeping baby, God's own Son, come at last into the world — and she held it all gently in her heart, full of quiet wonder and love.
