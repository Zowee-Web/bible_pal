Sarah's Promised Baby

Abraham and his wife, Sarah, were very, very old. Their hair was white, and their faces were soft with wrinkles. And though they had always, always wanted a child of their own, they had never had a baby. Now they were far too old, or so they thought.

But God had made Abraham a promise.

One warm day, three visitors came to Abraham's tent, and one of them carried a message from God himself. "When I come back next year," he said, "Sarah will have a baby boy of her very own."

Now Sarah was listening, just inside the tent. And when she heard it, she laughed quietly to herself. "Me? A baby? But I am so old!" It seemed far too wonderful to be true.

"Is anything too hard for God?" the visitor asked, kindly.

And it was not. For God always keeps his promises.

So a year went by. And then — just as God had said — Sarah had a baby boy! A real, tiny, wriggling, wonderful baby, all their own. Old Abraham and old Sarah could hardly believe their happiness.

They named the baby Isaac, a name that means "laughter." And Sarah held her little son close, and her eyes shone with happy tears.

"God has made me laugh," she said, "and everyone who hears about this will laugh and be glad along with me!"

And so the tent that had been quiet for so long was filled, at last, with the happiest sound of all — the sound of a tiny baby, and of laughter, and of two old people thanking God for keeping his promise.
