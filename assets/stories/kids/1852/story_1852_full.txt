Sarah's Promised Baby

Abraham and his wife, Sarah, were very, very old. Their hair had gone soft and white, and their faces were full of gentle wrinkles, and they had been married for more years than they could count. And in all those long years, though they had always, always longed for a child of their own, they had never had a baby.

Now, a long time before, God had made Abraham a wonderful promise. One night God had taken him outside and said, "Look up at the sky. Can you count all the stars? That is how big your family will be one day." Abraham had believed God. But the years had gone slowly by, and still no baby had come — and now he and Sarah were so old that having a child seemed quite impossible. Yet God had not forgotten his promise. Not for one moment.

One warm afternoon, as Abraham sat in the cool shade beside his tent, three visitors came walking up the path. Abraham jumped up to welcome them. He hurried to bring them cool water to drink and a good meal to eat, and they rested in the shade of the great tree. And one of the visitors carried a message that came straight from God himself.

"When I come back this way next year," he said, "your wife Sarah will have a baby boy of her very own."

Now, Sarah was just inside the tent, listening. And when she heard those words, she laughed softly to herself. "Me? A baby? But I am so old now!" It seemed far, far too wonderful to ever be true.

"Is anything too hard for God?" the visitor asked, gently.

And of course, the answer was no. Nothing at all is too hard for God. For God always, always keeps his promises.

So the seasons turned. Summer became autumn, autumn became winter, and winter became spring. A whole year went by, and all the while Abraham and Sarah waited, and wondered, and hoped.

And then — just exactly as God had said — Sarah had a baby boy! A real, tiny, wriggling, wonderful baby, all their very own. Old Abraham and old Sarah held him close, and looked at his little face, and could hardly believe how happy they were.

They named the baby Isaac, a name that means "laughter." And Sarah held her little son against her, and her eyes shone bright with happy tears.

"God has made me laugh!" she said. "And everyone who hears about this will laugh and be glad right along with me!"

And it was true. All their family and friends came to see the new baby, and the whole camp was full of smiles. The tent that had been so quiet, for so very many years, was filled at last with the happiest sound in all the world — the sound of a tiny baby cooing, and of laughter ringing out, and of two old people thanking God, with all their hearts, for keeping his wonderful promise.
