The Day the King Danced

Some days your heart feels so glad and so full that you simply cannot hold still. Your feet want to leap, and your whole self wants to sing. That is exactly how King David felt on one wonderful morning — because the Ark of God was coming home at last. The Ark was a very special chest, shining gold in the sunlight. It was the most precious thing God's people had, for it helped them remember that God was always, always with them. For a long, long time the Ark had been far from the city. But now, on this day, it was coming home.

David's whole heart was glad — glad all the way up to the very brim, the way a cup feels when it is filled so full it is about to spill over. He had waited so long for this day! So he sent word to all his people: "Come! Come and help me bring the Ark of God home!" And they came — crowds and crowds of them, lining the whole long road.

Then the celebration began. And oh, what a celebration it was!

Trumpets rang out, bright and golden, up into the blue sky. Horns blew long and low. Harps sang their happy songs, and the cymbals crashed together — clash, clash, clash! The people sang at the very top of their voices. They clapped. They shouted for joy, louder and louder, until the whole road seemed to ring and shimmer with happiness. Up the hill they went, all together, walking the Ark of God toward home.

But of all the glad and happy people on that road, do you know who was the gladdest of all? It was the king himself.

David was so full of joy that he simply could not walk along like an ordinary man. The happiness inside him was too big to hold. It bubbled up and up until it spilled right out — and so, right there in the middle of the road, in front of every single person, King David began to dance.

He leaped high into the air. He spun, and he twirled, and he leaped again. He danced with all his might — with his whole heart, with everything he had — down the road, before God. He was too thankful to stand still. He was too happy to stop. Round and round he went, dancing in the dust, while the music played and the people shouted and the golden Ark came up the road behind him.

A king! Dancing like a child! And King David did not mind even a little bit. He was not worried about looking grand or important. He only wanted God to see how glad his heart was — how very, very thankful he was that God was coming home to be near his people again.

At last, the long road ended. They carried the Ark of God to a special tent that had been made ready just for it, and they set it down, ever so carefully, in its place. And there it rested at last — home, right in the very middle of the city, where the people could remember every day that God was with them. The waiting was over. The Ark had come home.

You might think David was finished now. But he had one more glad thing left to do.

He turned around and looked at all the people — the whole enormous crowd who had sung and shouted and celebrated with him. And because his heart was so full and so happy, King David wanted everyone else to feel happy too. So he gave a gift. Not to a few people. To every single one.

To every man, he gave a gift. To every woman, he gave a gift. And to every child — yes, to every little child there — he gave a gift too. He shared out good bread, and sweet things to eat, so that not one person in all that great crowd went home empty-handed. Every family carried home a present from the king.

And so the people went home glad, all of them, with their gifts in their arms and the music still ringing in their ears. For as long as they lived, they would remember this day — the day the Ark of God came home, and the king danced in the road for joy.

Because there are some days when your heart gets so full, and so glad, and so thankful to God, that you simply cannot keep it inside. You have to sing. You have to shout. You have to dance, right where you are, with everything you have.

And that is a good and beautiful thing. For God loves a happy heart. He smiles to see his children that glad — leaping and singing and spinning — when they celebrate him with their whole, joyful, dancing hearts.
