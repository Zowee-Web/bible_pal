The Night Jacob Held On

Night had fallen, and Jacob was all alone.

The next morning, after many long years away, Jacob was going to see his brother again. And Jacob was afraid. Long ago, Jacob had wronged his brother, and then he had run far away to escape his brother's anger. All through the years since, that old trouble had sat heavy in Jacob's heart. And now, at last, they were about to meet. Would his brother still be angry? Would everything be all right? Jacob did not know what the new day would bring, and the not-knowing made his heart pound.

So that night, Jacob sent everyone he loved on ahead of him, across the river — all his family, and everything he had. And then he was by himself, in the dark, by the quiet water.

But Jacob was not as alone as he thought.

For in the darkness, someone came and was there with him. And all through that long night, the two of them held on to each other, and Jacob would not let go.

Hour after hour the night wore on, dark and quiet, with only the soft sound of the river going by. Jacob grew tired — so tired his whole body ached — but still he held on tight, with all the strength he had left. He would not give up. He would not let go. And at last, when the black sky began to turn soft and gray with the very first light of morning, the One in the dark said to him, "Let me go now. The sun is coming up."

But Jacob only held on tighter. "I will not let you go," he said, "unless you bless me." Then Jacob knew. He was holding on to God.

And God did bless him. "What is your name?" God asked. "Jacob," he answered. "From now on," said God, "your name will be something new. You will be called Israel — for you have held on to God, and you would not let go."

A brand-new name, given to Jacob in the soft gray light of dawn — a name to remind him, every time he heard it, of the night he held on to God and would not let go.

When the sun came up, Jacob walked on toward his brother. He still did not know exactly what the day would hold. But he was not afraid the way he had been the night before. For he had held on to God through the long, dark night — and God had stayed right there with him the whole time, and had not let go either, and had sent him into the morning with a blessing.

And God is still like that. Even on the darkest, most frightening night, when you feel completely alone, God is closer to you than you could ever know. You can hold on to him with all your might. And he will stay right beside you, and bless you, and carry you all the way to morning.
