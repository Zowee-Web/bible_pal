The Night Jacob Held On

Night had fallen, and Jacob was all alone by the river.

For many long years, Jacob had lived far away in another land. Now, at last, he was coming home. But coming home meant something that made his heart pound: in the morning, he would see his big brother again. And long, long ago, there had been trouble between them — Jacob had done something wrong, and his brother had been very angry, so angry that Jacob had run away.

Now they were about to meet once more. And Jacob was afraid. Would his brother still be angry after all these years? Would everything be all right? Jacob simply did not know. And not knowing is one of the hardest, most worrisome feelings there is.

So as the sun went down, Jacob did a careful thing. He sent everyone he loved on ahead of him, across the shallow river — all his children, and all his family, and everything he owned — so that they would be safe on the other side. And then Jacob was by himself, in the dark, beside the quiet, whispering water. Alone with his worry. Alone with the long night ahead.

But Jacob was not as alone as he thought he was.

For there, in the deep darkness, someone came and was with him. And all through that long night, the two of them held on to one another — and Jacob would not, could not, let go.

Hour after hour the night wore on. The stars wheeled slowly overhead, and the river kept whispering by in the dark. Jacob grew tired, so very tired, and his arms ached, and more than once he must have wanted to give up and rest. But he did not understand everything that was happening that night — only this one thing, deep in his bones: he must not let go. Whatever else happened, he would hold on. And so, through the cold and the dark and the long, long hours, he did. He held on tight with all the strength he had left. He would not give up. He would not let go.

And then, at last, the black night sky began to soften. A thin line of gray light crept up along the edge of the world. Morning was coming. And the One there in the dark said to Jacob, "Let me go now. The sun is coming up."

But Jacob held on even tighter than before. "I will not let you go," he said, "unless you bless me." For by now, deep down, Jacob understood something wonderful and a little bit frightening. Then Jacob knew. All night long, he had been holding on to God.

And God did not pull away. God blessed him.

"What is your name?" God asked him gently. "Jacob," he answered. "From now on," said God, "you will have a new name. You will be called Israel — for you have held on to God, and you would not let go."

A brand-new name, given in the soft gray light of dawn. A name to remind Jacob, every time he heard it, of the night he held on to God and did not let go.

And then the sun rose up, round and golden, over the river. Jacob got to his feet. And he walked on, toward the place where his brother was waiting.

He still did not know exactly what would happen when they met. But Jacob was not afraid the way he had been the night before. Something had changed in him in the dark. The worry that had sat so heavy in his heart for all those years did not feel so heavy anymore. He had held on to God, and God had blessed him, and now he could walk toward the hard thing instead of running away from it. For he had held on to God all through the long, frightening night — and God had stayed right there with him the whole time, holding on too, and had not left him alone for even one minute, and had sent him out into the morning with a blessing.

And God is still exactly like that, even now. On the darkest, most frightening night — the night when you feel most completely alone — God is closer to you than you could ever know. You can hold on to him with all your might, and not let go. And he will stay right there beside you, all the way through, and bless you, and carry you safely into the morning light.
