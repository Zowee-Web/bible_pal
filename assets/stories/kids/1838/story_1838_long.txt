Esther's Brave Choice

In a faraway land, in a palace full of marble floors and golden lamps and curtains the color of the sky, there lived a young queen named Esther.

She wore beautiful robes, and servants hurried to help her, and everyone bowed low when she walked by. But Esther carried a secret deep in her heart — a secret that not even the king knew. Esther was one of God's people. She had come from a faraway home, far from this grand palace, to live in this land.

One day, dreadful news reached Esther in the palace. A proud and powerful man named Haman had made a cruel plan. He did not like God's people, and he wanted to harm all of them — every family, in every town, all across the whole kingdom. And that meant Esther's own people. It meant her own family, too.

Esther's cousin Mordecai, who had raised her like a father, sent her an urgent message. "Esther," it said, "you must go to the king, and beg him to save our people. Who knows? Perhaps God made you the queen for a moment exactly like this one."

But Esther was afraid — and she had good reason to be. There was a strict rule in the palace: no one at all, not even the queen herself, was allowed to walk into the king's great throne room unless the king had called for them first. Anyone who came without being called could be in terrible danger. And the king had not called for Esther.

So Esther sat very still, and she thought, and she thought. Her heart felt small, and frightened, and unsure. Going to the king might save her people. But it might cost her everything. What should she do?

Slowly, in the middle of all her fear, a quiet courage began to grow inside her. "I will go to the king," she said at last. "Even though I am afraid. Even though it is against the rule. If it goes badly — well, I will go anyway."

But before she went, Esther did the most important thing of all. She did not rush off on her own strength. She stopped, and she prayed. She asked God to help her and to make her brave. And she sent a message to all of her people: "Pray for me. Do not eat, and pray, for three whole days and nights — and I and my servants will do the same."

So for three days, all across the land, God's people prayed. In their homes and in their villages, mothers and fathers and children all prayed for their brave young queen. And in the palace, the frightened young queen prayed too — quietly, deeply, hour after hour, and day after day — asking God to be with her, and to make her brave.

And slowly, as she prayed, something began to change inside her. Her trembling heart grew steady. She was still a little afraid — but now she was not facing it alone. God was with her, and her people were with her, and that made all the difference in the world.

Then, on the third day, Esther rose up. She put on her finest royal robes. She took one long, deep breath. And she walked — step, by step, by step — down the long hall and into the great throne room, where the king sat high upon his golden throne.

Her heart was pounding like a drum. This was the moment. She had prayed, and now she walked anyway. Every single step took courage. But she did not turn back — one step, and then another, and then another, all the way down the long hall toward the throne.

The king looked up. And when he saw his queen standing there, he smiled. He reached out and held his golden scepter toward her — and in the palace, that was the most welcome sign of all. It meant: Come close. I am glad you are here. You are safe.

So Esther came near, and she told the king everything — about the cruel plan, and about her people who were in danger. And the king listened to every word. And he stopped the cruel plan completely, and he kept Esther and all her people safe and sound.

One young queen — frightened, but brave enough to pray, and then brave enough to go — had helped to save them all. And God had placed her in that palace, all along, for just such a time as this.
