Noah's Rainbow

For many days and many nights, the rain had fallen, and Noah's great big boat had floated safely on the wide, wide water. There was water as far as anyone could see, in every direction. But inside the boat, Noah and his family and all the animals — two by two — were warm and dry and safe together.

Then one morning, Noah noticed something different. He listened. The drumming of the rain on the roof had stopped. The world outside was quiet and still. And with a gentle bump, the great boat came to rest on the very top of a mountain.

Noah wanted to know if the water had begun to go down. So he opened a window and let a little dove fly out. The dove flew far and wide over the water, but there was nowhere yet for her small feet to land. So she flew all the way back to the boat, and Noah reached out and gently took her in.

He waited seven days. Then he sent the little dove out again. This time, when she fluttered back, she carried something in her beak: a fresh green olive leaf! Noah held it and smiled. Somewhere out there, in the wide world, trees were growing again.

He waited seven more days, and he sent the dove out one last time. And this time, she did not come back at all. And Noah understood why. There was dry land now — grass and trees and hills — a whole new world for one little dove to make her home.

So Noah pushed open the great door of the boat. And out they all came, into the fresh air and the warm sunshine: Noah and his family, and the animals two by two, blinking and stretching and stepping onto the good, dry ground.

And then God did something so beautiful that everyone stopped to look. He stretched a great rainbow across the whole sky — red and orange and yellow and green and blue and purple — glowing softly against the last of the clouds.

"This rainbow is my promise to you," God said. "Whenever it appears, you will remember: I will always care for the earth, and for every living thing upon it."

And to this very day, when the rain clears away and a rainbow shines out across the sky, it is God, keeping his promise still.
