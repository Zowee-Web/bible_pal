Noah's Rainbow

For many days and many nights, the rain had fallen, and Noah's great big boat had floated safely on the wide, wide water. There was water as far as anyone could see, in every direction. But inside the boat, Noah and his family and all the animals — two by two — were warm and dry and safe together.

It was a long, long time to wait. The lions slept in their straw, and the little birds tucked their heads beneath their wings, and the children leaned against their mother and listened to the rain on the roof. Noah fed every animal, the big ones and the small ones, and he spoke to them softly so they would not be afraid. "God is keeping us safe," he told them. "We only have to wait."

Then one morning, Noah noticed something different. He stopped, and he listened. The drumming of the rain on the roof had stopped. The world outside was quiet and still. And with a gentle bump, the great boat came to rest on the very top of a mountain.

Noah pressed his ear against the wall of the boat and listened again. No rain. No splashing. Only the soft creak of the wood and the breathing of the sleeping animals. His heart began to hope. Could it be that the water was going down at last?

Noah wanted to know for certain. So he opened a window, and he took a little dove in his hands, and he let her fly out into the morning. The dove flew far and wide over the water, beating her small wings, searching and searching. But there was nowhere yet for her little feet to land. So she flew all the way back to the boat. And Noah leaned out of the window, and reached out, and gently took her in.

"Not yet, little one," he said, and he held her close so she would be warm.

So Noah waited. He counted the days, one by one — and waiting is one of the hardest things of all. The family kept on caring for the animals. The sun rose and set seven times. And on the seventh day, Noah carried the little dove to the window once more, and opened his hands, and let her go.

This time, the whole family watched the window. They waited. And when the dove came fluttering back, there was something in her beak — something small, and green, and alive. It was a fresh olive leaf!

Noah held it ever so carefully, and he smiled. "Look," he whispered. "Look what she has brought us." For a leaf could only come from a tree, and a tree could only grow on dry ground. Somewhere out there, in the wide world, the land was waking up again. Somewhere out there, things were growing.

He waited seven more days. The waiting was easier now, because hope had come into the boat with that little green leaf. And on the seventh day, he sent the dove out one last time.

This time, she did not come back at all.

The children looked for her, but Noah only smiled and shook his head, because he understood. There was dry land now — real, solid ground, with grass and trees and hills. There was a whole new world out there for one little dove to make her home. She had flown off to begin her new life, and that was a very good thing.

Then God spoke to Noah, and said, "Come out of the boat — you and your family and every animal with you."

So Noah went to the great door of the boat, and he pushed, and pushed, and it swung wide open. Light came pouring in. Fresh air came rushing in, smelling of wet earth and growing things. And out they all came: Noah and his wife, and his sons and their wives, and the animals two by two — the elephants and the rabbits, the sparrows and the sheep — blinking and stretching and stepping out onto the good, dry ground.

The children spread their arms wide and turned their faces to the sun. The animals scampered and galloped and flew away across the green hills, free at last. The whole earth was washed clean and new, and everyone stood very still, breathing it all in.

And then God did something so beautiful that everyone stopped to look. He stretched a great rainbow across the whole sky — red and orange and yellow and green and blue and purple — glowing softly against the last of the clouds. It curved up high and came down again, like a great shining bridge from one side of the world to the other.

Noah and his family looked up, and nobody said a word. They had never seen anything like it.

"This rainbow is my promise to you," God said. "Whenever it appears, you will remember: I will always care for the earth, and for every living thing upon it."

And Noah remembered all the long days of waiting, and the little dove, and the green leaf, and the door swinging open into the light. God had kept them safe through all of it. God had brought them home. And now God had given them this beautiful promise hung across the sky, where everyone could see it.

So whenever the rain falls and you have to wait, and wait, and wait, you can remember Noah, and you can remember the rainbow.

For to this very day, when the rain clears away and a rainbow shines out across the sky, it is God, keeping his promise still.
