The Ten Commandments

God's people had come a long way out of Egypt, and they camped at the foot of a great, tall mountain. Its top was wrapped in cloud, and thunder rumbled softly all around it. The people knew that God was very near.

God called Moses to come up the mountain. And there, God gave Moses a wonderful gift for the people — not a present wrapped in paper, but something even better. God gave them good ways to live, so they could live together happily — loving him, and loving each other.

"Love God most of all," God said, "more than anything else in the whole wide world. For I am the God who loves you, and brought you safely here."

"Take a special day each week to rest," God said, "and to be glad together."

"Love your mother and your father, and treat them with kindness."

"Always tell the truth. Do not take what belongs to someone else. And do not hurt one another — instead, be kind."

God gave ten of these good ways in all. They were not meant to be hard. They were like a warm and loving path through a forest — a safe, good way to walk, so that no one would get lost, and everyone could live together in peace.

Moses carried God's good words down the mountain to the people. And the people were glad. For now they knew how to live as one big family — loving God, and loving each other, every single day.
