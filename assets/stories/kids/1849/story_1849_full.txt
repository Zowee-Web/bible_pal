The Ten Commandments

God's people had come a long, long way out of Egypt. Many of them had been slaves their whole lives, and now they were free. They walked for many days through the wide, dry desert, following God every step of the way, until at last they came to a great, tall mountain called Sinai. And there, at the foot of the mountain, they set up all their tents and made their camp.

But this was no ordinary mountain. Its high top was wrapped in thick, swirling cloud. Thunder rumbled softly all around it, and now and then the ground itself seemed to tremble. The people looked up with wide, wondering eyes — because they could tell that God himself was very, very near.

Then God called Moses to come up the mountain and meet with him. So Moses began to climb, up and up, until he disappeared into the cloud. And the people waited down below.

There, high on the mountain, God gave Moses a wonderful gift for the people. It was not a present wrapped up in paper and ribbon. It was something far, far better. God gave them good ways to live — so that they could live together happily, loving him, and loving one another, all their days.

"Love God most of all," God said, "more than anything else in the whole wide world. For I am the God who loves you, and who brought you safely out of Egypt."

"Take a special day, every single week, to stop your work and rest," God said, "and to be glad together."

"Love your mother and your father, and treat them with kindness and care."

"Always tell the truth. Do not take what belongs to someone else. And do not hurt one another — instead, be kind, and look after each other."

Every one of these good ways was really about love. Loving God with all your heart, and loving the people all around you — that was the heart of every single one.

God gave ten of these good ways in all. And he wrote them down, with his very own hand, on two flat tablets of stone, so that the people would always have them and never forget. They were not meant to be hard, or heavy, or frightening. They were like a warm and loving path through a forest — a safe and gentle way to walk, so that nobody would ever get lost, and everybody could live together in peace.

When Moses came back down the mountain at last, he carried God's good words carefully in his arms, down to all the waiting people. And the people were so very glad. For now they knew how to live — not as strangers who pushed and quarreled, but as one big, happy family. Loving God. And loving each other.

And whenever they followed God's good ways, the whole camp became a kinder, gladder, more peaceful place. Because that is exactly what God's good ways are for.
