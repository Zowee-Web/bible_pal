Daniel in the Lions' Den

In a great and faraway kingdom, there lived a man named Daniel, and he loved God with all his heart. Three times every day — in the morning, at noon, and in the evening — Daniel would kneel down by his open window and pray to God. It was his very favorite thing to do, and he had done it for as long as he could remember. Praying to God was as much a part of Daniel's day as the sunrise.

Now, the king of that land liked Daniel more than all his other helpers, because Daniel was wise, and good, and could always be trusted. The king was planning to put Daniel in charge of the whole kingdom. But this made some of the other men terribly jealous. They did not want Daniel set above them, and they began to look for a way to get him into trouble.

But try as they might, they could not find a single bad thing Daniel had done. He was too honest, too good. "The only way we will ever catch Daniel," they finally said, "is to use his love for his God against him." And so they came up with a sneaky, sneaky plan.

They went to the king with smiling faces and flattering words, and they tricked him into making a new rule. For thirty whole days, they said, no one in all the land was allowed to pray to anyone at all — except the king himself. And anyone who broke the rule would be thrown into a den full of lions. It sounded grand and important, so the king signed the rule, not knowing the trap that was hidden inside it.

Now Daniel heard about the new rule. But Daniel still loved God, just as much as ever. So Daniel did exactly what he had always done: he went home, and he knelt down by his open window, and he prayed to God, giving thanks just as before. He was not loud about it, and he was not showing off. He simply would not stop talking to the God he loved.

The jealous men had been watching and waiting, and the moment they saw him, they hurried off to tell the king. And now the trap snapped shut. The king suddenly understood what he had done — and oh, he was so sorry. He did not want to hurt Daniel at all! All day long, until the sun went down, the king worked and searched and tried his very hardest to find a way to save his friend. But the rule had been signed, and it could not be unmade. So at last, with a heavy, heavy heart, the king gave the order, and Daniel was lowered down into the den of lions.

"May the God you serve, the God you never stop praying to, keep you safe," the king said softly into the dark. And then a great stone was rolled across the mouth of the den.

All night long, the king could not sleep. He would not eat. He paced up and down in his palace, worrying and waiting for the morning.

But down in the dark den, something wonderful was happening that the king could not see. God had sent an angel, and the angel had gently closed the lions' great mouths. The lions did not roar. They did not bite. They simply lay down beside Daniel, warm and quiet and calm, like enormous gentle cats curling up for the night. And Daniel was not afraid, not even a little — because he knew, deep in his heart, that God was right there with him in the dark.

As soon as the very first light of morning crept into the sky, the king leaped up and ran all the way to the den. His voice trembled as he called out, hardly daring to hope. "Daniel! Daniel — was your God able to keep you safe?"

And up from the den came Daniel's voice, strong and glad and bright. "O king, live forever! My God sent his angel, and the angel shut the lions' mouths. They have not hurt me at all — not even a scratch!"

The king's heart leaped for joy. He had Daniel lifted up out of the den at once, safe and whole and well. Not a mark was found on him, because he had trusted in his God.

And from that day on, the king sent a message to everyone in his whole, wide kingdom. "Let everyone everywhere honor the God of Daniel," he said. "For he is the living God, who saves and rescues, and who kept his servant safe all night long, among the lions."
