The Garden at Night

It was late at night, and very dark. Jesus and his closest friends had just finished sharing a special supper together. And now, quietly, Jesus led them out of the city, through the still streets, and up a hillside, to a peaceful garden full of old olive trees. Their branches stood dark and twisty against the starry sky. It was a place Jesus loved, where he often came to pray.

But this night was not like other nights. For Jesus knew that a very hard day was coming — the hardest day he would ever have to face. And as he walked into the garden, a great heaviness settled over his heart. He felt deeply sad. He felt troubled. And yes — he even felt afraid. Even Jesus, God's own dear Son, felt afraid that night.

He turned to his three closest friends — Peter, and James, and John. "My heart is so full of sorrow," he told them, "that I can hardly bear it. Please — stay here. Stay close to me, and keep watch, while I go a little farther to pray."

So Jesus walked on, just a little way deeper into the garden, all by himself. And there, beneath the olive trees, he knelt down low on the ground. And he began to pour out his heart to his Father in heaven.

He did not hide a single thing. He did not pretend to be braver than he felt. He prayed honestly, with all his heart. "Father," he said, "if there is any other way — please, please let this hard thing pass me by. I do not want it." And then he prayed the bravest words of all. "But not what I want, Father. What you want. Whatever you ask of me, I will do it."

After a while, Jesus rose, and walked back to his three friends. But when he reached them — they were fast asleep! Their eyes had grown so heavy that they simply could not keep them open. Gently, Jesus woke them. "Could you not stay awake with me, even a little while?" he asked them, kindly. "Stay close, and keep watch."

Then Jesus went away once more, and knelt, and prayed the very same prayer again. "Not what I want, Father — but what you want." And once again, when he came back, he found his tired friends sleeping. They loved Jesus dearly, but the night was so very late, and their eyes were so heavy, that they simply could not help it.

A third time, Jesus went away to pray. And his sorrow was so deep, and the prayer was so hard, that he prayed it with all his strength: "Father — your will be done." The night was so dark all around him, and the garden so quiet, and the hard thing ahead so very heavy. For a little while, it seemed the loneliest place in all the world.

But here is the most important part of all. Jesus was not left alone in that dark garden. For God saw his dear Son, kneeling there in the night, so sad, and so afraid. And God did not leave him on his own. He sent help — straight down from heaven. An angel came, and stood beside Jesus in the dark, and gave him strength, and comforted him. Quietly, and gently, the angel stayed close beside him, so that Jesus would not have to face that hard night all alone.

And there, in the quiet garden, with the angel beside him, Jesus felt his strength come flowing back. His heart, though still heavy, grew steady and calm. He was comforted. And he was ready, now, for whatever was to come — not because he was no longer afraid, but because he trusted his Father, and because he knew, deep down, that he was not alone.

That is what real courage is, little one. It is not never being scared at all. It is praying honestly, and trusting God, and doing the hard thing even while your heart feels afraid.

So whenever you feel frightened — at bedtime, in the dark, or any time at all — remember Jesus, in the garden at night. You do not ever have to hide how you feel, or pretend to be brave. You can tell God everything, exactly as it is. You can be brave and afraid at the very same time. And you are never, ever alone in the dark. For God is right there beside you — to give you strength, and to comfort you, just as he did for his own beloved Son.
