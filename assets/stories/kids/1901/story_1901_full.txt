The Garden at Night

It was late at night. Jesus and his friends had just shared a special supper together. And now Jesus led them outside, through the quiet streets, to a peaceful garden on a hillside — a garden full of old olive trees, with their twisted branches dark against the night sky. Jesus often came here to pray.

But this night was different. For Jesus knew that a very hard day was coming — the hardest day he would ever face. And his heart was heavy with sorrow. He felt sad, and troubled, and yes — even afraid. Even Jesus, the Son of God, felt afraid that night.

He turned to his three closest friends — Peter, and James, and John. "My heart is so full of sadness I can hardly bear it," he told them. "Please — stay here with me. Stay close, and keep watch, while I go and pray."

Then Jesus walked a little way farther into the garden, all by himself. And there, beneath the olive trees, he knelt down on the ground. And he began to pray to his Father in heaven.

It was an honest prayer. Jesus did not hide how he felt, or pretend to be brave. He poured out his whole heart. "Father," he prayed, "if there is any other way — please, let this hard thing pass me by. I do not want it." And then he said the bravest words of all. "But not what I want, Father. What you want. Whatever you ask, I will do it."

Three times Jesus prayed that same prayer, there in the dark. And it was a hard, hard prayer to pray. For Jesus was truly afraid — and yet he was willing to do the difficult thing anyway, because he trusted his Father, and because he loved us so. That is what real courage looks like. Not never feeling scared — but trusting God, even when you are scared right down to your toes.

Meanwhile, his tired friends had fallen fast asleep nearby. It had been such a long day, and their eyes were so heavy, that they could not stay awake, even though they dearly loved him.

But Jesus was not left all alone in the dark. For God saw his dear Son kneeling there, and God did not leave him on his own. He sent an angel — straight down from heaven — to come and stand beside Jesus, to give him strength, and to comfort him. And there, in the quiet garden, Jesus was comforted, and his strength came back to him, and he was ready for whatever was to come.

So whenever you feel scared, little one — at bedtime, in the dark, or any time at all — remember Jesus, in the garden at night. You can tell God exactly how you feel. You do not have to hide it, or pretend. You can be brave and afraid at the very same time. And you are never, ever alone in the dark — for God is right there beside you, to give you strength, and to comfort you, just as he did for his own dear Son.
