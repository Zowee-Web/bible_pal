Jesus Calms the Storm

One evening, when the sun was setting and the sky was turning pink and gold, Jesus said to his friends, "Let us go across to the other side of the lake." So they climbed into a little fishing boat and pushed out onto the water.

It had been a long, busy day, and Jesus was very tired. He went to the back of the boat, lay down, and rested his head on a soft cushion. Soon, gently rocked by the water, he was fast asleep.

But while Jesus slept, the wind began to blow. At first it was only a breeze. Then it grew stronger, and stronger still, until it was howling all around them. The calm lake turned wild. Big waves rose up high and crashed over the sides of the boat. Cold water splashed in, and the little boat began to fill up.

Jesus' friends held on tight. Many of them were fishermen who had sailed this lake all their lives — but they had never seen a storm as fierce as this one. They scooped the water out as fast as they could, yet more kept pouring in. They were frightened.

And through it all, Jesus stayed fast asleep on his cushion, as peaceful as a child.

At last his friends could wait no longer. They woke him, shouting to be heard above the wind. "Teacher, wake up! Don't you care that we are going to sink?"

Jesus opened his eyes. Slowly, he stood up in the rocking boat. He was not afraid, not even a little. He looked out at the howling wind and the wild, crashing waves, and he spoke to them as if they could hear him.

"Peace," he said. "Be still."

And all at once — the wind stopped. The waves lay down. The lake grew calm and still, with the last soft light of evening shining on the water. Not a single ripple stirred. The only sound left was the gentle drip of water falling from the sail.

Jesus turned to his friends. "Why were you so afraid?" he asked them, gently.

They could hardly speak. They looked at the calm water, and then at Jesus, and then at one another. "Who is this?" they whispered. "Even the wind and the waves do what he says!"

And the little boat sailed softly on, steady and safe, all the way to the other side, beneath the quiet evening stars.
