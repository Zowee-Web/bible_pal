The Ascension

After Jesus rose from the dead, he did not hurry away from his friends. Instead, he stayed with them for many days. He walked with them and talked with them. He ate meals with them. He showed them his hands, and let them see that he was truly, wonderfully, really alive again. After all their sadness, how happy his friends were to have him back among them!

But Jesus knew the time was coming for something new. So one day, he led his friends out of the city and up onto a hillside, and he gathered them all close around him. They could tell, somehow, that this was an important moment.

Jesus had wonderful things to tell them. "Soon," he said, "you will tell people everywhere about me — about how much God loves them. You will be my helpers and my friends, all across the whole world." And then he lifted up his hands over them, and he blessed them, warmly and tenderly, the way you might bless someone you love before they set out on a long journey.

"It is time now for me to go back to my Father in heaven," Jesus told them. "But listen — do not be sad, and do not be afraid. I am not leaving you all alone. I will be with you always. Every single day. Even to the very end of time. And very soon, I will send you a special helper — the Holy Spirit — who will be with you, and inside you, and will give you courage." It was the kindest, most comforting promise his friends had ever heard, and they held onto it tightly.

And then something amazing happened. As his friends stood watching, Jesus began to rise up off the ground. Gently, slowly, he lifted up into the air — up, and up, and up toward the sky. Higher he went, and higher still, until at last a soft white cloud came and covered him, and his friends could not see him any longer.

For a long moment, they simply stood there, heads tipped back, gazing up and up into the wide blue sky, wondering at it all.

And then — they were not alone. Two angels, dressed in shining white, were suddenly standing right beside them. The angels spoke to them, gently and kindly. "Friends of Galilee," they said, "why are you standing here, looking up at the sky? Jesus has gone up into heaven. But do not worry — one day, he will come back again, in just the same way you have seen him go."

So the friends turned, and they walked back down the hill, and home to the city. But they were not crying, and they were not gloomy. No — they went with great big smiles, and hearts full to the brim with joy and hope. Because they understood now: Jesus had not truly left them. He was with them still, exactly as he had promised. And he always, always would be.
