Mary and Martha

As Jesus traveled from town to town, he came one day to a little village, where two sisters named Martha and Mary lived. He stopped to visit them in their home, and the sisters were overjoyed to see him! Jesus was their dear, dear friend, and there was nothing they loved more than when he came to stay at their house.

Now, Martha wanted everything to be absolutely perfect for such a special guest. So the moment Jesus arrived, she got busy — very, very busy indeed. She swept the floors until they shone. She hurried to the kitchen to cook a wonderful meal. She set out the cups and the plates, and fetched the water, and stirred the pots, and arranged everything just so. Back and forth she went, working as hard as she possibly could, wanting it all to be lovely for Jesus.

But Mary did something different. While her sister bustled about, Mary came quietly and sat down on the floor, right at Jesus' feet. And there she stayed, listening. She listened to every single word Jesus spoke. She soaked it all in, like warm sunshine, perfectly content just to be near her friend and hear what he had to say.

After a while, Martha began to feel tired, and hot, and a little bit flustered. There was so much to do, and it seemed she was doing every bit of it all by herself, while Mary just... sat there! At last it spilled out of her. "Lord," she said, "don't you mind that my sister has left me to do all the work alone? Please — tell her to come and help me!"

Jesus looked at Martha, and his eyes were full of kindness. He was not cross with her, not even a little. He could see how hard she was working, and how much she wanted to please him.

"Martha, dear Martha," he said gently. "You are working so hard, and you are worried and upset about so many things. But really, only one thing is needed most of all. Your sister Mary has chosen that one good thing — she has chosen simply to be here, close to me. And no one is going to take that away from her."

He was not saying Martha's work was bad. Of course not — it is kind and good to work hard for the people we love. He was only reminding her, softly, of something easy to forget when we are busy: that the cooking and the cleaning and the rushing can wait a little while. Because sometimes, the very best thing in all the world is simply to stop, and to rest, and to be close to the ones we love most.

And that day, in that little house, both sisters were dearly loved by Jesus — Martha who worked, and Mary who listened — each one precious to him. And Mary stayed right where she was, close by his feet, listening to her friend — and there was no happier, more peaceful place in all the world that she could have been.
