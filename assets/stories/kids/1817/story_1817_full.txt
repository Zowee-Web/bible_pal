The Loving Father

One day, Jesus told a story about a father who had two sons, and who loved them both with all his heart.

The younger son was restless. He wanted to go far away and see the wide world for himself. So he came to his father and asked to take his share of all that the family had, and to leave. It made the father very sad. But he did not hold his son back. He gave him what he asked for, and then he stood at the gate and watched him walk down the long road, until he was only a tiny speck, and then gone.

The young man traveled to a land far, far from home. At first it was exciting, and he had plenty of everything. But little by little, he spent it all — every last coin — until one day he looked in his pockets and found nothing there at all.

Now he was hungry, and tired, and very lonely. The only work he could find was feeding pigs in a muddy field. He was so hungry that even the pigs' food began to look good to him. And there, sitting among the pigs, he thought about home — about his father, and the warm bread, and the door that was always open.

"What am I doing here?" he said to himself. "I will get up and go back to my father. I will tell him, 'I am sorry. I have done wrong.'"

So he stood up, and he began the long, long walk home.

Now, here is the most wonderful part. Every single day that his son had been gone, the father had walked out to look down that road, hoping and watching. And on this day, while his son was still far, far away — just a small shape in the distance — the father knew him at once. His heart leaped with joy.

He did not wait. He did not stand and frown with his arms crossed. He ran. The old father ran all the way down the road, and threw his arms around his dusty, tired son, and held him close, and would not let go.

"Father," the son began, "I am so sorry —"

But the father was already laughing and crying at the same time, calling out for all to hear: "My son was gone, and now he is home again! Bring him a warm coat. Let us have a feast tonight!"

And there was such great joy in that home, it lasted all the evening long.
