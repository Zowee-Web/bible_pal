Nehemiah Builds the Wall

There is a special kind of sad you feel when something you love gets broken — broken so badly it looks like it could never be fixed. That is just how a man named Nehemiah felt one day. Far away in the king's palace, word reached him that the great stone wall around God's city had fallen down. It lay in heaps of broken stone, with wide gaps where the strong gates used to stand. The news made Nehemiah so sad that he sat right down and wept. And then he prayed, and prayed.

The king saw how sad he was. "Go," the king said kindly. "Go and help." And so Nehemiah set out on the long road home.

When he arrived, he waited until night. Then quietly, by moonlight, he walked all the way around the broken wall. He climbed over fallen stones. He looked at every crack and every gap. And in his heart, a big idea was growing.

The next morning, he gathered all the people together. "Come," he said, his voice full of hope. "Let us rise up and build!"

And oh, how they answered! Every family chose the piece of wall nearest their own home. Side by side they worked, all along the wall. One stone. Then another. Then another. Up it rose, stone upon stone, all around the city.

Some men stood off to the side and laughed. "That little wall?" they jeered. "Why, a fox could knock it down!" But the builders did not stop, not even for a moment. They just kept right on going — lifting, setting, building — one stone at a time, together.

And then, one wonderful day, the very last stone went into place. The people looked up, and there it was: the whole wall, finished, all the way around. They had done it! Cheers rang out across the city, and there was singing, and there was dancing, and the people were filled with so much joy.

For some big, broken things really do get fixed, one small stone at a time. And the people of God had not built that wall alone. They had built it shoulder to shoulder, family beside family — and they had built it with the help of God, who loves to give His people the strength to do hard and beautiful things together.
