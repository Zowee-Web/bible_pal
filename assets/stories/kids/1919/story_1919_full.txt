Nehemiah Builds the Wall

There is a special kind of sadness that comes when something you love is broken — broken so badly that fixing it seems much too big a job for anyone at all. That was exactly the sadness a man named Nehemiah felt one day, far, far away in the king's palace, where he worked. Word had reached him from his homeland, and it was heavy news: the great stone wall around God's own city — the tall, strong wall that once wrapped all the way around to keep the people safe — had fallen down. It lay in broken heaps now, the stones tumbled every which way, with wide gaps where the strong gates used to stand.

When Nehemiah heard, the news sat heavy on his heart. He sat right down, and he wept, and for days he could hardly eat. And then he did the most important thing of all: he prayed. "O God," he said, "please help your city. And please help me." He prayed and prayed, and he waited to see what God would do.

Now Nehemiah served the king, carrying his cup. And one morning the king looked at him and saw the sadness on his face. "Why do you look so sad?" the king asked. So Nehemiah took a deep breath, and he told him all about the broken-down wall. And do you know what the king said? He said, "Go. Go, and help your people build." He even sent Nehemiah on his way with wood for the new gates. Nehemiah knew, deep in his heart, that this was God helping — opening the door.

So Nehemiah set out on the long, dusty road home. Mile after mile, day after day, until at last he came to the city with its sad, broken wall.

He did not start building right away. First, he waited until night, when the streets were quiet. Then, by the light of the moon, with only a few friends, Nehemiah walked all the way around the wall. He climbed over fallen stones. He squeezed past piles of rubble. He looked carefully at every crack, every gap, every place that needed mending. And all the while, in his heart, a big and hopeful idea was growing, stone by stone.

The next morning, Nehemiah gathered all the people together in one place. They did not know yet why he had come. He looked out at their faces, and his voice filled up with hope. "Come," he said. "You can see the trouble we are in — our wall is broken down. But God is with us. Let us rise up and build!"

And oh, how the people answered! "Let us rise up and build!" they cried back. Then everyone got to work.

Here is the wonderful part. They did not all crowd onto one spot. Instead, every family chose the piece of wall nearest to their own home, and they worked on that. So all along the wall, families stood side by side — fathers and mothers, sons and daughters, neighbors next to neighbors — each one mending their own little stretch. One stone. Then another. Then another. Stone upon stone, the wall began to rise, all the way around the city.

But not everyone was happy about it. A few men came and stood off to the side, and they laughed and laughed. "Look at them," they jeered. "That little wall? Why, if even a fox climbed up on it, the whole thing would come tumbling down!" They tried hard to make the builders feel small, and to make them want to quit.

But the builders did not quit. They did not even slow down. When the unkind words came, the people just prayed, and then they kept right on building — lifting, setting, lifting, setting — one stone at a time, all together. They knew the wall mattered. They knew God was helping them. And they were not about to stop.

Day after day the wall grew taller. The gaps closed up. The gates went back in place. And then, one truly wonderful day, the very last stone was lifted, and set, and pressed into place.

The people stepped back. They looked up. And there it was — the whole wall, finished at last, standing tall and strong, all the way around the city. They had done it!

What a day that was! Cheers went up all across the city. There was singing, and there was music, and there was dancing in the streets. People who had wept over the rubble now laughed out loud with happiness. And the joy was so great that people far away could hear the sound of it.

For some big, broken things really can be fixed — not all at once, but one small stone at a time. And the people had not built that wall alone. They had built it shoulder to shoulder, family beside family, friend beside friend — and they had built it with the help of God, who loves to give His people the courage and the strength to do hard and beautiful things, together.
