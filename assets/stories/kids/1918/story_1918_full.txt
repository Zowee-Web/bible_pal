The Girl at the Well

A servant set out on a long, long journey, with ten camels and a very important job to do. His master, Abraham, was growing old, and he had sent his most trusted servant far away, back to the land of his family, to find a wife for his son Isaac. She had to be a kind woman, and one who loved God. But how in the whole wide world would the servant ever know which girl was the right one?

He traveled for many days, across the hot desert, until at last he came to a town. And just outside the town was a well, where the women of the village came in the cool of evening to draw their water. The servant made his ten camels kneel down to rest beside the well. And then, before he did anything else, he prayed.

"O God of my master Abraham," he said quietly, "please help me today. Be kind to me. Here is what I will do. I will ask one of the girls for a drink of water. And if she is the right one — the one you have chosen for Isaac — then let her answer, 'Yes, have a drink. And I will draw water for your camels, too.'"

He had not even finished praying when a girl named Rebekah came walking up to the well, with her water jar balanced on her shoulder. She was lovely, and there was kindness in her face.

The servant hurried over to meet her. "Please," he said, "may I have a little drink of water from your jar?"

"Of course you may! Drink, sir," said Rebekah right away. And she lowered her jar down into her hands and gave him a cool, refreshing drink. And then — then she looked over at his ten dusty, thirsty camels, and she said, "I will draw water for your camels too, until every one of them has had enough to drink."

Now, ten camels can drink an enormous amount of water! But Rebekah did not mind one bit. Down to the well she went, and up again, and down, and up, hurrying back and forth, again and again, pouring water into the trough, until every last camel had drunk its fill. What a kind and willing heart that girl had.

The servant watched her, and he could hardly breathe for wonder. It was the very thing he had prayed for — almost word for word! God had heard him. God had answered him. God had led him, across all those long and dusty miles, straight to the right girl.

So the servant bowed his head and thanked God right there. Then he went home with Rebekah and met her family, and told them the whole wonderful story. And Rebekah was willing and brave, and she chose to go. She traveled back across the desert with the servant — and there she met Isaac, and became his wife. And God had been guiding every single step of the way, the whole long journey through.

God is like that. He leads us, and he listens when we pray — and sometimes he answers in the kindest, most wonderful ways.
