The Hills Full of Fire

In those days, an enemy king was at war with God's people, and he wanted very badly to defeat them.

Over and over, the enemy king would make a secret plan. "We will hide our soldiers in that valley," he would whisper, "and surprise them there." But every single time, before the trap could spring, God's prophet Elisha would quietly send word to his own king: "Be careful — do not go past that place. The enemy is hiding there, waiting." And so, again and again, God's people stayed safe, and the enemy's clever traps came to nothing.

This made the enemy king furious. "Someone is giving away my secrets!" he shouted. "Which one of you is it?" But his servants said, "It is none of us, O king. It is Elisha, the prophet of God. He knows the very words you whisper in your own bedroom."

"Then we will go and capture him," the king decided.

So one night, while the whole little town of Dothan lay fast asleep, the enemy king sent a great army — soldiers, and horses, and chariots, more than you could count — to creep up quietly and surround the city where Elisha was staying. They spread out all across the hills in the dark, and they waited for morning.

Early the next morning, Elisha's young helper got up and stepped outside — and his heart nearly stopped.

For all around the city, as far as he could possibly see, was that great enemy army. Soldiers everywhere. Horses everywhere. Chariots covering the hills and ringing the whole town. They had come in the night, and now there seemed to be no way out at all.

The young man spun around and ran back inside. "Oh, my master!" he cried. "What are we ever going to do? They are all around us!"

But Elisha was not afraid. Not even a little bit. "Don't be afraid," he said gently. "There are more on our side than there are on theirs."

More on our side? The young man looked all around the room. There was only him, and Elisha. Just the two of them, and no one else. How in the world could that be more than that whole enormous army outside?

So Elisha closed his eyes and prayed. "Lord," he said, "please open his eyes, so that he can see."

And God answered. God opened the young man's eyes.

And oh — what he saw then took his breath away! All over the hills, all around Elisha, were horses and chariots of fire. Shining, and blazing, and glowing bright — far too many to count. They had been there the whole time, all along, watching over them. There were far, far more of God's fiery horses on the hills than there were enemy soldiers down below.

And just like that, the young man's fear melted clean away. He had been so sure they were all alone, with danger pressing in on every side. But they had never, ever been alone. God had been right there, all around them, the entire time. He just hadn't been able to see it before.

Then something wonderful and surprising happened. God reached down and made that whole enemy army unable to find their way — they blinked and stumbled, lost and confused. And Elisha walked calmly right out to them and said, "Come. Follow me." And he led the whole army, gently, like a shepherd leading sheep, all the way to the city where his own king lived.

When they arrived, God's king looked at all those helpless enemy soldiers standing there, and his eyes went wide. "Elisha," he asked, "shall I strike them? Shall I do something to them?"

"No," said Elisha firmly. "Do not harm them. You would not harm someone you had simply caught, would you? No — do the opposite. Set food and water in front of them. Give them a feast!"

So that is exactly what they did. They set out a great big meal before the enemy soldiers — plates piled high with good food, and jugs of cool, fresh water. And the soldiers, who had come to do harm, sat down and ate and drank until they were completely full. And then Elisha sent them home, safe and sound, all the way back to their own land and their own king.

And do you know what happened after that? After that one surprising day of kindness, those enemy raiders stopped coming to trouble God's people. The war was over.

The young helper had woken up that morning thinking that he and Elisha were all alone — surrounded, and trapped, with no hope left at all. But they had never been alone for even a single moment. God had been with them the entire time — nearer, and closer, than they could ever have imagined, far more than they could ever see.
