The Hills Full of Fire

In those days, an enemy king was at war with God's people. Again and again he made secret plans to attack them — but every single time, God's prophet Elisha would quietly warn his own king: "Be careful! The enemy is hiding over there." And so God's people would stay safe.

This made the enemy king very angry indeed. "Who keeps telling them my secrets?" he demanded. And when he learned that it was Elisha, he decided to capture the prophet once and for all. So one night, while the whole town lay sleeping, he sent a great army — soldiers, horses, and chariots — to creep up and surround the city where Elisha was staying.

Early the next morning, Elisha's young helper stepped outside — and his heart nearly stopped.

All around the city, as far as he could see, was that great enemy army. Covering the hills. Ringing the whole town. There seemed to be no way out at all.

The young man ran back inside. "Oh, my master!" he cried. "What are we going to do? They are all around us!"

But Elisha was not afraid. Not even a little. "Don't be afraid," he said gently. "There are more on our side than on theirs."

More on our side? The young man looked all around. There was only him, and Elisha. Just the two of them. How could that possibly be more?

So Elisha prayed. "Lord," he said, "please open his eyes, so that he can see."

And God opened the young man's eyes.

And oh — what he saw then! All over the hills, all around Elisha, were horses and chariots of fire. Shining, and blazing, and more than he could ever count. They had been there the whole time, all around Elisha — far, far more than all the enemy soldiers below.

And just like that, the young man's fear melted away. They had never been alone. God had been right there, all around them, the entire time.

Then something surprising happened. God made the whole enemy army unable to find their way at all. And Elisha walked right out to them and said, kindly, "Come — follow me." And he led them, gently, all the way to his own king's city.

Now, God's king looked at all those helpless enemy soldiers, and he asked Elisha, "Shall I do something to them?"

"No," said Elisha. "Do not harm them. Instead — give them a feast!"

So they set a great meal before the enemy soldiers, with plenty of good food and cool water. The soldiers ate and drank until they were full. And then Elisha sent them safely home, all the way back to their own land.

And do you know what? After that day of surprising kindness, those enemy raiders stopped coming to trouble God's people at all.

The young helper had begun that morning thinking they were all alone — surrounded, and trapped, with no hope at all. But they had never been alone for a single moment. God had been with them the whole time, closer than they could ever see.
