Joseph Forgives His Brothers

When Joseph was a boy, he had eleven brothers. His father loved him dearly, and gave him a beautiful coat woven in every color you can imagine. But the wonderful coat made his older brothers jealous — so jealous that one day they sent Joseph far away from home, all the way to the land of Egypt.

It was a hard and lonely thing to happen to a young boy. To be so far from his father, in a land where he knew no one at all! But here is the thing to remember: God did not leave Joseph. Not for one moment. God was with him through every single part of it — in the good days, and in the hard ones too.

Many years went by. And all through those years, in a land far from home, Joseph kept on trusting God — even when things were hard, even when they seemed unfair. And God kept on being with him, helping him to be wise, and patient, and good.

Joseph grew up into a wise and gentle and trustworthy man. And in Egypt, something amazing happened. Little by little, Joseph was given more and more important work to do, until at last he became a great leader — the right-hand helper of the king himself! The king saw how wise Joseph was, and he put him in charge of a very big job: saving up food, year after year, storing it in great barns, so that if a hungry time ever came, no one would have to go without. Year after year, while the sun shone and the harvests were good, Joseph filled those great barns higher and higher, until there was more grain stored away than anyone could count.

And a hungry time did come. The rain stopped falling. The crops would not grow. Food became very hard to find — not just in Egypt, but in faraway lands too. And so people began to travel from all over the world to Egypt, because Egypt was the one place that had food to spare. All because Joseph had saved so much.

And one day, who should come walking up the road, tired and hungry and looking for food to buy — but Joseph's very own brothers.

They came and bowed down low before the great and important man, for they did not know who he was. How could they? The last time they had seen Joseph, he had been only a boy. But Joseph knew them the moment he saw their faces. His heart began to pound, and pound, and pound.

For a moment, he could hardly breathe. He looked at his brothers, all grown up now, bowing before him and not knowing — and his heart became so full that he could not hold it in for one second longer. The tears came, and he began to cry.

"It is me," he said to them. "I am Joseph. I am your brother."

The brothers froze. They were afraid, and they did not know what to say. Surely, after everything they had done so long ago, after all these years — surely now Joseph would finally be angry. Surely now he would send them away.

But Joseph was not angry. Instead, he came down from his great chair and walked right up to them. And he wrapped his arms around them — one by one by one — and hugged each brother close.

"Do not be afraid," he said softly. "Yes, long ago, you meant to hurt me. But all that time, God was still working for good. Look — God brought me here, to this very place, with food enough to keep our whole family alive! So do not be sad, and do not be afraid. I forgive you, my brothers. I forgive you with all my heart. Now please — let us go and bring our father here, and all of us will be together again."

And so they did. They hurried home and brought their old father, Jacob, all the long way to Egypt. And when Jacob saw the son he had thought was lost forever — alive, and grown, and a great man now — he wept for joy and held him tight.

And the brothers wept too, and they held on to Joseph, and they held on to one another. A family that had been broken for so very many years was whole again at last — together, and forgiven, and home.
