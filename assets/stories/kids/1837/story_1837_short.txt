Ruth and Naomi

On a long, dusty road, two women walked together. One was old, and her name was Naomi. The other was young, and her name was Ruth.

Naomi had once had a husband and two sons. But a very sad thing had happened — they had grown sick and died, and now Naomi had no one left but Ruth, who had married one of her sons. Naomi's heart was heavy, and she was far from home.

"You should go back to your own family," Naomi told Ruth gently. "You are young. You could stay here, where you grew up."

But Ruth took Naomi's hand and would not let go. "Please do not ask me to leave you," she said. "Where you go, I will go. Where you live, I will live. Your people will be my people, and your God will be my God."

So the two of them walked all the way to Naomi's homeland, to a little town called Bethlehem.

They had very little to eat. So every morning, Ruth went out to the fields, where the workers were gathering grain. She picked up the leftover bits they dropped, so she and Naomi would have bread.

The owner of the field was a kind man named Boaz. He saw how hard Ruth worked, and how she cared for old Naomi. "Let her gather all she needs," he told his workers. "And leave a little extra for her, on purpose."

Boaz watched over Ruth, and in time he came to love her, and they were married. And God gave Ruth and Boaz a baby boy.

Old Naomi held that baby in her arms, and her sad heart filled all the way up with joy. She was not alone anymore. Kindness had carried them home.
