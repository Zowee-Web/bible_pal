Ruth and Naomi

Once, in a little town called Bethlehem, there lived a woman named Naomi, and her house was a happy one. It was full of the sound of laughing, and the warm smell of supper cooking, and the voices of her husband and her two grown sons. Naomi's heart was full.

But then a hard time came to the land. The rains stopped, and the crops would not grow, and there was not enough food. So Naomi and her family packed up and traveled far away, to a country called Moab, to find food and make a new home.

And in that faraway land, two sad things and one happy thing happened. The happy thing was this: Naomi's two sons each married a kind young woman of Moab. One was named Orpah, and the other was named Ruth. But then came the sad things. Naomi's husband grew sick and died. And after a time, her two sons died as well. And Naomi was left with no one in all the world but her two sons' wives, far, far from the place where she was born.

Naomi's heart, that had once been so full, now felt empty and heavy with sorrow. And she decided it was time to go home, back to Bethlehem at last.

So the three women set out together along the road. But after a little way, Naomi stopped, and turned to the two younger women, with tears in her eyes. "You should go back, dear ones," she said. "Go home to your own mothers and fathers. You are young. You should stay among your own people, where life will be easier for you. May God be kind to you, as you have been kind to me."

They wept together on the road. And at last, Orpah kissed Naomi goodbye, and turned back toward her old home.

But Ruth would not go. She took hold of Naomi's hand, and she held it tight, and she would not let go.

"Please do not ask me to leave you," Ruth said. "For wherever you go, I will go. Wherever you live, I will live. Your people will be my people. And your God will be my God. Where you stay, I will stay, right beside you — always."

And so Naomi saw that it was no use arguing. And the two of them, the old woman and the young one, walked all the long, dusty way together, until at last they came to Bethlehem.

When they arrived, the whole town stirred. "Naomi? Can it really be Naomi?" the people whispered, for she had been gone so long. Naomi and Ruth had come home — but they were very poor, with almost nothing at all to eat.

Now, it was the time of year when the workers were out cutting the barley in the fields. So every single morning, Ruth got up early and went out to work. She followed along behind the workers, and gathered up the bits of grain they left behind, so that she and Naomi would have a little bread for the day. It was hot, tiring work, bending in the sun from morning till evening. But Ruth never once complained, for she loved Naomi, and she would do anything to care for her.

The field where Ruth worked belonged to a good and gentle man named Boaz. One day, Boaz came out to his field, and he noticed Ruth working there, so steady and so hard. "Who is that young woman?" he asked. And his workers told him the whole story — how this kind young woman from a faraway land had left everything behind, just to care for old Naomi.

Boaz was deeply moved. He went to Ruth himself, and spoke to her kindly. "Stay here in my field," he said. "Gather all you wish. I will make sure no one bothers you. When you are thirsty, drink from my water jars. And come, share our meal with us." And quietly, he told his workers, "Drop some extra grain on purpose, just for her. Let her gather plenty."

So Ruth went home that evening with more grain than ever before, and she told Naomi all about the kind man named Boaz. And for the first time in a long, long while, Naomi smiled. "God has not forgotten us after all," she said softly.

As the days and the weeks went by, Boaz came to love Ruth, and Ruth came to love Boaz. And so, in time, the two of them were married. Oh, what a happy day that was! And before long, God gave Ruth and Boaz a wonderful gift: a baby boy.

When the baby was born, the women of the town gathered around old Naomi, full of joy. They placed the tiny child gently in her arms. And as Naomi held that little baby close — as she felt his warm weight, and his soft breath — her heart, which had been so heavy and so empty for so long, filled all the way up again, right to the very top, with happiness.

She was not alone anymore. She never truly had been — for God had been quietly caring for her, all along. And kindness, in the end, had walked her all the way home.
