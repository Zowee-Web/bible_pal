Crossing the Red Sea

For many long years, God's people had been slaves in the land of Egypt. They had worked hard and cried out for help, and at last God had set them free. Now, at last, they were on their way to a new home — a great crowd of mothers and fathers, grandparents and children, with their flocks and their bundles, all walking together. God himself led the way, in a tall pillar of cloud that went before them.

They walked and they walked, until they came to the shore of a great, wide sea. And there they stopped to rest by the water.

But then someone looked back the way they had come — and gasped. Far off on the road was a cloud of dust, rising higher and higher and coming closer and closer. It was the king of Egypt, and his whole army, with horses and chariots, thundering after them to drag them back into Egypt.

The people were terrified. The deep sea was in front of them. The great army was rushing up behind them. There was nowhere to run, and nowhere to hide. "What will we do?" they cried. "We are trapped!"

But Moses, their leader, was not afraid. He lifted up his voice so they could all hear him. "Do not be afraid," he said. "Stand still, and watch. You will see how God takes care of us today."

And then God did something wonderful. The tall pillar of cloud that had been leading the people lifted up, and moved, and came down again — right behind them, standing between God's people and the army. To the army, the cloud was dark, so they could not find their way. But to God's people, it gave soft light. All through the night, God himself stood between his children and the danger, like a mother standing between her little ones and the storm.

And so the people waited through that long night, with the dark cloud behind them and God's soft light all around them. They could hear the army somewhere beyond the cloud, but the army could not reach them, not at all. And little by little, as the hours passed, the people's fear grew quiet. They began to trust: surely God had not brought them all this long way only to leave them now.

Then God spoke to Moses. "Lift up your staff," he said, "and stretch out your hand over the sea."

So Moses lifted his shepherd's staff high over the water. And all that night, God sent a strong, strong wind that blew and blew across the sea. And then the most amazing thing of all happened. The water itself split right apart, down the middle! It piled up high on the one side, and high on the other, like two great shining walls of water. And there, in between them, lay a wide path of smooth, dry ground.

"Come!" Moses called. And God's people walked forward, out onto the path, right through the very middle of the sea. The walls of water stood tall on their left hand and on their right. The children stared up with wide eyes — they could see fish flickering and darting inside the walls of water! But not a single drop fell on them. Step by step, the whole great crowd walked across the dry seabed, all the way to the far shore, safe and dry.

And when the very last of them had stepped safely onto the other side, Moses stretched out his hand over the sea one more time. With a great rushing sound, the walls of water came tumbling back together, and the sea closed up again — so the army could not follow them anymore. God's people were safe.

They stood on the far shore, safe and sound, and looked back at the wide water in wonder. God had made a road for them, straight through the middle of the sea! And right there on the shore, a woman named Miriam picked up a tambourine, and all the people began to sing, and to dance, and to thank God with all their hearts. "Sing to God!" they sang, "for he has done a wonderful thing!" The children danced and clapped, and splashed their toes in the shallow water at the edge, and the whole great crowd sang together on the safe, dry shore — because God had brought them safely, every single one, through the sea and home.
