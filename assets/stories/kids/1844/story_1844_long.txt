Joseph's Colorful Coat

It was the most beautiful coat anyone in the family had ever seen — long and flowing, and bright with every color you could imagine. It belonged to a boy named Joseph; and this is the story of that coat, and of the God who never once let Joseph go.

Joseph's father was named Jacob, and Jacob had twelve sons. He loved every one of them. But the son his heart held most tenderly, the one he watched for at the end of the day, was a boy named Joseph.

To show how dearly he loved him, Jacob decided to make Joseph a present. Not a small one. He gathered thread in every color he could find, and he worked at it, and at last he held it up for Joseph to see — a beautiful coat, woven in every color you could dream of. Red like sunset and gold like wheat. Blue like the sky and green like spring grass. All of it together, bright as a whole garden of flowers.

Joseph slipped his arms into the sleeves, and the coat fit just right. He spun around once to feel it move. "Thank you, Father," he said, and Jacob smiled the way a father smiles when his whole heart is in his eyes.

After that, Joseph wore the coat everywhere. He wore it in the morning and he wore it in the evening, and when the sun touched it, the colors shone so bright that people turned to look. Joseph loved that coat. He loved that his father had made it just for him.

But when Joseph's older brothers saw the coat, something happened inside them. A sour feeling crept into their hearts, small at first, then bigger. They felt jealous. "Father made a coat like that for Joseph," they said to one another. "Where is ours?" And the more they looked at those bright colors, the more the sour feeling grew, until they could not even speak one kind word to him.

Then something else happened that made the feeling worse. Joseph had two strange and special dreams. In the first dream, it seemed as though bundles of wheat were bowing down. In the second, even the sun and the moon and the stars were bowing low. The dreams felt like a picture of his whole family bowing down to him, the way people bow to a king.

Now, Joseph did not understand what the dreams meant. But in the morning he told his brothers about them anyway. And when they heard, they did not wonder along with him. They folded their arms. "Do you think you will rule over us?" they said. And the sour feeling in their hearts grew bigger still.

Some days went by. The brothers took the sheep far out into the fields to find fresh grass, and they were gone a long while. So Jacob called Joseph and said, "Go and see how your brothers are doing, and bring word back to me." Joseph put on his coat — of course he did — and he set off across the hills to find them.

It was a long walk. He went down one valley and up the next, asking, "Have you seen my brothers?" until at last, far in the distance, he saw their flocks. He hurried toward them, his bright coat shining as he came.

And the brothers saw him coming. They saw those colors a long way off, and the old sour feeling rose up in every one of them at once. There, far from home, far from their father's watching eyes, the brothers decided to send Joseph away.

Just then a line of traders came along the road, riding on their camels, their bags heavy with spices, traveling the long road down to the land of Egypt. And the brothers sent Joseph along with them — away from the fields, away from his father, away from home. The traders went on down the long road, and the boy in the colorful coat went with them, smaller and smaller, until the hills hid him from view.

It was a long and dusty road to Egypt. The land grew flat and strange. Joseph did not know these traders, and they did not know him. Each night the sky filled with stars he could see from a place farther from home than he had ever been. Joseph, all alone, must have felt so frightened. He must have missed his father so very much, and wondered if he would ever see those hills again.

But here is the most important part of the whole story. Here is the part to remember when you lie down tonight. Joseph was not as alone as he felt.

God was with Joseph. On that long, lonely road, in the dust and the dark, God was with him. When the traders' camels carried him into Egypt, a strange land where he knew not a single face, God was with him there too. Not for one single moment did God leave Joseph's side. The boy could not always feel it, the way you cannot always feel the sun behind the clouds. But it was true the whole way down that road, and it stayed true in Egypt, and it never once stopped being true.

And though Joseph could not possibly see it yet, God was already weaving something good — the way Jacob had once sat and woven that coat of many colors, thread over thread, until it became something beautiful. Joseph could only see one thread at a time, and the thread he was holding now was a sad and frightening one. But God could see the whole pattern. One day, God would lift Joseph up, and use him to help save his whole family — every brother, and his old father too — from a terrible hunger that was still years away. The story was far, far from over.

So if you ever feel left out, or far from home, or alone in a place you do not know, you can remember Joseph and his coat. The boy in the colorful coat was not alone, and he never would be. God was watching over him on the long road and in the strange land, all along, the whole time, with a love far bigger and far brighter than any coat of many colors.
