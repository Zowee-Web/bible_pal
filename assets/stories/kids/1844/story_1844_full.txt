Joseph's Colorful Coat

Long ago there lived a man named Jacob, who had twelve sons. He loved all of them — but the one his heart held most tenderly was a boy named Joseph.

To show how dearly he loved him, Jacob made Joseph a wonderful present: a beautiful coat, woven in every color you could dream of. Red like sunset and gold like wheat, blue like the sky and green like spring grass — all of it together, bright as a whole garden of flowers.

Joseph loved that coat. He wore it everywhere, and it shone in the sunshine.

But when Joseph's older brothers saw the coat, a sour feeling crept into their hearts. They felt jealous. "Father loves Joseph more than he loves us," they grumbled to one another, and they could not even speak kindly to him.

Then Joseph had two strange and special dreams. In the dreams, it seemed as though his whole family was bowing down to him, the way people bow to a king. When Joseph told his brothers about the dreams, they felt more jealous still.

One day, the brothers were far out in the fields, looking after the sheep, when Joseph came walking toward them in his bright coat. And there, far from home, the brothers decided to send Joseph away. A line of traders was passing by on their camels, traveling down the long road to the land of Egypt — and the brothers sent Joseph along with them, far away from his father and his home.

It was a long and dusty road to Egypt. Joseph, all alone, must have felt so frightened, and missed his father so very much.

But here is the most important part of the whole story — the part to remember when you lie down tonight. God was with Joseph. On that long, lonely road, God was with him. In Egypt, a strange land where he knew not a single face, God was with him still. Not for one moment did God leave Joseph's side.

And though Joseph could not possibly see it yet, God was already weaving something good — the way Jacob had once woven that coat of many colors. One day, God would lift Joseph up to help save his whole family, brothers and all, from a terrible hunger. The story was far from over.

The boy in the colorful coat was not alone, and never would be. God was watching over him, all along, with a love far bigger than any coat.
