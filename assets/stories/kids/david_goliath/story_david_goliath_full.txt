David and the Giant

In the hills near Bethlehem, there lived a shepherd boy named David. He was the youngest of his brothers, and while they grew tall and strong, David spent his days out on the green hillsides, watching over his father's sheep.

David loved those quiet hills. He knew every sheep by name. When the sun was warm, he played his harp and sang songs to God. And when danger came — once a lion, once a bear — David was not afraid, because he trusted that God was with him. God had helped him keep his little flock safe, and David never forgot it.

Far away, the king's army had gathered for war. David's three oldest brothers were soldiers there. One morning his father said, "Take this bread to your brothers, and see how they are." So David left his sheep with a helper and set off down the road.

When David reached the army, something was wrong. The soldiers were not marching or training. They were crouched behind the rocks, peeking out, their faces pale. Then David heard a sound — heavy footsteps, thud, thud, thud. A giant stepped into the valley. His name was Goliath, and he was taller than any man David had ever seen. "Send out a man to fight me!" he shouted. The soldiers ducked lower behind the rocks. Not one of them moved.

David turned to the men beside him. "Why is everyone so afraid?" he asked.

"He is too big," they whispered. "No one can stand against him."

But David remembered the lion. He remembered the bear. He remembered that God had been with him every single time. "God kept my sheep safe," David said quietly. "God will help us now."

Word reached the king, and David was brought before him. "You are only a boy," the king said. But he saw something steady in David's eyes, and at last he agreed. The king dressed David in his own armor — a heavy helmet, a great coat of metal, a long sword. But it was all far too big. David could hardly walk. So he took it off and set it down.

Instead, David walked to a little stream that ran through the valley. The water was clear and cool. He crouched down and chose five smooth stones, round and cool in his hand, and slipped them into his shepherd's bag. Then, with his sling in his hand, he walked out to meet the giant.

Goliath looked down at the shepherd boy and laughed. "Am I a dog, that you come to me with sticks?"

David looked up at the giant, and his voice did not shake. "You come to me with a sword and a spear," he said. "But I come in the name of God, who is with me."

David reached into his bag and took out one smooth stone. He fitted it into his sling. Round and round it swung, faster and faster — and then the stone flew, straight and true, through the air. The giant swayed. And then the giant fell down to the ground, and the valley grew still.

For a moment, no one moved. Then the soldiers stood up from behind their rocks. The fear that had held them was gone. They cheered and lifted their voices to the sky, because they had seen that God was greater than the greatest giant.

And David — the shepherd boy who trusted God — went home to his hills and his sheep and his songs, the same boy he had always been, with the same God beside him.
