Joseph and the King's Dreams

Joseph was living far from home, in the land of Egypt. It had not been an easy road to get there — but through all of it, God had been with him. And God had given Joseph a special gift: with God's help, Joseph could understand what dreams really meant.

Now, the king of Egypt — called Pharaoh — had two strange dreams that worried him. In one, seven thin, hungry cows gobbled up seven fat, healthy cows. In the other, seven thin stalks of grain swallowed up seven plump ones. Pharaoh woke up troubled. "What can these dreams mean?" he asked. But no one in all of Egypt could tell him.

Then someone remembered Joseph. So Joseph was brought before the king.

"I have heard that you can understand dreams," said Pharaoh.

"I cannot do it on my own," Joseph answered. "But God can show us what your dreams mean."

And God did. "The dreams are a warning," Joseph explained. "Seven good years are coming, when the fields will grow plenty of food. But after them will come seven hard years, when food will be very scarce. So here is what you should do: during the good years, save up food. Store it away carefully. Then, when the hard years come, there will be enough for everyone."

Pharaoh was amazed at Joseph's wisdom. "God has shown you all this," he said. "So I will put you in charge." And Joseph filled the great barns full, all through the good years. So when the hungry years came at last, there was plenty of food — and Joseph's wise plan saved a whole land full of people from going hungry.
