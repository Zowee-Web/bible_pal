Joseph and the King's Dreams

Joseph was living far, far from the home where he had grown up, in the land of Egypt. It had not been an easy road that brought him there. But through every part of it, the good days and the hard ones, God had stayed close beside him. And God had given Joseph a special gift: with God's help, Joseph could understand what dreams truly meant.

Now, the most powerful man in all of Egypt was the king, who was called Pharaoh. And one night, Pharaoh had two very strange dreams.

In the first dream, he saw seven fat, healthy cows grazing by the river. Then up came seven thin, bony, hungry cows — and they gobbled the fat cows right up! In the second dream, he saw seven plump, golden stalks of grain growing on one stem. Then seven thin, dried-up stalks sprang up — and swallowed the good ones whole.

Pharaoh woke up troubled and puzzled. What could such strange dreams possibly mean? He called for all his wisest men and helpers, but not one of them could tell him. Pharaoh grew more and more worried.

Then one of his servants remembered something. "There is a young man named Joseph," he said. "I have seen that he can understand dreams, with the help of his God."

So Pharaoh sent for Joseph at once, and Joseph was brought to stand before the king.

"I have had dreams that trouble me," said Pharaoh, "and no one can tell me what they mean. But I have heard that you can understand them."

Joseph answered humbly. "I cannot do it on my own," he said. "But God can show us what your dreams mean." And then Joseph listened, and God gave him the understanding.

"The two dreams are really one message," Joseph explained. "They are a warning from God, to help you get ready. Seven good years are coming to Egypt — years when the rain will fall and the fields will grow more food than you can imagine. But after those seven good years will come seven hard years, when almost nothing will grow, and food will be very hard to find."

"So here is what you must do," Joseph went on. "During the seven good years, do not use up all the food. Save some of it. Store it away, carefully, in great barns all across the land. Then, when the seven hard years come, there will be plenty saved up — and no one will have to go hungry."

Pharaoh was amazed. He had never heard such wisdom. "God has shown all of this to you," he said, "so there is no one wiser than you in all the land. I will put you in charge of everything." And so he did.

For the next seven years, while the harvests were rich and golden, Joseph worked hard and wisely. He gathered up food, and filled the great barns higher and higher, until there was more grain saved than anyone could count.

And then, just as God had warned, the seven hard years came. The crops stopped growing. People grew hungry — not only in Egypt, but in lands all around. But Egypt had food, because Joseph had saved it. And so, because one young man had listened to God and used his wisdom to help, a whole land full of people — and many beyond it too — were kept safe and fed through all the hungry years.
