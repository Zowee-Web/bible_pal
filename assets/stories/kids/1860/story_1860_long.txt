Joseph and the King's Dreams

Joseph was living far, far from the home where he had grown up, in the land of Egypt. It had not been an easy road that brought him there. But through every part of it, the good days and the hard ones alike, God had stayed close beside him. And God had given Joseph a special gift: with God's help, Joseph could understand what dreams truly meant.

Now, the most powerful man in all of Egypt was the king, who was called Pharaoh. And one night, Pharaoh had two very strange dreams, one after the other.

In the first dream, he was standing by the river, when up out of the water came seven fat, healthy cows, munching the green grass. But then up came seven thin, bony, hungry-looking cows — and they gobbled the seven fat cows right up! Pharaoh woke with a start. Then he fell asleep again, and dreamed a second time. Now he saw seven plump, golden ears of grain, all growing on a single stalk. But then seven thin, dried-up ears sprang up beside them — and swallowed the seven good ears whole.

Pharaoh woke up troubled and puzzled. What could such strange dreams possibly mean? In the morning, he called for all his wisest men and advisers, and he told them his dreams. But not one of them could tell him what they meant. And the more he wondered, the more worried Pharaoh became.

Then one of his servants suddenly remembered something. "There is a young man named Joseph," he said. "I have seen for myself — he can understand the meaning of dreams, with the help of his God."

So Pharaoh sent for Joseph at once. Joseph made himself ready, and he was brought in to stand before the mighty king of Egypt.

"I have had dreams that trouble me," Pharaoh said, "and no one in all my kingdom can tell me what they mean. But I have heard that you are able to understand such things."

Joseph answered humbly, and honestly. "I cannot do it on my own," he said. "But God can show us what your dreams mean." And then Joseph listened quietly, and God gave him the understanding.

"Your two dreams are really one message," Joseph explained. "God is showing you what is going to happen, so that you can get ready. Seven good years are coming to Egypt — years when the rains will fall and the fields will grow more food than anyone can imagine. But after those seven good years will come seven hard years, when almost nothing will grow, and food will be very hard to find anywhere."

"So here is what you should do," Joseph went on. "During the seven good years, do not eat up everything. Save some of it. Store the extra food away carefully, in great barns all across the land. Then, when the seven hard years come, there will be plenty saved up — and no one will have to go hungry."

Pharaoh was amazed. He had never in his life heard such wisdom. "God has shown all of this to you," he said, "so there is surely no one wiser in all the land. From now on, you will be in charge of everything." And the king placed his own ring on Joseph's finger, and fine robes upon his shoulders, so that everyone would know Joseph now spoke with the king's own authority. And so Joseph, who had come into the palace as a stranger, was given the great task of getting Egypt ready.

For the next seven years, while the harvests came in rich and golden, Joseph worked hard and wisely. Year after year, he gathered up the extra food and filled the great barns higher and higher, until there was more grain stored away than anyone could count.

And then, just exactly as God had warned, the seven hard years arrived. The rains stopped. The crops would not grow. People began to grow hungry — not only in Egypt, but in all the lands round about. Families everywhere wondered where they would find enough to eat. But Egypt had food, because Joseph had saved it. And so the people came, from near and far, and there was enough for everyone.

Because one young man, far from home, had listened to God and used his wisdom to help — a whole land full of people, and many, many beyond it, were kept safe and fed all through the hungry years.
