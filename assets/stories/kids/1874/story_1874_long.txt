Esther Becomes Queen

Once there was a girl named Esther.

She did not live in a grand palace. She did not wear fine clothes or a golden crown. Esther lived in an ordinary house, on an ordinary street, far away from any throne. But Esther had something more beautiful than the finest clothes in the world. She had a kind and gentle heart.

Now, Esther had no mother or father of her own to care for her. But she was not alone. She had a cousin, a good and faithful man named Mordecai. When Esther was very small, Mordecai took her into his home, and he raised her like his very own daughter. He gave her bread to eat and a warm place to sleep. He told her stories in the evening. He taught her to be brave and good and true. And Esther loved him with all her heart.

Each morning, Mordecai would walk to the city gate and sit with the other men. And each morning, before he went, he would look back at Esther and smile. "Be kind today, little one," he would say. And she would.

Now, in the land where Esther lived, there was a great and powerful king. He lived in a palace so big you could walk all day and never see every room. And one day, the king decided it was time to choose a new queen.

So the king sent his helpers out across the whole kingdom. Down every road they went. Into every town. Along every street they walked, searching for the kindest and loveliest young woman in all the land, to come and live at the palace.

And one day, as they searched, the king's helpers came to the place where Esther lived. They saw her gentle face. They saw her quiet, kind way. And they stopped, and they looked at one another, and they said, "Surely this one must come to the palace."

So Esther had to say goodbye to her home. She had to say goodbye to her ordinary street and her ordinary house. And hardest of all, she had to say goodbye to Mordecai, who had loved her since she was small.

Mordecai walked with her to the palace gate. He held her hand the whole way. "Do not be afraid," he told her softly. "Be kind, as you have always been. And whatever happens, I will not be far away." And do you know what he did? Every single day after that, Mordecai walked to the palace courtyard. He could not go inside. But he walked there each morning, just to be near, just to make sure his Esther was well.

And oh — when Esther stepped inside that palace, what a place it was! She had never seen anything like it in all her life. There were enormous doors, taller than three men standing one upon another. There were floors of shining marble, smooth and cool beneath her feet. There were golden lamps that filled every room with a soft, warm glow. And there were robes — beautiful robes, made of the finest, softest cloth, in colors like jewels — and they were given to Esther to wear.

But Esther was not the only young woman in the palace. Many others had been brought there too, each one hoping to be chosen as queen. And as the days passed, something began to happen. The helpers who looked after the young women began to notice Esther. Not because she pushed to the front. Not because she asked for the finest things. But because she was gentle. Because she said thank you. Because she was kind even when she thought no one was watching.

Day after day, Esther waited. And day after day, the people of the palace grew to love her more and more. The maids who brought her food loved her. The helpers who walked the halls loved her. Everyone who met Esther could not help but love her, for it was not her beautiful robes that made her special. It was her heart.

And all that while, just outside, Mordecai kept walking to the courtyard each morning, watching, and waiting, and hoping for his Esther.

At last, the day came. Esther was brought before the king himself. She stepped through the great tall doors. She walked across the shining floor. And the very moment the king saw her — her kind face, her gentle ways, her quiet and humble heart — he loved her more than any other young woman in all the land.

So the king reached for a golden crown. And he placed it, ever so gently, upon Esther's head. And from that day on, the ordinary girl from the ordinary street was the queen.

Just think of it! Only a little while before, Esther had been a girl with no palace and no crown at all. She had walked ordinary streets and slept in an ordinary house. And now, here she stood — Queen Esther, with a crown of gold and a whole palace around her.

And somewhere out in the courtyard, Mordecai heard the news. And he smiled the biggest smile of all.

But here is the most wonderful part of the whole story. Something nobody knew yet — not the king, not the helpers, not even Mordecai, and not even Esther herself.

God had placed Esther in that palace for a reason.

All those years, while Mordecai raised her with such love — God was watching. While the helpers searched the land and found her — God was working. While Esther waited, and was kind, and was loved — God was quietly, gently making a way. You could not see Him. You could not hear Him. But He was there in every step, working out a plan so good that no one could have guessed it.

For one day soon, Esther's people would be in great danger. And brave Queen Esther — kind, gentle Esther — would be the very one God would use to help save them all.

She did not know it yet. But God knew. God had placed her exactly where she needed to be, at exactly the right time, for exactly the right reason. That is the kind of God He is — a God who is working, even when we cannot see Him. A God who loves an ordinary girl, and never, ever leaves her on her own.

But that, little one, is a story for another day.
