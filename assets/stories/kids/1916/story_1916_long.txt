The Army That Sang

One day, frightening news came rushing into the city to King Jehoshaphat. Messengers ran in, all out of breath. "O King!" they cried. "A huge army is marching toward us — a great crowd of soldiers from far away, far too many to count. And they are already on their way!"

When the king heard it, he was afraid. And when the people heard it, they were afraid too. An army that big! What could they ever do? They had no army anywhere near so large. It seemed there was no way they could win.

But King Jehoshaphat, even though he was afraid, knew exactly what to do with his fear. He did not run, and he did not panic. He turned to God.

He called for all the people to gather together — every single one, from every town and village across the whole land. And they came. Mothers and fathers came. Grandmas and grandpas came, leaning on their walking sticks. Boys and girls came. Even the tiniest babies came, held close in their parents' arms. The whole great crowd of them stood together in one place before God. And the king stood up in front of them all, and he prayed out loud.

"O God," he prayed, and he was completely honest about it, "we do not know what to do. This army coming against us is far, far too big for us. We are not strong enough. But, God — our eyes are on you. We are looking to you. Please help us."

And God heard that honest prayer. And God answered.

Right there in the middle of the crowd, the Spirit of God came upon a man, and he began to speak God's own words, loud and clear, so that everyone could hear. "Listen, all of you!" he said. "King, and all you people — do not be afraid. Do not be frightened of that great big army. Because this battle is not yours. It belongs to God. You will not even need to fight it. Tomorrow, just go out and face them, and then stand still, and stay calm, and watch what God will do to save you."

Imagine hearing that! You will not even need to fight. Just trust, and watch.

So the very next morning, the people got up early and did something that no army in all the world had ever done before. King Jehoshaphat gathered everyone, and then he chose who would go out in front. He did not pick his biggest, strongest soldiers to lead the way. No — he picked the singers. He sent the singers out in front of everyone!

And as all the people walked out of the city, toward that huge and frightening army, they did not shake with fear, and they did not turn around and run. Instead, they opened their mouths, and they began to sing. They lifted up their voices to God, loud and happy and strong: "Give thanks to the Lord, for his love goes on forever and ever!"

And here is the most wonderful thing of all. At the very moment they began to sing — that very moment — God set to work. He took care of that whole enormous army, all by himself, in his own mighty way. And by the time God's people came up over the last hill and looked down, the danger was simply gone. Completely gone. They had not had to lift a single finger. They had only had to trust God, and walk, and sing.

The army had left so many good things behind that it took God's people three whole days just to gather it all up — more than they could carry in one trip. And on the fourth day, they all came together in a wide valley and praised God, thanking him over and over, until their voices filled the whole place and echoed off the hills.

Then they turned, and they marched all the way home — and they sang the entire way, every step of the journey, their joyful voices ringing out across the land. And all the countries round about heard the story of how God himself had fought for his people, so that they did not even have to lift a sword.

When something feels too big, and far too scary to face, remember the day God's people sang. You do not have to face the hard thing on your own. You can do just what they did. You can look up to God, and trust him with all your heart — and let him fight for you.
