David Plays for King Saul

In a grand palace, with high stone walls and golden lamps, there lived a king named Saul. He had everything a king could possibly want — a crown, a throne, servants, and soldiers. But none of it could make him happy. For often, his heart felt sad and troubled, and try as he might, he could not find any peace at all. On those hard days, a heavy gloom would settle over him like a dark cloud, and nothing anyone did seemed to help.

His servants did not like to see their king so troubled. They talked together, wondering what might bring him comfort. "Perhaps some music would soothe him," one of them said at last. "Gentle music can quiet a troubled heart. Let us find someone who plays beautifully."

And one of the servants remembered something. "I know of a boy," he said. "He is a shepherd, the youngest son of a man named Jesse. He keeps his father's sheep out on the hills — and he plays the harp more beautifully than anyone I have ever heard. And he loves God, and sings the gentlest songs."

"Bring him to me," said the king.

So they sent for the shepherd boy, whose name was David. And David came to the great palace, carrying his harp in his hands — the very same harp he played out on the quiet hillsides.

From then on, whenever the sadness came over King Saul, and the heavy gloom settled down upon him, David would come and sit nearby. He would rest the harp against his shoulder, and he would begin to play. His fingers moved softly and surely over the strings, and out floated the most gentle, lovely music — the same peaceful tunes he had once played to his sheep, beneath the wide and starry sky.

And as David played, something wonderful would happen, every time. Slowly, the king's tight, tired shoulders would loosen. The heavy gloom would begin to lift, little by little, the way dark clouds drift apart to let the sunshine through. King Saul would close his eyes, and breathe slowly and deeply, and his troubled, restless heart would grow calm, and quiet, and peaceful once again.

For there was something in David's music — something gentle, and good, and full of God's own peace — that settled over the king and eased all his worry away. It was as if the peace David had found out on the hills with God came right into the room with him, riding on the music.

And so it went, day after day. Whenever King Saul was troubled, the shepherd boy would play his harp. The soft music would fill the great stone room, drifting into every corner — and quietly, gently, peace would come stealing back into the heart of the king.

And King Saul would rest at last, calm and quiet, glad in his heart for the gentle shepherd boy who had come to play for him. The music had done what no crown, and no palace, and no soldier ever could. It had brought him peace.
