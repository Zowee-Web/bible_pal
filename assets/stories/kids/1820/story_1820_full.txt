God Calls Samuel

Long ago, there was a boy named Samuel who lived and helped in the house of God. While he was still very small, his mother had brought him there to serve, and an old priest named Eli looked after him. Samuel slept at night in a quiet little room, not far from where the lamp of God burned. Night after night he had slept there, and nothing unusual had ever happened.

One night, Samuel lay down to sleep as he always did. The house was still. The lamp of God glowed soft and golden in the dark, not yet gone out. Samuel's eyes were just growing heavy when he heard a voice call his name.

"Samuel!"

He sat right up. Someone had called him! He jumped out of bed and ran to old Eli. "Here I am," he said. "You called me, didn't you?"

But Eli blinked, half asleep. "No, my child," he said. "I did not call you. Go back and lie down." So Samuel padded back to his room, and lay down again.

"Samuel!" The voice came a second time, clear in the quiet.

Again the boy got up, and again he hurried to Eli. "Here I am. You called me."

And again the kind old man shook his head. "I did not call, my son. Go and lie down."

It happened a third time. Poor Samuel was quite puzzled now, padding back and forth in the dark. And this time, as Samuel stood before him, old Eli understood. It was not Eli calling at all. It was God — calling this little boy by name, in the stillness of the night. How wonderful, that God would speak to someone so small.

A gentle smile came over Eli's face. "Go and lie down, Samuel," he said softly. "And if you hear the voice once more, you must answer it. Say this: 'Speak, Lord, for your servant is listening.'"

So Samuel went back to his bed. He lay down in the quiet, and he waited, listening with all his might.

And the voice came once more, soft and close in the dark. "Samuel. Samuel."

The boy was not afraid. "Speak," he said. "For your servant is listening."

And God spoke to Samuel there in the stillness of the night. From that night on, all through his whole life, Samuel listened for God's voice — the small boy who first learned to listen, there in the gentle stillness.
