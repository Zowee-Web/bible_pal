The Joyful Parade

One bright and sunny morning, Jesus and his friends came walking along the road toward the great city of Jerusalem. And on this special day, Jesus was going to be welcomed like a king.

Now, when a proud and mighty king came to a city in those days, he would ride in on a big, strong war horse, to show everyone how powerful he was. But Jesus was a different kind of king. So Jesus did not ask for a war horse at all. Instead, he sent two of his friends ahead to fetch him a little donkey — a small, gentle, ordinary donkey. And Jesus climbed up onto its back, and rode toward the city, slow and humble and kind.

When word reached Jerusalem that Jesus was coming, oh, the excitement! People came pouring out of their houses and down the streets, more people than you could ever count. They had heard how Jesus healed the sick, and blessed the children, and loved everyone. And they were so happy he was here that they wanted to give him a welcome fit for a king.

So they began to get ready, right there on the road.

Some of the people took off their very own coats and laid them down across the dusty road, one after another, to make a soft and special carpet for the little donkey to walk on. Others ran to the tall palm trees and cut down big green branches, and they waved them high above their heads — back and forth, back and forth — like flags in the wind.

And then everybody began to shout for joy, all together.

"Hosanna!" they cried out. "Hosanna! Blessed is the one who comes to us! Welcome, welcome to our king!"

The children waved their palm branches too, and skipped and danced along beside the donkey, laughing. The whole long road became a sea of green leaves and bright coats and happy, smiling faces, as far as the eye could see. It was the most joyful parade the city of Jerusalem had ever known.

And there, right in the middle of all the singing and the waving and the cheering, rode gentle Jesus on his little donkey. He did not need a war horse, or a golden crown, or a grand and shining throne. He was a king of love — and he was welcomed that day by all the glad and joyful people, singing him through the gates of the city.
