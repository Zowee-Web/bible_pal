The Joyful Parade

One bright and sunny morning, Jesus and his friends came walking along the road toward the great city of Jerusalem. The sky was blue, the sun was warm and golden, and on this special day, Jesus was going to be welcomed into the city like a king.

Now, in those days, when a proud and mighty king came to a city, he would ride in on a big, strong war horse — tall, and fierce, and fast — to show everyone just how powerful he was. People would step back from the road, a little bit afraid of him.

But Jesus was a different kind of king altogether. So Jesus did not ask for a war horse. Instead, he called two of his friends and sent them ahead with a gentle errand. "Go into the village," he told them, "and you will find a young donkey tied up there. Untie it, and bring it to me." So off they went. And they found the little donkey, just exactly as Jesus had said, and they led it carefully back to him.

It was a small, soft, ordinary little donkey — the kind a mighty king would never, ever ride. But that was exactly the donkey Jesus wanted. His friends laid their cloaks gently across its back to make a soft seat, and Jesus climbed up, and began to ride toward the city — slow, and humble, and kind.

When word raced on ahead that Jesus was coming, oh, the excitement that swept through Jerusalem! People came pouring out of their houses, and down the streets, and into the road — more people than you could ever hope to count. They had heard all the wonderful stories about Jesus: how he healed the sick, and made blind eyes see again, and gathered the little children up into his arms, and loved every single person he ever met. Many of them had been waiting and hoping to see him for ever so long. And now — here he was, coming to their very own city! They were so happy and so excited that they wanted to give him the grandest, gladdest welcome they could possibly imagine.

So they got ready, right there on the road, all in a joyful rush.

Some of the people pulled off their very own coats and laid them down across the dusty road, one after another after another, making a soft and colorful carpet for the little donkey to walk upon. Others ran to the tall palm trees and cut down big green branches, and they waved them high above their heads — back and forth, back and forth — like bright flags fluttering in the breeze.

And then everybody began to shout for joy, all together, until the whole city seemed to ring with the happy sound of it.

"Hosanna!" they cried out. "Hosanna! Blessed is the one who comes to us! Welcome, welcome to our king!"

The word "Hosanna" was their happiest way of shouting, "Hooray — God has come to save us!" And they shouted it again, and again, and again, until it echoed off the city walls and floated up into the bright blue sky. The little donkey plodded gently on through it all, carrying Jesus right into the middle of the celebration. Never had the road to Jerusalem been so full of joy. Can you shout it too? Hosanna!

The children joined right in too. They waved their own little palm branches, and they skipped, and they danced, and they twirled alongside the donkey, laughing all the way down the road. Mothers lifted up the smallest ones to see over the crowd. The whole long street became a sea of waving green leaves, and bright colored coats, and happy, smiling, shining faces, as far as the eye could see in every direction. It was, without a single doubt, the most joyful parade the great city of Jerusalem had ever seen.

And there, right in the very middle of all the singing, and the waving, and the cheering, and the dancing, rode gentle Jesus upon his little donkey. He did not need a war horse. He did not need a golden crown, or a shining sword, or a tall and lofty throne. He was a different kind of king — a king of love, and gladness, and peace. And on that bright and beautiful morning, he was welcomed by all the happy, joyful people, who sang him, and cheered him, and waved him all the way through the gates of the city.
