Jairus' Little Girl

In a town beside the sea, there lived a father named Jairus, and he loved his little girl with all his heart. She was twelve years old, and she was the light of his home. But she had become very, very sick. Day by day she grew weaker, until she could no longer get up to play. She lay in her bed, and Jairus sat beside her, and he did not know what to do.

Then Jairus heard that Jesus had come to the town. He had heard the stories — that Jesus made sick people well. So he ran. He pushed through the great crowd until he found Jesus, and he fell to his knees right there in the road.

"Please," he said. "My little girl is so sick. Please come and put your hands on her, so that she will get well."

Jesus looked at him with kindness. "Take me to her," he said.

So they set off together. But the crowd was thick and slow, and it seemed to take forever. And then — before they reached the house — some people came toward them with sad and heavy faces.

"It is too late," they said softly to Jairus. "Your little girl is gone. There is no need to trouble the Teacher now."

Jairus's heart broke. But Jesus turned to him, and his voice was gentle and sure. "Don't be afraid," he said. "Only believe."

When they came to the house, everyone outside was weeping and crying. Jesus looked around at them. "Why all this weeping?" he said. "The little girl is not gone. She is only sleeping." Some of the people did not understand him, and a few even laughed. But Jesus did not mind. He took the little girl's mother and father, and he walked quietly into her room.

He sat down on the edge of her bed. He reached out and took her small hand in his. "Little girl," he said, "it's time to get up."

And at once, the little girl opened her eyes. She sat up in her bed and looked around — and the first thing she saw was Jesus, smiling down at her. She was well again, all the way from her head to her toes.

Her mother and father could hardly believe their eyes. They laughed and they cried, both at the same time. Their little girl was up, and walking, and running into their arms.

Jesus watched them with a smile. Then he said something so kind and so ordinary that they would never forget it. "Now," he said, "give her something to eat."

And there was more joy in that little house than there had ever been before.
