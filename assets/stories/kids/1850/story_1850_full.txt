The Last Supper

It was a special evening, and Jesus wanted to share one more supper with his closest friends. So he sent two of them on ahead into the city. "You will find a large room upstairs, all ready and waiting," he told them. "Go and get everything ready for us there." And his friends went, and found it just as he had said.

When evening came, Jesus and all his friends gathered together in that quiet upstairs room. Soft lamplight glowed warmly on the table. The food was ready. And they were all together — the whole little family of friends — just exactly the way they loved to be. Jesus looked around the table at each of their faces — friends he had walked so many dusty miles with, friends he had laughed with and prayed with — and his heart was full of love for them.

But Jesus knew something that his friends did not know yet. He knew that very soon, he would have to go away from them for a while. And oh, how he loved them. He loved them all the way to the very end, with his whole heart. So on this last quiet evening together, he wanted to give them something to hold onto — something that would help them remember him, and remember his love, whenever he was gone.

During the supper, Jesus picked up a loaf of bread. He held it gently in his hands, and he gave thanks to God. Then he broke the bread and shared a piece with each one of his friends, passing it around the table, one by one.

Then Jesus took a cup, and once again he gave thanks to God. And he passed the cup around the table too, so that every one of them could share it together.

"Whenever you eat this bread, and drink this cup," Jesus told them, gently and lovingly, "remember me. Remember that I love you. And remember that I am always, always with you."

His friends did not understand all of it yet. There was so much, that night, that they could not see. But they could feel how much love filled that little room, warm as the lamplight. They ate together. They sat close together. And when the supper was over, they sang a song to God, right there in the soft lamplight, before they went out into the night. It was peaceful, and quiet, and good.

And ever since that evening, all around the whole wide world, people who love Jesus still do what he showed his friends to do. They share a little bread, and a cup, together — to remember him, and to remember that he loves them, even now. Big people and little people, in great stone churches and quiet little homes, all remembering the very same thing: Jesus loves us, and Jesus is near.

So whenever you miss someone you love, or you feel far away from Jesus, you can remember it too, just like his friends did: Jesus loves you. And he is always, always near.
