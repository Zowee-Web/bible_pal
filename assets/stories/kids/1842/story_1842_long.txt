Fire on Mount Carmel

In the days of the prophet Elijah, a sad thing had happened among God's people. Many of them had forgotten the true God — the God who had made the sky, and the sea, and every living thing. Instead, they had begun to bow down to a pretend god, a lifeless statue called Baal. But Baal was not real. He could not see, and he could not hear, and he could not help anyone at all.

And because the people had wandered so far from God, the land itself had grown dry. For a long, long time, no rain had fallen at all. The rivers shrank, the grass turned brown and crackly, and everyone, everywhere, longed for the rain to come again.

Elijah loved the one true God, and it made him sad to see the people so confused, and so lost. So he called everyone to come together, up on the top of a great mountain called Mount Carmel. And there stood Elijah — just one man, all by himself — before an enormous crowd of people, and all the prophets of Baal besides.

"How long will you keep wobbling back and forth between two gods?" Elijah asked them gently. "Today, let us find out for certain who is really God. Here is what we will do. The prophets of Baal will build an altar, and I will build an altar to the Lord. We will each say our prayers. And the God who answers by sending down fire from heaven — he is the one true and living God."

"Yes!" cried all the people. "That is a fair test."

So the prophets of Baal went first. They built their altar, and they began to call out to Baal. "O Baal! Answer us! Send us fire!" They called all morning long. They shouted his name, louder and louder and louder. They danced round and round their altar. But nothing happened. There was no voice. There was no fire. There was not so much as a whisper. They kept on calling, all the way into the afternoon — but of course, Baal could not answer, for Baal was not really there at all.

At last, Elijah said, "Come close to me now." And all the people gathered around to watch.

Elijah built his altar carefully, out of twelve large stones. He laid the wood upon it. And then he did a most surprising thing. "Fill four big jars with water," he said, "and pour it all over the altar." So they did. "Now do it again," said Elijah. And they poured a second time. "And a third time," he said. So they poured water over the altar three whole times, until it was soaking, dripping wet, and the water ran all the way down and filled a little trench around the bottom. Now everyone could see, plain as day: no ordinary fire could ever burn something so wet. Only the true and living God could do a thing like that.

Then Elijah stepped forward, and he prayed. It was not a loud, shouting prayer. It was just one quiet, trusting prayer, spoken gently from the heart. "O Lord," he said, "let everyone here know today that you are the true God, and that you are turning their hearts back to you again."

And the very moment Elijah finished speaking — whoosh! — fire came rushing down out of heaven! It blazed up bright, and roared over the altar. It burned up the offering, and the wood, and even the stones themselves, and it dried up every last drop of the water, too.

The people gasped. And then, all together, they fell down on their knees, full of wonder and awe. And they began to call out the name of the one true God. For in the Bible, God's own special name is Yahweh. And the people cried it out together, over and over, with all their hearts: "Yahweh, he is God! Yahweh, he is God!"

That day, the people remembered who God really was. And they turned their hearts back to him.

And then — at long last — Elijah told the people that the rain was coming home again. He climbed up high on the mountain, and he bowed his head down low, and he prayed once more. At first, the sky was clear and empty. But then, far out over the sea, a tiny cloud appeared — no bigger than a person's hand. And it grew, and it grew, until the whole sky turned dark and heavy and full. The wind began to blow. And then, after all that long, dry time — down came the rain! It poured, and it splashed, and it soaked the thirsty ground, and ran in happy little streams, and watered the whole tired land.

God had sent the fire. And God had sent the rain. And brave Elijah, who had stood so bravely all alone for the true God, lifted up his heart and gave thanks. For he had known it all along — that the Lord is the one true God, who hears, and answers, and loves his people still.
