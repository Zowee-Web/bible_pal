Fire on Mount Carmel

In the days of the prophet Elijah, many people had forgotten the true God. They prayed instead to a pretend god named Baal — a statue that could not see, or hear, or help them at all.

So Elijah climbed up Mount Carmel, and all the people came to watch. There stood Elijah, just one man, brave and sure.

"Let us find out who the true God is," he said. "Let the prophets of Baal build an altar, and I will build an altar to God. Then we will each pray. The one who answers with fire from heaven — he is the true God."

So the prophets of Baal prayed first. They called out to Baal all morning. They shouted, louder and louder. But nothing happened. No voice. No fire. Only silence, for Baal could not answer.

Then it was Elijah's turn. He built an altar of stones. And then — just to show how great God is — he poured water all over it, until it was dripping wet. Once, twice, three times.

Elijah prayed one quiet prayer. "God, let everyone know that you are the true God."

And at once, fire came rushing down from heaven! It burned up the offering and the wood and the stones, and dried up all the water too.

The people fell to their knees in wonder. And they began to call out God's special name — for in the Bible, God's own name is Yahweh. Over and over they cried it: "Yahweh, he is God! Yahweh, he is God!"

That day, everyone remembered who God really was. And Elijah, who had stood so bravely, gave thanks in his heart.
