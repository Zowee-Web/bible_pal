Fire on Mount Carmel

In the days of the prophet Elijah, something sad had happened: many of God's people had forgotten him. Instead of praying to the God who made the heavens and the earth, they bowed down to a pretend god — a statue called Baal, that could not see, or hear, or help anyone at all.

Elijah loved the true God, and it made him sad to see the people so confused. So he gathered everyone together on top of a mountain called Mount Carmel. There stood Elijah, just one man, before a great crowd of people.

"How long will you keep wobbling between two gods?" he asked them gently. "Today, let us find out for certain who is real. Let the prophets of Baal build an altar, and I will build an altar to the true God. Then we will each pray. The God who answers by sending fire from heaven — he is the true and living God."

"Yes!" cried the people. "That is fair."

So the prophets of Baal went first. They built their altar and called out to Baal. They called all morning long. They shouted his name, louder and louder. They danced around the altar. But nothing happened. No voice. No fire. Not even a whisper. They called until the afternoon, but Baal could not answer.

Then Elijah said, "Come close to me now." And the people gathered round to watch.

Elijah built an altar out of twelve stones. And then he did something surprising. "Pour water over it," he said. So they soaked the altar with water — once, twice, three whole times — until it was dripping wet and a little pool ran all around it. Now everyone could see: only the true God could light a fire on something so wet.

Then Elijah prayed. Not a loud, shouting prayer — just one quiet, trusting prayer. "God, let everyone here know today that you are the true God, and that you are turning their hearts back to you."

And the very moment he finished — whoosh! — fire came rushing down from heaven! It burned up the offering and the wood, the very stones, and dried up all the water too.

The people gasped. Then they fell to their knees, full of wonder. And they began to call out the name of the one true God. For in the Bible, God's own special name is Yahweh. And the people cried it out together, over and over: "Yahweh, he is God! Yahweh, he is God!"

That day, the people remembered who God really was. And Elijah, who had stood so bravely all alone, gave thanks in his heart.
