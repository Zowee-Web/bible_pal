The Night God Set His People Free

In the land of Egypt, God's people had been slaves for a long, long time. Their parents had been slaves, and their grandparents before them — so long that hardly anyone could remember being free. From the first light of morning until the sun went down, they worked under the hot sun, making bricks and carrying heavy loads for a king who would not let them rest. They were tired, and they were sad, and they longed to go home. So they cried out to God to help them.

And God heard them. God had not forgotten his people — not for one single day. He had a plan to bring them out. And now, at last, the night had come.

Through his servant Moses, God told the people exactly what to do. "Tonight is the night," he said. "Get ready to go."

So in every home, the families did just as God said. They cooked a special supper together — and they baked their bread flat and quick, because there would be no time to let it rise. They marked their doorways, so that each home would be known as one of God's own families. And then they ate standing up, all dressed and ready — sandals already on their feet, walking sticks in their hands, their bags packed by the door.

The little children must have wondered at it all. "Why are we eating standing up?" they would have asked. "Why tonight?" And their mothers and fathers would have smiled and said, "Because tonight, at last, God is taking us home."

So they waited together in the soft lamplight, listening to the quiet, holding hands in the dark.

That night, God passed through the whole land of Egypt. But every home with the mark over its door, God kept safe. He passed right over them, and not one of his people was harmed. They were safe inside, all together, while God himself watched over them through the night.

And then, before the sun had even come up, the words they had waited so many long years to hear came at last. The king of Egypt said, "Go! Take your families, take your flocks — go, and be free!"

And so, that very night, God's people went out of Egypt. They scooped up their flat bread, still warm, and lifted the little ones onto their shoulders. Mothers and fathers, grandmas and grandpas, children and babies wrapped up warm, and bleating flocks crowding all around — a great, long line of them, walking out into the cool night air, free at last. No more bricks. No more hot sun. No more crying out. And out in front of them all, leading the way into the dark, went God himself.

"Remember this night, always," God told them. And they did. Every year that followed, they shared the very same special meal, and parents told their children the story, just the way it had been told to them: the night God reached down and set his people free.

God had heard them. God had not forgotten. And God brought them home.
