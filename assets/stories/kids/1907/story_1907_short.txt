The Night God Set His People Free

In the land of Egypt, God's people had been slaves for a long, long time. They worked hard under the hot sun, and they cried out to God, longing to be free. And God heard them. God had a plan to bring them out.

"Tonight is the night," God told his people. "Get ready."

So in every home, the families got ready, just as God said. They cooked a special supper. They put a mark over their doorways to show this was one of God's families. And they ate quickly, standing up, with their sandals already on their feet and their bags packed — because tonight, at last, they were going home.

That night, God passed through the land of Egypt. But every home with the mark over the door was kept safe. God passed over them, and not one of his people was harmed. They were safe inside, together, while God watched over them in the dark.

And before the sun even came up, the king of Egypt said the words they had waited so long to hear: "Go! You are free to go!"

So God's people went out of Egypt — mothers and fathers, grandmas and grandpas, children and babies and bleating flocks, a great long line of them walking out into the cool night air, free at last.

"Remember this night, always," God told them. And every year after, they shared the same special meal and told their children the story: the night God reached down and set his people free.

God had heard them. God had not forgotten. And God brought them home.
