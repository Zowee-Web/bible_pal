Zacchaeus

Have you ever wanted to be part of something so much, but you were too little to see, and nobody made room for you? That is just how it was for a man named Zacchaeus.

In the town of Jericho, there lived this little man named Zacchaeus. Now, Zacchaeus was not very tall — in fact, he was one of the shortest men in all the town. And I am sorry to say, he had not been very kind, either. His job was collecting money from people, and the trouble was, he often took more than he should have, and slipped the extra into his own pocket.

So you can guess how the people of Jericho felt about Zacchaeus. When he walked down the street, people turned their faces away. Nobody wanted to sit with him. Nobody invited him to dinner. And though Zacchaeus had a big house and plenty of money, deep down inside, he was often very lonely. He would look out his window in the evening at all the families eating together, and there he sat — all by himself.

Then one day, the most wonderful news came rushing through the streets of Jericho. Jesus was coming! People hurried out of their houses, calling to one another. "Jesus is coming! Jesus is here!" Zacchaeus heard the news too, standing in his doorway. Jesus — the one everybody was talking about, who healed the sick and loved the people nobody else loved. And oh, how Zacchaeus wanted to see him! Something deep inside him whispered that he simply had to.

So out he went, hurrying toward the road. But there was a problem. A huge crowd had already gathered all along the way, packed shoulder to shoulder, everyone pushing to get a closer look. And little Zacchaeus, being so short, could not see over them at all.

He stood on his tiptoes. He could see nothing but backs and elbows. He jumped up and down, as high as his short legs would jump, but the crowd was a tall, thick wall. He tried squeezing to one side. He tried peeking between two people, but they only stepped closer together. He tried wriggling forward, but the crowd would not let him through. "Out of the way," someone muttered, and nobody moved aside, not even a little. It was no use. All Zacchaeus could see were the backs of tall people, and Jesus was getting closer every minute.

His heart sank. After all this, was he going to miss Jesus completely?

Then Zacchaeus had a clever idea. He knew which way the road went. He knew where Jesus would pass next. So he picked up the edges of his robe in both hands, and he ran. He ran on ahead, down the road, past the crowd, his sandals slapping the dusty ground. And there, just where the road bent, he found exactly what he was hoping for — a big sycamore tree, with branches low enough to climb.

Up he went. Up, and up, and up, scrambling from branch to branch, higher than he had climbed since he was a boy. The bark was rough under his hands, and the leaves brushed his face, but he did not stop until he was sitting on a strong branch, high above everyone's heads. And from up there — oh, from up there he had a perfect view of the whole road below.

So Zacchaeus waited. He could hear the crowd coming nearer, a great happy noise of voices, rolling toward him like a wave. He held very still and watched the road, his heart thumping. Closer the sound came, and closer. And then, around the bend, there was Jesus, walking right in the middle of all those people.

Closer and closer Jesus came. Zacchaeus leaned forward in his branch, holding his breath. And then — Jesus stopped. Right underneath that very tree. The whole crowd stopped with him, wondering why.

Slowly, Jesus looked up. Up through the leaves, up through the branches, until he was looking straight at Zacchaeus, sitting there high in the tree. And Jesus smiled.

"Zacchaeus!" he called out warmly. "Hurry and come down. For today, I am coming to your house!"

Zacchaeus could hardly believe his ears. Jesus knew his name? Out of all those people, Jesus had looked up and found him — and called him by his very own name! And Jesus wanted to come to his house? But nobody ever wanted to be with Zacchaeus. Nobody, ever.

He did not wait to be asked twice. He scrambled down out of that tree as fast as his legs could carry him, branch by branch, and his heart was simply bursting with joy.

Now, all around, some of the people began to grumble. "Why is Jesus going to that man's house?" they whispered. "Doesn't he know what Zacchaeus is like?" But Jesus did not turn away, not for one moment. He had looked at Zacchaeus and loved him just the same.

And that day, being near Jesus changed everything inside Zacchaeus. The lonely man who had spent so long taking and keeping did not want to take and keep anymore. He wanted to give. He looked at Jesus, and his words came tumbling out, full of happiness.

"Lord," he said, "I will give back all the money I ever took from anyone — and I will give back even more besides!"

For Jesus had not minded the grumbling crowd one bit. He had not come for the people who pushed to the front. He had come looking for the one small person everybody else had walked right past — the one nobody made room for. He had stopped, in front of all of them, just to look up into a tree and say one lonely man's name.

And that is the kind of love Jesus has. He sees the ones the world looks over. He knows your name, and he comes near, and being close to him can make a sad and lonely heart brand new. So little Zacchaeus, who had felt so lonely for so long, climbed down from his tree a different man — happy that day, all the way down to his toes.
