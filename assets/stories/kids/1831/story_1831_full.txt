Zacchaeus

In the town of Jericho, there lived a little man named Zacchaeus. Now, Zacchaeus was not very tall — in fact, he was one of the shortest men in all the town. And I am sorry to say, he had not been very kind, either. His job was collecting money from people, and the trouble was, he often took more than he should have, and slipped the extra into his own pocket.

So you can guess how the people of Jericho felt about Zacchaeus. Nobody wanted to sit with him. Nobody invited him to dinner. And though Zacchaeus had a big house and plenty of money, deep down inside he was often very lonely.

Then one day, the most wonderful news came rushing through the streets of Jericho. Jesus was coming! Jesus — the one everybody was talking about, who healed the sick and loved the people nobody else loved. And oh, how Zacchaeus wanted to see him!

But there was a problem. A huge crowd had already gathered all along the road, packed shoulder to shoulder. And little Zacchaeus, being so short, could not see over them at all. He stood on his tiptoes. He jumped up and down. He tried peeking between elbows. But it was no use — all he could see were the backs of tall people.

Then Zacchaeus had a clever idea. He picked up the edges of his robe and ran on ahead, down the road where Jesus would pass. And there he found a big sycamore tree, with branches low enough to climb. So up he went — up, and up, and up — until he was sitting in the branches, high above the crowd, with a perfect view.

Closer and closer Jesus came. And then — Jesus stopped. Right underneath that very tree. Slowly, he looked up, through the leaves and the branches, until he was looking straight at Zacchaeus. And Jesus smiled.

"Zacchaeus!" he called out warmly. "Hurry and come down. For today, I am coming to your house!"

Zacchaeus could hardly believe his ears. Jesus knew his name? Jesus wanted to come to his house? But nobody ever wanted to be with Zacchaeus! He scrambled down out of that tree as fast as his legs could carry him, and his heart was simply bursting with joy.

And that day, being near Jesus changed everything inside Zacchaeus. He did not want to take and keep anymore. He wanted to give. "Lord," he said happily, "I will give back all the money I ever took from anyone — and I will give back even more besides!"

For Jesus had not minded the grumbling crowd one bit. He had come looking for the one person everybody else had walked right past. And little Zacchaeus, who had felt so lonely for so long, was happy that day all the way down to his toes.
