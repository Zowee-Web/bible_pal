The Burning Bush

Out in the wild desert, a shepherd named Moses was leading his sheep near a tall, quiet mountain. It was an ordinary day — until Moses saw something that made him stop and stare.

A bush was on fire. Bright flames danced all over it. But the strangest thing was this: the bush did not burn up. The flames burned and burned, and yet the little bush stayed green.

"How wonderful," Moses said. "I must go closer and see."

As he came near, a voice called out from the middle of the fire. "Moses! Moses!"

Moses froze. "Here I am," he answered.

"Do not come any closer," said the voice, gently. "Take off your sandals, for the ground where you are standing is holy." And Moses understood: this was God himself, speaking to him. Moses covered his face, for he was afraid to look.

"I have seen that my people are sad and hurting," God said. "And I am going to rescue them. Moses, I am sending you to help."

"But who am I?" Moses whispered. "I am only a shepherd."

"Do not be afraid," said God. "I will be with you, the whole way."

Then Moses asked, "When the people ask me your name, what shall I tell them?"

And God gave Moses something very special — his own name. "My name is Yahweh," God said. "Tell them I have sent you." It was God's holy name.

So Moses took off his sandals on the holy ground. And though the road ahead was long, Moses was not alone — because God had promised to go with him every step of the way.
