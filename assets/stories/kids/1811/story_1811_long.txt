The Burning Bush

It began as the most ordinary of days. Out in the wild desert, far from any town, a shepherd named Moses was leading his sheep, the way he had on a thousand quiet days before. He had been a shepherd for many years, and he knew these quiet hills the way you know the path to your own front door. He knew where the soft grass grew. He knew where the water hid among the rocks. On this day, he led his flock farther than usual, all the way to the foot of a tall mountain.

The sun was warm. The sheep nibbled and wandered. Moses walked slowly behind them, in no hurry at all. It was an ordinary day — until Moses saw something that made him stop and stare.

A bush, off among the rocks, was on fire. Bright orange flames leaped and danced all over it. Now, a fire in the dry desert was not so strange by itself. But the strangest thing was this: the bush did not burn up. The flames burned and crackled and climbed, and yet the little bush stayed green. Its leaves stayed whole and bright, as if the fire were only resting there, gentle as light.

Moses rubbed his eyes. He looked again. The bush was still burning, and still green.

"How wonderful," he said softly. "I have never seen anything like it. A fire should turn a bush to ashes. But this one stands as green as the morning. I must go closer and see why this bush does not burn up."

He left the sheep and stepped toward it, slow and careful. The fire did not shrink as he came near. It did not grow. It simply burned, steady and bright, as if it were waiting for him. Moses leaned in, peering at the leaves, trying to understand. He was so close now that the warmth touched his face.

And then a voice called out — not from behind him, not from the sky, but from the very middle of the fire.

"Moses! Moses!"

Moses froze where he stood. His heart pounded. The fire knew his name.

"Here I am," he answered.

"Do not come any closer," said the voice, gently but clearly. "Take off your sandals, for the ground where you are standing is holy ground."

And in that moment, Moses understood. This was no ordinary fire, and this was no ordinary voice. This was God himself, speaking to him from the flames. Moses quickly covered his face with his hands, for he was afraid to look at something so holy.

For a moment there was no sound but the soft crackle of the fire. Moses waited, his face still hidden, listening with his whole heart.

"I have seen my people, far away in Egypt," God said. "I have seen that they are sad and hurting, and I have heard them cry. Not one of their tears has slipped past me. I am going to rescue them. And Moses — I am sending you to help set them free."

Moses lowered his hands just a little. He could hardly believe it. Him? A shepherd, out here with the sheep and the rocks?

"But who am I?" he whispered. "I am only a shepherd. I am not strong. I am not important. How could I do such a big thing?"

It was the kind of question you might ask, too, when something feels much too big for you.

"Do not be afraid," said God, kindly. "You will not go alone. I will be with you — I will be with you the whole way."

Moses stood very still and let those words settle over him, the way a warm blanket settles on a cold night. He would not go alone. The God who made the stars, the God who saw every tear in Egypt, the God whose fire did not burn up the bush — that same God would walk beside him.

But Moses had one more question, a question the people would surely ask him.

"When your people ask me your name," he said, "what shall I tell them? Who shall I say has sent me?"

And here God gave Moses something very, very special. Not a sign. Not a stick or a stone to carry. God gave Moses his own name.

"I AM WHO I AM," God said. "My name is Yahweh. Tell them I have sent you."

Moses held those words close. Long, long ago, people had names that told a little about who they were. And God's name told the most wonderful thing of all: that he simply is, and always has been, and always will be. He needs no one to make him strong, because he is the One who makes everything strong. It was God's holy name, more precious than gold, and God had trusted it to a shepherd standing in the desert with his face wet from the warmth of the fire.

So Moses slipped off his sandals, one and then the other, and stood quietly on the holy ground. The dirt was warm beneath his bare feet. He did not understand everything that was about to happen. The road ahead would be long, longer than he could see, and Moses still felt small.

When at last he gathered his sheep and turned toward home, he walked a little differently than before. He kept looking back toward the mountain, toward the place where the fire had burned without burning up. The bush was only a bush again now. But Moses would never forget it.

Because God had spoken his name. God had seen the tears of his people. God had given his own holy name, Yahweh, into Moses' keeping. And best of all, God had made a promise that Moses would carry with him through every long day still to come.

He was not alone. He would never be alone. For the God of the burning bush had promised to go with him, every single step of the way.
