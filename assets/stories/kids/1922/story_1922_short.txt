Miriam's Song

The sea was behind them now. All of it — the deep water, the danger, every worried day — was behind them at last. God's people stood on the far shore with the sand warm under their feet, and they were safe. Truly, finally safe. They could hardly believe it.

For a moment nobody moved. They just breathed, and looked at the wide water, and felt their hearts grow lighter and lighter inside them. God had carried them all the way across, on dry ground, every single one. The danger was all behind them now.

And there was one woman whose heart grew so light it could not stay quiet. Her name was Miriam, and she was the sister of Moses. The gladness rose up in her, higher and higher, until it had to come out — and Miriam reached for a tambourine.

She lifted it high above her head. She gave it a shake, and it rang out bright and merry over the shore. Then her feet began to move. Miriam started to dance.

And she was not dancing alone for long. All the women saw her, and the joy leaped from heart to heart like a little flame catching. They caught up their own tambourines, and they lifted them high, and they followed Miriam down to the edge of the sea.

The whole shore filled with music. Tambourines rang and rang. Feet whirled and skipped in the warm sand. And Miriam sang out, strong and glad, and all the women sang the words right back to her:

"Sing to the Lord, for he has done a wonderful thing!"

Over and over they sang it. The women danced by the shining sea, tambourines lifted high, spinning and singing under the wide open sky. God had rescued them — every one — and their hearts were too full and too glad to do anything but sing.

So they sang. And they danced. And the glad, ringing, happy noise of a rescued people rose up over the water and floated out across the sea.
