Miriam's Song

The sea was behind them now. All of it — the deep water, the long worried days, the danger that had chased them so far — every bit of it was behind them at last. God's people stood together on the far shore with the warm sand under their feet, and they were safe. Truly, finally safe. After everything, they had reached the other side.

For a little while, nobody said a word. They just stood and breathed. They looked at the wide, shining water in front of them, and they could hardly believe where they were standing. God had brought them all the way across, on dry ground, every single one of them — the mothers and the fathers, the grandparents, the babies, the little children holding tight to someone's hand. Not one was missing. The danger was all behind them now.

And as they stood there, something began to happen inside their hearts. The worry that had sat so heavy for so long began to lift, and lift, and lift — until it floated clean away. In its place came something warm and bright and bubbling. It was gladness. It was thankfulness. It was joy, filling them all the way up to the very top.

Now there was a woman on that shore named Miriam, and Miriam was the sister of Moses. The joy rose up in her higher than in anyone. It filled her heart so full that her heart could not hold it, and it filled her hands so full that her hands could not stay still. The gladness had to come out somehow. It simply had to.

So Miriam reached for a tambourine.

She lifted it high above her head, up toward the wide blue sky. Then she gave it a shake — and it rang out, bright and merry and clear, ringing all the way down the shore. She shook it again, and again, and the happy sound went dancing out over the water. And then Miriam's feet began to move. Right there in the warm sand, by the edge of the sea, Miriam began to dance.

She did not dance alone for very long.

All up and down the shore, the women saw her. They saw Miriam dancing, and they heard her tambourine ringing, and the joy leaped from one heart to the next like a little flame catching and spreading. One by one, the women caught up their own tambourines. They lifted them high, just like Miriam. And they came hurrying down to the water to follow her, until the whole shore was full of women dancing by the sea.

Oh, what a sound it was! Tambourines rang and rang and rang. Bare feet whirled and skipped and stamped in the warm sand. The women spun in circles, their arms lifted up, their tambourines shaking high above their heads, every face shining with gladness.

And in the middle of it all, Miriam lifted her voice and sang. She sang strong, and she sang glad, and she sang for everyone to hear:

"Sing to the Lord, for he has done a wonderful thing!"

And all the women sang the words right back to her. "Sing to the Lord, for he has done a wonderful thing!" They sang it over, and they sang it again, calling the glad words back and forth across the shore. Miriam would sing it out, and the women would sing it home. Their voices rose up together, louder and brighter, until the whole sky seemed to ring with the song.

This is the part to remember, the picture to carry with you. The women on the far shore, by the great shining sea — tambourines lifted high in the sunlight, feet dancing in the sand, voices singing out over the water. That is what God's people looked like on the day they were rescued. Not quiet. Not still. Singing. Dancing. So glad they could hardly stand it.

For God had done a wonderful thing. He had carried his people, every one, safely to the other side. And when God does something that wonderful, sometimes the only thing in all the world to do is to pick up a tambourine, and lift it high, and sing — and dance, and dance, and dance.

So that is just what they did. The tambourines rang on. The women whirled and sang in the warm sand by the sea. And the glad, ringing, happy noise of a rescued people rose up over the water, and floated far out across the wide and shining sea.
