The Good News Is for Everyone

There was a soldier named Cornelius, and he was good and kind. He loved God with all his heart, and every single day he prayed. He gave to people who were poor, and he helped whoever needed helping. Cornelius was a man who truly loved God.

But Cornelius came from a faraway people. He was not one of God's own people the way the others were. He had grown up in a different land, far from God's city, far from the place where the people gathered to pray. And sometimes, deep down, he must have wondered: Does God even hear a prayer like mine? I am not one of his own people. Am I really someone God loves too?

One afternoon, while Cornelius was praying, something wonderful happened. A shining messenger from God stood right there before him, bright as morning. Cornelius was afraid, the way anyone would be. But the messenger had only good news to bring.

"Cornelius," he said gently, "God has heard your prayers. He has not missed a single one. Every prayer you have ever prayed has come up to him like a sweet gift. Now send your servants to a town by the sea, and ask for a man named Peter. He has something to tell you."

Cornelius did not wait one minute. He sent for Peter right away.

Now, far away in that town by the sea, God was teaching Peter something too — something Peter had never understood before. God was showing him that his love is not only for one kind of person. It is not only for the people who had always known him. God's love is for everyone — every people, every land, every single person on the whole wide earth. No one is left out. Not one.

So when the servants came and asked Peter to travel to the house of Cornelius, Peter went, even though Cornelius came from a faraway people. He was beginning to understand.

And when Peter stepped through the door of Cornelius's house, his eyes grew wide. The house was full! Cornelius had not gathered just himself. He had called everyone he loved — his whole family, and all his friends, and all his neighbors too. They filled the room, shoulder to shoulder, waiting and hoping and wondering what would happen next. A whole houseful of people. People who had always thought, deep down, that the good news must be for somebody else.

Peter looked slowly around the room, at all those waiting faces. And a wonderful smile spread across his own.

"Now I truly understand," he said. "God does not have favorites! He does not love one kind of person more than another. He loves people from every nation on earth, every single one. And I have come to tell you the best news there has ever been."

So Peter told them. He told them all about Jesus — how much God loved them, how Jesus had come for them, how the door to God was open wide, and they were welcome, every one.

And then something happened in that room that no one there would ever, ever forget.

As Peter was still speaking, God's own Holy Spirit came down — right there, on every person in the house. On Cornelius the soldier. On the children. On the family and the friends and the neighbors. On every single one. Not one person was passed by. Not one was left out.

And the whole houseful of people — all those people who had thought they were outsiders, who had wondered if the good news was meant for somebody else — lit up with the joy of God. They began to praise him out loud, their voices rising together, their faces shining bright. They were glad all the way up to the brim, the way a cup feels when it is filled so full it spills right over.

They could hardly believe it. It was for them. The good news was for them — and it always, always had been.
