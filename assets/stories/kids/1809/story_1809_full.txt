The Wise Men and the Star

One clear night, far away in the East, some wise men were watching the sky, the way they did every night. They knew the stars by heart. But on this night, among all the familiar stars, they saw something they had never seen before: a brand-new star, bigger and brighter than all the rest.

The wise men gazed up at it in wonder. They had studied the old, old writings, and they knew what such a star could mean. "A new king has been born," they said to one another. "A very great king. Let us follow the star, and go and find him."

So they made ready their camels, and they packed gifts fit for a king, and they set out into the night. It was a long, long journey. Night after night, the bright star went on ahead of them, leading them over hills and across wide deserts and through faraway lands they had never seen. Sometimes the way was cold, and sometimes the sand stretched on for days and days. But each evening, when the sky grew dark, the star would shine out again — steady and bright — and the wise men would lift their eyes, and smile, and follow. They did not know how long the road would be, but they trusted the star, and they kept on going.

They traveled for many, many miles. And still, faithfully, the star went before them — until at last, one night, the star stopped. It came to rest, shining and shimmering, right over a little house in a small town called Bethlehem.

The wise men's hearts filled with wonder. They climbed down from their camels and stepped softly inside. And there he was — the child they had crossed the whole world to find: little Jesus, with his mother, Mary, beside him.

The wise men knelt down low, and for a long moment they were perfectly still. Then they opened the treasures they had carried so far from home, and they gave the child their very finest gifts: shining gold, and sweet-smelling frankincense, and precious myrrh.

They had set out following a star, not quite knowing where it would lead them. And it had led them here — to Jesus, just as God had planned all along.

And when, at last, the wise men journeyed home again, they carried the good news in their hearts the whole way: the King they had searched the heavens for had truly, wonderfully come.
