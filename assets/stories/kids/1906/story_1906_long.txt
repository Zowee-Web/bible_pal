Joseph Far From Home

Joseph was a long, long way from home.

He had grown up the favorite of his father, safe among his big family, with a beautiful coat his father had given him. But his older brothers had grown jealous of that coat, and jealous of their father's love. And one terrible day, they took the coat away and sent Joseph off with strangers — all the way down to Egypt, a land far from home, where the people spoke words he did not know and no one had ever heard his name.

How Joseph must have missed his father. He must have lain awake those first nights, missing the smell of home, missing the sound of his own language, wondering how he had ended up so far from everyone he loved. It would have been easy to feel that God was far away too.

But God was not far away. Even in that strange land, far from everyone who loved him, Joseph was not alone. God was with him.

Joseph was brought to work in the grand house of an important man named Potiphar. Now, Joseph could have sulked. He could have decided that since life was unfair, he would stop trying. Instead, he got up each morning and worked hard and did what was right, day after day, even when no one was watching. And God was with him in it. Everything Joseph put his hands to seemed to turn out well. Potiphar watched him, and was amazed, and little by little he trusted Joseph with more and more — until at last he put Joseph in charge of his whole house. For he could see that God was with this young man, and made everything he did go well.

But then something unfair happened again. Joseph was blamed for something he had not done. He had done nothing wrong at all — and still, he was taken away and shut up in a prison.

It was dark in that prison, and quiet, and lonely. The days were long. Anyone would have wondered if God had finally forgotten them, in a place as low and dark as that. Surely, here, Joseph was on his own.

But he was not. God had not forgotten Joseph. God was right there beside him in the dark, just as faithfully as he had always been. And little by little, even in that prison, the things Joseph did began to go well again. The prison keeper watched him, the same way Potiphar once had. He came to trust Joseph completely, and put him in charge of all the other prisoners.

One night, two of the king's own servants, who had been locked up there too, each had a strange and troubling dream. In the morning their faces were heavy with worry. Joseph noticed. "Why do you look so sad today?" he asked them. "We have each had a dream," they said, "and there is no one here to tell us what they mean."

"God knows what dreams mean," Joseph said gently. "Tell them to me." So they did. And God helped Joseph understand. He told the first man his dream meant good news — soon he would go free and serve the king again. And just as Joseph said, in three days it all came true, exactly so.

"When all is well with you again," Joseph had asked him, "please remember me. Tell the king I am here." The man promised. But once he was free and back in the king's bright halls, he forgot all about Joseph in the prison.

So Joseph waited. The days became weeks, and the weeks became months, and the months stretched on and on — and still he sat in that prison, remembered by no one at all. He must have wondered, on the long quiet nights, whether anything would ever change.

Yet he was never, ever truly alone. God was with him in the waiting, just as God had been with him on the long road, and in Potiphar's house, and in the dark. And God was doing something Joseph could not see — working quietly, patiently, behind the scenes, getting everything ready to lift Joseph up one day in a way that would astonish the whole land, and save more people than Joseph could count.

That part of the story was still to come. But this much was already true, through every step of the long, hard road: the brothers, the strange land, the years of work, the unfair prison, the waiting that would not end. In all of it, God never once left Joseph's side. God was with him, all the way through.
