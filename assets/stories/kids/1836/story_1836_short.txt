David the Shepherd Boy

Out in the green fields near Bethlehem, a boy named David watched over his father's sheep. He was the youngest of all his brothers, the smallest one, the one who was sent to mind the lambs while the others did important things. But David did not mind. He loved the sheep. He knew each one by name. When a little lamb wandered too far, he carried it home in his arms. And in the quiet of the field, he sang soft songs to God.

One day, a wise old man named Samuel came to David's house. God had given Samuel a secret. "I have chosen a new king," God had told him. "You will find him here, among this family."

So Samuel looked at David's brothers, one by one. The oldest was tall and strong. Surely, thought Samuel, this must be the one! But God said, "No. People look at how big and strong a person is on the outside. But I look at the heart."

One by one the brothers came, and one by one, God said no. At last Samuel asked, "Are there any more?"

"Only the youngest," said the father. "He is out in the field with the sheep."

So they sent for David. And the boy came in, still smelling of grass and sunshine, his cheeks pink from the wind. And God said softly to Samuel, "This is the one."

Samuel took out a horn full of sweet oil, and poured it gently over David's head. It ran warm down his hair. God had chosen the shepherd boy — the small one, the one nobody else had thought to call. For God had seen, all along, the love inside his heart.
