Deborah, the Wise and Brave

Every single day, beneath a tall palm tree, in the cool of its green shade, a woman named Deborah sat and listened. She was one of the judges — the special, wise leaders God gave his people to help them. And Deborah was so very wise that people came from all over the land, walking up the dusty road, just to ask her what to do.

Some came with worries. Some came with quarrels. Some just did not know the right thing to do. And one by one, Deborah listened to each of them. She tilted her head, and she thought, and she waited quietly. Then she would tell them what was true and good and kind. God had given Deborah a wise and understanding heart, and she loved him with all of it.

But in those days, God's people were in great, great trouble. A powerful king who lived nearby had a huge army. He had hundreds of strong iron chariots — big rumbling carts pulled by horses, all made of hard metal. And that king was making life hard and frightening for God's people, day after day.

The people felt small. They felt scared. Year after year went by, and they hoped, and they prayed, and they wondered: would anyone ever come to lead them to safety? Sometimes a worry can go on so long that you start to think it will never, ever get better. That is just how God's people felt.

But God had not forgotten his people. He never does.

One day, God gave Deborah a brave and clever plan, and Deborah knew exactly what to do. She sent a message far across the land for a man named Barak. And Barak came hurrying to the palm tree, wondering what the wise judge wanted with him.

"Listen, Barak," Deborah said. "God has spoken, and he has a job for the two of us. Go and gather our people together, up on the top of the mountain. And do not be afraid. For God himself will go out ahead of us, and he will keep us safe. This is the day God will help us."

Barak's eyes grew wide. The mountain? The whole army? With all those iron chariots down below? It was a very big thing God was asking. Barak shuffled his feet. He felt nervous, and unsure, and his heart beat fast.

"I will go," he said slowly. "But only if you come along with me, Deborah. I am braver when you are there beside me."

Deborah smiled a warm and gentle smile. "Then I will surely come with you," she said. For Deborah was brave, with the courage God himself had given her. She was not afraid, because she knew Whose plan it was.

So together, the wise judge and the nervous soldier set out. And as they walked, more and more of God's people came to join them — until there were thousands and thousands of them, all climbing up the mountain side by side. They reached the very top and stood there together, looking down.

Below them, far across the valley, they could see the great enemy army. The horses. The soldiers. And all those iron chariots, gleaming hard and heavy in the sun. For one quiet moment, everyone held their breath and waited.

Then Deborah turned to Barak, and her voice was strong and sure. "Go, Barak!" she said. "This is the day! God has gone out ahead of us already. Don't you see? He is leading the way!"

And Barak went. He did not feel big, and he did not feel mighty. But he trusted what God had said. And down the mountain went all of God's people, following the plan God had given them.

And just as God had promised — God went ahead of them.

Down from the sky came a great storm! The clouds rolled in dark and thick, and the rain poured and poured and poured. The rivers rose up high. The ground turned to thick, sticky, slippery mud. And all those mighty iron chariots — the very ones the enemy was so proud of — sank down deep and got stuck fast in the mud, and could not move at all!

The proud soldiers slipped and slid and tumbled about. Their heavy chariots, that had once seemed so frightening, could not help them one little bit now. And so the great enemy army was beaten, and they turned, and they ran far, far away.

God's people were safe again. Every one of them. Not because they had the biggest army, and not because Barak was the strongest, and not because of anything they had done at all. They were safe because God had gone out ahead of them, just exactly as he said he would.

When it was all over, the rain stopped, and the sun came shining out again over the wet, glistening hills. And Deborah was so thankful, so full of joy, that she did the most wonderful thing. She sang!

She sang a beautiful song to God — a song of thanks, and gladness, and praise — and Barak sang it right along with her. The whole tired, happy crowd lifted up their voices on that mountain.

"The Lord is our strength!" they sang. "He is the one who kept us safe! He went out ahead of us, and he won the day!" And the song rose up to heaven like a bright and shining gift.

So remember this, little one. God gives his wisdom, and his courage, to all kinds of people — to boys and girls, to men and women, to the quiet and the small and the ones who feel afraid. God can use anyone who loves him and listens.

And here is the most wonderful part of all. Whenever God calls you to be brave, you will never, ever have to go alone. Just like he did for Deborah, and for Barak, and for all his people on that mountain so long ago, God will go out ahead of you — every single step of the way.
