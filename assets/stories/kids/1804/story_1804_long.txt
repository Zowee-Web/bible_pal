Jesus Walks on the Water

It had been a long and busy day, full of crowds and teaching. When the day was over, Jesus wanted to be quiet for a while. So he said to his friends, the disciples, "Get into the boat and go on ahead to the other side. I will come to you later." And he sent them off across the wide lake.

Then Jesus went up a quiet hillside, all by himself, to pray. The crowds were gone now. The evening was still, and the first stars were coming out. And there, alone with God his Father, Jesus prayed long into the night.

Far out on the lake, the disciples were rowing. At first the water was calm and the going was easy. But as the night came on, the wind began to blow. It blew harder, and then harder still, until the waves rose up and slapped against the sides of the little boat. The disciples gripped the oars and pulled with all their strength — but the boat would hardly move. The wind pushed them back almost as fast as they could row.

Hour after hour they struggled. Their arms grew tired. Their hands grew sore. The night wore on and on, and still the far shore seemed no closer at all. They were cold, and wet, and weary, and more than a little afraid.

But Jesus had not forgotten them. Even alone on the hill in the dark, he knew exactly where their little boat was, tossing far out on the wind-blown water. And when the night was at its very darkest, he came to them.

One of the disciples looked up from his oar — and gasped. Out on the water, something was coming toward them. A figure. Walking. Right on top of the rolling waves, as if the wild sea were a solid road.

The disciples were filled with fear. "What is it?" they cried, huddling together and gripping the sides of the boat. They had never seen such a thing in all their lives.

But then a voice came across the water — warm, and steady, and kind. "Take courage," it said. "It is I. Don't be afraid."

It was Jesus. He was walking across the stormy sea as easily as you might walk across a quiet garden path.

Peter, one of the disciples, could hardly believe his eyes. He leaned out toward the figure. "Lord," he called, "if it is really you — let me come to you on the water!"

"Come," said Jesus.

And Peter did a very brave thing. He swung one leg over the side of the rocking boat. Then the other. And slowly, carefully, he put his feet down — not into the water, but onto it. And the water held him! It was firm beneath his feet, like a path made just for him.

Peter let go of the boat and stood up tall on top of the sea. And then, step by step, with his eyes fixed on Jesus, he began to walk across the waves. The wind howled all around him. The water heaved and rolled beneath his feet. But Peter kept his eyes on his friend, and he kept on walking, closer and closer to Jesus. He was really doing it!

But then Peter looked away. He looked at the wild wind. He looked at the tall, dark waves crashing all around him. And suddenly, he was afraid. He forgot to look at Jesus — and the very moment fear crept into his heart, he began to sink down into the cold water. "Lord!" he cried out. "Save me!"

And right away — at that very moment, before Peter could sink any lower — Jesus reached out his hand and caught hold of him. He lifted Peter up and held him fast, safe above the waves.

"Why were you afraid?" Jesus said gently. "I am right here with you."

Together, hand in hand, they made their way back to the boat and climbed inside. And the moment Jesus stepped in, something amazing happened. The wind stopped blowing. The waves stopped crashing. The whole sea grew quiet and calm and still, smooth as glass beneath the night sky. Not a single ripple stirred.

The disciples could only stare. Their hearts were full of wonder, and one by one they bowed low before him. "Truly," they said, "you are the Son of God."

And Peter — wet, and safe, and warm again — sat in the boat as it glided gently toward the shore. He never, ever forgot the feeling of that strong and gentle hand, reaching out to catch hold of him the very moment he called.
