The World God Made

In the very beginning, before there was anything at all, there was only darkness. Everything was empty and still. And then God spoke.

"Let there be light," God said. And light came shining into the darkness, bright and warm! God saw the light, and it was good. He called the light "day" and the darkness "night." That was the very first day.

On the second day, God made the wide blue sky, arching high overhead.

On the third day, God gathered the waters together into deep blue seas, and dry land rose up between them. Then God covered the land with soft green grass, tall leafy trees, and flowers of every color, with mountains rising tall behind them. And God saw that it was good.

On the fourth day, God made the bright sun to warm the daytime, and the gentle moon and the twinkling stars to glow all through the night.

On the fifth day, God filled the seas with fish — great big ones and tiny darting ones — and filled the sky with birds. They splashed in the water and sang in the air. And it was good.

On the sixth day, God made all the animals: lions and lambs, rabbits and elephants, tall giraffes and tiny mice, creatures that hopped and crawled and galloped and climbed. And then God made people, and placed them in the beautiful world he had made. God looked at everything, and it was very, very good.

And on the seventh day, God rested. The whole world was quiet and peaceful and new.

The sun set softly over the seas and the green hills and the sleeping animals. Every star that God had made shone in the sky. And the good world God had spoken into being settled down for its very first, peaceful night.
