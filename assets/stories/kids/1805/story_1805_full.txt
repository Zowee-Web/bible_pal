The World God Made

In the very beginning, before there was anything at all, there was only darkness. There was no sun and no moon, no land and no sea, no birds and no trees — only quiet, empty darkness, everywhere. And then God spoke.

"Let there be light," God said. And light came shining into the darkness, bright and warm and beautiful. God saw the light, and it was good. He called the light "day," and the darkness he called "night." Morning came, and evening came — the very first day.

On the second day, God made the sky — the wide, high sky, arching far above everything. He stretched it out like a great blue roof over all the world, with room for clouds to drift and soft breezes to blow.

On the third day, God gathered all the waters together into deep blue seas, and dry land rose up between them. Rivers wound down through the hills, and waves rolled gently onto the sandy shores. But the land was bare and brown, so God covered it with life. Soft green grass spread over the hills. Tall trees lifted up their leafy branches. Flowers of every color opened their petals — red and yellow and purple and white. Mountains rose up tall, and gentle valleys dipped down low between them. And God saw that it was good.

On the fourth day, God filled the sky with lights. He made the bright sun to shine and warm the daytime. And he made the gentle moon and scattered the twinkling stars to glow softly all through the night, each one shining just where it belonged. There were more stars than anyone could ever count.

On the fifth day, God filled the seas and the skies with living things. Into the water he put fish — great big whales and tiny darting minnows, and shimmering fish of every shape in between. Into the air he put birds of every kind, to soar and to sing — tall storks and tiny sparrows, and bright birds with feathers of every color. They splashed and they fluttered and they filled the quiet world with sound. And it was good.

On the sixth day, God made all the animals of the land. Lions and lambs, rabbits and elephants, creatures that hopped and crawled and galloped and climbed. Some were great and tall, and some were tiny and quick. The whole earth was full of them. And then, last of all, God made people, and placed them in the beautiful world he had made. God looked at everything, every single thing he had made, and it was very, very good.

And on the seventh day, God rested. The whole world was quiet and peaceful and new.

The sun set softly over the seas and the green hills and the sleeping animals. Every star that God had made shone in the dark blue sky. And the good world that God had spoken into being settled down, safe and whole, for its very first, peaceful night.
