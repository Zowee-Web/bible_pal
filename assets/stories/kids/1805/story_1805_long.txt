The World God Made

In the very beginning, before there was anything at all, there was only darkness. There was no sun and no moon. There was no land and no sea. There were no birds, no trees, no flowers, no children — nothing at all but quiet, empty darkness, everywhere.

And then God spoke.

"Let there be light," God said.

And light came shining into the darkness — bright, and warm, and beautiful. It spilled out across the emptiness and chased every shadow away. God looked at the light, and he saw that it was good. He called the light "day," and the darkness he called "night." Evening came, and morning came. And that was the very first day.

On the second day, God made the sky. He stretched it out high and wide, like a great blue roof over all the world. He made room up there for clouds to drift along, slow and white and puffy, and for soft breezes to blow. Now there was a wide, open space above everything, reaching up and up, farther than anyone could ever see.

On the third day, God gathered the waters together. He pulled the seas into deep blue pools, and up between them rose the dry land. Rivers came winding down through the hills, sparkling as they went. Gentle waves rolled up onto sandy shores and slipped back again.

But the land was still bare and brown. So God filled it with growing things. Soft green grass came spreading over the hills. Tall trees lifted their leafy branches toward the sky, and some of them grew heavy with sweet, ripe fruit. Flowers opened their petals one by one — red and yellow, purple and white, more colors than you could ever name. Tall mountains rose up high, and quiet green valleys dipped low between them. And God saw that it was good.

On the fourth day, God filled the sky with lights. He made the great golden sun to rise in the morning and pour its warmth over the whole world. And when the sun went down, he made the gentle silver moon to glow in the night, and he scattered the stars — thousands upon thousands of them, more than anyone could ever count, each one set twinkling in just the right place. Now the day had its sun, and the night had its moon and stars, and the world was never fully dark again.

On the fifth day, God filled the seas and the skies with living things. Into the water he put the fish — enormous whales that spouted and dived, and tiny silver minnows that darted through the shallows, and shimmering fish of every shape and color in between. And into the air he put the birds — great storks with wide white wings, and little brown sparrows, and bright birds that sang sweet songs. They splashed, and they soared, and they swooped, and they filled the whole quiet world with happy sound. And God saw that it was good.

On the sixth day, God made the animals of the land. Out they came, every kind you can imagine! Lions with golden manes and woolly white lambs. Rabbits that hopped and elephants that lumbered slowly along. Tall giraffes that nibbled the very tops of the trees, and tiny mice that scurried through the grass. Creatures that galloped and creatures that crawled, creatures that climbed and creatures that leaped. The whole earth was full of them, every one a wonder.

And then, last of all, on that same day, God made people. He made them special — to know him, and to love him, and to talk with him. And he placed them in the beautiful world he had made, to care for all the animals and to enjoy every good thing in it.

Then God looked around at everything — at the light and the sky, the sea and the dry land, the sun and the moon and the stars, the fish and the birds, the animals and the people — at every single thing he had made. And it was very, very good.

And on the seventh day, God rested. He did not make anything new. He simply looked at his beautiful world and was glad. Everything was finished. Everything was good. And the whole world was quiet, and peaceful, and new.

The sun set softly over the seas, and the green hills, and the sleeping animals. Every star that God had made came out to shine in the deep blue sky. And the good world that God had spoken into being — out of nothing but darkness and his own kind words — settled down, safe and whole, for its very first, peaceful night.
