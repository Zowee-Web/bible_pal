Abraham and the Stars

Abraham loved God, and he tried to follow him in everything he did. He had left his old home, long ago, because God had asked him to, and he had traveled far to a new land, trusting God all the way.

But Abraham carried a quiet sadness in his heart. He and his wife, Sarah, had grown old together — their hair had turned silver, and many years had passed — and in all that time, they had never had a child of their own. More than anything else in the whole world, Abraham longed for a family. And by now, he had begun to wonder, deep down, if such a thing could ever truly happen for them.

One night, when Abraham was feeling this sadness, God spoke to him, gently and kindly. "Do not be afraid, Abraham," God said. "I am with you, and I have not forgotten you. Come — come outside with me."

So Abraham got up, and he stepped out of his tent, into the cool and quiet night air. There was no sound but the soft wind. And above him, the sky stretched out, deep and dark and still.

"Look up, Abraham," said God. "Look up at the sky. Now — count the stars, if you are able to count them."

So Abraham tipped his head all the way back, and he looked up. And oh — the stars! There were thousands upon thousands upon thousands of them, scattered like sparkling dust across the great dark sky. Some were big and bright, some were tiny and faint, and together they shone and twinkled, more than anyone could ever, ever count. Abraham looked and looked, and he could not even begin to number them.

"That," said God softly, "is how big my promise to you is. One day, Abraham, your family will be as many as all of those stars in the sky. There will be more of them than you could ever count."

Abraham stood there in the dark, with the cool grass beneath his feet, gazing up at all those countless, shining stars. He was old. Sarah was old. By everything he could see, it seemed quite impossible. And yet — God had said it. God had promised. And Abraham knew, deep in his heart, that God always keeps his word.

So Abraham believed him. He did not understand how it could happen, and he could not see how it would happen — but he trusted that God would do exactly what he had said. And that trust made God very glad. For Abraham could not bring the promise about himself, and he could not hurry it along. All he could do was trust the One who had made it — and for Abraham, that was enough.

And from that night on, for the rest of his long life, whenever Abraham looked up at the stars, his heart would lift, and he would remember: God had made him a promise as big, and as beautiful, as the whole wide night sky.
