The River That Stood Still

For many long years, God's people had wandered through the wilderness. But now, at last, they had come to the very edge of the new land — the good land God had promised to give them, long, long ago. They could see it, right there on the other side. There was just one thing standing in their way.

A river.

And not a small one. The Jordan River was deep, and fast, and very, very wide. At that time of year it was full right up to the brim, rushing past strong. There were no boats, and no bridge. How in the world could so many people — mothers and fathers, little children, and all their animals — ever get across a river like that?

But God had a plan. He spoke to Joshua, the leader of his people. "Here is what you must do," God said. "Tell the priests to lift up the holy ark, and to carry it down to the river. And tell them to step right in. Then watch — and see what I will do."

Now, this took real courage. The water was deep, and cold, and rushing by so fast. To step straight into it seemed a frightening thing. But the priests trusted God with all their hearts. So they lifted the ark up high onto their shoulders, and they walked down to the water's edge, and they did not stop. They stepped right in.

And the very moment their feet touched the water — the river stopped.

It truly did! Far away upstream, the rushing water piled up high, higher and higher, into a great heap, and stood there still, like a tall wall of water, waiting. And right there in front of everyone, the muddy riverbed dried up into firm, dry ground. Where there had been deep, rushing water only a moment before, now there was a wide, dry path, leading all the way to the other side.

So the people crossed over. Every family, every grandmother and grandfather, every little child, and every animal — all of them walked straight through the middle of the Jordan River, on dry ground, while the water stood waiting on either side. Not a single foot got wet!

When every last person was safely across, God told Joshua to do one more thing. He chose twelve strong men, and each one carried a big stone up out of the riverbed. They piled the twelve stones together, to build a special marker that would stand there always.

"One day," said Joshua, "your children will look at these stones and ask, 'What are these for?' And you will tell them: this is the place where God stopped the river, and made a dry path, and brought us safely home."

And the people never, ever forgot. For they had learned something they would tell their children, and their children's children: when you trust God, and take that first brave step, he can open up a way where there seems to be no way at all.
