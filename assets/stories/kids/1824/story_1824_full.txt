Mary Says Yes

In a little town called Nazareth, in a small and ordinary house, there lived a young woman named Mary. She was not rich, and she was not famous. But she loved God with a quiet and gentle heart, and God had chosen her for something wonderful.

One ordinary day, while Mary was sweeping the floor of the little house, God sent an angel to visit her. The angel's name was Gabriel, and he appeared right there in her room, and the plain walls shone bright as morning with his light. Mary had never seen anything like it in all her life. She caught her breath, and she did not know what to think.

"Greetings, Mary!" said the angel. "You are dear to God, and the Lord is with you." Mary was puzzled by his words, and wondered what kind of greeting this could be.

"Do not be afraid, Mary," the angel went on, in the kindest voice. "God is very pleased with you. I have come to tell you wonderful news."

Mary listened, her heart beating fast.

"You are going to have a baby," said Gabriel. "A very, very special baby. He will be God's own Son, sent to the whole world. He will be very great, and he will be a king, and his kingdom of love will never end. And you will give him the name Jesus."

Mary's eyes grew wide with wonder. It was the most amazing thing anyone had ever told her. "But how can this be?" she asked softly. "How can it possibly happen?"

"It will happen because God will make it happen," the angel answered gently. "And remember this, Mary — with God, nothing is impossible. Nothing at all."

Mary was quiet for a long moment, thinking it all over in her heart. It was a very big thing the angel was asking of her. Her life would never be the same again. But Mary trusted God. She believed that God was good, and that God's plans were good. And so, in the quiet of that little room, Mary gave her answer.

"I am God's servant," she said. "Let it happen to me, just as you have said." And a small, happy smile came to her face.

Then the angel went away, and the room was ordinary and quiet once more. But everything had changed. Mary stood there, holding the most wonderful secret in all the world — that God's own Son was coming, coming as a tiny baby, and that she, Mary, had said yes.
