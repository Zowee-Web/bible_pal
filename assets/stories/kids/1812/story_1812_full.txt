The Empty Tomb

Very early on Sunday morning, while the sky was still gray and soft and the birds were only just beginning to sing, some women walked quietly along a path toward a garden.

Their hearts were heavy and sad. Their dear friend Jesus, who had loved them and healed so many people, had died. Now they were walking to the garden where his body had been laid to rest, in a tomb cut out of the rock. They carried sweet-smelling spices, to gently care for him.

As they walked, one worry filled their minds. A large, heavy stone had been rolled across the door of the tomb. "Who will move the stone for us?" they wondered aloud. It was far, far too big for them to push aside on their own.

But when they came around the last bend and reached the garden, they stopped, and stared in surprise. The great stone had already been rolled away from the door! The tomb stood wide open — and inside, it was empty.

As they stood there, wondering and amazed, a shining angel appeared beside them, bright as lightning, in clothes as white as snow. The women were frightened and stepped back. But the angel spoke to them kindly and gently.

"Do not be afraid," the angel said. "I know you are looking for Jesus, who died. But he is not here. He is alive! He has risen, just as he promised he would. Come and see the place where he was lying."

The women looked, and it was true. Where Jesus had been laid, there was nothing left but the linen cloths, folded neatly where he had lain. The tomb was empty. And slowly, their sadness began to melt away, and in its place a great, bright joy rose up inside them. Jesus was alive! Jesus was alive!

"Now go, quickly," said the angel, "and tell his friends this wonderful news."

So the women turned and ran. They ran as fast as their feet could carry them, back along the path, their hearts bursting with the most wonderful news the world had ever heard. They could not keep it in. "Jesus is alive!" they called out as they ran. "He is alive!"

And it was true. It is still true today. That is why, every year, all around the world, people remember and celebrate the morning the tomb was found empty — the happiest, most hopeful morning there has ever been.
