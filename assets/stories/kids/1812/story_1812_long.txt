The Empty Tomb

Very early on Sunday morning, while the sky was still gray and soft and the birds were only just beginning to sing, some women set out along a quiet path toward a garden.

Their hearts were heavy and sad — as sad as they had ever been. Just a few days before, their dear friend Jesus, who had loved them and healed so many people and gathered the little children into his arms, had died. Now they were walking to the garden where his body had been gently laid to rest, in a tomb cut out of the rock. In their arms they carried sweet-smelling spices, the way you might bring flowers to someone you love, to care for him one last time.

As they walked along in the early light, one worry kept turning over in their minds. "Who will move the stone for us?" they asked one another. For a large, heavy stone had been rolled across the door of the tomb — far, far too big for them to push aside on their own. They did not know how they would ever get inside. But still, they kept walking, because love does not stay home.

Then they came around the last bend, and reached the garden — and they stopped, and they stared.

The great stone had already been rolled away! It lay off to one side, and the tomb stood wide open. Carefully, the women crept closer and peeked inside. And the tomb was empty.

As they stood there, wondering and amazed and a little afraid, a shining angel appeared beside them, bright as lightning, in clothes as white as snow. The women drew back. But the angel spoke to them, kindly and gently, the way you speak to someone you do not want to frighten.

"Do not be afraid," the angel said. "I know you are looking for Jesus, who died. But he is not here. He is alive! He has risen, just as he told you he would. Do you remember? He promised. Come — come and see the place where he was lying."

So the women looked. And it was true. Where Jesus had been laid, there was nothing now but the linen cloths, folded neatly and left behind. The tomb was empty. He was not there at all.

"Now go, quickly," said the angel, "and tell his friends this wonderful news!"

So the women turned and ran. With every step, their sadness was melting away, and something new was rising up in its place — a great, bright, bursting joy. They could hardly believe the wonderful thing that seemed to be true. Could it really be? Was Jesus truly alive?

And now, as they hurried along, they began to remember something. Long before this morning, Jesus had told them that this very thing would happen — that he would die, and then, on the third day, rise again. They had not understood it back then. But now the angel's words and Jesus' own promise came rushing together in their minds, and their hearts beat faster and faster with hope.

And then — oh, the most wonderful thing of all! — right there on the path, Jesus himself came to meet them.

He was alive. Truly, really, wonderfully alive, standing right in front of them in the morning light. "Greetings!" he said warmly, and he was smiling at them. The women could hardly breathe for the joy of it. They came and knelt down and held on to him, and they knew, deep in their hearts, that it was really him — their friend, their teacher, alive again, just as he had said.

"Do not be afraid," Jesus told them gently. "Go now, and tell the others that you have seen me."

So off they ran again, faster than ever, their tears all turned to laughter. They had come to the garden in sorrow, carrying spices for someone who was gone. And they were running home in joy, carrying the happiest news the whole world has ever heard. "Jesus is alive!" they called out as they ran. "We have seen him! He is alive!"

And it was true. It is still true today. That is why, every single year, all around the whole world, people remember and celebrate that morning — the morning the sad, slow walk turned into the gladdest run there ever was, when the empty tomb meant that Jesus was alive. It was, and it always will be, the happiest and most hopeful morning there has ever been.
