The Oil That Did Not Stop

Long ago, there lived a poor woman whose husband had died. She had two sons, and she loved them dearly. But she was in great trouble. Her husband had left behind a debt — some money that was owed — and she had no way at all to pay it. She was so worried she hardly knew what to do.

So she went to find Elisha, a man who served God and listened to him. "Please, please help me," she begged. "My husband is gone, and now I have nothing left. I do not know how we will manage."

Elisha listened kindly. Then he asked her a gentle question. "Tell me," he said, "what do you have in your house?"

The woman shook her head sadly. "Nothing at all," she said. "Nothing — except one little jar of olive oil."

Elisha's eyes brightened. "Then here is what you must do," he said. "Go all around to your neighbors, and borrow every empty jar you can find. Big jars and little jars — gather as many as you possibly can. Do not gather just a few. Gather lots and lots! Then go into your house, shut the door behind you, and start pouring your little jar of oil into all the empty ones."

Now, that seemed a very strange thing to do. How could one tiny jar of oil fill so many? But the woman trusted God, and she did exactly as Elisha said. She and her two boys hurried up and down the street, knocking on doors, borrowing every empty jar their neighbors could spare. They carried them home, armful after armful, until the whole house was crowded with empty jars.

Then the woman shut the door. She lifted her one little jar. And she began to pour.

And the oil flowed out — and it kept on flowing! She filled up one jar to the very top, then moved to the next, and filled that one too. And still the oil poured from her little jar, as if it would never, ever run out. "Bring me another!" she called to her sons. And they brought another, and another, and she filled them all.

At last she said, "Bring me one more jar."

But her son looked around the crowded room and said, "There aren't any more. Every single jar is full!"

And right then, the oil stopped pouring.

The woman could hardly believe her eyes. She ran straight back to Elisha to tell him. "Now," he said, smiling, "go and sell all that oil. You will have more than enough to pay what you owe — and enough left over for you and your boys to live on."

God had taken her one small jar of oil, and her trust in him, and he had made it more than enough.

So whenever your hands feel empty, little one, and you do not know what to do, you can bring them to God. He loves to take the little that we have, and fill it right up to the brim.
