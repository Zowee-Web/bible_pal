The Shepherd Who Knows My Name

There was once a shepherd, Jesus said — a good shepherd, who took care of his sheep.

When night came, the good shepherd led all his sheep into a safe place called a sheepfold — a cozy pen with strong walls all around and just one little gate. Inside those walls, with the shepherd himself watching over the gate, the sheep could lie down and sleep soundly, safe and warm, all night long. Nothing could get in to hurt them there.

Then, in the morning, the shepherd would come and call. And here is the wonderful thing about a good shepherd. He doesn't just see a big woolly crowd of sheep that all look the same. Oh no. He knows every single one of them. He knows the little lamb with the wobbly legs, and the old sheep with the gentle eyes, and the bouncy one who is always wandering off. He has a name for each and every one — and he calls them out by their names.

And the sheep lift up their heads, because they know that voice. It is the voice that has fed them, and led them, and kept them safe, every single day of their lives. So out through the gate they come, crowding close around him, ready to follow wherever he leads.

Now, if a stranger were to come and call them, the sheep would not follow him at all. They would huddle together, unsure, and stay right where they were — because they do not know a stranger's voice. But their own shepherd's voice? That they know. That voice they trust completely.

And so, all day long, the good shepherd leads them. He walks out in front, and they follow along behind, out to the green hills where the grass is sweet and they can eat their fill, and down to the quiet pools where the water is cool and still and they can drink.

And if ever any danger came creeping near — if a wolf slipped out from the shadows, or a fierce storm came rolling in over the hills — the good shepherd would put himself right between the danger and his sheep. He would face the wolf. He would face the storm. There is nothing, nothing at all, that he would not do to keep his sheep safe, because he loves them so. And when evening came again, he would lead them all the way home, and count them in through the gate one by one — and not a single one would be missing.

And do you know what Jesus said, after he told all of this? Jesus said, "I am the good shepherd."

He meant: that is how much I love you. I don't see you as just one face in a big crowd. I see you — you, all by yourself. I know your name. I know your voice. You belong to me. And I will lead you, and feed you, and stand in front of you, and keep you close, and safe, and near.

So when the world feels very big, and you feel very small, you can remember the voice of the Good Shepherd — the One who knows your name, and calls you his very own.
