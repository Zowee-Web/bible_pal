Noah and the Great Flood

Noah was a good man, and he loved God with all his heart. In all the busy, noisy world, he was the one who still stopped to listen when God spoke.

One day, God said to him, "Noah, a great flood is coming to wash the world clean. But do not be afraid. I will keep you and your family safe. I want you to build a big boat — an ark — strong enough to float upon the water. Make it large, with room inside for your family, and for two of every kind of animal."

It was a huge thing God asked. But Noah trusted God, so the very next morning, he began.

Plank by plank, day after day, season after season, Noah built the enormous ark. It was taller than a house and longer than you could throw a stone. His neighbors walked by and scratched their heads. "What is that strange old boat for?" they asked. "There isn't even any water here!" Some of them laughed at Noah. But he just kept on building, because he believed what God had told him.

At last, after a long, long time, the ark was finished.

And then the animals came. Two by two they came, from every corner of the land — walking and waddling and hopping and slithering up the long ramp. Great gray elephants and tiny brown mice. Lions with golden manes and woolly white lambs. Owls that hooted and rabbits that twitched their noses. In they all went, pair by pair, into the warm and waiting ark. Then Noah and his whole family climbed inside too.

And then God himself shut the great door behind them. Now they were safe.

Outside, the first drops of rain began to fall. Then more, and more, until the rain came pouring down from the sky. It rained for days and nights without stopping. The waters rose higher and higher — until, slowly, the huge ark lifted up off the ground and began to float upon the water.

The wind howled. The rain drummed on the roof. But inside the ark, it was warm and dry and calm. Noah moved among the animals, giving each one its food and a gentle pat. The great boat rocked softly on the water, like a cradle rocked by a loving hand.

And through the whole long storm, God did not forget them — not for a single moment. He watched over Noah, and his family, and every last animal, holding them safe upon the wide, dark water until the day the rains would stop.
