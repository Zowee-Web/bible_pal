Noah and the Great Flood

Noah was a good man, and he loved God with all his heart. In all the busy, noisy world, Noah was the one who still stopped to listen when God spoke.

One day, God said to him, "Noah, a great flood is coming. But do not be afraid — I will keep you safe. Build a big boat, an ark, strong enough to float upon the water. Make it large, with room for your family, and for two of every animal."

So Noah began to build. Plank by plank, day after day, he worked on the enormous ark, while his neighbors stood about and wondered what he could possibly be doing. Some of them laughed. But Noah just kept building, because he trusted God.

When at last the ark was finished, the animals came. Two by two they came, walking and waddling and hopping up the long ramp — great gray elephants and tiny brown mice, lions with golden manes and woolly white lambs, hooting owls and twitchy-nosed rabbits. In they all went, into the warm, dry ark. Then Noah's whole family stepped inside.

And then God himself shut the great door behind them. Safe.

Outside, the rain began to fall. It fell and fell, and the waters rose, until the huge ark lifted up off the ground and began to float. The wind howled, and the rain drummed on the roof all through the night.

But inside, it was warm and dry and calm. Noah fed the animals, and the ark rocked gently on the water, like a cradle rocked by a loving hand. And God watched over every single one of them, all the long storm through.
