Noah and the Great Flood

Noah was a good man, and he loved God with all his heart. In all the busy, noisy world, Noah was the one who still stopped to listen when God spoke to him.

One day, God said to Noah, "A great flood is coming to wash the world clean and start it fresh. But do not be afraid, Noah. I will keep you and your family safe. I want you to build a big boat — an ark — strong enough to float upon the water. Make it large, with room inside for your family, and for two of every kind of animal in all the world."

It was an enormous thing for God to ask. The ark would have to be huge, and it would take a very long time to build. There was not even any water nearby! But Noah trusted God. He did not argue, and he did not put it off. The very next morning, he picked up his tools and began.

Plank by plank, day after day, season after season, Noah and his family built the great ark. They sawed, and they hammered, and they fitted the boards together, and slowly, slowly, the enormous boat began to take shape. It grew taller than a house, and longer than you could throw a stone. And all the while, Noah's neighbors walked past and scratched their heads and laughed. "What is that funny old boat for, Noah?" they called out. "There's no water out here for miles!" But Noah just smiled, and kept on building, and kept on trusting — because he believed what God had told him, even when no one else did.

At last, after a long, long time, the ark was finished. Every plank was in its place. The roof was on, the rooms were ready, and the great door stood open wide, waiting.

And then the animals came.

Two by two they came, from every corner of the earth — walking, and waddling, and hopping, and slithering, and padding up the long ramp. Great gray elephants with swinging trunks, and tiny brown mice. Lions with golden manes, and woolly white lambs. Tall giraffes that had to duck their long necks, and little rabbits that twitched their noses. Slow tortoises, and leaping frogs, and buzzing bees, and birds of every color. Creatures of every shape and size you could ever imagine, all coming along in their pairs. In they all went, two by two, into the big, warm, waiting ark. And then Noah and his whole family climbed inside too.

And then God himself shut the great door behind them. It closed with a soft and solid thud. And inside, every single one of them was safe.

Outside, the first drops of rain began to fall. Pitter. Patter. Then more, and more, and more — until the rain was pouring down from the sky in great gray sheets. It rained for days, and it rained for nights, and it did not stop. The puddles became streams, and the streams became rivers, and the water spread out wider and wider across the whole land. The waters rose higher, and higher, and higher still — until, slowly, gently, the huge ark lifted up off the ground, and began to float upon the water.

Outside, the wind howled, and the rain drummed hard upon the roof. It might have been a frightening sound — but it was not frightening at all, not inside the ark. For inside, it was warm, and it was dry, and it was calm, and it was safe. Noah walked among the animals, giving each one its food, and a gentle pat, and a kind word. The lambs slept. The little mice nestled down in the straw. Day after day, Noah cared for them all — the big ones and the small ones, the fierce ones and the gentle ones — and not a single one was forgotten. Outside, the whole world had become water; but inside the ark, it was like one big, warm family, all kept safe together. And the great boat rocked softly back and forth upon the water — like a cradle, rocked by a loving hand.

And through the whole long storm, God did not forget them. Not for a single moment. He was watching over Noah, and over his family, and over every last animal tucked safely inside — holding them all, warm and dry and safe, upon the wide dark water, until the day the rains would stop.
