The Big Picnic

One bright day, a great big crowd of people followed Jesus up a grassy green hillside. There were so many of them that no one could ever count them all — mothers and fathers, grandmas and grandpas, and ever so many children, running through the grass.

They had come from far and wide, because they loved to be near Jesus. And all day long, on that sunny hill, Jesus taught them and healed them and cared for them.

But as the day went on, the sun began to dip low in the sky. And one by one, the people started to grow hungry. Their tummies began to rumble. There was no food anywhere on that hillside — no shops, no kitchens, nothing for miles around.

Jesus' helpers were worried. "How will all these people ever eat?" they said to him. "There are thousands and thousands of them. We have no food to give, and not enough money to buy it. Whatever shall we do?"

Then something small and wonderful happened. A little boy stepped forward out of the great crowd. In his two hands he held his very own lunch — five little loaves of bread, and two little fish. It was all he had brought for himself. But he held it up and offered it to Jesus.

"You may have mine," the boy said.

Now, five loaves and two fish is not very much at all — not for thousands of hungry people. But the little boy gave everything he had, with a happy and willing heart. And that was enough for Jesus to work with.

Jesus smiled, and he took the little lunch in his hands. He looked up to heaven, and he thanked God for it. And then Jesus began to share the food out among the people.

And here is the wonderful part. He gave, and he gave, and he gave — and somehow, the food did not run out! The helpers carried it through the crowd, passing out bread and fish, and there was always more. Everyone ate. Every single person on that hillside ate until they were full and happy. All those thousands of people, fed from one boy's little lunch!

And when everyone had eaten all they wanted, Jesus said, "Now gather up what is left, so that nothing is wasted." So the helpers gathered up the leftover pieces — and they filled basket after basket after basket.

The people could hardly believe their eyes. From one small gift, given with a glad heart, God had made far, far more than enough for all.
