The Big Picnic

One bright and sunny morning, Jesus went out to a quiet hillside, beside a wide blue lake, to rest a little while. But he was not quiet for long. For the people loved Jesus, and wherever he went, they followed after him.

And so they came. From every town, and every village round about, the people came streaming out to find him — walking along the roads, and over the hills — more and more of them, until there was an enormous crowd gathered together on that grassy green hillside. There were so many people that no one could ever have counted them all. There were mothers and fathers, and grandmas and grandpas, and ever so many children, running and laughing through the long grass. Thousands and thousands of people, all in one place — just to be near Jesus.

And Jesus was not bothered by the great crowd, not one little bit. He was glad that they had come. So all day long, under the warm sun, he taught them wonderful things about God. He made sick people well again. He gathered the little children close to him. He cared for every single person there, all the day long.

But as the afternoon wore on, the sun began to sink lower in the sky. And one by one, all across that hillside, the people began to grow hungry. Tummies started to rumble. The little children began to tug at their parents' sleeves and whisper that they were getting hungry. And there was no food to be found anywhere — no shops, no kitchens, nothing at all, for miles and miles around.

Jesus' helpers came to him, looking worried. "It is getting late," they said, "and all these people are hungry. But there are thousands and thousands of them! We have no food to give them, and not nearly enough money to buy it. Whatever are we going to do? Perhaps you should send the people away now, so they can go and find their own suppers."

But Jesus had a much better idea.

And just then, something small and wonderful happened. Out of all that enormous crowd, one little boy stepped forward. In his two hands, he carried his very own lunch — five small loaves of bread, and two little fish. It was every bit of the food he had brought for himself, for the whole long day. But he looked up, and he held it out, and he offered all of it to Jesus.

"You can have mine," the little boy said.

Now, five little loaves and two little fish is not very much at all — not for thousands and thousands of hungry people. Jesus' helpers might have laughed, or shaken their heads. What good was one boy's tiny lunch among such an enormous crowd? But the little boy had given everything he had, gladly, with a happy and willing heart. And that, as it turned out, was just exactly enough for Jesus to work with.

Jesus smiled, and he took the little lunch into his hands. Then he looked up to heaven, and he gave thanks to God for the food. "Thank you, Father," he said. And then Jesus began to break the bread into pieces, and to share it out among all the people.

And here is the truly wonderful part. The food did not run out!

Jesus kept on breaking the bread, and breaking it, and there was always more. His helpers carried it out, piece after piece, all through the great crowd — handing bread and fish to this family, and to that one, and to the next, and the next — and still, there was always more to give. The people ate, and they ate, and they ate. Every single person on that whole hillside had as much as they wanted, until their tummies were full and happy. All those thousands and thousands of people — fed from one little boy's lunch!

And when everyone had finished, and no one was hungry anymore, Jesus said to his helpers, "Now go and gather up all the leftover pieces, so that nothing at all goes to waste." So they went up and down the hillside with their baskets, picking up every single piece that was left behind.

And how much do you think was left over? Why, they filled one whole basket. And then another. And then another! They filled twelve great baskets, right up to the top, with the bread that was left over. Twelve whole baskets — far, far more food than the little boy had brought in the very first place.

The people could hardly believe their eyes. They had seen something truly wonderful that day. From one small lunch, given by one little boy with a glad and willing heart, God had made far more than enough — enough for everyone, with baskets and baskets to spare.

So you are never too small to give, little one, and what you have is never too little for God. When you bring him what you have, and share it with a happy heart — even something small — God can do more with it than you would ever dream.
