The Stairway to Heaven

Once, long ago, a young man named Jacob set out all alone on a very long journey.

He had said his goodbyes at home — to his mother, and to his father — and now he was traveling far, far away, to a distant land where some of his family lived. It was a journey of many, many days. And Jacob walked it all by himself, with only the road ahead of him, and his own quiet thoughts for company.

All day long he walked. And as he walked, he thought about how far behind he had left his home, and his family, and everything he had ever known. The farther he went, the smaller and more alone he felt.

Then, slowly, the first day began to end. The sun sank lower and lower in the sky, turning the clouds gold, and pink, and deep red. And then it slipped away behind the hills, and the light began to fade, and the cool of evening crept softly in.

Jacob stopped, and he looked all around him. He was out in the wild, open country. There was no town anywhere in sight. There was no house, and no warm bed, and no kind face — no one at all to say goodnight to him. Above him stretched the enormous evening sky, and Jacob felt very, very small beneath it.

But the night had come, and he was weary right down to his bones, and he had to rest. So Jacob looked about, and he found a smooth, round stone. He set it down on the ground, and he lay down, and he rested his head upon the stone, like a pillow. And there, out under the open sky, as the stars came out one by one, twinkling far above him, Jacob's tired eyes slowly closed, and he drifted off into a deep, deep sleep.

And as he slept, Jacob began to dream. And it was the most wonderful dream that anyone has ever dreamed.

He dreamed that, right there beside him, a great stairway appeared. It rose up out of the ground, and it climbed up, and up, and up — higher than the hills, higher than the clouds — all the way up into heaven itself. And the whole stairway was filled with light. For up and down its shining steps went the angels of God — beautiful, gleaming angels, climbing up toward heaven and coming gently back down to earth, up and down, up and down, on and on.

And then Jacob looked all the way up to the very top of the stairway. And there, at the very top, in glory and in light, stood God himself.

And God began to speak. His voice was great and strong — and yet it was so warm, and so full of love, that it was not frightening at all. "Jacob," God said. "I am the Lord — the God of your father, and the God of your grandfather, who loved you long before you were ever born. And I want you to know something. Listen closely, Jacob. I am with you. I will keep you safe, and watch over you, wherever you go in all the wide world. I will never leave you — not ever, not for a single moment. And one day, when the time is right, I will bring you safely all the way back home again. This I promise you."

And then, gently, Jacob woke up.

All around him, the night was still and quiet. The stars still twinkled softly overhead, just as they had before. And yet — nothing felt the same anymore. Jacob lay very still, and a deep sense of wonder washed all the way through him, from the top of his head right down to his toes.

"God was here," he whispered into the dark. "God was in this very place — and I did not even know it!" And he was filled with awe. "How wonderful this place is," he said softly. "Surely this is the house of God, and the very gateway to heaven."

But most of all, Jacob was no longer lonely. And he was no longer afraid. For now he knew — he truly knew, deep in his heart — that he had never really been traveling alone at all. God had been with him the whole time. And God would stay with him, every single step of the long road still ahead.

In the morning, when the sun rose, Jacob took the stone that he had slept upon, and he stood it up tall, like a little monument, so that he would always remember that place. And he made a promise that he would love and follow God all the days of his life. Then he set off once more down the road — but this time, he walked with his head held high, unafraid, because he carried God's promise tucked close in his heart.

So wherever you may find yourself, little one — even far from home, even in the deep dark of the night — remember Jacob, and his dream, and the promise that God made to him. For that very same promise is for you, too. You are never, ever alone. God is right there beside you, closer than you could ever know. He watches over you all through the night, while you sleep. And he will never, ever leave your side.
