The Brother Who Ran to Hug Him

After many long years away from home, Jacob was finally coming back. He had a wife now, and many children, and great flocks and herds. But as he traveled the last part of the long road home, his heart was heavy with worry. For somewhere up ahead lay his brother's land — and his brother's name was Esau. And Jacob was afraid to face him.

You see, long, long ago, when the two of them were young, Jacob had done something that hurt Esau very deeply. He had taken something that should have belonged to his brother. And Esau had been so angry, and so hurt, that Jacob had run away from home — far, far away — and the two brothers had not laid eyes on each other for years and years.

Now Jacob had to pass right through his brother's land to get home. And he had heard some frightening news: Esau was coming out to meet him — and four hundred men were coming with him! Was Esau still angry, after all this time? Was he coming to fight? Jacob did not know. And the not-knowing filled him with fear.

So Jacob did everything he could think of to make peace with his brother. He chose out a wonderful gift — a great many animals from his flocks and herds. There were goats, and sheep, and camels, and cattle, ever so many of them. And he divided them into groups, and sent each group on ahead, one after another, each with a servant to lead it. "When my brother Esau meets you," Jacob told each servant, "tell him this is a gift from your servant Jacob — and that Jacob himself is coming along just behind." Wave after wave of gifts, he sent on ahead, hoping to show Esau how very sorry he was.

Then, when night fell, Jacob was left all alone in the dark. And there, by himself, he prayed to God. "O God," he prayed, "I am afraid. Please rescue me. Please watch over me, and over my wife, and over all my children. You promised me long ago that you would always be with me — so please, be with me now." And God was there, in the dark, just as he had promised on the night of the shining stairway, so long ago.

When morning came, Jacob lifted up his eyes and looked into the distance — and his heart gave a great thump. For there was Esau, coming toward him at last, with all four hundred of his men. This was the very moment Jacob had feared for so long.

So Jacob walked out ahead of his whole family, to meet his brother first. And as he came near to Esau, he bowed himself down low to the ground — once, and again, and again, all the way down — to show his brother honor, and love, and how truly sorry he was.

And then it happened. But it did not happen at all the way Jacob had feared.

For Esau did not raise his hand in anger. Esau did not even slow his step. Esau began to run — he ran — straight toward his brother! And he threw both his arms around Jacob's neck. And he hugged him, and hugged him, and kissed him. And the two brothers stood there together in the morning light, and they wept — but oh, they were happy tears, the kind that come pouring out when something that has been broken for so very long is finally, wonderfully mended.

All those years apart, all that old, old hurt — in that one running hug, it melted clean away.

Then Esau looked up, and saw all the women and children gathered behind Jacob. "And who are all these with you?" he asked, with a great warm smile. So Jacob, his heart full to bursting, brought them forward one by one, and introduced his whole dear family to the brother who had forgiven him.

"And what about all those animals I met along the road?" asked Esau. "All those gifts?"

"They are for you, my brother," said Jacob, "to show you how very glad I am to see your face again."

At first Esau shook his head. "I already have plenty, my brother," he said warmly. "Keep what is yours." But Jacob wanted so much to give it, and at last Esau accepted, just to make him glad. And the two brothers, who had been apart for so long, stood together at last — not as enemies, but as friends.

So if you have ever had a falling-out with someone you love, little one, do not ever lose hope. Hurt does not have to last forever. Sorry can be said. Forgiveness can be given. And sometimes — wonderfully, and surprisingly — the very person you are most afraid to face will be the one who comes running, with arms thrown open wide, just to hold you close again.
