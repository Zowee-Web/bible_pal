Naaman and the Servant Girl

Far from home, in the house of a great army commander named Naaman, there lived a little servant girl. She had been taken from her own land and her own family, and now she worked in Naaman's house. Yet even so, her heart stayed kind.

Now, Naaman was strong and important — but he had a sickness on his skin that no one could cure, and it made him very sad. The little servant girl saw how sad he was. And even though Naaman was not always kind to her, she wanted to help him. "If only my master would go to God's prophet, Elisha, in Israel," she said gently. "He could make him well."

When Naaman heard this, he decided to go. He traveled a long way to Israel, all the way to the prophet Elisha. But Elisha did not even come outside. He simply sent a message: "Go and wash in the Jordan River seven times, and your skin will be healthy again."

Naaman was angry, and his pride was hurt. The Jordan was just a muddy little river! He had expected something grand. He turned to stomp away. But his servants spoke to him kindly. "Master," they said, "if the prophet had asked something hard, you would have done it. Why not try this simple thing?"

So Naaman went down to the river. He dipped under the water — once, twice, three times... and on the seventh time, he came up, and his skin was soft and healthy and new, just like a little child's!

Naaman was amazed, and his proud heart grew humble. He thanked God with all his heart — all because one little servant girl had been brave and kind enough to point him to God.
