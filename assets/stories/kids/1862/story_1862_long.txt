Naaman and the Servant Girl

In a faraway land, in the grand house of a great and powerful army commander named Naaman, there lived a little servant girl. She was far from home — for soldiers had taken her away from her own family and her own country, and now she lived and worked in a stranger's house, in a strange land. It would have been so easy for her heart to grow hard and bitter. But somehow, it did not. Her heart stayed gentle, and kind, and good.

Now, her master, Naaman, had everything a soldier could dream of. He was strong, and brave; he won great battles, and everyone honored him. But Naaman carried a secret sadness: he had a sickness on his skin that no doctor anywhere could cure. And it troubled him deeply.

The little servant girl noticed how sad and worried her master had become. And even though Naaman was a great man, who was not always kind to a little servant like her, she found that she truly wished she could help him. So one day, gently and bravely, she spoke up. "If only my master would go and see the prophet Elisha, who lives back in my homeland of Israel," she said. "I know God could make him well again through Elisha."

Word of what the little girl had said reached Naaman's ears — and it gave him a spark of hope. So Naaman set off on a long journey. He gathered his horses, and his servants, and many fine gifts, and he traveled all the way to the land of Israel, until at last he came and stood outside the house of the prophet Elisha.

But then something happened that Naaman did not expect at all. Elisha did not even come outside to meet him! Instead, the prophet simply sent a servant out to the door with a short, plain message: "Go and wash yourself in the Jordan River seven times. Then your skin will be healed, and you will be well."

Well! Naaman was furious, and his proud heart was stung. "Is that all?" he huffed. "I am a great commander! I came all this way — and the prophet won't even come out to see me? And the Jordan, of all rivers — why, it's just a muddy little stream. The rivers back in my own land are far grander than that!" And he turned to march off home in a huff, still sick, and sadder than ever.

But Naaman's servants loved him, and they were wise. They came up close and spoke to him very gently. "Master," they said, "please don't go just yet. If the prophet had asked you to do something big, and hard, and brave, you would have done it in a heartbeat, wouldn't you? So why not do this one small, simple thing? Just go and wash. What harm could it possibly do?"

Naaman stopped. He stood very still, and he thought about their wise words. And slowly, he decided to set his pride aside, and to try.

So Naaman walked down to the bank of the muddy Jordan River, and he waded out into the water. And he dipped himself all the way under — once. He came up, and looked at his skin. Nothing had changed. He dipped again — twice. And a third time. A fourth. A fifth. A sixth. Each time he went under and rose back up, dripping and hoping. And then came the seventh time. Naaman went down under the water one last time — and when he rose back up...

His skin was healed! It was soft, and clean, and healthy, and brand new — just exactly like the smooth skin of a little child.

Naaman stared at his own hands and arms in wonder. He was completely, perfectly well! And in that moment, all his pride melted clean away, and his heart grew humble and full of thanks. He hurried straight back to Elisha and said, "Now I know that there is no God in all the world except the God of Israel." And he gave thanks with his whole heart.

And it had all begun — every bit of it — with one little servant girl. Far from home, and not always treated kindly, she had been brave enough, and kind enough, to point a great and proud man all the way to God. And that, perhaps, is the most wonderful part of the whole story.
