Naaman and the Servant Girl

In a faraway land, in the house of a great and powerful army commander named Naaman, there lived a little servant girl. She was far from home, for she had been taken away from her own family and her own country, and now she lived and worked in Naaman's house. It would have been easy for her to grow bitter. But she did not. Somehow, her heart stayed gentle and kind.

Now, Naaman had everything a soldier could want — he was strong, and brave, and important. But he also had a sickness on his skin that no doctor could cure, and it made him very unhappy.

The little servant girl noticed how sad and worried her master was. And even though Naaman was not always kind to her, she wished she could help him. So one day she spoke up, gently and bravely. "If only my master would go and see God's prophet, Elisha, who lives in Israel," she said. "I believe he could make my master well again."

Naaman heard about what the little girl had said. And it gave him hope. So he set off on a long journey, all the way to the land of Israel, with his horses and his servants, until he came to the house of the prophet Elisha.

But when Naaman arrived, the prophet did not even come outside to greet him! Instead, Elisha sent a messenger out with a few simple words: "Go and wash yourself in the Jordan River seven times. Then your skin will be healthy and clean again."

Well, Naaman was furious. And his proud heart was wounded. "Is that all?" he huffed. "I came all this way, and the prophet won't even come out to see me? And the muddy little Jordan — why, the rivers back home are far finer than that!" He spun around to march away in a huff, still sick, and still sad.

But his servants came near, and they spoke to him gently and wisely. "Master," they said, "if the prophet had told you to do something big and difficult, you would have done it gladly, wouldn't you? So why not do this simple little thing? Just go and wash, and see."

Naaman stopped. He thought about it. And slowly, he decided to swallow his pride and try.

So he walked down to the edge of the Jordan River, and he stepped into the water. He dipped all the way under — once. He came up. Nothing. He dipped again — twice. Three times. Four. Five. Six. Each time he went under and came back up, watching, waiting. And then, the seventh time — when he rose up out of the water — his skin was soft, and clean, and healthy, and new, just exactly like the skin of a little child!

Naaman could hardly believe it. He was completely well! And in that moment, his proud heart grew humble and thankful. He hurried back to Elisha and said, "Now I know that your God is the true God, the only God in all the earth." And he gave thanks with his whole heart.

And it had all begun with one little servant girl — far from home, treated unkindly — who was brave enough, and kind enough, to point a great man toward God.
