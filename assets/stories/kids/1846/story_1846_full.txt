David and Jonathan, Best Friends

When David was a young man, he came to live at the king's house. He had grown up as a shepherd, out in the quiet hills — but now everyone in the land was talking about the brave things he had done, and the king wanted him close by. It was a big, new place, full of important people and busy halls. And David did not know a single one of them.

But on his very first day, David met the king's son. His name was Jonathan. He was a kind and gentle young man — older than David, and brave, and good. And from the moment the two of them began to talk, something wonderful happened. They became friends. The very best of friends, the kind that comes along only once in a whole lifetime.

David and Jonathan liked the same things. They laughed at the same silly jokes. They told each other their secrets, and their hopes, and their worries, the way the closest friends do. It was as if their two hearts had been tied together with one strong, warm thread. Jonathan loved David as much as he loved his own self — and David felt exactly the same way about Jonathan.

One day, Jonathan wanted to show his friend just how much their friendship meant to him. Now, remember, Jonathan was a prince. He wore fine and beautiful things, the kind a king's son wears. But Jonathan took off his own royal robe — the beautiful one, right off his own shoulders — and he placed it around David. Then he gave David his belt, and his bow, and even his sword. He held nothing back at all. And that is the gift of a true friend.

Then the two of them made a promise to one another. "We will be friends always," they said, "no matter what happens. You and me — forever." And they meant it with their whole hearts.

But after a while, a hard time came. Through no fault of David's own, it was no longer safe for him to stay at the king's house, and he had to go far away. This made both friends very sad, because they did not know when they would ever see each other again.

So before David left, the two friends slipped away to a quiet field to say goodbye. They held tight to their promise one more time. "Go in peace," Jonathan told him. "God is between us — watching over you, and watching over me — always, no matter how far apart we are." And then, with sad and loving hearts, they said goodbye.

That promise never broke. Not ever. Even when the two friends were far, far apart, each one kept the other safe and warm inside his heart.

For that is what a true friend is. Someone who loves you just the way you are. Someone who shares what they have, and holds nothing back. Someone who stays your friend, near or far, on the happy days and the hard ones too.

And as long as he lived, David never, ever forgot his dear friend Jonathan.
