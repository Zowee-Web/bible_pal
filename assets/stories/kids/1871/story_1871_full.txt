Thomas Wanted to See

After Jesus rose again, alive, his friends could hardly believe the wonderful news. That very evening, they were all together in a room, with the doors shut tight, when suddenly — there was Jesus, standing right among them! "Peace be with you," he said. And oh, how their hearts leaped for joy to see him alive.

But one of Jesus' friends was not in the room that evening. His name was Thomas. He had missed the whole thing.

When Thomas came back, his friends rushed to tell him. "Thomas! We have seen the Lord! Jesus is alive — we saw him with our own eyes!"

But Thomas found it very hard to believe. It seemed far too wonderful to be true. He shook his head slowly. "I want to believe you," he said. "I really, truly do. But I cannot, not yet. I need to see him for myself. I need to see the marks on his hands with my own eyes."

And so a hard week began for Thomas. All around him, his friends were full of joy. But Thomas could not feel it, not yet. He wanted to believe — oh, how he wanted to. But he had not seen Jesus with his own eyes, and his heart was still full of questions he could not answer.

Now, some people might have scolded Thomas for that. They might have told him he should have more faith. But here is the wonderful part of what happened next.

A whole week passed. And then, once more, the friends were gathered together in the room — and this time, Thomas was with them. The doors were shut tight again. And once again, all at once, Jesus was standing right there among them. "Peace be with you," he said.

Then Jesus turned, and he looked straight at Thomas. He did not sigh. He did not frown. He did not say, "Thomas, why did you doubt?" Instead, his eyes were full of kindness, and he spoke very gently.

"Come closer, Thomas," Jesus said. "Here — look at my hands. Reach out, and touch them. See? It is really me. Do not be afraid to believe."

Thomas stepped closer. He looked. And there was Jesus — real and alive, standing right in front of him, just exactly as his friends had said. Every single doubt melted clean away. Thomas's whole heart filled up, and he could only whisper, "My Lord! It really, truly is you!"

Jesus had not been angry that Thomas had questions. He had not been upset that Thomas needed to see. He was simply glad — glad to come close, and to help his friend believe.

And it is just the same with you, little one. If you ever have questions, or if something about God feels too big to understand, you never have to hide it away. You can bring every question straight to God, just the way Thomas did. God is never cross when you wonder. He simply draws near, and says, ever so gently, "Come closer."
