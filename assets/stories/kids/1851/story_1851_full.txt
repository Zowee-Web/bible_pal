Paul Meets Jesus on the Road

There once was a man named Saul. Saul was clever, and strong, and very sure of himself — and he did not like the friends of Jesus at all. In fact, he wanted to stop them, so they could not tell people the good news about Jesus anymore.

So one day, Saul set off on a journey. He took the long road toward a faraway city called Damascus, where he meant to look for more of Jesus' friends.

But God had a very different plan for Saul.

Saul was walking along the hot, dusty road, thinking his own proud thoughts, when suddenly — flash! — a light blazed down from heaven. It shone all around him, brighter than the noonday sun, brighter than anything Saul had ever seen. It was so bright, and so sudden, that Saul fell right down to the ground and threw his arm across his eyes.

Then, out of the middle of the light, came a voice. "Saul," it said. "Saul. Why are you trying to hurt me?"

Saul trembled. "Who — who are you?" he whispered.

"I am Jesus," said the voice — gentle, but strong and clear. "Now get up, and go on into the city. There you will be told what to do."

Slowly, Saul climbed to his feet. He opened his eyes — but everything was dark. He could not see at all! The men traveling with him had heard the voice too, and they were amazed. They took Saul by the hand and led him, step by step, the rest of the way into the city.

For three whole days, Saul could not see a thing. He did not eat. He did not rush about giving orders, the way he used to. He simply sat, very still, in the quiet dark — and he thought, and he thought, and he prayed.

Then God spoke to a kind man named Ananias, who loved Jesus. "Go and find Saul," God told him, "and help him." Now, Ananias had heard about Saul, and at first he felt a little afraid. But he trusted God, and so he went. He came to Saul and gently laid his hands on him. "Jesus, who met you on the road, has sent me," he said, "so that you may see again."

And at that very moment, something like scales fell away from Saul's eyes — and he could see! He could see the room, and the daylight, and the kind face of Ananias smiling down at him.

But his eyes were not the only thing that had changed. Deep inside, Saul was not the same man anymore. His proud, hard heart had grown soft and warm and full of love for Jesus. The man who had once wanted to stop Jesus' friends now wanted nothing more in all the world than to be one of them.

From that day on, Saul — who came to be called Paul — was no longer a man who fought against Jesus. He became one of Jesus' very happiest followers. And he spent the rest of his whole life traveling from place to place, telling everyone he met, everywhere he went, how very, very much God loved them.

For God can change any heart, and he loves to give people a fresh new beginning.
