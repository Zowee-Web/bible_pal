The Two Tiny Coins

One day, Jesus and his friends were at the temple — the great and beautiful place where God's people came to pray and to worship. Jesus found a quiet spot to sit down, and from there he could see the place where people brought their gifts for God. There was a special collecting box, made all of metal, and one by one, people walked past it and dropped in their coins.

Jesus watched them as they came.

First, many rich people came by. They were dressed in fine clothes, and they carried plenty of money. As each one passed the box, he poured in a big handful of coins.

Clink! Clink! Clink!

The heavy coins tumbled in, making a loud and important sound. Some of the rich people looked very pleased, hoping that others would see how much they had given.

Then, behind all of them, a poor woman came quietly along. She was a widow — her husband had died long ago — and she had almost no money at all. She had no fine clothes, and no big handful of coins. In her hand, she carried only two tiny coins. They were the smallest, thinnest coins anyone could have. And yet — those two little coins were every last bit of money she had in all the world.

She came to the box. And without a word, she dropped both of her coins inside.

Ting. Ting.

It was such a tiny, gentle sound. The rich people's coins had gone clink, clink, clink — but hers went only ting, ting, soft as a whisper. Hardly anyone even turned to look.

But the widow did not mind that no one noticed. She had not come to be seen. She had come for one reason only — to give her little gift to God, with all the love in her heart.

But Jesus saw. Jesus saw the whole thing. And a warm smile spread across his face.

He called his friends close. "Do you see that woman, there?" he asked. "I tell you the truth — she has just given more than every single other person here today."

His friends were puzzled. More than everyone? But her two coins were so very small, and the rich people had given so very much!

So Jesus explained, in his gentle way. "All those rich people," he said, "gave only a little piece out of the great big pile of money they had at home. They will hardly miss it at all. But this woman — she was poor, and she gave everything she had. She kept nothing back for herself. And that is why her gift is the greatest one of all."

For Jesus does not measure a gift the way people do. People look at how big a gift is. But God looks at the love inside it. And the woman's two tiny coins were filled, right up to the brim, with love.

So remember this, little one. You do not need to have a lot to make God glad. You do not have to give something big. Whatever you have — even something very small — when you give it with your whole heart, it is precious to him.
