The Red Cord in the Window

In the great city of Jericho — a city with tall, thick walls all around it, and strong gates that shut up tight at night — there lived a brave woman named Rahab. And more than anything else in all the world, Rahab loved her family. Her mother and her father, her brothers and her sisters: she would have done anything to keep them safe.

Now, Rahab had been listening, and she had heard the stories — all the wonderful things God had done for his people. How he had opened a dry path right through the middle of the sea, and how he had cared for them, all along their long journey. And deep down in her heart, Rahab had come to believe. She knew that the God of Israel was the one true God, the God of heaven above and the earth below.

So one day, two of God's people came quietly into Jericho — travelers, who needed a safe place to stay. And Rahab, brave and kind, opened her home to them. Her house was built right into the great city wall, and she welcomed the two men in.

But that very night, the king's men came pounding on her door, demanding the two strangers. Rahab was brave. Quick as anything, she had already hidden the two men safely up on her flat rooftop, tucked beneath some stalks of flax laid out to dry. And she did not give them away. The king's men hurried off to search somewhere else, and the two travelers were safe.

When all was quiet again, Rahab climbed up to the roof to talk with the two men. And she asked them for the one thing she cared about most in all the world. "I know that God is going to give you this whole city," she told them. "So please — I beg you — when you come, will you show kindness to my family, the way I have shown kindness to you? Will you keep them safe?"

"We will," the two men promised her. "We give you our word. Now here is what you must do. Take this bright red cord, and tie it here in your window, where it can be seen. Then gather your whole family inside this house — your mother, your father, everyone you love. And every single person under this red sign will be kept safe. Only stay inside, and trust us."

Then, because her house was built on the wall, Rahab helped the two men climb out her window and down a rope, so that they slipped safely away into the night.

And right away, Rahab took the scarlet cord — red as a sunset — and she tied it tight in her window, where everyone could plainly see it. Then she gathered her whole family inside her house — her mother and her father, her brothers and her sisters, every single one she loved. And together they waited there, all of them safe in one place, trusting the promise of the red cord.

And the promise held. When God's people came at last, they saw that bright red cord hanging in the window, exactly as they had said they would. And Rahab, and all her family — every last one of them, young and old — were brought out and kept completely safe.

Rahab had been brave, and she had trusted God's promise with her whole heart. And because of that one red cord in the window, every single person she loved was saved.
