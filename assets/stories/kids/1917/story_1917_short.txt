The Red Cord in the Window

In the great walled city of Jericho, there lived a brave woman named Rahab — and more than anything in all the world, Rahab loved her family. Her mother and her father, her brothers and her sisters: she wanted, with her whole heart, to keep every one of them safe.

Now, Rahab had heard the wonderful stories of all the things God had done for his people — how he had opened a dry path right through the sea, and cared for them every step of the way. And deep in her heart, Rahab had come to believe. She knew that the God of Israel was the one true God.

So one day, when two of God's people came to Jericho — travelers who needed a safe place to hide — Rahab took them in. And when the king's men came knocking at her door, looking for the two strangers, Rahab was brave. She hid them safely up on her roof, and she did not give them away.

Later, when all was quiet, Rahab asked the two men for the one thing she wanted most in all the world. "I know that God is going to give you this city," she said. "So please — when you come, will you keep my family safe?"

"We will," the two men promised her. "Here is what to do. Tie this bright red cord here in your window, where it can be seen. Then bring your whole family inside your house. And every single person under this red sign will be kept safe. You have our word."

So Rahab took the scarlet cord, red as a sunset, and she tied it tight in her window, where it could be plainly seen. Then she gathered her family inside — her mother and her father, her brothers and her sisters, every one she loved — and together they waited, trusting the promise of the red cord.

And the promise held. When God's people came, they saw that red cord hanging in the window, exactly as they had said they would. And Rahab, and all her family — every last one of them — were kept completely safe.

Rahab had been brave, and she had trusted God's promise. And because of that one red cord in the window, every single person she loved was kept safe.
