The sun hung low, casting long shadows across the desolate road that led out of Jerusalem. The echo of hurried footsteps accompanied the gathering twilight, each pair a captive to the approaching night. Among them, two figures stood forth — a tall, weathered disciple with a weary countenance, and a younger companion, eyes ablaze with fear.

His name was Luke. He had followed the risen Jesus, his heart yet beating from the wonder of the empty tomb. But their joyful meeting had been short-lived. The rulers, enraged by the threat to their power, had cornered them in a desolate valley. Now, with no refuge left, they trudged toward an uncertain fate.

The younger companion, Mary Magdalene, wrung her hands in trouble. She had cleaved unto the side of Jesus through thick and thin, beholding with her own eyes the cruelty of their persecution. Her faith remained unshaken, but the trials before them filled her with a chilling foreboding.

As they journeyed onward, Luke recounted the stories of them that had stood by Jesus, their unwavering faith a testimony to his power. He spake of the centurion who trembled at the sight of the risen Lord, and the women who had followed him from afar. His words offered a flickering light in their growing despair.

The way before them was fraught with peril. The soldiers, their faces pale beneath the twilight, awaited their return. But Luke knew that their hearts were filled with fear, not faith. They had never known defeat, and the thought of losing their dominion struck them with terror.

At length they came unto their destination — a desolate hill that looked out upon the city. Jesus, his voice calm and full of comfort, spake his final words unto his disciples. "Remember that I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world."

His words brought a fleeting peace unto their hearts. They knew that his sacrifice had not been in vain. Though they might never behold his bodily presence again, his spirit would abide with them, guiding them through the days to come.

With one final glance at the city below, Jesus ascended into the heavens. A blinding light filled the sky, then vanished as swiftly as it had appeared. The disciples stood in silence, their eyes fixed upon the empty sky. They had beheld a wonder, but their hearts were filled with a greater sorrow — the loss of their beloved teacher and friend.

The deafening silence that followed Jesus' ascension hung in the air as a suffocating blanket. Luke and Mary Magdalene stood together, their faces pale beneath the pale moonlight. The greatness of their loss began to settle upon them.

They had faith, but their hearts were burdened with the weight of the absence of their leader. The soldiers below would without doubt intensify their search, driven by the fear of dissent. But the disciples knew that they must remain of one accord, to uphold the message of their teacher.

Inspired by the words of Jesus, Luke began to gather the remaining followers. Together they devised plans to continue his work, sharing his teachings and doing acts of kindness in his name. They knew that they must keep the faith alive, even in the darkest of times.

Mary Magdalene, with steadfast resolve, took upon herself the care of the women that had followed Jesus. She gathered them, providing comfort and guidance. Her compassion and strength became a wellspring of comfort unto the grieving hearts round about her.

The disciples encountered many trials in the days that followed. The soldiers pursued them without ceasing, their cruelty growing with every passing day. But the disciples remained steadfast, their faith in Jesus giving them strength.

One night, as they were huddled in a secret shelter, a fiery zeal was kindled within Luke. He perceived that the only way to truly honour the legacy of Jesus was to contend for the oppressed and the lowly. Inspired by the teacher who had given himself up for the salvation of others, Luke became a voice for them that had no voice.

Together, the disciples formed a hidden company, performing acts of resistance and compassion. They laid bare the cruelty of the ruling class, while providing aid unto the poor and the afflicted. Their works sounded forth throughout the land, stirring up others to join their cause.

As the days went on, the disciples came to be known as the early Christians. Their company waxed stronger, defying all expectation. And at the heart of it all was the remembrance of their beloved teacher, Jesus Christ. His words and example continued to guide them, putting them in remembrance that even in the darkest of nights, faith would prevail.
