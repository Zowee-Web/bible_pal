In the beautiful land of Persia, Esther lived in the king’s royal palace, a place full of gold, high ceilings, and thick, colorful curtains. She was the queen, though not everyone knew she was part of God’s people. Her cousin Mordecai also lived there, not far from the palace gates. One day, a sad and worrisome message came. Haman, a man who worked for King Xerxes, had convinced the king to make a rule that would bring great trouble for Esther’s people. Everyone was afraid and did not know what to do.

When Mordecai heard the news, he wore rough clothes and went out into the city crying to show how sad he felt. Many people all across the kingdom were also sad. Esther, safe inside the palace, heard that Mordecai was upset and sent her helper to ask what was wrong. Mordecai sent a message back to Esther and told her about the terrible rule. He asked her to go to the king and beg for help. Esther felt scared. She remembered that anyone who came to the king without being called might be in great danger. But Mordecai said to her, “Maybe you became queen for just this special time.”

Esther thought carefully. She asked Mordecai and all her people to pray and not eat for three days. She promised she would do the same with her helpers. After three days, dressed in her best royal robes, Esther walked quietly to the throne room. The gold shone, thick curtains hid parts of the room, and the air felt heavy. Esther approached, her heart beating fast. The king saw her standing in the inner court and held out his golden scepter. Esther felt breathless relief, for now she was safe.

King Xerxes smiled at Esther and asked what she wanted. She kindly asked if he and Haman could come to a special dinner she would prepare. The king agreed, and Haman felt proud to be invited. During the meal, the king asked Esther again what she wished for. Esther invited them both to a second dinner the next day. Later, Haman walked home feeling happy because he had been with the king and queen. But when he saw Mordecai at the palace gate, who did not stand or show fear, his joy faded.

That night, the king could not sleep and asked for old stories about his kingdom to be read aloud. The tales told of how Mordecai had once helped save the king. The king wondered if Mordecai had ever been thanked and learned that he had not. The next morning, Haman arrived, wanting to ask the king for something unkind toward Mordecai. Before he could speak, the king said, “What should be done for the man the king delights to honor?” Haman thought the king meant him, so he suggested a royal parade. The king said, “Hurry, do exactly as you said for Mordecai!” Haman had to honor Mordecai just as he described.

That evening, Esther prepared her second dinner. The king asked her again what she wished for, promising to give it. Esther gently said, “Please let my life and the lives of my people be saved, for someone plans to cause us harm.” The king was surprised and asked, “Who would do such a thing?” Esther bravely answered, “This enemy is Haman!” Haman was very afraid.

King Xerxes became angry and stepped into the garden. Haman begged Esther for help, but the king returned and saw him troubling Esther. The king gave an order about Haman, and the danger to Esther’s people was stopped. Haman faced the harm he had planned for others.

Mordecai came before the king, and the king gave to Esther all that Haman had owned. Esther told the king that Mordecai was her cousin, and Mordecai became important in the palace. The rule that Haman had made was changed so that Esther’s people were safe. They were very happy and thankful. Many people all around the kingdom learned how kind and brave Queen Esther had been, and they celebrated in joy, because God had helped them in a very special way.

Esther and Mordecai often remembered that day, feeling grateful and quietly hopeful for each new morning that dawned.
