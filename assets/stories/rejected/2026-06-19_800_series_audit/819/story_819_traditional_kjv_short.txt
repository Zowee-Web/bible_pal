Esther dwelt in a great palace with king Ahasuerus. She was queen, but she had a secret: she came of the people of Israel. Mordecai her cousin loved her exceedingly. On a day, Mordecai learned that a sore thing should come upon the people of Esther. A man named Haman loved them not. He devised a plan and bade the king write a new decree. This decree was unjust and should bring hurt upon Esther's people.

Mordecai rent his clothes, and put on sackcloth with ashes, and sat without the king's gate. He was very sorrowful. He sent word unto Esther and told her all things. He besought her to go in unto the king and help. Esther was afraid. All in the palace knew that none might come into the throne room except the king called him. Whosoever should come in uncalled might be sent away. But Mordecai said unto Esther that perchance she was come to the kingdom for such a time as this.

Esther sent word unto Mordecai to gather together all her people, and that they should neither eat nor drink for three days. She and her maidens would do likewise. Then she would go in unto the king, though it were perilous. After three days, Esther put on her royal apparel. The palace was quiet as she went through the heavy curtains. The throne room was overlaid with gold, and king Ahasuerus was set upon his throne. All beheld Esther, and the chamber was still. The king saw Esther and was pleased to behold her. He smiled and held out unto her the golden sceptre. This signified that Esther was safe and welcome.

Esther touched the top of the sceptre and drew near. The king said, "What is thy request, queen Esther?" Esther bade the king and Haman come unto a banquet she had prepared. At the banquet, the king asked again what she would have. Esther bade them unto yet another banquet on the morrow. On the second day, the king asked once more. Esther told him of Haman's device against her people. The king was astonished and stayed Haman's plan straightway.

Esther was brave and kind. Mordecai was safe, and the people of Israel were delivered. The palace was filled with gladness and thanksgiving.
