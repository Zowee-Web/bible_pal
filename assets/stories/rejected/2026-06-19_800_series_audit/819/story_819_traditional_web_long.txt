Esther lived in the grand palace of King Xerxes, surrounded by shiny golden columns and walls covered in bright royal colors. Heavy purple curtains hung in front of tall windows. Each step on the thick carpets made only a soft sound. There were servants with quiet feet, and the rooms smelled of fresh flowers. Esther wore a beautiful dress and a glittering crown. Yet her heart carried a secret hope for her people, the Jews, who lived in many towns across the land. Her cousin Mordecai, who had raised her like a daughter, lived outside the palace gates. He often sent messages to Esther, reminding her that she was not alone.

One day, news swept through the palace like a cold wind. Haman, who worked for the king and wore rich robes, was angry. He wanted things his way. Because Mordecai would not bow to him, Haman made a plan to harm all Jewish people far and near, even those Esther loved. With clever words, Haman convinced King Xerxes to make a new rule. The king took off his royal ring, giving Haman power to write anything he wished. Haman wrote a law and sent it everywhere. The new law frightened the people. When Mordecai read it, he put on rough sackcloth and sat by the palace gate, weeping. Many other people wore sackcloth too, showing their sadness and worry.

Esther’s servants hurried to her and said, “Mordecai is upset! He is sitting at the gate in sackcloth.” Esther felt worried. She sent clean clothes to Mordecai, but he would not wear them. So Esther sent her helper, Hathach, to ask what was wrong. Mordecai explained everything. He gave a copy of the new law to Hathach for Esther. He asked her to go to the king and beg for help. Hathach brought Mordecai’s words to Esther.

Esther thought about what Mordecai had said. She told Hathach, “There is a rule in the palace. No one may see the king unless called. If they go without being asked, the king may not accept them. But if the king holds his gold scepter out to them, they can come in safely. I have not been called to see the king for thirty days.” Hathach ran to Mordecai to tell him.

Mordecai sent back wise words. “Do not think that just because you live in the palace, you alone will escape. If you stay silent, help for the Jews will come from somewhere else, but you and your family will miss this chance. Maybe you have come to this royal place for such a time as this.” Esther listened carefully to Mordecai’s message. Her hands trembled a little, but she felt a new bravery growing inside.

She sent a reply to Mordecai. “Gather all the Jews in the city. Tell them not to eat or drink for three days, night or day. My helpers and I will do the same. Then, I will go to the king, even though it is against the rule. If I must face trouble, I will be brave.” Mordecai did as Esther asked. There was quiet in the city, as people fasted and prayed.

On the third day, Esther dressed in her finest royal clothes. She took a deep breath. The palace hallway was long and silent, filled with the glow of the morning sun. Golden lamps shone on red carpets. Esther’s footsteps were soft. As she walked closer, she could see the king sitting on his tall golden throne. The throne room was wide and grand, with high ceilings and great windows draped with curtains. The king was surrounded by guards dressed in shining armor, standing perfectly still.

Esther stood just inside the doorway, holding her breath. Her heart beat quickly as she waited, still and brave. The king looked up. For a moment, the room was completely quiet. Esther did not speak or move. The king saw her and smiled. He lifted his mighty golden scepter and stretched it towards Esther. At once, Esther came near and touched the tip of the scepter. The king spoke kindly, “What do you wish, Queen Esther? I would give you even half of my kingdom.”

Esther bowed low. She answered, “If it pleases the king, will you and Haman join me at a special banquet today?” The king nodded, pleased, and called Haman. At the meal, the food was delicious, and golden dishes sparkled on the table. The king asked again, “What is your wish, Queen Esther?”

Esther gently replied, “Please come to another banquet tomorrow, with Haman.” The king agreed, and so did Haman, who hurried home that evening, full of pride. Yet when Haman saw Mordecai at the gate and noticed that Mordecai still did not bow, Haman became angry. At home, Haman gathered his friends and told them how great and honored he was. He planned to have Mordecai punished. Still, he waited for the next day’s banquet.

That night, the king could not sleep. He asked his servants to read from the big book that kept the story of his kingdom. As they read, the king learned that Mordecai had once saved his life by warning him about a secret plan against him. The king asked, “What has been done to thank Mordecai?” The servants replied, “Nothing has been done yet.” Just then, Haman entered the hall. The king asked, “What should be done for the man whom the king wishes to honor?” Haman thought the king meant him. He answered, “Dress him in royal clothes, let him ride the king’s horse, and a prince should lead him through the city shouting, ‘This is what is done for the man the king honors!’”

The king then said, “Quickly! Do this for Mordecai. Don’t leave out anything you have said.” So Haman had to lead Mordecai through the city, even though it was hard for him. Afterward, Haman hurried home, still upset and worried. Soon, the king’s servants arrived to take Haman to Esther’s next banquet.

The second banquet was peaceful, with the soft light of lamps. Delicate food filled the table, and Esther sat in her place. King Xerxes spoke, “Queen Esther, what is your wish? It will be given to you, even up to half the kingdom.” Esther answered with care and courage, “If I have found favor in your eyes, please let my life be spared and the life of my people. Someone wishes to harm us.” The king asked, “Who is this, and where is he?” Esther replied, “It is Haman.”

The king stood up in anger and left the room. Haman grew very frightened. He asked Esther for mercy, but soon, the king returned. The king ordered that Haman be taken away. After this, the king gave Haman’s house to Esther. Mordecai was honored by the king and wore royal robes of blue and white, with a great golden crown. The Jewish people felt joy and peace throughout the kingdom. They celebrated with gladness, for hope had come. Esther’s bravery and Mordecai’s wisdom kept the people safe, and God’s care was shown to all.

In quiet moments, Esther looked out her window, thankful. She knew courage could grow even in the most uncertain night.
