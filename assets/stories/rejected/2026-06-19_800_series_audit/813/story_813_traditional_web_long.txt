It was evening by the Sea of Galilee. The sun had dipped low behind the hills, painting the sky in soft purple and deep blue. Jesus was with his disciples after a busy day by the water’s edge, teaching crowds and telling stories. People had gathered from many places to listen to him. As the sky grew darker, Jesus said to his disciples, “Let us go over to the other side.” He wanted to leave the crowds behind and find a quiet place across the sea.

So the disciples helped Jesus into a small wooden boat. The water was gentle at first. Other boats floated nearby, following along. The air was turning cool as night began to fall. As they moved away from the shore, the lights and sounds of the crowd grew quiet. Jesus found a place near the back of the boat and lay down, resting his head on a cushion. He was very tired, and soon he was fast asleep.

Suddenly, without warning, the wind changed. Black clouds rushed across the sky, and a strong wind began to blow. The quiet lake turned wild. Waves started to rise and crash against the boat, spraying cold water over the sides. The boat rocked hard as more wind howled around them. The little boats nearby tossed in the same fierce wind. The disciples tried to row, but the waves were too big. The water was dark and deep all around them. Every crash of a wave shook the boat and made it tilt and creak. Water splashed into the boat, soaking their feet. It sloshed around their toes and splatted onto their clothes. The air was full of noise—wind howling, water breaking, timbers groaning.

The storm grew stronger. The wind battered their faces, wild and cold. The boat rocked and tipped in the choppy water. The disciples could not see the shoreline anymore. The sky was dark and heavy with clouds, and even the stars were hidden. They could hardly hear themselves over the storm. Each time a wave crashed into the boat, more water poured in. The disciples’ hearts began to pound. They worried the boat might sink. They felt tired, cold, and afraid. They had sailed this sea before, but they had never seen it so wild.

While the wind roared and the disciples worked hard to keep the boat straight, Jesus was still asleep. He slept calmly at the back, even as water spattered close by. His breathing was peaceful, and his face was quiet. The disciples looked back at him, amazed he was still sleeping. Their voices rose above the wind as they shouted to each other. They thought about how much water was in the boat, and how strong the wind had become.

At last, one of the disciples ran to Jesus. He knelt beside him and called out, “Teacher, don’t you care that we are about to drown?” The others crowded close, their eyes wide and afraid. The sound of the storm was loud. It was hard to speak, but they needed Jesus to wake up. They all felt anxious. They waited as Jesus slowly opened his eyes. He sat up, yawning, and looked around. He saw the wind, the high waves, and the soaked disciples. He stood up in the rocking boat. The wind whipped his hair and pulled at his clothes, but he was not afraid.

Jesus looked out over the swirling water. He raised his hand and spoke to the wind. His voice was calm and sure. “Peace, be still,” he said. At once, the wind listened. The wild air grew quiet. The waves stopped rising and falling. The water became flat, smooth, and gentle again. The boat rested softly on the calm lake. The dark clouds disappeared, and only the gentle sound of quiet water and a soft breeze could be heard. Everything was still.

The disciples stared at Jesus. Their hearts still beat fast, but now it was for a new reason. They felt amazed and wondered how someone could tell the wind and waves what to do. Jesus turned to them and said, “Why are you afraid? Do you still have no faith?” His eyes were gentle as he looked at them. The disciples did not answer. They looked at each other in awe. They whispered to one another, “Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?”

All around the boat, the night was quiet again. The lake looked as calm as a mirror. The little boats nearby now floated gently, no longer tossed by wild waves. The air felt safe and cool. The disciples’ clothes were still wet, but they could breathe easy again. The fear from the storm left them, replaced by wonder and peace.

Jesus remained with his friends on the boat as they floated on the Sea of Galilee. The storm had come and gone. The lake was calm and safe. Jesus had shown his power, and the disciples remembered how he had brought peace with only his words.

For a long moment, no one spoke. The men watched Jesus as he gazed out over the peaceful water, his face soft in the moonlight. Their hearts were still pounding, but slowly the fear faded, replaced by something lighter—like a gentle glow growing inside them. A small splash echoed from somewhere in the darkness, and one of them reached out to steady the side of the boat, feeling the boards no longer tremble under his hand. They saw their own faces in the reflection on the lake—tired, wet, and wide-eyed. But now there was relief and something like hope reflected there as well.

Peter let out a shaky breath, hardly believing the storm had truly passed. He looked at the others, saw the same silent question in their eyes, but also a hint of a smile twisting on a few lips. The wind was gone, but in its place was a sense of something more—the feeling that they were held safe, even in the wildest moment, even when it seemed the world might sink beneath them. James stared at Jesus, still not finding words, but his shoulders slowly relaxed. Somewhere behind them, another small boat called out, checking if everyone was safe. The disciples answered, their voices steadier now, and the simple sound warmed the darkness.

Jesus, seeing their wonder and relief, sat down again. He looked at each one of them, kind and understanding, as if he already knew all their thoughts and questions. “We will go to the other side,” he said quietly, his tone gentle and steady. The disciples nodded, trusting him in a new way. They took up the oars again, working together this time, not so much with fear but with a quiet sense that all would be well. The night air carried the scent of lake water and cool stone. The splash of oars was steady and soothing.

Slowly, across the quiet Sea, the lights of distant houses came into view. The storm was only a memory now, edged with fear but also edged with faith. As they drew nearer to the shore, each man knew deep down that, no matter what storms might come, the one who calmed the sea was with them. A hush settled over their hearts—a hush filled with quiet hope for whatever lay ahead.
