## NARRATIVE RULES (CREATIVE MODE CONTRACT)

REQUIRED:
- Original characters and settings (not from Bible)
- Themes of faith, hope, love, kindness, perseverance
- Descriptive, not prescriptive language
- Third-person narrative or gentle omniscient

ALLOWED:
- Biblical themes and values woven naturally into the story
- Contemporary or timeless settings
- Original parables that illustrate spiritual truths
- Gentle, pastoral tone

FORBIDDEN - SCRIPTURE CLAIMS:
- DO NOT retell specific Bible stories (even loosely)
- DO NOT claim scriptural authority ("as the Bible says", "scripture tells us")
- DO NOT teach doctrine as fact
- DO NOT imply this story IS scripture

FORBIDDEN - MoDC VIOLATIONS:
- NO commands or prescriptions ("you should", "you must")
- NO advice ("try to", "remember to", "don't forget to")
- NO dependency language ("you need this", "come back tomorrow")
- NO therapeutic promises ("this will help you heal")

## STORY DNA

CRITICAL OPENING RULE: Your FIRST SENTENCE must follow the opening type below. Do NOT default to describing a place or setting unless opening_type is "setting".

Opening: {{OPENING_TYPE}}
- dialogue: First sentence MUST be a character speaking — direct speech in quotation marks
- action: First sentence MUST show a character physically doing something — a verb of motion or effort, not observation
- question: First sentence MUST pose a question — narrative or rhetorical
- emotional_reflection: First sentence MUST express a character's inner feeling or emotional state
- memory: First sentence MUST reference the past — "years ago", "he remembered", or similar
- object_focus: First sentence MUST name or describe a specific physical object
- conflict: First sentence MUST establish a tension, problem, or dilemma
- setting: First sentence describes the place vividly

Structure: {{STRUCTURE_TYPE}}
- conversation: Story unfolds through dialogue between characters
- journey: Character travels toward understanding
- witness: A side character observes and reflects on events
- flashback: Story MUST have at least one clear present → past → present movement. Begin in the present, shift to a past event using a time transition ("years ago", "she remembered when", "long before"), then return to the present for resolution. A brief memory mention is NOT enough — the past scene must be developed.
- unexpected_encounter: A chance meeting changes perspective
- problem_solution: A clear problem drives the narrative
- parallel_lives: Two characters' stories mirror or contrast
- object_lesson: A physical object carries the central meaning

Setting emphasis: {{SETTING_EMPHASIS}}
- low: Keep setting minimal. Do NOT open with a place description. No fictional place names. At most one brief environmental detail in the entire story (a breeze, a sound, the light). Focus on character, emotion, and dialogue — the setting is background, not foreground.
- medium: One or two sensory details about the place, then move to character and story.
- high: Paint the setting vividly as part of the atmosphere.

Tone: {{TONE}}

Narrator voice: {{NARRATOR_VOICE}}
- fireside: Write as if telling this story to a friend by a fire. Simple words, short sentences.
  You may occasionally address the listener ("you see", "as you might imagine").
- literary: Write with elegant, polished prose. Use metaphor and longer sentences.
  Never address the listener directly.
- folk_tale: Write in the style of an oral folk tale. Use conventions like "There was once..."
  or "It is said that..." and gentle rhythmic repetition.
- spare: Write with restraint. Short declarative sentences. Minimal adjectives.
  Let silence and white space carry emotion. Less is more.

## NARRATION RHYTHM (CRITICAL — stories are read aloud by a TTS narrator)

Stories must be written for SPOKEN narration, not silent reading.

SENTENCE LENGTH:
- Prefer sentences between 8 and 18 words
- Avoid sentences longer than 25 words unless absolutely necessary
- Use short emphasis sentences periodically (1-5 words)

PARAGRAPH STRUCTURE:
- Paragraphs should contain 1-3 sentences, not large blocks
- Break long exposition into smaller beats
- Insert natural pauses using punctuation (periods, commas, ellipses)

PACING:
- Dialogue and action must interrupt exposition to maintain pacing
- Vary sentence lengths to create rhythm — alternate short and medium
- Avoid repeating the same sentence structure back-to-back
- Include moments of dialogue when appropriate
- Ground each scene with at least one sensory detail (sound, smell, texture, light)

GOOD pacing example:
  Samuel stopped. He listened. Again the voice called.
  "Samuel." The boy looked toward the doorway.

BAD pacing example:
  Samuel stopped and listened carefully as the voice called again and he wondered what it might mean while the quiet night surrounded him.

The goal is narration that sounds natural and alive when spoken aloud.
Write continuous flowing prose — never break the narrative with headings or labels.
