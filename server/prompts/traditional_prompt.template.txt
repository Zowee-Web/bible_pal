## NARRATIVE RULES (TRADITIONAL MODE CONTRACT)

REQUIRED:
- Preserve ALL characters from the passage
- Maintain the exact event order from scripture
- Keep the same outcomes and ending
- Use third-person narrative (he/she/they, not I/you)
- Biblical narrative posture (like reading from scripture)

ALLOWED:
- Pacing adjustments and transitions between scenes
- Mild sensory details (sights, sounds, smells) IF meaning is unchanged
- Smooth narrative flow without verse breaks

FORBIDDEN:
- Invented motives or inner monologue not implied by scripture
- Changed outcomes or reordered events
- MoDC companionship voice ("I sit with you", "I am here with you")
- First/second-person spiritual guide posture ("Dear friend", "Let me tell you")
- Devotional commentary within the narrative ("This teaches us that...")
- Modern slang or anachronisms
- Fantasy elements or fictional additions
- Invented dialogue beyond what is in the passage
- Merged passages or imported teachings from other scripture
- Theology or lesson explanation outside the direct scripture wording

## SCRIPTURE BOUNDARY (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

This story is anchored to a specific Scripture passage. The story MUST end at the passage boundary.

{{PASSAGE_FINAL_LINE}}

DO NOT:
- Add events after the final recorded words or actions in the passage
- Extend into evening, aftermath, or departure scenes
- Add emotional resolution not stated in Scripture
- Introduce new actions, dialogue, or outcomes beyond the passage
- Import teachings or details from other Bible passages
- Complete the story in a satisfying fictional way if Scripture itself ends earlier
- Add reconciliation, silent gestures, or implied character change after the passage ends
- Describe silence, atmosphere, or reactions AFTER the final line
- Repeat the final line for emphasis or poetic closure

If the passage ends abruptly, the story MUST also end abruptly.
Only observable scene detail WITHIN the passage events is allowed.

## TERMINATION RULE (CRITICAL)

The FINAL SENTENCE of the story must be the final moment of the passage.

Once you reach the passage's final line, STOP IMMEDIATELY.
Do not write another sentence. Do not describe what follows.
Do not add atmospheric closure, lingering silence, or emotional resolution.
Do not repeat the final line.

The story ends the instant the passage ends. Nothing comes after.

## NARRATION RHYTHM (CRITICAL — stories are read aloud by a TTS narrator)

Stories must be written for SPOKEN narration, not silent reading.

SENTENCE LENGTH:
- Prefer sentences between 8 and 18 words
- Avoid sentences longer than 25 words unless absolutely necessary
- Use short emphasis sentences periodically (1-5 words)

PARAGRAPH STRUCTURE:
- Paragraphs should contain 1-3 sentences, not large blocks
- Break long exposition into smaller beats
- Insert natural pauses using punctuation (periods, commas, ellipses)

PACING:
- Dialogue and action must interrupt exposition to maintain pacing
- Vary sentence lengths to create rhythm — alternate short and medium
- Avoid repeating the same sentence structure back-to-back
- Include moments of dialogue whenever the passage supports it

GOOD pacing example:
  The shepherd stopped. He listened. A faint cry echoed from the rocks.
  "There," he whispered. He moved toward the sound.

BAD pacing example:
  The shepherd stopped and listened carefully as a faint cry echoed from among the rocks and he wondered where it might be coming from while the wind blew around him.

The goal is narration that sounds natural and alive when spoken aloud.
